## Logline

A man who has spent a term and a half refusing to be findable, and who last week put his own name on a card in a rail in a building with a plate on the outside of it, is now helping four people open a room that anybody may walk into — and the volume's question is not whether a public technique library is a good idea, and it is not who owns the practice, and it is **whether a room with a door, a key and a bell-push can be public and not be findable, and the answer is that the two have never once been separable in this city and the whole of what this volume builds is the part of the answer that is not a key.**

## Central pressure

Four layers. The first three are load-bearing and the fourth is the volume.

- **Immediate pressure: the room, and the fact that a room is the first thing in this case that has ever cost four people their names at once.** A converted shop unit off a service road in the Lower Wards, four rooms and a back one, a shutter, a counter, nine chairs, a ring binder, a kettle, a plug socket that works and a bell-push that does not ring by itself. **A freehold that is an entry on a schedule of title, a letting clerk of thirty-nine with a key and a file, and a first year at no rent that nobody in the room can find the reason for and that the clerk states in a sentence about a vacant frontage which is true and does not answer.** The lease is the first document in this case with four names on it, and signing it is the movement's cost, and the four names are the first four people in this manuscript who have signed anything together on purpose.
- **Structural pressure: a public technique library has to record techniques, and recording a technique means somebody's words, and whose words is the whole of the argument.** Volume 04's finding was that a rule is only as real as the copy people open. **Volume 05's is the working consequence of it: a library that records a technique in the practitioner who owns it is a place where a person can be found, and a library that records it in the room's own words is a place where a person cannot be traced and the technique cannot be checked.** **The Commons resolves this, in Movement III, by refusing to record anything it cannot take back, and the price is that it has to tell a person what the room will not hold before it takes anything from them at all.** That sentence is the volume's spine and it is the first of the "what the room will not do" lines on the wall in Chapter 210.
- **The private pressure: the word, unchanged, and the findability, spent.** *Confirmed.* Nine, ten and eleven a minute, uncorrectable, told to nobody. **Chapter 200 took the last thing he had — that nobody could find him — and this volume is the bill for it.** A man with a card in a rail is a man who can be asked to open a room, and the room is going to need somebody to be findable about, and the only person anybody in this city has learned to find in the last six weeks is him. **The volume's turn comes when he says out loud, in the room, that this is the same argument at a different scale, and two of the three other people in it disagree with him, and the one who agrees with him is the one who is going to be proved right in Movement III.**
- **The volume's own discovery, and it is the stone it lays for the ending without spending it: *a boundary only exists at somebody's edge, and the person at the edge is the only one who can take it back, and every attempt in this city to put the boundary somewhere more convenient has failed for the same reason in the same way.*** The Return Path was required, is required, and has never been withdrawn, and Volume 04 proved that the illegality is fourteen years of quiet non-compliance with nobody's name on it. **Volume 05 does not repeal anything, does not enforce anything and does not campaign for anything. It builds a room where a person can take a thing back, and the finding is that the room is much smaller than anybody hoped and much more useful, and the door of it is the only new thing in the city in fifty chapters.**
