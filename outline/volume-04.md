## Logline

A man who spent a term and a half making himself unfindable finds out that the corridor nobody could close is kept open by a service plate printed off a company's list, that the only box in this city which can take a name has just been filled in by somebody who was not the person it was for, and that a rule everybody in Nacre has been quietly not obeying for fourteen years was never withdrawn — and the volume's question is not who is responsible for any of it but **who is supposed to be the difference between a form that has one box in it and a form that has none.**

## Central pressure

Four layers. The first three are load-bearing and the fourth is the volume.

- **Immediate pressure: the corridor, and the plate, and the fact that the plate is not a building's.** A wall plate in room **2-07** says **2-14** in a typeface that belongs to a maintenance contractor and not to the building; about four hundred consent leaflets in the rack are all printed 2-07; a clean rectangle in the dust is two inches wider than the plate, which is the width of a different plate; a fault ticket with no name on it came in the week of week thirty-seventh, was read against the wall, was answered with the same number in the same typeface, and was closed, **and that was the correct action and it changed nothing.** **The room is on a shelf, not out. About forty-one people in that district can get out of a thing closed since the Tuesday of week thirty-eight. The only route out of that corridor is a counter on the ground floor with about two hundred forms on it and a box in pencil with a name in it that a third party wrote.** And the plate is not a swap. **It is a service plate. The room has never had its own number on the door.** That is the whole of the first movement's discovery and it is not a conspiracy; it is a list in a company that prints doors for a living, and about nine hundred doors in four buildings, and a landlord who does not care.
- **Structural pressure: a seal is a line on a list, and the list is in another building.** The old network archives are a public reading room with a counter, a card catalogue, a trolley and a rule that is a rule and not a mood: a box does not come down without a slip, and a slip is a piece of paper and a person. **The Marrow Falls material is not in the room. It was never in the room. What is sealed is a list, in a building in Crown Terrace, of what was taken out of this room and when and by whose hand — and a list can be read by anybody who can get into a building, and there is a counter in that building too, and there is no post whose job it is to stand at it.** Nobody in this city can point at a lock, because there is no lock. **The volume's antagonist is a column heading in a register of removals and a habit of not asking.**
- **The private pressure: the word, and it is now a condition of evidence.** *Confirmed.* Nine, ten and eleven a minute on the back of a docket in an inside pocket, uncorrectable, told to nobody, and he has said he will not say it twice. **The consequence has changed form for the third time and this is the form that costs him work rather than comfort: he has talked over three people in a fortnight and has failed to hear one, and the one he failed to hear is the reason a thing was not noticed. In Volume 04 he is the only person who can say what he heard in a corridor, and he cannot hear a corridor, and everybody in this case discovers that the second half matters more than the first.**
- **The volume's own discovery, and it is the stone it lays for the ending without spending it:** *a rule that exists and a rule that is printed in the binder nobody reads are two different objects, and a practice that is quietly not obeying a live requirement is not a technicality — it is fourteen years of not doing something that was never withdrawn.* **The Civic Compact required every technique to carry a visible exit. It still requires it. The public copy in a public room in Lattice Ward says so in print this volume, on a page a member of the public can ask to see. And about eleven thousand premises have been paying a levy for a series about a fifth line, and four hundred and thirty-two cards have gone out of a mailbox, and nobody has broken a rule, because the rule has never been withdrawn and the only copy anybody reads is the one nobody opens.**

7. **The resolution is not a city that agrees to investigate.** The sketch has *the city agrees to investigate the unauthorized amendment*. **Re-based, because nothing in this manuscript may be settled by a form, a minute, a notice, a report, a committee or a leaflet.** What happens instead is that within a fortnight request slips with names on them begin arriving at a counter from people who are not the team; the Continuity Office agrees to nothing and refuses nothing; a man of fifty-eight says one sentence with a count in it; and emergency authority is retained and is stated as retained by a person, out loud, in a corridor.
8. **No new office, no new post, and no person whose job it is to look at a stranger's wrist.** The reading room has a rule and a woman who works three days a week, and the building has a woman with a trolley, and the Continuity Office has a register and a counter, and none of the three is a mechanism and all three can refuse. **The only box in the manuscript is one a clerk rules in pencil with no reference number, and in this volume it takes a name and cannot be corrected.**
9. **No new final enemy, and the volume's antagonist is a column heading.** Threadline's maintenance contract and a landlord who does not care and a habit of not asking are the whole of it. Nobody is a villain, the licensor stays unnamed, and the volume ends with an office and a man in it and a count, and not with a defeat.

## A note on the volume's method

**Volume 03's stated failure mode was a batch of ten chapters in which everybody is reasonable and nothing is at stake, and Volume 04's material is worse than Volume 03's: a volume about archives is a volume that can be ten chapters of rooms, and every one of the four movements has a natural shape that is a person reading a document in a building.**

So the volume's rule is this: **a record in this volume is only ever on the page because somebody walked, carried, signed, refused, or got hurt.** A seal in this volume is a line in a column, not a lock. A room in this volume has a person in it, and the person can refuse, and three of the four people who run the rooms in this volume say a rule out loud and it is a rule and not a mood. **And the volume's second rule, new here and inherited from Volume 03's: a correct action that changes nothing must be on the page at least twice, in somebody else's movement, at a cost to somebody who is not the man who performed it.** The fault ticket closed in week thirty-seven was Volume 03's. The walk-round sheet signed for nineteen years is Volume 04's, and it is a woman of forty-six's, and she has to say a man's name out loud to explain the pencil.

**The volume's method, in one sentence: Volume 04 is the first volume in this manuscript whose subject is a record, and it insists, in every movement, that a record is a person carrying something.**

**And the sentence that governs the whole volume, which is the sentence the volume has to earn and not to announce: nobody in this city is going to say whose practice this is, and in the meantime there is a rack, and about eleven people a week are still waiting, and a rack is not a rota.**
