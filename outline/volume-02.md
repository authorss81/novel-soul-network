## Logline

A man who has spent a term proving that a missing fourth beat was written down on purpose is offered a wage, a training certificate and an address by the company that sold the kit, and has to work out which of the three he is allowed to want.

## Central pressure

Three layers, and the first two are load-bearing.

- **Immediate pressure:** Out of term, four weeks before the new term's intake, a man with no licence, a conditional extension, four named partners who can end his share and a transcript line that stands must decide what to do with a job offer from Threadline — a real wage, a real rota, a real training place, and a condition that he take the company's own course on 4-19 within his first month, which issues a certificate. **The certificate is a door for a twenty-year-old whose middle finger has not moved. That is the whole of the volume's moral centre and it is never said out loud in those terms.**
- **Structural pressure:** Threadline's advantage is not concealment. It is that a standardized technique with a trained holder in it produces fewer reportable accidents, and a city that has just been frightened by a hinge wants fewer accidents. **The company will not be defeated in this volume and must not be.** It will be shown to be a conduit, made answerable on a narrow set of facts, and left standing as a legal and political power.
- **The volume's own discovery, inherited from Chapter 50 and unrepeated:** *a rule that governs a page cannot govern a mouth.* Volume 02's job is to build the things that are left, in public, where they can be refused, and to show that **every one of them turns a person into a row**, and that the man building them is the man best placed to notice.

## Starting state (the Monday of week 15, 09:00)

- **Marek Senn, 22.** No Solo Seal and never one. A provisional Working Share with seven headings, no word *scope*, four named partners who may end it by word, one term, a review, and a **fourth condition that he may not hold a practice alone on Institute equipment, on Institute premises, or under the Institute's insurance, at any time, unlimited by hours** — signed by the deputy registrar and not by the department. A one-term conditional extension on six conditions. **Four people can end anything he does in a fortnight.**
- The maintenance ledger opened on the Monday of week 15 at nine and **nobody came.** The job list is on the wall in a plastic sleeve with his initials at the bottom of the fifth line, and the initials mean *not done*. **Rule 1 is copied from Bea Nunn's board with the credit on it, and the line under it says a rule cannot stop a man in a doorway.**
- **A woman's wrist under his bedroom, unwatched, discharged, unchanged.** A records request for ninety households, in a drawer under a battery. **A blank licensor field in two printed documents.** A boy of twenty whose column two has been broken in silence for a fortnight that ran out on the Saturday of week 15. A woman on a rail finishing on four. About nine flood rounds left, every one a Monday at ten past six. **Thursdays, and *we had an hour*, and two workrooms four miles apart.**
- He is a key-holder by reputation with no register on which the fact can be struck off, and he is about to be handed a **legitimate reason** to do the thing he has refused nine times, and the reason is a man in an office being kind to him on purpose.

## Starting relationships

- **Marek and Talia Venn** are in chosen proximity and in separate workrooms, and **the volume's requirement from the series sketch is that they work together and do not trust each other's judgment.** Milestone 2 (limited partnership) is spent. **Their Volume 02 work is friction with receipts: a request each of them has to refuse in a room with a man who writes things down, and neither apologises, and neither goes and stands somewhere else.**
- **Rafi Pell** is proud to be the first person in the network, counts to two out loud, and **has no fourth beat at all, which is the best news of the term and the sentence nobody believes.** His dependence and recovery belong to this volume and Volume 01 did not begin them.
- **Sera Quill refuses to let Marek bring company policy into Civic Spine 7.** She has the department's demonstration equipment for the term, on a transaction, and she will not read the wall as an instruction and will not sign anything that says what happens on a customer's site.
- **Dessa Kwan, 27**, will not have a kitchen used as a laboratory, and signed her own name on a card in a book for the first time in nine years, and is the most exact critic in the case.
- **Tam Arrick, 20**, is the volume's first cost and its moral centre, and his recovery is permanent.

## Starting power level

**Stage 1, First Knot.** A stable two-person line that can collapse in four seconds. Shared aftertaste that arrives cold and does not lift. Attention debt written by hand. One clear failure on the record with a person hurt, and a second worse failure — that the thing which made him the fourth thing in the room is the thing which made a rail go across a girl — which nobody has asked him about and which he cannot write down.

## Major factions

- **Threadline** — contracted assembler, seller, and now employer. Standardized techniques make work safer, and the company can prove it in a way a college cannot. **Oren Vey is not the author, is not defeated, is not the final enemy, and in this volume does the one thing nobody predicted: he carries the blank field up into his own board.**
- **The Continuity Office** — still declines to trace the licensor, in writing, with a count and a reason. Four assessors. Not defeated, not defeated again.
- **The Choir of Practice** — 212 people, a quarterly to nine hundred addresses, and a ruled empty box headed UPSTREAM LICENSOR. Capable of being wrong, unkind on purpose, and the only body that printed the case.
- **Civic Spine 7 crews** — the maintenance hands, and the ones who pay for every shortcut.
- **The Exchange** — a monthly meeting, four minutes per item, no vote for anybody, a tin, and a four-column book.
- **A new one: the drop-in.** A Saturday free refresher in a borrowed room, with coffee and a certificate at the end. **It is not a conspiracy and it is not a villain. It is the exact mechanism Nkechi Osei named in a repair shop, arriving as a kindness.**

## Midpoint reversal (Chapter 75)

**A district training supervisor has cut reportable accidents on the Meridian runs by sixty per cent in eleven months, and she has done it by making every difficult release wait for a company representative's word.** The crews love her. The numbers are real. The mechanism is the fourth beat with an instructor's cue in it, moved out of a school and into a working street, where there is no panel, no transcript, and nobody whose job it is to say no. **The injury that proves it is caused by the intervention:** a courier who did everything the new procedure asked of him, waited the correct number of seconds for a word, and had the load come down.

## Escalation sequence

### Movement I — The Blank Field (Chapters 51–60, weeks 15–16)

- **Pressure type:** paperwork, character, offer, local work. The quietest movement of the volume and the one that has to be read as a threat.
- **The trust's condition three expires on the Wednesday of week 16**, and Marek asks what a failed condition does after thirteen weeks of writing down that he has not asked. The answer is not a punishment. It is that all five other conditions lapse with it, the ledger stops being open to a department and a trust, the list comes off the wall — **and the trust will lift the other five the same day if the request is filed.** He is handed, by a man who likes him, a perfect reason to convert ninety houses into a career. He does not take it, and the reason he does not take it is not a virtue.
- **The second shelf of the Threadline depot goes upward without a conspiracy.** A visitor column has said the same four words twice, six weeks apart, in two depots in the district, and the four words are not on any Threadline document the service engineer has ever seen. A stock docket, a recycled manifest, and a signature that is not a driver's.
- **Oren Vey comes back on stage** and offers a job in writing: a technician, a wage, a rota, and a condition that Marek take the company's own 4-19 course in his first month. He gives him the **consolidation point address**, which is a customer's and not Threadline's, and he is owed a reply about the blank intake field and gets one.
- **Yvette Kohl, district safety and training supervisor**, appears for the first time in a Saturday drop-in at the Exchange, giving it away, and the room is full, and Rafi is in it, and there is a certificate at the end. **The thing on the wall becomes a place, exactly as a woman of thirty-three said it would, and it happens by being generous.**
- **Tam Arrick says column two.** The fortnight ran out on the Saturday of week 15 and he says nothing until the Thursday of week 16, and what he says is that there is a two-day course with a certificate at the start of next term and he wants it. Marek's answer costs him more than the boy's question did, and he refuses to make the decision for him, and he points him at the one person whose certificate is not in question and is told no.
- **What changes:** he is not solving a supply chain. He is discovering that the two things he built in a term are the two things a piece of paper can take off a wall, and that he is about to be offered a third thing with his name on it.

### Movement II — The First Term (Chapters 61–70, weeks 16–19)

- **Pressure type:** work, training, money, teaching.
- The new term starts. **Forty-one people are taught revision 4-19 out of a manual that is still 3-08**, by a man who has said on the record that he would have said the word again. **Marek cannot teach them: his own fourth condition forbids holding a practice alone on Institute equipment, on Institute premises, or under Institute insurance, at any time, unlimited by hours.** The condition that was written to keep him safe is the condition that keeps him out of the room where it matters most, and he does not ask for it to be read differently.
- He starts at the depot four days a week. He learns what a service engineer already knows: that a standardized technique with a named holder produces fewer reportable incidents, and that this is a true thing, and that it is the true thing the hinge needs.
- **A courier injury** at a yard off the Meridian. **A licensing complaint** that cannot be brought, because a complaint needs a person with a certificate who has been harmed, and there is not one.
- **Relationship milestone: friction with receipts.** Talia is refused a mechanism in Bower Street and Marek is refused a room, and neither of them is wrong, and neither apologises.

### Movement III — The Supervisor's Word (Chapters 71–80, weeks 19–20)

- **Pressure type:** investigation, temptation, public pressure.
- **The midpoint at Chapter 75.** The sixty per cent, the eleven months, the crews who would follow Yvette Kohl into a river, and the injury caused by the pause for the word.
- **The Quiet House leaks Threadline's records** — not the design record, which nobody in the company has ever seen, but a training register, a rota and a set of medical notes on eleven named people. **The leak exposes private data, names workers who did not consent to being named, and endangers the investigation by making every worker in the company afraid to say anything.** Leo Marr is not on stage and the movement is not to be resolved here.
- Oren Vey defends the company and is right about a great deal, and **loses the only thing he wanted: the intake field is completed or refused at intake, on his own authority, because he has been given the reason in four sentences by a third-year he is paying.**
- **What changes:** the case stops being about a school and becomes about a company that is profitable from the pattern, and the question gets harder and smaller: if a company only profits from it, who licensed the first omission into its upstream bundle.

### Movement IV — Isolated (Chapters 81–90, weeks 20–22)

- **Pressure type:** consequence, isolation, recovery.
- **Rafi is isolated with a key-dependent balance technique.** Not by malice. The company puts him on a course, and the course's fourth beat is a supervisor's confirmation, and a boy who has never had a fourth beat at all is the easiest person in the city to convert, because he has nothing to lose and something to be given. **He is the first person in the case to ask a company for a thing and get it.**
- The consequences of the Quiet House leak arrive one at a time and are personal: a worker who cannot pay rent, a woman whose name is in a document she never consented to, a depot that stops answering its own service line.
- **Reuben Daw's six weeks of Saturdays come back on the Wednesday of week 20**, four words in a doorway, and the way back is worse than the way out, and no rule was broken to make it happen.
- **The review in person, at the end of the term, before a board that is not the panel.** He attends and is examined. **Roisin Keogh's return on Rhoda Ilesanmi's forearm reads in public and the first-year's own words are read out by her, not by him.**
- **What changes:** he learns that a certificate is a portable dependency and that the boy he hurt most is the one most likely to want one.

### Movement V — The Recall (Chapters 91–100, weeks 22–24)

- **Pressure type:** public proof, choice, climax, aftermath.
- **The public demonstration of a non-hinged maintenance sequence** in a room that is public because a company has made it public, which is the worst possible reason and the only one available. **Marek can identify a hinge by this chapter. He cannot yet repair a public system, and the demonstration fails in a way nobody planned.**
- **The choice.** Chasing the proof takes him to a building on a corridor with a lot onward, and it is the first time in two volumes he could be the fourth thing in a room and would not be wrong to be. Helping Rafi without further sharing means using a share he has been given, once, in writing, by a twenty-one-year-old who has counted to two out loud in front of witnesses. **He chooses Rafi, and the proof is still there on the Monday because four people went and got it without him, and he has to sit in a room and watch.**
- **Climax.** The recall is won on a narrow set of facts: a local lab, Threadline's relevant supply records, and an intake field that is now completed or refused. The kits go. **The demonstration that was supposed to prove it does not prove it, and the thing that proves it is a stock docket, a visitor column that says the same four words in two buildings, and a woman's four sentences in a boy's handwriting on a card in a ledger.**
- **Concrete resolution.** **Threadline survives as a legal and political power and a conduit. It is not the author and this volume ends without its being the author.** Rafi recovers with a counterpoint routine he wrote himself and joins a worker co-operative that is not the Exchange and not the Choir. **Marek's power state is Stage 2 Counterpoint: he can identify a hinge and he cannot repair a public system.**
- **What is not resolved:** the licensor. The second shelf's four words. Enid Sarkis's wrist. Nkechi Osei's count. The fifth room and who takes the name out. The boy in a corridor. **And the job.**

## Volume climax (Chapters 96–98)

The demonstration room fails, and the failure is not a rail across an arm. It is that four hundred people watch a man try to show a practice that does not exist yet, and the honest thing he can offer is that he cannot do it, and the room has no shape for that. **The proof arrives anyway, at about half past three in the afternoon, from four people who went and got it without him**, and the finding is a building and a blank field and a number, and the company is recalled on it and not on him.

## Concrete resolution (Chapters 99–100)

- **The local lab and Threadline's relevant supply records are exposed and the kits are recalled.** The recall is a company doing the right thing for the wrong reason, and Marek is not the reason for it, and he does not get the credit.
- **Threadline is not the author.** The lot leads to a building and the building leads to a lot onward, and the only man anybody has met inside the company has never seen a page of the design record.
- **Rafi recovers with a counterpoint routine he wrote himself** and joins a worker co-operative. **His routine has no certificate and two named people who can end it.**
- **Marek keeps the job and loses the argument**, and the job is the thing that makes him a key-holder on a company's rota with a supervisor who can confirm his releases, and he takes it anyway, because a wage is a wage and because he is still trying.
- **The fifth room stays shut.** The question of who takes the name out for the people who will sit in rooms that cost them one is answered with **nobody, and a list of the four hundred people who will be told by somebody kind that it was the price of the room.**
- **The Crown Clause is not named.** Iona Sorn is not on stage and is not to be. Leo Marr is not on stage; a Quiet House leak is an event and not a person.

## Power state at volume end

**Stage 2 Counterpoint, with a costly relay and a permanent sensitivity.**

- He can compare several independent lines, **identify a hinge**, and pass a bounded observation through a relay.
- **He cannot repair a public system.** The demonstration in Chapter 97 fails and he is the reason.
- **Disagreement can create paralysis**, and once in this volume it does.
- **Relay privacy is imperfect:** a bounded observation passed through a relay arrives with the shape of the person who carried it still on it, and somebody downstream can tell.
- **Permanent sensory sensitivity:** he can no longer be in a room with a certified holder doing a confirmed release without hearing the word, and there is no way to turn it off, and he says so in a room and is not believed.
- **He still cannot invent a practice.** The counterpoint routine Rafi writes is Rafi's. The one sequence in this volume that is his is a *detection*, not a technique, and it has a name on it that is not his.

## Deviations from the series sketch

Seven, recorded so that the next writer does not read the sketch and undo them.

1. **The starting relationships are Volume 01's delivered relationships, not the sketch's.** The sketch says *Marek and Talia work together but do not trust each other's judgment* — kept, and it is now friction with receipts rather than a fresh start, because milestone 5 already happened and is not a resolution. The sketch says *Rafi is proud to be the first person in the network* — kept, and his dependence and recovery belong to this volume, which Volume 01 explicitly did not begin. The sketch says *Sera refuses to let Marek bring company policy into Civic Spine 7* — kept, and it is the reason **Sera is not in the demonstration room in Chapter 97**, which is the correct outcome and not a hole.
2. **The midpoint supervisor is a woman and the accident rate is real.** The sketch says *a Threadline supervisor*. She is Yvette Kohl, 46, and **she is not defeated and is not exposed as a fraud.** The accident reduction is true and stays true, and the finding is that a true thing and a hinge are the same shape.
3. **The Quiet House's leak is of training and personnel records, not of the design record.** The sketch says *the company's records, exposing private data and endangering the investigation* — kept exactly, and the design record is not in it, because **no one inside Threadline has ever seen a page of it.** The leak names eleven workers. It is Volume 02's cost and it is not redeemed inside the volume.
4. **Oren Vey's job is taken and kept.** The sketch has him offer a legitimate job and frame the hinge as a safety feature. **He does frame it as a safety feature and he is right, and Marek takes the job in Chapter 88 and is still employed at the end of the volume**, which is the correct outcome for a man who has no licence, four people who can end his share, and a conditional extension. **Taking the job is the volume's last honest mistake and it is not undone.**
5. **The climax's demonstration fails and the proof arrives without him.** The sketch has Marek and Talia publicly demonstrate a non-hinged maintenance sequence. **There is no non-hinged sequence.** Volume 01's own guardrail is that he cannot invent a practice, and Rafi Pell has no fourth beat, and the volume's climax is Stage 2 — *he can identify a hinge and cannot repair a public system*. So the demonstration is attempted in public, fails in a way nobody planned, and the finding is brought by four other people, and he sits in a room and watches, and that is the growth.
6. **Rafi is isolated by a course rather than by a technique imposed on him, and he recovers into a co-operative that is neither the Exchange nor the Choir.** Volume 01's own note — *nobody took a vote, and the Exchange has a rule about that and you are not in the Exchange* — is Rafi's sentence for the whole volume, and the ending must not quietly give him one.
7. **Volume 02 ends without the licensor and without Threadline as the author, and it opens a different door.** The sketch's next-volume question is preserved in full. The added door is that **a Quiet House cell has published a counterfeit hinge technique**, so that by the end of the volume the city believes the danger is a group of people, and that belief is about to be worth money to somebody. **That is the bridge to Volume 03 and it is not a new enemy — it is a false copy of an old one.**

## A note on the volume's method

Fourteen of Volume 01's fifty chapters were about a rule, a form, a page, a booking sheet, a register, a reference number, a stock docket, a van book, a visitor column, a quarterly or a ledger. **The failure mode for Volume 02 is to make the paperwork the plot.** It must not be. **Every document in this volume has to cost a person something, be refused by a person, or be carried by somebody who walks.** A document that only moves the investigation is a chapter that should not have been written.
