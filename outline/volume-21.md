## Central pressure

**A sentence that is true, repeated by people who were not in the room, becomes the room's description — and the person who said it first will not say it again, and cannot be asked to, and the room has no way to say anything about being described.**

Volume 20 ended by answering the question Chapter 1000 left open. A man of about thirty-nine stood in a room in Saltmarket on a Friday and said, in about eleven words, that **nothing** is what the room is for, and then you decide; and she refused to be thanked and nobody repeated one word of it. **Chapter 1060 carried that answer's room three hundred chapters after Chapter 759 carried it first, and eleven people in it were asked three things at a bench who had been asked nothing in advance.**

**So the answer was true, and it was the last thing said in that room, and it is now being said out loud at counters in two districts by women who were not in the room. Nobody has lied. Nobody can stop it. A sentence that nobody invented and nobody wants is the most efficient description any room has ever had, because it needs no printing, no list and no office, and it is travelling faster than any of those ever travelled.** That is the engine of this volume, and it is Volume 19's engine — a thing said by mouth is not the thing printed and cannot be taken back once it is out of a mouth — walked one step further, into a place where the sentence is not wrong.

**The contest is not over the sentence.** The contest is over whether people who were helped by a room may say so out loud, once, without it becoming the room's name — and over what happens to a person who did the one thing they did on one night and is now the person who does it.

## The subject, which is coined here, and the question, which is inherited and answered at the climax

**COINED: *a description is not a room.*** The chain of subjects is: Volume 14's *an answer is a shape and a shape can be printed*, Volume 15's *many hands is a figure and not a room*, Volume 16's *a right is not a kindness*, Volume 17's *a practice is older than the question*, Volume 18's *a quiet is not a choice*, Volume 19's *a date is not an agreement*, Volume 20's *a room is not a purpose*, and this one. **It is the exact inversion of Volume 20's phrase and the inversion is the point: Volume 20 said a room is not a purpose, and Volume 21 discovers that a purpose is not what arrives. What arrives is a description, and a description is not the room either.** The string `a description is not a room` is at zero across all one thousand and sixty chapter files on disk, and so are `not a description`, `description is not` and `a room is not a description`. **All four were run against the chapter files and not against this file, before this file was written, and none of the four is asserted here after the fact.** It is coined here and not printed on any page of this volume.

**INHERITED AS A QUESTION AND ANSWERED AT THE CLIMAX:** *did it do anything?* **It is asked on Chapter 1063, at about half past two, of Marek by a man of about forty-four at a counter, and that page does not carry what either of them said about it afterwards.** It is answered at Chapters 1117 and 1118, in that shed, by a person who is not the lead, in five words: **it let me stop.**

**AND THE ANSWER IS PRINTED WITHOUT A COUNT ON IT, AND THAT IS THE FIRST TIME IN SIX VOLUMES.** Volume 20 spent `about eleven words` three times on one answer and `about nine words` forty-five times on refusals, and its close recorded as unsettled whether a count of an answer's length is a description or a frame, and this volume's guardrail 16 forbids the word `true` and its whole family on all sixty pages because the volume's subject is a description. **A volume whose engine is a description declines to put a frame around its own answer, and it says so as a fact about the page rather than as a rule it invented.** No page counts the five words.
