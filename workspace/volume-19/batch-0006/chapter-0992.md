# Chapter 992 — The Room Could Take No More Of It

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then about nine minutes on foot to a first floor off a line in Saltmarket, and that room had about eleven people in it before nine o'clock and one of them had been on the stair since the middle of the afternoon.**

---

That Tuesday took eight callers through that shop and all eight were served without any of them being kept waiting on anything but the till, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and twenty-eight days, two hundred and sixty-one weeks and one day. The request in the drawer in a second district was seventy-seven days old that Tuesday and a hundred and fifty sheets of a different kind had gone out of a door in a fourth district in the morning.**

---

**He had not gone up there to ask her anything. He had gone up there to stand in it, because there had been about four people in it on Thursday and he wanted to know what a full one of those rooms sounds like.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you are stood in the middle of it.**

Marek said: **I am stood in the middle of it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there is no room for that on a Tuesday and there was none last Thursday either.**

Marek said: **that is not an answer.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is the only one I have got and I have got about eleven people through that door since half past five and I have not asked one of them what they came for.**

---

**A man of about thirty was at the wall with his coat still on and had asked for a chair, and a woman of about twenty-seven had been at that wall for about two hours before he came in, and about four of the people in that room have said since that nobody there knew which of them had been there longer.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you can stand or you can go and you cannot have both.**

The man of about thirty said: **I have not asked for a chair. I have asked how long.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there is no time.**

The man of about thirty said: **there has to be a time.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **not in this room.**

Marek said: **then give him an hour.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then it is a diary and it is in the drawer and it has his name in it and the next person reads it.**

Marek said: **the next person would read a name.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is what I have been telling you for six years and you have only just started hearing it.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three came in at about half past seven with his hands in his pockets and did not take his coat off, and about four people in that room have it that he came in knowing there were about eleven people there and came in anyway.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **there is a sheet going up somewhere this week and I have been outside that passage all afternoon watching for it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is not up and I would not put my room on it if it were.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **you have told me about a sheet with a door on it and I have been outside a clinic in a fourth district since two this afternoon watching about nine people go past the other one.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and how many of them stopped.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **about four stopped and about five did not, and they are all stopping at a form that is about nine weeks old.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then it is doing what a notice does.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **what is it doing that is worth paying for.**

---

**She did not answer that, and Marek has said since that the silence went on long enough that he counted about nine seconds of it, and that he did not interrupt because it was not his to interrupt.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I am going to ask you one thing and you are going to tell me no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **ask it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **is one of the three of us holding what she said on that Friday holding it wrong.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **that is not a no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is the only one I am giving and I am giving it to about eleven people instead of to you.**

---

**And Marek asked her the next thing in the doorway on the stair, and she was on the step above him, and neither of them raised their voice about it.**

Marek said: **the sheets have not gone up. A hundred and fifty of them, and about nine of them are in a bag on a counter and the rest are in a box under it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **where.**

Marek said: **on the same wall as the form in a passage in a fourth district, low down, where somebody has to bend to read it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **who put it there.**

Marek said: **the man who put that form up is the one who keeps that clinic and he has not read it and I have not shown it to him.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and did he say anything.**

Marek said: **he is going to ask me whether it is going to be a queue.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you told him.**

Marek said: **I am going to tell him it will be a queue.**

---

**And then she said the thing that ended that Tuesday, and she said it on the stair with her hand on the rail and about four people on that landing heard it and none of them has repeated it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **if about four of about nine people read it this week then I will have to stop running that room and open it on days when it is full.**

Marek said: **you would not have to do that.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I would have to do that, because I cannot have a door that people walk into and cannot come back into.**

Marek said: **that is the cost of it, then.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is the cost of it. It is mine.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and I am not going to have you put it in a list.**

Marek said: **I am not going to put it anywhere.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have a hundred and fifty sheets with a door on them.**

Marek said: **and a door with nothing behind it on the page is the most honest thing this city has printed in four years.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and the first thing that will come through it will not be honest and will not knock.**

Marek said: **I know.**

---

**He was back at that counter at about nine and the till was on and the last of the eight had gone at about half past eight, and the sheet with an empty fifth column was where a woman of about sixty-two had said it should be, on its fortieth night on that table, and nobody had picked it up.**

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and that room was shut at half past six like every other day with a woman of about thirty behind the door, and the binder stayed where it has stayed all winter.**

---

**Two rooms and that yard took the rest of that Tuesday along with the shop and that kitchen, and four jobs went into them.**

A cooker in that second room had been put in with its flex taken through the plaster and no sleeve in the hole at all.

He cut back, sleeved the flex properly and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A flex through plaster with nothing in the hole is a flex somebody will find with a screwdriver one day and not be holding it. Four of those flexes in that street are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has been careful in since the year the roof was done.**"

A switch in that yard had been put in over a cold pipe with the plaster cut away all round it.

He made that pipe safe, filled the hole where the plaster had been cut away and refitted the plate true.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A switch over a pipe is a switch somebody will cut into the day they are hanging a cupboard. Switches in that block are over a pipe, and one of them has been an outside wall somebody has been working at for the whole of the time he has been in trade.**"

A ceiling holder in that shop had been screwed to a plate that was not a ceiling plate and had been painted the colour of the ceiling.

He took it off, fitted a short length of pipe back to the ceiling and hung the holder off that.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A holder on the wrong plate comes down with the first good pull and takes the flex with it. Ceilings in that block are like it, and one of them has been a shop that has had a shade in the air about two years.**"

A meter tail in that kitchen had been cut short and left with the ends bare at the board.

He cut it back to the meter, sleeved it, fitted a gland and proved the board dead.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A tail cut short and left bare is a tail somebody puts a thumb on one day. Tails in that street are cut short, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has kept locked since before the shop changed hands.**"

**That Tuesday's four came to thirty-six pounds between them, exact.**

---

**From half past six on that Tuesday the fourth of those rooms was shut and a woman of about thirty was behind the door, and the binder stayed on the back shelf where it has been all winter, and no figure for what is in it is printed here.**
