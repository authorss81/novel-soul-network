# Chapter 998 — A Pencil Line Nobody Would Name

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and a woman at that counter told a man she had written a line in pencil and given it to one person, and a man who holds a records room came up that stair unasked about half an hour later with nothing under his arm.**

---

That Monday took ten callers and ten names were marked against it, none of them out of order, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and forty-one days, two hundred and sixty-three weeks to the day. The request in that second-district drawer was ninety days old that Monday, and the man it was signed by had been paid for two printings out of his own shop.**

---

**She had not telephoned and nobody sent for her. She came in at about half past seven with her coat off and did not sit down, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had been in that room on the Thursday and had not slept much in the three days since, which is not a thing anybody in that shop said out loud.**

Marek said: **you have done something.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **I have written a line down in pencil and I have given it to one person.**

Marek said: **what is on the line.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **a date that is not the one anybody thought, and a place, and nothing else.**

Marek said: **whose.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **mine and one other person's and I am not giving you the other one.**

---

**And about nine seconds went by at that counter with the till on and neither of them moving, and about four people in that shop have it that she said the next part before he had got to the second half of his face.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **and you are not going to ask me and go and look at that person.**

Marek said: **I was going to ask you.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **and if I gave you the name you would be at their door in a day and they would know they were on a piece of paper, and about nine of us decided in a room about six weeks ago that nobody is finding out that way.**

Marek said: **you decided that.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **I carried it out of that room in my coat. Deciding is cheap and carrying is not, and I am the one who carried.**

Marek said: **you have not said any of that.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **no. And I am not going to, and I have been asked by about four people whether it was worth it and I have sent all four of them away.**

---

**And then Marek asked her the thing that had been sitting in him since the Thursday, and he asked it badly and she answered it straight away.**

Marek said: **am I holding this.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **you have been holding it since a Tuesday when you signed a piece of paper in your own name.**

Marek said: **I did not know that was what signing it was.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **neither did he. He thought he was getting a stranger kept out of it and he did.**

Marek said: **that is the same thing said two ways.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **it is the same thing said two ways and you are the only one of the three who did it.**

---

**And then the door of that shop went and the man of about sixty-one was in it with nothing under his arm at all, and about four people who come to that shop have said that he had come up that stair without being asked and had not sat down.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I have come about the second printing.**

Marek said: **you said you would not print it twice.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I said it to a man I had not met. You have been in my room twice and you have asked me for nothing for about a fortnight.**

Marek said: **I have asked you for one thing and you have given me an answer.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I have given you nine years. That is a longer answer and it is the one I keep on the shelf.**

---

**And then he said the condition, and it was the only condition, and about four people who were in that shop have said since that neither of the other two asked him what it was.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **twice is a thing a landlord does for a person he has met.**

Marek said: **what does it come to.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I will not tell you what it comes to and you will not ask me again.**

Marek said: **I have just told a woman what a hundred and fifty of these cost me.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is your shop and this is a building and a building does not put a shop on its invoice.**

Marek said: **that is decent of you and I am not going to argue with it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **do not hand me a thing I have not picked up.**

---

**And Marek said the cost out loud at that counter in about nine words, and the woman of about forty-five heard it and did not turn round, and about four people in that shop have said that they heard it and that she had been told nothing.**

Marek said: **the second run came out of this shop too.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **you did not tell her whose room it is.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **you have paid twice for a room you have not asked to name.**

Marek said: **she has refused twice and had the better of it twice. I am not asking again.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **that is the first thing you have said on that counter for about a month that I am not going to argue with.**

Marek said: **I know. You have told me.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and the man of about sixty-one left that shop with a piece of paper with a hundred and fifty sheets' worth of settings on it and did not say one word about it, and the sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its fifty-third night.**

---

**One kitchen, one shop and one yard took the last of that Monday, and four jobs went into them.**

A free-standing heater in that shop had been run off an extension lead that was under the rug it was standing on.

He took the lead out from under the rug, gave it a proper plug and proved the heater earthed.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A heater on a lead under the rug is a heater nobody can pull the lead out of. Leads under rugs in that shop are like it, and one of them has been a counter somebody has had a man stand on for about two years.**"

A lamp holder in that shop had been left on a lamp that made the holder hot enough to turn away from.

He took the lamp off, checked the holder against the fitting and put it back.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A holder hotter than the lamp is a lamp on the wrong wattage. Holders in that shop are on the wrong wattage, and one of them has been a counter somebody has switched off about four times a day.**"

A socket in that kitchen had been put in on a wall too close to the sink for anybody to put a wet hand on it and stay dry.

He moved it round the corner, filled the old chase and made the wall good.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A socket round a corner from a sink is a socket somebody has dried their hands on. Sockets in that kitchen are round a corner from a sink, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has kept a tea towel over for four years.**"

A bell transformer in that second room had been mounted on the pipe that the hot water came out of.

He moved it off the pipe onto a cold one, made good both fixings and proved it.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A transformer on a hot pipe is a transformer that cooks itself. Transformers in that room are on pipes, and one of them has been a corridor somebody has not had a bell out of for about four months.**"

**The four jobs done on that Monday came to thirty-six pounds, exact.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six on that Monday with a woman of about thirty behind the door, and the binder stayed put at the back of a shelf, and no figure for what it holds is written anywhere on this page.**

*Conditions of the close.* **By the end of that Monday the book in the green binding had been shut and the tin beside it had its own lid on, and this page prints nothing about what stands between them.

That Monday ended with the room under a building in a first district as dark as it has been since it was made, and nothing at ten that night lit it.
The register of correct acts that changed nothing was at four at the start of that Monday and at the finish of it, and no one in this city puts it in a column. Paying twice is not on it.**
