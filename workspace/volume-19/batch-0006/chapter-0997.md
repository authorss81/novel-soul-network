# Chapter 997 — He Said What The Page Said About Him

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then about nine minutes on foot to a first floor off a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were in a room that holds nine chairs, and a man stood up in the middle of that floor and said one thing about himself out loud and nothing about anybody else.**

---

That Thursday took twelve callers through that shop, and twelve names were taken down on that day's book without one of them being asked about anything, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and thirty-seven days, two hundred and sixty-two weeks and three days. The printed form up on that passage wall was one hundred and seven days old that Thursday and there were about four people in that city who had been told by a woman at a counter where it was.**

---

**He had not told anybody he was going. He went up at about half past six, when the room fills, and about nine people were in it, and about four of them had come through the door off that line without anything on them and had not been asked what they were for.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not said you were coming.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **why not.**

Marek said: **because if I tell a room I am coming then the room is waiting for me, and it is not waiting for me, and I would like it not to have started waiting for me.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **sit or stand.**

Marek said: **stand. It is nine seconds and I would rather not do it sitting down.**

---

**And then he stood in the middle of that floor and about four people who were in that room have said since that he turned round once so that he was not saying it to a wall, and that nobody helped him and nobody tried.**

Marek said: **there is a sheet up in a passage in a fourth district with a door on it and no list on it, and there is a page in a book in a second district with nine names on it, and the door is on the sheet because of me.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **go on.**

Marek said: **I signed a request asking that room to look at that book, and my name is on it, and it is the only name on any paper in this that is not one of the nine's, and it is mine, and there is no page in front of anybody tonight.**

---

**And about nine seconds went on after that, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it in about nine seconds and that everybody in that room was counting.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have said what it says about you.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not said what it says about them.**

Marek said: **no. And I am not going to, and not because I have been told not to. I do not know it in a form I could say out loud in a room.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is the first honest thing anybody has said in this room about a paper.**

---

**And then the room did nothing, and about four people in it have said since that the not-accepting and the not-refusing went on for about four minutes and that the two of them were done at the same time and by different people.**

A woman of about thirty-four said: **he has put himself in the middle of a thing that is not his.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I am not going to agree with that in front of him.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **and you are not going to disagree with it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no. He came and he stood up and he told us what is on a piece of paper. What the room does with a man telling us that is a thing the room has not got a procedure for, and I am not going to invent one at half past six on a Thursday.**

Marek said: **I am not going to argue with that.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then do not.**

Marek said: **I was not going to.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-four asked him the question she had been carrying about since the Tuesday she read it, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody in it heard the answer coming.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and why is it out on a wall in a fourth district.**

Marek said: **I signed it to keep a stranger off it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is nine words and it is the first reason anybody has given me for anything.**

Marek said: **it is the only reason I have.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then it is not a reason. It is a thing you did, and a thing you did can be looked at.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is the whole of what you came up here to say and it took you about nine seconds, and it is not in a room in Saltmarket, it is in every room in this city.**

---

**And then Sera Quill said it from the side of the room with her coat still on, and about four people who were in that room have said that it came in four sentences short of the length everybody in that room had been expecting.**

Sera Quill said: **I am not going to read anything out and I have said that in this room before and I am not going to take it back tonight.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **nobody thought you would.**

Sera Quill said: **I know. That is what I wanted the room to have.**

---

**And then the woman of about forty-five said the thing that was the point of that Thursday, and she said it standing up where she had been sitting, and it came out in about ten words and she has not said it since.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **a person can be found by another who is answerable.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **answerable to whom.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **to the person who looked.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and if that person looks again.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **then she answers again. That is the whole of it and there is nothing else in it.**

---

**And nobody thanked him for standing up, and about four people in that room have said since that the woman of about forty-five said one more thing to him on her way past and that it was not a kindness.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **I have known that since the Tuesday I read it and you are the first person who has said it in a room.**

Marek said: **you knew it and you have not said it.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **no. And I am not going to thank you for it and I am not going to say it was worth it, and about four people have been going to tell me that it was worth it and I have told all four of them where to go.**

Marek said: **you told me you would not be told.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **I have not told you. I have told four other people. You were not one of them and I want that noted.**

---

**And a man at the back of that room said one thing about somebody who was not in it, and the man he was speaking about had said in this city that he would not be in that room at all and had said why.**

Marek said: **he is not here.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **he said he would not be.**

Marek said: **he gave me the reason for it on a Tuesday and I have not argued with it since.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and nobody has asked him twice.**

Marek said: **nobody has asked him twice, and nobody is going to.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and about nine people came out of that room at about half past seven into a street that had nothing written on it, and not one of them looked at the passage wall on the way to the bus, and the sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its forty-ninth night.**

---

**That shop and that kitchen took what was left of that Thursday, and four jobs went into them.**

A lamp in that shop had a rose painted into the ceiling with the edge gone so that it could not be told from the plaster by looking at it.

He cut the paint back round the whole of it, made the edge clean and proved the fitting.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A rose painted into a ceiling is a lamp that can only be found by feeling for it. Roses in that shop are painted into the ceiling, and one of them has been a bedroom somebody has had to feel along the wall for about two years.**"

A plug in that shop had been opened and closed twice with a knife and had been put back with the pins a little apart.

He cut it off, made a proper one on the tail and proved it.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A plug closed with a knife is a plug waiting for a socket. Four of those plugs in that row have been opened, and one of them has been a shop counter somebody has had to keep a spare on for about a winter.**"

A heater in that kitchen had been put in on a lead that ran across the doorway to a socket on the far side.

He re-routed it along the skirting, made a proper end at it and proved the floor dry.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A lead across a doorway is a lead somebody pulls and a floor that is wet. Leads in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a doorway a child has been tripped over for four years.**"

A wall light in that shop had been put in on a junction box with no earth terminal on it at all.

He took the box out, fitted one with an earth terminal and proved it with an instrument.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A junction box with no earth in it is a box that has never been thought about. Boxes in that row have no earth in them, and one of them has been a shop front somebody has had to wash around about two years.**"

**Those four on that Thursday came to thirty-one pounds, exact.**

---

**From half past six on that Thursday the fourth of those rooms stood shut with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the ring binder stayed on the shelf at the back of it, and this page prints nothing about the page in it.**
