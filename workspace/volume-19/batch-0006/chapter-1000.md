# Chapter 1000 — Then Whose Was It

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then about nine minutes on foot to a first floor off a line in Saltmarket, where a book came down off its shelf and was left open on a table with about nine people in the room, and a man asked a woman one question at about half past eight.**

---

That Wednesday took six callers into that shop and six names were entered onto that day's line, and the last of the six was let away at about nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and fifty days, two hundred and sixty-four weeks and two days. The printed form up on that passage wall was one hundred and twenty days old that Wednesday and had been taken down once and put back up by the same man.**

---

**The book came down off the shelf at about half past six and it was not shut again that night, and about nine people were in that room when it came down and about nine of them had come through the door off that line without anything on them.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is the book.**

A man of about thirty-seven said: **it is open this time.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is open this time and I have not read it and I am not going to, and I do not know why it is open this time and I have not asked.**

Marek said: **you have kept that room open for six years and there have been people in it about every four weeks.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have opened the book twice and shut it twice and done nothing else to it in six years, and it was about four weeks since the last time before today.**

---

**And about nine people were in that room at about half past seven and nobody was asked to leave and nobody was asked to stay, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that it went on until about half past eight and that nothing was decided in it.**

Sera Quill said: **I have not read anything out and I am not going to tonight.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have said that in this room three times.**

Sera Quill said: **I have said it in this city about nine times and I would like it written down once and I am not going to be the one who writes it.**

Marek said: **I will.**

Sera Quill said: **no. If you write it down it is a thing you know.**

---

**And then Marek said the thing about the holders, and he said it out loud in a room with about nine people in it and about four of them have said since that it was the first time a piece of paper in this matter had had two holders spoken against it in one sentence.**

Marek said: **there are two people in this city who hold this. She does, because she found it, and I do, because I signed the paper that got it out of a room in a second district.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have both said that to me before.**

Marek said: **not both of them together, and not with the other one in the room.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **where does that go.**

Marek said: **nowhere. There is nowhere in this matter to put two names and there is not going to be any.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then say it again and let about nine people hear it, because that is the only copy of it there is going to be.**

---

**And then he said what was left in the box, and he said it once and out loud and in about nine words, and he did not say a figure and nobody in that room asked him for one.**

Marek said: **that box is empty and nothing is in it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **whose box.**

Marek said: **mine. The first was a fortnight of a counter and the second was five days.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you did not tell me before you paid.**

Marek said: **you would have said no and I would have paid it anyway and we would both have been worse off.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **yes. I would have said no.**

Marek said: **I know. I have been waiting about a month for you to have that view.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you have paid twice to avoid having it.**

Marek said: **yes.**

---

**And the room went on without anything happening in it for about four minutes, and about four people who were in it have said that the thing they remember about that Wednesday was not what anybody said but that nobody left, including two people who came in at about half past eight and stood at the back.**

A man of about thirty-four said: **is there anything we are supposed to do.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **we came for that.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you came for that and you have had it and there was never any more than that. That is not a thing anybody put in front of you to be sorry about later. I have thought about that for about four years and it is the only part of this I am certain of.**

---

**And at about half past eight Marek asked her the question, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that nobody in it knew it was coming, and that he did not build up to it, and that he asked it the way a man asks a thing he has been carrying in a coat for about ten weeks.**

Marek said: **there is a question in this that came to me and it has got no owner.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I know the one you mean.**

Marek said: **you do not, because nobody has asked you it and I have been the one who has not asked you it since about ten weeks.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then ask it and stop standing there with it.**

Marek said: **then whose was it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and nobody in that room was asked to leave it, and the last two people who came in at about half past eight stood at the back of it until the end, and the copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on a table at the other end of that city for its sixty-second night.**

---

**What was left of that Wednesday went into one shop, one yard and that kitchen, and it took four jobs and not a fifth.**

A shop front lamp in that shop had a metal box mounted behind a sheet of perspex, with the box's back against the perspex and nothing between them.

He took the perspex off, moved the box onto the frame and proved the lamp.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A box behind a perspex sheet is a box that cannot be seen and cannot be reached. Boxes in that row are like it, and one of them has been a shop front somebody has had to change a lamp over the pavement for about two years.**"

A yard light in that shop had been fitted with no gland at all where the cable left the fitting and had been left with the cable going straight out.

He made a gland, re-made the entry and proved the fitting with the lamp on.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A cable out of a fitting with nothing round it is a cable that gets wet at the edge. Fittings in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard somebody has had to wipe about four times a day.**"

A lamp in that kitchen had been switched from two places by two two-way switches put in on a lighting circuit.

He took the two-way switches out, put ordinary ones in and proved both points.

"**Ten pounds,**" she said. "**A two-way switch on a lighting circuit is a switch that hands the lamp to a different lamp. Switches in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen where the light comes on in the wrong place for four years.**"

A meter box in that shop had a fuse holder in it with no fuse in it at all and a piece of copper bridging the gap.

He took the copper out, fitted a fuse of the size that board wanted and proved it with an instrument.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**Copper across a fuse holder is a fire waiting for a damp afternoon. Holders in that row are like it, and one of them has been a meter cupboard somebody has opened about four times and shut again.**"

**That Wednesday's four came to forty-four pounds, exact.**

---

**That Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the binder never came off the back shelf, and no figure for the page inside it is printed here or in any file this pass wrote.**
