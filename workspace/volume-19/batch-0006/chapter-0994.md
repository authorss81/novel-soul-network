# Chapter 994 — The Refusal That Was An Answer

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and a man stood in a queue at a desk in a second district and asked one question twice and was told no twice by a woman with a form and five columns in it, and about six people in that queue heard both times and have not repeated either.**

---

That Thursday's callers ran to ten and every one of the ten was written onto the page before going out, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and thirty days, two hundred and sixty-one weeks and three days. The request in that second-district drawer was seventy-nine days old that Thursday and had been asked about once more in about six weeks and had been answered once.**

---

**She had three sheets in a folder in front of her and she had not turned them over, and about four people who come to that desk have said since that the folder had been face down all that Thursday and that she put her hand flat on it once and did not turn it.**

Marek said: **I want to ask you about the mark.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I know what you want to ask me about.**

Marek said: **then I will ask it. Something is written against every row of the sixth column on the third of those sheets. Who put it there.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no.**

Marek said: **you have not said what your no means.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **it means I did not put it there and I will not say who did.**

---

**And about nine seconds passed at that counter with six people in the queue and none of them moving, and about four of them have said since that they each assumed somebody behind her knew the answer and that nobody behind her knew the answer either.**

Marek said: **you have made the word available up yourself. You said that in front of a queue.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I did.**

Marek said: **then you could say no about one line and no about the other and I would hear which.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no.**

Marek said: **that is three times.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **it is three times and I have said the same three words three times and I am going to say the same three words if you ask me on the last day of this city.**

---

**And then she gave him the reason, and it was not the reason he had come for, and about four of the six people at that counter have said since that they heard it and understood it and did not agree with it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **whoever wrote it has not been asked either.**

Marek said: **I understand that.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **then you will understand that you are the wrong person to ask.**

Marek said: **why am I the wrong person.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **because you have got an idea and you have brought it to my counter and I am not going to help you put it on a person.**

Marek said: **I have not got an idea.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **you have had one since you came through the door and you have been in this queue about ten minutes.**

---

**And he did not ask a fourth time, and about four people who come to that desk have said that the whole of what he wanted from that counter came out of him and not out of her.**

Marek said: **then I will not ask you again.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **good.**

Marek said: **and I will say what I am giving up, and I will say it here where six people can hear it and not in a corridor.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **nobody asked you to say it here.**

Marek said: **the mark stays and I stop asking after who.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **that is the first thing you have said at this counter that I did not have to stop.**

Marek said: **I have not thanked you.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. And I have not said anything that deserves it, so the two of us are level, and that is how a queue works.**

---

**The man of about thirty-three was at that shop at about half past seven with his coat off for the first time in about four weeks, and he had the look of a man who had walked a long way and had not decided whether he was glad about it.**

Marek said: **the mark on the third sheet is not yours.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **no.**

Marek said: **you did not hesitate.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have been in that room. I would know it if it were mine and I would know it if it were hers, and it is not either and I am not going to tell you whose it is.**

Marek said: **you have told me two things today that you did not have to.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have told you two things because you stopped asking in front of a queue, and I would not have told you either of them if you had asked a fourth time.**

---

**And about nine seconds went by at that counter, and about four people in that shop have it that neither of them said anything for the length of it and that the till went on through all of it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **there are about nine people in this city who could tell you who wrote it.**

Marek said: **no. There are not. If there were it would have been said.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **then it is a mark and nobody made it on purpose, and that is worse than a person.**

Marek said: **it is worse than a person.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I did not expect you to say that.**

Marek said: **I have been sitting in this shop for about two months waiting for a person to say something worse than a person.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and the woman of about forty-three put the folder away face down with her hand on it, and the sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its forty-second night, and one sheet of a hundred and fifty was low on a passage wall in a fourth district while the other hundred and forty-nine were still in a bag and a box under that counter.**

---

**What was left of that Thursday went into one shop, one room and that kitchen, and four jobs went into them.**

A socket in that room had been put in on a wall where somebody could reach it from the side of a bath.

He moved it round to the other side of the room, took the old one out and made good the two holes.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A socket you can touch with wet hands is a socket somebody is going to touch with wet hands. Four of those plates in that building are within reach of a bath, and one of them has been a bathroom somebody has been afraid of for four years.**"

A lighting circuit in that kitchen had a fuse in it two sizes over what the cable was carrying.

He took the fuse out, fitted one of the rating that cable wanted and proved the board with an instrument.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A fuse that is too big is a fuse that has never been asked to do anything. Fuses in that row are too big, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has had to keep the lights off in since the shop changed hands.**"

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a flex that had been painted at both ends where it came out of the holders.

He cut both ends back, re-terminated them and proved the lamp held its heat.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**Paint on a flex end is a flex end that has been made to look finished. Flexes in that row are painted, and one of them has been a shop light somebody has had to hold for about two years.**"

An earth clamp in that kitchen had been put on the wrong terminal of a board, on a pipe instead of on the earth bar.

He took it off the pipe, fitted it to the bar and proved the earth with an instrument.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A clamp on a water pipe is an earth that works until somebody puts a stopcock in. Clamps in that street are on pipes, and one of them has been a kitchen that has been fine for a year and a half.**"

**Forty-three pounds is what the four of that Thursday came to, exact.**

---

**That Thursday shut the fourth of those four rooms at half past six, and a woman of about thirty was behind that door, and the ring binder stayed where it has stayed all winter, and no figure for the page inside it appears anywhere here.**
