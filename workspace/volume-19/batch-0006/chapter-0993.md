# Chapter 993 — Another Nail Beside That Form

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and a man carried nine sheets on a bus to a passage in a fourth district and pinned one of them low on a wall under a form that is not coming down, and then took a bus to a second district and asked a man behind a desk to print the same thing again.**

---

That Wednesday took seven people to that counter and seven names were taken onto the book's line for it, the last of them at about a quarter to ten, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and twenty-nine days, two hundred and sixty-one weeks and two days. The printed form up on that passage wall was ninety-nine days old that Wednesday and had not been straightened once.**

---

**The passage outside that clinic is about four feet wide and about nine people go through it between four and six on a Wednesday, and the form goes up at the height of a grown man's eye and the address went in below it at the height of a child, because that was the height it went in at.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **low.**

Marek said: **I know.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **say why you have done it low and not at the level of the one above it.**

Marek said: **because at the level of the one above it your form and mine would be read as one thing, and yours is about a thing nobody asked about and mine is about a room a person may walk into, and I do not want them joined on one nail.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **they are on the same wall.**

Marek said: **they are on the same wall and they are not the same notice and now they are not the same height.**

---

**And he stood and looked at it for a while, and about four people who use that passage have said since that the man of about thirty-eight read the sheet twice and that the second time he put his thumb on the line about anybody may come.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **it says anybody.**

Marek said: **it says anybody.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **that is nine years of a clinic and your form has been on my wall since the start of the year and now there is a door under it.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **what do you want me to do about it.**

Marek said: **nothing. I want to tell you what it is before somebody does it for me.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **and if there is a queue.**

Marek said: **there will be one and you should put a bench out for it.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **where would I put a bench.**

Marek said: **under the one about nine people who read it in the morning and said they were looking for something else.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **that is my corner.**

Marek said: **I know it is your corner and I am telling you what is going to happen to it and you can decide what to do with it.**

---

**And the man of about thirty-eight gave the thing he gives, and he gave it in about nine words, and he has given it once before in this matter and both times it was nine years of a clinic.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **nine years of a clinic. That wall is mine.**

Marek said: **it is.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **then leave the low one up and I will not touch your nail.**

Marek said: **I am not going to touch the form.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **I know you are not. I have watched you not touch it for about a year and a half.**

---

**And then he went on the bus for twenty minutes to a second district and up a corridor to a post with the face worn halfway up, and the man of about sixty-one who holds that room was at his desk with a lamp on it and nothing else on it at all.**

Marek said: **I need a second printing of that sheet. A hundred and fifty. The same one.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I printed it once.**

Marek said: **once is a hundred and fifty and it went out of a bag on a bus and about four of them are in a passage and the rest are in a box under a counter.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then you have a hundred and fifty.**

Marek said: **I have a hundred and fifty in a box.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is not the same sentence.**

Marek said: **it is the same hundred and fifty.**

---

**And the man of about sixty-one asked him one question, and it was the first question he had asked Marek in about nine weeks, and neither of them has said since that it was a good one.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **who is paying for the second one.**

Marek said: **not the desk.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then the answer is no and you have had it.**

Marek said: **the answer is yes and you have had that too.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I am not going to have that in my room twice. Once is a thing a landlord does for a room that asks him. Twice is a thing a landlord does for a person he has not met.**

Marek said: **you have not met me.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I have not met you and that is the arrangement and it has worked for nine years.**

---

**And then he said what the first one had cost the building, and he said it in one line and he did not say the figure and he has never said the figure.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **the first came out of a year of paper.**

Marek said: **you do not have to tell me what it was.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I am telling you what it came out of, not what it was, and there is a difference and you will not like the difference.**

Marek said: **I have not come for the figure.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then take your hundred and fifty back down the corridor and do not put them on my desk.**

Marek said: **they are a bag on a floor.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **they are a bag on my floor and I want them off it.**

---

**He was back at that counter at about eight on the Wednesday and the till went on twice while he told nobody anything, and a woman of about thirty-two came in and asked him whether the thing about the clinic was true, because a woman at a bus stop had told her there was a room in Saltmarket where somebody would tell you whether a day was yours.**

Marek said: **somebody might.**

The woman of about thirty-two said: **that is not a yes and not a no.**

Marek said: **no. It is a room and no list and whoever keeps it will not read anything out to anybody.**

The woman of about thirty-two said: **then how do I find out.**

Marek said: **you walk in and you ask, and she will not tell you and that is what you have come for.**

The woman of about thirty-two said: **and if it is mine.**

Marek said: **then nobody in this city will be able to take it off you. I said that to another person in about six weeks and I am saying it again and I still do not know if it is true.**

The woman of about thirty-two said: **you have said that one twice today.**

Marek said: **I have said it to more people than I can be carrying and it has not stopped being true.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and nine sheets went out of that shop in a bag on a bus and one of the nine is low on a passage wall that a man of about thirty-eight has now read twice, while a hundred and forty-one of them are in a box under a counter, and the sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its forty-first night and nobody in that shop knows why it is still there.**

---

**That shop, that yard and that kitchen had about two hours left in them on that Wednesday, and four jobs were done.**

A lamp in that shop had been left with a holder painted round so that it could not be unscrewed by hand.

He cut the paint back off that holder, unscrewed it and proved the lamp would light.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A holder painted in is a bulb that cannot be changed in a room somebody has to light by hand. Four of those holders in that row are painted in, and one of them has been a bedroom somebody has carried a lamp out of since the shop changed hands.**"

An isolator in that kitchen had been fitted on the wrong side of a boiler and had been switched on by somebody at some point in its life.

He took it off, moved it to the other side and proved the boiler cold.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**An isolator on the wrong side of a thing is the one somebody switches first. Isolators in that street are on the wrong side, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has turned off in the dark and not known why.**"

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a lead that had been spliced with tape inside the rose and left that way.

He opened the rose, cut the splice out, re-terminated the lead and made it good.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A splice under a rose is a splice somebody finds when the ceiling comes down. Splices in that block are taped, and one of them has been a shop ceiling nobody has had up since the year the roof was done.**"

A bell in that yard had been left on the line so that it hummed whenever anything at all was switched anywhere in that row.

He put an isolator in ahead of it and proved the bell dead.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A bell that is never quite off is a bell nobody believes is a bell. Four of those bells in that row are on the line itself, and one of them has been a yard somebody has stopped hearing over about a winter.**"

**The four done on that Wednesday came to forty-one pounds, exact.**

---

**On that Wednesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind its door, and the binder did not come down off the shelf at the back, and this page will not put a number on the page it holds.**
