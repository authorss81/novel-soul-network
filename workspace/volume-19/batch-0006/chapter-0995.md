# Chapter 995 — Somebody Told Her Where To Go

**A Saturday was a bench until noon and a counter until four and then four converted units off that service road, and a woman who had heard about a room from a woman at a counter she could not name came in at about half past five to ask whether a day was hers, and a second woman came in at about seven to ask the same question and got a different answer.**

---

That Saturday took eleven callers through that shop and eleven names went onto the line for it, the eleventh of them at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and thirty-two days, two hundred and sixty-one weeks and five days. The printed form up on that passage wall was one hundred and two days old that Saturday and had been straightened twice by somebody who is not in this matter.**

---

**The sheet had been up three days. About four people in that city had come across it, on his own count, and about four of them were the kind of person who reads a thing because they are already looking for something, and not one of them had come to that shop.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **a woman at a counter in a fourth district told me there was a room where somebody would tell you whether a day was yours.**

Marek said: **did she say a day.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **no. That is what she said it was for. She said somebody would tell you whether a day was yours.**

Marek said: **I am the person who put the sheet up.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **I did not think it was you.**

Marek said: **it was me and it went up three days ago low down on a wall in a passage and it does not have a day on it and it does not have anybody's name on it.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **then what would the woman do if I went.**

Marek said: **she would tell you it is not hers, or she would tell you she does not know, and one of those is the whole of it.**

---

**And then he asked her the thing he had walked to the middle of the shop to ask, and she refused it in about nine words, and about four people who were in that shop have said that she did not refuse him unkindly.**

Marek said: **what was her counter.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **I am not taking a name off her for you.**

Marek said: **I am not going to do anything to her.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **you are going to go and stand where I stood.**

Marek said: **I might.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **then she will have told two people instead of one and that is how it starts, and I have been where you are.**

Marek said: **where am I.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **at the counter asking somebody else to carry it.**

---

**And about nine seconds went past at that counter and the till went on through all of them, and about four people in that shop have it that the woman of about twenty-nine said one more thing before she left and did not say it to him.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **she was not the first.**

Marek said: **the first of what.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **the first one I asked. That is twice I have been told where to go and twice it was a woman at a counter and neither of them was a notice.**

Marek said: **was the first one accurate.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **no. That is the thing. She was wrong and she told me it was a room anyway.**

Marek said: **that is worse than an accurate one.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **it is worse and it is going faster than your sheet.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-one came in at about seven with her coat buttoned to the neck and did not sit down, and she had been in that room on the Wednesday before last and had not slept much since, and she asked her question about nine seconds after she got to the counter.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **is there a day on the sheet.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **then how do I know when to go.**

Marek said: **any day. It says anybody may come and it does not say when and it does not say what to ask about.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **that is not a thing anybody can bring a child for.**

Marek said: **no. It is not. I have thought about that for about three days and I have not found a way round it.**

---

**And then she asked the second thing and he answered it before she had got to the end of it, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had clearly been expecting the other answer and had prepared for it.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **will you be there.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **why not.**

Marek said: **because it is a door and not a service. If I am stood in it then people come to see me and not to walk in and ask her.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **and if you were stood in it, would you tell me.**

Marek said: **no. I do not know what it is. If you asked me I would have to say I do not know and that is the second person in a day who has said that to you.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **and I would rather have that.**

Marek said: **I know. You said that to me about six weeks ago.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **I know that you know. I came anyway.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody, and a woman of about twenty-nine went out knowing nothing, and a woman of about fifty-one went out knowing that nobody was going to be there, and the sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its forty-fourth night and had not been picked up by anybody in that shop in about six weeks.**

---

**One shop, one yard and one of those two rooms had about two hours of that Saturday in them and there was not a fifth job.**

A switch in that shop had been fitted over the frame of a door so that the door could not open past about four inches.

He shifted the plate round onto the wall beside that opening and filled the two holes properly.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A switch on a frame is a door that has got used to being hit. Four of those plates in that block are on a frame, and one of them has been a door somebody has squeezed at every time they came in and out of that shop.**"

A lamp in that shop had a rose screwed to nothing but plaster, with the screw turning in the plaster and nothing behind it.

He cut the box out, chased the cavity, fitted a new box and hung the rose on that.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A rose on plaster is a rose waiting for a gust. Roses in that block are on plaster, and one of them has been a shop front somebody has had a ladder under twice.**"

An outside light in that yard had been put in on a lead that lay in the puddle it was meant to light, in the water, under the lamp.

He lifted it out of the water, re-routed it up the wall and fitted a gland.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A light lying in the water it lights is a light that is out. Four of those leads in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard somebody has stood in about a winter wondering why it is dark.**"

Two bells in that second room had been wired to two different transformers with two different polarities so that they sounded at each other.

He put both on one transformer and proved them dead with it off.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**Two bells on two transformers is one bell that hums and one that does not. Bells in that row are like it, and one of them has been a corridor somebody has heard a person coming for about four months.**"

**That Saturday's four came to twenty-nine pounds and went no further than that, exact.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six on that Saturday with a woman of about thirty behind it, and the binder kept to the shelf at the back, and this page says no figure for what it holds.**
