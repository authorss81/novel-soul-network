# Chapter 991 — The Room That Would Not Go On Paper

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then about nine minutes on foot up a first floor off a line in Saltmarket, where a woman has kept a room with about nine chairs in it for six years, and a man went up those stairs with a printed sheet in his coat and came back down them with the same printed sheet.**

---

That Monday took nine callers through that shop and the last of them was let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and twenty-seven days, two hundred and sixty-one weeks to the day. The request in the drawer in a second district was seventy-six days old that Monday and had not been asked about twice.**

---

**He had had a hundred and fifty of them printed, and every one of them carried a door on it and nothing else, and he carried the proof of it up the stairs in his coat and did not take it out of the coat until he was in the room.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **what is that.**

Marek said: **a hundred and fifty sheets. A door on each one and no list. A person reads it and walks to it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and what does it say when the person gets there.**

Marek said: **nothing. It says a room and it says anybody may come. That is the whole sentence.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then it is a true sentence and I am not going to put my name under it.**

Marek said: it is your room.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is a first floor I have been let to sit in. If the landlord lets it to somebody else in two years that sheet is still in a passage telling this city where to find me.**

---

**And he did not argue with that, and about four of the people who come through that shop have said that the thing that stopped him was not an argument and was that he had already thought of it.**

Marek said: **then it goes up without your name on it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it goes up wherever you like and it is not my sentence and it is not my room.**

Marek said: **I have to put something at the top of it. It cannot go out with nothing at the top.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **put nothing at the top.**

Marek said: **people are looking for a thing and if it has no name on it they will assume it is theirs.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then let them assume it. That is the only version of this that does not cost anybody.**

---

**And then she said the thing he had come up with the sheet in his coat for, and she said it standing with her hand on the back of one of the nine chairs.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **paper outlives rooms and I do not own this one.**

Marek said: **no. You did not and I know that and I am not going to argue it a second time.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not asked me a second time about anything in six years.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I noticed.**

---

**And then, because he was standing in her room with nothing to do in it, he told her what the sheets had come to, and he told her in about nine words and did not round it and did not put it in a sentence with anything else in it.**

Marek said: **more than the desk has, and I paid the difference.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **out of what.**

Marek said: **out of a shop's takings. It is a fortnight of the counter.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you have not told them you did it.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and they will find out from the invoice, which is how everything finds out in this city.**

Marek said: **then they will find out from the invoice.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **good. A thing found out from an invoice is a thing nobody has to be thanked for.**

---

**She sat down after that and he stood, and about eight of those nine chairs were empty and one of them was not, and neither of them said anything for a while that either of them has repeated since.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there were about four people in here on Thursday and about nine of them were not on Thursday at all. That is not a fault of the room.**

Marek said: **no. I know that is not a fault of the room.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you know it because you have never once asked me to make anybody come here.**

Marek said: **I am not going to start by asking you for that.**

---

**And he went back down the stairs and across and into that shop at about half past eight, and the man of about thirty-three was at the customer side of that counter with his hands in his pockets, and he had been there long enough that the till had gone on twice without him moving.**

Marek said: **you have been standing there a while.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **since before you got back.**

Marek said: **it is no use to you yet. There is no list and there is no date and there is no time anybody is to come.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **there is a door.**

Marek said: **there is a door and it is a sheet of paper with a door on it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **then somebody will read it who is one of the nine and nobody in this city will have to know she was.**

Marek said: **and nobody will have to know she was, and that is what you have been angry about since the winter.**

---

**And the man of about thirty-three said the other thing then, and he said it the way he says it, and it is the third time he has said it in front of a person who came for nothing.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I am one of three people holding what she said on that Friday, and I am not going to say which of us has it wrong.**

Marek said: **you have said that twice.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have said it twice and I will say it again, and I will not say which, and that is the last of it.**

Marek said: **you could tell me whether it is me.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **no. It is not you. You have never held it.**

Marek said: **I know I have never held it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **you know that because I have told you. I am telling you one thing that is not mine to hold and I am keeping the other.**

---

**And about nine seconds passed at that counter, and about four people in that shop have it that the till went on through the whole of them and that neither of them looked at it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **what happens when somebody walks into that room.**

Marek said: **nothing happens. They walk in and they stand there.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **and what will you have told them to do.**

Marek said: **nothing. I have not told them to do anything because I do not know what it is.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **then you have printed a door for a room with no instructions in it.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **that is the only honest notice anybody in this city has ever printed.**

Marek said: **and I have paid for it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I know you have. It came past you about half past seven.**

Marek said: **then you have costed it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **no. You did not do it in front of me and I have not asked and I am not going to.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and that man of about thirty-three went out at about nine without saying what he had come for, and the sheet with an empty fifth column was still lying on that table where a woman of about sixty-two had said it should lie, and it was the thirty-ninth night of its lying there.**

---

**One shop, one yard and one kitchen took what was left of that Monday, about two hours of it, and four jobs went into them.**

A flex in that yard had been run across a floor and pinned down under the corner of a rug.

He took it up, cut it back to the socket and made a gland at it.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A wire held down by a rug is a wire somebody will tread on for years and never know it is there. Four of those runs are in that yard. One of them has been a floor a child has walked on.**"

A lamp in that shop had its holder screwed to a length of pipe let into the ceiling where no ceiling was.

He took the holder off the pipe, hung it off the rose and proved the lamp.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp on a pipe hangs off the pipe and the pipe is not holding it up. Every one of those in that row is like it, and one of them has been a shop somebody has been lighting by hand since the shop changed hands.**"

A bell in that second room had been wired straight onto the line with nothing at all between the line and the bell.

He fitted an isolator ahead of the bell and proved it dead with the transformer switched off.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A bell on the line hums when nobody is pressing it and after a while nobody hears anything. Nine of those bells in that row are straight on the line. One of them has been a kitchen somebody has blamed on the wiring before either of them moved in.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a lead that came off a joint buried under the paving.

He lifted the paving, dug the buried joint out, made it good and laid the paving back over it.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A joint under paving waits for the day somebody digs, and on that day it is not the digger's fault. Joints in that yard are under paving, and one of them has been a path somebody has dug at for four years.**"

**Thirty-one pounds was the whole of that Monday's four, exact.**
