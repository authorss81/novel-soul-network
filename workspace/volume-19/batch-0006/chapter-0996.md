# Chapter 996 — What An Address Is A Thing To

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then about nine minutes on foot to a first floor off a line in Saltmarket, where about thirteen people went through a room that holds nine chairs, and a woman came into a shop at about half past seven who could have got that number in a week and did not.**

---

Nine callers went through that shop on that Monday and the ninth of them stood at that counter for about ten minutes before she paid, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and thirty-four days, two hundred and sixty-two weeks to the day. The request in that second-district drawer was eighty-three days old that Monday and a hundred and fifty sheets had been in the city for five days.**

---

**There were about thirteen people in that room and nine chairs and about four people who sit in that room have said since that it was the fullest it has been in six years and that nobody in it knew why.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **count them.**

Marek said: **I am not going to count them in your room.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have counted them and it is about thirteen and there are nine chairs and there are four of us on our feet and I am one of them.**

Marek said: **that is not a reason.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is the reason I have not given you the address of this room and it is the reason I am not going to.**

Marek said: **you said the reason was that a sheet outlives a room.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is the reason for the paper. This is the reason for today.**

---

**Sera Quill was at the side of that room with her coat on and had not sat down, and the woman of about sixty-two was at the back with her hands in her pockets, and neither of them said anything at all while that was going on.**

A man of about forty said: **is there a list.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there is no list.**

The man of about forty said: **then how do we know there is room.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you do not. You come and you stand and if there is no room you stand anyway, and that has been the arrangement for six years.**

The man of about forty said: **that is not an arrangement. That is a queue with the word taken off it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is a queue and I have never once pretended it is not, and the bench a man in a fourth district has been told to put out is in his passage and not in this room.**

The man of about forty said: **who.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **a man with a sheet and a nail. Sit down.**

---

**He was at that counter at about half past seven and Talia came in with her coat over her arm and put it on the back of a chair, which she has not done in about four months, and about four people in that shop have said that she looked at the cage down one side of the counter for a while before she said anything.**

Marek said: **you have not been in for a while.**

Talia said: **no.**

Marek said: **anything I can put on the docket.**

Talia said: **no.**

Marek said: **there is a sheet up in a passage in a fourth district and it has a door on it and no list and no day.**

Talia said: **I have read about that. Everybody has read about that.**

Marek said: **there is a woman in a first floor off a line who has been keeping a room open to anybody for six years and I have not asked her to put its address on it.**

Talia said: **I know you have not.**

Marek said: **you do not.**

Talia said: **I did not ask either, and I want that written down somewhere, and I am not going to write it down anywhere.**

---

**And then she asked him to go and look at it with her, and they went on the bus, and about four people who come to that shop have said that the man of about thirty-eight in that clinic let both of them stand and read it and did not ask who the second one was.**

Talia said: **this is low.**

Marek said: **it is low on purpose.**

Talia said: **so that a person has to bend.**

Marek said: **so that it is not read as the same thing as the one above it.**

Talia said: **who decided that.**

Marek said: **I did, and the man who put the one above it up read it twice and did not object.**

Talia said: **that is two men who did not object and neither of them can say what it does.**

Marek said: **it does not do anything. It says a door and that anybody may come and that nobody will read anything out.**

Talia said: **and who is going to be standing behind that sentence on a Tuesday.**

Marek said: **she is, on Tuesdays, for about six years.**

---

**And then Talia said the thing she came to say, and she said it on the pavement with the passage behind her, and she had not said it once before and she said it in one go.**

Talia said: **a printed door can be angry at as well as a person.**

Marek said: **I know.**

Talia said: **you know it because somebody has already been angry at it and told you about it. There is a man in this city who has been reading that wall for a hundred and four days and has not been asked about it once.**

Marek said: **I have spoken to him.**

Talia said: **he is not the one I mean and you know he is not.**

---

**And Marek did the thing on that pavement that about four people who know about it have said he does about twice a year, and he asked her the thing that has been sitting in that shop for about six weeks.**

Marek said: **there is a thing you could do in a week that I have not asked you to do.**

Talia said: **no you have not.**

Marek said: **no.**

Talia said: **I noticed that you had not and I have not said anything about it for six weeks and I am not going to say anything about it now.**

Marek said: **why not now.**

Talia said: **because you have just told me you paid for a hundred and fifty sheets out of a shop's takings and you have not told the woman whose room it is, and if I had done it in a week you would have had to tell her afterwards instead of before.**

Marek said: **that is not why.**

Talia said: **no. It is not. I am not going to tell you why and you are not going to ask me twice.**

Marek said: **I was not going to ask you once.**

Talia said: **you were about to.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about thirteen people had gone through a room with nine chairs in it without one of them being asked what they came for, and a hundred and fifty sheets of a door had been in that city for five days and about four of them had been read, and the copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its forty-sixth night.**

---

**Four jobs went into that yard, that kitchen and that second room on that Monday, and about two hours of it went with them.**

A cooker in that second room had been put in on a ring that was not earthed at all and had been used like that for years.

He earthed the ring off the box, fitted a proper tail and proved it with an instrument.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**An unearthed ring is a ring somebody has been standing over with wet hands. Rings in that block are not earthed, and one of them has been a kitchen a man has cooked in every night for four years.**"

A lamp over a hatch in that kitchen had been wired into a lighting circuit with no fitting in it at all.

He cut the circuit back, fitted a lamp holder and a flex to the circuit and proved it.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A light on a lighting circuit with nothing in it is a light somebody has changed three times and thrown away three times. Lights in that row are like it, and one of them has been a hatch somebody has worked under in the dark for about two years.**"

A socket in that second room had a faceplate fixed straight over a hole in the wall that was never cut out.

He took the plate off, cut the hole out properly, boxed it and made the plaster good.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A plate over a hole is a socket waiting for a screwdriver. Four of those plates in that building are over holes, and one of them has been a room somebody has been plugging in and out of for the whole of a winter.**"

A stair light in that yard had a metal fitting screwed to a plastic box with nothing at all behind the plastic.

He replaced the box with a metal one, earthed it off the board and proved the stairwell.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**Metal on plastic is a fitting that waits for the plastic to go. Fittings in that block are on plastic boxes, and one of them has been a stair somebody has put a hand on about a hundred times.**"

**What those four on that Monday came to was forty-three pounds, exact.**

---

**That Monday closed the fourth of those four rooms at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind the door, and the binder stood at the back of the shelf and did not stir, and no figure for its page is printed here.**
