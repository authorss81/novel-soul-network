# Chapter 972 — She Would Not Lend Her Own Sheet

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and a woman of about forty-three was asked for a sheet of her own before the shutter came down and said no in about nine words, and about four people who were waiting on that counter have said since that nobody breathed for the length of it.**

---

Six people used that counter on that Thursday and the last of them was let away at half past eight, with the shutter an hour and a half later.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and ninety-five days, two hundred and fifty-six weeks and three days. The request in the drawer in a second district was forty-four days old on that Thursday.**

---

**She had come up with a request written on the back of a job card and it was for a single sheet, and she had it ready before she was through the door, and she said the word *sheet* before she said anything else.**

The woman of about forty-three said: I want one sheet of that form.

Marek said: for what.

She said: to print a notice on.

Marek said: you are making a notice.

She said: **the woman with nine chairs has to put a card by a door and a card the size of that one will not tell anybody anything, so I am making a bigger one and I am printing it on a sheet.**

Marek said: and you want it off your own form.

The woman of about forty-three said: **I want it off my own form and I am not going to have it, so say the rest of it.**

---

**And about nine seconds went past at that counter, and the queue did not move, and about four people in that queue have said since that the two of them were both waiting and that it was the woman who had come to ask who was doing the waiting.**

Marek said: somebody will print it for nothing.

She said: no. **Somebody will print it for nothing this month and for something in four months, and I have watched that happen to two other things in this city.**

Marek said: what is on a sheet that makes it worth that.

The woman of about forty-three said: **five columns and a line for a name, and if you take a column away it is still the same sheet with a hole in it, and if you put a notice on it then somebody will fill the hole in.**

Marek said: who will fill it in.

She said: whoever wants it filled in. That has been the answer every time since the winter before last.

**Nobody at that counter said anything for about nine seconds and one man near the back said *that is not a sheet* out loud, and nobody turned round, and the woman of about forty-three did not hear him and Marek did.**

Marek said: then what will you print it on.

The woman of about forty-three said: **on nothing. On a piece of paper with no lines on it. I have got about nine hundred of those behind the tray and I keep them for exactly this.**

Marek said: nine hundred.

She said: **they come in bundles and they are not a form of anything and nobody has ever written a name on one of them.**

---

**And then he asked her the question he had come in with, and about four people at that counter have said since that he had it in his hand and that he did not want to have asked it in that room.**

Marek said: what is in the tray.

The woman of about forty-three said: that is shut for a month.

Marek said: it is five days since you shut it.

She said: **it is shut for a month and it was shut for a month before that, and if you count the days out loud in a queue I will not be here on the day you run out of them.**

Marek said: nobody in that building was told anything.

The woman of about forty-three said: **I know nobody in that building was told anything. I made that sheet and I did not write a name on it and I have not written a name on one in four years, and I am not going to start because a room with nine chairs in it cannot get anybody through a door.**

**And then she put a piece of plain paper on that counter and said it was for the notice and that it was not hers and that nobody was to write a column on it, and about four people at that counter have said since that she said the word column first and that she said it the way she says a word she has been sick of.**

Marek said: no columns.

The woman of about forty-three said: **no columns, no lines, no boxes, and no names, and if one of those four things is on it I will take it off the wall myself and you will not stop me.**

Marek said: that is all four of them gone.

She said: that is what is left.

**Nobody thanked anybody at that counter on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and she took the job card back off him without reading the rest of what he had written on it.**

---

**That shop, a store and two flats took the tail of that Thursday, and four jobs went into about two hours of it.**

A light in that store had been wired through the lamp holder itself, so that changing the bulb meant cutting the lamp out of the circuit.

He took the lamp off the circuit, gave the holder its own way in and made dead at the fitting with a tester.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp wired through its own holder is a lamp that is in the way every time a bulb goes. Four of those lamps are wired through the fitting. One of them is a stair somebody has had to carry a spare bulb up, and it has been four years.**"

A socket in that flat had a fused plug left in it that nobody ever pulled out, so that it could never be used and could never be seen.

She pulled it out, proved the socket and put a blank in its place.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A plug that is never taken out is a socket nobody will ever use and nobody will ever look at. Four of those flats in that block have a plug standing in a socket, and one of them has been a room somebody has run a lead out of for four years.**"

A bell on that shop counter had its wire stapled to the frame in two places, so that the wire had nowhere to move.

He took the staples out, put the wire in a clip and rang the bell from the far end of the counter.

"**Six pounds,**" she said. "**A wire stapled twice is a wire that pulls the bell crooked before it moves. That fault is four times over in that row. The one that has been going longest is a counter somebody has had to lean over to reach for, and it is four years into it.**"

A light in that shop had its switch in a cupboard, so that the light over the bench could only be reached from the doorway.

He brought the switch out to the wall and proved it at the bench.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A light you switch on from the doorway is a light you walk back to the doorway for. Four of those in that block are switched from somewhere else, and one of them has been a bench somebody has kept getting up from for four years.**"

**Twenty-nine pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**That Thursday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder kept its place on the shelf at the back of it, and this page sets down nothing about what is written on the page in there.**

*975.
Thursday of week 324, at ten. That is the two hundred and seventeenth day of this stretch of days. Six callers came through that shop on that Thursday and six names went down on that day's book, the last of them at half past eight.
**No figure was spoken in any room in this city on that Thursday. A woman of about forty-three was asked for one sheet of her own form to print a notice on and said no in nine words, and said a notice printed on a sheet with five columns in it is a hole waiting for somebody with a pen, and gave them plain paper instead and forbade all four of the things that could be written on it.** Twenty-nine pounds, exact.*

*Conditions of the close.* **The green-covered book was still lying open on that table off the line at the end of that Thursday, and the tin that stands beside it stood with the lid down, and this page will not set the two against each other.
That ninth chair went on facing the wall all evening and no leg of it turned, and these days do not name who was the last person to swing it about.
Beneath a building in a first district there is a room and at ten that night nothing in it was lit and nothing in it burns now, and it has been like that from the day it was made.
Four is the number against the register of correct acts that changed nothing at both ends of that Thursday, and not one person in this city puts it on paper. A sheet with four things forbidden on it is not on that register.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **A passage under this one runs through ten objects and never brings two of them into one breath. A rail with two cuts in it. A creased card holding a door open where it always has. A board ruled to nineteen and left. Nine fronts with a corner off each. A shut tin. The shutter. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back nobody has taken down. A slip with a date nobody touched. A pencil in somebody else's margin.
Outside all ten of them stands the cage down one side of that counter, and beside it stands the request lying in a drawer in a second district with a name struck through and a name underneath it. A page of nine names is not one of the ten, is counted into nothing on this page, and is not printed on it.
Paper with nothing on it is not a form and it is not an answer and it is not a record.
A dated rule lies across a question that is not the asker's, and the writing behind it can be read by any person who comes and asks.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody. One woman refused one sheet of paper and handed over another, and neither of them said the word why.**
