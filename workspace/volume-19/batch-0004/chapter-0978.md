# Chapter 978 — Chairs Are Not A List Of People

**A Saturday was a bench until noon and a counter until four and then a warm room with about four people in it, and about nineteen people went through a door off a line that has nobody behind it, and none of them was asked anything at all on the way in.**

---

That Saturday took nine callers, the ninth of them going through at about twenty-five past nine, and it was ten before the shutter came to.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and four days, two hundred and fifty-seven weeks and five days. The printed form on a wall in a passage in a fourth district was seventy-four days old that Saturday and had not been straightened once.**

---

**The woman of about sixty-two was not in that room on that Saturday, and about four people who sit in it have said since that they had all four noticed by about ten past four and that none of them said so to anybody, and that a man of about thirty-three was the only one who said anything about it and only to the woman of about fifty-four.**

The man of about thirty-three said: she is not here.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **she comes when she comes and she does not come when she is asked, and I have not asked her and I am not going to.**

The man of about thirty-three said: it is her son's fault.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is nobody's fault. That is what a fault is for, and I have said that to two men in six years and it is the only sentence I have that has ever been any use.**

**Neither of them said anything for a count of about nine, and they counted the chairs twice that afternoon, and the count came to nine both times, and nobody in that room was doing the count who was there for anything.**

---

**And the woman of about fifty-one was in that room on that Saturday, and she came in at about half past three and sat down in the same place she has sat in three times, and she had her coat on, and nobody asked her to take it off.**

Marek said: you have been before.

The woman of about fifty-one said: **three times at this counter and four times at the sitting, and one sitting was shut when I got to it.**

Marek said: you are the fourth thing in this room that is not what it is for.

The woman of about fifty-one said: **that is the first thing anybody has said to me in about a year and it is the first true one.**

Marek said: why do you come.

**And about four people who sit in that room have said since that he asked it in a different voice from the one he used at a counter on a Sunday, and that it was not a question so much as handing something over, and that she looked at him for about four seconds before she answered him.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **because there is about nine of us and none of us has been told anything and there is a room where a book is opened about four times a year and I would like to be in the room when they find out.**

Marek said: find out what.

The woman of about fifty-one said: **whatever it is they open it for. I do not know and that is why I keep coming.**

Marek said: and if they open it and it is not about you.

The woman of about fifty-one said: **then I will have been in the room, and I will have been in the room on four days out of about nine months, and that is more of it than anybody else has got.**

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds and the woman of about fifty-four got up off her chair at that point and went out to the passage and stood there until the end, and she did not come back in before about four of them had gone.**

Marek said: you should not have to sit in a room like that.

The woman of about fifty-one said: **I know exactly where I have to sit. That is the whole of what I have been doing since before you were at that counter.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-four came back in and put ten sheets on the table, one in front of every chair, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that the sheets were plain and had nothing ruled on them and that she put one at every place in that room including the place behind hers, and that she did not count anybody while she did it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is a sheet for everybody who comes. It has nothing on it and there is one at every chair, and I have never done that before and I am not going to start doing it again.**

Marek said: why not.

She said: **because a sheet at every chair is an invitation, and an invitation is a list with the names left off, and I have not got the nerve for that at six years and four weeks.**

Marek said: what is in the place behind your chair.

**About nine seconds passed in that room and the woman of about fifty-four did not answer him, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that she put her hand on the back of her own chair instead, and that nobody has ever put a sheet at that place in six years, and that the place behind her chair had been there the whole of that Saturday.**

Marek said: I am sorry. That was a question I should not have asked.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is a question everybody asks once. You are the ninth this year and you will be the tenth.**

---

**That Saturday's last stretch went into one shop, one yard and two rooms, and four jobs went into them.**

A light in that yard had been put on a fitting that was open at the bottom, so that rain could get into it.

She put a cover on the fitting and left it out in the rain overnight to see whether it filled.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A fitting open at the bottom is a fitting that fills with water and then with ice. Four of those fittings are open. One of them is a yard somebody has had to keep off in bad weather, and it has been four years.**"

A socket in that shop had been put in under a window with nothing over it, so that a wet hand on a plug had gone straight through to the case.

He fitted an enclosure and proved the case earthed.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**An unprotected socket by a window is a socket that is live and wet. Four of those sockets in that row are under glass, and one of them has been a sill somebody has been careful about for four years.**"

A switch in one of those rooms had its faceplate fixed over a hole in the plaster that had never been filled, so that the box showed through the plate.

She filled the hole, sanded it flat and screwed the plate back on square.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A plate over a hole is a hole with a lid. Four of those plates in that row are like it, and the one I would not leave is a landing somebody has had to look at twice for four years.**"

A lamp in the second of those rooms had been fitted with a holder that was the wrong pattern for the shade, so that the shade hung loose on it.

He changed the holder and proved the lamp lit.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A shade loose on its holder is a shade waiting to come down on somebody. Four of those in that row are loose, and one of them has been a front room somebody has kept the shade off for four years.**"

**Thirty-eight pounds is what those four came to on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**That Saturday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on the shelf at the back where it stays, and this page offers no figure for the page inside it.**
