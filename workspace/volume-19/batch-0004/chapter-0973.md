# Chapter 973 — A Word Nobody Had Written Down

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and the room off the line emptied down to about four people by half past four, and about eleven of them had come through that door on that day without a paper on them and not one of them had been asked what they were for.**

---

That Friday put nine names on that shop's day book and the ninth of them was taken at about twenty-five past nine, with the shutter down by ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and ninety-six days, two hundred and fifty-six weeks and four days. The request in the drawer in a second district was forty-five days old on that Friday.**

---

**He had been in that room for about half an hour before the woman of about sixty-two stood up, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that she had not asked him a single question all the time and that he had said nothing to her either, and that at about half past four she put a hand on the back of the chair in front of her and got herself up off a seat she had been in for an hour.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: I am going to say one word and then I am going to go.

Marek said: you do not have to.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **I have had it since a Wednesday in the spring four years back and I have said it to nobody and I am saying it here because there is nobody in here.**

Marek said: nobody in here is not the same as nobody.

She said: no. It is not.

---

**And she said it, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that it took about four seconds and that it was one word and that none of the four of them could afterwards agree what it was, and that two of them think it was about four years ago and two of them think it was on the day itself.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **asked.**

**About nine seconds went past in that room and Marek did not use any of them, and he has said since that the word was not about the nine and that he could not work out what it was about and that he wanted very much to ask and did not.**

Marek said: who did not ask.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **a man in a corridor, in a building, on a Wednesday, and he said the word to me and he did not say it to my sister.**

Marek said: he said it to you and not to her.

She said: he said *asked* to me. **He never said it to her and he never said anything else to either of us, and I have gone over that corridor about nine times since and there is nobody at the end of it now.**

Marek said: and you have carried that word.

She said: I have carried it.

Marek said: since the spring.

She said: **since the Wednesday. It has been in that flat with me and it has not been on a bit of paper and it has not been in a drawer and it has not been on a shelf.**

**Not one person in that room spoke for about nine seconds, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that the woman of about fifty-four looked at the floor for the whole of it and that afterwards she did not look up and did not write and did not put a card on the table.**

Marek said: what do you want done with it.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **nothing. I have wanted it said and it is said and I want to go home now and I have wanted to go home for about an hour.**

---

**And then nobody asked her to sit down again, and nobody said one word to her about it, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that she got to the door in about four seconds and that the door was heavy and that she had to lift it with both hands and that nobody offered to.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: you are not going to write that down.

Marek said: no.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you are not going to say it to anybody in this building.**

Marek said: no.

The woman of about fifty-four said: good. **If it is on anything in here then it is on a card by the door and the next person who reads that card reads that word, and I have not written a list in six years and I am not starting with a woman's word.**

**Nobody thanked that woman on that Friday as she went out, and nobody asked her to wait, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that saying nothing there was the correct thing to do and that not one of them has ever said so out loud, and that she was the eleventh person to come through that door that week and the only one who gave anything away.**

---

**What was left of that Friday was one shop, one landing and a yard, and four jobs went into it.**

A light on that landing had its switch on the wrong side of the door frame, so that it was lit from the landing and not from the room.

He moved the switch and proved it from inside the room.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A light switched from the wrong side is a light somebody cannot turn off without standing in the cold. Four of those landings in that block are like that, and one of them has been a door somebody has shut on for four years.**"

A switch in that shop had come loose from its plate and was hanging by its own flex, so that it moved the fitting only when it happened to be hanging square.

She fixed the plate and proved it both ways with a lamp.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A switch hanging by its own flex is a switch that works when it feels like it. Four of those in that row are loose, and one of them has been a light somebody has argued with for four years.**"

An outside light at the back of that shop had been wired from a socket inside, so that a lamp outside went dark every time a kettle came on.

She gave the outside light its own way in and proved it with the row on.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**An outside light on an inside way is a yard you cannot see from the yard. Four of those lights in that row are like it, and one of them has been a back door somebody has not been able to open after dark for four years.**"

A meter cupboard in that shop had its door off its latch, so that anybody coming in could get at the terminals behind it.

He re-latched it and proved the cupboard dead with a tester.

"**Four pounds,**" she said. "**A meter cupboard that a stranger can open is a cupboard with somebody else's money in it. Four of those in that row are off their latches, and one of them has been a shop somebody has had to keep an eye on for four years.**"

**Thirty-one pounds is what that Friday's four came to, exact.**

---

**That Friday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the ring binder stayed on its shelf at the back of it, and this page gives no figure for the page that is in it.**
