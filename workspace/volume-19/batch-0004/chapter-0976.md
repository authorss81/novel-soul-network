# Chapter 976 — Nobody Can Unhear It Now

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and a man of about thirty-three came in at about half past four with something he wanted to hand over, and about four people in that shop have said since that he had it folded in his coat and that he took it out twice and put it back twice.**

---

That Thursday brought nine through that shop, and the ninth of them at about twenty-five past nine, with that shutter down by ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and two days, two hundred and fifty-seven weeks and three days. The request in the drawer in a second district was fifty-one days old on that Thursday.**

---

**He had not been in that shop for a fortnight and he came straight to the rail and did not take his coat off, and he had the air of a man who has driven somewhere to do a thing and has stopped doing it about halfway back.**

The man of about thirty-three said: I have come about a room.

Marek said: which room.

The man of about thirty-three said: **the one with nine chairs in it. I have walked past it four times and I have been in it once and anybody may walk in.**

Marek said: yes.

The man of about thirty-three said: **then there is a thing I have not been able to give away for a fortnight that I can put in there and nobody can say I have given it to the wrong person.**

Marek said: what is the thing.

The man of about thirty-three said: **the only copy there is of that sheet with nothing in the fifth column. It is lying on your table and it has been lying there a fortnight.**

---

**And then Marek said no, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it in about four seconds and that he said it before he had sat down and that the man of about thirty-three took his coat back off the rail and put it on without being asked.**

Marek said: no.

The man of about thirty-three said: **a room that anybody may walk into is the only kind of room in this city where a piece of paper cannot hurt anybody.**

Marek said: it can hurt nine people.

The man of about thirty-three said: it is a sheet with nothing written in it.

Marek said: **it is not nothing. It is a front with a fifth column on it, and a front is not a secret, and the day it stands in a room anybody walks into is the day it is a fact about nine people in a room with about nine other people in it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: it is a fact nobody can put a name to.

Marek said: **a fact nobody can put a name to is still a fact, and putting it where nobody can reach it is the only reason it has not done anything yet.**

**About nine seconds passed in that shop with the two of them not talking, and the man of about thirty-three took the folded paper out of his coat at that point and put it on the counter, and did not push it and did not take it back.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **do you know what I am. I am the man who put a sheet down and walked out of it.**

Marek said: you are.

The man of about thirty-three said: and I have been not able to sleep in a way I could describe, and you have had a sheet on your table for a fortnight doing nothing at all.

Marek said: it is not doing nothing.

The man of about thirty-three said: **that is the same sentence four people have said to me about other things and I did not believe it of them either.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three told him something he had told nobody, and he said it flatly and without any weight on it at all, as though he had read it off a job card.**

The man of about thirty-three said: she was in the room on Friday.

Marek said: which woman.

The man of about thirty-three said: **the one of about sixty-two. She is not one of the nine and she has never been near any of it and she sat down at the back of that room and said one word.**

Marek said: how do you know how many people she is.

The man of about thirty-three said: **she is my mother's.**

Marek said: you did not tell me that.

The man of about thirty-three said: **there is nothing to tell. She has never been on a sheet with nine rows on it and neither have I and neither has my mother and that is why she has never asked anybody for anything in her life.**

Marek said: how many people know that word.

The man of about thirty-three said: **I do not know. I know three. One of the three has it wrong.**

Marek said: wrong how.

The man of about thirty-three said: **he has it as a thing he was told about the nine and it is not a thing about the nine and by the end of this week he will have it as one, and he is a man who tells people things he is sure about, and he is sure about it because I am.**

**He took nine seconds over it and used none of them in words, and about four people in that room have said since that Marek put his hand flat on that counter where a sheet with nine rows on it had lain once, and that his hands were no good that afternoon and that he left the hand there.**

Marek said: tell him.

The man of about thirty-three said: **he will ask me where I got it and I will say my mother and then it is a thing a man said to another man over a table, and that is how it got here in the first place.**

Marek said: then tell him it is yours and not hers.

The man of about thirty-three said: **he will say that is the same thing. It is nearly the same thing. I have been sitting with that since Friday.**

---

**That Thursday went, at the end, into one shop, a second shop and a stair, and four jobs went into those three.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in with the neutral and the earth the wrong way round, so that the earth was live whenever the lamp was off.

He put the earth back where it belonged and left the tester sitting on the bench while he worked.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**An earth on the switched side is a live fitting with the switch off. Four of those lamps are wired that way. One of them is a bench somebody has not wanted to put a hand near, and it has been four years.**"

A switch in that second shop had been fitted into a box with the plaster pushed in behind it and cracked, so that the box was loose in the wall.

She packed the box and proved it with a lamp.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A box loose in the wall is a switch that works loose in the wall. Four of those boxes in that row are packed badly, and one of them has been a landing somebody has had to hold the switch for four years.**"

A bell on that stair had its cable run through a hole in the frame with no grommet on it, so that the cable wore where it went through.

She fitted a grommet and pushed the cable back and forth through the hole until the grommet took the wear.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A cable with no grommet is a cable that wears through in about a year. That is four of those runs in that stair, all like it, and one of them is a stair somebody has not been able to call up for four years.**"

A fuse in that shop had been replaced with one of a rating above what the circuit was meant to carry.

He put the rating back and proved the board with a tester.

"**Four pounds,**" she said. "**A fuse rated above the circuit is a fuse that is there for show. Four of those boards in that row have been done that way, and one of them has been a shop somebody has been worried about for four years.**"

**Twenty-three pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**That Thursday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder did not come off the shelf at the back of it, and nothing on this page measures what is written on the page it keeps.**
