# Chapter 974 — The Question That Had No Owner

**A Sunday was a bench until noon and a counter until one and a shutter at two as it always is, and a woman of about fifty-one came into that shop for the fifth time in about four weeks and nobody in it had any business asking her a question at all.**

---

A Sunday morning in that shop brought five callers, the last of them in about a minute before one, and the shutter was pulled to at two.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and ninety-eight days, two hundred and fifty-six weeks and six days. The printed form on a wall in a passage in a fourth district was sixty-eight days old that Sunday and had not been touched by a hand since it went up.**

---

**She came in about ten and sat down on the customer side of that counter where the rail is, and about four people who have been in that room have said since that she did not ask for anything and that she did not look at the cage down one side of the counter and that she had a coat on that was buttoned and she did not take it off, and that the other woman at that counter had been in since about nine and had said nothing to anybody.**

Marek said: you have been in about four times.

The woman of about fifty-one said: five, counting today.

Marek said: five.

The woman of about fifty-one said: **I have not come for anything. I have told you that on three of those and I am telling you on the fourth and I will go on telling you.**

Marek said: why do you come.

**And then he stopped, and about four people in that room have said since that he stopped with his mouth open and that the stopping took about four seconds and that he did not finish it, and that the woman of about fifty-one waited for the rest of it and then answered something else.**

**And the other woman at that counter had not moved through the whole of it, and about four people in that room have said since that Marek looked at her once while he was stopped and that she looked back at him and that neither of them said a word, and that she is the one person in this city who is not permitted to ask a woman what she meant.**

Marek said: when do you have to be back.

The woman of about fifty-one said: **a quarter past eleven.**

Marek said: every week.

She said: every week that there is a sitting in that room, and there is one about every four weeks, so no.

Marek said: how many have there been.

The woman of about fifty-one said: three, and one that was shut when I got there.

**The two of them left that silence standing for about nine seconds, and about four people who have been in that room have said since that she had told him a plain fact and that he had asked for it in the wrong order and that neither of them had any way of putting that back.**

Marek said: I am sorry.

The woman of about fifty-one said: **do not be sorry, because if you are sorry then I have to do something about it and I have not got the day for it.**

Marek said: what would you like me to do.

She said: nothing.

Marek said: I could ask why you come.

**The woman of about fifty-one said: no, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it before he had finished and that she said it to the counter and not to him and that she has not taken her coat off at any point in the whole of that conversation.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **no. If you ask me why I come then I will tell you, and then you will have to carry it, and you have a sheet with a name on it already and that is one too many for you.**

Marek said: I would not write it down.

She said: you would not have to write it down. That is the trouble with you and it is not a fault.

---

**And then the woman of about forty-five came in off the street at about half past ten with two bags and stood at the back of that shop until the woman of about fifty-one had gone, and about four people in that room have said since that she had been on the far pavement for about four minutes before she came in and that nobody could have said afterwards how long she was standing there.**

The woman of about forty-five said: you stopped in the middle of that.

Marek said: I stopped.

Talia said: **there is one person in this shop who could not have finished it either, and she is sitting at that counter, and neither of you two has noticed that for about a minute.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **there is a question in this city with nobody standing behind it and it has been standing there since before either of us, and you nearly picked it up in front of a woman who came five times.**

Marek said: I nearly asked her why she comes.

The woman of about forty-five said: **then ask the question you can carry. Ask her when she has to be back, and be given the truth, and let that be the whole of what you did.**

Marek said: that is not the same thing.

The woman of about forty-five said: **it is a smaller thing and you did it and it was worth something and it did not cost her anything, and I have watched a man ask the other one and I would not sit behind him while he did it.**

Marek said: whose question is it.

The woman of about forty-five said: **it has not got one and that is not a reason to leave it there. It has not got one because every one of us that could stand behind it is somebody it is about.**

---

**And that Sunday was a bench until noon and a counter until one and about an hour of the two with nothing to do at all, and about four people who have been in that room have said since that the hour is why that shop still has callers on a Sunday and not a queue.**

---

**That Sunday's remainder went into one shop, a passage and two flats, in four jobs.**

A light in that shop had been put on a switch that also carried the shop's front roller, so that opening the door put the lights out.

She took the roller onto its own way in and proved it from the doorway.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A roller and a light on one way is a shop that goes dark every time it opens. Four of those in that row are shared, and one of them has been a counter somebody has had to find the switch for four years.**"

A lamp in one of those flats had been wired with no return at the fitting, so that the holder carried the whole of the supply.

He put a return in at the holder and proved it with a lamp.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A holder with no return is a holder that is the supply. Four of those in that block are like it, and one of them has been a ceiling somebody has been afraid to touch for four years.**"

A socket in the second of those flats had its earth taken off the box, so that the plate was live whenever the ring main was on.

She put the earth back on and proved the plate dead.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A plate with the earth off is a plate that is live with nothing switched off. Four of those plates in that block have no earth, and one of them has been a skirting somebody has been careful around for four years.**"

A bulb holder in that shop had its centre contact flattened by a screw that had been driven crooked.

He fitted a proper contact and proved the lamp lit.

"**Four pounds,**" she said. "**A centre contact run flat is a bulb that has about a month left in it. Four of those holders in that row are flattened, and one of them has been a bench lamp somebody has been putting new bulbs into for four years.**"

**Twenty-six pounds is the sum of those four and no halfpenny came off it, exact.**

---

**That Sunday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on the shelf at the back, and no figure for the page inside it is put anywhere on this page.**
