# Chapter 980 — She Would Not Decide That Week

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and a woman who has read a page nobody else has read said in a room with four people in it that she was not going to decide anything this week, and about four of them have said since that she had said it in about nine words and had then refused to say why twice.**

---

That Monday put nine names on that day's book, the last of them at twenty past nine, and the shutter came to at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and six days, two hundred and fifty-eight weeks and four days. The request in the drawer in a second district was fifty-five days old on that Monday.**

---

**She came in at about seven and sat down with her coat still on and did not take the seat she usually takes, and about four people who have been in that room have said since that she took a chair against the wall and that she put her hands flat on her knees and that she had done that before about two other times in about two months.**

Sera Quill said: I am not deciding this week.

Marek said: you said that on the Monday before.

Sera Quill said: **and I will say it on the Monday after that, and if I say it eleven Mondays running then I will have said it eleven times and not once have decided, and that is not a trick, it is what I have.**

Marek said: have you come nearer.

Sera Quill said: no.

Marek said: is that the answer to the question or the answer to me.

Sera Quill said: **it is the answer to you and it is the answer to the question and the two have been the same for about four months.**

---

**And about nine seconds passed in that room and Marek said nothing at all, and about four people who have been in that room have said since that he did not ask her the next question and that he has said that he wanted to and that he did not.**

Marek said: four months.

Sera Quill said: about four months.

Marek said: of somebody's life.

Sera Quill said: **of somebody's life and I do not know which year and I have not known since the Tuesday I read it, and saying the number again does not make it a decision and I would like it noted that I said it again.**

Marek said: noted.

Sera Quill said: **by whom.**

Marek said: by nobody. It is noted.

Sera Quill said: then it is not noted.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds and about four people who have been in that room have said since that the woman of about forty-five, who had come in at about half past seven and had not sat down, said one sentence at that point from the back and that it was the only time in four months she had spoken in that shop first.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **she is not deciding in here, and if anybody in this room makes her feel as though she is deciding in here, then this room is worse than a drawer.**

Marek said: nobody is doing that.

The woman of about forty-five said: **you were about to. You have been about to on about nine of the last eleven Mondays and I have watched it and so has she.**

Sera Quill said: he has not.

The woman of about forty-five said: **he has, and it is not a fault and it is the reason I hold a door, and neither of you is going to say that out loud again this week.**

---

**And then Marek asked the only question he had wanted to ask since the winter, and about four people who have been in that room have said since that he got it out in about four seconds and that she answered it before he had finished the last word of it.**

Marek said: what happens if you wait until the year runs out.

Sera Quill said: **then it is somebody else's year that ran out and it will not have been done by me and I will not have been the one who did it.**

Marek said: that is not the reason you gave me last time.

Sera Quill said: **that is the reason I gave you last time and it is the reason I am giving you now and I have given you about nine of them and you have believed none of them and that is correct of you.**

Marek said: I believe it.

Sera Quill said: you believe it about four months and not about four years and you are the only person in this city who has told me the difference and I have not thanked you for it and I am not going to.

**And about four people who have been in that room have said since that she said that without any weight on it, and that she was not being cold, and that nobody in that room believed that for a second.**

Marek said: can I do anything this week.

Sera Quill said: **you can shut at ten and you can not ask me on any Monday what I have decided, and you can leave the sheet on the table where the man of about thirty-three left it, and that is three things and it is not nothing.**

Marek said: the sheet.

Sera Quill said: **it stays where it is lying. Nobody has picked it up for about two and a half weeks and it is in nobody's way, and a page that is in nobody's way is the only kind of page in this matter that has never cost anybody anything.**

Marek said: what happens when it does.

Sera Quill said: **I do not know, and if I knew then I would have decided, and I am not going to be able to tell you that this week either.** And she said: **not this week.**

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that room have said since that the woman of about forty-three had come in at about eight and had not come to the table and had gone out again at about ten to twelve, and that nobody stopped her, and that she was not carrying anything.**

---

**One shop, one yard and one kitchen took the rest of that Monday, and four jobs went into them.**

A light in that kitchen had been put in over the sink on a fitting with no earth, so that the fitting was live with the switch off.

She earthed it at the fitting and proved it with a tester.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A light over a sink with no earth is a light over water with no earth. Four of those fittings in that row have no earth, and one of them has been a sink somebody has been standing over carefully for four years.**"

A socket in that shop had been put in on an extension lead that was run under a rug and pinned with two tacks.

He took the lead out of the rug and put the socket on the wall and proved it with a lamp.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A lead run under a rug is a lead nobody can see and nobody can cut. Four of those in that block are under a floor covering, and one of them has been a shop somebody has been unable to sweep for four years.**"

A switch in that yard had been put in on a round box with no box behind it at all, so that the switch was mounted on nothing.

He fitted a box and proved it with a lamp.

"**Six pounds,**" she said. "**A switch on no box is a switch on the plaster. Four of those in that row are like it, and one of them has been a wall somebody has put a hand flat on for four years.**"

A lamp holder in that kitchen had been fitted with a ring that had been painted into the shade, so that the shade could not be taken off.

He freed the shade and refitted the ring and proved the lamp lit.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A shade painted to its holder is a shade that will come down when somebody needs a bulb. Four of those in that row are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has done without a bulb in for four years.**"

**Thirty-two pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**That Monday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on its shelf at the back, and this page puts no figure on the page it keeps.**

**AND THE SIX THINGS A LATER PASS MUST CARRY FORWARD FROM THESE TEN. *One:* a woman of about fifty-four has kept a room off a line in Saltmarket for six years, running a sitting about every four weeks, and the room is open to anybody because there is no list and she will not write one; she put ten blanks down on the Saturday, one at every place in that room including the place behind her own chair, and said she would not do it again. *Two:* on the Friday of that week a woman of about sixty-two stood up in that room and said one word she had carried since a Wednesday four years earlier, and the word is not printed on any page; nobody wrote it down, nobody repeated it, and she was not thanked and was not asked to stay, and three people have it and one of the three has it wrong, which the man of about thirty-three, who is her son, brought into a shop on a Thursday. *Three:* Marek refused to say the word back to her on the second Sunday and gave the reason in one line, and she said she would rather she had kept it, and neither of them said sorry in a way that meant anything. *Four:* the sixth column now stands on three sheets in one tray, the tray is shut for a month and has now been shut for sixteen days of it, and the woman of about forty-three said out loud in front of a queue that she made the word available up herself, that she filled the space in her own form with about nine hundred blanks, and that she will do nothing about the sixth column when it is asked about. *Five:* the woman of about fifty-one was asked why she comes, out loud, by Marek and not by anybody with an office, and she answered him, and she said that if the room is not about her then she will still have been in it, and the question with no owner still has no owner and the woman who keeps that room has not been asked it either. *Six:* Sera Quill has still not decided, and said on the last Monday that she would not decide that week and would not be asked on any Monday, and named three things he could do, one of which is that a sheet with an empty column should stay lying on a table in that shop where nobody picks it up.**
