# Chapter 979 — She Asked Him To Say It Back

**A Sunday was a bench until noon and a counter until two, and a woman of about sixty-two came in at about eleven with her coat buttoned and asked a man to say one word back to her, and about four people in that room have said since that he would not and that neither of them raised her voice once in it.**

---

That Sunday was four callers in that shop and the last one was away by about twelve, and the shutter came down at two as it does.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and five days, two hundred and fifty-seven weeks and six days. The request in the drawer in a second district was fifty-four days old on that Sunday.**

---

**She stood at the customer side of that counter for about two hours and did not sit down and about four people in that room have said since that she did not look at the bench or the cage or the rail once, and that she spent the first hour not saying anything and about four minutes of the second hour asking for what she came for.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: say it back to me.

Marek said: what.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **the word. I have been sitting here about an hour trying to remember where I said it and I cannot remember whether it was in that room on a Friday or in a corridor four years back on a Wednesday.**

Marek said: you said it in that room.

She said: **I know I said it in that room. I want to hear it in another voice before I go home because I have started to think I made it up.**

**About nine seconds went by at that counter, and the last caller of that morning had gone out about ten minutes before, and the shop was empty, and neither of them said anything for the length of it.**

Marek said: no.

The woman of about sixty-two said: you have not said it.

Marek said: no.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **you were in the room. Nine of us were in the room and you were one of them.**

Marek said: **I was, and I am not going to be the second person carrying it, and I have spent a fortnight deciding that and I am not going to undo it in about four seconds because it is you asking.**

---

**And then she asked him why, in two words, and he answered her plainly and at some length, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the plainest either of them had ever said anything and that it was not unkind.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: say why.

Marek said: because a word two people hold is a thing that can be checked against another person.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **a word one person holds is a thing that can be checked against nobody.**

Marek said: **and that is what you have been carrying for four years and you came in here on a Friday and gave it to about nine people in a room and two of them have it wrong.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: three people have it and one has it wrong.

Marek said: how do you know that.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **because my son came and told me on a Thursday that there are three of them, and he did not say which three, and he has never told me anything in his life without being asked twice.**

**She said that last part to the counter and not to him. Her hands were in her coat pockets throughout, and they were the only part of her he could see.**

Marek said: you could ask him.

She said: **I have asked him twice and he has told me once and the once was on a Thursday and I have not asked a third time because the third time is when he tells me something to be kind to me.**

Marek said: what will you do.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **nothing. I will go home and I will not have said it and I will not have heard it and both of those are how it started.**

---

**And then she said the thing that made that Sunday the one that was in the shop on about four people's minds that week, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it in a flat voice and that she did not stop for breath in the middle of it.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **you have all had it for about nine days and not one of you has done a single thing with it, and I gave that up for that.**

Marek said: there is nothing to do with it.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **then I would rather you had not been in that room and I would rather I had said it to a wall on the way home, and I would rather it was still mine.**

**She did not say anything else and neither did he, and about four people in that room have said since that the woman of about forty-five was not in the shop that day and that two of them have said since that they would have told her, and that one of them has said since that she would not have.**

Marek said: I am sorry.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **do not be sorry. I have had four years of not being sorry and one more will not take long.**

Marek said: can I ask you one thing.

The woman of about sixty-two said: no.

Marek said: you do not know what it is.

The woman of about sixty-two said: **it is whether I said it in that room or in that corridor, and if I said it in that corridor then it happened four years ago and there is nothing in this city anybody can do about it, and if I said it in that room then it is about nine days old and there is still nothing anybody can do about it.** And she said: **both of those are nothing and you would not have got a different one out of me.**

**Nobody thanked that woman on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody and she was out of that door inside about four minutes of saying that, and she did not put her coat down on her way out, and nobody offered to.**

---

**What that Sunday had left was a shop, a passage and a flat, and four jobs went into them in about two hours.**

A light on that shop counter had been put in behind the till so that the till was lit from behind and the front of the counter was not lit at all.

She brought the fitting out in front of the till and stood where the counter stands to see what the till had thrown.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A light behind a till is a light on the money and not on the counting. Four of those fittings are behind something. One of them is a counter somebody has bent over, and it has been four years.**"

A bell push in that passage had been wired with no return at the push, so that the whole of the supply was at the button.

He put a return in at the push and proved it with a lamp.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A bell push with no return is the supply at somebody's thumb. Four of those pushes in that row are like it, and one of them has been a door somebody has had to lean on for four years.**"

A socket in that flat had been put in on a skirting board with the earth taken off the board, so that the plate had no earth at all.

She earthed it off the box and made the plate dead with a tester before she put the skirting back.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A plate with no earth on a skirting is a plate on a piece of wood. That is four of those plates in that block, all like it, and one of them is a hall somebody has been careful with for four years.**"

A switch in that flat had been put in over a joist with the box set at an angle, so that the plate stood proud of the wall by about the width of a coin.

He re-set the box and proved the plate flush.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A plate standing proud is a plate somebody catches a sleeve on. Four of those boxes in that block are set at an angle, and one of them has been a corner somebody has walked into for four years.**"

**Twenty-eight pounds is what that Sunday's four came to, exact.**

---

**That Sunday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on the shelf at the back of that room, and nothing on this page is offered about the page it keeps.**
