# Chapter 977 — A Room That Anybody May Enter

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and a woman of about forty-three had said a tray would be available in a month and it was not a month, and about four people who work in that building have said since that the tray was not touched on that Friday by anybody at all.**

---

Eight callers in that shop on that Friday and the last of them was in about ten minutes before nine, with that shutter going down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and three days, two hundred and fifty-seven weeks and four days. The request in the drawer in a second district was fifty-two days old on that Friday.**

---

**He had come in on the Friday with the tray written on a job card in his own hand and he stood at the customer side of that counter and read it out and about four people who work in that building have said since that he read it out in the voice of a man reading out a rule rather than a favour.**

Marek said: it is not a month.

The woman of about forty-three said: it is not a month.

Marek said: it is thirteen days since you shut it.

She said: **it is thirteen days and it will be a month, and the third sheet went in on the Saturday before I shut it and I put it at the back and I shut the drawer on it.**

Marek said: you told me a month in that shop.

The woman of about forty-three said: **on the same day. I shut the drawer and then I said it, in that order, and I have thought since that a person should say the month before the drawer and not after.**

Marek said: so when.

The woman of about forty-three said: **when it is a month. That is what a month is and I am not going to have you come in here and turn it into something else because you want it.**

---

**About nine seconds went past at that desk without a word at it, and about four people who work in that building have said since that there was a queue of nine behind him that day and that none of the nine said one word, and that two of them left.**

Marek said: three sheets in it and one of them has been filled in.

The woman of about forty-three said: yes.

Marek said: then somebody has written that somebody was told.

The woman of about forty-three said: **somebody has written down that somebody was told and the two are not the same and I read it on the Saturday and I will read it again when it is a month.**

Marek said: you read it and you have not said a word to anybody about it since.

The woman of about forty-three said: I have said one word to you about it. That is what you are.

Marek said: what word.

She said: **wait.** And then she said: **and I will say the same word in a month and you may come to this desk and stand where you are standing and I will not have said anything else in between.**

**About four people at that counter have said since that she put both hands flat on the desk at that point and that the queue began to move again and that the woman of about forty-three did not look up at any of the nine.**

Marek said: what happens in a month.

She said: **it is available and it is not read out and it is not copied and it goes back on the shelf, and that is the whole of what available means and I made the word up in the winter before last because there was not another one.**

---

**And then she said the thing that made that Friday different from the other four, and about four people who work in that building have said since that she said it to the queue and not to him.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I am going to be asked one day why there is a sixth column on a sheet I made with five.**

Marek said: by whom.

She said: **by the man who filled it in, in about a year, in this building, and he will say *you made a sheet that would take it* and he will not be lying.**

Marek said: and what will you say.

The woman of about forty-three said: **I will say that I made a sheet with five columns for five things that happen to a person, and that nobody has ever told me what the sixth one is, and that I have not left a space.**

Marek said: there is a space.

She said: **there is a space in the paper and I have filled it with about nine hundred blanks.**

**That queue kept its own counsel for about nine seconds, and about four people who work in that building have said since that a man near the door laughed once and that she turned round and looked at him and that he did not do it again.**

Marek said: will you fight it.

The woman of about forty-three said: I will do nothing about it. **That is the only thing I have got and it has been the whole of this counter for four years.**

Marek said: that is not fighting.

She said: no. **And if he has to go to a room to say it out loud then nine people will hear it said out loud before anybody has answered it, and that is how it goes on the wall and it is how it got here in the first place.**

---

**A shop, a cellar and two flats took the rest of that Friday, in four jobs and about two hours.**

A light in that cellar had been put on a lamp holder with no earth at all, so that the holder was live whenever the circuit was live.

She put an earth on it at the holder and proved it with a tester.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A holder with no earth is a holder that can put a hand on the shop's supply. Four of those holders in that row have no earth, and one of them has been a cellar somebody has not been able to go into for four years.**"

A switch in that shop had been put on a pendant and dropped so far that it hung against the bench.

He shortened it and clamped it and proved it from the bench.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A pendant that hangs on the bench is a switch that gets knocked with everything. Four of those in that row are that short, and one of them has been a bench somebody has had to clear by hand for four years.**"

A meter board in one of those flats had its main switch wired on the wrong side, so that the board could not be made dead.

He wired it so the supply is on the load side and proved the board dead.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A board that cannot be made dead is a board with a supply on it always. Four of those in that block are wired that way, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has been frightened of for four years.**"

A lamp in the second of those flats had a shade that had come away from its holder and was hanging on by the flex.

She refitted the shade and proved the lamp lit.

"**Four pounds,**" she said. "**A shade hanging by its flex is a shade over somebody's head. Four of those shades in that block are like it, and one of them has been a front room somebody has only used in daylight for four years.**"

**Twenty-six pounds is what those four came to on that Friday, exact.**

---

**That Friday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the ring binder stayed where it has stayed on the shelf at the back, and this page offers nothing about what is written on the page it holds.**

Friday of week 325, at ten. That is the two hundred and twenty-second day of this stretch of days. Eight callers came through that shop on that Friday and eight names were written onto that day's book, the eighth of them a little before nine.

*Conditions of the close.* **That book in the green binding lay open at the end of that Friday and the tin beside it had the lid down as it has had since the last winter, and this page will not put one against the other.
The ninth chair went on facing the wall of that hall and it had not been dragged a step by anybody that day, and nothing in these days says who turned it the last time it was turned.
There is a room under a building in a first district and at ten that night there was no fire in it and there is none in it now and there never has been.
Four stood against the register of correct acts that changed nothing at both ends of that Friday and nobody in this city enters that number anywhere. A tray that stays shut for a month is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **A passage under this one sets down ten objects and never brings two of them into one sentence. Two notches in that rail. A card creased once. A board up on two nails. Nine fronts with a corner off each. The tin. The shutter. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back. The dated slip. The pencil in a margin.
The cage down one side of that counter is outside all ten, and so is the sheet in a drawer in a second district with one name struck through and one written underneath it. A page of nine names is none of these ten, enters no figure here, and is not printed on this page.
A tray is not a holder and a sheet is not a column.
A dated rule stands above somebody else's question and every word written behind it may be read by any person who comes and asks.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody. One woman made a word up in a winter and used it twice in one afternoon and would not be moved on anything, and one man went out with a word on a job card that is not worth the paper it is on.**
