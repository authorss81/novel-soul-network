# Chapter 975 — She Would Not Put A Word On Paper

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and a man went about nine minutes on foot to ask a woman of about fifty-four for a piece of paper and was told in about nine words that she would rather the word went on being carried by a person.**

---

Seven callers used that shop on that Tuesday and the seventh was through the door at about twenty past nine; that shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred days, two hundred and fifty-seven weeks and one day. The request in the drawer in a second district was forty-nine days old on that Tuesday.**

---

**He had worked out what he was going to say on the bus and about four people have said since that he said a different half of it, and that the woman of about fifty-four was straightening a table at the far end of that room when he came in and did not stop doing it.**

Marek said: I want to write it down.

The woman of about fifty-four said: write down what.

Marek said: what she said on Friday.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I do not hold it and it was not said to me, so there is nothing for me to refuse.**

Marek said: you were in the room.

She said: I was in the room with about nine other people and I have not got what she said and neither have you.

Marek said: I have got it.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have got it in your head and that is a place that does not answer to anybody and cannot be asked for it in four years when somebody wants to know.**

---

**About nine seconds went by with the two of them not saying anything, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that a bus went past outside in the middle of it and that the woman of about fifty-four waited for it to go before she said anything else, and that she had not asked him to sit down at any point.**

Marek said: what happens to it.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it stays where it is.**

Marek said: it is in me now.

She said: **it is in you now and in her and it is in about four other people and it will be in nine by the winter, and there is not one piece of paper in this city with a hand against it, and that is the only reason it has not got anybody's name on it this week.**

Marek said: I would not put her name to it.

She said: **you would not. Somebody would. That is how it works and it has worked three times in this city that I have counted and I have been here six years.**

Marek said: what is the other way of doing it.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **the other way is that a person carries it, and a person can be asked, and a person can say she will not.**

Marek said: she has said she wants nothing done with it.

She said: then ask her in four years and she will say it again.

---

**And then she told him the thing she keeps at the back of that room for anybody who asks her to put a thing on paper, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that she said it standing up and to the window and that it took her about four seconds.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have run this room for six years on four-week spacing and I have never had a thing on my table that came out of a person.**

Marek said: what would you do with it if it came.

She said: I would file it. **And a filed thing is a thing that answers to whoever opens the drawer, and I have opened that drawer about four times in six years and every one of those times it was somebody who wanted a thing off a person.**

Marek said: and nobody ever has.

She said: **nobody ever has, and that is not because of me, and one day somebody will and I would like to have been asked about it before that day and not on it.**

**And about nine seconds passed in which Marek took nothing at all from that, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that he has said that he has never been so glad to be told no by anybody in this matter, and that she did not believe him, and that he has said that he was.**

Marek said: can I tell somebody.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you can tell nine people and you will be telling ten by Friday and you cannot un-tell them and that is not my trouble and I have been carrying my own for six years.**

Marek said: I will not use it.

She said: I know. **You have got a name on a piece of paper already and a person who reads anything you write, and that is the whole of what you are, and it would not survive you using one word.**

---

**A shop, two stores and a boiler house were the whole of what was left on that Tuesday, and four jobs were put into them.**

A light in one of those stores had been put in at a high level with nothing under it, so that nothing on the floor was ever lit.

He brought a fitting down to working height and proved it over the counter.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A light with nothing under it is a light that lights the ceiling. Four of those fittings in that row are at the wrong height, and one of them has been a shop floor somebody has worked at in the dark for four years.**"

A lamp in the second of those stores had been wired into a joint box above the ceiling with the box left open.

She closed the box, made good and proved it dead with a tester.

"**Ten pounds,**" she said. "**An open joint box above a ceiling is a ceiling with somebody's supply in it. Four of those boxes in that row are open, and one of them has been a room somebody has been careful about for four years.**"

An outside light in the yard behind that shop came on a photocell that was under a shelf, so that it came on in the middle of the afternoon and stayed on all night.

He moved the cell out from under the shelf and proved it in the yard.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A cell under a shelf is a light that thinks it is night. Four of those in that row are shaded, and one of them has been a yard somebody has had to get used to for four years.**"

A boiler in that boiler house had its flue taken out of a room and not reconnected, so that it was heating nothing.

He reconnected it at the flue and proved it drawing.

"**Four pounds,**" she said. "**A flue that is cut off is a boiler that is a shape in a room. Four of those in that block are cut off, and one of them has been a hot water somebody has had to buy for four years.**"

**Thirty-eight pounds is all those four came to on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**That Tuesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed where it stays at the back of it, and this page puts no figure on the page inside it.**

A person can be asked what a piece of paper cannot and a person can also say no.
