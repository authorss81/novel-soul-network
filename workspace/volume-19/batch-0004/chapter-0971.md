# Chapter 971 — A Room Opened To Anybody

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then about nine minutes on foot to a long room where nine chairs stand in rows facing nothing much, and the book came down off its shelf at four in front of eleven people who had not been told what it was for.**

---

That shop took nine callers on that Wednesday and the ninth of the nine came through at twenty past nine, and the shutter was down by ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and ninety-four days, two hundred and fifty-six weeks and two days. The request in the drawer in a second district was forty-three days old on that Wednesday.**

---

**The woman of about forty-five came in at about two with her coat still on and stood at the customer side of that counter with her hands empty, and about four people who have been in that room have said since that she had come to say one thing and had it ready before the door shut behind her.**

The woman of about forty-five said: I am not going with you this afternoon.

Marek said: why not.

She said: **a room with a book open in it is not a room I will stand in, and that is not about the book.**

Marek said: what is it about.

She said: about being the second person in a matter where there is only meant to be one.

**About nine seconds went past at that counter and he did not fill any of them, and Marek has said since that he had a question ready and that it would have been the wrong one.**

Marek said: you told me once you would hold a door.

She said: **I will hold a door. I will not stand in front of a shelf and be asked afterwards whether I knew.**

Marek said: nobody is going to ask you.

She said: then nobody will have asked me and I will have been there, and those are not the same.

---

**And at four o'clock on that Wednesday he walked about nine minutes to a room off a line in Saltmarket where nine chairs stand in rows and a woman of about fifty-four has kept that room for six years, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that she stood at the door and counted nobody, which is a thing she does.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: you are the fourth one today who has asked me who it is for.

Marek said: who it is for.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is the whole of it, and you have asked me it four times today.**

Marek said: I want an answer.

The woman of about fifty-four said: then you will want it more than you want the answer.

Marek said: what is the sitting.

She said: it is a sitting. That is the whole of what a sitting is.

Marek said: who is it about.

**And the woman of about fifty-four took a piece of card out of her apron and put it on the table by the door, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that the card was the same on every one of the last four of these and that it said what a sitting is and not a single thing about anybody.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **the telling has to go in advance, and to go in advance it has to say who it is for, and to say who it is for somebody has to write a list.**

Marek said: and there is no list.

The woman of about fifty-four said: there is no list.

Marek said: then who is in the room.

She said: **anybody. It is for anybody and it is open and the chairs are nine and I have never had to write anything down to fill them.**

**It went on in that room for about nine seconds with nobody speaking in it, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that it was the quietest that room has been and that two of them looked at the ninth chair and neither of them said anything about it.**

Marek said: you could put a notice on the wall.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **of what. I have a card that says what a sitting is. If I put up a notice that says who it is for then I have written a list, and if I have written a list then somebody will ask me whose hand it is in.**

Marek said: whose hand would it be in.

She said: **the first one who comes in here and asks me a second time.**

---

**The book came off the shelf at about four and about eleven people stood or sat and none of them had a paper with their own name on it, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that the woman of about fifty-four read the first line out and then stopped reading and stood there with her finger on it.**

At about five a woman of about sixty-two came in with her coat buttoned to the neck and sat down at the back and did not speak for the rest of it, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that she had a piece of paper folded into four in her fist and that she kept the fist shut on her knee for an hour and ten minutes.

Marek said: are you the last.

The woman of about fifty-four said: I am never the last. **That is the whole of what this room does.**

---

**A shop, a flat and a stair were what was left of that Wednesday, and four jobs were fitted into about two hours at the end of it.**

A light in that flat had a lamp in it of a rating the holder was not made for, so that the holder had come loose where it sat.

He changed the lamp and proved the fitting with the shop dark.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp that is wrong for its holder is a holder working itself loose over about a year. Four of those in that block are lit wrong, and one of them has been a ceiling somebody has stopped looking up at for four years.**"

A switch on that stair had been put in with the wires on the wrong way round, so that putting it up put the light out.

She turned the switch round and proved both positions with a lamp.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A switch that works backwards teaches a landing the wrong thing and half of it never learns. Four of those switches in that block are in that way round, and one of them has been a stair somebody has shouted up in the dark for four years.**"

A bell in that shop had been screwed to a door frame with two short screws, so that it worked loose every time somebody pulled it.

He put it on proper fixings and proved it on the frame.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A bell on two short screws is a bell that stops answering and nobody ever finds out why. Four of those bells in that row are on short screws, and one of them has been a door somebody has stopped knocking on for four years.**"

A socket in that shop had been put in behind the leg of a bench, so that it could not be reached and nothing could be plugged into it.

She brought the socket out past the bench leg and proved it with a lamp.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A socket behind a bench leg is a socket that is a rumour in a shop. Four of those in that block are behind something heavy, and one of them has been a bench somebody has worked round for four years.**"

**Thirty-seven pounds is all that Wednesday's four came to, exact.**

---

**That Wednesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on the shelf at the back where it has been, and nothing here is offered about the page in it.**
