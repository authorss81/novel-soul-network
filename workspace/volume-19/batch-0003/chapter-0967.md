# Chapter 967 — She Asked To Stand Where The Reader Stood

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a bus of twenty minutes into a second district, and a woman of about forty-five went up a stair she had not been up before with nothing in her hands and came back down it two hours later with nothing in her hands.**

---

Nine callers went through that shop on that Wednesday, the ninth near twenty-five past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and eighty-seven days, two hundred and fifty-five weeks and two days. The request in the drawer in a second district was thirty-six days old on that Wednesday.**

---

**She had not told Marek and she had not told the woman of about forty-three, and about four people on that stair have said since that she had gone on a Wednesday because it is the quietest day the building has and she had worked that out from the doorway in about nine seconds.**

The woman of about forty-five said: I would like to see the shelf.

The man of about sixty-one said: no.

She said: I have not asked yet.

He said: **you were standing in the room.**

**About nine seconds went past and she took none of them, and the woman of about forty-five has said since that she had prepared about four sentences on the bus and that the two words were the whole answer.**

She said: I am holding it.

He said: I do not know you.

She said: I have been in this room.

He said: **you stood on a threshold once and you were let in the way a person is let in who is carrying something for somebody else, and that is not the same as being let in.**

She said: what would make it the same.

He said: nine years would make it the same, and I have not got nine years spare.

---

**About nine seconds passed in which nobody in that room said one word, and about four people who work in that building have said since that they heard the exchange from the other end of the room and that nobody moved and that the labels on the shelves all faced out at the same angle while it happened.**

**And then the man of about sixty-one said the thing he said to nobody in nine years, and about four people in that building have said since that he said it to a stranger because a stranger could not do anything with it.**

He said: I let it out.

She said: what.

He said: **a page left this shelf on a Tuesday morning inside a coat, and it came back the same day, and it is the only time in nine years I have let anything out of that room.**

She said: why did you tell me.

He said: because you cannot use it.

She said: I can use that. I am holding it.

He said: **you cannot. You have never seen the page and you have said out loud that you have not, and that is the only thing about you that makes you safe in this room.**

**It was about nine seconds before she answered, and she has said since that the last part was the first time in her life that a rule was used to keep her somewhere rather than to keep her out, and that she did not know what to do with it.**

She said: are you frightened.

He said: no.

She said: then what are you.

He said: **I have been careful for a very long time and it has cost me a very long time, and I did not know that was what I was buying until this week.**

She said: what did you think you were buying.

He said: a room that nobody could come into and take anything out of. **I bought it and people have been in it the whole time, and nine years is how long it took me to work that out.**

**Nobody said anything else to each other in that room, and about four people who work in that building have said since that she stood there for a while and then said goodnight to a man who had not said goodnight to a caller in four years, and that he did not say it back.**

---

**About two hours at the end of that Wednesday went into that shop and one flat, and four jobs went in.**

An outside light on that shop had been wired so that the lamp could not be changed without cutting the whole row off.

He gave it its own way in and proved it with the row on.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A light you cannot change is a light that stays on till somebody finds the meter. Four of those in that row are like it, and one of them has been a wall somebody has stopped looking at for four years.**"

A cooker in that flat had been left connected to a socket that was not on its own way.

She put the cooker on its own way and proved it from the wall.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A cooker on somebody else's way is a cooker that somebody else can switch. Four of those cookers in that block are on a shared way, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has not been able to use at night for four years.**"

A socket in that flat had been fitted behind a wardrobe that had been pushed back against it.

He brought the socket forward and proved it with a lamp.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A socket behind a wardrobe is a socket that is a rumour. Four of those in that block are behind something, and one of them has been a bedroom somebody has searched twice for four years.**"

A light switch in that shop had been fitted into a metal back box with the earth strap left off it.

She fitted the strap and proved the box with a tester.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A metal box with no strap is a box that carries whatever the fitting carries. Four of those boxes in that row have no strap, and one of them has been a bench somebody has worked over every day for four years.**"

**Thirty-six pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**That Wednesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on its shelf at the back, and this page sets down no figure for the page that is in it.**
