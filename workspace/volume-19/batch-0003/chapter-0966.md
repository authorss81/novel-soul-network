# Chapter 966 — Four Names On The Back Of A Card

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a first-floor room in a second district that is somebody's office and not a shop, and a man of twenty-two put a job card on that desk face down and then turned it over.**

---

Nine callers went through that shop on that Monday, the last near twenty-five past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and eighty-five days, two hundred and fifty-five weeks to the day. The request in the drawer in a second district was thirty-four days old on that Monday.**

---

**She had a file open on that desk before he came in, and it was a thin one, and about four people who have been through that office have said since that she does not open a file before the person arrives unless she has decided what she is going to say.**

She said: before you say anything, this file is about the request you signed and not about the nine, and you will say if you understand that.

Marek said: I understand it.

She said: then put it face down.

Marek said: it is already going face down.

She said: then put it down.

Marek said: it is a job card.

She said: **it is a job card with four names on the back of it and a line under each name.**

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and Talia has said since that she let the quiet stand because a file requires it and not because she wanted to.**

Marek said: it is not a list.

She said: it has four names on it and a line under each.

Marek said: I have not written a thing beside them.

She said: you have not. There is nothing beside them. That is what makes it a list and not an argument.

---

**He had done it on the Sunday night, and about four people in that building have said since that a young man sat on a bench after a shutter had come down and wrote down four names he already knew and no reason at all.**

Marek said: I did not ask them.

She said: I know.

Marek said: I was asked to and I said no.

She said: I know that too. **You wrote them down instead of asking them, and that is worse than asking, because asking at least puts it in front of the person it is about.**

Marek said: no it is not. Asking puts it in front of them and makes it theirs to carry.

She said: yes.

Marek said: yes.

---

**And then she made him do it, and about four people in that building have said since that this is the only time anybody has ever been asked in that office to say four names out loud instead of writing them down.**

She said: say them.

Marek said: to you.

She said: **to me. Out loud. And I will write nothing and I will keep nothing.**

**He has said since that this was the worst minute of the four minutes it took, and that he has thought about the order of it more than he has thought about the page, and that he said the first one to himself before he said it to her.**

Marek said: the first one keeps the wall at the clinic.

Marek said: the second one asks what a copy is for four years before he asks anything else.

Marek said: the third one has never been in a room where a name was said and does not want to be.

Marek said: the fourth one is the maker of the sheet.

Talia said: **and what is under the fourth name.**

Marek said: that she made it to give a day and take no answer.

Talia said: **that is not a note about her. That is an excuse for her. Do you know the difference yet.**

Marek said: no.

---

**Nobody in that room said one word for about nine seconds, and Marek has said since that he did not say anything, and that the not saying was the first honest thing he had managed in a month.**

She said: I have heard them.

Marek said: and you have them now.

Marek said: and they are not on that sheet.

Talia said: **they are four people who know a word, and not one of them has ever been on a sheet with nine rows on it. If any of them had been you would not be telling me about them here.**

Marek said: no.

She said: **I will not keep them. I cannot unhear them. That is the whole of what my office can do and you have had all of it.**

Marek said: can it do anything else.

She said: it can write down one thing.

Marek said: which thing.

She said: **that a person brought a list into this room on a Monday and it did not stay in it. That is a record about you, and about the request you signed, and about nobody else, and I would have it in the file where you cannot get at it.**

Marek said: put it in.

**And he was about half a sentence from asking her whether she thought she ought to be the person, and he has said since that he got as far as her first name, and that she looked up, and that he did not finish it.**

Talia said: you nearly said something.

Marek said: no.

Talia said: **I noticed. Do not do it on a Monday.**

---

**There was a stair and there was that shop, and four jobs in about two hours before the shutter came down on that Monday.**

A stair light in that block had been wired with no earth on the metal box, so that a fault on the fitting had nowhere to go.

He put the earth on and proved it with a lamp.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A stair box with no earth is a fault with nowhere to land. Four of those boxes in that block are un-earthed, and one of them has been a stair somebody has gone up in the dark for four years.**"

A bell at a door in that block had a short on its transformer, so that the bell went off whenever the door was shut.

She changed the transformer and proved the bell with the door shut.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A bell with a short on it is a door that rings when it closes. Four of those transformers in that block have gone, and one of them has been a flat somebody has stopped knocking at for four years.**"

A socket in that stair had a face that had been painted over along the edges, so that the screws had no bite.

She cleaned the edges and proved the face with a lamp.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A face painted at the edges is a face held on by paint. Four of those faces in that block are painted over, and one of them has been a socket somebody has stopped using for four years.**"

A light in that stair had been put in with the flex running through the metal of the fitting.

She rewired it and proved the fitting with a meter.

"**Six pounds,**" she said. "**Flex through a fitting is a fitting that wears through where you cannot see it. Four of those lights in that block are wired that way, and one of them has been a ceiling somebody has stopped looking up at for four years.**"

**Twenty-nine pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**That Monday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed where it stands, and no figure is offered here for the page it keeps.**

*969.
Monday of week 323, at ten. That is the two hundred and eleventh day of this stretch of days. That Monday brought nine callers into that shop and put nine names onto that day's book, the last of them at about twenty-five past nine.
**Nothing sat in on that Monday and no figure was said out loud in any room in this city. A young man carried four names on the back of a job card to a first-floor office and did not ask the four people anything. He said the four names out loud instead and the office kept nothing and could not unhear them. One line went into a file and it is about him.** That Monday was worth twenty-nine pounds, exact.*

*Conditions of the close.* **The book in the green cover and the tin beside it are standing as they were left, and nothing is said about either one here and nothing is set beside the other.

Under a building in a first district there is a room with no light in it and no fire in it, and at about ten that night there was nothing, and now there is nothing, and it has been like that since it was made.

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named in a passage under this one and no two of them are named in one breath. Two spaces in one rail. A card creased once, holding a door. Two nails holding a board. Nine fronts with a corner torn off each. A tin, shut. The shutter. A book in a green binding. A binder at the back of a shelf. A slip with a date on it. A pencil in somebody else's margin.
The cage down one side of a counter is outside all ten, and so is the request in a drawer in a second district with one name struck out and one name written underneath. A page of nine names is not one of the ten, is not counted in any figure here, and is not printed on this page.
Saying a name out loud is not writing it down, and an office that has heard one cannot forget it.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and what is written behind it is open to anybody who comes and asks for it.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody. Four names were spoken in an office and one line was written down, and the line is about the man who spoke them and not about anybody else.**
