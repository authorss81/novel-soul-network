# Chapter 969 — Two Sheets In One Tray

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until six and a tray in a building in a second district with two pieces of paper in it, and the woman of about forty-three looked at both of them for a long time before she asked anybody to come over and look.**

---

Nine callers went through that shop on that Friday, the last of them at about twenty-five past nine, and the shutter was down by ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine days, two hundred and fifty-five weeks and four days. The request in the drawer in a second district was thirty-eight days old on that Friday.**

---

**She had put both sheets on the counter side by side with the ruled columns not touching, and about four people in that queue have said since that she stood with her back to the window so that nobody could see what she was looking at from the door. One of the two is the sheet a man left on that counter on the Thursday and the other is the sheet that lay on a table in a warm room in this city with a word pencilled in its margin.**

She said: one of these two I made myself and one of them has been round nine rooms since it was printed. Look at them and tell me what you see.

The man of about fifty-two said: two columns.

She said: two of them are in the same place.

He said: no they are not.

She said: **that is what I want a person to say out loud in a room, so that there is one more person who has said it.**

**Nobody at that counter said anything for about nine seconds, and about four people in that queue have said since that somebody at the back of it said the word pencilled and somebody else said the word ruled and neither of them was talking to anybody.**

---

**Then she put them down flat and put her thumb on each in turn, and about four people at that counter have said since that this was the first time anybody had ever compared two copies of that sheet in front of anybody.**

She said: that one is pencil. That one is ruled.

The man of about fifty-two said: and they are in different places on the sheet.

She said: one is between the fourth column and the fifth. One is in the margin outside all six.

He said: **then it is not one person's wish twice. It is two people who wanted the same thing.**

She said: or it is one person who did it twice and moved it.

He said: nobody moves a thing and rules it again in ink.

She said: **a person does if they have been told it is not their margin.**

**Nobody in that room said one word for about nine seconds, and Marek has said since that he stood at that counter and understood that a thing which had been one man's small private wish for seventeen days had become a thing anybody with a ruler and a biro could do.**

---

**And then she asked him for the one thing she has never asked anybody for in four years, and about four people at that counter have said since that she asked it out loud and that the queue did not move.**

She said: I would have to ask who in this building can run a sheet.

The man of about fifty-two said: you would not.

She said: I would.

The man of about fifty-two said: **and then every person in this building who has ever made a photocopy would be somebody you had asked about nine people.**

She said: I know what that is.

The man of about fifty-two said: **and I am not asking you to do it, because you have spent four years making sure that no sheet in this building is about anybody.**

She said: then it stops here.

The man of about fifty-two said: **it stopped here two days ago. It has been made twice by somebody and you have found it twice because you are the only person in this city who still looks in that tray.**

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and the woman of about forty-three has said since that she put both sheets back in the tray and shut it and did not say anything else about it for the rest of the day, and that her hands were not steady and that she did not mention that either.**

---

**That Friday finished in a shop and on a stair, four jobs, and a bit over two hours of what was left of it.**

A socket in that shop had been run off the lighting circuit with the conductor not in the brown, so that a lamp could be put in upside down and nothing would show.

He put the conductors in order and proved the lamp both ways up.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A socket with no brown is a lamp that can go in backwards. Four of those in that row are like it, and one of them has been a bench somebody has had a bulb out of four times for four years.**"

A light on that stair had been put in under a metal cover with no earthing to it.

She earthed the cover and proved it with a tester.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A cover with nothing on it is a cover waiting for a fault. Four of those covers in that block are un-earthed, and one of them has been a landing somebody has not looked up at for four years.**"

A switch on that stair had been wired to a bell circuit, so that the bell rang when the light was put on.

She moved it onto its own way and proved the bell with the light off.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A switch on the bell is a bell that tells the whole landing your business. Four of those switches in that block are on bell circuits, and one of them has been a door somebody has had to close for four years.**"

A meter tail in that shop had its earth taken off the board, so that the board could float.

He put the earth back where the board sat and proved it against the incoming.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A board with no earth is a board that floats up when a fault comes. Four of those boards in that block are floating, and one of them has been a shop somebody has never been able to prove anything on for four years.**"

**Thirty-three pounds is what those four came to on that Friday, exact.**

---

**That Friday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed where it stays, and no figure for the page inside it is set out here.**
