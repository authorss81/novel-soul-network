# Chapter 962 — A Man In The Building Tomorrow

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and the woman of about forty-five came at a quarter to eight with her coat on and did not take it off, and she had come about the next day and everybody in that room knew it inside four seconds of looking at her.**

---

Nine callers went through that shop on that Monday, the last at about twenty past nine, and the shutter was down by ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and seventy-eight days, two hundred and fifty-four weeks to the day. The request in the drawer in a second district was twenty-seven days old on that Monday.**

---

**She stood with her back to the rail and did not sit down, and about four people in that room have said since that she had dressed for a day out rather than for an evening, and that the coat was still buttoned at the top.**

She said: tomorrow there is a woman reading a page in my flat.

Marek said: in your flat. Not in yours.

She said: in a room off my flat with my table in it.

Marek said: yes.

She said: and I am standing on the other side of my own door.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and the woman of about forty-three has said since that she counted them and that the counting was hers and not his.**

She said: I want you in the building.

Marek said: no.

She said: you did not ask why.

Marek said: you were going to tell me anyway.

---

**And then she said why, and she said it to his face and not to the room, and about four people in that room have said since that this was the first time she had asked him for anything at all.**

She said: if anything goes wrong in there I want one person on this street who has been in the room and knows what was on the table.

Marek said: I have not been in the room.

She said: you know what was on the table.

Marek said: **if I am under that roof tonight then I am not out of it, and I am not out of it for anybody else, and I signed for being out of it.**

She said: you signed for yourself. You did not sign for me.

Marek said: no. That is the whole of what is wrong with it and I cannot get round it tonight.

**And then Sera Quill came in at about ten past eight with her bag over her arm and heard the last of it, and about four people in that room have said since that she did not ask what had been said and worked it out in the time it took her to put her bag down.**

Sera Quill said: would you have said yes.

She said: yes.

Sera Quill said: I would have said yes as well, and I did not say it, and you asked him first and that has done the work of the asking.

She said: so what do I do.

Sera Quill said: **stand on your own landing with your back to your own door and listen to nothing, for as long as it takes, and go out for a walk if you cannot stand it.**

---

**Nobody in that room said one word for about nine seconds, and about four people in that room have said since that the woman of about forty-five put her hand flat on the counter the way she had heard somebody else do it, and that nobody told her she had done it.**

She said: and if she does not call me in.

Sera Quill said: you knock once and you go for a walk and you come back in an hour.

She said: and if it is not finished.

Sera Quill said: then it is not finished, and that is a Tuesday like any other Tuesday.

She said: **I will be standing outside a door I am not allowed to know anything about.**

Sera Quill said: you chose that on a Monday and I have not let you choose it again since, and that is the last thing either of us is going to say about it tonight.

**And then the woman of about forty-five said the thing she came to say and had been holding since the counter, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it to Marek and not to Sera Quill, which was the second time in six days.**

She said: you did not offer once.

Marek said: no.

She said: you were not going to.

Marek said: I was not going to. I signed that before I met either of you.

**That was that Monday's business with the three of them, and nobody in that room asked her one question about what she would do if the reader finished and said nothing at all.**

---

**After that Monday the evening went into a flat and its close, and four jobs went in over the better part of two hours.**

A lamp in that flat had been wired behind a one-way switch, so that it came on from two places and could not be turned off from either.

She replaced the switch and proved the lamp from both places.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A light with two switches and no off is a landing that stays lit till somebody climbs up and finds the switch. Four of those switches in that block are one-way, and one of them has been a stair somebody has walked up in the dark for four years.**"

An electric fire in that flat had been plugged into an adaptor that was too small for it, so that the pin got hot inside the plastic.

She put it on a proper plug and proved it with a meter.

"**Ten pounds,**" she said. "**A fire on a plug that is too small is a fire that softens its own holder. Four of those adaptors in that block are undersized, and one of them has been a chair somebody has moved away for four years.**"

A socket in that flat had a switch in it that had been broken off flush, so that the face would not close.

He changed the face and proved it with a lamp.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A face that will not close is a socket with a live somewhere behind it. Four of those faces in that block are broken flush, and one of them has been a lamp somebody has stopped plugging in for four years.**"

A cooker in that flat had been earthed to the gas pipe, so that the pipe carried anything the case carried.

He moved the earth to the socket and proved the pipe clear.

"**Ten pounds,**" she said. "**An earth on a gas pipe is a pipe that is part of the wiring. Four of those cookers in that block are earthed to gas, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has cooked in twice a day for four years.**"

**Thirty-seven pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**That Monday the fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder did not come down off the shelf at the back, and no figure for the page inside it is set down here.**
