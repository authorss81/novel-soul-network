# Chapter 968 — A Copy With Nothing In The Column

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and a man of about thirty-three came in at twenty past seven with a folded copy of that sheet and put it down open at the fifth column, which was the only part of it anybody in that room could see was empty.**

---

Nine callers went through that shop on that Thursday, the ninth at about twenty past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and eighty-eight days, two hundred and fifty-five weeks and three days. The sixteenth of those ruled lines, never once written on, stood at one thousand five hundred and seventy-eight days, two hundred and twenty-five weeks and three days.**

---

**His copy was old and its fifth column was empty, and about four people in that room have said since that he put it down open at that column, not at the front, which is the only reason anybody in that shop knew it was empty.**

He said: this one is blank in that column.

Marek said: I can see it is.

He said: the others have a date in it.

Marek said: I know.

He said: **so it is the only one in this city with nothing in it, and I would like to give it to the woman who came in on the Saturday.**

Marek said: the woman of about fifty-one.

He said: I have never known her name.

**He took about nine seconds over it, and Marek has said since that this was the first time in five weeks that a person in that room had wanted to give somebody something rather than keep it.**

Marek said: you do not know her name.

He said: no.

Marek said: and you are going to hand a sheet to a woman in that shop and she is going to have it.

He said: I am going to ask her first.

---

**And then Sera Quill was at the counter, and she had not stood at that counter on a Thursday before, and about four people in that room have said since that the man of about thirty-three heard her come in and did not turn round.**

Sera Quill said: what do you want to give her.

He said: a sheet with nothing in it.

Sera Quill said: with nothing in it that she can fill in.

He said: yes.

Sera Quill said: **you want to give her a sheet she can put a name in.**

He said: I want to give her a sheet of her own. That is what I have got.

**He said the last part to the table and not to her, and about four people in that room have said since that he worked it out about nine seconds after he had said it and that he worked it out by himself.**

Sera Quill said: I have read it.

He said: I know.

Sera Quill said: **I cannot give you the names, and that is the whole of what I have that is worth anything to you today.**

---

**And then the woman of about forty-three came round from the back with her sleeves up, and about four people at that counter have said since that she did not sit down and that everybody at that counter stood up, and that this happens about four times a year.**

She said: a sheet in a person's bag is a record about that person.

He said: it is a sheet with nothing in it.

She said: **it is a sheet with nothing in it that only somebody who has read nine names would think was worth carrying home.**

He said: then what do I do with it.

She said: nothing. You keep it.

He said: **I have kept it for four years and it is the reason I asked you for it in the first place, and I gave it away for a week and it did not come back, and that is the whole of my own business with it.**

**The woman of about forty-three did not come in for about nine seconds, and she has said since that she had heard that sentence twice in nine years from two different people and had never once heard it mean anything else.**

Marek said: what would go in it.

He said: nothing. That is the point of it.

Marek said: **then you would be handing a woman a sheet with nothing in it and she would not know what it is for, and she would keep it for that reason.**

He said: or she would not keep it.

Marek said: and then where is it.

He said: back in my coat.

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three left the copy on that table and put his hands in his pockets, and about four people in that room have said since that this was the first time anybody had left anything on that table in nine years that they had not immediately picked up again.**

He said: I will come back for it.

The woman of about forty-three said: it stays because nobody here holds it.

He said: **it was never my copy to hold. I told you that on a Thursday and I have not changed my mind about it since.**

**About four people in that room have said since that after about nine seconds of nobody speaking the copy stayed where he had put it, and that nobody put a hand on it for the rest of that evening, and that at about ten it was still there.**

---

**Four jobs went into that shop and the flat above it on that Thursday, and about two hours went into them as well.**

A spur in that shop had been run off the lighting circuit, so that the machines came on when the lights were switched off.

He put the spur on its own way and proved it from the switch.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A spur off the lights is a machine that cannot be switched off without going dark. Four of those spurs in that row are off the lighting, and one of them has been a bench somebody has had to leave on for four years.**"

A fuse in that flat had been replaced with one of the wrong rating, so that the circuit was carrying more than it was meant to.

She put in a thirteen-amp fuse and proved it with a tester.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A fuse of the wrong rating is a circuit that never trips and never should. Four of those boards in that block are rated wrong, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has had one spare fuse for in four years.**"

A socket in that flat had a face on it with the fixing screws in the wrong place, so that the face sat proud of the box.

She changed the face and proved it with a lamp.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A face sitting proud is a face with a gap behind it. Four of those faces in that block are on the wrong boxes, and one of them has been a lamp somebody has moved every time it is plugged in for four years.**"

A socket in that shop had been painted round, so that a plug could not go home and had to be held.

He cleaned it back and proved the fit with a plug.

"**Ten pounds,**" she said. "**A socket painted round is a socket that has to be held in with two fingers. Four of those in that row are painted, and one of them has been a counter somebody has cut their hand on for four years.**"

**Thirty-eight pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**That Thursday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed at the back of its shelf, and no figure for the page inside it is given here.**
