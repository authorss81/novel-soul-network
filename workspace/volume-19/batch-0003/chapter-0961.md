# Chapter 961 — Two Copies Of One Pencil Line

**A Sunday was a bench until noon and a counter until two, and the shutter came down at two, and a man of about fifty-two sat down on the customer side of that counter with a register under his arm and would not put it on the table.**

---

Five callers went through that shop on that Sunday, the last near half past one, and the shutter came to at two.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and seventy-seven days, two hundred and fifty-three weeks and six days. The request in the drawer in a second district was twenty-six days old on that Sunday.**

---

**He had set it down on the customer side of the counter and kept his own hand flat on it, and about four people in that room have said since that he had the air of a man holding a plate of food he did not want to spill and not the air of a man holding a book.**

He said: I have copied a word out of a margin into my own book.

Marek said: whose margin.

He said: not mine. That is the whole of the trouble and I am not going to say whose it is.

Marek said: you have not said what the word is either.

He said: you know what word.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and Marek has said since that the nine seconds were him deciding not to ask a question he could have asked and would have got an answer to.**

---

**Then he opened the book at a page near the back, and about four people who have handled that register have said since that nothing has ever been entered in it without a name set against the entry, and that there was one line with no name against it.**

He said: there. Nineteen ruled lines and one of them is that.

Marek said: when did you copy it.

He said: the Wednesday after. I wrote it out so that I would stop seeing it on somebody else's paper.

Marek said: and now you want to rule it out.

He said: I want it out of my book.

Marek said: and ruling a thing out is denying it.

He said: I know what it is. I have not decided to do it.

**And then he asked Marek to go and ask instead, and about four people in that room have said since that this was the first anybody had tried to do in this matter, which was to hand a question to somebody else and keep the hands clean.**

He said: ask the four of them.

Marek said: no.

He said: you are on paper in this. You are the only one of us with a name on anything at all.

Marek said: and that is exactly why I cannot ask. If it comes back from one of them it goes on paper, and it goes on paper with their name at the top of it, and a page with one of their names on it is the last thing anybody in this matter needs.

He said: **then you are the wrong man in this room tonight, and you are the only one in it.**

Marek said: I will say so in front of them, and that is all I will do.

He said: that is more than I am getting. I will take it.

---

**Nobody at that counter said one word for about nine seconds, and about four people in that room have said since that Marek did not say it, and that not saying it was the whole of his answer, and that the man of about fifty-two wrote something down when he heard the silence and then took his hand off the page.**

**And then the man of about fifty-two shut the register and did not rule his line out, and about four people who have seen that book have said since that he ruled a line under his own copy instead, and that he sat looking at the two of them for a while before he did that.**

He said: a copy you rub out is a copy you have denied.

Marek said: and what is a copy you rule under.

He said: **a copy you have kept. I have had that book in my coat since a winter and I have never denied anything in it, and I am not starting on a Sunday.**

Marek said: then somebody asks you in four years who wrote it.

He said: then I will say I do not know, and I will have said it years earlier, and I will have said it to nobody at all.

**That was the whole of that Sunday, and about four people in that room have said since that he did not go until the light had gone off the bench, and that nobody asked him one question about it on the way to the door.**

---

**That Sunday took four jobs into a shop and the flat above it, and about two hours at the end of it went into them.**

A pull switch in that flat had its flex trapped under the rose, so that the switch pulled the ceiling instead of the lamp.

She freed the flex and proved the switch with a lamp.

"**Six pounds,**" she said. "**A switch that pulls the ceiling is a light you switch on by hand. Four of those switches in that block are trapped under a rose, and one of them has been a bedroom somebody has got up to in the dark for four years.**"

A lamp holder in that shop had the neutral taken to the switched side, so that the fitting stood live with the switch off.

He re-wired the holder and proved it dead at the fitting.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A fitting live with the switch off is a shop that is never quite off. Four of those holders in that row are wired that way, and one of them has been a counter somebody has not been able to stop for four years.**"

A bell at the rear of that shop had been fixed through the face of a hollow block with two short plugs, so that it worked loose in the frame every time it was rung.

She changed the fixings and proved the bell on the frame.

"**Five pounds,**" she said. "**A bell that works loose is a bell that stops answering. Four of those bells in that row are fixed into hollow blocks, and one of them has been a door somebody stopped opening for four years.**"

A socket in that flat had no earth at all on it, so that a metal case on an appliance could come live.

He bonded the case and proved the bond at the socket.

"**Six pounds,**" she said. "**A socket with no earth is a case waiting for a fault. Four of those sockets in that block are unearthed, and one of them has been a kettle somebody has been careful with for four years.**"

**Twenty-four pounds is what those four came to on that Sunday, exact.**

---

**That Sunday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on the shelf at the back, and this page offers no figure for the page it keeps.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **A passage under this one calls out ten things and never brings two of them together. Two places cut in a rail. One creased card holding a doorway. A board ruled with nineteen lines and nothing on them. Torn corners on nine copies of a front page. A tin with its lid down. A shutter. A green binding standing on a shelf. Standing clear of all ten there is the cage down one side of a counter, and standing clear with it is a sheet in a drawer in a second district with one name struck through and one written underneath it. A page of nine names is not one of the ten, is not totalled here, and is not printed on this page.
A line kept under is not a line answered. A man who will not deny a thing can still be asked about it twice.
A rule that is dated stands over somebody else's question and not over this one, and the records behind it can be read by anybody who comes and asks.
No one thanked anyone that Sunday and no one forgave anyone. A man carried a book he could not close and a young man told him why he could not be sent to ask, and both of them went home with the line still on the page.**
