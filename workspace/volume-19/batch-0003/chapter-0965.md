# Chapter 965 — Nobody Asked Her Why She Came

**A Saturday was a bench until noon and a counter until four, and a woman of about fifty-one stood in that doorway for a while before she came in, and eight people came and went through that shop that Saturday without one of them knowing who she was.**

---

Eight callers came through that shop on that Saturday, the last of them at half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and eighty-three days, two hundred and fifty-four weeks and five days. The printed form on a wall in a fourth district was fifty-three days old and had not moved.**

---

**She had come in at about four and she had not sat down, and about four people in that room have said since that she had chosen the one afternoon when the room was at its busiest on a Saturday, and that this had not been an accident.**

She said: I have come back.

The woman of about forty-three said: I see that.

She said: nobody has written my name anywhere.

The woman of about forty-three said: no.

She said: I have been five weeks finding out whether that day was mine.

**Nobody in that room said anything, and about four people in that room have said since that eight people went past her in about half an hour and not one of them looked up, and that this is what a room with no names in it looks like from inside.**

---

**Talia was at the far end of that counter about a clinic rota and heard all of it, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody looked at her once while the woman was talking, and that she did not look at anybody either.**

**Marek put the pen down and asked the one question nobody in that shop had ever asked her, and he had been in that shop four times in a month and had asked her two other things.**

Marek said: I asked you two things in a month and I never asked you why you came.

She said: no.

Marek said: and nobody else did either.

She said: nobody else did.

Marek said: **that question was not mine to ask.**

She said: **then whose was it.**

**About four people in that room have said since that the woman of about fifty-one stood absolutely still through about nine seconds in which nobody spoke, and did not help anybody, and that she had been waiting a long time for one of them to reach for the answer.**

Marek said: hers.

She said: she has not said so.

Marek said: no.

---

**And then Talia spoke for the first time in that conversation, and she spoke to the woman and not to Marek, and about four people in that room have said since that she did it in her office voice and that it made it a rule instead of a remark.**

Talia said: my office asks what a person wants and does not ask what a person meant.

She said: and you are her office.

Talia said: **I am, and an office does not put a person on paper to be asked about, and if I opened that file you would be in it for the rest of your life with a number on it.**

She said: so the question goes nowhere.

Talia said: **the question goes to the only person in this room who is not allowed to be the one to ask it, and he has just said so himself, and that is not a failure, that is the arrangement.**

**Nothing was said in that room for about nine seconds, and Marek has said since that he stood there and let it be put on him in front of a stranger, and that he has not decided yet whether he will forgive her for it.**

She said: I will come back when somebody asks.

Marek said: nobody is going to ask.

She said: **then I will come back until somebody does, and I have five weeks to wait and nowhere to be.**

**And then the woman of about fifty-one went out into that Saturday at about half past four and nobody stopped her, and about four people in that room have said since that she did not look back at the doorway on the way and that two people at the counter watched her all the way to the end of that row.**

---

**That shop and that close got four jobs between them on that Saturday, and about two hours of what was left of it.**

A window in that close had a restrictor on it that had been glued rather than screwed, so that it could not be opened and could not be removed.

She unscrewed the restrictor and proved the opening with a stick.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A restrictor glued on is a window that is shut and cannot be anything else. Four of those restrictors in that block are glued, and one of them has been a bedroom somebody has wanted to open for four years.**"

A fire door in that shop had a closer on it that had been set to slam, so that it came down on anybody coming through.

She reset the closer and proved it with the shop lit.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A closer set to slam is a door that argues with the people who work here. Four of those closers in that row are set wrong, and one of them has been a shop somebody has stopped going in and out of for four years.**"

A socket in that shop had been fed off a lamp in the ceiling, so that the lamp could not be changed without losing it.

He put it on its own way and proved the lamp from the switch.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A socket fed off a ceiling lamp is a lamp you cannot replace. Four of those sockets in that row are fed off a light, and one of them has been a counter somebody has changed the bulb over for four years.**"

A cooker point in that close had been mounted with the face about half an inch off the wall, so that the plug had to be held in at an angle.

He packed the box out and proved the plug with a lamp.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A cooker point standing off a wall is a plug that comes out in your hand. Four of those boxes in that block are packed short, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has held steady for four years.**"

**Forty-one pounds is what those four came to on that Saturday, exact.**

**That Saturday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder was where it has been for four years, and this page prints no figure for the page inside it.**
