# Chapter 963 — She Came Back At Nine

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six, and a woman went out at half past seven with her coat buttoned and came back at about nine, and what she said when she came in was two sentences long and neither of them was about the page.**

---

Nine callers went through that shop on that Tuesday, the ninth of them at about twenty-five past nine, and the shutter came to at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and seventy-nine days, two hundred and fifty-four weeks and one day. The request in the drawer in a second district was twenty-eight days old on that Tuesday.**

---

**She took a page off a shelf in a records room in a second district, inside her coat, on a bus that takes twenty minutes, and put it on her own table with the door shut and the woman of about forty-five on the landing outside it, and then put it back in her coat and carried it to the same room and gave it back the same day by her own hand. This page does not follow her, and what was read at that table is not on this page in any form and will not be.**

**She came back at about nine and put her bag on the floor and did not take her coat off, and the woman of about forty-five was on the rail and Marek was at the counter, and about four people in that room have said since that the woman of about forty-five had gone out with her at half past seven and had come back at nine and had said nothing whatever about the two hours and had not asked one question about them.**

She said: I have read it.

Marek said: and.

She said: **that is all you get.**

**Nobody in that room said one word for about nine seconds, and Marek has said since that he had the whole night worked out and about four different sentences ready and that he stood there and used none of them, because she had told him on a Friday to say it again on the day and he had said it again on the day by saying nothing.**

---

**And then the woman of about forty-five asked to be told, and about four people in that room have said since that she asked it in a flat voice and that she had clearly decided to ask before she had come.**

She said: I am going to hold this.

Sera Quill said: you are.

She said: I am going to hold something I have never seen.

Sera Quill said: **you did not read it, and if I tell you what is on it then you will never stop reading it in your own head, and that is a worse thing than not knowing.**

She said: is it bad.

Sera Quill said: it is work. It is not bad. It is work and it is slow and there is more of it than there is room for.

She said: how much work.

Sera Quill said: **about nine things, and a road, and a decision at the end of the road that is mine and not yours.**

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and the woman of about forty-three has said since that the quiet at that end of the counter was the loudest thing she has ever heard in that shop.**

---

**Marek asked the only question he had allowed himself and he asked it about himself and not about the page.**

Marek said: does the finding have anybody's name on it.

Sera Quill said: no.

Marek said: not even yours.

Sera Quill said: **not one of the nine, and not me, and not him, and the only name on any paper in this is the one he put there himself.**

Marek said: then it is going to end up being mine.

Sera Quill said: it is nobody's finding until somebody says they will stand in front of it, and that has not happened and I am not doing it on a Tuesday night in this room.

**And then she said the thing she had come back to say, and about four people have said since that she said it standing up and with her coat still on.**

Sera Quill said: I have read it and I have a decision to make and the decision is mine and not yours.

Marek said: when will you know.

Sera Quill said: when I have been out on it. Not tonight. Not in this room.

---

**What was left of that Tuesday went into that shop and the flat above it, four jobs in about two hours, and only two of those four were done with a hand that held.**

---

**His hands had gone at about half past two that afternoon, before any of the above, in the middle of a docket, and they stayed gone for about two hours, and the woman of about forty-three took the pen out of his hand at the counter and wrote out the rest of that day's docket in her own writing and signed for it, and about four people who work in that building have said since that nobody there has ever signed for him before and that she did not think about it.**

He said: I cannot hold anything.

The woman of about forty-three said: you cannot hold anything and you have four jobs on still to do.

Marek said: then the afternoon is not mine.

She said: the afternoon is mine and I am telling you what it came to and you will pay for it.

**He has said since that this was the day the arrangement cost him something he could put a number on, and that the number was smaller than the cost of breaking the arrangement and he has never been sure which of those two facts is the worse one.**

A window in that flat had been fixed into a frame cut short, so that the glass rocked in the wind.

He packed the frame out and proved the glass with a hand.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**Glass that rocks in the wind is a window that lets a room hear the street. Four of those frames in that block are cut short, and one of them has been a bedroom somebody has had to sleep with a gap in for four years.**"

A cupboard light in that flat had been wired with no earth on its metal reflector, so that a fault on the fitting had nowhere to go.

He earthed the reflector and proved the earth at the fitting.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A cupboard light with no earth is a fitting waiting for a wet room. Four of those lights in that block are un-earthed, and one of them has been a cupboard somebody has opened with a wet hand for four years.**"

A tub in that shop had been trapped off a stopcock that fed the whole run, so that running it emptied the sink as well.

She changed the trap and proved it on the far tap.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A tub off the main stopcock is a sink that empties when you wash. Four of those traps in that row are off the run, and one of them has been a trough somebody has had to keep half full for four years.**"

A socket in the shop had been fitted under a shelf where nothing could get at it, so that the plug had to be pushed in at an angle.

She moved the socket clear and proved it with a lamp.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A socket under a shelf is a plug that has to be pushed in by feel. Four of those sockets in that row are behind something, and one of them has been a counter somebody has reached under for four years.**"

**Thirty-three pounds is what those four came to on that Tuesday, exact, and sixteen of the thirty-three was work she did alone.**

---

**That Tuesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stood where it stands, and nothing is given here about the page it holds.**

Tuesday of week 322, at ten. That is the two hundred and eighth day of this stretch of days. That Tuesday brought nine callers into that shop and put nine names onto that day's book, the last of them at about twenty-five past nine.

*Conditions of the close.* **The green book is shut where the last sitting shut it and the tin beside it has its lid down, and neither one is written about here and this page would not know what to make of the two together.
That ninth chair keeps its back to the room and it did not shift on that Tuesday, and no part of these days puts a name to whoever turned it round last.
There is a room under a building in a first district and at about ten that night there was no light in it and no fire and none now either, and it has been made and left dark from the start.
Four is the count in the register of correct acts that changed nothing, at both ends of that Tuesday, and nobody in this city enters it anywhere. Reading a page and telling nobody is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **A passage beneath this one names ten objects and no sentence holds two of them at once. Two slots in one rail. A card with a single crease, wedging a door open. A board carried by two nails. Nine copies with a corner torn off each. A tin with a closed lid. The shutter. A book in green boards. A binder at the back of a shelf. A slip with a date on it and no hand on it. A pencil in a margin.
Outside the count stands the cage down one side of the counter, and outside it with the cage stands a request in a drawer in a second district, one name struck and one name added. A sheet of nine names is not one of the ten, is not added up here, and is not printed anywhere on this page.
Holding something you did not read is not the same as knowing nothing. A man who signs to stand outside is outside.
A rule with a date on it stands over a different question, and what stands behind it is written down and open to anybody who comes and asks to see it.
Nobody thanked anybody that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody. One woman read a page and told two people one sentence each, and one of those two is not him.**
