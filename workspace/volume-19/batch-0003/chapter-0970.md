# Chapter 970 — He Asked If He Was Allowed To Help

**A Saturday was a bench until noon and a counter until four and then a warm room with about four people in it, and a man of twenty-two had been carrying one question round that shop since the Monday and asked it before he had the counter tidied.**

---

Nine callers came through that shop on that Saturday, the last of them at about twenty-five past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and ninety days, two hundred and fifty-five weeks and five days. The printed form on a wall in a fourth district was sixty days old and had not moved.**

---

**She was on the rail before he had the counter tidied and he said the thing without sitting down, and about four people in that room have said since that he had it ready since the Monday and had been carrying it round all week waiting for a room with only the two of them in it.**

Marek said: am I allowed to help.

Sera Quill said: **no, and the day you help is the day the finding is yours.**

Marek said: I am not asking to find them.

Sera Quill said: there is no finding that is not you finding them.

Marek said: and if I am not in it.

Sera Quill said: **then you wait, and waiting is the whole of what you signed for, and I am not going to soften it because it is a Saturday.**

**He had about nine seconds in which to have said something and he did not use them, and Marek has said since that he had expected her to say it kindly and that she had said it plainly and that this was the first time in nine days that he had been glad of anything.**

Marek said: I could carry things.

Sera Quill said: no.

Marek said: I could be in the room.

Sera Quill said: **no. You are the only person in this matter with a name on a piece of paper, and the day you walk into a room where a page is open, that page is about you.**

---

**And then she said the thing that turned it from a refusal into an answer, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it to the window and not to him.**

Sera Quill said: I have a decision to make and it is about four months of somebody else's life.

Marek said: I could help you make it.

Sera Quill said: **you could help me carry it afterwards, and afterwards is not this year, and I do not know which year it is.**

Marek said: that is not a no to what I asked.

Sera Quill said: no. It is a yes to a different question and I would have said that one too.

**And then the woman of about forty-five, who had been standing at the back of that room for about nine minutes, said one sentence and left, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it to the door and not to either of them.**

She said: **I am glad somebody asked you and I am sorry the answer was no, and those two things are going to sit on me until Wednesday.**

---

**And then at about nine the woman of about forty-three came in off the street with her coat open, did not sit down, and put one sheet on that counter, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the third of the three in the tray and that the column on it is in neither of the two places the other two are in, and that there is something written in it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: there is a third one.

Marek said: from the tray.

She said: from the tray, and it came in with about nine other things on the Friday, and I put it at the back because I was busy and I did not look at it again.

Sera Quill said: it has a column.

The woman of about forty-three said: it has a column and **there is something written in it.**

**He was not ready for that, and it took him about nine seconds to stop standing as if he were, and Marek has said since that he had been ready for anything else on that Saturday and that he sat down on the edge of the counter and did not get up again until about ten.**

Sera Quill said: you have not read it.

The woman of about forty-three said: **I have read it and I am not going to read it out in front of anybody, and that is not modesty, it is that I do not know yet who put it there.**

Marek said: you know as much as anybody in this city.

The woman of about forty-three said: **I know that somebody in this city has answered the only question nine people in this matter have agreed to agree on, and that they did it without telling me, and that they did it with a pen.**

Marek said: what is written.

The woman of about forty-three said: **I am not going to tell you and I am going to shut that tray and if you want it in a month you can have it in a month.**

**For about nine seconds he said one word to himself and no word at all to anybody in that room, and about four people in that room have said since that Marek put his hand flat on that counter where a page had once lain with nine rows on it and no names, and that his hands were no good and that he left it there anyway.**

---

**That shop and that close, four jobs and about two hours, was the whole of what was left of that Saturday.**

A window in that close had been sealed with a strip of tape over the joint, so that the frame had rotted behind it.

She took the tape off, cut the frame back and proved the closing with a stick.

"**Twelve pounds,**" she said. "**A taped joint is a joint somebody has decided not to look at. Four of those windows in that block are taped, and one of them has been a bedroom somebody has had to open in summer for four years.**"

A bell in that shop had been fixed with a screw through the gong, so that the gong had a chip in it and rang thin.

She took the gong off, filed the chip and proved the bell.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A bell with a chip in it is a bell everybody stops hearing. Four of those gongs in that row are chipped, and one of them has been a counter somebody has stopped listening to for four years.**"

A light in that shop had a lamp in it of a type that had not been made for the fitting, so that the holder had come loose in it.

She put in a lamp of the rating the holder was made for and proved it lit.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**The wrong lamp in a fitting is a holder working loose for four years before anybody says so. Four of those fittings in that row have the wrong lamp in them, and one of them has been a bench somebody has had to hang on to for four years.**"

An outside light at the back of that shop had been controlled from inside, so that whoever was out at the back could not see what they were doing.

He moved the switch and proved it from the yard.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A back light with its switch inside is four years of working by torch. Four of those lights in that row are like that, and one of them has been a yard somebody has fallen over in for four years.**"

**Forty-three pounds is what those four came to on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**That Saturday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on its shelf at the back, and this page gives no figure for the page it keeps.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects get their names said in a passage under this one and no two of them are ever said in one breath. A rail with two places cut in it. A card creased once, keeping a door open. A board carried up by two nails. Nine corners torn off nine copies of a front. A tin with its lid down. The shutter. A green book. A binder at the back of a shelf that nobody has opened. A slip with a date on it and no hand on it. The pencil in that margin.
The cage down one side of that counter stands outside all ten of them, and so does the request lying in a drawer in a second district with one name struck through and one name written under it. A page of nine names is none of the ten and is not added to any figure here and is not printed on this page.
A column that has been filled in is not a person who has been told. A tray is not a register.
A dated rule stands over a question that belongs to somebody else, and what is written behind it is open to anybody who comes and asks.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody. One man asked whether he was allowed to help, one woman told him why not, and one other woman shut a drawer with a pen mark in it and would not say whose.**

**AND THE SIX THINGS A LATER PASS MUST CARRY FORWARD FROM THESE TEN. *One:* the page was read on a Tuesday on Sera Quill's own table, with the second holder on the landing outside the door, and the page itself was taken off a shelf in a second district inside her coat and carried back to the same room the same day by her hand, and she came into this shop at about nine and said one sentence to a man and a different sentence to the woman holding with her, and neither sentence was about what is on it. *Two:* the finding has nobody's name on it and the decision is Sera Quill's and not anybody else's, and she said it will be about four months of somebody else's life and that she does not know which year. *Three:* Marek asked to help and was refused twice in the same conversation and has said since that he was glad of the plainness of it. *Four:* Talia used her office on him once in this movement, on a file that is about the request he signed and not about the nine, and the subject of it was the four names he had written on the back of a job card instead of asking. She made him say them out loud, kept nothing, could not unhear them, and wrote one line into the file that is about him and about nobody else. *Five:* three sheets now carry a sixth column headed told and all three of them are in one tray in a building in a second district, which is shut again now. One was ruled by hand by the man of about fifty-two, one is pencil in a margin and has been round nine rooms, and the third came in on the Friday and **has been filled in once**, and nobody in this city will say by whom. *Six:* a man of about thirty-three left the only copy of that sheet with an empty column lying on a table in that shop and nobody there holds it. A woman of about fifty-one came back and was not asked why and says she will come back until somebody asks. The man of about sixty-one told a stranger that people have been in his room the whole nine years.*
