# Chapter 990 — Nobody Asked What She Had Read

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and a woman told a man at that counter what his Wednesday had cost, and she was not being cruel about it and she said it anyway.**

---

That Thursday took six callers through that shop and the last of them was let away about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and twenty-three days, two hundred and sixty weeks and three days. The printed form up on a wall in a passage in a fourth district was ninety-three days old that Thursday and had not been straightened once.**

---

**The woman of about forty-five came in at about half past seven and did not sit down and about four people in that shop will tell you that she had been in the room and had come out of it and had walked across a city with one sentence and had been holding it for about a day.**

Marek said: you were there.

The woman of about forty-five said: **I was there.**

Marek said: you have not said anything about it since.

The woman of about forty-five said: **I have been walking.**

---

**And then she said it, and she said it flat and in one go, and nobody in the room could have stopped her and she had not come in to be stopped.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **she stood up and said she found it and said she would answer for finding it and read nothing out, and about nine people in that city now know a woman found something on a Tuesday.**

Marek said: about nine people were in that room.

The woman of about forty-five said: **about nine people were in that room and about nine of them will tell about nine other people by Friday, and by Friday this city knows that something exists and not one person in it knows what.**

---

Marek said: that was the arrangement.

The woman of about forty-five said: **that was the arrangement, and I asked her for it, and I am telling you what it cost and you are not going to be the one who tells me it was worth it.**

Marek said: I am not going to.

The woman of about forty-five said: **good. I have had about four of them say it was worth it and every one of them said it about four days after somebody else paid for it.**

---

**Two callers came through that counter and were served while neither of them said anything, and the till went on the whole time and that neither of the two at it looked up.**

Marek said: the woman of about forty-five.

The woman of about forty-five said: **she was the only person in that room who said nothing about the page, and I have not told her that I noticed, and I am not going to, and that is the last thing I have got.**

Marek said: I could tell her.

The woman of about forty-five said: **you could and you will not, and if you do I will know inside a day, and I am telling you that so that it costs you something to do it.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-one came in at about half past eight with her coat buttoned to the neck and asked one question, and she had been in that room and had not slept, and she asked it about nine seconds after she got to the counter.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **was it about me.**

Marek said: **I do not know.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **you were in the room.**

Marek said: **I was in the room and nobody read anything out, and that is the true answer and I am not going to make it into a better one for you.**

---

**And the woman of about fifty-one said the thing that ended that Thursday, and she said it with her coat still on and did not sit down for it.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **that is what I came for. I have been coming to that counter for about four months to find out whether a thing is about me, and I have got told no, and I would rather be told no by somebody who was standing in the room.**

Marek said: **I am sorry.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **do not be sorry. You were not in a position to say yes and you said no and you said it straight and about four of us have noticed the difference between that and being kind.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three came in at about nine with about half an hour left before the shutter, and he came in without his coat, having been outside for about twenty minutes before he came in.**

Marek said: you were not in that room.

The man of about thirty-three said: **I was not in that room and I have not slept and she has not spoken to me since Wednesday.**

Marek said: you said that was the whole difficulty.

The man of about thirty-three said: **that was the whole difficulty and it is still the whole difficulty and both of those are the same thing now.**

---

**And Marek said nothing at all for about four seconds, and about four people in that shop will tell you that he put a box of lamps down on the counter and did not open it and that the man waited for the whole of the four seconds.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **are you going to say anything.**

Marek said: **no, and I want you to hear the shape of it, and the shape of it is that there is nothing in this shop that helps, and I have known that about this shop for about four months.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **then I have come in for nothing again.**

Marek said: **you have come in to have said it out loud in a room with somebody in it, and that is not nothing at all, and I am not going to tell you that it was.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and both women were gone before half past nine and the last of them went out about twenty minutes before the shutter came down, and about four people who come in will tell you that nobody went and looked for anybody else.**

---

**One shop, one yard and one kitchen took what was left of that Thursday, about two hours of it, and four jobs went into them, and there was not a fifth.**

An outside light in that shop had been put in on the alarm circuit so that it came on with the alarm and stayed on.

He took it off that way and put it on its own and proved it.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A light that comes on with the alarm is a light that is on for the alarm and not for the yard. Two of those are like it. Fittings in that row are on it, and one of them has been a yard somebody has walked in the dark for four years.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a lead that came off a joint buried under the paving, so that the joint was live whenever the socket was.

He dug the joint out, made it good and re-made the paving over it.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A joint under paving is a joint nobody finds until the paving comes up. That fault is four times over in that row. Joints in that yard are under paving, and one of them has been a path somebody has dug at for four years.**"

A switch in that kitchen had been put in with a fuse bigger than the circuit and a spare way left blank in front of it.

He fitted a fuse of the size that circuit wanted, blanked the spare way and proved the board.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A blank way left open is an invitation with a cover on. Four of those boards in that block are left blank, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has been afraid of for four years.**"

A lamp in that shop had been left with a holder that had been painted round so that it could not be unscrewed.

He cut the paint back, freed the holder and proved the lamp lit.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A holder painted in is a bulb that cannot be changed in a room somebody has to light. Four of those holders in that row are painted in, and one of them has been a room somebody has had to carry a lamp out of since the shop changed hands.**"

**Those four came to thirty-nine pounds on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**That Thursday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder was still on the shelf at the back of it, and this page carries no figure for the page inside it and never has.**
