# Chapter 986 — The Page Went Into A Drawer

**A Saturday was a bench until noon and a counter until four and then four converted units off that service road, and a man asked a man to hold a sheet of paper in a drawer instead of leaving it on a table, and about four people in that shop have said since that it was the first time anything in this matter had been picked up by anybody.**

---

That Saturday took nine callers through that shop and the ninth of them was let away near twenty-five past nine, and the shutter came to at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and eighteen days, two hundred and fifty-nine weeks and five days. The request in the drawer in a second district was sixty-seven days old on that Saturday.**

---

**He came in at about four with his mother not with him, and about four people in that shop will tell you that he had been outside for about ten minutes deciding whether to come in and that he came in.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I asked her.**

Marek said: on a Saturday.

The man of about thirty-three said: **on a Saturday, in front of my mother, at about eleven this morning, and I had it ready for about a fortnight.**

Marek said: what did she say.

The man of about thirty-three said: **she said she would answer to a room and not to her son.**

---

Marek said: that is not nothing.

The man of about thirty-three said: **it is nothing at all, and about four people in that house have heard nothing at all, and I am the one who has to go back in there on Wednesday with what I have got.**

Marek said: you can tell her you asked.

The man of about thirty-three said: **I can tell her I asked. I cannot tell her what she should have said.**

---

**And then he asked for the sheet, and about four people in that shop will say that he asked for it about a foot away from where it was lying and that he did not touch it while he asked.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **it has been on that table for thirty days.**

Marek said: **it has been on that table for thirty days and she named that as one of three things, and the second thing was that I do not ask her on a Monday, and the first was that I shut at ten.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I am not asking you to break any of the three. I am asking you to take it off the table and put it in a drawer.**

Marek said: **that is breaking the first one and not the third.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **is it.**

---

**And about nine seconds went by at that counter and about four people in that shop have it that Marek looked at the sheet for the whole of them and did not touch it and that he then picked it up with two fingers as though it had been lying in water.**

Marek said: **it stays on the table.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **you have just said it stays on the table.**

Marek said: **it stays where she said it stays, and she said it stays where it lies, and if I put it in a drawer then in about four months somebody will ask me who moved it and I will have to say a man asked me to and that is a worse sentence than the one I have now.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-one came in at about half past six with her coat already buttoned and asked one question, and about four people in that shop have said since that it was the only question asked in that shop that evening and that Marek could not answer it.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **am I to come on the Wednesday.**

Marek said: **I cannot tell you that.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **you cannot or you will not.**

Marek said: **I cannot. If I tell one person then I have told, and then there is a thing that has been told, and this whole matter has been four years of nobody being told anything.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **then I will come on the Wednesday.**

Marek said: **you will come on the Wednesday.**

The woman of about fifty-one said: **I have never once been asked to stay away from a room in my life, and about four of us have been in on the same Saturdays, and I would rather be there and wrong than not be there.**

---

**And then he came back in at about half past seven because he had been standing outside, and about four people in that shop agree that he had not come back for anything and that he had come back because he had one more question.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **has she said anything to you.**

Marek said: no.

The man of about thirty-three said: **has she said anything to anybody.**

Marek said: **I do not know, and I am not going to find out, and that is the second time today that somebody has asked me a thing I have to refuse.**

---

**The man of about thirty-three did not argue with it, which was the first time in about a month, and about four people in that shop have said since that he turned round and looked at the shutter and then at the sheet, and that the count of the silence was not done by anybody.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **you have heard it.**

Marek said: **I have heard that somebody said yes in about nine words. You have not heard it from me and you are not going to, and if you had heard it from me then you would have been able to say afterwards that a shop had it before her mother did.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **that is the second true thing anybody has said to me this month.**

Marek said: **and I am not going to be thanked for either of them.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody, and the man of about thirty-three went out at about seven with his hands in his pockets and the sheet was still lying on that table when the shutter came down.**

---

**One yard, one kitchen and two shops took what was left of that Saturday, about two hours of it, and four jobs went into them.**

A floodlight in that yard had been mounted on a bracket that was bolted to nothing but a wooden lintel.

He cut two fixings into the brick above the lintel and made the bracket solid.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A floodlight hung off a lintel is a floodlight waiting for weather. Every one of those is the same brackets in that row are on timber, and one of them has been a yard somebody has been watching in bad weather for four years.**"

A cooker in that first shop had been put in with a flex that came out of the wall and immediately through the back of the cupboard above it.

He rerouted the flex through the side of the cupboard and sleeved it.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A flex that goes straight into the back of a cupboard is a flex that gets nicked every time anything is taken out. Three of those in that block are like it in that row are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has taken things out of carefully for four years.**"

A socket in that kitchen had been put in on an old bakelite plate with the earth taken off the conduit it sat on.

He earthed it off the box, fitted a modern plate and proved it with a tester.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**An old plate with no earth is the oldest lie in this trade. Four of those plates in that street are like it, and one of them has been a wall somebody has been careful with for as long as anybody there can remember.**"

A light in that second shop had been put in on a pendant that hung over the middle of a doorway with the flex twisted round itself.

He cut the flex out, shortened the drop and rewired it without a twist.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A flex twisted into itself is a flex that has been cut shorter than it says. Four of those pendants in that row are, and one of them has been a doorway somebody has walked under ducking before either of them moved in.**"

**Those four came to forty pounds on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**That Saturday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder went on being on the shelf at the back of it, and this page does not offer a figure for what is on the shelf.**
