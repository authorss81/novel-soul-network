# Chapter 988 — He Will Not Stand Beside Her

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and a man said at that counter that he would not be in a room on the Wednesday, and about four people in that shop have said since that nobody in that shop asked him why until he had already told them.**

---

That Tuesday took four callers through that shop and the last of them was let away about ten past nine, and the shutter was down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and twenty-one days, two hundred and sixty weeks and one day. The request in the drawer in a second district was seventy days old on that Tuesday.**

---

**He came in at about half past four and said it before he had taken his coat off, and about four people in that shop will tell you that he had it written on something and did not read it out.**

Marek said: you will not be in the room.

The man of about thirty-three said: **no.**

Marek said: your mother will be in it.

The man of about thirty-three said: **she will be in it and that is the whole difficulty and it has been the whole difficulty since a Friday about three and a half weeks ago.**

---

**And then he told them, and about four people in that shop will say that he told it standing in the middle of the floor and not at the counter and that the till went on the whole time.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **there are three people holding one word my mother said out loud in that room, and one of the three has it wrong, and I am one of the three.**

Marek said: you have said that before.

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have said the first two parts before. I have not said the third part, and I said it about four minutes after I came in, and I have been carrying it since a Sunday.**

Marek said: which of the three has it wrong.

The man of about thirty-three said: **I am not going to tell you and I am not going to tell her.**

---

Marek said: why will you not tell her.

The man of about thirty-three said: **because if I tell her she stops carrying it, and if she stops carrying it she is a woman of sixty-two with a thing she has given away and nothing in her hands.**

Marek said: **that is the most careful thing anybody has said in this shop in four months and it is also the reason I am not going to argue with it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **then you have about four seconds to say the rest of it.**

---

**And he said the rest of it without stopping, and about four people in that shop have it that he had it ready before he came in and that he came in at about half past four to say it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I will not stand beside her on Wednesday while she stands in front of it.**

Marek said: you do not have to stand beside her.

The man of about thirty-three said: **and I am not going to be at the back of it either, because the back of it is still in it, and about nine of them would spend the whole hour not looking at her, and she came to say one sentence.**

---

**And about nine seconds passed in that shop and about four people in it have said since that nobody said anything for the length of it and that the shutter was half up and a bus went past outside.**

Marek said: you could sit down.

The man of about thirty-three said: **I could sit down.**

Marek said: **and it would be the same room and about the same distance.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **it would be the same room and I would be in it, and I have been asked that twice this week by men who are being kind to me.**

---

**And then he went across to the room off the line and asked the woman who keeps it, and about four people who sit in that room agree that he asked her one question and did not give her any of the story attached to it.**

Marek said: a man has told me he thinks one of three people has a thing wrong. May he stand in your room on Wednesday.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **yes.**

Marek said: you have not asked me who.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have not asked you who, and I am not going to, and I would have said yes before you had finished the sentence and you knew that or you would not have come.**

---

Marek said: why.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **because a man who is not sure of a thing is more use in a room than a man who is sure of it, and because in six years the only people who have ever come in here are people who could not be sure, and about four of them have been sure and they have all been wrong about something.**

Marek said: that is a rule.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is six years of a room and I have never written it down and I am not starting now.**

---

**And then he counted the three things out loud in that room with about four people in it and the till in another building, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that he counted them on the fingers of his left hand and that he had never done it before in four months.**

Marek said: **shut at ten. Do not ask her on a Monday. The sheet stays lying.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and now you have said them out loud in a room with nobody in it who is the matter.**

Marek said: **I said them out loud in that shop on the Tuesday to one woman and she did not thank me, and on Saturday about nine people asked me two things and I refused both, and that is what keeping three things is.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and he went out at about five having said all of it out loud, and nobody asked him a second time and about four of them did not sleep.**

---

**One shop and one kitchen took what was left of that Tuesday, about two hours of it, and four jobs went into them.**

A plug in that shop had been rewired so that the pin went in the top on one side of the room and the bottom on the other.

He took it out and rewired it the way the rest of the room was wired.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A plug that goes in two ways is a plug somebody is going to put in the wrong way. Four rows in that street are like it in that building are, and one of them has been a counter somebody has been unplugging for four years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had a trailing lead that had been taped along the skirting and had gone under the fridge.

He took the lead out, shortened it and brought it up at the worktop.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A lead under a fridge is a lead that gets crushed every time the fridge moves. Half of those are like it in that street are, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has moved about for four years.**"

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose screwed to a ceiling that had a pipe running under the plaster across it.

He moved the rose clear of the pipe, made the two holes new and proved it.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A rose over a pipe is a hole that goes straight through somebody's water. Four of those in that row are over something, and one of them has been a ceiling somebody has been careful with since long before the street was paved.**"

A switch in that kitchen had been put in next to the boiler so that the boiler's own supply came off it.

He moved the switch onto a proper way and proved the boiler came off its own isolator.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A boiler on a light switch is a boiler nobody can isolate. Four of those in that block are, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has been nervous of and nobody has ever asked about it.**"

**Those four came to thirty pounds on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**That Tuesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on the shelf at the back of it, and this page puts nothing against the page in the ring.**
