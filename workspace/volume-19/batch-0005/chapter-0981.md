# Chapter 981 — The Finding Has Nobody Standing In Front Of It

**A Saturday was a bench until noon and a counter until four and then four converted units off that service road, and a woman who holds a door and what is known behind it asked a man for one sentence and was refused, and nobody in that shop expected her to ask him for anything at all.**

---

That Saturday took nine callers through that shop and the ninth of the nine was onto that day's book at about twenty past nine, about ten minutes after the eighth had gone, and the shutter was down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and eleven days, two hundred and fifty-eight weeks and five days. The request in the drawer in a second district was sixty days old on that Saturday.**

---

**The woman of about forty-five came in at about half past three and did not take her coat off, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had it ready before she came through the door and had been carrying it since the Monday.**

Marek said: you want a sentence.

The woman of about forty-five said: I want one sentence, said out loud, saying the finding is not his.

Marek said: that is not the same sentence.

The woman of about forty-five said: **it is the same sentence with the end left off, and a person who has read a thing is a person somebody can ask about a thing.**

Marek said: you have read it.

The woman of about forty-five said: **I stood outside a door on a Tuesday while a woman put a page back on a shelf in a coat, and holding what I know is not the same as being able to say where I know it.**

Marek said: you could say there is a finding and not say what it is.

The woman of about forty-five said: **no. If I say there is a finding then I have said who found it, and she has not said it and I am not going to be her second voice.**

Marek said: I am not going to ask you to be.

The woman of about forty-five said: **you can stop me asking. You cannot stop me having asked.**

---

**And about nine seconds went by at that counter with the till going and nobody in it saying anything at all, and Marek has said since that he spent that time trying to work out whether a person who will not speak is holding something back or holding it.**

Marek said: who would you have said it.

The woman of about forty-five said: **her, or me, and I will not do it, so it is her, and you are not going to get any nearer than you are standing now.**

Marek said: I know that.

The woman of about forty-five said: **then say it to her yourself and stop looking round that counter for a woman with a door.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three came in at about half past five with his hands in his coat pockets and asked for the sheet, and about four people in that shop will tell you that he had not asked for it in front of anybody before that Saturday, and that it had plainly been in that pocket for about a week.**

Marek said: no.

The man of about thirty-three said: it has been lying there about three weeks.

Marek said: **it has been lying there about three weeks and she named that as one of three things on the Monday, and I did not argue with her about it then and I am not going to start on a Saturday.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **so I walk past it every morning and I am told by a woman who will not decide that it is where it lies.**

Marek said: yes.

The man of about thirty-three said: **and if she never decides.**

Marek said: **then it lies there, and that is worse for you than it is for me, and I would rather it was worse for me.**

---

**He stood at the customer side of that counter with his hands still in his pockets for about a quarter of an hour and did not go, and about four people who were in that shop have said since that he said the thing that made that Saturday the Saturday it was, and that it took him about nine seconds to get to it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: ask her.

Marek said: no.

The man of about thirty-three said: you have not asked.

Marek said: **she has said it twice and I am not going to spend that.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I do not care which day of the week it is. I am asking you to be the one who asks.**

Marek said: **if I ask her then the asking is mine and so is the finding.**

**And about nine seconds passed at that counter, and about four people who were in that shop have it that neither of them said anything for the length of it and that four callers came through the door in that time and were served without either of them noticing.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **then I will ask her.**

Marek said: **you will ask her yourself, and not on a Monday, because she will tell you what she told me.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **she comes when she comes.**

Marek said: **so does the woman who has kept that room off the line for six years.**

---

**And she said the thing that went out of that shop with him, and about four people in it have it that she said it at the door with her hand still on it.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **I am going to find somebody to say it, and it will not be you and it will not be me, and when I have found them I will not tell either of you first.**

Marek said: I would rather you told me.

The woman of about forty-five said: **no. You would arrive early, and about nine of them would arrive with you.**

---

**One shop, one yard and two rooms took what was left of that Saturday, about two hours of it, and four jobs went into them.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a length of flex that came round a corner and was held there by a nail.

He took the flex off the nail, cut it back to the socket and made a proper gland at it.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**Nothing in this row has held a wire but a nail. Every one of those is the same. They are like it, and one of them is a corner somebody has swept round a hundred times and never looked at.**"

A lamp in that shop had a holder screwed to a ceiling rose that was not the same size as the rose.

He fitted a short length of pipe back to the ceiling and hung the holder off that.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A holder bolted to the wrong rose comes down with the first good pull. Three of those in that block are like it. Ceilings have one, and the one I would not leave is a shop that has had a shade in the air for four years.**"

A switch in one of those rooms had been fitted over the lintel of a door so that the door could not open past about four inches.

He moved the plate onto the wall beside the opening and made good the two holes.

"**Six pounds,**" she said. "**A switch on a lintel is a door that has been got used to hitting it. Four of those plates in that block are on a frame, and one of them has been a door somebody has squeezed for as long as anybody there can remember.**"

A bell in that second room, about nine feet back from the door, had been wired to its own transformer with nothing between the bell and the line.

He put an isolator in ahead of it and proved the bell dead with the transformer off.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A bell wired straight to a line is a bell that hums when you are not pressing it. Four of those bells in that row are on the line itself, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has blamed on the wiring before either of them moved in.**"

**Twenty-seven pounds is all that Saturday's four came to, exact.**

---

**That Saturday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on the shelf at the back where it has been all winter, and this page puts no figure on the page it keeps.**
