# Chapter 983 — Asked In Front Of A Queue

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road from about half past six, and a woman who made a form with five columns in it was asked about a sixth out loud in front of a queue, and about four people standing at that counter have said since that she had told him to ask her in front of them.**

---

That Monday took six callers through that shop and the last of them was let away at half past eight, and the shutter was an hour and a half after that.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and thirteen days, two hundred and fifty-nine weeks to the day. The printed form up on a wall in a passage in a fourth district was eighty-three days old that Monday and had not been straightened once.**

---

**Nobody at that counter said the name of the woman who has read a page on that Monday, and about four people who have stood at it will tell you that it was the first Monday since the one before it that she had said nothing about at all, and that nobody asked her, and that about four people noticed that nobody asked her.**

Marek said: nothing from her today.

The woman of about forty-three said: **you are not going to say that where the queue can hear it.**

Marek said: why not.

The woman of about forty-three said: **because it is about her and not about me, and the moment you say it in here it is a thing this shop is waiting for.**

---

**And then she came in off the street at about eight with her bag over her arm and did not sit down and put the bag on the floor by the till, and about four people at that counter will say that she had known which shop she was coming to and had not known what she was going to be asked.**

Marek said: I am going to ask you about the sixth column.

The woman of about forty-three said: **I know what you are going to ask. Ask it in front of them or do not ask it.**

Marek said: I am asking in front of them.

The woman of about forty-three said: **good. Then it cannot be taken back in a corridor and you will have to live in it.**

---

Marek said: what is in the sixth column.

The woman of about forty-three said: **I will do nothing about the sixth column. I said that once in a queue in this city about a month ago and I am saying it a second time in a queue on a Monday, and neither of those two is a decision and both of them are a fact.**

Marek said: you said it would be available in a month.

The woman of about forty-three said: **it is shut, and it stays shut until I open it, and I said a month because a month is a thing a person can say out loud in a corridor without explaining it.**

Marek said: then it was never a month.

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. It was a thing to say. About four months of my life have gone on that tray and not one of them has been for me.**

---

**She did not look round at the queue once in the whole of it and she had her hands flat on the counter the whole time, and about four people at that counter have it that the queue moved behind her and that neither of them filled the gap with anything.**

Marek said: when will you open it.

The woman of about forty-three said: **when there is somebody in front of it. I have not got a date for that and I am not going to invent one to keep a drawer shut.**

Marek said: and if nobody stands in front of it.

The woman of about forty-three said: **then it stays shut, and that is the correct outcome and not a failure, and you can put that in your own words and not in mine.**

Marek said: there is not one of us who can say what it says.

The woman of about forty-three said: **no, and I have had a form with five columns in it for years to learn that, and I am not going to be asked to read the other one out loud in a shop.**

---

**Nobody thanked that woman on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and she picked her bag up off the floor at about ten past eight and went out again, and she did not say what else she had come in for, and nobody asked her.**

---

**And the man of about thirty-three came in at about half past four and asked whether the woman of about forty-three had meant what he thought she had meant, and about four people at that counter have said since that he had not come in for a job at all.**

Marek said: she meant that she will open the drawer when somebody stands in front of the finding.

The man of about thirty-three said: **has anybody?**

Marek said: not yet.

The man of about thirty-three said: **I am asking her on Saturday.**

Marek said: **not on a Monday.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have worked out that. I have worked it out in about nine seconds standing on the corner and I am telling you so that you cannot say afterwards that nobody told me.**

Marek said: **and nobody did.**

**And then Marek said the thing that was said about that Monday by about four people who were in that shop and who agree that he said it to the counter and not to the man, and that it took him about nine seconds.**

Marek said: **I asked a woman in front of six people about a thing she had already said no to twice, and she said it a third time, and I did that because I wanted it in front of witnesses. It is not the same thing as asking her once in a corridor and it is worth a good deal less.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **it is worth less to her.**

Marek said: **it is worth less to her by about the whole of what I did it for, and I am telling you that because you will tell somebody else and I would rather it went out of here in my own words.**

---

**One shop, one yard and two rooms took the rest of that Monday, and four jobs went into them.**

A socket in that yard had been put in above a water butt on a piece of slate bedded in cement, so that the plate was bedded in cement as well.

He cut the slate out, bedded a box properly and proved the plate with a lamp.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**Anything bedded in cement is a thing you break to get at. Four rows in that street are like it plates in that row are bedded in something, and one of them has been an outside wall somebody has been afraid of leaning on for four years.**"

A switch in that shop had its box mounted on the plaster with a screw through the plaster only, so that the whole of it came away from the wall with the plate off.

He cut two fixings into the brick and made the box solid in the wall.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A box screwed to plaster is a box waiting to be a hole. Half of those are like it switches in that row are fixed to nothing but plaster, and one of them has been a counter somebody has hit with a tray for four years.**"

A light in one of those rooms had been wired into a lamp holder with the supply and the return both in the brass, so that there was nothing to earth.

He rewired it through the holder and proved it with a lamp and a tester.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A lamp with its earth in the brass is a lamp that cannot be made dead. Four of those fittings in that block are like it, and one of them is a room somebody has had a rug pulled out of since long before the street was paved.**"

An outside light in that yard had been left with its lamp out and its holder full of water.

He dried it, cleared it, proved it lit and put a cover on it.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp full of water is a lamp that has already gone and taken the fitting with it. Four of those covers in that row are cracked, and one of them has been a yard somebody has walked round in the dark and nobody has ever asked about it.**"

**Those four came to thirty-four pounds on that Monday, exact.**

---

**That Monday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder did not come down off the shelf at the back, and this page says no more about the page in it than any other page in this stretch of days does.**
