# Chapter 985 — Why She Keeps The Room

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six, then about nine minutes on foot to a first floor off a line in Saltmarket and back, and a man asked a woman there why she has kept that room for six years, and she answered it in one go.**

---

That Thursday took five callers through that shop and the fifth of them was let away about twenty to nine, and the shutter was down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and sixteen days, two hundred and fifty-nine weeks and three days. The request in the drawer in a second district was sixty-five days old on that Thursday.**

---

**He went across after the bench with a coil of wire in his bag that he did not need for about half an hour, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that he was not carrying it for the wire and that he wanted about half an hour with it in his hands.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: you have got that bag for a reason.

Marek said: I have got it because it is mine.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have got it because you have not got anything else to hold, and I have watched a man carry a bag about a shop for a fortnight now.**

---

**And then he asked her, and about four people who sit in that room will tell you that he asked it badly and on purpose, and that he did not sit down when he asked it.**

Marek said: why do you keep this room.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **six years and four weeks, and because a room nobody is asked anything in is the only kind I have ever been able to stay in.**

---

**She did not stop and she did not soften it, and about four people who sit in that room have said since that nobody at the far end of it said anything while she did it and that nobody in that room was going to get anything else out of her that day.**

Marek said: that is the answer to why and not to why now.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is the answer to both and you have had six years to notice there was no other one.**

Marek said: I am sorry.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **do not be sorry. Six years and four weeks and nobody has asked me that, and about four of them have told me what I ought to do with the room, and you are the first one who has asked me why I have got it.**

Marek said: what would you tell them.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that it is mine, and that they may sit in it or not, and that is the whole of what I have ever said in six years and it has been enough.**

---

**And then she said the thing about the Wednesday, and about four people who sit in that room have it that she said it while she was putting the chairs back and did not stop doing it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there is one on the Wednesday of next week. There is one every four weeks and I have not moved it once and I am not going to move it now.**

Marek said: about nine people will be in it.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **about nine people were in it last time and about nine of them were not told and about nine of them came, and that is not a coincidence I have to fix.**

Marek said: there will be a woman standing up in it.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have not been told that and I am not going to ask.**

Marek said: you knew it was coming.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have known since a Tuesday that somebody was going to have to, and I am not going to put a notice up about it and I am not going to put an extra sheet out.**

---

Marek said: what is behind your chair.

**And about four people who sit in that room have it that she put her hand on the back of her own chair instead of answering, and that the place behind it had been there the whole of that Thursday, and that nobody has ever set anything on it in six years.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no.**

Marek said: I am not going to ask again.

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you are the ninth person this year and you will be the tenth, and I have never once told anybody not to be sorry about it.**

---

**And then Talia was at that counter at about half past six with a bag of her own and nothing in it that belonged to her office, and about four people who come into that shop agree that she came in and sat down and did not take her coat off and did not say why.**

Marek said: you have come a long way for a shop.

Talia said: **I have come about twenty minutes and I am going to drink something and you are going to tell me about a Tuesday.**

Marek said: a Tuesday.

Talia said: **you have been carrying something since Tuesday and you have not mentioned it and I am not asking you to. I am asking whether you want me to know about it.**

---

**And Marek said no, and about four people who come into that shop have said since that he said it in about four seconds and that it was the shortest answer he has given anybody this month.**

Marek said: no.

Talia said: **that is the end of it.**

Marek said: there is a drawer in a building in a second district.

Talia said: **I know which drawer. I am not permitted to open it and you know I am not permitted to open it, and neither of those is why you said no.**

Marek said: **if I ask you, it is a request in writing, and it goes in a week, and it goes through a desk that is not yours.**

Talia said: **and if you do not ask me.**

Marek said: **then a woman opens a drawer when she decides to, and nobody in this city can say that I made her do it.**

---

**Talia put her cup down and did not pick it up again, and about four people who come into that shop will tell you that neither of them said anything else about it and that they talked about a bell for about ten minutes afterwards and that it was the only ten minutes of that week he had not spent on this.**

Talia said: **I am not going to ask you why. That is my job and you would have been offended if I had.**

Marek said: I would have.

Talia said: **then you have about nine seconds to change your mind and about nine seconds after that, and then I have to go and catch a bus.**

---

**One shop and one hall took what was left of that Thursday, about two hours of it, and four jobs went into them.**

A bell in that shop had been fitted on a door that opens outwards, so that the bell could not be seen from inside and the door could not be held open.

He took the bell off and re-hung it where a hand could reach it from inside.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A bell you cannot see is a bell you cannot answer. Two of those are like it are on outward doors in that row, and one of them has been a shop somebody has had to prop for four years.**"

A light in that hall had been put in on the emergency circuit, so that it came on with the alarm.

He took it off that way and put it on the house circuit and proved it.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A light on the alarm circuit is a light that tells you about a fault instead of about a room. That fault is four times over in that row fittings in that block are on it, and one of them has been a landing somebody has walked up in the dark for four years.**"

A socket in that shop had been put in on the underside of a counter with the earth taken off the metalwork of the counter.

She earthed it off the box and made the counter dead with a tester before she put it back up.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A counter is not an earth. Four of those sockets under counters in this city are on metalwork, and one of them has been a shop somebody has been careful over for four years.**"

A switch in that hall had a knob on it that had been replaced with one of the wrong pattern, so that it came off in the hand.

He fitted a knob that stayed on and proved the switch held.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A knob that comes off in your hand is a switch somebody has hit in the dark. Four of those knobs in that block are wrong, and one of them has been a doorway somebody has felt for in the dark since the shop changed hands.**"

**Those four came to thirty-seven pounds on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**That Thursday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on the shelf at the back of it, and this page prints no figure for the page that is in it and has printed none on any of the days before it.**
