# Chapter 982 — He Will Not Carry It Home For Her

**A Sunday was a bench until noon and a counter until two and then four converted units off that service road, and a man of about thirty-three came into that shop at about eleven and asked the same question twice, once at the counter and once at the door, and about four people in that shop have said since that he had not asked it twice anywhere else.**

---

That Sunday brought four callers into that shop and the last of them was away about a few minutes to twelve, and the shutter came down at two as it does on that day of the week.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and twelve days, two hundred and fifty-eight weeks and six days. The request in the drawer in a second district was sixty-one days old on that Sunday.**

---

**He came in at about eleven with his coat off and sat down on the bench by the door, which he had never once done, and about four people in that shop will tell you that he sat down before he said anything at all and that that was the whole of what he had come for, and that he was about a quarter of an hour early.**

Marek said: you have been in twice this week and I have not had anything out of either of them.

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have, and I have not said anything useful in either of them.**

Marek said: you asked for the sheet on Saturday.

The man of about thirty-three said: **I asked for the sheet on Saturday and you said the woman named it as one of three things, and I have been thinking about one of the three things since and not about the sheet.**

Marek said: which one.

The man of about thirty-three said: **the one where you do not ask her on a Monday what she has decided.**

---

**And Marek put both hands flat on that counter and about four people in that shop will say that he did not answer it for about nine seconds, and that he had said on the Saturday that he would not spend it, and that the man had waited him out.**

Marek said: it is not a kindness. It is her condition and I agreed to it.

The man of about thirty-three said: **it is three days in every seven that you do not go near it, and one Monday running is not three days in seven.**

Marek said: **it is one Monday running so far, and the reason it is one Monday running and not three days in seven is that she has said it twice and stopped at twice, and a woman who has said a thing twice has a number in her head and I am not going to be the third.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **ask her on a Tuesday, then. You have not asked her on a Tuesday.**

**Neither of them said anything for the length of a short count, and about four people in that shop have it that the whole of the silence was in him and not in the man and that neither of them counted it.**

Marek said: **she told me the decision is hers and not anybody else's, and if I go at her on a Tuesday because a Monday is shut then I am the man who went at her on a Tuesday, and she will know exactly what I did.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **she will know and she will say yes.**

Marek said: **she will say yes the way she says everything, in about nine words, and then it will be mine, and she has spent a year keeping her name off this.**

---

**And then he asked the thing he had come for, and about four people in that shop have said since that he asked it standing up and that he had not meant to stand up.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **what happens if I ask her and she says no.**

Marek said: then you will have asked her.

The man of about thirty-three said: **and that is worse than if I had not asked.**

Marek said: **yes. It is worse for you and it is better for her, and I am not going to sit here and tell a man his own mother is safer.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three said the sentence that made that Sunday the Sunday it was, and about four people in that shop agree that he said it to the door and not to Marek, and that the door was shut.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **will you carry it home for her.**

Marek said: **no, and I will not carry it home for you either, and you have asked me two things this morning and the answer to both of them is the same word.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **she cannot get there on her own. She has not got a car and she is not going to get one and she is the wrong age for a bus in the dark.**

Marek said: **then take her, and that is not me carrying it and it is not me refusing to.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **take her where.**

---

**And Marek said a thing about that room he had not said to anybody in this matter, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said it carefully and that he looked at the floor while he said it.**

Marek said: **the woman who keeps the room has one every four weeks and she has never told anybody in advance what it is for, because telling in advance would want a list.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **and there is no list.**

Marek said: **and there is no list, and every person in it is one nobody invited and nobody told, and your mother would walk into that room and nobody would know what she was.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **she would know what she was.**

Marek said: **she would know what she was and nobody else in that room would, and I do not know which of those two is the worse one to hand to a woman of sixty-two.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and he went out into the street at about half past twelve and stood on the pavement for about a minute and then walked off towards the tram, and Marek has said since that he did not offer to walk with him and would not have been allowed to, and that about four callers came through in the half hour after he had gone.**

---

**That Sunday's work went into one shop, one passage and one kitchen, about two hours of it and about nine jobs' worth of mess, and four jobs went in.**

A lamp in that kitchen had been put in with the flex running through a joint that had been filled and made good twice.

He opened the joint back up, found the flex buried in filler and drew it out and put it in a moulded box.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**Filler over a joint hides the one thing you would want to see. All four of those are the same job. Joints in that street have been made good twice, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has opened up twice for other reasons.**"

A socket in that passage, about nine feet in from the door, had been put in on a plate that was a different size from the box behind it.

He took the plate off, packed the gap and fitted one that sat square.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A plate that does not cover its own box is an open edge you can put a screwdriver in. Not one of those is unusual. Plates in that row are undersized, and one of them has been a passage somebody has walked along in the dark for four years.**"

An outside light on that shop had been wired on the far side of a meter cupboard, so that the switch did nothing to it.

He brought it back onto its own way and proved it off with the lamp out.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A light on the wrong side of a cupboard is a light somebody has been getting up a ladder for. Four of those switches in that row are on the far side, and one of them has been a yard somebody has not been able to leave alone and it has never once been looked at.**"

A freeholder in that flat had been put in on an unfused spur off a bigger circuit, so that the whole of it went on one unlabelled fuse.

He took the spur off, put it on its own way and labelled the fuse.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**Four sockets on somebody else's fuse is four sockets waiting for one fault to take the lot. Four of those spurs in that block are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen somebody has been afraid of switching off for four winters and one summer.**"

**Thirty-one pounds came to on that Sunday for those four, exact.**

---

**That Sunday the fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder was still on the shelf at the back of it, and nothing on this page is an offer about the page it holds.**
