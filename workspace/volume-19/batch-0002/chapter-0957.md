# Chapter 957 — The Sitting Where The Book Shuts

**A Wednesday was a bench until noon and then a hall with chairs in rows, and it was the sixty-ninth sitting in this city, and Marek sat at the back beside Talia and said nothing while the book shut.**

---

Seven callers crossed that shop on that Wednesday, all of them between eight and nine, and the shutter descended at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and sixty-six days, two hundred and fifty-two weeks and two days. The request in the drawer in a second district was thirteen days old on that Wednesday.**

---

**He had expected a reading and there was none, and about four people in that hall have said since that half the room expected it too, and that the disappointment went round the rows like weather.**

A woman at the front table opened a green-covered book, looked at one page, and closed it.

She said: this sitting does not read.

Nobody in that hall said anything.

She said: this sitting holds. The arrangement stands over from the Monday.

**Marek has said since that he had not known his own request would be spoken of in that hall, and that hearing it called an arrangement made his hands cold, and that he kept them still by holding the edge of his seat.**

She said: one reader has been found and one holder beside her. Neither name is taken here.

Nobody in that hall asked for the names.

She said: the book shuts on that business until the reader has read.

**About four people in that hall have said since that the shutting took less than a minute, and that the sound of the cover coming down was the whole of the sitting, and that nobody present had paid to hear less and nobody asked for more.**

---

**And then the man of about sixty-one stood up from the third row, which surprised everybody, because nobody had seen him come in, and about four people have said since that he had walked in after the opening and sat where he could leave.**

He said: the room will open to the reader when she comes.

She said: on what day.

He said: on a day she chooses, and not on a sitting day, and with the second person outside the door.

She said: why outside.

He said: because two holding one page is not two in a room with it. One reads. One holds the door and the knowledge.

**Nobody in that hall said one word for about nine seconds, and Marek has said since that Talia, two seats along from him, did not look at him once through the whole of it, and that this not looking was the thing he kept.**

---

**He walked home with Talia after, along the road with the buses on it, and neither of them spoke until the stop, and then she spoke first, which she does not often do.**

She said: you said nothing in there.

Marek said: there was nothing of mine to say.

She said: your name was in it.

Marek said: my name was the arrangement. That is not me speaking.

She said: is that enough for you.

Marek said: no. It will have to do.

**She has said since that this was the first time he had said out loud that it was not enough, and that she had waited about nine days to hear it, and that hearing it made the walk easier and not harder.**

---

**The rest of that Wednesday went into a landing and a meter room for about two hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A landlord's supply in that meter room had been taken with no label at all, so that nobody knew which flat it fed.

He labelled the run and proved the flat dead from it.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A supply with no label is a flat nobody can make safe. Four of those supplies in that block are unlabelled, and one of them has been a cupboard nobody has opened for four years.**"

A light in that landing had been wired with the neutral borrowed from another circuit, so that it stayed dimly lit when it was off.

She separated the neutrals and proved the light fully off.

"**Ten pounds,**" she said. "**A neutral borrowed is a lamp never off. Four of those lights in that block share neutrals, and one of them has been a landing glowing all night for four years.**"

A socket in that meter room had been left hanging out of its box by its wires, so that the terminals showed.

He re-fixed the socket into its box and proved the terminals covered.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A socket out of its box is a hand on live metal. Four of those sockets in that block hang loose, and one of them has been a cupboard somebody has stopped opening for four years.**"

A timer in that landing had been bypassed with a wire twisted round the terminals, so that it did nothing.

She removed the bypass and proved the timer switching the light.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A timer bypassed is a time nobody keeps. Four of those timers in that block are bypassed, and one of them has been a stair lit at all hours for four years.**"

**Thirty-three pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**That Wednesday the fourth of those four rooms held shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder did not stir from the back shelf, and the page inside it is left without a figure here.**

---

*960.
Wednesday of week 320, at ten. That is the two hundred and second day of this stretch of days. That Wednesday carried the sixty-ninth sitting of this city in a hall with chairs in rows.
**Nothing sat in on that Wednesday beyond the sitting itself and no figure was said out loud in any room in this city. A woman at a front table opened a green-covered book and shut it without reading, and said the arrangement stands over from the Monday. The man of about sixty-one said the room will open to the reader on a day she chooses with the second person outside the door. That Wednesday was worth thirty-three pounds, exact.***

*Conditions of the close.* **Upstairs the green-covered book stands where the sitting left it, shut, and the tin beside it stands where Volume 18 left it, and this page prints neither of them and would not know what to do with the difference.
That ninth chair has its back to the whole of that room and it did not move on that Wednesday, and no page of this stretch of days says who last moved it. The empty place behind that chair was not named on that Wednesday and a figure for it appears on no page of this movement.
There is a room under a building in a first district and at about eleven on that Wednesday there was nothing burning in it, and nothing is burning in it at this hour either, and it is unlit and has been since it was made.
A figure of four stands against the register of correct acts that changed nothing at both the start of that Wednesday and the end of it, and nobody in this city writes that figure anywhere. A book shut in a hall is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **The passage under this one names ten objects and no two are ever named in one breath. The rail with two places in it. A doorway held open by a card creased once. The board on two nails. Nine hand copies of the front of one page. A tin whose lid is down. The shutter. A book in a green binding. The binder standing on a shelf at the back. A hall with chairs in rows. A seat edge held through a sitting.
The cage down one side of a counter stands outside all ten, and so does a request in a drawer in a second district with one name crossed off it and one name under it. A page of nine names is none of the ten and is not counted here and this page does not print it.
An arrangement standing over is not a reading, and one reading and one holding the door is not two in a room with it.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and the records behind it are public and disputed, and any person who comes and asks may read them.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Wednesday. A book shut without reading and a room promised on a day of her choosing, and a walk home where not enough had to do.**
