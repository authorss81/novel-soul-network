# Chapter 954 — The Copy That Did Not Come Back

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a warm room, and a man of about thirty-three came in to say what a copy is, and the man of about sixty-one had already said it better on a Monday.**

---

That Thursday saw nine callers through that shop, the last one at twenty past nine, with the shutter dropping at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and sixty days, two hundred and fifty-one weeks and three days. The request in the drawer in a second district was nine days old on that Thursday.**

---

**He came in at twenty past seven with a folded copy of the form in his hand, and about four people in that room have said since that he held it like a man holding proof and that the proof was not in the paper.**

He said: I have been told the room does not chase paper.

Marek said: who told you that.

He said: everybody tells everybody everything in about nine days in this city.

**Nobody in that room laughed, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody laughed because it was true.**

He put the copy on the table. It was the sheet with nine rows and no names, and the fifth column empty on his copy, because his copy was old.

He said: I asked for one once. Four years ago.

Marek said: and it did not come back.

He said: I did not ask for it back. I gave it to a man who needed it for a week, and the week ended, and I did not go and knock.

Marek said: why not.

He said: because I had given it. A thing given for a week is given, and going to knock makes it lent, and I did not want it to have been lent.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and the woman of about forty-three has said since that she had never heard him say it plain before and that she had waited four years for the plain of it.**

---

**And then she said the thing from behind the table, and she said it to Marek and not to the man, and about four people in that room have said since that this was the turn of the evening.**

She said: that is why your request stays in the drawer.

Marek said: because I gave it.

She said: because you signed it. A paper with your name on it cannot be given. It can only be held against you or for you, and nobody in this room knows which yet.

Marek said: Sera Quill will hold it for me.

She said: she will hold the page. Nobody holds a signature for anybody. That stays where you put it.

**He has said since that he had not understood that until she said it, and that he had thought the signing was the price of the finding, and that it turned out the signing was the finding, and the page was something else.**

---

**He asked the man the question the Monday had left open, and the man answered it without looking up from the copy.**

Marek said: would you ask for your copy back now.

He said: no.

Marek said: even to get the nine told.

He said: even then. The nine are not my copy to spend.

Marek said: what would you do then.

He said: what you did. Sign something new and let the old thing lie.

**About four people in that room have said since that this was the first time anybody had said out loud that Marek had done a thing worth copying, and that nobody in the room marked it, and that this not marking it was the mark of it.**

---

**The rest of that Thursday went into a block and a court for about two hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A door entry panel in that block had been wired with the release on the same button as the light, so that the door buzzed whenever the stair light came on.

He separated the two and proved the door silent with the light on.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A door on a light is a door open all night. Four of those panels in that block are wired together, and one of them has been a door buzzing at midnight for four years.**"

A bulkhead light in that court had its gasket gone, so that water sat inside the lens.

She renewed the gasket and proved the lens dry.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A lens with no gasket is a light full of water. Four of those lights in that court are gone at the seal, and one of them has been a lamp hissing in the rain for four years.**"

A socket in that block had been wired with no slack at all, so that the face could not come off without pulling the wires out.

He cut back and re-terminated with slack and proved the face off and on.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A socket with no slack is a face nobody can take off. Four of those sockets in that block are tight to the box, and one of them has been a point somebody has left alone for four years.**"

A gate light in that court had been put in with the sensor facing the road, so that it burned every time a bus went past.

She turned the sensor into the court and proved the light still with the road busy.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A sensor on a road is a light on all night. Four of those lights in that court face the road, and one of them has been a yard lit till morning for four years.**"

**Thirty-six pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**That Thursday the fourth of those four rooms closed at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder at the back never moved from its shelf, and this page sets down nothing for the page it holds.**

*957.
Thursday of week 319, at ten. That is the one hundred and ninety-ninth day of this stretch of days. That Thursday brought nine callers into that shop and put nine names onto that day's book, the last of them at about twenty past nine.
**Nothing sat in on that Thursday and no figure was said out loud in any room in this city. A man of about thirty-three said he gave a copy away four years ago and did not knock when the week ended, and the woman of about forty-three said a signed paper cannot be given and can only be held against a man or for him. That Thursday was worth thirty-six pounds, exact.***

*Conditions of the close.* **Upstairs the green-covered book stands where Volume 18 left it and the tin beside it stands where Volume 18 left it, and this page prints neither of them and would not know what to do with the difference.
That ninth chair has its back to the whole of that room and it did not move on that Thursday, and no page of this stretch of days says who last moved it. The empty place behind that chair was not named on that Thursday and a figure for it appears on no page of this movement.
There is a room under a building in a first district and at about eleven on that Thursday there was nothing burning in it, and nothing is burning in it at this hour either, and it is unlit and has been since it was made.
A figure of four stands against the register of correct acts that changed nothing at both the start of that Thursday and the end of it, and nobody in this city writes that figure anywhere. An old copy nobody knocked for is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **The passage under this one names ten objects and no two are ever named in one breath. The rail with two places in it. A doorway held open by a card creased once. The board on two nails. Nine hand copies of the front of one page. A tin whose lid is down. The shutter. A book in a green binding. The binder standing on a shelf at the back. A copy of a sheet with an empty column. A door that buzzes with a light.
The cage down one side of a counter stands outside all ten, and so does a request in a drawer in a second district with one name crossed off it and one name under it. A page of nine names is none of the ten and is not counted here and this page does not print it.
A signed paper cannot be given and a lent copy cannot be asked for, and both of those were said out loud on one day.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and the records behind it are public and disputed, and any person who comes and asks may read them.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Thursday. A man laid an old copy on a table and a woman said a signature stays where it is put, and nobody marked the first time that was said.**
