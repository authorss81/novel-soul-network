# Chapter 956 — The Second Name

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a small room with nothing worth taking, and Sera Quill brought a woman of about forty-five into it and asked her to hold the other end of a page.**

---

Nine callers stepped through that shop on that Monday, the ninth of them at twenty-five past nine, and the shutter settled at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and sixty-four days, two hundred and fifty-two weeks to the day. The request in the drawer in a second district was eleven days old on that Monday.**

---

**The woman came at half past seven with her coat still buttoned, and about four people on that stair have said since that she looked at the empty shelf first, as though she were checking what the room held.**

She said: this is the room.

Sera Quill said: this is where I sleep. Nothing here is the office.

She said: and the page comes here.

Sera Quill said: the book does not come here. I go there, I read, I come back with the holding. The page itself never leaves its shelf.

She said: then what do I hold.

Sera Quill said: the knowledge that I have read it, and the decision about what is done with it.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and Marek, who had been asked to wait on the stair, has said since that he heard the quiet through the door and did not knock.**

She said: why me.

Sera Quill said: because you asked the cost before you said yes, and I did not know it then and I know it now.

She said: tell me the cost now.

Sera Quill said: you will know nine things you cannot write down, and you will be asked about them for the rest of your time in this city, and you will not answer.

She said: that is the whole of it.

Sera Quill said: that is the whole of it, and you will carry it with me and not instead of me.

---

**And then the woman said yes, and she said it standing, with her hand on the back of a chair, and about four people have said since that she said it the way she says everything, without moving her eyes.**

She said: I will hold it with you and I will not read it.

Sera Quill said: you will not be asked to read it. The holding is the second name and not the second reading.

She said: where do I sign.

Sera Quill said: nowhere yet. You sign when I have read, and what you sign is that you are holding.

She said: and him.

Sera Quill said: he stays out. He signed the asking and he does not sign the holding.

**About four people on that stair have said since that Marek heard that through the door and stood still, and that Talia, who was two steps below him, put her hand on the rail beside his and did not speak.**

---

**The rest of that Monday went into a flat and a close for about two hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A storage heater in that flat had been wired with no off-peak supply at all, so that it heated in the day at full price.

He corrected the supplies and proved the heater charging at night.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A heater with no night supply is a day heater at night money. Four of those heaters in that close are wired day only, and one of them has been a bill somebody has paid without asking for four years.**"

A light in that close had been left with the timer set to noon, so that it burned through the day and went off at dark.

She reset the timer to dusk and proved the light off at noon.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A timer set to noon is a day lamp and a dark close. Four of those timers in that close are set wrong, and one of them has been a stair lit at midday for four years.**"

A socket in that flat had been put in behind a cupboard, so that it could not be reached without moving the cupboard.

He moved the point to the side of the cupboard and proved it reachable.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A socket behind a cupboard is a socket in a wall nobody owns. Four of those points in that close are boxed behind furniture, and one of them has been a kettle plugged in blind for four years.**"

An extractor in that close had its duct crushed flat where it went through the wall, so that it moved nothing.

She opened the duct and re-formed it round and proved the air moving.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A duct crushed flat is a fan moving nothing. Four of those ducts in that close are crushed, and one of them has been a bathroom steamed shut for four years.**"

**Thirty-eight pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**That Monday the fourth of those four rooms lay shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder kept to its shelf at the back, and the page within it gets no figure on this page.**

---

*959.
Monday of week 320, at ten. That is the two hundred and first day of this stretch of days. That Monday brought nine callers into that shop and put nine names onto that day's book, the last of them at about twenty-five past nine.
**Nothing sat in on that Monday and no figure was said out loud in any room in this city. Sera Quill asked a woman of about forty-five to hold the other end of a page she will not read, and the woman said yes standing with her hand on a chair. Marek waited on the stair and was not brought in. That Monday was worth thirty-eight pounds, exact.***

*Conditions of the close.* **Upstairs the green-covered book stands where Volume 18 left it and the tin beside it stands where Volume 18 left it, and this page prints neither of them and would not know what to do with the difference.
That ninth chair has its back to the whole of that room and it did not move on that Monday, and no page of this stretch of days says who last moved it. The empty place behind that chair was not named on that Monday and a figure for it appears on no page of this movement.
There is a room under a building in a first district and at about eleven on that Monday there was nothing burning in it, and nothing is burning in it at this hour either, and it is unlit and has been since it was made.
A figure of four stands against the register of correct acts that changed nothing at both the start of that Monday and the end of it, and nobody in this city writes that figure anywhere. A yes to holding without reading is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **The passage under this one names ten objects and no two are ever named in one breath. The rail with two places in it. A doorway held open by a card creased once. The board on two nails. Nine hand copies of the front of one page. A tin whose lid is down. The shutter. A book in a green binding. The binder standing on a shelf at the back. A shelf with nothing worth taking. A chair with a hand on its back.
The cage down one side of a counter stands outside all ten, and so does a request in a drawer in a second district with one name crossed off it and one name under it. A page of nine names is none of the ten and is not counted here and this page does not print it.
Two people holding one page is not two people reading it, and the second name is not the second reading, and both of those were said in a small room.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and the records behind it are public and disputed, and any person who comes and asks may read them.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Monday. A woman said yes to holding without reading, and a man waited on a stair and was not brought in, and a hand stayed on a rail beside his without a word.**
