# Chapter 951 — The Room That Keeps The Book

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until half past five and then a bus of twenty minutes into a second district, and Marek carried a signed request in his coat and put it on a desk in a records room, and the man behind that desk did not pick it up.**

---

Nine callers moved through that shop on that Monday, the last of them at twenty-five past nine, and the shutter fell at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and fifty-seven days, two hundred and fifty-one weeks to the day. The request in the drawer in a second district was six days old on that Monday. The printed form on a wall in a fourth district was twenty-seven days old and had not moved.**

---

**He had rehearsed the sentence on the bus and it fell apart at the desk, and he knew it had fallen apart because he had led with the paper instead of the person.**

He said: I have brought the request back to you.

The man of about sixty-one looked at the folded sheet without touching it. The room behind him went up in shelves to the ceiling, and every shelf carried boxes with labels in his own hand, and the labels all faced out at the same angle.

He said: you signed it on the Wednesday.

Marek said: I did.

He said: and you want me to open the book for somebody.

Marek said: I want to know how the holding works.

**About four people who work in that building have said since that nobody had ever asked him that, and that men came in asking for copies and for pages and for names, and that no man had asked him about the holding.**

He said: sit down.

Marek sat on a chair with a file on it, and moved the file to the floor, and the man watched him do it and did not tell him where to put it.

He said: I hold the room and I let the shelf out. That is the whole of it.

Marek said: who can read it.

He said: not you. Not the woman of forty-three, because she made the sheet. Not me, because I have had it nine years.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and Marek has said since that he counted them by the clock on the wall and that the clock ticked loud enough to count by.**

Marek said: then who.

He said: somebody who will carry it and will not bring it back here.

Marek said: that is everybody and nobody.

He said: that is why nobody has done it.

---

**And then the man did a thing he has not done for any caller in four years, and about four people in that building have said since that they heard about it before the end of the day.**

He stood up and took down a ledger from a shelf at shoulder height and put it on the desk between them, and opened it at a page with a date at the top and a line drawn under the date.

He said: this is the last time somebody asked for a copy. One line. One date. One signature that is not mine.

Marek looked at the line and did not read the name on it, because the hand over it covered the name.

He said: did the copy come back.

He said: no.

Marek said: what did you do then.

He said: nothing. The room does not chase paper. The room waits until the paper is the only thing left in the drawer.

**Marek has said since that this was the sentence he carried home on the bus, and that he wrote it on the back of a job card and then crossed it out because he did not want to be a man who writes sentences down.**

---

**He asked the question he had come to ask, and he asked it standing up, with his coat already half on.**

Marek said: if Sera Quill reads it, what does she sign.

He said: nothing yet. She signs when she has read it, and what she signs is that she is holding it.

Marek said: and the second person.

He said: the same. Two people holding one page is not two people reading it.

Marek said: where does she read it.

He said: not here. This room has spent nine years learning that a name said in it stays in it.

**About four people in that building have said since that he walked Marek to the door himself, which he does not do, and that he stood at the door until the bus came, which he has never done.**

---

**The rest of that Monday went into a stair and a landing for about two hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A stair light in that block had been wired with the live taken off a landing socket, so that the light went off whenever the socket was worked.

He took the light off the socket and gave it a way of its own and proved it from the landing.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A stair light off a socket is a stair with no light in it. Four of those lights in that block are taken off sockets, and one of them has been a landing somebody has missed his footing on for four years.**"

A bell push at a door in that block had been fixed with the wires twisted and taped behind it, so that the bell rang when the door shut.

She remade the joint and boxed it and proved the bell with the door shut.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A bell that rings off a door is a door nobody knocks on. Four of those pushes in that block are twisted and taped, and one of them has been a flat somebody has stopped calling on for four years.**"

A socket in that landing had its face cracked across the middle, so that the switch sat loose in it.

He changed the face and proved the switch with a lamp.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A face cracked across is a switch held by nothing. Four of those faces in that landing are cracked, and one of them has been a socket somebody has stopped using for four years.**"

A handrail in that stair had come off its bracket at the turn, so that it moved when it was held.

He re-fixed the bracket and proved the rail with his own weight.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**A rail that moves is a stair nobody holds. Four of those rails in that block are loose, and one of them has been a turn somebody has gone down sideways for four years.**"

**Thirty-five pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**That Monday the fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder stayed on its shelf at the back, and no figure for the page within it is given here.**

*954.
Monday of week 319, at ten. That is the one hundred and ninety-sixth day of this stretch of days. That Monday brought nine callers into that shop and put nine names onto that day's book, the last of them at about twenty-five past nine.
**Nothing sat in on that Monday and no figure was said out loud in any room in this city. Marek carried a signed request on a bus into a second district and put it on a desk, and the man who holds that room showed him a ledger with one line in it where a copy went out and did not come back. He said the room does not chase paper, that the reader cannot be the man who asked, and that two people holding one page is not two people reading it. That Monday was worth thirty-five pounds, exact.***

*Conditions of the close.* **Upstairs the green-covered book stands where Volume 18 left it and the tin beside it stands where Volume 18 left it, and this page prints neither of them and would not know what to do with the difference.
That ninth chair has its back to the whole of that room and it did not move on that Monday, and no page of this stretch of days says who last moved it. The empty place behind that chair was not named on that Monday and a figure for it appears on no page of this movement.
There is a room under a building in a first district and at about eleven on that Monday there was nothing burning in it, and nothing is burning in it at this hour either, and it is unlit and has been since it was made.
A figure of four stands against the register of correct acts that changed nothing at both the start of that Monday and the end of it, and nobody in this city writes that figure anywhere. A ledger opened on a desk is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **The passage under this one names ten objects and no two are ever named in one breath. The rail with two places in it. A doorway held open by a card creased once. The board on two nails. Nine hand copies of the front of one page. A tin whose lid is down. The shutter. A book in a green binding. The binder standing on a shelf at the back. A ledger with one line in it. A bus that takes twenty minutes.
The cage down one side of a counter stands outside all ten, and so does a request in a drawer in a second district with one name crossed off it and one name under it. A page of nine names is none of the ten and is not counted here and this page does not print it.
A room that does not chase paper waits until the paper is the only thing left, and nobody in this city has said what that costs the man who waits.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and the records behind it are public and disputed, and any person who comes and asks may read them.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Monday. A man put a signed sheet on a desk and was shown one line where a copy went out, and he came home with one sentence and crossed it out.**
