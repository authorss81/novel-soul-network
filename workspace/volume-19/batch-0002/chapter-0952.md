# Chapter 952 — Four People In Her Head

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a first floor above a line, and Sera Quill sat at a table with four names she had not written down and asked Marek to tell her which of them could hold a page.**

---

Nine callers passed through that shop on that Tuesday, the last at twenty past nine, and the shutter was down by ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and fifty-eight days, two hundred and fifty-one weeks and one day. The request in the drawer in a second district was seven days old on that Tuesday.**

---

**She had the four of them in her head and she laid them out the way she lays out jobs, and Marek has said since that he had never watched her do that with people and that he did not like watching it.**

She said: one of them works nights and will not come in the day.

Marek said: then she cannot hold it with you.

She said: one of them has held a thing before and will tell everybody she held it.

Marek said: then she will make it hers by talking.

She said: one of them will say yes too fast and will mean it too little.

**Nobody at that table said anything for about nine seconds, and about four people in that room have said since that the quiet was her counting and not his.**

Marek said: and the fourth.

She said: the fourth will ask me what it costs before she says yes, and I do not know the answer.

Marek said: then ask her last.

She said: I am asking her first. A person who asks the cost is the only one of the four I trust with it.

---

**And then she told him what she had decided on the stair on the Thursday, and she told it to his face and not to the table.**

She said: I will not read it in that room and I will not read it alone.

Marek said: the man said not in his room either.

She said: then we need a room that has never held a name, and there is no such room, so we need a room that holds very little.

Marek said: where.

She said: my room. Four walls, one shelf, nothing on it worth taking.

**About four people in that room have said since that he shook his head before she had finished, and that she saw it and did not stop, and that both of them went on as though it had not happened.**

Marek said: your room is the ombud's room. That makes it an office.

She said: my room is where I sleep. The office is where I sit in the day. If you cannot tell those apart then nobody can hold anything.

---

**He asked her the question he had been told not to ask, and she answered it the way she answers everything, by giving him the work of it.**

Marek said: what do you want from me.

She said: nothing on the page and nothing after it. Stay out of the finding.

Marek said: for how long.

She said: until I tell you I have read it or I tell you I have not.

Marek said: and if you never tell me.

She said: then you carry that, because you signed for it.

**Nobody at that table said one word for about nine seconds, and she has said since that she watched him decide not to argue and that she was glad and sorry at once.**

---

**The rest of that Tuesday went into a kitchen and a hall for about two hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A cooker hood in that kitchen had been wired with no isolator at all, so that it could not be worked on without taking the whole kitchen off.

He put an isolator in above the units and proved the hood dead from it.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A hood with no isolator is a hood nobody can touch. Four of those hoods in that block have no means of working, and one of them has been a filter somebody has not cleaned for four years.**"

A light in that hall had been put in on a two-way with only one switch connected, so that it worked from one end and not the other.

She completed the strappers and proved the light from both ends.

"**Ten pounds,**" she said. "**A two-way with one end is a hall walked in the dark. Four of those lights in that block are half wired, and one of them has been a stair somebody has gone up blind for four years.**"

A socket in that kitchen had been spurred three times off one point, so that the whole wall went off when a kettle went on.

He cut the spurs back and gave the wall two ways and proved both with a kettle.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**Three spurs off one point is a kitchen on one fuse. Four of those walls in that block are spurred like that, and one of them has been a kettle somebody has stopped boiling for four years.**"

A fan in that hall had been boxed in behind a panel with no access, so that it hummed and could not be reached.

She cut an access hatch and cleaned the fan and proved it quiet.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A fan with no hatch is a hum nobody can stop. Four of those fans in that block are boxed in, and one of them has been a hum somebody has slept through for four years.**"

**Thirty-nine pounds is all those four came to on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**That Tuesday the fourth of those four rooms stayed shut from half past six with a woman of about thirty behind its door, and the binder never left the back shelf, and this page carries no figure for what is inside it.**

*Conditions of the close.* **Upstairs the green-covered book stands where Volume 18 left it and the tin beside it stands where Volume 18 left it, and this page prints neither of them and would not know what to do with the difference.

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **The passage under this one names ten objects and no two are ever named in one breath. The rail with two places in it. A doorway held open by a card creased once. The board on two nails. Nine hand copies of the front of one page. A tin whose lid is down. The shutter. A book in a green binding. The binder standing on a shelf at the back. A table in a first floor room. A shelf with nothing worth taking.
The cage down one side of a counter stands outside all ten, and so does a request in a drawer in a second district with one name crossed off it and one name under it. A page of nine names is none of the ten and is not counted here and this page does not print it.
Four people weighed and none asked is not a finding, and a room that holds very little is still a room that holds.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and the records behind it are public and disputed, and any person who comes and asks may read them.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Tuesday. A woman laid four people out without writing one name, and a man was told to stay out of the finding and said nothing back.**
