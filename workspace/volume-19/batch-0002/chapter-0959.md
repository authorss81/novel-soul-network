# Chapter 959 — The Sunday He Did Not Ask

**A Sunday was a bench until noon and a counter until two, and the shutter came down at two, and Marek walked Talia to the stop and did not ask her the thing he had carried for nine days.**

---

Five callers wandered through that shop on that Sunday, the last near half past one, and the shutter dropped at two.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and seventy days, two hundred and fifty-two weeks and six days. The request in the drawer in a second district was seventeen days old on that Sunday.**

---

**They walked because the bus was late, and about four people on that road have said since that the two of them walked apart at first and then together, and that the joining was not marked by either of them.**

She said: Sera Quill has her room ready.

Marek said: she has.

She said: and her second person.

Marek said: she has that too.

She said: and you are out of it.

Marek said: I am out of it.

**Nobody on that road said anything for a while after that, and he has said since that he had the sentence in his head the whole length of the road, and that the sentence began with her name and had nothing in it about the room, and that he did not say it.**

She said: you have something you are not saying.

Marek said: yes.

She said: is it about the nine.

Marek said: no.

She said: is it about me doing it instead.

Marek said: no. It is about you being the only other person who could have done it in nine days.

**She has said since that this was the sentence she had waited for without knowing she was waiting, and that she had decided on the stair on the Thursday not to look at him while he decided, and that on the road she looked at him and decided to go on looking.**

She said: and you did not ask.

Marek said: I did not ask.

She said: why not.

Marek said: because you are the person I love and the office you hold could get it in a week, and I did not want it got that way.

**Nobody on that road said one word for about nine seconds, and about four people on that road have said since that a bus went past without stopping and neither of them raised a hand.**

She said: that is the whole of it.

Marek said: that is the whole of it, and neither of us has said why till now.

She said: we have said it now.

---

**They stood at the stop until the next bus came, and she got on it and he did not, and about four people at that stop have said since that they did not touch and did not wave, and that this was not coldness.**

**He walked back alone and let himself into the warm room and sat at the table with the sheet on it, and the nine rows with no names, and the fifth column with nothing new in it, and he sat until the light went.**

---

**The rest of that Sunday went into a room and a yard for about an hour, and four jobs went into them.**

A ceiling rose in that room had been hung with the flex too long, so that the shade sat crooked.

She shortened the flex and proved the shade level.

"**Six pounds,**" she said. "**A flex too long is a shade hung crooked. Four of those roses in that block hang long, and one of them has been a room lit sideways for four years.**"

A gate in that yard had its hinges set so that it dragged on the ground.

He lifted the gate on its hinges and proved it swinging clear.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A gate that drags is a yard nobody shuts. Four of those gates in that yard drag, and one of them has been a yard left open for four years.**"

A switch in that room had been painted over so many times that it stuck.

She stripped the paint and proved the switch moving free.

"**Four pounds,**" she said. "**A switch painted over is a room that cannot be turned off. Four of those switches in that block are painted shut, and one of them has been a light left on for four years.**"

A tap in that yard had its washer gone, so that it dripped all night.

He renewed the washer and proved the tap shut.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A tap that drips is a yard awake all night. Four of those taps in that yard drip, and one of them has been a bucket filled by morning for four years.**"

**Twenty pounds is what those four came to on that Sunday, exact.**

---

**That Sunday the fourth of those four rooms did not open, and no page of this movement says what is or is not behind that door.**

Sunday of week 320, at two. That is the two hundred and fourth day of this stretch of days. That Sunday brought five callers into that shop and put five names onto that day's book, the last of them at about half past one.

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **The passage under this one names ten objects and no two are ever named in one breath. The rail with two places in it. A doorway held open by a card creased once. The board on two nails. Nine hand copies of the front of one page. A tin whose lid is down. The shutter. A book in a green binding. The binder standing on a shelf at the back. A bus stop with no bus. A sheet with nine rows and no names.
The cage down one side of a counter stands outside all ten, and so does a request in a drawer in a second district with one name crossed off it and one name under it. A page of nine names is none of the ten and is not counted here and this page does not print it.
He did not ask and she noticed he did not ask, and neither of them said why till the road, and that is the whole of milestone ten.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and the records behind it are public and disputed, and any person who comes and asks may read them.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Sunday. Two people walked to a stop and said the unasked thing out loud, and one got on the bus and one walked back and sat with a sheet till the light went.**
