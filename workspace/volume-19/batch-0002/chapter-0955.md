# Chapter 955 — The Notice On The Passage Wall

**A Saturday was a bench until noon and a counter until four and then a passage in a fourth district, and the woman of about forty-three stood under a form on a wall and asked for it to come down, and the man who put it there said no.**

---

Eight callers drifted through that shop on that Saturday, the last at half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and sixty-two days, two hundred and fifty-one weeks and five days. The printed form on a wall in a fourth district was thirty-two days old and had not moved.**

---

**She had come across the city with a bag of tools she did not use, and about four people in that passage have said since that she stood under the sheet for a long time before she spoke, and that the man let her stand.**

She said: it has done what it came to do.

He said: it has done nothing. Nobody has read it.

She said: that is what it came to do. Show that a notice with no names reaches nobody.

He said: then it should stay, so everybody can see that.

She said: a clinic passage is not a room for showing. People come past it frightened.

**Nobody in that passage said anything for about nine seconds, and Marek has said since that he heard the pipes in the wall while they stood, and that this was the only Saturday he can remember hearing them.**

Marek said: whose wall is it.

He said: the clinic's. Nine years I have kept that wall clear for one sheet at a time.

She said: and this sheet has had its month.

He said: it has had thirty-two days. A sheet gets its season.

---

**And then the man of about thirty-eight said the thing he has said twice before in this matter, and about four people in that passage have said since that he said it slower this time, as though he were charging himself for it.**

He said: I will not take down what I put up.

She said: I am asking you, not telling you.

He said: I know. Asking is worse. An order I could appeal.

She said: on what ground do you refuse.

He said: nine years of a clinic. People look at a wall while they wait, and a wall with nothing on it is a wait with nothing in it.

**Marek has said since that he had expected to be the one to decide this, because he signed the request, and that standing in the passage he understood he had no standing in it at all, and that this was a relief with a cost inside it.**

Marek said: leave it till the end of the month.

She said: no. A frightened passage is not a season.

He said: then you take it down, and it will be you who took it.

---

**Nobody in that passage moved toward the two drawing pins, and about four people have said since that all three of them looked at the pins and none of them put a hand out, and that this was the decision.**

She said: I will not take down what I did not put up.

He said: then it stays.

Marek said: it stays till somebody it is about asks for it.

**About four people in that passage have said since that the woman nodded at that before the man did, and that the man nodded after, and that the two nods were different and meant the same.**

---

**The rest of that Saturday went into a passage and two rooms for about two hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A light in that passage had been put in with the diffuser missing, so that the bare tube showed.

She fitted a diffuser and proved the passage lit without glare.

"**Eight pounds,**" she said. "**A bare tube in a passage is a corridor nobody looks down. Four of those lights in that clinic are missing their covers, and one of them has been a passage somebody has hurried through for four years.**"

A socket in one of those rooms had been wired with the live and neutral crossed, so that the switch broke the wrong side.

He corrected the polarity and proved the switch breaking the live.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A switch on the wrong side is a room switched and still live. Four of those sockets in that clinic are crossed, and one of them has been a bedside somebody has stopped touching for four years.**"

A heater in the other room had been fixed with no guard at all, so that a hand could go straight onto the hot face.

She fitted a guard and proved the face untouchable.

"**Twelve pounds,**" she said. "**A heater with no guard is a burn waiting for a sleeve. Four of those heaters in that clinic have no guards, and one of them has been a corner children are kept from for four years.**"

A pull cord in that passage had been tied up short, so that a person in a chair could not reach it.

He let the cord down to chair height and proved it from a chair.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A cord tied short is a bell nobody can ring. Four of those cords in that clinic are tied up, and one of them has been a night somebody has spent unheard for four years.**"

**Thirty-five pounds is what those four came to on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**That Saturday the empty place behind the chair of the woman of about forty-three was spoken of in that passage as the place where she stands when she reads a sheet, and no figure is printed for it on this page or on any page of this movement.**
