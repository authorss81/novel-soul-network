# Chapter 958 — A Room That Holds Very Little

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a small room with nothing worth taking, and Sera Quill moved two things in it and said this is where it will be read, and the man who owns the shelf said nothing because the shelf was not his.**

---

Nine callers filed through that shop on that Friday, the last at twenty past nine, and the shutter clicked down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and sixty-eight days, two hundred and fifty-two weeks and four days. The request in the drawer in a second district was fifteen days old on that Friday.**

---

**She had asked the man of about sixty-one to walk the room, and he had refused to come, and about four people in that building have said since that the refusal was polite and complete, and that he sent a folded note instead.**

The note said: *not my shelf, not my say.*

Sera Quill put the note on the empty shelf and left it there while she moved the table under the working light.

She said: he is done with it once it leaves his shelf. That is the arrangement.

The woman of about forty-five said: and when it goes back.

Sera Quill said: he takes it back the same day, by hand, and nobody follows him.

Marek said: who carries it here.

Sera Quill said: I do. Alone. On a bus that takes twenty minutes, with it inside my coat.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and Marek has said since that he had imagined carrying it himself and had not said so, and that her saying it made the imagining shameful.**

---

**And then she showed them the holding, and it was nothing, and about four people have said since that the nothing was the point and that they had expected a ritual and got a chair.**

She put a chair by the table for herself, and no chair for anybody else.

She said: she stands outside the door. She does not sit. She does not come in until I call.

The woman said: and if you do not call.

Sera Quill said: then you knock once, and if I do not answer you go home, and we do not speak of it the next day.

Marek said: for how long do you read.

Sera Quill said: until I have read it. Not long. A page is a page.

She said: and then.

Sera Quill said: then I put it back in my coat, I take it to him, and you and I hold what I saw between us and tell nobody, not even him.

**About four people have said since that Marek nodded at that before the woman did, and that his nod came too fast, and that Sera Quill saw it and waited until he stopped nodding before she went on.**

Sera Quill said: you will want to ask. Do not ask.

Marek said: I will not ask.

Sera Quill said: you say that now. Say it again on the day.

Marek said: I will not ask on the day either.

---

**The rest of that Friday went into a maisonette and a stair for about two hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A shower in that maisonette had been wired with no pull cord at all, so that it could only be worked from the landing switch.

He put a ceiling switch in the room and proved the shower off from it.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A shower with no cord is a room nobody can make safe wet. Four of those showers in that block have no local means, and one of them has been a landing switch somebody has run for wet-handed for four years.**"

A landing light in that stair had been put in with the fitting loose on its screws, so that it swung when the door shut.

She tightened the fitting and proved it still with the door shut.

"**Six pounds,**" she said. "**A fitting loose on its screws is a light that moves with the door. Four of those fittings in that stair hang loose, and one of them has been a landing somebody has stopped looking up at for four years.**"

A socket in that maisonette had been wired with the earth sleeving missing, so that bare earth showed in the box.

He sleeved the earths and proved the box with the face off.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**An earth unsleeved is a box nobody should open. Four of those boxes in that block show bare earth, and one of them has been a point somebody has left sparking for four years.**"

A fan in that stair had been left running with no grille at all, so that a finger could go straight into the blades.

She fitted a grille and proved the blades untouchable.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A fan with no grille is a stair with teeth in it. Four of those fans in that block have no guards, and one of them has been a humming somebody has kept children from for four years.**"

**Thirty-four pounds is what those four came to on that Friday, exact.**

---

**That Friday the fourth of those four rooms was fast at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder rested untouched on the back shelf, and no figure for its page is written on this page.**

*961.
Friday of week 320, at ten. That is the two hundred and third day of this stretch of days. That Friday brought nine callers into that shop and put nine names onto that day's book, the last of them at about twenty past nine.

*Conditions of the close.* **Upstairs the green-covered book stands where the sitting left it, shut, and the tin beside it stands where Volume 18 left it, and this page prints neither of them and would not know what to do with the difference.
That ninth chair has its back to the whole of that room and it did not move on that Friday, and no page of this stretch of days says who last moved it. The empty place behind that chair was not named on that Friday and a figure for it appears on no page of this movement.
There is a room under a building in a first district and at about eleven on that Friday there was nothing burning in it, and nothing is burning in it at this hour either, and it is unlit and has been since it was made.
A figure of four stands against the register of correct acts that changed nothing at both the start of that Friday and the end of it, and nobody in this city writes that figure anywhere. A folded note saying not my shelf is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **The passage under this one names ten objects and no two are ever named in one breath. The rail with two places in it. A doorway held open by a card creased once. The board on two nails. Nine hand copies of the front of one page. A tin whose lid is down. The shutter. A book in a green binding. The binder standing on a shelf at the back. A folded note on an empty shelf. A table under a working light.
The cage down one side of a counter stands outside all ten, and so does a request in a drawer in a second district with one name crossed off it and one name under it. A page of nine names is none of the ten and is not counted here and this page does not print it.
One reads and one holds the door and the knowledge, and the page itself never leaves its shelf except in a coat.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and the records behind it are public and disputed, and any person who comes and asks may read them.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Friday. A room was set with one chair and a promise not to ask was made twice, and the second time was for the day itself.**
