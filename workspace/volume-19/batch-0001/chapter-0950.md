# Chapter 950 — Somebody Else Has To Read It

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until about six and then a warm room and a stair and about nine people on a stair, and a man asked a woman he works for to read a page, and she said yes before he had finished the sentence and then told him what yes would cost him, and then told him what it would cost her.**

---

Nine callers came through that shop on that Thursday between about seven and twenty past nine, and that shutter came down at about ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and fifty-three days, two hundred and fifty weeks and three days. The request in the drawer in a second district was two days old on that Thursday. The printed form on a wall in a fourth district was twenty-three days old and had not moved.**

---

**He caught her on that stair and it was about four past eight and she was on her way down and she had been up there to look at a thing that is not this one.**

He said: I need somebody to read a page.

She said: whose.

**He said: mine. I cannot read it, because I asked for it, and the man who holds it will not read it out, and there is nobody else on that list except the woman of forty-three and she made the sheet.**

**About four people on that stair have said since that he said it in the wrong order, and that he put the request before the reason, and that he has thought about the order since.**

She said: no.

**He said nothing for about four seconds.**

She said: I said no to the page. I am going to say yes to reading it and I am not going to let you put my yes in the same breath as my no.

---

**Then she said yes, and she said it in the middle of that stair with her hand on the rail, and about four people on that stair have said since that they had been up and down that stair for years and had never heard her say yes to anything that fast.**

She said: yes, and I will not do it on a Thursday, and I will not do it in that room, and I will do it somewhere I have chosen.

He said: why not that room.

She said: because that room has spent four years learning that it cannot hold a person's name, and I am not going to open it in that room and put nine of them on a table.

---

**And then she asked him the only question she needed to ask, and she asked it before she had taken her coat off, and she has said since that she asked it in the wrong place and that she would have asked it better over a bench.**

She said: who asked for it.

He said: I did.

She said: whose hand wrote it.

He said: hers, to begin with, and it is crossed off.

**Nobody on that stair said anything for about nine seconds.**

She said: show me.

He gave her the docket and she read it standing up on the stair with the light over the landing that works and she turned it round and read it and turned it back and gave it to him.

She said: that is correct.

---

**And then she said the thing about the holding, and she said it to him and not to the stair, and about four people have said since that she said it fast.**

She said: when I open it I am holding it. Not the man in the second district. Me.

He said: yes.

She said: do you understand that I will have to decide what I do with nine names before I tell you whether I have read them.

**Nobody on that stair said one word.**

He said: no.

She said: no. So you will not know what is in it, and you will not be able to help, and you will find that very hard, and I have watched somebody find it very hard before.

---

**And Talia came down that stair at about four past eight and she had heard the whole of it from half a landing up, and she said one thing and then she went down and out and did not say anything else on her way.**

She said: if she reads it then it is hers and not yours, and if you go on asking her about it then you have made it yours again in the only way that was left.

**Nobody on that stair said one word back to her about that. She has said since that she had said it to the wall rather than to him and that she has been annoyed with herself about that for two days.**

---

**And Sera Quill said the last thing about it on that stair and it was about herself and not about him, and she has said since that she had thought about it for about nine seconds on the way up and not at all on the way down.**

She said: I am not doing it on my own.

He said: you want somebody else in the room.

She said: I want somebody else holding the other end of it. Two people holding one page is not two people reading it. It is one page with two names against it, and if I am alone in a room with nine names then the nine names and I are the same thing, and I have been the only one in a room too many times.

**He said: who.**

**And she said: I have not asked anybody yet.**

---

**And she has said since, to him and to nobody else, that she had four people in her head and that she had not asked any of them and that she would ask one of them within about nine days and that if she had not asked by the end of the month she would not read it at all, and that the man who signed it will find that out at the same time as everybody else.**

**Nobody in this city has been asked and nobody in this city has said yes and the page is in a room in a second district in a drawer with a name in it that is not one of the nine.**

---

**The rest of that Thursday went into a corridor and a yard, about two hours of it, and four jobs went into them.**

An emergency light in that corridor had been wired with a non-maintained fitting on a supply that is switched off at the isolator at night, so that it did nothing after about midnight.

She put a maintained fitting on and proved it on its own supply with the isolator open.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**An emergency light on a switched supply is a light that is off when it is wanted. About four of those fittings in that block are non-maintained or badly wired, and one of them has been a corridor that has been dark at the end of it for about four years.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in with an earth taken off a water pipe at a point where the pipe had been cut and rejoined.

He put a proper earth on and proved it.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**An earth on a water pipe is an earth on a pipe somebody can cut. About four of those sockets in that yard have their earth off a pipe, and one of them has been a saw that somebody has started in the wet for about four years.**"

A light switch in that corridor had been put in above a shelf about six feet up, so that a person had to reach over the shelf to operate it.

He moved it to the side of the frame and proved it from the floor.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A switch above a shelf is a switch a person does not use. About four of those switches in that corridor are up out of reach, and one of them has been a light that has been left on through the day for about four years.**"

A junction box in that yard had its lid off and a screwdriver across the earth bar.

He cleared it, put the lid on, sealed it and proved the box with the lid off.

"**Seven pounds,**" she said. "**A box with a screwdriver in it is a box somebody has been at. About four of those boxes in that yard are open, and one of them has been an earth bar that has been shorted by a coin for about four years.**"

**Thirty-four pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**That Thursday the fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come off it, and no figure for the page inside it appears on this page.**

---

*953.
Thursday of week 318, at ten. That is the one hundred and ninety-fifth day of this stretch of days. That Thursday took nine names for the nine callers who came through, and the ninth of them at about twenty past nine.
**Nothing sat in on that Thursday and that day and no figure came out of a mouth in this city. A man of twenty-two caught a woman he works for on a stair at four past eight and asked her to read a page, and she said no to the page and yes to reading it and told him she will not do it in that room. She read the request standing up on that stair and did not say the name on it out loud. She said that opening it makes her the person holding it, that she will have to decide what to do with nine names before she tells him whether she has read them, and that he will not be able to help. She said she will not do it on her own and that she has four people in her head and has not asked any of them. That Thursday was worth thirty-four pounds, exact.**

*Conditions of the close.* **Upstairs the green-covered book stands where Volume 18 left it and the tin beside it stands where Volume 18 left it, and neither of those two figures is printed on this page in any form.
That ninth chair has its back to the whole of that room and it did not move on that Thursday, and no page of this stretch of days says who last moved it. The empty place behind that chair was not named on that Thursday and it is printed without a figure on every page of this movement.
There is a room under a building in a first district and at about eleven on that Thursday there was nothing burning in it, and nothing is burning in it at this hour either, and it is unlit and has been since it was made.
A figure of four stands against the register of correct acts that changed nothing at both the start of that Thursday and the end of it, and it stays at four without anybody doing anything about it. A page agreed to be read by somebody who is not the man who asked for it is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **The passage under this one names ten objects and it names them in single lines, and no two are ever named in one breath. The rail with two places in it. A doorway held open by a card creased once. The board on two nails. Nine hand copies of the front of one page. A tin whose lid is down. The shutter. A book in a green binding. The binder standing on a shelf at the back. A printed form up on a wall by two drawing pins. A drawer in a second district.
The cage down one side of a counter stands outside all ten, and so does a narrow strip of paper with one word on it, and it is a strip and not a sheet and this page does not fold it into the ten. A page of nine names in a book is none of the ten and is not counted here and this page does not print it.
Two people holding one page is not two people reading it, and nobody in this city has yet been asked to hold the other end of it, and the man who asked for the page will find that out at the same time as everybody else.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and the records behind it are public and disputed, and nothing about asking for them is restricted.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Thursday. A man of twenty-two asked a woman on a stair to do a thing he cannot do himself and she said yes and then said what the yes would cost, and he has not asked her again and has not asked anybody else, and the ninth day from that Tuesday is seven days off and about four people in this city are counting it and are not saying so.**

---

**AND THE SIX THINGS A LATER PASS MUST CARRY FORWARD FROM THESE TEN.** **The date has fallen. Nobody in this city knew it was the date, and one of the nine came across a city on a Saturday to find out whether it was hers and did not find out.** A name was given out loud in that room and was not written down, and about nine people hold it and nothing else does. A woman of about fifty-one is in this matter and nobody in this city can put her name on paper without her permission, which she has not given. **A man of twenty-two has signed a request about nine people he cannot name, and it is the first document in this matter with a name on it, and he has been told the page will not come to him.** A man of about sixty-one holds a book in a second district and has not read the page out. **A woman of about forty-five will read it and will not do it alone and has not asked the second person.** The register stands at four with no fifth printed, the binder has not come down, the ninth chair did not move, and the room under the building in a first district was dark at about eleven on all ten days.**
