# Chapter 947 — A Sixth Column Is Offered

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six and then a warm room, and a man who keeps a register put a thing on the table that had been in his coat for about four years, and about nine people looked at it and one of them said the reason it would not work and then he said the reason it would work anyway.**

---

Nine callers came through that shop on that Monday, and the last of them was at the counter at about twenty-five past nine, and that shutter came down at about ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and fifty days, two hundred and fifty weeks to the day. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at one thousand seven hundred and fifty-four days, two hundred and fifty weeks and four days.**

---

**He brought it out of the inside pocket of a coat that has been to about nine funerals in that city and put it on the table face up, and about nine people in that room looked at it and nobody in that room had seen it before.**

It was a sheet of the same five columns with a sixth ruled at the right-hand end, and over the sixth he had written one word in pencil in capitals, and the word was *told*.

**He said: that is all of it. A column, and the day, and who put it there.**

He said: I have had that in my coat since the winter before and I have taken it out about nine times and put it back, and I have taken it out tonight because there is a date in that form now and a date with nothing beside it is worse than no date at all.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.**

---

**And then the woman of about forty-three said the reason it does not work, and she said it into his face and she said it once.**

She said: that column will say what we told and it will not say what they were told.

**Nobody in that room said one word back to her about that, and about four people in that room have said since that they had each understood it at once and had not liked it.**

He said: it will say the day.

She said: the day you told them, or the day it was?

**And that stopped the room for about nine seconds, and about four people in that room have said since that they thought she had said it to be sharp and that she had not, and that she had said it because she could not see any other way of saying it.**

She said: if nobody was told anything then that column is a column of dates on which somebody in this room wrote something down, and it will look in about four years like a record of nine people who were told, and it will not be one.

---

**And the man of about thirty-three said the sentence that made it happen, and he said it standing up, and he has said since that he had been working out how to say it for about nine seconds before he said it.**

He said: it is a column and not a person and a column is cheaper than a person.

**Nobody in that room said anything to that.**

He said: that is why it will happen. It does not matter what any of us wants tonight. In about four years there will be a column on that form because a column costs nothing to argue with and a person costs nine pounds an hour and everybody in this room knows that.

**About four people in that room have said since that it was the truest thing anybody said that night and that they each wanted it to be a lie and that it has not been a lie since.**

---

**Then the man of about fifty-two said the part he had come with, and he said it to the middle of that room and not to anybody in it, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it very quietly and that you could hear it.**

He said: and it needs a name at the bottom of it.

He said: not mine. I am not the one. A column with nobody's name under it is a column nobody has to answer for and that is how we got here.

**Nobody in that room said anything.**

He said: I will do it.

---

**And that is where it stopped, and it stopped because about four people in that room said nearly the same thing within about nine seconds of each other and none of them said it to him.**

One of them said: I would rather you did not.

He said: why.

She said: because you have been right in that room about four times and you have not been the person who did anything about any of them, and I do not want the first thing you do to be a column.

**Nobody in that room said one word back to him about that. He has said since that she was right and that he has not been able to get past it and that he has carried the piece of paper in his coat through nine weeks since without taking it out again.**

---

**So nothing was decided on that Monday, and the sheet went back into the coat, and about four people in that room have said since that the whole evening could have gone four other ways and that it went the one it went because nobody in it wanted to be the first person to hold anything.**

---

**And then by half past nine, when that room was emptying, Marek noticed it and it was on the copy of that form lying on the table and not on the original.**

In the right-hand margin, outside all six columns and inside the edge of the paper, somebody had written one word in pencil in a small hand.

It said *told*.

**It had been rubbed out. Not scratched out. Rubbed out with a thumb or with the side of a hand, and about half of it is still legible, and the paper at that point is worn and slightly shiny and there is more of one thing on that margin than there is of the other.**

**About four people in that room have said since that they each know who wrote it, and not one of them will say, and one of them has said that she will say if anybody asks her twice and that nobody has asked her twice.**

**Nobody in that room said one word about it. Nobody turned that form over. Nobody in this city has said who wrote that word and it is on no paper in this city except a form on a table.**

---

**The rest of that Monday went into a yard and a shop behind it for about two hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A socket in that shop had been put in above a sink on a way that also runs a machine two rooms away.

She gave the socket its own way and proved the machine with the sink off.

"**Twelve pounds,**" she said. "**A socket over a sink on somebody else's way is a socket that trips a machine. About four of those sockets in that row of shops are shared, and one of them has been a sink that stops a compressor in another room for about four years.**"

An isolator on a machine in that yard had been mounted in a position where it could not be reached without reaching over a moving part.

He moved it onto the wall, put a guard over the moving part and proved the isolator from where it now was.

"**Nineteen pounds,**" he said. "**An isolator you have to reach over something to reach is an isolator that will not be used. About four of those isolators in that yard are mounted badly, and one of them has been a machine that has been left running after somebody is frightened for about four years.**"

A light over a doorway had been wired through the conduit from the next room, so that it went on and off with a switch nobody could see.

He rewired it to its own switch and proved it.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A light through the wall from another room is a light with a ghost. About four of those fittings in that yard are done that way, and one of them has been a doorway that has been lit at three in the morning for about four years.**"

A board had a two-pole breaker on a lighting circuit, so that the whole lighting went off when it tripped.

She put a single pole on and proved both halves.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**Two poles on a lighting way is a breaker that takes the lights with it. About four of those boards in that yard are fitted that way, and one of them has been a shop that has worked in the dark because a machine would not start for about four years.**"

**Forty-eight pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**That Monday the fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come off it, and the page inside it is carried without a figure on this page.**

---

*950.
Monday of week 318, at ten. That is the one hundred and ninety-second day of this stretch of days. That Monday put nine names onto that day's book for nine callers, the last of them at about twenty-five past nine.
**Nothing sat in on that Monday and that day and not one figure was said aloud in any room here. A man of about fifty-two who keeps a register put a six-column version of that form on a table with the word told penciled over the sixth, and said it had been in his coat since the winter before. A woman of about forty-three said the column would say what the room told and not what anybody was told. A man of about thirty-three said a column is cheaper than a person and that is why it will happen. A man offered to be the name under the column and about four people said they would rather he did not. Nothing was decided. At about half past nine the word told was found written in pencil in the margin of a copy of that form and rubbed out, and nobody in that room said one word about it. That Monday was worth forty-eight pounds, exact.**

*Conditions of the close.* **Upstairs the green-covered book stands where Volume 18 left it and the tin beside it stands where Volume 18 left it, and this page prints neither of those two numbers and does not set one beside the other.
That ninth chair has its back to the whole of that room and it did not move on that Monday, and nobody in this stretch of days has written down who moves it. The empty place behind that chair was not named on that Monday and no page here prints any figure at all for it.
There is a room under a building in a first district and at about eleven on that Monday there was nothing burning in it, and nothing is burning in it at this hour either, and nothing has been burning in there since it was made.
A figure of four stands against the register of correct acts that changed nothing at both the start of that Monday and the end of it, and nobody in this city has been asked to hold the number. An offer made and refused is not on it and a column not printed is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **The passage under this one names ten objects and no clause below sets any two of them side by side. The rail with two places in it. A doorway held open by a card creased once. The board on two nails. Nine hand copies of the front of one page. A tin whose lid is down. The shutter. A book in a green binding. The binder standing on a shelf at the back. A narrow strip of paper with one word on it. A form with five columns and a margin with something in it that has been rubbed at.
The cage down one side of a counter stands outside all ten, and so does a sheet with six columns and one penciled word, folded once and back in a coat. A six-column sheet is a different sheet and this page does not put it in with the ten.
A word written in a margin and rubbed out is the only mark made on any paper in this matter this week, and about four people know whose hand it was and not one of them has said, and the paper it was rubbed out on has not been turned over by anybody.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this one, and the records behind it are public and disputed, and they can be read by anybody who asks at the door.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Monday. A man put a word over a sixth column and took it back into his coat, and a woman said it would say the wrong thing, and a third man said it would happen anyway, and nobody was appointed to anything and nobody has said since that that is the end of it.**
