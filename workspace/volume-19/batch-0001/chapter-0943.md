# Chapter 943 — A Question She Could Answer Without The Office

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six and then a first floor in a second district where a woman keeps an office she does not use on him tonight. He asked her one question and she answered it in one word and then spent about nine seconds telling him what the answer costs, and he did not ask her for the second thing.**

---

Nine callers came through that shop on that Wednesday, the first of them a little after seven and the ninth taken at ten to nine, and that shutter came down at about ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and forty-five days, two hundred and forty-nine weeks and two days. The post at the far end of that corridor had its face worn halfway up one thousand two hundred and ninety-three days, one hundred and eighty-four weeks and five days.**

---

**He went up those stairs at half past seven with a question he had written out on the back of a docket and he put the docket face down on the table in front of her, and she read it upside down without turning it round, which is a thing she does.**

The question was eleven words long and it was the only thing he had got out of four days of thinking about it.

**Has anybody been told about the date on that sheet.**

She said: no.

**Then she said: no, and not one of the nine, and not anybody standing next to one of them, and that is not an oversight and it is not anybody's fault, and it is the shape of the thing.**

Marek said: that is not what I asked either.

She said: it is what you asked. You have written *anybody*. That is the widest word you could have used and you used it because you wanted to be able to say yes and you cannot.

---

**He has said since that she was right about that word and that he had not noticed choosing it, and that he has gone back to it more since that Wednesday than he has gone back to most words in his life.**

Then she said the thing he had come for, and she said it before he got to it, which put him out of step for the rest of the evening.

She said: does it have to be told.

And then she said: yes.

**Nobody in that room said anything else for about nine seconds, and there was nobody else in that room, and she has said since that she counted them and that she was not counting for anybody.**

Then she told him what the answer costs, and she told it in a list and she read it off the same docket, upside down.

One: to tell nine people there are nine records of nine people.

Two: each of those records has to have made it, and by somebody, and that somebody has to be the one who did it.

Three: the day after that there is a person in this city holding nine names that were written down once and never asked about.

Four: and that person is you.

**She said the fourth one and then she put her thumb on it and left it there, and about a minute passed, and he has said since that it was the worst minute of that week and that he was not in a position to notice at the time.**

---

**And then he asked her for the thing. He got as far as the first two words of it and she stopped him with a hand flat on the docket.**

She said: I know what the second half of that is and I am not going to do it and I want to say why in one sentence before you finish asking.

He said: all right.

She said: because I could do it in about nine days and then it would be a finding of this office, and then it would be on this office, and this office can be asked what it did with nine people, and the only way it can answer that is to hand them back.

**Nobody in that room said anything about that. He has said since that he had not thought of the handing-back part at all and that he had only thought of the finding, and that this is the second time in his life she has shown him the second half of his own sentence and the first time she did it by putting a thumb on it.**

---

**So he said the thing he had gone up the stairs to say, which was the only true version of it left.**

He said: I am not going to ask you.

**She said: you did ask me. You got two words into it.**

He said: I am not going to ask you again.

She said: all right.

**And then, after a while, she said one more thing, and she said it looking at the docket and not at him, and about nine seconds passed before she said it.**

She said: you have come up here four times in two years and you have got two words out of me twice and one whole question out of me once and it is always the ones that cost you something.

**He said: I know what it costs.**

She said: no, you know that you would pay it, and that is not the same, and I am not going to be the person who finds out which of those two it is.

---

**He left that room at twenty to eight and went down those stairs and got the bus and was at a counter in a fourth district at half past eight, and the four jobs of that evening went into a shop and a flat over it.**

A socket in the flat over the shop had been put in on a spur off the lighting at the far side of the landing.

She gave it a way of its own and proved it with the lights off.

"**Twelve pounds,**" she said. "**A socket on a landing spur is a socket that lights up the stairs when the kettle goes. About four of those spurs in that building come off the lighting, and one of them has been a landing that has been lit all night by somebody waiting for a sound in another room for about four years.**"

An extractor in a small kitchen had its flex cut through by a cupboard hinge and taped at the cut.

He took the flex back to sound, fitted a proper gland and proved the fan.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A taped flex behind a hinge is a flex that has already failed once. About four of those flexes in that building are taped, and one of them has been a fan that has not run for about four years.**"

A light over a back door had been put in with the switch on the wrong side of the door frame, so that a person standing on the step could not find it.

He moved the switch and proved it from outside.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A switch on the wrong side of a door is a door somebody has to be let into. About four of those switches on that stair are on the wrong side, and one of them has been a back door that has stayed shut through a winter for about four years.**"

A board in the shop had a spur on it for the till and the freezer, so that the till stopped every time the freezer cycled.

She gave the till a way of its own and proved both.

"**Twelve pounds,**" she said. "**A till on the freezer is a shop that cannot take money when it is cold. About four of those boards in that row of shops are done that way, and one of them has been a counter that closes on a cold morning about four times a year.**"

**Thirty-seven pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**That Wednesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come off that shelf, and this page prints no figure for the page inside it.**
