# Chapter 948 — He Signs A Request

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six and then a bus of about twenty minutes to a records room in a second district that is a landlord and a filing system. A man of about sixty-one read it and said the last request he had was for a copy and the copy did not come back, and a man of twenty-two signed his name at the bottom.**

---

Nine callers came in on that Tuesday, and there was a gap in the middle of them where nobody came for about twenty minutes, and that shutter came down at about ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and fifty-one days, two hundred and fifty weeks and one day. The eighteenth of that board, second up from its foot, stood at one thousand four hundred and sixty-nine days, two hundred and nine weeks and six days.**

---

**The room is on the first floor of a building with three doors on that landing and it is a landlord and a filing system and nothing else, and the man who holds it has been there for nine years and has one chair at the desk and a second chair on the landing that is for anybody waiting and that nobody waits in.**

**He read the request standing up. He did not sit down and he did not ask Marek to sit down, and about four people who have dealt with him have said that he reads everything standing up because sitting down makes it a meeting.**

He said: this is a request in writing for one page of one book, and it has a date on it, and it says why you want it.

**He said the last part as a question even though it was not one, and about four people who have dealt with him have said that is how he tells you whether he has understood it.**

---

**And then he said the reason, and he said all of it in about nine sentences and he did not raise his voice once and did not look up from the paper until the end.**

He said: about four years ago there was a request for a copy of a page of a different book, in a different month, from a room in a fourth district.

He said: it came in on a sheet with a reason at the top of it and a name and a room, and it was a good request and I would have said yes if I had been asked in person.

**Marek said: and you said yes.**

He said: I said yes on a Tuesday and I copied the page and it went out in an envelope with the same reference on it as the request, and it has not come back.

He said: I wrote for it once, in this room, in this hand, on this paper, and I got an answer that was about the envelope.

---

**Nobody in that room said anything. Marek has said since that he was on the point of saying that it was not the same thing, and stopped, and that he worked out about nine seconds later that it was the same thing.**

He said: I have been at this desk for nine years and I have not let anybody past it who has not given me a reason I could say out loud in a room with other people in.

He said: and that is not because I do not trust people. It is because when I did trust the last one I have not got the paper back and I have not got a person back either.

---

**And then he turned the request round and put it on the desk and pushed it about a foot and did not let go of it.**

He said: that is signed.

Marek said: no, it is not.

He said: there is a name at the top of it in a hand that is not yours.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.**

Marek said: that is the room it came from.

He said: no it is not. I have been the holder of that room for nine years and I have never once written a name at the top of anything in this room, and if I do not know whose name it is then I am not sending a page to a person.

---

**So Marek read the thing at the top of it again and the woman of about forty-three had written her name there on the Monday because it was the room it came from, and he had carried it across a city that takes about twenty minutes and had not read the top of it.**

He took his own name off the top of it with a pen he had in his pocket for exactly that and had never once used.

**The man of about sixty-one watched him do it and said nothing at all while he did it, and about four people who have dealt with him have said that he has never watched anybody do that before.**

And then Marek wrote his own name underneath the first line, in the space that was for it, and he wrote the date, and he did it standing up.

---

**And the man of about sixty-one read it and said the sentence that has become the reason the next three movements of this volume exist.**

He said: you have asked me for a page about nine people you cannot name, in your own name, on a piece of paper that will sit in a drawer in this room.

He said: so you are the one who will be holding that it came out of here.

**Nobody in that room said anything.**

Marek said: yes.

He said: then it will not come to you. Not the page. Not now and not after and not when you have made up your mind about it.

---

**Marek has said since that he did not know, when he signed, that the page would not come to him, and that he would have signed it anyway, and that the two of those being true at the same time is the thing he has not got round.**

The man of about sixty-one said: I will have an answer in about nine days and you will not like it.

**He said the second part flatly and without any weight and about four people who have dealt with him have said that he has never once said anything in that room that he did not mean.**

---

**The rest of that Tuesday went into two rooms above a shop in a fourth district for about two hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A socket in the back room had been put in on a way that also runs the freezer in the front.

She gave it a way of its own and proved the freezer with the room off.

"**Ten pounds,**" she said. "**A back socket on the freezer way is a back room that trips a shop. About four of those sockets in that row are on a shared way, and one of them has been a back office that has been unusable in hot weather for about four years.**"

A fan in the front room had been fixed into a window with the cable laid across the sill in the way of the sash.

He took the cable out of the way and proved the fan on the new path.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A cable across a sash is a cable the sash closes on. About four of those fan leads in that building are run across a window, and one of them has been a fan that stops every time anybody shuts the window for about four years.**"

A light in the stair of that building had been put in with the fuse in the lampholder, so that the holder could not be taken off without cutting the supply.

He moved the fuse into a proper enclosure and proved the holder from the switch off.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A fuse in a holder is a holder you cannot unmake. About four of those holders in that stair are done that way, and one of them has been a bulb that has had to be changed with a pair of pliers for about four years.**"

A board in the front room had one spare way left on it and about nine sockets and one spur.

She found the spur, cut it out and proved the board full and empty.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" she said. "**One spur left on a full board is the thing that happens just before somebody puts in a bigger board. About four of those boards in that row have one spare way left, and one of them has been a shop that has been about to have a fire for about four years.**"

**Thirty-nine pounds is what those four came to on that Tuesday, and that is the whole of it.**

---

**That Tuesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come off it, and this page puts no figure to the page inside it.**
