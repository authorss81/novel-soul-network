# Chapter 941 — The Date Is Not An Agreement

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and after that four rooms off a service road with one of them warm. He had been carrying a question since the Wednesday before and he put it to the woman who holds that room at half past seven, and it turned out to be a question about a list that nobody in that building has ever kept.**

---

Nine callers came through that shop on that Monday between about seven and twenty-five past nine, and that shutter came down at about ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and forty-three days, two hundred and forty-nine weeks to the day. The space on that sheet marked for a date stood at one thousand four hundred and thirty-three days, two hundred and four weeks and five days. The man of about fifty-one had not moved off that north wall for one thousand three hundred and forty-nine days, one hundred and ninety-two weeks and five days.**

---

**He asked it into the face of the woman of about forty-three and he asked it badly, and he knew he was asking it badly while it was still going out of his mouth.**

"Who is it for."

That is what he said. Not *who has been told*. Not *has anybody*. He stood in the warm one with his gloves half off and asked a woman who has spent four years on a piece of paper who the paper is for, and about four people in that room heard him say it and about four of them have said since that they would have asked it the same way.

She said: I do not know.

**Nobody in that room said anything after that for a while, and she did not say it again, and she went back to what she had been doing, which was nothing, which was standing at the table.**

Then she said: there are four lines at the top of that form and one of them says no person on this form was asked anything.

**That is true, and he had read it on the way to work on the Tuesday, standing at a bus stop, and he had read it twice, and he had come in on the Monday with it in his head as a thing that made the form honest.**

He said: that is not what I asked.

She said: I know what you asked. I have been asked it every day since the Wednesday and I have not got anywhere with it, and if you have a way of getting anywhere with it I would like to hear it before the end of the week.

---

**And that is where he found it, and it took him about four seconds and it is the thing he has gone back to since.**

He had come in with the question *who has been told*, and it turned out he had not said that question. He had said a different one, an older one, and the older one is the one this whole business is built out of: **if a room wants to do something to nine people, the room must know who the nine are.** That is the rule. He believed in it. He has said it out loud in rooms he was not sure he should have been in.

**And the answer in front of him, standing at a table with a form on it, was that nobody in that building knows. Not will not. Does not. There is no list. There has never been a list. The sheet carries nine rows and no names because a name on that sheet is an answer nobody gave, and that reason is not a mistake somebody made, it is the only reason the sheet exists, and it was arrived by about four people over four years and it is correct.**

You cannot tell a person about a day that is about them if you do not know which person it is about.

He said that out loud. He said it to the middle of that room and not to anybody in it, and about four people in that room have said since that they heard it said to the middle of the room.

Nobody argued with it. Nobody took it up.

---

**About four people in that room have said since that they had each assumed, for about four years, that one of the nine was somebody they had spoken to at some point in that room. A woman of about sixty has said that she spoke to two of them in about four years. The man of about thirty-three has said he has stood next to one of them at that rail. Nobody in that room can name one of the nine. Not one. They were asked separately afterwards and all of them gave the same answer and two of them gave it in the same words, which were I know who I mean and I do not know who you mean.**

The woman of about forty-three said: I have had this sheet on my table since before the spring and I have never once been able to put a face to a row on it.

**She said that as a fact about herself and not as a complaint, and about four people in that room have said since that it is the sentence that made them sit down.**

---

**He asked one more question and it was the one that mattered and he asked it at ten past eight with his gloves back on.**

"Then how did nine rows get on that sheet."

Nobody in that room said anything to that either, and it stood in the air for about four seconds, and then the man of about thirty-three said: somebody wrote them down.

That is all he said. He said it in the tone of a man who has thought about a thing a great deal and has got as far as the first step.

**Nobody in that room asked him what he meant, and Marek did not ask him what he meant, and that was a decision and it was the first one he made that week and he has not been able to explain to himself why he made it.**

---

**The rest of that Monday went into the same building and a yard behind it for about two hours, and four jobs went in there.**

An extension lead had been run from a socket in a kitchen under a rug and pinned with a tack every foot, so that the whole of it came up with the hoover.

She took it off the tacks and put it on a trunking at the skirting and proved it.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A lead under a rug is a lead somebody finds with a tack in the morning. About four of those runs in that building are under a rug, and one of them has been a socket that has only worked while the vacuum is on.**"

A light in a common stair had its earth taken off the metal box, so that the box was live when the lamp holder came off it.

He put the earth back where the box was and proved it with the lamp holder off.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A box with the earth off is a box that is live when the fitting is off. About four of those boxes in that stair are done that way, and one of them has been a stair light that has made two people step back for about four years.**"

A lamp holder in a kitchen ceiling had been rewired with the switched leg going to the neutral and the live going straight through, so that the holder sat live whatever the switch was doing.

He took it out, put the switch on the live, and proved it from the floor with a lamp holder on a stick.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A holder wired straight through is a ceiling that is on when the wall says off. About four of those holders in that kitchen are done that way, and one of them has been a ceiling somebody has stopped switching on for about four years.**"

A board in a shop had two separate circuits going out on one way back to it, so that one of them was dead whenever the other was worked.

He split them onto two ways and proved both.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**Two circuits on one way is one circuit with a bad day. About four of those boards in that row of shops are done that way, and one of them has been a freezer that trips the office lights for about four years.**"

**Thirty-nine pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---
