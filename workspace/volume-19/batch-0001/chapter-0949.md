# Chapter 949 — A Man Who Will Not Read It Out

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six and then a warm room, and a man of about sixty-one came up those stairs with a book under his arm the morning after that Tuesday and put it on a table and said he was not going to read it out, and the reason he gave was correct.**

---

Nine callers came through that shop on that Wednesday, and the ninth of them was written in the book at about twenty-five past nine, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and fifty-two days, two hundred and fifty weeks and two days. The man of about fifty-one had not moved off that north wall for one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight days, one hundred and ninety-four weeks to the day.**

---

**He came up those stairs on the Wednesday rather than sending anybody and nobody in that room had asked him to come and about four people in that room have said since that a man who does not let anybody past that desk walking up a stair uninvited is not a thing anybody had a category for.**

He put the book on the table. It is about the size of a novel and the spine has been re-taped twice.

He said: that is the book. It is not out of the room and it will not be.

**Nobody in that room said anything.**

He said: I have found the page. I am not going to tell you what is on it.

---

**And Marek said the obvious thing, and the man of about sixty-one said the rest of it before he had finished, and about four people in that room have said since that they had not noticed that happening and that it went faster than anybody would have thought a thing of nine seconds could.**

Marek said: you have found it and you are not going to read it out.

He said: no.

Marek said: why not.

**And he said this, and he said it in the flat voice he reads everything in, and about nine people in that room heard it and about four of them have said since that it is the sentence they have gone back to.**

He said: because I am a landlord and a filing system. I hold it and I let it out and I do not decide what is said about it.

He said: if I read nine names out in this room then those nine names have been read out in this room, and it will be because of me, and in about four years somebody will ask me why I did it and I will not have anything to say that is not *they asked me to*.

---

**He turned the book round on the table and put his hand flat on the cover and left it there while he said the next part.**

He said: it has to be read by somebody. That is not me and it is not the woman of forty-three and it is not you.

Marek said: and it is not you.

**He said: no.**

Marek said: it is not you because you have said so.

He said: it is not me because of what I said to you yesterday and you did not hear it.

---

**Nobody in that room asked him to repeat it. Talia, who was in that room on that Wednesday about a rota again, asked him, and she said one word, which was please, and about four people in that room have said since that the word please was the reason he answered.**

He said: the request is signed with your name on it.

He said: a man who asks about nine people should not be the man who finds out who they are.

**Nobody in that room said one word about that for about nine seconds.**

He said: that is not a rule anybody wrote down. That is me, and I have had nine years to find somebody who does not want the paper back, and I have not found him, and I am not going to start now by handing it to the one man in this city who has already signed something about it.

---

**And Marek did not ask the next question. He has said since, to about four people separately, that he had it ready and that he had had it ready since at half past six, and that he did not ask it.**

**It was the question about her. He has said that he had a sentence in his head and the sentence began with her name and the room did not come into it and it stayed in his head, and that he has not written it down anywhere and that he is not going to describe it to anybody.**

---

**Talia was in that room and she has said since that she knew what the unasked question was because she is the only other person in this city who could get it done in about nine days, and that she did not look at him while he was deciding, and that she decided not to look at him on purpose, and that both of those were decisions and one of them was a rule she made for herself and the other one was not.**

**Nobody in that room has ever asked her whether she would have answered it if it had been asked. She has said that she is not going to be asked and that she has a reason and that the reason is not modesty.**

---

**And then the man of about sixty-one said the part about the holding, and he said it to Marek and not to the room, and he said it as though he were handing over a tool.**

He said: whoever reads it is holding it from the moment they open it.

He said: that is the actual arrangement and there is no way round it and you have signed up for the other half of it and the other half is that you will never know.

**Nobody in that room said one word back to him about that, and he did not stand there waiting for one.**

He said: I have carried it for nine years. You are about to carry it for about nine days and you will not like that either.

---

**He took the book off the table, put it under his arm and went down those stairs, and it took him about four minutes to go down a stair he has gone up and down about nine thousand times, and about four people in that room have said since that they watched him go and that none of them said anything while he was going.**

**The book went back to a second district in a coat, on a bus that takes about twenty minutes, and it did not come back on that Wednesday and it did not come back on the Thursday either.**

---

**The rest of that Wednesday went into a yard and a garage behind it, about two hours of it, and four jobs went into them.**

A roller door in that garage had its guide rails set about an inch out from the jamb on one side, so that the door came down out of square.

He packed the gap out with shim and re-set the guide and proved the door up and down three times.

"**Twenty-six pounds,**" he said. "**A roller door out of square is a door that will come down on somebody. About four of those doors in that row of garages are set out like that, and one of them has been a shutter that has been coming down crooked for about four years.**"

A light over a bench in that garage had no earth at all, so that a fault in it had nothing to go to.

She put an earth in at the fitting and proved the case from the floor.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A light over a bench with no earth is a bench somebody is standing on. About four of those fittings in that row are un-earthed, and one of them has been a bench that two men have been frightened of for about four years.**"

A socket in the office of that garage had been put in on a way that also runs the compressor, so that the compressor stopped whenever anything was plugged in.

He gave the socket a way of its own and ran the compressor with a kettle on the socket.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A socket on the compressor is a compressor that cannot be worked against. About four of those sockets in that row are shared with plant, and one of them has been an office that has run a heater with a machine off for about four years.**"

A hand lamp on a lead in that garage had been dropped and had been put back with the flex doubled inside it and taped.

He cut it out, ran a proper lead and proved the lamp.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A flex folded back inside its own sheath is a flex that is warm where it is folded. About four of those leads in that row have been dropped and put back, and one of them has been a lamp that has been too hot to hold for about four years.**"

**Fifty-six pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**
