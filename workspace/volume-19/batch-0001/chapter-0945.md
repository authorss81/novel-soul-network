# Chapter 945 — The Day The Sheet Was For

**A Saturday was a counter until about one and then a room that was open all day with a form on its table, and about seven people came and went, and one of them came in at four in the afternoon and gave the room something it had been built for four years not to have.**

---

Seven callers came through that shop on that Saturday between about nine and about one, and that shutter came down at about two.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and forty-eight days, two hundred and forty-nine weeks and five days. That space on the sheet marked for a date stood at one thousand four hundred and thirty-eight days, two hundred and five weeks and three days, and the date had been standing in it for sixteen days.**

---

**Nobody in that room knew that it was the day. That is the whole of the first half of that Saturday and it did not take anybody any effort to not know it.**

The form was on its own table at twenty-five past nine and it lay there with a date in a column on nine rows and nothing whatever in the other four columns of any of them. Nobody turned it over. Nobody put a hand on it. Seven people came into that room over the course of that day and every one of them did something else in it, and about four of them have said since that they had walked past the notice in the passage and read it and that they did not know at the time that it had been made in that room, and that if they had known they would have stood in the doorway instead of coming in.

**Nobody in that room was told anything that day by anybody, and nobody in that room asked to be told anything, and the two of those facts sat on top of each other all afternoon without anybody noticing.**

---

**She came in at about four in the afternoon and she was about fifty-one and she was not from that room and she had not been in it before, and she did not sit down, and she said her own name out loud into the middle of about nine people.**

"I am on that."

**Nobody in that room said anything. She gave the name and then she said where she had read it, which was a wall in a passage in a fourth district, and she said it as a fact and not as an accusation, and she has said since that she had rehearsed it on the bus and that on the bus it had sounded like a complaint and that in that room it came out as a question.**

She said: I want to know whether that day is mine.

---

**And then the room stopped, and it stopped properly, and nobody in it tried to fill the gap, and about four people in that room have said since that the silence lasted about nine seconds and that it was the longest one of that week.**

The woman of about forty-three put her hand flat on the table about a foot from the form and did not touch it and said the thing that is the whole of this volume, and she said it into that woman's face and not into the room.

She said: if I write your name on that it is a name on that, and a name on that is an answer you did not give.

**Nobody in that room said one word back to her about that.**

Then she said: and if I do not write it down then you have said a thing in this room and it is gone.

**And that is where the evening of the Wednesday before last comes from, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody had thought of the second half of it and that about four people had only ever thought of the first.**

---

**The man of about thirty-three said the only thing anybody said for about a minute and he said it to that woman and not to the room.**

He said: I have stood at that rail next to you.

**He did not finish it. Nobody finished it for him and he has said since that he had been going to say that he did not know and that he had not been able to get to it, and that the part he did get out is the only part of it that is true.**

That woman said: I know. I remember you.

**Nobody in that room has said since whether that was a comfort and nobody has asked her.**

---

**And Marek asked her one question, and it took him about nine seconds to get to it, and he has said since that he wrote three questions down on the back of his hand and crossed two of them out.**

He said: is the day yours.

She said: that is what I came to find out.

**He said: do you know.**

She said: no.

**And that is the whole of it. A woman came across a city on a Saturday to find out whether a day on a piece of paper was hers, and nobody in that room could tell her, and she had not been asked anything by the time she left, and she had not asked for anything either.**

**About four people in that room have said since that it was at half past six when she went, and that she said goodbye to nobody in particular, and that nobody tried to.**

---

**He has said one thing about that Saturday that he has said to about four people separately and not put in writing anywhere, and it is that this is the first time in four years that anybody has put a name anywhere near this matter and that it was not him and that he could not stop it.**

---

**The rest of that Saturday went into a yard and two flats above it for about three hours, and four jobs went into them.**

A light in one flat had been put in with the switch on the neutral, so that the fitting held a charge with the switch off and the bulb showed that it was on.

He put the switch on the live and proved it from the door frame.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A switch on the neutral is a light that tells you it is on when it is off. About four of those switches in that building are on the neutral, and one of them has been a landing light that has been left burning for about four years.**"

A cooker in the next flat had been fitted with a thirty amp fuse on a thirteen amp spur.

She put a thirteen amp fuse in and proved it.

"**Four pounds,**" she said. "**Thirty amps on a thirteen is a fuse that will let the cable cook. About four of those cookers in that row are protected that way, and one of them has been a ring that has gone out every time the oven came on for about four years.**"

A socket in a yard had been mounted on a piece of plywood screwed to a fence post, so that the whole of it moved when the post moved.

She took it off the plywood, put it on a proper box on the wall and proved it.

"**Eleven pounds,**" she said. "**A socket on a ply on a post is a socket with a joint in it that nobody can see. About four of those sockets in that yard are on timber, and one of them has been a saw that has stopped when somebody leaned on the fence for about four years.**"

A meter box in the passage had its lid off and its fuses exposed and no cover anywhere on the wall.

He made a cover, fitted it, sealed it and proved the box with the lid off.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A meter box with no lid is a box a child can put a screwdriver into. About four of those boxes in that passage have no lids, and one of them has been a live busbar that nobody has looked at for about four years.**"

**Thirty-two pounds is what those four came to on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**That Saturday the fourth of those four rooms was shut at twenty-five past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come off that shelf, and this page prints no figure for the page inside it.**
