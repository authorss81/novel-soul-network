# Chapter 946 — Nobody Wrote It Down

**A Sunday was a counter from about ten and then a room with the shutter down at about two, and five people were in it, and one of them had been there on the Saturday and came back and nobody had been told. He asked her one question and she said no in about four seconds and the room did not write it down and that was the harder of the two.**

---

Four callers came through that shop on that Sunday between about ten and about one, and that shutter came down at about two.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand seven hundred and forty-nine days, two hundred and forty-nine weeks and six days. Nine copies of the front of one page, each of them torn at a corner, stood at one thousand three hundred and fifteen days, one hundred and eighty-seven weeks and six days.**

---

**She came back at about one and nobody in that room had been told she was coming and there is nothing in that room by which anybody could have been told, and about four people in that room noticed her come in and about four of them have said since that they did not say anything to each other about it afterwards and that they have each wondered why.**

She sat down. She had not sat down on the Saturday.

Nobody in that room said good afternoon to her and nobody in that room said anything else about her being in it.

---

**Talia was in that room on that Sunday for the second time in a week and she was not there about any of it, and she has said since that she was there about a clinic rota and that she comes on Sundays because there is nowhere else to be.**

**She heard the whole of it. She has said since that she did not come in on the Saturday and that if she had, that Saturday would have gone differently and worse, and that she has not been able to decide since whether that is a reason to come in more often or less.**

---

**Marek asked the question at twenty past one and he had been in that room since at half past six with it in his pocket.**

He said: I am going to ask you a question and you are allowed to say no to it.

She said: go on.

He said: do you want your name written down.

**And she said no in about four seconds, and she said it before he had finished the last word, and nobody in that room breathed.**

Then she said: no, and not because of me, and I will tell you why if you want it.

He said: I want it.

She said: because you would have to have a place to put it, and you do not have a place to put it, and if you wrote it on that sheet then the only copy of it in this city would be on a sheet nobody has ever asked anybody about.

**Nobody in that room said one word about that.**

---

**And then the woman of about forty-three said the thing that made the room small, and she said it standing up, because she had got up without noticing that she was getting up.**

She said: we will not do it and we will not write it and we will not say afterwards that we did not know her name.

**Nobody in that room said anything.**

He said: then say her name.

She said: no.

He said: that is the same as saying you did not know it.

**She said: it is not, and I am not going to be the person who says her name out loud in this room again, and about four people in that room have said since that she was right and that nobody has been able to work out how to say that she was right without it being about her.**

---

**And Talia said the only thing anybody said to that woman all afternoon, and she said it sitting down and she said it to her and not to the room, and it took about four sentences and she has said since that she had drafted about nine of them in her head for two days.**

She said: I cannot tell you whether that day is yours and I am not going to pretend that anybody in this building can.

She said: you are allowed to go and look. Nobody in this room will stop you and nobody in this room will go with you.

She said: and I want to say one thing about the looking, and it is the only thing I know that is worth anything on a Sunday.

**Nobody in that room said anything.**

She said: looking is free and going on your own, and if you find out that it is yours then you will have found out and it will be yours and nobody will be able to take it off you, and if you find out that it is not yours then you will have found out and nothing will have happened to you at all.

**Nobody in that room said one word back to her about that either.**

---

**And then that woman asked the question that the afternoon was actually about, and she asked it into the middle of about five people and not to anybody in it, and about four of them have said since that they heard it and that none of them has been able to put it down since.**

She said: and what do I do with the day.

**Nobody in that room said one word.**

Nobody in that room said one word for the rest of that afternoon, and about four of them have said since that the room was not being difficult and that the room did not have it.

---

**He said one thing before she went and he said it into her face and it was about four seconds long and it was the only thing he said to her on either day.**

He said: I am going to go and find out and it will not be this week, and if I find out I am not going to be able to tell you first.

**She said: I know you will not be able to do that and that is not what I would want.**

**About four people in that room have said since that she was right about that and that nobody in that room knows what she would want, and that she has not said, and that she was not asked.**

The shutter came down at about two. She left before it came down and she said nothing to anybody on her way out.

---

**The rest of that Sunday went into a kitchen and a hall for about an hour, and four jobs went into them.**

A light over a kitchen door had been put in on the way that feeds the sockets, so that the whole of that way was behind one switch.

He took it off and put it on a way of its own and proved it.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A light on the socket way is a way you switch off at the wrong end. About four of those fittings in that block are on a socket way, and one of them has been a kitchen that has gone dark when the kettle went for about four years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had its flex passed through the hinge of the door and pinched every time it was opened.

He cut it back, refitted the gland and proved it with the door open and shut.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A flex through a door hinge is a flex that has been pinched for about four years and does not know it. About four of those flexes in that building are in a hinge, and one of them has been an oven that trips when the door is shut.**"

A bell in a hall had its push mounted outside the flat it belonged to, so that anybody in the flat rang somebody else's door.

He took the push off the wall and put it inside and proved it.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A push on the wrong side of a wall is a bell the next flat answers. About four of those fittings in that hall are outside the flat, and one of them has been a door that has gone off at about four in the morning for about four years.**"

A socket in a kitchen had a face on it that had been painted over two or three times with the paint built up round the pin holes.

She cut a new face, refitted it and proved it.

"**Four pounds,**" she said. "**A painted socket face is a socket that has to be forced. About four of those faces in that kitchen have been painted, and one of them has been a plug that a man has had to prise out with a knife for about four years.**"

**Twenty-two pounds is what those four came to on that Sunday, exact.**

---

**That Sunday the fourth of those four rooms was shut by half past one with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come off that shelf, and this page prints no figure for the page inside it.**
