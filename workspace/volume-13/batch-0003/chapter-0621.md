# Chapter 621 — A Thursday, Four Jobs Left Standing On A Bench Because Of An Hour Taken Out Of The Middle Of A Counter, A Man Of About Thirty-One Back At That Counter To Say Something Had Come To An Address Written In Pencil On The Back Of His Own Form, A First Floor Above A Shop That Does Not Open, Eleven Envelopes In A Plastic Box Under A Stair, A Stair Going Down From That Landing, And One Thing Taken Back Off A Shelf By A Person Who Had Waited

**A bench carried the morning of this Thursday and the counter took it from two, and after half past six four rooms off a service road took the rest. The shape of the day was an hour taken out of the middle of that counter, four jobs left standing, and a man of about thirty-one saying a sheet had come to an address written in pencil.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday and a date against every one of them, eleven people over that counter, the last of them let in at ten past five. **The heating in that flat went above its winter setting in the spring and has stayed above it, four names on that one tenancy are covering the difference out of four separate incomes, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and has not been told that anybody in this city knows.** Those rooms have been standing one thousand one hundred and nine days, which is a hundred and fifty-eight weeks and three days, and there are four of them with a fifth behind, and they are warm.

The card standing on end in the rail at the front of that counter is one thousand one hundred and thirteen days old, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks to the day, which puts it four days ahead of the rooms, and the rail takes two and has taken two for longer than the man who set it up can remember.

**The twelfth line on the two nails over that bench is one thousand and twenty-nine days and the post at the end of a corridor is six hundred and fifty-seven days, and one of the two is the oldest number in the shop and the other is a piece of cast iron in a passage in a building he has been inside twice, and neither of the two has anything to do with the other and neither of the two is a day on which anything happened to the heating in that flat, and the sentence that says so is the whole of what this entry has to say about either of them.**

---

**The man of about thirty-one came in at ten past three and he was not a customer and he had a chair leg with him in a bag.**

**"You wrote it,"** he said. **"On the back of my form. I didn't write it."**

**"I wrote it in pencil."**

**"You wrote it in pencil,"** he agreed, and put the bag down, and then said, **"Something came to it."**

Marek did not say anything for four seconds. **A thing arriving at a room named in pencil is not a small thing and it is not a large one either, and the reason he said nothing is that the two things he could have said were both nine seconds long and one of them would have been a promise.**

**"What came."**

**"An envelope. Window in it. Under the door, not through the post, because the post doesn't go up and hasn't gone up since I was at school."** He turned the bag over. **"And there's eleven of them in a plastic box under the stairs and the man that lets the room out says he hasn't opened one in four years and nobody has ever asked him to."**

---

**The room is a first floor above a shop that does not open, in a second district, and it is let out by the hour and the hourly is what a man of about fifty-eight asks for, in coins, at the top of the stairs, and he does not give change if you do not ask.**

The man had a chair leg in his hand and looked at it the way a person looks at a thing they are about to be told is somebody else's fault.

**"I've had that leg back twice,"** he said. **"It's a leg. It's got a bolt through it and the bolt's not mine."**

**"I remember the bolt."**

**"Then you know I can't do anything with it."**

Marek did the bolt in four minutes, because there was nothing on the bench and the counter was empty, and then he went up.

---

**The landing had a window at the end of it with no glass in the bottom third, and a mat, and a landing light on a push button, and a door with a number plate on it that did not match any of the four things the man of about thirty-one had said about it.**

The plastic box was under the stair where the stair turns, and it was a box that had been a box of something in a shop, and it had eleven envelopes in it standing up on their short edges, and none of them had been opened, and the flap of the box was down.

**"I keep it under the stair because the stair is dry,"** the man of about fifty-eight said. **"I've never taken one of them out. I want to be exact with you, because you'll be the second person to ask me that. I have never opened one and I have never been asked to and I have never wanted to, and I have been here every working day for nine years and about four hundred people have come up those stairs."**

**"And the stair."**

**"The stair goes down."**

**"Down to what."**

**"Down to a room with a stopcock in it,"** said the man of about fifty-eight, and he said it the way he had said *the bolt's not mine*. **"There's a door at the bottom and it's got a bar across it and the bar's on this side. I go down there when the water goes, which is not often, and the last time was in the spring and there was nothing in the room at all but the stopcock and a smell of hot dust. I don't go down there because there's nothing to go down there for."**

**"Is it locked."**

**"It's got a bar on it,"** said the man of about fifty-eight, **"which is a thing you do from this side, and I have never once been on the other side of it, and I am telling you that as a fact about me and not as a reason."**

---

**And there was a stair going down from that landing, and a door at the bottom of it with a bar across the inside, and Marek stood at the top of it for eleven seconds and did not go down, and the reason he did not go down was four jobs on a bench and a queue that had formed at the counter while he was on a landing, and the reason the queue had formed is that the woman of about thirty-four was doing the work and doing it faster than he does it.**

**What he wanted to do was go down. What he wanted to say about it was *can I look*, and he did not say it, and not saying it cost him the only two hours in that week when nothing else was in the way.**

He said instead: **"When do you shut."**

**"Six."** The man of about fifty-eight put the coins in a tin on the shelf. **"I shut at six, and I'm shutting this week on the Friday, and if there's a reason to go down that stair then that is the one evening of the week I've got, because I am here on my own and there is nobody to hear it."**

**"Right,"** said Marek, and did not say whether he would be there, and the man of about fifty-eight went back to his chair, and neither of them mentioned it again.

---

**He got as far as the mat and then stopped, because the question he actually had was about the envelopes and he asked that one instead of the one he had come for.**

**"Do you know what's in them."**

**"No,"** said the man of about fifty-eight, from the chair, without turning round. **"And I'm not going to find out, and that's not a policy. If I opened one I'd have to decide what to do with it, and I'm a man that lets a room out by the hour. I put the box under the stair because the stair is dry. That's the whole reason the box is there and it's the only reason, and you'd be surprised how often that's the answer to a thing somebody has been carrying for four years."**

**"Would I."**

**"You've been up nine minutes,"** said the man of about fifty-eight, **"and you came up here about a chair leg with a bolt in it that isn't mine, and you've asked me two questions and both of them were the ones you actually had. People come up here about a room and they ask me about the rent, and it's never once been about the rent."**

Marek went back down the stairs with nothing in his hands, which is how he came up.

---

**Back at the counter the four jobs were on the bench where he had left them, and the bench had been doing nothing for an hour, and a queue of four people had been served by one person.**

He did the first of the four. The second went on a shelf with a ticket. The third he did badly, because the third wanted doing now and he had been on a landing, and he could hear that he had done it badly, and the woman of about thirty-four said so once, in four seconds, at half past four, and not unkindly.

**The fourth of the four was a valve seat for a woman of about fifty-eight who runs a laundrette on a side street and who had been in the shop at ten past three and had waited until half past four, and who took it off the shelf at five and said she would come back on the Monday, and she will, and she will not need to.**

**That is the small thing he gave up on the Thursday and it is on no form and no column anywhere in this city: he told a man of about fifty-eight that he would be there on the Friday, by not saying that he would not.**

---

**At half past six, the four rooms off that service road.** The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall with a newspaper folded the way he folds it, and no written thing has been put in his hands in four years and nobody has asked him for one. The chair nearest the door had the woman of about thirty in it for the whole evening, and nobody addressed her, nobody put a question to her, nobody has ever said sorry to her for any part of any evening, and the page that carries her name at the head of it, the eighth of eight, sitting in a ring binder on a shelf in the back, was shut inside it all evening and the binder was not opened.

**The tally of things done properly that turned out to matter to nobody is still two, and this Thursday does not add a third, and the reason takes one sentence.** A man who stands at the top of a stair and does not go down it has not done an act at all, and the two that have been on that tally for four years are still the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, and an hour pulled out of the middle of a Thursday has not improved on either of them.

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, nobody thanked the man of about fifty-eight, and nobody was thanked on a landing with a plastic box under a stair. **The shutter came down at ten, and it took two goes because the second go was half a bar and he left it, and four people have told him since that a shop that leaves its shutter half down after ten is a shop that has had something.**

**What is left is a shape and a set of figures and they stand once.** The man of twenty-two can do, on this first day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week of this stretch, and a stair he did not go down is not a thing that has been added to him. The rail holds two cards and the newer of them stands at one thousand one hundred and thirteen days and has not been turned round. Two nails on a board carry nineteen lines and the twentieth has never been begun and nothing is ever added to that board. **Nine hand copies of the front of a page have been in progress for six hundred and seventy-five days, ninety-six weeks and three days, and eight of the nine are not finished and the first row anybody disagrees about has still not been reached, and on this Thursday not one of the nine was laid against another.** The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and fifty-seven days, ninety-three weeks and six days. The separation is four hundred and eighty-nine days, sixty-nine weeks and six days, ended in a one-line box in a form, walked on this day, and not shorter and not longer and not a thing anything has been put back into. That drawer is shut with four sheets in it and the fourth of them has still never been read. The four words that name the public body are on no page of this one. Four objects are named on this page and this page does not put any two of them next to each other: a sheet that came back to a room, a card standing in a rail, a form with one box left empty, and an iron plate with four slots cut into it.
