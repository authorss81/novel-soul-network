# Chapter 624 — A Wednesday, Seven People Down A Stair In A Second District One After Another Over About Two Hours, Two Printed Lines On A Card Read Aloud Seven Times And Explained Not Once, Nine Sheets In A Coat Lining From A Water Office With Nine Rooms On All Nine Of Them, A Woman Of About Forty-One Saying In About Four Seconds The Thing Nobody Else In That Cellar Reached Until She Said It, And Nobody Writing It Down

**The Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter from two until four and a half, four rooms off that service road from half past six, and its shape was two hours in a cellar in a second district with five people in it who had all come about a card and not one of whom had come about the same thing.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, every one of them dated, eleven people over that counter, the last of them at ten past five. **The heating in that flat is above its winter setting and has been since the spring, and the four people on that tenancy are still covering the difference without having discussed it, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and nobody has told her that the number is known.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and fifteen days, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and nineteen days, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The fourteenth line on those two nails is nine hundred and fifty-one days and the fifteenth is nine hundred and thirty, and the older of the two is always twenty-one days the longer of the younger, and the board has carried that difference every day since before there was a rail in this shop, and there is no reason for it that anybody has ever found and no reason is looked for.**

---

He had told seven people. That is the whole of what happened before the cellar and it took four days and it is on this page because the alternative is a man who put nine people in a cellar for no reason.

**He told them nothing beyond what a card says.** He did not tell them what to think of it and he did not tell them what he thought of it, and when the man of about fifty-eight from the first cellar said *you're not going to explain it* at half past two on the Wednesday, Marek said no, and that is the sentence the whole of the evening stands on.

**He also did not write it down. There were four people in that shop on the Wednesday afternoon who could have copied two lines off a card and written them down and handed them to him and not one of them did, and one of them has thought about it since.**

---

**A man of about thirty-four came in at three with a thing in a carrier bag and put it on the counter, and it was a bracket he had brought back from that shop a fortnight ago, and it had not broken, and he wanted to know what a person is supposed to do with a bracket that has not broken.**

**"It hasn't gone."**

**"It hasn't gone,"** the man agreed. **"That's the problem with it. I'm not saying that's your fault. I'm saying I've got a bag with a bracket in it and there's nowhere to put a bracket that's fine."**

Marek did not take the money. **The man put a five-pound note on the counter anyway and Marek put it back in his hand, and the man did not take it back, and it went into the tin at the end of the day and the tin is a tin for coins and four people in that shop saw the whole of it and two of them have mentioned it since.**

**That is the small thing he gave up on the Wednesday, and it is on no form: he could have taken eleven pounds for work that has already been paid for, and the day sheet has a column for it, and the column is not for this and the column is for goods returned to us, and there is no column for a bracket that has not broken and there is not going to be one.**

* * *

**At four and a half the woman of about thirty-four took the counter and Marek went to a second district with his hands in his pockets.**

**A man of about fifty-eight went down first, because it is his cellar and the light is on his meter. A young man of about twenty-four from the water office came at half past five with nine sheets folded inside the lining of his coat, and a woman of about forty-one from a bus depot came with him and did not sit down. A man of about fifty who keeps a gate in another district came at six. A woman of about forty-five who runs a kitchen in a back yard came last, at half past six, and stood at the top of the stair for eleven seconds and then came down, and nobody has ever asked her why she came.**

---

**She was the one who got there, and it took her four seconds, and nobody else in that cellar had got there at all.**

**The young man of about twenty-four put the nine sheets on top of the cabinet in two rows.** He said they had come into the water office over six weeks, and that he had put them in his coat because he had been told to bring them, and that he had not read them, and that he had read the top of one on the bus and had not finished it.

**On the top line of every one of the nine was a room. And on the second line of every one of the nine was the same room. And the nine rooms on nine sheets were the nine rooms on the card in the holder, in the order they were on the card, and the man of twenty-two had a copy of that order on the back of a job docket in a pocket and he read it twice against nine sheets of paper and said nothing.**

**"How many of those come in."**

**"About nine hundred a year across the whole city,"** said the woman of about forty-one. **"That is not a figure anybody has taken, that is a figure a man in a depot office said out loud once and I have carried it two years. It is nearer a thousand and a half. And nobody has ever counted them, and I have asked twice."**

---

**"It isn't nine rooms,"** she said, and she was not looking at the card and she was not looking at the man of twenty-two. **"It is one room with nine signs on it. A depot is not nine doors. A depot is one door and about nine hundred parcels, and if you put nine doors on the front of it you have not made nine depots, you have made one depot and you have told nine people to come to it, and every one of those nine people is now a reason for the other about eight hundred and ninety to be told that they are somewhere else."**

**Nobody said anything for four seconds.**

**"That's not what's written on the card,"** said the young man of about twenty-four.

**"No,"** said the woman of about forty-one. **"What's written on the card is nine ends. What is on that card and in that coat is about nine hundred. You've all been sat in this cellar for an hour reading a piece of paper nine and there are about nine hundred of the other thing, and the reason nobody caught it is that nine is the smallest number anybody says out loud."**

---

**And then the five of them disagreed, and it went on for about an hour, and not one of the five was more right than the other four.**

The man of about fifty-eight said it was a stopcock and a cabinet, that he had been in that cellar twice in eleven years for water, that nobody had ever told him there was a card in the door, and that if it had been important somebody would have said so. The man of about fifty at a gate said it was a list, that a list is a thing somebody wrote down, that somebody wrote it, and that the somebody has either moved or died. The young man of about twenty-four said it said maintenance on it and that maintenance means maintenance. The woman of about forty-one said one door and nine signs and would not be moved off it. **The woman of about forty-five said almost nothing, and what she did say was that a kitchen is not an end, and that if an end is a place a person is expected to be in at a set time then she is a person who is expected to be in a kitchen and a kitchen with a set time in it stops being a kitchen, and then eleven people are hungry at eleven at night instead of not, and she would not be moved off that either.**

**And at seven o'clock the woman of about forty-one asked the man of about fifty-eight a question that has nothing to do with the cabinet and that is the only thing any of them asked anybody all evening.**

**"Who put the nine in."**

**"You've just told me nine hundred."**

**"I told you a figure a man said once. I did not tell you who put nine rooms in a list, and that is a different question, and I have been carrying the other one two years and nobody has ever given me that one."**

**And nobody in that cellar knew, and the man of about fifty-eight said, out loud, that it was not him, and the man of about fifty at a gate said, out loud, that if he had written it he would have signed it, and the young man of about twenty-four said that nobody signs anything any more, and the woman of about forty-five said, from the bottom of the stair, that it would not matter, because the room does not check.**

---

**The two printed lines are on a card in the holder in the door of that cabinet, in a typeface that makes everything in the room look equally unremarkable, and he read them out loud seven times that evening, one for each person, and he explained them to nobody.**

> A maintenance socket may only be used while a link is held open.
> A link is held open while every listed room keeps its own end, and any room may release its end at any time.

**What he wanted, for two hours, was to be asked what it meant. He was never asked. Five people heard two printed lines and worked it out in their own heads at their own speed, and one of them thought the time was seven o'clock in the morning and one thought it was eleven at night and one thought it was whenever, and none of them asked the man standing by the socket with the cover up, and that is correct, and he asked them for nothing, and doing that is the one thing he has never once had to be told to do by anybody.**

**The cost of the Wednesday is that the sentence is not on anything.** It is in five heads and it will be in nine by Friday and in about a month there will be four versions of it in this city, all four of them true, and there is no form on which the true one goes, and the reason it is not on anything is that he wanted it to be more than he has ever wanted anything in four weeks and he did not ask, and he has not asked since.

---

**The service road after half past six, and four people came in for the heat, and the man who has been against that north wall since the spring was against it again with a newspaper folded the way he folds it. The chair nearest the door was taken at half past six by a woman of about thirty and held until ten, and in those three and a half hours not one person in that building said a word to her or asked her a question or has ever said sorry to her, and the page with her name at the top of it stayed in the ring binder on the back shelf, and the binder was not opened in her presence or out of it.**

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, nobody thanked anybody in that cellar, and the woman of about forty-five was not thanked for coming down the stair and was not asked to come up it again. **The shutter came down at ten, and the woman of about thirty-four had it down and squared before Marek got there, and the two of them stood on the wrong side of it for two minutes without saying anything, which is a thing they do about once a fortnight and did not do for the four weeks before the counter at all.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is closed at two and the Wednesday does not touch it, and the reason is that a man who reads two printed lines out loud seven times has done a thing, and the thing he did not do was the only thing anybody has ever wanted him to do, and those are not the same shape.**

**And the figures, once, standing.** Nothing in that cellar has been added to the man of twenty-two, and on this fourth day he can do what he could do on the first page of the first week; what has happened instead is that five people in this city now hold a sentence that is on no paper, and the number of people who held it on Monday was nil. The rail holds two, the newer of them one thousand one hundred and nineteen days old and unmoved. Two nails, nineteen lines, the twentieth never begun. The hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and eighty-one days, ninety-seven weeks and two days, eight of the nine still going, the first row nobody has yet disagreed about still not found, and nothing was laid against anything on this Wednesday. The cast-iron thing at the end of that passage is six hundred and sixty-three days, ninety-four weeks and five days. The separation is four hundred and ninety-five days, seventy weeks and five days, closed in a box in a form, and still being subtracted, and it is not a shorter number because five people read a card out loud. That drawer is shut with four sheets inside it and the fourth is still unread. The four words that name the public body appear in none of this. Six articles are spoken of in this file and the file does not introduce any two of them: paper that came back to a room, a card standing in a rail, a form with one box left empty, a job docket with nine places pencilled on the back of it, a fold of nine sheets kept in a coat lining, and an iron plate with four slots in it.

*Two printed lines, seven readings, and no explanation.* **Seven people were told one thing over four days and nothing else was said to any of them, and when the man of about fifty-eight said *you're not going to explain it*, the answer was no, and the whole of the evening stands on that. At about half past five a young man of about twenty-four from the water office came down with about nine sheets folded inside his coat lining which he had not read, and put them on the cabinet in two rows, and on the top line of each was a room, on the second line the same room, and the nine rooms were the nine rooms on the card, in the card's order, and the man of twenty-two had that order on the back of a job docket and read it twice against nine sheets and said nothing. Asked how many come in, a woman of about forty-one from a bus depot said about nine hundred a year across the city, that this is a figure a man in a depot office said once and that she has carried it about two years, that it is nearer a thousand and a half, and that nobody has ever counted them and she has asked twice. And then she said, in about four seconds and to nobody in particular, that a depot is not nine doors, that it is one door and about nine hundred parcels, that nine doors on the front makes one depot and nine people told to come to it, and that every one of those nine is then a reason for the other eight hundred and ninety to be told they are somewhere else, and that what is on the card is nine ends and what was in the coat is about nine hundred, and that the reason nobody in that cellar had caught it is that nine is the smallest number anybody says out loud.**

**The five then disagreed for about an hour and none of the five was more right than the other four: a stopcock and a cabinet and eleven years and nobody ever said; a list, and a list means somebody wrote it, and the somebody has moved or died; it says maintenance and maintenance means maintenance; one door and nine signs; and a kitchen is not an end, and an end is a set time, and a kitchen with a set time in it stops being a kitchen and eleven people are hungry at eleven at night instead of not. He read the two printed lines off the card in the door seven times, one to each person, in a typeface chosen so that nothing in the room looks like anything that matters, and explained them to nobody, because he was never asked and because he has spent four weeks refusing to be the man a room sends its things to and this was the first evening on which refusing turned out to be the same thing as being the right person. What he wanted for about two hours was to be asked what it meant. Nobody asked. The cost is that the sentence is now on nothing at all: five heads tonight, nine by Friday, and in about a month four true versions of it in this city and no form on which the true one goes, because he wanted it on paper more than he has wanted anything in four weeks and he did not ask and has not asked since. Nobody was thanked at that counter, in that cellar, at the top of a stair, or in four rooms off a service road.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **About five people in this city now hold a sentence that is on no paper, and the number who held it on Monday was nil, and that is a movement of people and not of him.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped, and an offer of explanation was made and refused by the person it was made to. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and a man who reads two printed lines out loud seven times has done a thing, and what he did not do is the only thing anybody wanted. Six articles get named in this file and not one of them is laid beside another: a sheet that came back, a card on end in a rail, a form with a box left blank, a docket with nine places pencilled on the reverse, nine sheets in a coat lining, and a plate of iron with four slots cut into it. That drawer is shut and the fourth of its four sheets is unread. Sixty-one lines in the book and seventy-three in the tin, and this Wednesday carried no sitting, and this entry does not say what a sitting does.
