# Chapter 623 — A Monday, A School On A Wide Road With A Trestle Table In The Hall And A Bell That Goes At Four And Stops Everybody For About Eleven Seconds, A Young Man Of About Twenty-Four From The Water Office Saying That It Is Nine Rooms, A Woman Of About Fifty-Three In A Coat Correcting That In About Nine Seconds, And A Box Of Parts Carried Up Two Flights At About Half Past Four By A Man With Dust On One Side Of His Hand

**The Monday was a bench until two and a counter from two until six, and its shape was about an hour at a school on a wide road in the middle of the afternoon, a bell at four that stopped nine people for eleven seconds, and a box taken up two flights by a man who had a band of dust on one side of his hand.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, each of them dated, eleven people over that counter, the last let in at ten past five. **The heating in that flat is above its winter's setting for a sixth month and the four names on that tenancy have not said to one another that they are the ones covering it, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and has not been asked about it by anybody.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and thirteen days, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and seventeen days, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is seven hundred and forty-six days and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is seven hundred and nineteen days, and one is always twenty-seven days the shorter than the other, and there is nobody in this city who can say why a person and a yard of crates should be twenty-seven days apart from one another, and there has never been anybody in this city able to say it.**

---

**He had told the woman of about thirty-four where he was going, at one, and she had asked him one question about it, and the question was not the one he had prepared for.**

**"You're going to ask somebody to stand somewhere."**

**"I'm going to ask somebody whether they'd stand somewhere."**

**"Those aren't the same and you know they aren't,"** she said, and served the next person, and did not ask him for the address of the school, and has not asked for it since.

The school is on a wide road and it is an old building with a hall at the back and nine people in that hall on the Monday afternoon for reasons that had nothing to do with any of this.

**A man of about forty-four had a lorry backed up to the side gate and a survey on a trestle table and a woman of about twenty-six who keeps the supply room and who let the two of them in without asking either of them for anything, because that is what a supply room is for.**

And there was a woman of about fifty-three in a coat standing at the far end of that hall by the radiators with her hands behind her back, looking at the floor the way a person looks at a floor they have been asked about.

---

**The young man of about twenty-four was from the water office and had been there about an hour before Marek got to the school, and he had come to ask the school a question, and the question was on a card of nine rows and two columns that he had seen on the Wednesday in a cellar four steps under a first floor.**

**"It's nine rooms,"** he said. **"That's all it is. Nine rooms and a list, and if somebody stands in each of them at the same time then whatever it is, works."**

**"How do you know they're rooms."**

**"Because it says places on the card."**

**"It says places,"** said Marek. **"Places is what a sheet of paper says as well."**

**"Right, but —"**

---

**And the woman of about fifty-three turned round from the radiators, and it took her two seconds to get all the way round, and she said one thing, and it took nine seconds, and she did not say it to Marek and she did not say it to the young man, she said it to the middle of the room, which is a thing people do when the sentence is not addressed to anybody.**

**"It isn't nine rooms. It's nine ends. A room is a place a person happens to be in, and an end is a place a person is expected to be in, and the two of them have been the same shape for four years and they are not the same thing, and if you build it on the rooms you will be standing on four of the nine and you will not have any way at all of knowing which four."**

**Nobody said anything for four seconds. A man of about thirty-eight with a tape came out of a doorway and stopped.**

**"How do you know that."** The young man from the water office said it in the way a young man says a thing when he has already worked out that he does not want to be the one who is wrong.

**"I put a clause about it into a working copy of a document four years ago,"** she said, **"in nine seconds, during an outage, on a morning in that year, because somebody had put the wrong word in and the wrong word was going to cost a building four hours. It was the wrong word and I changed it and I wrote my initials in the margin and I did not tell anybody, and it has been the right word every day since, and that is the whole of what I know about it and I have not looked at it since."**

---

**About four people agreed with her. About five did not, and one of the five said out loud that she had come to move a stopcock and that ends were a word for a cable, and she did not answer him and did not look at him and went back to the floor.**

**She was not thanked by anybody in that hall. Nobody thanked her for the nine seconds and nobody thanked her for that morning four years ago, and there is no form in this city on which a thing like that gets entered, and the man of twenty-two who was standing nine feet away with a dust band on the side of his right hand did not say it either, and did not decide not to say it, and had the whole of the rest of the afternoon to decide not to say it in and did not use it.**

**She is on no list. There is a list of the people who have been asked about the floor at that school, and there is a list of the contractors, and there is a list of four people who have been told the wrong word and are waiting for somebody to come back about it, and her name is on none of the three and she did not ask to be on one and nobody has offered.**

---

**What he wanted, and did not have: a second question for her. He had it ready by the time she got to the door and it was about the outage and it was about the four hours and it was the wrong question, because it was a question about her, and he has spent four weeks refusing to be the person rooms tell things to and he wanted for eleven seconds to be the person a woman of about fifty-three told things to, and she went out through the side gate and got into a car that was not hers.**

**At half past four the woman of about fifty-eight with the laundrette came in, and she stood at that counter for four minutes with her hands in her pockets, and she did not bring the valve seat, and she did not say why, and he did not say either, and two people in that shop noticed the whole of it and neither of them said anything, and she bought a washer she did not need.**

**The small thing he gave up, and it is on no form: he had said at that counter on the Friday that he would carry a box up two flights for a woman of about twenty-nine who collects on a Tuesday, and on the Monday he did not carry it, because he was at a school from one until two, and she carried it up herself in two goes and did not wait for him and did not mention it and has not mentioned it since, and he found out at half past four that it had been sitting in the passage since the Friday, which is a fact about a box and not about a person.**

---

**He asked the woman of about twenty-six, at half past three, standing by a rack of brooms, and it took him longer than nine seconds and she was kind about it.**

**"If it was a set time,"** she said, **"then somebody has to be here at that set time, and I'm the one who's here at that set time, and this is a supply room and a supply room with nobody in it is a cupboard, and a cupboard is what we had before the front door was changed."** She thought about it for two seconds more and then added, **"I'd want to know what the time is before I said yes, and not on the day."**

He said he would not come back on the day. He has not come back on the day and he has not come back at all, and that is a Thursday's worth of a thing and it is owed to a woman of about twenty-six in a building on a wide road.

**The bell went at four o'clock, and nine people in that hall stopped what they were doing for eleven seconds, and not one of them looked at another of them, and then all nine went back to what they were doing.**

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter and nobody was thanked in a hall on a wide road. **The shutter came down at ten on the Monday and it came down easily, because the wind on the Friday had dried whatever the Thursday's rain had put in the frame, and he noticed that, and the noticing is not on anything.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is two and this Monday does not move it, and the argument is that a woman of about fifty-three who corrects a word in a document is not doing an act either, because the word was already right by the time anybody asked, and a thing that was already right cannot be a thing that changed nothing because it never changed anything at all.** Both of the two are still the woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns and neither has been improved on by a bell.

**At half past six the four rooms off that service road.** The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall. The chair by the door had the woman of about thirty in it for the whole evening and she was not spoken to and not asked and not apologised to, and the page that is hers, the eighth of eight, in a ring binder, on a shelf at the back, did not come out and the binder was not opened.

**And what is left, once, at the end of it, is a set of numbers and a man standing in the same place he started in.** He is twenty-two and he can do at ten on this third day precisely what he could do at ten on the first day of this stretch, and a correction made in a school hall about an hour before is not an addition to him. The rail holds two and the newer of the two is one thousand one hundred and seventeen days old and has not been turned over. Two nails carry nineteen lines and the twentieth has never been started and nothing is ever going to be added to that board. The nine hand copies of a page front are six hundred and seventy-nine days, ninety-seven weeks to the day, eight of nine still going, the first row anyone disagrees about still not found, and nobody has laid a sheet against a sheet on this Monday. The cast-iron thing at the end of that corridor is six hundred and sixty-one days, ninety-four weeks and three days. The separation is four hundred and ninety-three days, seventy weeks and three days; it was closed in a one-line box in a form and it is still subtracted every day, and nothing has been put back into it. That drawer stays shut with its four sheets in it, one of them with the word NO on it and four lines under it, and the fourth of the four has never been looked at. The four words that name the public body are nowhere in this file. Five things are named on this page — a returned sheet, a card in a rail, a form with an empty box, a docket with nine places on the back of it, and a plate of iron with four slots — and no sentence here puts any two of them together.
