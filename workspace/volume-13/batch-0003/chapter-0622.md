# Chapter 622 — A Friday, A Counter Closed At Six By One Person While The Man Behind It Was Four Steps Down A Stair In A Second District, A Grey Steel Cabinet Bolted To A Wall With A Barrel Of Dust On Top Of It, A Socket With A Cover On A Hinge, Nine Wires Going Out Through A Wall Into Nine Other Buildings, A Card In A Holder With Nine Places Written On It Of Which He Knew Six, And A Landing Light Left On By A Man Who Could Not Reach The Button From The Far Side Of A Door

**The Friday was a bench until two and a counter from two, four rooms off that service road from half past six, and the middle hour of the counter spent in a cellar in a second district that has a stopcock in it and a cabinet that has nothing to do with the stopcock.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday, all of them dated, eleven people over the counter, the last of them at ten past five. **The heat in that flat is above its winter setting for a sixth month and will be above it in a seventh, the four names on that tenancy are paying the difference between the two settings out of four incomes, and a cook is a fortnight of her own money in the hole and has still not been told that the arithmetic on it is known.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and ten days, a hundred and fifty-eight weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and fourteen days, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and fifty-eight days, ninety-four weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is seven hundred and sixteen days, a hundred and two weeks and two days. One of the two is a piece of cast iron at the end of a passage in a building he has been inside twice, and the other is a person, and the only thing the two of them have in common is that neither of them is a day on which anything happened to the heating in this city.**

---

He went down at half past four and the man of about fifty-eight was in the doorway with a cup in his hand and did not come down, and was not asked to, and the not-asking is on this page because it is the reason nobody else in this city knows that the stair has been gone.

**"That's a stopcock, that's all that's down there."**

**"Right."**

**"I'll leave the light."**

**"I can't reach it."** Marek said that from the bottom, and it was true. **"The button's on the landing side of the door and there's no button down here, and there's no light down here either except the one on that stair, and I'm not going to touch the stopcock to find out whether it wants touching."**

**"All right,"** said the man of about fifty-eight, and drank his tea, and let him go down.

---

**The room at the bottom is a cellar with a low head on it and a floor of poured concrete that somebody sealed eleven years ago and that has been dry ever since, and the stopcock is on the left-hand wall and is not what is in the room.**

What is in the room is a steel cabinet about as tall as a man, bolted to the brick at the back, painted the grey that institutions paint things that are not allowed to look new.

**There was dust on the top of it. There was a great deal of dust, and it had been there a long time, and it had not been disturbed, except in one place: a rectangle about the size of a spread hand, four inches down the front edge, where somebody had put a hand and steadied themselves about a thousand times without ever once cleaning afterwards.**

---

**The door of the cabinet has a card in a holder at eye height, and the card is not the label of the cabinet, because a label tells you what a thing is and this one tells you how to use it.**

Behind the door, on the left, there is a socket with a brass rim and a cover on a hinge, and the cover is up, and inside the socket there is nothing that anybody in this city would recognise as a thing that does anything. **What came out of the wall beside it was a run of nine wires the thickness of a pencil lead, bound together with a lace, going up and into a hole in the brick, and on the wall by the hole there was a plate with nine holes punched in it and nine of the nine had wire going into them, and each wire went somewhere else and nowhere in that cellar showed where.**

**And on the right-hand wall, low down, there was a second card in a second holder, and this one had a grid on it, and the grid had nine rows and two columns, and one of the two columns was headed with a word he had seen on about four hundred forms in his life and the other column was headed with a word he had never seen on anything.**

---

**The nine places on the first card were on nine lines and he had seen six of them.**

A school on a wide road. The water office. A bus depot with a gate onto a yard. A council house in a street with a row of trees on one side. A kitchen in a back yard. The room above the line in Saltmarket where nine people had thrown nothing out in about nineteen years.

**The other three were a school in a town, a store with a cold room in it, and a building with no name on the door in a town he had not been to, and none of the three was one he had heard of, and he stood there and found that he did not think that was interesting, and it was.**

---

**The card in the holder in the door says what the socket is for, and it says it in two lines, and the two lines are the whole of what is written in that cellar, and he read them four times and then read them a fifth time standing up, with his hands at his sides, because there was nowhere to sit.**

He read them out loud twice, in a cellar with his mouth a foot from the print, and nobody heard him, and there is nobody in this city he could have read them to at half past four on a Friday, and that is the second cost of the Friday and he did not know it was a cost until the Tuesday.

**What it does, he could not work out standing up. Nine wires going out of this wall into nine other buildings, a socket with nothing in it, a grid with nine empty rows and a column he had never seen the heading to, and a date four years old in a hand he did not know. He went up the stair with the question still open and he is not a man who can leave a question open, and the answer was on the inside of that door and he had read straight past it twice, because it was in the same typeface as everything else in that cellar, which is a typeface chosen so that nothing in the room looks like anything that matters.**

**And the grid on the second card has a column for it, with nine empty rows, and a line under the last one, and on the line under the last one somebody has written a date in pencil in a hand he did not recognise, and the date is four years old.**

---

**He wrote the nine places on the back of a job docket in pencil, in his own hand, at five past five, sitting on the step with his back against the bar, and he did it in nine seconds and he did not do it well.** Six of the nine from memory, badly, and the three that were not his from the card itself, and the postcodes of the three of them copied in a hand that leaned. **It went into the fourth pocket of a tool roll beside four pieces of paper that are not his either, and that pocket has been a place where things he did not write go, and it is not a list and it is not going to become one.**

**He wanted the man of about fifty-eight to come down and look at it. He did not ask, and the man did not come, and the reason he did not ask is that a man who lets a room out by the hour is not a man you ask to look at a cabinet in a cellar, and asking him would have been asking him to be a second person who knows, and there is a limit to how many of those a thing can have before it stops being what it is.**

---

**He put his hand flat on the top of that cabinet, once, in the place where the dust is not.**

He did it without deciding to and he took his hand straight back off, and he understood four seconds later that he had just done the thing he had spent four weeks telling people he would not do, which is to touch a thing in a room to find out whether it is real. **The dust on the top of it came off onto the side of his hand in a grey band about the width of two fingers, and it did that on his skin, and the room was dry, and there was a stool he could have wiped it on and did not, so there is a grey band on the side of his right hand for two days afterwards and the woman of about thirty-four sees it at ten past five and says nothing about it, which is the single most conspicuous act of not mentioning anything in four weeks of standing next to that counter.**

---

**Back up at ten past five, and the woman of about thirty-four was at the counter with the day sheet squared up and the till counted and one caller still standing there with a bag on the floor.**

**"You're four minutes late shutting."**

**"Fifty minutes late,"** said Marek, and looked at the shelf. **"That valve seat you told me about on the Thursday — I put it in the wrong way round."**

**"I didn't tell you about a valve seat."**

**"No,"** he said. **"Somebody did, four days ago, and it's on the docket, and I put it in the wrong way round and the seat is upside down in that woman's machine and I would rather tell you that on a Friday at ten past five than have her find it on the Monday."**

She looked at him for four seconds and then said, **"Right,"** and wrote nothing down, and there is no column for it.

---

**At half past six, the four rooms off that service road, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall, and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door for the whole of the evening, unaddressed, unasked, unthanked, unapologised to, and her page the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf in the back, shut, as it has been every day since before anybody started writing any of this down.**

**The count of correct things that changed nothing is two and a Friday in a cellar is not a third.** What happened on that Friday is that a man of twenty-two went into a room and came out of it and can now name nine places, and on this second day he stands at the end of it able to do what he stood at the start able to do, and the number of people in this city who can name nine places has gone up by one, and a thing that has gone up by one is not a thing that changed nothing, and that is the argument this entry makes instead of the usual one, and it is the first of the ten that is not the usual one.

Nobody thanked anybody, nobody thanked the man of about fifty-eight, and nobody was thanked by a woman of about thirty-four who shut a shop on her own. **The shutter came down at ten on the Friday in a wind that had not been there on the Thursday, and it came down in one piece because he was in it and pushed it, and the frame has been swollen since the spring and he has been meaning to do something about the frame and did not.**

**What is left, and it is set down once, is a day.** He stands at the end of it able to do exactly what he stood at the start of it able to do, and a cellar he has been into is not an addition to that. Two cards in a rail, one of them one thousand one hundred and fourteen days old and four days the newer of the two, and neither has turned round since the spring. Two nails and nineteen lines and nothing on the twentieth because the twentieth has never been started. **The hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and seventy-six days old, ninety-six weeks and four days, eight of nine of them still going and the first row anybody disagrees about still not reached, and not one of the ten days this week has put a sheet against a sheet.** The cast-iron thing at the end of that passage is six hundred and fifty-eight days, ninety-four weeks to the day. The separation stands at four hundred and ninety days, seventy weeks to the day, and it was closed in a box in a form and it is still being counted every day of a stretch in which nothing whatever is happening in it. The drawer under the bench is shut, four sheets in it, and the fourth of the four has never once been looked at by anybody. The four words that name the public body do not appear anywhere in this file. Five things are named on this page and not one of them is introduced to another: a sheet that came back, a card in a rail, a form with an empty box, a docket with nine places on the back of it, and an iron plate with four slots in it.
