# Chapter 626 — A Friday, A Brass Tongue And A Plate On The Wall Of Nine Buildings, Nine Hands About Four Minutes Past Five And An Eleven Minute Hold, One Hand That Came Off It At About Ten Past Five, A Column Headed Released With A Name And A Time And A Signature Written In It By The Person Whose Hand It Was, And Nobody Made Any The Worse For It

**The Friday was a bench until two, a counter from two to six, and four rooms off that service road from half past six, and its shape was a brass tongue held up against a plate by nine people in nine buildings, eleven minutes, one hand that came off it, and a signature at the foot of a card that was in that cabinet before any of them were born.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday, each of them dated, eleven people over that counter, the last of them let in at ten past five. **The heating in that flat is above its winter setting for a sixth month and going into a seventh, four people on that tenancy are paying the difference without ever having said so to each other, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and has not been told.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and seventeen days, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and twenty-one days, a hundred and sixty weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The ask is eight hundred and seven days old, a hundred and fifteen weeks and two days, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is seven hundred and twenty-three days, a hundred and three weeks and two days, and eighty-four days sit between them, which is twelve weeks to the day, and there is a reason for that and the reason is that both of those numbers are on this page today and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city.**

---

**The fitting is the same in all nine of them, and it took four days for nine people to find out that it was the same, because nobody had thought to look at a wall.**

A grey box about the size of two fists, screwed at shoulder height, with a brass tongue standing out of the middle of it and a plate above it, and the plate has been worn to a shine on the underside by about four hundred years of nothing at all. **A room holds its end by keeping the tongue up against the plate. That is the whole of it. There is no lever and there is no switch and there is nothing to be found out about it afterwards.**

They found it on the Wednesday, at the water office, in a drawer, in four minutes, under about nine hundred sheets.

---

**They had no time, and Marek chose one, which is the first thing he has done in five weeks that nobody else in this city asked him to do.**

**"Half past five,"** he said, at a counter, to six people, on the Thursday night, in a shop that shuts at ten. **"If you're not there, you're not there. Nobody's going to go and fetch you."**

He said that last part on purpose and four of the six of them noticed that he had said it on purpose.

---

**At four minutes past five on the Friday, nine hands went up in nine buildings.**

Nobody saw anybody. **The man of twenty-two had the cover of the socket up and had his right hand on the rim of it and a lead from a meter reader's van running into the plate, and the only account anybody in this city can give of what happened in the next eleven minutes is that the young man of about twenty-four at the water office shouted down a corridor that a needle on a gauge in that building went over about a finger's width and came back, and that nobody anywhere else saw anything at all, and that a man of about fifty at a gate said the word *rubbish* into a corridor and then apologised for it to four people who were not listening.**

**At ten past five the woman of about forty-five took her hand off the brass tongue in a kitchen in a back yard and wiped it on her apron first, which is a thing a person does who has been cooking and not a thing a person does who has been holding a fitting, and eleven minutes is a long time to hold a piece of metal.**

---

**And a needle in a building nine streets away went back down to where it was, and somebody put a hand on a bell that did not sound.**

Nobody said anything for four seconds in the cellar. Then the man of about fifty-eight said, **"That's it, then,"** and the man of about fifty at the gate said **"That's not it at all,"** and they were both wrong and neither of them was talking to anybody.

---

**The card in the second holder has two columns and the one on the right is headed *released*, and the nine rows under it were empty on the Wednesday and were empty at four minutes past five, and at ten past five one of them had a name in it and a time and then a signature underneath the last row, and it was written in pencil, and the pencil was one Marek had put down on the cabinet about an hour earlier and had not noticed going down to the floor.**

**She came down for it. She had a hand still pink from a brass tongue and she wrote her own name in her own hand and the time in her own time, and the time she wrote was four minutes and twenty seconds before the time Marek had written on the back of a docket, and both of those times are on this page and neither of them has been altered.**

> Any room may release its end at any time. The release is entered here and the release ends the inspection.

**That is printed at the foot of that card and it was printed before the building had a landlord, and she did not read it out and she did not need to, because she had said all of it in nine seconds on the Wednesday from the bottom of a cellar stair and the card agrees with her word for word.**

---

**What she said, at ten past five, in nine seconds, in a cellar, to a man standing in it with his hand on a socket: that a kitchen where eleven people come in at eleven at night is not an end, that an end is a set time, that a person stood at a set time is not serving, and that the moment there is a set time in that kitchen the room stops being a room and becomes a queue, and that eleven people will be hungry at eleven at night instead of not.**

**He did not say she was wrong. He had a sentence ready that would have taken four seconds and it would have said that the number of people in that yard at eleven at night had not been measured by anybody, and she would have agreed with that and it would have changed nothing, and five people in that cellar would have agreed with him and the woman of about forty-five would have taken her hand off the tongue anyway at the same second.**

**The young man of about twenty-four from the water office wanted to do it again at once, that night, with eight, and he was not being cruel and he was not being a child.** He said, in nine seconds, that eight was more than half and that half was the most anybody had ever had out of this city and that it ought to be written down while it was still true.

**The man of about fifty-eight said no to that in four seconds, and what he said was that a thing which is true for eleven minutes and then is not, and then is again, is not a thing anybody is holding, it is a thing two people are doing, and the two of them will be the ones holding it and not the nine.** The young man said *right* and went up the stairs two steps behind a woman of about twenty-three who works at the counter in the water office and who had been in the cellar for the whole of it without saying one word and has not said one word about it since.

**That was the only moment in eleven minutes when anybody in that cellar was wrong, and it was the wrongest thing anybody said all evening, and it was said by a person who had been the first in this city to say yes to a time and who is going to be the one who tells the other eight, in four days, that it is finished.**

**The other eight did not agree with her. Four of them thought she was wrong and said so. About four thought she was right and said so, including the man of about fifty-eight, who said that the kitchen was the only one of the nine that anybody actually needed, and that this was not the moment to find that out.**

**Nobody made anybody the worse for it.** The woman of about forty-five is not a villain and is not a woman who has stopped a thing, and she has not been thanked, and she has not been asked to come up a cellar stair again, and she has not asked. **He asked her, at the top of the stair, whether she wanted him to say to the other four that she had not been asked to hold anything in the first place, and she said no, in two seconds, and he did not say it to them anyway, and there are four people in this city who think a woman let them down on a Friday in the spring and that is the cost of two seconds of a person being right.**

---

**The small thing he gave up: his left thumb had a cut on it from a job on the Tuesday, and eleven minutes on a brass tongue opened it from the knuckle to the first joint, and the woman of about thirty-four put two plasters on it at half past five out of a tin on a shelf, and neither of them said anything while she did it, and there is no form in this city on which a plaster is entered.**

**What he wanted, and did not have: nine seconds of an apology, ready since ten past five, in which he would have said to her that she was owed an answer and not a kitchen. He used those nine seconds writing the time down on the back of a docket, and the time was wrong by four minutes, and the docket is the only record of the eleven minutes that exists anywhere in this city, and it is a docket.**

---

At half past six, the service road. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall. **The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door from half past six until ten, and nobody said anything to her, and nobody has ever said sorry to her, and the page that is hers, the eighth of eight, in a ring binder, on a shelf at the back, was shut inside it and stayed shut.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is two and this Friday does not move it and the reason is the shortest one of the ten. A person who puts their hand on a piece of metal for eleven minutes and then takes it off is not a correct thing that changed nothing; a thing that changed nothing does not have a name and a time and a signature at the foot of it in pencil.**

Nobody thanked anybody in a cellar, on a stair, at a counter, or in four rooms off a service road. **The shutter came down at ten and the woman of about thirty-four did it, because his left hand was bandaged, and neither of them remarked on it, and the roller is stiff on the left and he had been the one to swear at it for two years.**

**And the figures, once, and then this stretch of days is four fifths done.** He is twenty-two, and on this sixth day he finishes having exactly what he started it with. The rail holds two; the newer of them is one thousand one hundred and twenty-one days old and has not been turned over. Two nails carry nineteen lines and the twentieth has never been started. Nine hand copies of the front of a page, six hundred and eighty-three days, ninety-seven weeks and four days, eight unfinished, the first row nobody has yet disagreed about still not found, and not one sheet was laid against another on this Friday. The cast-iron thing at the end of that passage: six hundred and sixty-five days, ninety-five weeks to the day. The separation: four hundred and ninety-seven days, seventy-one weeks to the day, shut inside a form five weeks ago and still being subtracted, and shorter by nothing, and with nothing put back into it. That drawer remains shut with four sheets inside, one of them with NO on it and four lines underneath, and the fourth of the four has never been looked at by anybody. The four words that name the public body are not on this page.
