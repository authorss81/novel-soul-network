# Chapter 625 — A Thursday Cut Into Four Pieces Between Two O'Clock And Five, A Water Office Where A Young Man Agrees And Asks For A Time Nobody In This City Can Supply, A Council House With A Fireguard Standing In Front Of A Hearth In The Warm Weather And Nobody Having Told Him He May Stop, A Kitchen Where A Woman Asks The Same Question And Sends Him Away, A Depot Where A Woman Agrees And Then Says Something That Is True, And A Counter Left To One Person For Two And A Half Hours

**The Thursday was a bench until two, a counter from two to five, and four rooms off that service road from half past six, and its shape was four rooms in four districts in three hours, one question asked in each of them that he could not answer, and nobody angry anywhere.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, all dated, eleven people over the counter, the last at ten past five. **The heat in that flat is above its winter setting for a sixth month, four names on that one tenancy are carrying the difference, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and has still not been told a single thing about it by anybody.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and sixteen days, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and twenty days, a hundred and sixty weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The ask is eight hundred and six days old, a hundred and fifteen weeks and one day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is seven hundred and forty-nine days, a hundred and seven weeks to the day, and the difference between those two numbers is fifty-seven days, and there is no form in this city on which those two figures are ever required to sit in the same column, and there never has been.**

---

He wrote nine names on the back of an envelope on the Wednesday night and did not post it, and on the Thursday he did not post it either, and by Friday it had gone into the fourth pocket of a tool roll, which is a place where things he has not written go to be somebody else's problem.

**He went to the water office at half past two and it took eleven minutes on a bus and the office closes at four.**

**He had the nine sheets with him in an envelope, folded, and he did not take them out, and the reason he did not take them out is that he had told the man of about fifty-eight on the Wednesday that he was going to explain nothing, and nine sheets in an envelope in a hand is explaining, and the young man of about twenty-four in this city is the sort of person who would have looked at them and asked a second question and then a third, and every one of those questions would have been about about nine hundred, and about nine hundred is a number nobody in this city is entitled to give anybody.**

**He has been caught, before, being the man with the number. He caught himself on a bus on the Thursday with an envelope in his hand and put it under his coat and left it there, and the young man of about twenty-four did not see it, and the cost of that is that the man of about twenty-four does not know that the sheets he carried down a cellar in a coat lining on Wednesday night have been in a shop in Lattice Ward ever since.**

**He went to the water office at half past two and it took eleven minutes on a bus and the office closes at four.**

---

**"I'll do it,"** said the young man of about twenty-four, and then, in the same breath, without being asked: **"What time."**

**"I don't know."**

**"Right."**

**"I mean I don't know and I've not looked properly and the card's got no time on it."**

**"Then don't ask me to stand somewhere and don't tell me I've said yes,"** said the young man, and he was not angry and he was not a boy, and he was twenty-four. **"I'll say yes when you bring me a time. That's not a no. I'd like it to be understood that's not a no."**

**It is not a no. It went on a list in Marek's head at half past two on the Thursday in the third position out of four, and the list is not on anything.**

---

**The council house is a man of about sixty who has kept everything since before the spring, and there is a fireguard standing in front of an open hearth in warm weather, and nobody in that house has ever told him that he may stop.**

He agreed to a time before Marek had said a time, and then asked the question.

**"How many of you have actually done it."**

**"None of us."**

**"Then what happens if four of you do it and five of you don't."**

**"I don't know that either,"** said Marek, and the man of about sixty did not say anything for four seconds, and then said, **"Right,"** and did not take it back, and put a plate of something on the table in front of Marek that he did not eat and that is still on the table in that house.

**The fireguard is in front of a hearth that has not been needed since the spring. There is no form for a fireguard and no form for a hearth and no form for the fact that a man of about sixty in this city cannot be told a thing like that, because telling him would mean somebody coming twice.**

---

**The kitchen is in a back yard and the woman of about forty-five put her hand on the door and asked the same question the young man had asked, in the same words, which is a thing that happens four times a year in a city this size.**

**"What time."**

**"I don't know."**

**"Then you can come back when you do,"** she said, and shut the door, and it was not shut in his face, and she was right, and four people were in that yard at the time and two of them looked at him and neither of them said anything and he has not been back to that yard since and the door has not been shut on him since and both of those are the same fact.

**"You know what the problem with you is,"** said a man of about thirty-four at ten past four, at the counter, in a queue, while she was still in the yard. **"You've got nine rooms and no hours."**

**"I've got nine rooms and no hours."**

**"Then you've not got nine rooms, you've got nine addresses,"** said the man of about thirty-four, and paid for a washer, and left, and he is not on any of the lists and is not a person in this story and does not come back, and he was the only person in four days who said the whole of it in one sentence and he said it in a queue about a washer.

---

**Between the council house and the kitchen there is half an hour that he spent walking, because the bus does not go to either and he had about forty minutes and a decision to make in it.**

**What he did in the forty minutes was stand outside a shop in a wide road and work out, on the back of a docket, what a person would have to be doing in a room for the printed two lines to mean anything at all, and he got as far as writing the word *stand* and then he crossed it out, because the card does not say stand, and a man who puts a word into a card that is not on the card is a man who has started writing the card himself, and he has done that before and it took four weeks to stop.**

**Then he walked. That is all he did with the forty minutes and it is on no form, and the docket is in the fourth pocket with the other two papers that are not his, and the word *stand* is still on it with a line through it.**

---

**The depot is a yard with a gate and the woman of about forty-one was at the gate at about a quarter to five, and she said yes before he had finished the sentence, and then she said the other thing.**

**"Yes,"** she said. **"And I'll tell you what yes costs me, because you'll write it down wrong if I don't. If I'm an end then I'm about nine hundred ends, because the sheets come off my gate and go to a building and I am the last person in this city who stands where about nine hundred of them were before they went. There is no way of being my end of it and not being the end of all of them, and if that is what the card means then the card is asking me to be about nine hundred people and I am one."**

**"That's true."**

**"I know it's true, that's why I said yes,"** said the woman of about forty-one, and opened the gate a foot, and Marek did not go through it, and she shut it again.****

**He stood outside that gate for eleven seconds afterwards and did not go back to it, and what he wanted to say and did not say was *so don't*, and the not-saying was four seconds long and it was the only time in four days that he came close to taking back a thing he had asked for, and he went and got the bus instead, and the bus was due in nine minutes and it came in six.**

---

At five he was back at that counter and three people had come and gone in his absence, and one of them had left a job and gone again, and it is on the shelf for the Monday and it is the only thing about the Thursday that will still be true of it in a week.

**The woman of about thirty-four told him, at five, that the day had taken eleven pounds, which is the least it has taken on a working day since the spring.**

**"You've not lost anything,"** she said. **"You'd have been standing here doing the same four things with the same four people in the same order."**

He said nothing to that, and she was right, and being right by somebody who is owed nothing is the cheapest thing anybody has handed him in four weeks.

---

**At half past six, the service road, and the man of about fifty-one against the north wall, and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door for the whole evening, nobody having said one word to her, and the eighth of eight in a ring binder unopened on the shelf in the back.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is two and this Thursday does not move it.** A man who walks into four rooms in four districts in three hours and is refused nothing has not done a correct thing that changed nothing; he has done a thing that changed nine people, three of them in the right direction, and the fact that it is not the register is the only reason the register is worth having.

Nobody thanked anybody in four rooms in four districts and nobody thanked the man of about thirty-four in a queue, who has not been thanked because he does not exist as far as this entry is concerned. **The shutter came down at ten and it stuck halfway and had to be lifted and dropped, and four people have since asked whether that shop is closing, and it is not closing, and the frame is to blame, and the frame has been to blame since the spring.**

**And the rest of it, set down once, at the end of a day spent walking.** A man of twenty-two ends this fifth day able to do exactly what he began it able to do. The rail keeps two, and the newer of the two is one thousand one hundred and twenty days and has not been turned. Two nails, nineteen lines, nothing ever added to that board. Nine hand copies of a page front, six hundred and eighty-two days, ninety-seven weeks and three days, eight unfinished, the first row nobody disagrees about still not found, and nothing was set against anything on this Thursday. The cast-iron thing at the end of that passage, six hundred and sixty-four days, ninety-four weeks and six days. The separation, four hundred and ninety-six days, seventy weeks and six days, shut in a form and still subtracted daily, and not shorter, and with nothing put back into it. That drawer, shut, four sheets, the fourth never read. The four words that name the public body are in none of this. Six articles appear on this page and no two of them are put together: a sheet that came back, a card on end in a rail, an envelope with nine names on it that was not posted, a form with a blank box, a docket with nine places on its back, and a plate of iron with four slots.

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **Three people in three districts agreed to a thing they have not been told the time of, and one of them agreed and said the cost out loud, and one sent him away, and one put a plate in front of him.** Nobody was thanked at any point in any of the four of those rooms, and nobody was taught anything, shown anything, assessed, or helped. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and being refused nothing all day is not nothing, and the two on it are still the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns.
