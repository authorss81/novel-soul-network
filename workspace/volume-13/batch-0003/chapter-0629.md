# Chapter 629 — A Thursday, Five More People Told On Foot And Two In Towns Who Will Hear It From Somebody Else, A Driver Of About Twenty-Four At The Counter At About Half Past Two Asking How Long Nine Crates Can Be Held In A Yard, A Number That Is Also A Day, A Man Against A North Wall Whose Number Is Also A Day, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Three Behind A Counter Who Was In The Room And Has Not Said Anything To Anybody

**A bench until two, a counter from two to six, four rooms off that service road after half past six, and the whole of the middle of this Thursday spent walking between rooms in four districts, telling five people something and failing to reach two.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, every one of them dated, eleven people over that counter, the last let in at ten past five. **The heat in that flat is above its winter setting and has been for six months, four people on that tenancy are paying for it out of four incomes, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and has not been told that anybody in this shop knows the figure.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and twenty-three days, a hundred and sixty weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and twenty-seven days, a hundred and sixty-one weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The twelfth line on those two nails is one thousand and forty-three days and the thirteenth is nine hundred and ninety-four, and both of those come out on a whole week this week, which they did not do last week, and which is the only thing the board did all week.**

---

**The driver came in at half past two and he was not a customer and he had about an hour to wait for a collection.**

**"You've got a number in your head,"** he said. **"Seven hundred and fifty-six days. A man with a clipboard said it to me at a gate on Monday and he said it like it was a date."**

**"It is a number of days,"** said Marek. **"Nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns, and the hold began seven hundred and fifty-six days ago, and the man who owns the crates is about thirty-three, and he has not been able to hand them over since, and that is the whole of what I know and it came off a card in nine seconds from a man who was not asked to give it to me."**

**"What do you want it for."**

**"Nothing,"** said the driver of about twenty-four. **"I wanted to know if it was a number or a date. It's a number. That's all right. You can go back to your bench."**

He bought nothing and left at half past three and has not been back, and four people in that shop heard the whole of it, and the woman of about thirty-four has said since that that is the first time in four years anybody has been told the answer to a question of that shape and then told not to buy anything.

---

**And at half past six, in a building off a service road, a man of about fifty-one was against a north wall, and he has been against that wall for seven hundred and twenty-nine days, a hundred and four weeks and one day, and the number has not been mentioned to him by anybody in this city and will not be.**

**Seven hundred and twenty-nine is how long that man has been sitting there. It is also the day the hold on nine crates began. And seven hundred and fifty-six is how long that yard has been holding them; it is also the day he first sat down against that wall with a newspaper. So in each of those two numbers one half is an age and the other half is a day a thing happened, and a figure standing on its own cannot tell you which half of itself it is, and neither of the two numbers is a day on which anything at all happened to the heating in that flat, and this entry has said so on every day of this stretch and says it again here because this is the day both of them are on the same page.**

---

**Five more were told on foot on the Thursday and two of the nine could not be reached at all.**

The first was a man of about fifty-eight who had already been told. The second was a woman of about twenty-three who works at the counter in the water office and who was in a room above a line in Saltmarket on the Wednesday and has not said one word about it to anybody, including the young man she works beside.

**"You were there."**

**"I was there,"** she said.

**"You didn't say anything."**

**"No,"** said the woman of about twenty-three. **"I was stood at the back and there was eleven of you and the woman who holds that room asked me nothing and I was not asked, and if I had stood up there and said *this is not what a return is* nine of you would have looked at me and four of those nine would never have spoken to me again and one of them is a man I go to a bus with on a Tuesday. So I said nothing. I have been saying nothing since. It's a skill."**

He said nothing back to that, and it is the best answer she could have got and he gave it her.

---

**He did not say it to her in a queue. He waited four minutes and asked her to step outside, and she came out and stood on the step of that building in the cold with her arms folded and she did not ask him why they were outside, and that is a thing about her and not about him.**

**"The woman who holds that room has a book,"** he said. **"There's a line gone into it. It happened on Wednesday and I wasn't there and I'm telling you because you're one of the nine and you were owed it before anybody else."**

**"Was I."**

**"You were in the room."**

**"I was stood at the back with my coat on,"** said the woman of about twenty-three. **"Nine people and a book and four minutes each, and if I'd been a sheet of paper I'd have been filed. That's not a complaint. That's just what it was."**

**He had a thing ready. It was about her and it was about a trade and it took eleven seconds to say and it would have put her in a room she has spent nine years learning to stand in the back of, and he did not say it, and the not-saying took four seconds and the four seconds were worse than the eleven.**

---

**The third was a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a counter in one of the two schools, and she took it in four seconds and said *fine* and went back behind her counter and has not mentioned it since and will hold an end if anybody ever comes with a time.**

**The fourth and the fifth were in the first and the third of the four towns, four days of bus travel each way, and the bus on a Friday gets there when it gets there, and neither of them is going to hear this from him.**

**That is the cost of the Thursday and it is the only cost on this page and it is going to get worse rather than better: two people in two towns are about to hear from a man with a clipboard that the link is finished, and that man's version has a kitchen in it, and the woman who runs that kitchen is four days away and will find out about herself from a stranger in a yard.**

**He has not found a way to prevent that and he has been thinking about it since two o'clock and the thinking has not produced anything, and four seconds ago he worked out that preventing it would mean being in a yard in the second of the four towns on about Friday, and being in that yard is a thing that ends with him being the man people go to about returns, and he worked that out in four seconds and then went and did the fourth job of the day anyway.**

---

**At half past five, back at that counter, the woman of about thirty-four said the thing she had been holding since two.**

**"The yard man came past this afternoon,"** she said. **"The one with the clipboard. He didn't come in. He stood at the end of your road for four minutes and then he wrote something on his arm, which men do, and then he went."**

**"Did he come in."**

**"He did not come in,"** said the woman of about thirty-four, **"and I have watched about nine hundred doors from that counter in eleven years and I am telling you that a man stood at the end of your road for four minutes and did not come in, because that is the only part of it that is any use to anybody and it is four minutes long and it is going to cost you something and I do not know what."**

He said right and went and did the fourth job of the day.

**The four rooms off that service road after half past six, with four people in for the heat and nobody asked anything by anybody. The man who has been against the north wall since the spring was against it. The chair nearest the door was taken by a woman of about thirty at half past six and kept until ten, and in three and a half hours not one person said anything to her, and the page with her name at the head of it stayed shut inside a ring binder on the back shelf.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is closed at two and this Thursday does not add to it, and the reason is that a man who tells another man that a figure is a figure and not a date has handed four people a way of asking a question, and the person they will want to ask it of is not in this city and is not going to be.**

**The small thing he gave up and it is on no form: the docket in the fourth pocket with four words on the back of it, six, tray, dark, nobody told me, and he has not had the sentence itself and cannot have it, and the four words are all he has of a thing that has by now been said to eleven people in a room he was not in.**

**What he wanted all Thursday and did not have was to be the person the woman of about thirty-two told it to. She has not come back into that shop and there is no address in this city for a corner grocery and he has the name of a street and not the number, and he did not ask the woman of about thirty-four for it, and she has not offered it, and that is the second time in three weeks that he has had the asking available in four seconds and has not spent it.**

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, nobody thanked the woman of about twenty-three for the skill, and nobody was thanked in four rooms off a service road. **The shutter came down at ten and came down straight, and the woman of about thirty-four said *there it goes* to nobody, and it was the first time in four weeks that either of them had said anything at all about the shutter.**

**And what is left of a Thursday, given once and then the week is not done but the stretch of days nearly is.** He is twenty-two, and on this ninth day he finishes holding exactly what he started it holding, plus five people out of eight told and two towns in which a man with a clipboard is standing. A rail of two, the newer of them one thousand one hundred and twenty-seven days old, unmoved. A board on two nails with nineteen lines on it, and the twentieth not begun. Nine hand copies of a page front, six hundred and eighty-nine days, ninety-eight weeks and three days, eight of them short of the whole, the first row nobody disagrees about still not found, and nothing laid against anything today. A piece of cast iron at the end of a passage, six hundred and seventy-one days, ninety-five weeks and six days. The separation, five hundred and three days, seventy-one weeks and six days, closed in a form five weeks back, subtracted every day since, no shorter, nothing put into it. That drawer is shut with four sheets in it and the fourth has never been looked at. The four words that name the public body are not on this page. Six objects are named below and not one pair of them is set beside another: a sheet that came back, a card in a rail, a form with an empty box, a job docket with nine places on its back, a hand copy of the front of a page, and a plate of iron with four slots.
