# Chapter 627 — A Monday, A Counter Left Shut From Two Until Five, A Second Cellar In Another Part Of The Second District With An Identical Cabinet In It And A Chair That Was Not There On Friday, Eight People To Be Told On Foot, Three Told, And A Man At A Gate Asking Whether A Woman Blamed Herself And Being Told That She Did Not

**The Monday was a bench until two and a counter from five until six, four rooms off that service road from half past six, and its shape was one cellar walked into with a man of about fifty-eight, one chair nobody in this city put there, and three people told something on foot in three hours.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, every one of them dated, eleven people over that counter before two, the last of them at ten to two. **The heating in that flat is above its winter setting for a sixth month, four names on that tenancy are carrying it, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and has still not been told a thing about it.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and twenty days, a hundred and sixty weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and twenty-four days, a hundred and sixty weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**Those four rooms come out at a hundred and sixty weeks to the day and the nine hand copies of the front of a page come out at ninety-eight weeks to the day, and both of those figures land on the day and not near it, and this is the first week of this stretch in which two of the series do.**

---

**He had a list in his head of eight names and no way of getting to any of them that did not involve his own two legs, and he wrote the eight on the back of an envelope in the morning before he left, and the envelope went in the fourth pocket, and the fourth pocket now holds four pieces of paper that are not his, a docket with nine places on the back of it, a docket with four words on the back of it, an envelope with nine names on it that was never posted, and an envelope with eight names on it, and none of those five things knows about any of the other four and that is not an accident and it is not a method, and the man standing in that shop on the Monday morning would have told you there is no word for a pocket.**

**The counter was shut for three hours on the Monday and nobody told anybody why, and four people came in that afternoon and went out again, and one of them has not come back and is not going to.**

He had eight to tell. There were nine, and one of the nine had already been told by being the one who told them, and eight people in four districts knew a thing on a Friday and it was now a Monday and a Monday is the day that gets worked out.

---

**The man of about fifty-eight had a key that was not his and said so before Marek asked for it, which is the right way round and is rare.**

**"It's on a loop,"** he said. **"There's nine of them on a loop of keys in a tin in the water office and I had one off it in the spring for a stopcock and I never gave it back and there's four people in that building who know I've got it and four who don't. There's another cellar under a yard in the back of this district and there's one in it, and I saw it nine years ago when the stopcock was moved, and it's the same, and I never told anybody because nobody ever asked."**

**"Did you ask."**

**"I asked the man who read the meters,"** said the man of about fifty-eight, **"and he said he didn't know there was one. That's a true answer from a man who reads meters."**

---

**The second cellar is under a yard and the door has a bar on it and the key on that loop fits, and the cabinet in it is the same cabinet — the same height, the same grey, the same bolt head, the same nine wires the thickness of a pencil lead going up into the same kind of hole in the same kind of brick, and a card in a holder with nine places on it, and the nine places are the nine places.**

**And there is a chair in that cellar.** A wooden chair, a kitchen chair, with the paint worn off the back of it in two places where hands go. **It was not there on the Friday and the man of about fifty-eight was in this cellar in the spring and there was nothing in the room then but a stopcock, and neither of them put it there, and the man of about fifty-eight stood looking at it for eleven seconds and then said the only thing anybody in this city is entitled to say about it, which is that he did not put it there and he is not going to be the one who takes it out.**

**Nobody in this city has any way of finding out who did.** That is a fact about the Monday and not a mystery, and the two are not the same thing, and Marek noticed the difference at the time and it did him no good.

---

**Between the two cellars there is twenty minutes of walking and he spent the whole of it deciding, and the decision took four seconds and then the other sixteen minutes went on being walking.**

The decision was whether to say, at the water office, in front of four people, that nine sheets had been on a desk in that building since Wednesday and that nobody had asked. He could have said it in four seconds and it would have been a true thing said to a room in the ordinary way, and it would have moved nine sheets from a desk into a drawer, and a man of about twenty-four would have been the man who had left nine sheets on a desk in a water office, and that would be on a form in four days.

He did not say it. **The reason he did not say it is that saying it is a thing a man does with a room, and a man who does things with a room is a man rooms do things with, and he has been refusing that since the winter and has been right four times.**

**The young man of about twenty-four had the nine sheets out on the desk at the water office on the Monday, in two rows, and he had not put them back in the drawer, and there was nothing on top of them and nothing written down about them anywhere, and the woman of about twenty-three behind that counter walked past them about thirty times and did not ask about them once.**

Marek saw them and did not mention them, and four other people came into that office in the time he was there and none of them mentioned them, and that is how a room works, and there is no form for it and there has never been one.

---

**Three of the eight were told on foot on the Monday, between three and half past five, and the first two of them said nothing at all when he told them, and both of them had already stopped, and neither of them had a question.**

The third was the man of about fifty who keeps a gate, and he had one.

**"Did she say it was her."**

**"No."**

**"Because if she said it was her then that's a kitchen with a problem,"** said the man of about fifty, **"and if she didn't then it's a thing in a cellar and the cellar is a room I have never been in."**

**"It's a thing in a cellar,"** said Marek, **"and she said in nine seconds that a kitchen with a set time in it is a queue, and she was right, and four people in that cellar thought she was wrong and one of them wrote it down on a day sheet they keep."**

**"So it's a kitchen with a problem."**

**"No,"** said Marek, and the man of about fifty said *right* and went back to his gate.

---

**And that is the whole of what he gave up on the Monday, and it is on no form and it is the largest thing in this entry.** He could have said *yes, she said it was her*, in three words, and the man at the gate would have carried that back to a yard and the yard would have carried it to a kitchen, and the whole of the eleven minutes would have become the fault of a woman of about forty-five who had said nine true seconds, and it would have been easier, and it would have taken four seconds to say, and everybody in four districts would have been relieved.

**He did not say it, because a woman who releases an end of a thing is doing what the card says she may do, and the moment that becomes a fault then every release in this city is a fault, and every room that lets go first is a room that has let everybody down.** The cost is that the man of about fifty now believes the kitchen stopped it and will believe it for as long as he lives, and the kitchen does not know that, and if it finds out it will be harder to ask a second time, and Marek is the only person in this city who could correct that account and has decided not to.

**He decided it in four seconds, standing at a gate, and he has not been able to give a better account of those four seconds since.**

---

**The service road after half past six, and four people in for the heat, and the man who has been against that north wall since the spring against it again with his paper folded the way he folds it. The chair nearest the door was taken by a woman of about thirty at half past six and held until ten, and no one in that building spoke to her, asked her anything, or has ever apologised to her, and the page with her name at the head of it stayed shut inside the ring binder on the back shelf.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is two and this Monday does not move it, and the argument is that refusing to say a thing about a person who has behaved well is not a correct thing that changed nothing, because the not-saying changes what four people believe about a woman who is owed nothing and has done nothing wrong, and it is on no form and it is permanent.**

**At about one, before he went out, the woman of about thirty-four asked him one question and it was not about the cabinet.**

**"Is there a version of this where somebody pays for it."**

**"Not one I can see."**

**"Right,"** said the woman of about thirty-four, and priced a part, and did not ask again, and that is the only time in four weeks that anybody in that building has put a question about money to a man in it, and it was put by the person in it who is owed nothing and gets none of it.

**The woman of about thirty-four shut that shop by herself on the Monday for the second time in four days, and she has not mentioned it either time, and on the Monday she was two minutes late on the shutter because she was doing the counter and the roller and the till at once, and nobody in the street noticed and she noticed.**

Nobody thanked anybody on the Monday. **The shutter came down at ten and the woman of about thirty-four said, without being asked, that it had been a strange Monday for a shop that shuts at six, and then did not say anything else about it, and Marek agreed that it had, and neither of them said the rest of it, and it is the second time in four days that the two of them have agreed about a thing neither of them has said.**

**And in the four rooms off that service road on the Monday evening, four people came in for the heat and none of them was asked anything by anybody, and one of them said that the corridor light on the second landing had been out since the Tuesday, and the man of about fifty-one at that wall said *about that* and nothing else, and that is the whole of what two people in this city have said to each other in four years.**

**What he wanted, and did not get, all day, was somebody to ask him whether he had been right to leave the nine sheets on a desk. Nobody in that office asked. He had a sentence ready for the whole of the walk between the two cellars and it never got used, and the sentence was four seconds long, and four people in that water office have since told other people that the young man had nine sheets out on his desk for a fortnight, which is not what happened and is what will be said.**
