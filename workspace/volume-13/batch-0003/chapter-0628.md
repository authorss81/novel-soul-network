# Chapter 628 — A Wednesday, A Grocery On A Corner And A Tray At The Back Of It, Nine Seconds At A Counter At About Half Past Three That Nobody Repeats Anywhere, Nine Items At About Four Minutes Each In A Room Above A Line, A Count Of Fifty-One Of Which Forty-Six Correspond, An Empty Place Behind A Chair That Went To Hospital On Saturday, And A Pen Put Down And Picked Up Again In About Four Seconds

**Two hours at a bench, two more behind a counter, then a room up a stair in Saltmarket, and after that four rooms off a service road. What this Wednesday turned out to be about is a woman of about thirty-two who keeps a grocery on a corner, and nine seconds in which she said that she has a tray at the back of her shop.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, a date on each, eleven people over that counter, the last of them let in at ten past five. **The heat in that flat has been turned above where it goes in the winter since the spring and is still above it now, four people on that one tenancy are between them making up the difference without ever having said they were, and there is a fortnight of a cook's own money that she is still out of pocket and that nobody in this city has told her about.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and twenty-two days, a hundred and sixty weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and twenty-six days, a hundred and sixty weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The eighteenth line on the two nails is eight hundred and forty days, which is a hundred and twenty weeks to the day, and those four rooms are a hundred and sixty weeks and two days, and one of those numbers lands on a whole week and the other does not, and that is the whole of what those two figures have ever said to one another and it is on this page because this is the day the book in another building opened.**

---

**She came in at half past three and she had a sheet with her and she wanted to know what a return was, and she had asked before and had been told, and the telling is why she came back.**

**"I sent this back in the spring,"** said the woman of about thirty-two. **"To a room. Somebody wrote the room on it and it's not a number, it's a room with a name on it. And I was told it was received. I sent it again and I was told it was received."**

**"What do you want."**

**"I want to know whether it's received."**

Marek said it in nine seconds and he said it to her face, and four people were in that shop, and none of them understood one word of it and two of them have repeated it since.

**"The room it names is a room four steps below a pavement in a second district,"** he said, **"with a bar on the outside of the door and a light on a timer that goes off at six. It is let by the month for eleven pounds. From the Monday anybody anywhere in this city can tell you it is with the landlord, and that is true, and nobody has to say one word more than that. I have not been down those four steps. I was told not to go down them and I did not go down them."**

**"So it's not received."**

**"I didn't say that,"** said Marek. **"I said where it is."**

**"That is the same thing,"** said the woman of about thirty-two, and put the sheet on the counter, and then did not pick it up, and it lay on that counter for eleven seconds, and then she picked it up and put it in her pocket, and said, **"Right,"** and went out, and the shutter on her corner goes down at six.

**At about a quarter to four the woman of about thirty-two was still at the counter with the sheet in her pocket, and she said one more thing, and it was not a question, and Marek wrote it on the back of a docket because there was a pen on the counter and because he did not want to lose the shape of it.** He wrote: *six, tray, dark, nobody told me.* Four things and no sentence. He has still got the docket and it is the fourth pocket and it is not a list and it is not going to become one.

**He has thought about that eleven seconds every day since. She put it down and she picked it up. She has not sent it again and she has not told anybody, and the two halves of that are not the same decision and he was the only person in the room who could see both of them.**

---

**And at four minutes past four a woman of about sixty opened a book with a green cover on a table in a room above a line in Saltmarket and said the number first, which is what she has always done.**

**"Fifty-one."**

**"Of which?"** said the man of about sixty-one.

**"Forty-six correspond."** She said it the way a person reads a temperature. **"Up by one. The five that do not correspond are the same five and I have never shown them to anybody and I am not going to start this afternoon."**

**Nobody asked her about the five.**

---

**There were eleven people in that room.** A woman of about sixty who has held it for about nineteen years and is paid by nobody. A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard and two thousand lines in one column that has never been totalled. A woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs for another body and was there as a person. A man of about thirty-three with nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over. A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm. Six others, two of them new.

**And behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place, and the place has been empty since Saturday, and two people in that room had noticed it by the middle of the hour and neither of them said anything about it at any point.** The man who has stood behind that chair for eleven years is in a hospital bed four districts away and has been since the Saturday, and it is the first Wednesday in eleven years that he has not been in that room, and there is no form on which a person records that they were not in a room.

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room. It has been there as long as anybody can remember and it did not move on this Wednesday and nobody has ever said who put it there.**

---

**Nine items, four minutes each, and they went the way nine items have gone every fourth Wednesday for about nineteen years.**

A boiler that a landlord said was the tenant's. A dispute about a date printed in two hands. A request that a thing be sent to a work address and not to a house. A woman who wanted to know whether she was still on a list. A question about a name in a ledger. Something about a van that had not come. A person who wanted a copy of a letter that had already been answered. A request for a form to be reprinted with a bigger box. A man who wanted to know whether he had to keep a thing he had already handed in, and was told in four seconds, by a person entitled to say it, that he did not, and wrote nothing down.

**The book with the green cover was on the table face down until half past five. It has sixty-one lines in it and it had sixty-one lines in it for four years. At the far end of that same table there is a tin, it has a lid, and seventy-three is what is in it, and nobody lifted that lid at any point of the hour, and nobody in that room has ever converted those two figures into a single one and this entry is not going to convert them either.**

---

**The tenth thing was not an item and it took nine seconds and it came up the stair on its own.**

The woman of about thirty-two from the corner grocery had not been in that room in her life. She had a carrier bag and she had put the sheet in it before she came and she took it out and she did not read from it.

**"I don't know what a return is,"** she said. **"I never have. I've had a tray at the back of my shop since the spring for things that are paid for and not taken, and nobody ever told me what a return was, and I asked a man with a bag once and he looked at me like I'd asked him for money. There are nine things in that tray. And my shutter goes down at six."**

**"Yes,"** said the woman of about sixty.

**"So at six that's nine things in a dark room."**

**"Yes."**

**"Right,"** said the woman of about thirty-two, and four people in that room looked at the table.

---

**And the woman of about sixty turned the book over, and picked up the pen, and wrote one line in it, and the pen was the room's own pen and the line took four seconds and it is the only thing that has gone into that book in four years.**

> **A tray is a room if the light goes off on a timer.**

**The book is on sixty-two lines at the end of the hour. It was on sixty-one lines at the start of it. The tin is on seventy-three and it was not opened. The man of about sixty-one put a number in his column and did not write the sentence down, and has not written it down, and was asked about it six weeks afterwards and said *it was in the book*.**

**Nobody in that room asked the woman of about thirty-two whether she was all right. About eleven people were in it. Eleven people began putting their coats on at ten past six, and the woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs said *that is a very good line* and the woman of about sixty said nothing at all, and the man of about thirty-three with the crates in a yard said nothing at all, and the woman of about twenty-nine who types said nothing at all.**

---

**And the man of about forty-nine, who would have known in four seconds that the woman of about thirty-two had been told something at half past three in a shop in Lattice Ward, was four districts away in a bed, and nobody in that room knew that the sentence in the book was a sentence that a man said to a grocer about a bar on the outside of a door, and no one in this city knows it to this day, and there is no column anywhere for it.**

**The service road after half past six. The man who has been against that north wall since the spring was against it again. The chair nearest the door was taken at half past six by a woman of about thirty and held until ten, and nothing was said to her in that building for three and a half hours, and the eighth of eight, in a ring binder on a shelf in the back, was shut inside it the whole time.**

**He does not know about the book. That is the true position on the Wednesday evening and it is not a thing he chose and it is not a thing anybody did to him, and he went to four rooms off a service road and stood in a doorway for about an hour and did not once think about it, and six days afterwards somebody in a queue mentioned it and he did not believe her.**

**About a minute before eleven people began putting their coats on, the man of about thirty-three with the nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over said, in nine seconds, that he had a question about the tray, and the woman of about thirty-two said she did not have a tray she could describe to a man who was asking about a tray, and he said *right*, and that was the only exchange of the evening that anybody in that room has repeated since, and it was repeated wrong twice.** He is not on a list and nothing in that room was decided by him and he has told nobody what he was going to do about the yard and has not done anything about the yard.

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is two and this Wednesday does not move it.** A woman who writes one sentence because another woman said a true thing out loud in a room she had never been in is not a correct thing that changed nothing, because a thing that changed nothing does not get a book opened for it and the man of about sixty-one put a number in his column for it and eleven people put their coats on after it.

Nobody thanked anybody on that stair, in that room, or at that counter. **The shutter came down at ten and it came down hard enough to shake the cage, and the woman of about thirty-four said that in eleven years that shutter has done that twice, and did not say when the other one was.**

**And the figures, once, and this is the last Wednesday of this stretch of days.** He is twenty-two, and on this eighth day he ends it with exactly what he began it with, and he does not know what happened upstairs. The rail holds two and the newer of them is one thousand one hundred and twenty-six days and has not been turned. Two nails, nineteen lines, no twentieth. Nine hand copies of a page front, six hundred and eighty-eight days, ninety-eight weeks and two days, eight unfinished, the first row nobody disagrees about still not found, nothing laid against anything. The cast-iron thing at the end of that passage, six hundred and seventy days, ninety-five weeks and five days. That drawer under the bench stays shut with the same four sheets in it and the fourth of those has never been looked at by anybody. The four words that name the public body are not on this page.
