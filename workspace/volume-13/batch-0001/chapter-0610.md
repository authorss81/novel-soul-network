# Chapter 610 — A Thursday, Eleven Jobs, One Proposal To Pay A Woman In Another Building Four Hours For Answering Questions Honestly, Nine Seconds Of Refusal From A Person With An Ordinary Job Who Was Right, And About Four Hundred And Fifty People In This City Who Can Each Say A True Thing Out Loud And Not One Of Them Has Anywhere To Put It

**The Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was a proposal made to a woman who could not accept it and refused it in about nine seconds, and the true reason for the refusal, and both of them were correct.**

---

The Thursday's eleven jobs were all dated and eleven people came through the counter, the last at about ten past five. Two hours at the bench, four at that counter, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six onward. **The heat in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that one tenancy are carrying it, and a fortnight of a cook's own money is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month, and on that Thursday she put four pounds of it on the table in coins and counted it and put it back in her pocket.** Those four rooms are one thousand and eighty-eight days, a hundred and fifty-five weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in that rail is one thousand and ninety-two days, a hundred and fifty-six weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The eighteenth line on the two nails is eight hundred and six days, one hundred and fifteen weeks and one day, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is six hundred and ninety-four days, ninety-nine weeks and one day. The first of those is a line on a board and the second is a man, and one of the two numbers is a day something happened and the other is a number of days and they are five weeks apart in a sentence and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city.**

---

**He made the proposal at about half past two on the Thursday, at that counter, to the woman of about thirty-four, and he had had it in his mouth since the Friday before and had said it to nobody.**

**"What would it take to ask her properly."**

**"To ask her what."**

**"To ask her how it works. All of it. Somebody sat down and asked her, in order, and wrote it down, and gave her the copy."**

**"That is four hours,"** she said. **"Maybe five with the walking and the tea. Four or five hours, once, and she'd do it in a morning because she's not doing anything else that morning, and she'd be good at it, and she'd be better at it than anybody in this city."**

**"Then we pay her for it."**

---

**"No,"** she said, in about nine seconds, and then she told him why, and both halves of why are on this page, and the second half is the one he has been carrying for two weeks and did not know he was carrying.

**"You cannot pay her out of a till in Lattice Ward. If she is to be paid it has to come off something with a name on it, and there is not one thing in this city with a name on it that would put four hours of a woman sorting paper into a budget line, and if you found one, it would be a charity, and she would know it was a charity inside about a minute, because she has been the woman somebody is kind to for about four months and she did not ask for that either."**

**"Right."**

**"And that is not the reason either,"** she said, and went and served a man of about forty-four who had come in about a kettle, and came back, and said the rest of it while the kettle went on boiling behind her.

**"Four hours is not why she is doing it. She is doing it because it is hers and nobody has taken it off her, and she has been the only person in that room since the spring, and the day somebody takes it off her to pay her for it, she is a person with a job. And the day she is a person with a job she has to be told what the job is. And the thing she is doing has never been written down, so there is nothing to tell her, so you would have to go and write it down, and writing it down is taking it off her, and then you would be giving it back having taken it, and she would know that inside about four seconds and she would be right to."**

---

**He said, and it took about four seconds and it is the last thing he says on this page: "Then somebody should write it down."**

**"Yes,"** she said. **"And you are the only person in this city anybody would believe while they wrote it, and if you write it down, she is not the person who did it. You will be. And she will know that inside about four seconds too, and she will be right about that as well, and about a week later she will stop."**

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, and she was not thanked, and he did not ask her a question about it, and there is no form in this city on which a person's telling you something at a counter is anything.

---

**What was said in a face, on the Thursday, at about twenty to four, to about four people, by a man of about fifty-two who buys a part every fortnight and who does not normally talk at a counter, and who was not agreed with by anybody and who is owed nothing and is owed a great deal that nobody has offered him:**

**"There is a page at the back of that form that everybody in this city can say something true about, and about four hundred and fifty people could each say a true thing about it this afternoon, and not one of them has anywhere to put it. Not one. There is no box for it and there is no room for it and there is no person in this city who is paid to be given it."**

He said it looking at the man of about thirty-four and not at the man of about twenty-two, and about four people in that shop heard it and one of them repeated it to a man of about sixty-one in a corridor two days later, and it has not been written down anywhere, and the man of about fifty-two does not know that it is the sentence this whole volume is built out of, and would not have cared, and was not thanked, and has gone on buying a part every fortnight.

---

**And the rest of it is a figure and a shape rather than a scene, and it is set down here once at the end of the first three weeks of the book. The man of twenty-two can do, on this tenth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off him, and the only thing that has happened in three weeks is that he has found out where the returns go and has not found out whether they come.**

The card in the rail is one thousand and ninety-two days in a rail that holds two and it did not move on any of the ten days. Two nails, nineteen lines, and no twentieth line in the whole history of that board. **The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and fifty-four days, ninety-three weeks and three days, eight of the nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line still not found, and no two of the nine were compared on any of the ten days of these three weeks.** The post at the corridor end is six hundred and thirty-six days, ninety weeks and six days. The separation is four hundred and sixty-eight days, sixty-six weeks and six days, and it was ended in a one-line box on the Friday of the second week, and the figure is walked on this Thursday and on the forty days after it and is not going to be stopped.

**The drawer under that bench is shut and holds four sheets, one of which has the word NO on it and four lines under it, and the fourth of which came through a hatch off a man who was on a road he was already on, and it was opened for about nine seconds on the Monday of the second week and not read and it has not been opened since.** He has been asked five times for nine lines and has not given them a sixth time, and they are on nothing. The four words that name the public body were said once, in a room, in a room in another book, by the person entitled to say them, and are on none of the ten days and are not going to be on any of the other forty.

**Under a floor in the first of the four towns there is a plate of iron with four slots, and on none of the ten days of these three weeks did it come out from under that floor, and it is still not called anything by anybody. A woman of about thirty-one who keeps the supply room of a school has not been found. A woman of about thirty sits in the chair nearest the door in four rooms off a service road and is not spoken to and is not asked anything and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf and it has not been read on any of the ten days and the binder has not been opened. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall has not been handed a written thing by anybody and nobody has asked. About four hundred people in this city each know half of an address and about four of those halves are in a tool roll and one of them is partly on a shirt sleeve, and nobody has put them together, and the woman of about sixty-eight who holds the four trays has not been asked, and the number in the four trays is not known, and the chair at the end of the table on the second floor in Crown Terrace was empty on one Monday and nobody said why.**

Nobody thanked anybody on any of the ten days of these three weeks, and the shutter came down at about ten on all ten of them, and there is no Sunday among those ten days and no shop in this city opens on a Sunday.

*Four hours, and the reason the four hours was not the reason.* **The proposal: that somebody should sit down with the woman of about sixty-eight, in order, and write down what the four trays and the one rule and the one bag on a Monday actually are, and give her a copy. The price, as estimated by the woman of about thirty-four at that counter: four hours, or five with the walking and the tea, once, in a morning, because there is nothing else in that morning. The refusal, in about nine seconds, and the first reason: it cannot be paid out of a till in Lattice Ward, it would have to come off something with a name on it, and there is nothing in this city with a name on it that would carry four hours of that work, and if there were it would be a charity. The second reason, which is the one that decides it: four hours is not why she is doing it; she is doing it because it is hers and nobody has taken it off her; the day somebody pays for it, she is a person with a job, and a person with a job has to be told what the job is, and what the job is has never been written down, so somebody would have to write it down, and writing it down is taking it off her, and giving it back after that makes it hers again in about four seconds in her own estimation and she would be right. What he said to that, in about four seconds: then somebody should write it down. What she said to that: yes, and he is the only person in this city anybody would believe while they wrote it, and if he writes it down she is not the person who did it, and about a week later she will stop. What was not asked: whether stopping is bad. What was not offered by anyone: to be the person who is paid.**

*A true sentence, said once, in a face, and agreed with by nobody.* **Who said it: a man of about fifty-two who buys a part from that shop every fortnight and does not normally speak at a counter. To whom: to about four people, and to the man of about thirty-four rather than to the man of about twenty-two, and the man of about twenty-two was stood four feet away and was not addressed. What was said, in short: that there is a page at the back of a form that about four hundred and fifty people in this city could each say one true thing about this afternoon, and that not one of them has anywhere to put it, and that there is no person in this city who is paid to be given it. Who agreed with it: nobody, at the time, and one man repeated it to a man of about sixty-one in a corridor two days later and he is not sure whether the second man agreed with it. What happened to it: it was not written down, and it is not on a form, and the speaker does not know that it is the sentence this whole business is built out of, and was not thanked and has gone on buying a part every fortnight.**

*What three weeks did, and what they did not do.* **Three weeks found out where the returns go, in a room in a second district, with a landing and a hasp that is not locked and four trays out of a kitchen. Three weeks did not find out whether the returns come, and did not count them, and decided on a Monday on a second floor in Crown Terrace not to count them, on a correct reason, given by a person entitled to give it. Three weeks lost one proposal and one customer who came four days and one job four hours of somebody's life that nobody has been paid for, and gained about four halves of an address, one of which is on a shirt sleeve. Three weeks did not open a drawer for any purpose other than looking at four sheets, and read none of them.** Nobody thanked anybody on any of the ten days. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped at any point in the ten days. The register of correct acts that altered nothing is closed at two across the three weeks, unmoved, and both of the two are the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, and neither has been improved on, neither has been thanked, and she does not know that a man drove four days in another volume to repeat what she said at a gate. The drawer is shut on nine of the ten days and was opened for about nine seconds on the fourth. The four words that name the public body are on none of the ten days. The book stands at sixty-one lines and the tin at seventy-three on all ten days, and the sitting after these three weeks is the forty-fifth.
