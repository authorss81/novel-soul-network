# Chapter 607 — A Friday On Which A One-Line Box Was Filled In On A First Floor In A Room He Was Not In And About Two Hundred Miles Of Nothing In Either Direction, And A Man Of About Thirty-One Who Came To A Counter With A Sheet And Could Not Fill In The Address Of The Place His Return Was Supposed To Go

**The Friday was a bench until two and a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was a man who could not fill in one line on a form because there was no room to write it in, and a woman four hours away filling in a different one-line box, and the two of those had nothing to do with each other.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday, all eleven dated, eleven people through the counter, the last of them at about half past five. The bench took him to two, the counter had him to about six, and the four rooms off the service road had him from about half past six. **A cook put a fortnight of her own money into that flat's heating and nobody owes it back to her, and four people on that tenancy are covering the bill between them, and it is costing more than the same flat cost through the winter.** Those four rooms are one thousand and eighty-two days, a hundred and fifty-four weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in that rail is one thousand and eighty-six days, a hundred and fifty-five weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The sixteenth line on the two nails is eight hundred and sixty-one days, a hundred and twenty-three weeks to the day, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is seven hundred and fifteen days, a hundred and two weeks and one day. One of those is an exact number of weeks and the other is not, and the first is a line on a board and the second is a length of time a man has been holding something in a yard he cannot hand over, and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city.**

---

**She filled the box in at about eleven on the Friday morning, in a first floor, in a room with a landing outside it, with a date in one box and a number in another and her name at the bottom, and the number was the ninth, and the man of twenty-two was at a bench in Lattice Ward at about eleven doing a job that did not need him.**

**The separation is four hundred and sixty-two days, sixty-six weeks to the day, and that figure is a subtraction and the subtraction has been correct every day of this book and every day of the four volumes before it, and it is ended, and the ending was a box about the size of a thumbnail and took about eleven seconds and was four days and about four hours from that bench.**

**He was not told on the Friday. He was not told on the Saturday, because there is no chapter on the Saturday and there is no shop in this city that opens on a Sunday and he does not go to a building in a second district on a Sunday. He was told on the Monday of the week after, in a queue, by a person who was not looking at him, in about two seconds, and it is in that chapter and not in this one.**

**And the figure on this Friday is still four hundred and sixty-two days and it is walked on this Friday and it is walked on the nine days after it and it is walked on every day that follows this one, and it is not stopped, and the reason it is not stopped is this: a series that is not printed when the thing it counts has stopped is a series that has been given a favour, and this city does not give figures favours. The number is a subtraction and the subtraction does not know what it is subtracting from and it will go on being right until the day somebody changes the anchor, and the anchor is four hundred and eighty-two days back from a Wednesday in an earlier volume and nobody has moved it.**

---

**At about ten past four on the Friday a man of about thirty-one put a sheet on that counter and it was the same sheet and it was about the size of a docket and there was a name on it and a date and two boxes on the back of it and both of them empty.**

**"I need the return address on this,"** he said. **"I've got to post it back tonight and I can't fill it in."**

**"Where does it go."**

**"That's what I'm asking."**

He turned the sheet round. The top of it had a heading and under the heading there were two lines, one of which was for a name and one of which was for a place to send it.

**"Who gave you the form."**

**"A man at a counter in a first floor over a line, about six weeks ago. He said fill it in when something goes wrong and send it back."**

**"Has anything gone wrong."**

**"No."** The man of about thirty-one said it in the tone of a person who had been asked that in about nine different shops. **"I want to know where it goes before it does."**

---

**He asked the three questions that are available and there was not a fourth.**

**"Is there a room on it anywhere."** No. **"Is there a name of a person on it anywhere."** No. **"Does it say what the form is for."** It did not. **It said, in the four words that were printed on every one of these, what the four words were, and the four words were a description of a piece of paper and not an address to anything.**

**So he wrote one in.**

He took a sheet off the wall list sleeve and he wrote on the back of the man's form, in pencil, not ink, the address of a first floor in a second district with a landing outside it and about nine names on a pad on the door, **and he did not write Gwen Ashby's name, because she would then be the address, and the day somebody's name is on a return is the day that person cannot say no, and he worked that out in about four seconds standing at a counter with a biro in his hand.**

**"That's not the office."**

**"It isn't anything. It's a room with a landing and a door and a man who comes up it,"** he said. **"If it goes there and it can't be read by somebody standing in that room, it goes in a tray, and at least it will have gone somewhere. That's more than the blank."**

**"And if somebody in that room reads it."**

**"Then somebody in that room reads it,"** he said, **"and that is the only version of this that has ever been available, and you have just found out about it in about four minutes and I have been finding out about it for about two weeks."**

---

**He wrote it in pencil and he did not know why he wrote it in pencil, and the not-knowing is on this page and is not a trick, and the reason is this: pencil can be corrected, and a return that is written in something that can be corrected is a return that expects to be corrected, and a person who expects to be corrected is not a person who has been given a place.**

The man of about thirty-one took the sheet and did not thank him. **"Right,"** he said, and went out, and it was still in his hand at the door, and he looked at the back of it once before he went through it and did not say anything about what was written there.

**That is the whole of what this Friday did. A room in a second district now has one more thing in it that is going to a place, and the man of twenty-two does not know whether that is a good thing, and on a Thursday some weeks from now he will still not know, and he is not going to be told, and the not-knowing is not going to be a chapter.**

---

Four rooms off that service road from about half past six. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall. **The chair by that door is hers on a Friday evening. She was not spoken to and she was not asked anything, her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf, the binder was not opened, and nobody has ever apologised to her for any of it.**

**Correct acts that changed nothing remain at two, and the Friday is not one of them.** Writing a room on the back of another person's form in pencil in about eleven seconds is not a correct thing that changed nothing, because it changes everything for one man and it changes it wrong in a way that may take four days to find out and may then be nothing, and the man who wrote it is owed nothing and is owed a great deal that nobody has offered him. **The two on that register are still the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns.**

There was no thanking on that counter on the Friday. The shutter was down at about ten. Shops in this city do not trade on Sundays and this week contains no Sunday.

**The remainder is a shape and four figures and not a scene, and it is written out here once. The man of twenty-two can do, on this ninth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off him. The card in the rail is one thousand and eighty-six days in a rail that holds two and it did not move. Nineteen lines on two nails and no twentieth. The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and forty-eight days, ninety-two weeks and four days, eight of the nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and no two of the nine compared. The post at the corridor end is six hundred and thirty days, ninety weeks to the day. The separation is four hundred and sixty-two days, sixty-six weeks to the day, and it was ended at about eleven on the Friday morning in a first floor in a room he was not in, by a person who filled in a box and a date and a signature, and the figure above has not changed and will not change on any of the forty days after this one. The drawer is closed. It holds four sheets. Nobody opened it. The four words naming the public body do not appear in this file. Under a floor in the first of the four towns is a plate of iron with four slots, and on the Friday it stayed there, unnamed, unlifted and unmentioned.**

*610. Friday of week 222, at ten, and this entry is a bench, then a counter, then a service road, at two, at six, and at about half past six, over eleven dated jobs and eleven customers with the last at about half past five, one form sent back with a blank where a place should be, three questions asked and one sheet written on in pencil, one man who said right and went out and looked at the back of it once, one box filled in on a first floor at about eleven by a person who was not speaking to him about it, and nobody thanked at any point in it.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Friday held eleven jobs, a date on each of them, and eleven customers, the last of them at about half past five.** Three parts to the day and no fourth: bench to two, counter two to six, the service-road rooms from about half past six onward. There is, behind that counter, and nobody has ever rearranged any of it: a bench; a cage with a man in it, turned to the wall; a roller shutter; a tool roll; a wall list in a sleeve; a shelf where the finished work is; and a rail for two. Callers: eleven. Refusals: four, of which one was a request to be told where a form goes. Work: eleven, four declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: ten pounds and then twelve, nothing out. The heating in those rooms: running, above the winter's setting, carried by four names, with a cook a fortnight of her own wages out of pocket and owed for it by nobody. The rooms: one thousand and eighty-two days, a hundred and fifty-four weeks and four days, warm. Rail card: one thousand and eighty-six days, a hundred and fifty-five weeks and one day, four days past the room, rail of two. The board on two nails: twelfth, one thousand and two; thirteenth, nine hundred and fifty-three; fourteenth, nine hundred and eighteen; fifteenth, eight hundred and ninety-seven; **sixteenth, eight hundred and sixty-one, a hundred and twenty-three weeks to the day**; seventeenth, eight hundred and fifty-four; eighteenth, eight hundred; nineteenth, seven hundred and seventy-eight, a hundred and eleven weeks and one day. Added to that board: nothing. The post at the corridor end: six hundred and thirty days, ninety weeks to the day. The nine hand copies: six hundred and forty-eight days, ninety-two weeks and four days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, no two compared. The hold in another city: seven hundred and fifteen days, a hundred and two weeks and one day. The man of about fifty-one at the wall: six hundred and eighty-eight days, ninety-eight weeks and two days. The ask: seven hundred and seventy-two days, one hundred and ten weeks and two days. **The separation: four hundred and sixty-two days, sixty-six weeks to the day, ended at about eleven on this Friday morning in a one-line box on a first floor in a room the man of twenty-two was not in, and the figure is not altered by the ending and is walked on the forty days that follow it and on the nine days that precede it.**

*Four minutes, three questions, and a name that was not written down.* **What came to the counter: a sheet off the back of a form, with a man's name and a date on it and two boxes on the back of it, both empty, and no address anywhere on it, and the form itself saying four words that describe a piece of paper. What was asked: where it goes. What was available to answer with: three questions. Whether the form names a room — it does not. Whether it names a person — it does not. Whether it says what it is for — it does not. What was done: an address was written on the back of it in pencil, being a first floor in a second district with a landing and about nine names on a pad on the door, and the name of the woman who is in that room was deliberately not written, in about four seconds of standing at a counter, and the reason is in the body: a person's name on a return is the day that person cannot say no. What was said to the man: that it is not the office, that it is a room with a landing and a door, that if it cannot be read by somebody standing in that room it will go in a tray, and that at least it will have gone somewhere, which is more than a blank. What the man asked back: what if somebody in that room reads it, and the answer, which is true and which is the whole of the Thursday: then somebody in that room reads it, and that is the only version of this that has ever been available. Why pencil, and the man did not know: pencil can be corrected, and a return written in something that can be corrected expects to be corrected, and a person who expects to be corrected is not a person who has been given a place. What the man did: said right, went out, and looked at the back of it once at the door and said nothing. Who was thanked: nobody, on either side of that counter, or in a first floor in a second district at about eleven, or in four rooms off a service road.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A separation of four hundred and sixty-two days was ended in a box about the size of a thumbnail by a person who was not speaking to him about it, and a man of twenty-two spent the same morning not knowing, and about four hours away a man of thirty-one walked out of a shop with a pencil address on the back of a form and neither of those two facts has anything to do with the other one.** **  Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts that altered nothing is closed at two, and a pencil address written in eleven seconds is not a third instance, and the two on it are still the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns. The rail card is one thousand and eighty-six days and stood in a rail that takes two, and a return sheet, a form with a blank, and a card in a rail are three different objects and no sentence on these pages joins any two of them. The rooms are one thousand and eighty-two days and warm. Nineteen lines on two nails and no twentieth. Four sheets are in that drawer and the drawer is shut, and the fourth one remains unread. Not one word from the man against that wall, and not one sheet of paper from anybody. Seven o'clock, four rooms, the woman of about thirty in the door chair, silence, and the eighth of eight in a ring binder unread on the shelf where it is always unread. The four words that name the public body are in none of this Friday and are not alluded to. No sitting fell on this day and no count was announced.**
