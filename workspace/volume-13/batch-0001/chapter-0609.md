# Chapter 609 — A Wednesday, Eleven People Through A Counter, About Four Of Whom Were Asked One Question And Answered It And About Four Hundred In This City Who Each Know Half Of One Address, And A Man Of About Twenty-Nine Who Offered To Type The Four Halves Into One List And Was Refused In About Nine Seconds For A Reason That Came Out Of The Same Room He Had Been Standing In

**The Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was one question asked about four times to about four people who did not know they were being asked it, and one refusal of about nine seconds, and a screwdriver in the wrong coat.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, all eleven dated, eleven people through the counter, the last of them at about ten past five. Bench until two, the counter from two until six, and the service-road rooms from about half past six, in that order and no other. **The heat in that flat is on and is the third month it has been on above the winter's setting, four names on that one tenancy are carrying it, and a fortnight of a cook's own money is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** Those four rooms are one thousand and eighty-seven days, a hundred and fifty-five weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in that rail is one thousand and ninety-one days, a hundred and fifty-five weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two and has held two on every day of the three weeks.

**The nineteenth line on the two nails is seven hundred and eighty-three days, a hundred and eleven weeks and six days, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is seven hundred and twenty days, a hundred and two weeks and six days. Both of those two figures end on the word *days* and neither of them is an exact number of weeks, and one of them is a line on a board and one of them is a length of time, and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and this entry gives that heating no days at all.**

---

**He asked the question four times on the Wednesday, in about nine seconds each, and the question was: who do you send things back to.**

He asked it the way you ask a person who is waiting for a kettle. **He did not say why. He did not say the word *return*. He did not say that a room in a second district has four trays in it.**

**A man of about fifty-two who buys a part every fortnight: *I don't know, it's a room number, I've never seen the room.* A woman of about twenty-seven with a bag of a boiler part: *I sent it to a place in Ashfields and I don't know anybody there.* A man of about forty-one with a torch: *Somebody wrote it on the form. I wouldn't have remembered it otherwise.* A woman of about sixty-three who came in for a key and who was not in a hurry: *There's a woman sorts them. About four weeks. I had a thing back once that I never sent.**

**Four halves, and not one of them wrong, and about four hundred people in this city who each have one, and the two people who had more than half between them gave him the same half twice from different sources in different words, which is what a correct answer does.**

He wrote the four on four pieces of paper, one to each, in pencil, and he did not read any of them back to the person who had said it, and he did not tell any of the four that he was writing it down.

---

**The man of about twenty-nine who types for a firm said it at about half past four, on his way out, and he was not a customer and he had been in the shop for about nine minutes.**

**"You've got four halves."**

He had been in the shop for about nine minutes and had been at the end of the counter the whole time, which is a thing this counter is good at.

**"You've got four halves and you could type them into a list in about ten minutes on my machine and I would do it tonight, and you would have the whole of it by tomorrow, and it would be a clean document."**

**"No."**

**"You don't know whether they're right."**

**"I know they're right. That's the problem."**

---

**"Say the problem, then, because I have got about nine minutes and I would rather help."**

**He said it in about nine seconds, and it is the whole of what the Wednesday is standing on, and it came out of a room in a second district with a hasp on a cupboard that is not locked, and he did not tell the man where it came from and did not say that a woman of about sixty-eight had said it.**

**"If it's on one sheet, it's a return that can't be read by somebody standing in the room it names. There is no room it names. It names about four halves and a building and about nine names on a door, and a person could stand in that building and not be able to read your one sheet without me, and if they can't read it, it's a notice, and notices go in a tray, and your four sheets and my four halves and that man's trade counter all become the same piece of paper, and the day they do that is the day nobody can get anything back to anybody at all."**

**"You could put an address on it."**

**"Then it's one address again and I'm the one holding it, and in about a month a person with a bag comes up a stair and asks for it, and there is no way for me to say no to him, and that is the thing I am not going to do."**

The man of about twenty-nine stood in that shop for about nine seconds afterwards and then said *right* and went out, and he has not offered again, and he was right to offer and the offer is not going to be recorded anywhere except in these pages.

---

**The small thing he gave up, and it is on no form and about four people in that shop noticed it and none of them said anything: the four pieces of paper went into the fourth pocket of the tool roll, which is the pocket the screwdriver is supposed to be in, and the screwdriver came out and went into the side of his coat, and the coat is not the one he came in, and on the Thursday the coat is at a house he is not going to be at until about nine in the evening, and the four pieces of paper are in it.**

**He will wash two shirts that evening. One of the four halves is on the sleeve of a shirt that is not his, because the man of about fifty-two wrote it on the back of his own hand and then put his hand on the counter for about four seconds while a woman of about thirty-four took the money, and he washed it, and the pencil has come off most of it and not all of it, and about half of a building is now written on a shirt sleeve in a flat where a cook is owed a fortnight by nobody.**

He could have asked him to write it again. He did not, because asking a man to write an address twice is asking him twice whether he is sure, and he was sure, and the certainty was the only part that was any good.

---

Four rooms off that service road from about half past six. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall. **On that Wednesday the chair nearest the door held a woman of about thirty and the whole of what happened to her was that the evening went past. Nobody spoke to her, nobody asked her a thing, and the page with her name on it, the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf, stayed where it has always stayed.**

**The register of right acts with no effect stays where it was, at two, and the Wednesday leaves it there.** Four people answering a question they did not know they were being asked, and about four hundred of them doing the same thing in about four hundred rooms in this city, is not a correct thing that changed nothing: it is a supply, and a supply is not an act, and the two on that register are both a woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns doing something she chose on a day about four years ago. **And the woman of about thirty-one who keeps the supply room of a school is not on this Wednesday and was not found, and he did not look for her, and that is the second cost he is carrying and has not told anybody about either of the two.**

Say thank you to nobody at that counter: not on the Wednesday, not at about ten when the shutter came down, and not on any of the working days, and a shop in this city is shut on a Sunday and this is not one.

**What is left below is a form and a column of figures rather than a scene, and it is given once. The man of twenty-two can do, on this ninth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off him, and about four hundred people knowing a half each is not a power and is not a network. The card in the rail is one thousand and ninety-one days in a rail that holds two and it did not move. Nineteen lines on two nails and no twentieth. The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and fifty-three days, ninety-three weeks and two days, eight of the nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and no two of the nine compared. The post at the corridor end is six hundred and thirty-five days, ninety weeks and five days. The separation is four hundred and sixty-seven days, sixty-six weeks and five days, and it is not shorter, and it was ended in a form four days ago, and the figure is not stopped and is not to be stopped. The drawer was closed on the Wednesday with its four sheets still in it. No four-word name for the public body is in this file. There is a plate of iron with four slots beneath a floor in the first of the four towns, and the Wednesday neither lifted it nor named it nor sent it anywhere. The two sheets with a true sentence on them that he made in a doorway in another volume are in a drawer in his own room and he has told nobody he made them.**

*612. Wednesday of week 223, at ten, and this entry is a bench until two, that counter from two to six, the four service-road rooms after half past six, and eleven dated jobs with eleven people over the counter, the last at ten past five, one question asked four times in about nine seconds each to four people who did not know they were being asked it, four halves written in pencil on four pieces of paper, one offer of a machine and one refusal of about nine seconds, a screwdriver in a coat and four halves in a tool roll, and nobody thanked at any point in it.*

*Four halves, and the sentence that came out of a landing in a second district.* **What was asked, four times, in about nine seconds each, to four people who were not told why: who do you send things back to. What was given: a room number nobody has seen; a place in a town; something written on a form by somebody else; and a woman who sorts them, who takes about four weeks, and a thing that came back once that had never been sent. Whether the four answers agree with each other: they do not, and every one of them is correct. What was written: four pieces of paper, one answer to each, in pencil, none of them read back. The offer, at about half past four, by a man of about twenty-nine who types for a firm: to type the four halves into one clean list in about ten minutes, delivered the next morning. The refusal, in about nine seconds: a single sheet names no room, so it is a return that cannot be read by a person standing in the room it names, and an unreadable return is a notice, and a notice goes in a tray, and the four sheets and the four halves and every trade counter in this city become one piece of paper on the day that happens. The second refusal inside it: adding an address would make one address again, and the man holding it would be the man a person with a bag comes up a stair to ask, and he would not be able to say no. Where the sentence came from: a room in a second district, a woman of about sixty-eight, a landing, a Friday, and about nine seconds, and the man of twenty-nine was not told any of that and has not been told any of that. What went where: four pieces of paper into the fourth pocket of a tool roll and a screwdriver into a coat that is not the one he came in, and about half a building on a shirt sleeve that is not his, washed, with the pencil mostly off.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A man of twenty-two asked about four hundred people's worth of one question four times and got four right answers and would not put them together, and refused a clean list in about nine seconds using a rule he did not learn from a book and did not explain the source of.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts that altered nothing is closed at two, and a supply is not an act, and the two on it are still the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns. A return sheet, a form, a card in a rail and a piece of paper with four halves of an address on it are four different objects and no sentence on these pages joins any two of them. Nobody opened the drawer on the Wednesday. There are four sheets in it and the fourth has never been read. Sixty-one lines in the book and seventy-three in the tin, and neither figure is one that was announced. The four words that name the public body are in none of these pages, and the number of returns in four trays is in none of these pages, and the woman of about thirty-one with the school supply room has not been found.
