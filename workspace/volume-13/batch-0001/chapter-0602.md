# Chapter 602 — A Thursday, A Woman Of About Thirty-One Who Keeps The Supply Room Of A School And Who Came In Without A Machine, Nine Seconds Of True, And A Refusal Of About Nine Seconds That Was Correct And Cost Her About Nine Weeks She Had Already Spent

**The Thursday was a bench until two and a counter from two until six and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was a person who had not brought anything to be fixed and wanted to be told why a thing she had sent back in the spring had not been answered, and one sentence that was true, and one that was not given.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, all eleven dated, eleven people through the counter, the last of them at about half past five. A bench until two, the counter from two until six, and from about half past six the four rooms off that service road. **That flat is being heated again and at a setting above the one it had through the winter, and four names on that tenancy are paying for the difference between the two, and a cook is still out of pocket by a fortnight of her own money, which is not going to be paid back to her before the month turns.** Those four rooms are one thousand and seventy-four days, one hundred and fifty-three weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in that rail is one thousand and seventy-eight days, a hundred and fifty-four weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two and had two in it.

**The ask is seven hundred and sixty-four days, one hundred and nine weeks and one day, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is six hundred and eighty days, ninety-seven weeks and one day. Seven hundred and sixty-four is the day the ask was put to a person and not to an institution. Six hundred and eighty is an age in days and nothing else, and it is the age of a man who has been sitting against a wall since a Wednesday in a season nobody in that room is going to name. Neither figure has anything to do with that heating, and this entry gives the heating no days at all.**

---

**She came in at about four and she had not brought anything with her, and that is the first thing about her, and the woman of about thirty-four at that counter noticed it before he did and did not say so.**

She is about thirty-one. She keeps the supply room of a school and she had a canvas bag with her and the bag had nothing in it that he could see. She was not angry. **That is the thing about her that he noticed and wrote down afterwards and did not say to anybody: she was not angry, and she had clearly rehearsed it, and the rehearsing had taken the anger out of it and left the rest.**

**"I sent one of these back in the spring,"** she said, and she put a folded sheet on the counter, and he unfolded it, and it was the same size as the one from the Monday, and it had the same two boxes on the back of it and one of them was ticked. **"They have told me twice that it was received. I would like to know what happened to it."**

He looked at the front of it. It did not have three words in pencil on it. It had a date, and it had a room, and the room was a building in a first district and the number was a number, and there was no way on that sheet to tell whether the room was still a room.

**"Your return has to arrive somewhere,"** he said. **"If the place it names is not a place any more, the sheet does not know that, and nothing in the building it went to was going to say so to you, because nobody in it has done anything wrong."**

**"So somebody has my sheet,"** she said.

**"Somebody has your sheet. That is a different thing from somebody has read it, and I cannot tell you which of those two you have, and neither can the firm, and the reason I cannot tell you is that nobody has written down anywhere what happens to a sheet after it gets there, and I have looked."**

---

**"What do I do,"** she said, and that was the whole of it, and it took about a second and a half.

**He did not answer it, and the not-answering took him about four seconds, and the reason was correct and he did not give it to her, and the reason is printed here because a refusal with no reason on the page is a trick and this one is not a trick.**

A person who is told what to do by a man at a counter is a person whose sheet goes to that counter. **She had come four days on a bus to be told something by somebody, and if he told her what to do she would do it, and then in about a month there would be a woman in a first district doing the correct thing that a man in Lattice Ward told her to do, and the thing would work, and the working of it would be indistinguishable from the thing that is going on here, and the only difference between them would be that this one has a face.**

**"I can only tell you what I know,"** he said, **"and what I know stops at the door, and I am not going to stand in a doorway and tell a person to go and do something with it, because you have come four days and you will go back four days, and whatever I say you will have done once and told somebody you did it because I said so."**

**"That is a yes with the yes taken out of it,"** she said.

**"That is what it is,"** he said, **"and I am not going to improve it."**

---

**She folded the sheet back up and put it in the bag and said thank you to the woman of about thirty-four, who had not said anything at all and who was not thanked by him for saying nothing.**

The cost of that Thursday is not his and he is the only person in the building who could have made it his, and it is this: **she had nine weeks of letters already sent and nine weeks of being told it was received, and she went back on the bus with one true sentence she had not had in the spring, and the true sentence is worth exactly nothing to anybody and she is the one who has to carry it four days.** About nine people on wages at a firm in the second of the four towns are on the wrong side of him being right and were not asked and have not been told.

**The small thing he gave up, which is on no form: there is a column on that day sheet headed for a customer's name and a fault, and he had the pen out, and he did not write her name, and the line stayed empty, and the day sheet went on the wall with that line empty on it, and he is not going to find her again and he wanted to.** He will want to for about nine months.

---

Four rooms off that service road from about half past six. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall. **The woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not spoken to and was not asked anything and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf and it was not read on the Thursday and nobody apologised to her and the binder was not opened.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing stands at two and is not moved by this Thursday.** A refusal given in about four seconds, for a correct reason, to a person who came four days for it, is not a correct thing that changed nothing: **it changed what one woman knows, it cost her nothing that anybody is paying for, and it will cost her a bus fare she has already paid twice.** The two instances on the register are both a woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns and both are acts she was already doing, and neither of them was done to a person in the room.

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter and the shutter came down at about ten, and there is no Sunday in this day and no shop in this city opens on one.

**And the rest of it is a figure and a shape rather than a scene. The man of twenty-two can do, on this second day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week of the book, and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off him. The card in the rail is one thousand and seventy-eight days in a rail that holds two and it did not move. The board carries nineteen lines and no twentieth has ever been started. The ninth line of that board, which is not one of the eight, is the only line that anybody has ever added a figure to and the figure is a date, and the date is not printed in this entry because it is not a day anything happened to the heating. The nine hand copies are six hundred and forty days, ninety-one weeks and three days, eight of the nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and no two of the nine compared. The post at the corridor end is six hundred and twenty-two days, eighty-eight weeks and six days. The separation is four hundred and fifty-four days, sixty-four weeks and six days, and it is not shorter and it is not ended, and the woman who filled in nothing today is four days away and neither of them has said a word about the other one in the third person on any of these pages. The drawer is shut and holds four sheets and was not opened. The two sheets with a true sentence on them that he made in a doorway in another volume are in his coat and he has told nobody he made them.**

*605. Thursday of week 221, at ten, and this entry is a bench until two, a counter until six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, eleven jobs dated and eleven people through the counter and the last of them at about half past five, one person who came in without a machine and with a bag that had nothing in it, one true sentence of about nine seconds, one refusal of about four seconds with a reason that was not given to her and is printed here, one empty line on a day sheet where a name goes, and nobody thanked at any point in it.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Thursday held eleven jobs, a date on each of them, and eleven customers, the last of them at about half past five, and one visitor who was not a customer and was not counted as one.** How the day was laid out: the bench first, to two o'clock; the counter, two to six; and after that the service-road rooms, from about half past six. Along that counter from the working end: a bench, the cage with a man in it facing the wall, the roller shutter, the tool roll and its fourth pocket, a wall list in a sleeve, a shelf of finished work, a rail for two, and a day sheet with one empty line on it. Callers: eleven, plus one. Refusals: four, of which one was a request for an answer and not a repair. Work: eleven, four declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: nine pounds and then eleven, nothing out. Heating in that flat: on, and up from the winter's, and four names on the tenancy meeting it, and a cook a fortnight out of pocket. The rooms: one thousand and seventy-four days, one hundred and fifty-three weeks and three days, warm. Rail card: one thousand and seventy-eight days, a hundred and fifty-four weeks to the day, rail of two. The board on two nails: twelfth, nine hundred and ninety-four; thirteenth, nine hundred and forty-five; fourteenth, nine hundred and ten; fifteenth, eight hundred and eighty-nine; sixteenth, eight hundred and sixty-four; seventeenth, eight hundred and forty-six; eighteenth, seven hundred and ninety-two; **nineteenth, seven hundred and seventy, a hundred and ten weeks to the day**. Added to that board: nothing. The post at the corridor end: six hundred and twenty-two days, eighty-eight weeks and six days. The nine hand copies: six hundred and forty days, ninety-one weeks and three days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and no two compared. The hold in another city: seven hundred and seven days, a hundred and one weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one at the wall: six hundred and eighty days, ninety-seven weeks and one day. The ask: seven hundred and sixty-four days, one hundred and nine weeks and one day. The separation: four hundred and fifty-four days, sixty-four weeks and six days.

*Nine weeks, four days each way, and a sentence that is worth nothing.* **Who came: a woman of about thirty-one who keeps the supply room of a school, with a canvas bag, with nothing in it that could be seen, at about four on a Thursday. What she wanted: to know what had become of a sheet she had sent back in the spring, having been told twice that it was received. What was true and said out loud in about nine seconds: that a return has to arrive somewhere; that if the place it names is not a place any more, the sheet does not know that; that nothing in the building it went to was going to say so to her, because nobody in it had done anything wrong. What was not given: what to do about it. The refusal, about four seconds, and the reason, which is correct and which was not said to her: a person told what to do by a man at a counter is a person whose sheet goes to that counter, and if he had told her she would have done it and told somebody she had done it because he said so, and the only difference between that and the thing that is going on here would have been that this one has a face. What she said to it: that it was a yes with the yes taken out of it, which is true. Who carried the cost: her, and it is about nine weeks already spent and a bus each way, and nobody in this city is paying any of it. What was left on the counter: a folded sheet, in a bag, taken away. What was left on the paper: nothing, because the name line on the day sheet was left empty on purpose. The two figures: the ask at seven hundred and sixty-four days, one hundred and nine weeks and one day, and the man of about fifty-one at six hundred and eighty days, ninety-seven weeks and one day; the first is a day something happened, being the day the ask was put to a person, and the second is an age in days and is nothing else, and neither is a day anything happened to the heating in this city. Who was thanked: the woman of about thirty-four for saying nothing was not thanked by him, and he was not thanked for the sentence, and neither of them thanked the other.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A person came four days to be told a thing and was told it and was not told what to do about it, and a man of twenty-two let her leave with that, and the whole of what he did on that Thursday was not put a name in a column.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts that altered nothing is closed at two, and a refusal given for a correct reason to a person who had to carry it home is not a third instance, and each of the two is a woman of about fifty-two doing something she was already doing. A return sheet is a different object from the card in the rail and did not go into the rail and did not go into the sleeve. The drawer is shut with four sheets and was not opened and the fourth of them has still not been looked at by anybody. The book stands at sixty-one lines and the tin at seventy-three, and neither of those two figures moved that day.
