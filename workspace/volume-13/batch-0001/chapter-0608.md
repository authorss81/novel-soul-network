# Chapter 608 — A Monday, A Second Floor In Crown Terrace With About Nine Chairs In It And About Nine People In It And One Chair At The End Of The Table That Was Empty And Nobody Said Why, And Nine Seconds In Which It Was Said Out Loud That Nobody Is Going To Count Them

**The Monday was a bench until two, a counter from two until about four, a second floor in Crown Terrace from about four until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was about nine people deciding not to do the one thing they had come to do, in about nine seconds, and being right.**

---

Eleven dated jobs again on the Monday, eleven customers through the counter, and nobody after about ten past five. The bench until two, that counter two until about four, a second floor in Crown Terrace from about four until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and the meter in that building has been read every month since the spring, four names on one tenancy are carrying it, and a fortnight of a cook's own money is owed by nobody and remains owed by nobody.** Those four rooms are one thousand and eighty-five days, a hundred and fifty-five weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in that rail is one thousand and eighty-nine days, a hundred and fifty-five weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The eighteenth line on the two nails is eight hundred and three days, one hundred and fourteen weeks and five days, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is six hundred and ninety-one days, ninety-eight weeks and five days. The first is a line on a board and the second is a man against a wall, and one of the two numbers is a day something happened and the other is not a day anything happened to anything, and both of them are in this entry because this Monday is the fourth Monday this shop has carried since the spring and every Monday carries them.**

---

**The second floor in Crown Terrace has about nine chairs in it and a table and a window that faces the back of a building. It has been used for four years on Fridays, once on a Wednesday last month, and on Mondays three times, and on this Monday about nine people came.**

The chair at the end of that table was empty and nobody said why. **The man of about thirty-three who drives for a firm and has nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over was in the fourth chair. A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard was in the seventh. A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm four days of bus travel away was in the second, and had come in on the Sunday night, and had not been asked why she came on the Sunday night and did not say.** A man of about thirty-one was in the sixth. Nobody looked at the empty chair more than once.

**"Right,"** the man of about sixty-one said, at about ten past four. **"We're here about the number. Nobody's counted them."**

**"No."**

**"Why not."**

**"Because you can't,"** he said, **"and I've been asking, and the answer is the same from everybody, and the answer is that the trays belong to a woman who is not paid and who does not want to be asked, and that is not a legal objection, it is just what she has said, and she said it to me on a Friday and she said it twice."**

**"So we go in there and we count them."**

**"No."**

---

**The woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs came in at about half past four and sat down and did not have a sheet put in front of her.**

She chairs about nine chairs for a body of about nine and about a hundred and forty. **She is not a member, she is not paid, and she is the only person in that room entitled to say the second of the two things that were said on that floor, and about four of the nine people in that room had known before about half past four that she was going to say something and about five had not.**

**"You are not going to count them,"** she said, **"and you are right, and I am going to say the reason so that none of you spends the next fortnight working out that you have persuaded me."**

Nobody said anything. A window was doing what windows do.

**"If you count them, you have to open the trays, and the trays are hers, and she has not been asked and has said no, and a no from a person who is not paid and owns nothing is not a preference. It is a wall. And on the day you open them you will find out how many there are and every one of you will be pleased, and about four of you will then say *we should have known sooner* in a room in about four months, and she will still be the woman who said no, and she will still be doing it, and you will have taken her work off her and given it back to her inside one day."**

**"That's not what would happen."**

**"That is exactly what would happen,"** she said, **"and I have watched it happen about four times to about four other people in about nine years and I am not going to watch it happen to a woman who sorts paper into four trays for nothing in a room with a hasp that is not locked."**

---

**About nine seconds of nothing, and he was in it.**

**He wanted to be the one who counted them.** It is the plainest thing he has wanted in about four volumes and it is not a wish to be important and it is not a wish to be the man everybody goes to: **he wants to be the man who is allowed to open a cupboard, because a man who is allowed to open a cupboard is a man who is trusted with what is in it, and he has spent thirteen volumes being trusted with what is in rooms and has never once been the person who was allowed to look.**

**He did not offer. He said nothing, for about nine seconds, in a room of about nine people, and at about the eighth second a woman of about forty-four who had come in late looked at him to see whether he was going to say something, and he did not, and she went back to the sheet in front of her.**

**That is the small thing he gave up on the Monday, and it is on no form, and about four people in that room noticed it and about five did not, and one of the four was the man of about thirty-three, who wrote nothing down and has not repeated it and is not going to.**

---

**"Then what do we do,"** the man of about thirty-one said.

**"You go and find out what a return is,"** the woman of about fifty-two said, **"and not how many of them there are. There are a lot. There are going to be a lot more, because every place you have all been in for two years has just found out that it can send one, and none of them has anywhere to send it to, and that is the part that is going to get worse and it is not going to get worse faster because you counted."**

**"That's not an answer."**

**"No,"** she said. **"It is the reason. There is no answer. I have about nine chairs and I have not got an answer and I have never had one and I chair them anyway, and the four of you who are about to be disappointed are going to be disappointed on a Tuesday as well as on a Monday, and the sooner you get that into your heads the less of it you will waste."**

Nobody thanked her. Nobody agreed with her. The man of about thirty-one said *right* in a voice that meant something else, and the woman of about twenty-nine who types said nothing at all and has since said nothing at all.

---

Four rooms off that service road from about half past six. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall. **She is about thirty and she is in the chair by that door and nobody in that room says anything to her, ever; the eighth of eight pages in a ring binder on a shelf is hers, and the binder has not been opened in her presence on the Monday or on any day.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing stands at two and is not moved by this Monday, and the standing of the register is a harder case than most.** A room of about nine people deciding in about nine seconds not to do a thing that would have been useful, on a correct reason, given by a person entitled to give it, is not a correct thing done in about nine seconds: **it is a decision not to act, and a decision not to act is not an act, and the register is a register of acts.** The cost of it is on the page and is not on a form either: **nobody in that room can now say whether what they are about to do is safe, and that is the price of being right, and the woman who made them pay it knew that when she charged it to them.**

Nobody thanked anybody on that second floor or at that counter, and the shutter came down at about ten, and no shop in this city opens on a Sunday and no day of this week is one.

**The rest of it is set out as outline and figures, and not as anything a person did, and it is set out once. The man of twenty-two can do, on this eighth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off him, and saying nothing for about nine seconds in a room of about nine people made him no larger. The card in the rail is one thousand and eighty-nine days in a rail that holds two and it did not move. Nineteen lines on two nails and no twentieth. The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and fifty-one days, ninety-three weeks to the day, eight of the nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and no two of the nine compared. The post at the corridor end is six hundred and thirty-three days, ninety weeks and three days. The separation is four hundred and sixty-five days, sixty-six weeks and three days, and it is not shorter, and it was ended in a form on the Friday before this one, and the figure has not been stopped and will not be stopped. A shut drawer with four sheets in it, and the Monday did not open it. The name of the public body, in four words, is not in this page. The chair at the end of that table was empty and nobody said why and it is not said here either, and the mover of that chair is not named here and is not going to be named anywhere.**

*Nine seconds, about nine people, and a thing that is not going to be done.* **What the room came for: the number of returns in four trays in a room in a second district. What was established before anybody spoke: that nobody had counted them and that the reason nobody had counted them is not a legal objection and is not a policy and is a woman saying no to being asked. What was not said by the man of twenty-two, for about nine seconds, and what the not-saying cost: he wanted to be the man allowed to open a cupboard, and he did not offer, and a woman of about forty-four looked at him at about the eighth second to see whether he was going to, and he was not. What is now unknown and cannot be known this month: whether the thing the nine of them have started is safe, and nobody asked that question on the Monday and one of them has since said that it should have been asked first.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **About nine people did not do the one thing they came to do, on a correct reason, given by a person entitled to give it, and the cost of being right was charged to the room in about nine seconds and the man of twenty-two was the one who paid it, and it was nine seconds of not speaking.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.
