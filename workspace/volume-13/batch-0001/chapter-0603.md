# Chapter 603 — A Friday, Eleven Minutes On Foot And A Stair In A Second District, A Landing, A Cupboard With A Door On It, Four Trays, And A Woman Of About Sixty-Eight Named Gwen Ashby Who Has Been Doing It Since The Spring And Is Not Paid And Who Said The Whole Of It In About Nine Seconds

**The Friday was a bench until two, a counter from two until about half past three, a stair in a second district from about half past three until about four, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was four trays and a sentence about nine seconds long that nobody who has ever been in that room wrote down.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday, all eleven dated, eleven people through the counter, the last of them at about ten past four. The bench until two, the counter two until about half past three, a stair in a second district from about half past three, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and the bill for it has gone up twice since the spring, four names on that one tenancy are carrying it between them, and a fortnight of a cook's own money is still owed to nobody.** Those four rooms are one thousand and seventy-five days, one hundred and fifty-three weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in that rail is one thousand and seventy-nine days, a hundred and fifty-four weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The nineteenth line on the two nails is seven hundred and seventy-one days, a hundred and ten weeks and one day, and the ask is seven hundred and sixty-five days, one hundred and nine weeks and two days. One of those two numbers is the day the ask was put to a person and not to an institution and the other one is simply as old as a line on a board, and they are six days apart and they are in the same sentence, and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city.**

---

**The building is four streets and about eleven minutes from that counter and he went there at about half past three on a Friday, which is the hour he had not taken on the Monday, and which he took on the Friday because on the Friday the counter was quiet and the last customer came at about ten past four and he could not think of a reason that was better than the one the street was.**

There is a stair. It is not a good stair. On the half-landing there is a door with a pad on it and a pen on a string, and nobody came, and the pad had about nine names on a pad and eight of the nine had gone in years ago.

**The room is the first floor and it is two rooms and a landing, and the door that is not the door is a cupboard with a door on it, and the door on the cupboard has a hasp on it and the hasp is not locked, and there is no sign on the cupboard and there has never been a sign on the cupboard.**

Four trays. They are steel and they came from a kitchen and one of them has a chip out of the near corner that has been chipped since before anybody in this building came to it.

**"You are the fourth,"** she said, before he had said anything.

---

**She is about sixty-eight. She has been doing it since the spring. She is not paid and the rent of the room is paid by somebody else and has never been on her bill and the electricity is in the same arrangement. She was standing on the landing with a pair of scissors in her hand and a sheet of paper in the other one, and she was not surprised to see him, and she was not unpleased, and she did not put the scissors down.**

He said his name and she did not write it anywhere.

**"What is this room,"** he said.

**"It is where they bring them."**

**"Who brings them."**

**"A man with a bag, on a Monday."** She said it the way a person says the name of a bus. **"He has had it since he was thirty-one. He does not come up. He puts the bag on the step at the bottom and he goes, and I go down at about eleven and I bring it up, and on a Monday when he is late it is a Tuesday and I do not go down on a Tuesday, it sits on the step, and it has sat on the step four times."**

He looked at the trays. He did not ask how many sheets were in them. **She did not offer, and the not-asking was the first silence of this week that had a shape to it, and the shape was this: the number of sheets in those four trays is the number this whole volume is going to want, and it is in a room, and it belongs to a woman who is not paid.**

---

**"May I ask what the rule is,"** he said.

**"There is no rule."**

**"There is something you do."**

**"Yes."** And she said the rest of it, in about nine seconds, standing on a landing, and she said it once and she did not say it again and nobody in that room explained it afterwards.

**"If it cannot be read by somebody standing in this room, it is not a return. It is a notice. Notices go in the tray."**

He waited for the rest of it and there was no rest of it.

**"Who decided that."**

**"Nobody. It works."** She looked at the tray nearest the door and then at him. **"I have never asked. You can ask me anything you like and I will tell you the truth and most of it is that I do not know, and that one I do know and I have never seen it written down by anybody in any form."**

**"Including the part about what a room is."**

**"Especially the part about what a room is."**

---

He looked at the four trays. He asked what each of them was for, because that is a question about a job and not about a rule, and she told him: one for sheets with a date on them that are not yet due back, one for sheets that came back to a room that is not there, one for sheets where the name on them is a name of an organisation and not a person, and one that is older than the other three and that she did not describe and that she put her thumb on when she named it.

**He did not ask about the fourth tray. She had offered three of them and not the fourth, and he noticed, and he did not ask, and the not-asking is on this page and is not a trick and he does not know yet why he did it.**

---

**"Why are you doing it,"** he said.

**"It came in a bag on a Monday in the spring and there was nobody in the room."**

**"Who pays you."**

**"Nobody."** She said it the way she had said the name of a bus. **"It is a Tuesday in four weeks when the step gets a mat on it, and I will be here, and after that it is not mine and I do not know what it is and neither does he."**

He said the thing he had come to say, which was that there were about eleven people in a building in another district who could be asked, and she said, in about nine seconds, the true thing and then the other true thing.

**"You are the fourth person in four months. Two of the others wanted exactly what you want. Neither of them was the person who asked."**

**"Which person asked."**

**"I did."**

**"You asked."**

**"I asked in the spring and nobody came, and I have not asked since, and that is not a complaint, it is just what the asking is now."**

**He wanted to be told that he had been right to come. What he was told instead was that he was the fourth, and that two of the first three had been the sort of person who asks and stops, and that the reason he had come at all was that he had walked past the same door on a Monday morning for four months without going up the stair.**

---

Four rooms off that service road from about half past six. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall. **A woman of about thirty was in the chair by that door the whole evening. Nobody spoke to her, nobody asked her anything, nobody has ever apologised to her, and the page belonging to her is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf, which stayed shut and unread on the Friday.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing stands at two and is not moved by this Friday.** A woman being right about every sheet in front of her for about four months is not a third instance, because she has not done anything, she has continued, and a thing that continues is not a thing that is done. **Both of the two on that register are a woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns and both of them were acts she chose on a day, and this is a woman in a second district of this city who has not chosen anything since the spring and is being asked to stop.**

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter or on that landing and the shutter came down at about ten, and there is no Sunday in this day and no shop in this city opens on one.

**What follows is a shape and a set of figures rather than anything that happened, and it is set down once here. The man of twenty-two can do, on this fifth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off him, and the only thing that has happened in five days is that he has been told he is the fourth. The card in the rail is one thousand and seventy-nine days in a rail that takes two and it did not move. Nineteen lines stand on two nails and a twentieth has never been started. The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and forty-one days, ninety-one weeks and four days, eight of the nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and no two of the nine compared on the Friday or on any other day of this week. The post at the corridor end is six hundred and twenty-three days, eighty-nine weeks to the day. The separation is four hundred and fifty-five days, sixty-five weeks to the day, and it is not shorter and it is not ended. The drawer under that bench is shut and holds four sheets and was not opened. The number of sheets in four trays in a room in a second district is not printed in this entry, because it was not counted, because nobody counted it, and because the only person in this city who could count it is a woman of about sixty-eight who is not paid and was not asked.**
