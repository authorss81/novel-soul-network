# Chapter 604 — A Monday, Seven Hundred And Ninety-Six Days On A Board Above A Bench And Six Hundred And Forty-Four Days On A Shelf Beside It Which Are Each Other, A Drawer Under That Bench Opened For About Nine Seconds And Not Read, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Who Said Two Words In A Queue

**The Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was two numbers on the same wall that turn out to be each other, nine seconds with a drawer, and two words said in a queue by a person who did not know what she was saying.**

---

The Monday's work was eleven jobs, dated, and there were eleven callers at that counter, the final one a little after five. Until two it was the bench, from two to six the counter, and from about half past six the four rooms off that service road. **The heat in that flat is on, four names on that one tenancy are carrying it, and a fortnight a cook put in of her own money is owed by nobody and remains owed by nobody this month.** Those four rooms are one thousand and seventy-eight days, a hundred and fifty-four weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in that rail is one thousand and eighty-two days, a hundred and fifty-four weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The eighteenth line on the two nails above that bench is seven hundred and ninety-six days, a hundred and thirteen weeks and five days, and the nine hand copies of the front of a page on a shelf at the end of the room are six hundred and forty-four days, ninety-two weeks to the day. Seven hundred and ninety-six is the day nine hand copies of the front of a page were begun. Six hundred and forty-four is the day the eighteenth line was written. One of those two numbers is an age and one of them is a day something happened, and they are on the same wall on the same Monday in the same room, and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and this entry gives that heating no days at any point.**

**And on that Monday nobody in that room compared two of the nine copies with any other of the nine, and eight of the nine are still unfinished, and the first disagreement in the fourth line is still not found, and those two facts are printed in the same paragraph as the two numbers because the two numbers are the ages of the page and not of the copies, and a person who has read a paragraph quickly will do what this paragraph is designed to stop.**

---

**The drawer under that bench has been shut since before anybody in this shop could remember, and it is not locked and it is not labelled, and it is not on anything, and on the Monday of the second week it was open for about nine seconds and nothing was taken out of it and nothing was put back into it and none of the four sheets inside it was read.**

It holds four sheets. One of them has the word NO on it in capitals and four lines under it, and the four lines have been folded in half and unfolded and folded in half again. The second is a docket for a bench in a shop in a second district that shut in the spring. The third is a page of a form with a box ticked and a room in it. The fourth is a sheet a man of about thirty-three brought on a road he was already on in another volume and put in through the hatch and did not explain and has not been asked about.

**He looked at the four of them. He did not read the four lines under the word NO, because they are somebody else's and they are four lines long and there was no reason on that Monday that made them his to read. He did not read the fourth one either, and that one is his, in the sense that it came into a shop he works in and nobody has ever picked it up, and the reason he did not read it is that it had been in that drawer since the last volume and there is no hurry in a drawer.**

He took a blank sheet off the top of the bench instead. He wrote the address off the Monday of the first week on the back of it in pencil, and he wrote it the way a person writes a thing down so that it can be found again, and he put it in the wall list sleeve, and the sleeve now holds five dockets and four of them are about repairs.

**The cost of this Monday is about nine seconds and a drawer, and there is no form for it, and nobody in the shop saw it happen, and nobody would have stopped him.**

---

**At about ten past four, with four people in the queue, the woman of about thirty-four at that counter said the following and she was serving a customer while she said it and it took about two seconds.**

**"She is back."**

He did not ask which she. **He did not ask which she and he did not say anything at all, and the two of them stood there for about nine seconds with four people in the queue, and then a customer took a part out of his coat and he went to the bench and looked at a job for about four minutes that did not need looking at.**

She is twenty-four. She came back on the Sunday. **The office has moved rooms, from a floor it had held for four years to two rooms on a first floor in a second district, and the moving was the reason, and the reason is a reason and not a rule, and it is in a letter that a person came into the shop at about eleven on the Monday to read out loud because he asked her to and she read it out loud in a corridor.**

**He did not go and find her on the Monday. That is not in this entry as a thing he decided at the time and it is in this entry as a thing he noticed at about six o'clock in four rooms off a service road, which is that a man who goes and finds somebody on the afternoon he hears she is in the city is a man rooms send their returns to, and the reason is the same reason as the one from the first Monday and he noticed that it was the same reason and he did not do anything about that either.**

---

**What he wanted on the Monday, and did not say, in a face, to about four people, was to be the man who found the room. About four people at that counter had worked it out on their own across the week — one of them is the woman of about thirty-four and she did not say she had worked it out, and one of them is a man of about fifty-two who has bought a part from that shop every fortnight for about six years and said, to nobody, in the middle of a queue, *you found that street then*, on the Thursday, and he did not correct him, and he has not told anybody that he did not.**

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter and the shutter came down at about ten, and no shop in this city opens on a Sunday and no day of this week is a Sunday.

**The register of correct things that changed nothing stands at two and is not moved by this Monday. Opening a drawer in a shop where you work for about nine seconds and reading none of what is in it is not a correct thing done in about nine seconds, because it changes no household in any of the four places and nobody is owed anything for it, and the man who opened it is owed a great deal that nobody has offered him.**

**The remainder of this entry is arithmetic and outline and not a scene, and it appears once. The man of twenty-two can do, on this sixth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off him, and a drawer being open in a shop is not a stage. The card in the rail is one thousand and eighty-two days in a rail that holds two and it did not move. Nineteen lines on two nails, no twentieth, and the eighteenth of them is seven hundred and ninety-six days, which is the day the nine hand copies were begun, and the two are not joined to each other on any of these pages. The post at the corridor end is six hundred and twenty-six days, eighty-nine weeks and three days. The separation is four hundred and fifty-eight days, sixty-five weeks and three days, and it is not shorter and it is not ended, and she came back on the Sunday and neither of them has said a word about the other one in the third person. The nine lines he has now been asked for five times did not get written a sixth time and are still on nothing at all. A plate of iron with four slots in it is still under the floor in the first of the four towns, was not lifted, and has not been called anything by any of the four people who found it. Those four words, the ones that name the public body, are in none of this file.**

*607. Monday of week 222, at ten, and this entry runs, in that order, a bench to two, a counter to six, and the four service-road rooms from about half past six, over eleven dated jobs and eleven customers, the last at about ten past five, two numbers on one wall that are each other, a drawer opened for about nine seconds with four sheets in it of which none was read, five dockets in a sleeve where there were four, two words in a queue at about ten past four, a letter read out loud in a corridor, and nobody thanked at any point in it.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven jobs on the Monday, every one of them dated, and eleven people over the counter, the final one arriving at about ten past five.** The shape of the day, in order: bench, counter, service road. The bench ran to two, the counter from two to six, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. What stands behind that counter: first the bench, then a cage with a man sitting in it looking at the wall, then the shutter, then the tool roll and its fourth pocket and the screwdriver that belongs there, then a list on the wall in a sleeve, a shelf where finished work waits to be collected, and a rail with room for two. Callers: eleven. Refusals: three. Work: eleven, three declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: twelve pounds, nothing out. The heating in that flat: on, above the winter's, four names carrying the difference, a fortnight of a cook's own wages owed by nobody. The rooms: one thousand and seventy-eight days, a hundred and fifty-four weeks to the day, warm. Rail card: one thousand and eighty-two days, a hundred and fifty-four weeks and four days, four days past the room, rail of two. The board on two nails: twelfth, nine hundred and ninety-eight; thirteenth, nine hundred and forty-nine; fourteenth, nine hundred and fourteen; fifteenth, eight hundred and ninety-three; sixteenth, eight hundred and sixty-eight; seventeenth, eight hundred and fifty; **eighteenth, seven hundred and ninety-six, a hundred and thirteen weeks and five days; nineteenth, seven hundred and seventy-four, a hundred and ten weeks and four days**. Added to that board: nothing. The post at the corridor end: six hundred and twenty-six days, eighty-nine weeks and three days. The nine hand copies: six hundred and forty-four days, ninety-two weeks to the day, eight of the nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and no two of the nine compared on this day. The hold in another city: seven hundred and eleven days, a hundred and one weeks and four days. The man of about fifty-one at the wall: six hundred and eighty-four days, ninety-seven weeks and five days. The ask: seven hundred and sixty-eight days, one hundred and nine weeks and five days. The separation: four hundred and fifty-eight days, sixty-five weeks and three days, not shorter and not ended. The drawer: shut before about two and opened for about nine seconds at about two and shut again, holding four sheets, one with the word NO on it and four lines under it that were not read, and the fourth not read either.

*The two numbers, and why they are printed in the same paragraph as the copies.* **The eighteenth line above that bench: seven hundred and ninety-six days, a hundred and thirteen weeks and five days. The nine hand copies on the shelf: six hundred and forty-four days, ninety-two weeks to the day. What those two figures are: seven hundred and ninety-six is also the day nine hand copies of the front of a page were begun, and six hundred and forty-four is also the day the eighteenth line was written. So one of the two numbers is an age and one of them is a day something happened, and which is which is stated in the body and is not a matter of opinion. What neither number is: a day anything happened to the heating in this flat, in this building, or in this city, and this entry attaches no days-off to that heating. What was not done with the copies: nothing. No two of the nine were compared with each other on this Monday, or on the six days of this week before it, or on the four days of it after it, and eight of the nine are unfinished and the first disagreement in the fourth line has not been found, and the day it is found is not this day. The other figures on that Monday: the room one thousand and seventy-eight days and the rail card one thousand and eighty-two days, four days apart, and the rail holds two.**

*Nine seconds, a drawer, and two words in a queue.* **What was opened: a drawer under a bench, not locked, not labelled, not on anything, holding four sheets, of which one carries the word NO in capitals with four lines folded under it, one is a docket for a shop that shut in the spring, one is a form page with a box ticked and a room in it, and one came through the hatch off a man who was on a road he was already on and has not been asked about. How long it was open: about nine seconds. What was read: none of the four, and the four lines were not read and the fourth sheet was not read and the reason for each is in the body. What went in: one blank sheet off the top of the bench with an address written on the back of it in pencil, into a sleeve that now holds five. What was said at the counter at about ten past four, in a queue of four, by the woman of about thirty-four, in about two seconds: she is back. What was not said: which she, and what she came back for, and whether the office is in the same building, and there were four people in that queue and none of them was told any of it. What was read out loud in a corridor at about eleven on the Monday morning by a person who was asked to and who did it because she was asked: a letter, about rooms, about a floor, about two rooms on a first floor in a second district. What was not done: he did not go and find her, and the reason, which he noticed at about six in the evening rather than at four in the afternoon, is the same reason as the one from the first Monday, and noticing that it was the same reason is also on this page. What was said about him by a man of about fifty-two in a queue on the Thursday, and not corrected: you found that street then. Who was thanked: nobody, at the counter, on the bench, in a corridor, or in four rooms off a service road.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **Two numbers that are each other were put on the same wall in the same paragraph as a shelf of unfinished copies, a drawer was opened for about nine seconds by a man who read none of it, and two words were said to him in a queue by a person who was serving somebody else.** **  Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts that altered nothing is closed at two, and a drawer opened and not read is not a third instance, and the two on the register are still the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns. The rail card is one thousand and eighty-two days and stood in a rail that takes two, and a return sheet and a blank sheet with an address on the back of it are both different objects from it and are not joined to each other. The rooms are one thousand and seventy-eight days and warm. Nineteen lines on two nails and no twentieth. The man of about fifty-one said nothing on the Monday evening and has still not been handed anything in writing. At about seven there were four people in that room and one of them was a woman of about thirty in the chair by the door, and the one thing that is true of the whole evening is that she was not spoken to and the ring binder on the shelf stayed shut. The four words that name the public body are in none of these pages, and the number of returns in the four trays is in none of these pages because it is not known and has not been counted by anybody entitled to count it. No sitting fell on this day and no count was announced.**
