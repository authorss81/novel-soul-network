## 2. WHAT THIS MOVEMENT IS FOR, FROM THE PLAN OF RECORD

1. **A SHEET COMES BACK TO A REPAIR COUNTER BECAUSE THE ROOM PRINTED ON IT IS A STAIR CUPBOARD, AND NOBODY IN THE BUILDING IT WENT TO DID ANYTHING WRONG.** The address is correct on paper. It was correct when it was printed. That is the whole of the volume's first move and it is not a mystery and it is not a fault.
2. **A WOMAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-EIGHT HAS BEEN SORTING RETURNS INTO FOUR TRAYS SINCE THE SPRING AND IS NOT PAID AND IS GOING TO STOP, AND SHE IS RIGHT ABOUT EVERY SHEET SHE HAS.** She is named once, at 603. The rule she follows — a sheet that cannot be read by a person standing in the room is a notice, and a notice is filed — is said out loud once, by her, in about nine seconds, and it is not explained by anybody afterwards and is not softened and is not called a rule by anybody.
3. **ABOUT FOUR HUNDRED PEOPLE EACH KNOW HALF AN ADDRESS AND EVERY HALF IS CORRECT, AND HE REFUSES, IN ABOUT NINE SECONDS, TO PUT THE FOUR HALVES TOGETHER**, because a man who has the address is a man the returns go to. That refusal is correct and it is the movement's hinge.
4. **HE LOSES, IN ABOUT NINE SECONDS, THE FIRST PROPOSAL HE MAKES IN THIS VOLUME, WHICH IS TO PAY ONE WOMAN FOR THE TIME IT TAKES TO ANSWER QUESTIONS HONESTLY.** Nobody thanks anybody. She is not paid. He is not thanked.
5. **THE SEPARATION ENDS IN A FORM AT CHAPTER 607, AND IT IS NOT A SCENE, AND HE IS TOLD ABOUT IT BY A THIRD PERSON AT A COUNTER IN A QUEUE, AND THE CONDITION OF HER RETURN IS THAT SHE CAN REMOVE HIM FROM A PLAN AND THE REMOVAL IS RECORDED AND HE IS NOT ASKED TO AGREE TO IT FIRST.** He agrees in about nine seconds and does not know what he has agreed to until Movement IV.
6. **THE MOVEMENT CLOSES WITH THE VOLUME'S OWN UNSOLVED THING SAID IN A FACE AND AGREED WITH BY NOBODY.** About four hundred and fifty people in this city can each invoke a true sentence and not one of them has anywhere to put an objection, and this is inherited from Volume 12 and is not resolved here.
