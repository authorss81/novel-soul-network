# Chapter 648 — A Friday, A School On A Wide Road At About Half Past Eleven With A Yard That Had Something In It For The First Time Since The Spring, A Time Of Day Four Weeks Old Asked For Out Loud Because Nobody Wrote It Down, A Condition That Is On No Piece Of Paper In This City, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Six Who Says The Thing Is The Fault Of The Man Standing In Front Of Her

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a school on a wide road in the middle of the morning. This one is four weeks old and out loud, a condition with nothing to sit on, and a woman who points at the shape of the arrangement rather than at the man who built it.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Friday and eleven people came over the counter, the last let in at ten past five, and about nine hours of that Friday were on a bench and a counter and about forty minutes of it were on a wide road in the middle of the morning.

Those four rooms off that service road are one thousand one hundred and sixty-six days, a hundred and sixty-six weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, warm. **A card on end in that rail is one thousand one hundred and seventy days, a hundred and sixty-seven weeks and one day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The hardboard's eighteenth line is eight hundred and eighty-four days, a hundred and twenty-six weeks and two days. The hardboard's seventeenth is nine hundred and thirty-eight, a hundred and thirty-four weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is seven hundred and seventy-two, a hundred and ten weeks and two days.

The separation is five hundred and forty-six days, seventy-eight weeks to the day, shut in a one-line box in a form about nine weeks ago, walked every day since, and not one day of it shorter than the day before.

---

**There was a lorry in that yard and it had not been in that yard since the spring, and he noticed it from the gate in four seconds and stood there for about nine more.**

The lorry is a different firm's and it is not on any day that this entry holds a figure for, and the yard has been empty on every other day he has stood in it since the Monday of week 230, and he has noticed the emptiness every time and has never written it down. **He has started checking, which is a new habit and a bad one, and the reason it is a bad one is that a man who checks a yard for a lorry is a man who has begun to expect a van.**

The girl of about seventeen was at the gate and let him in without asking him for anything, which is what that gate is for, and she said **"She's in the supply room and she's got nine trays out and she's counting them again,**" and went back inside, and neither of them said anything else, and about four months ago he said something in that doorway and she heard all of it.

---

**The supply room had nine trays out on a table and a woman of about twenty-six counting clips into them, and she did not stop counting, and he stood in the doorway for the four seconds it took her to finish a tray.**

"Which room," she said.

"That's not what I'm here for."

"**Then it'll have to wait, because if I stop in the middle of a tray I lose the count of the tray.**" She put the last row down. **"Right. Go on.**"

---

**He had rehearsed this one on a bus and it came out wrong anyway, and the wrongness was in the first four words.**

"It's about the time you asked me for."

"**I did ask you for it,**" she said. "**You gave it to me and you told me you weren't writing it down and you told me why, and I've not thought about it since, so whatever this is, it's about four weeks late.**"

"It is. What time of day is it."

She looked at him for about two seconds and then she said, **"It's a quarter past six in the morning, and before you say anything about it, it's not the time anybody starts. It's the time a box goes out, and I have to be standing in this room when it goes out or there's nobody here, and a supply room with nobody in it is a cupboard, and that hasn't changed since the day you stood in that doorway.**"

"**Right,**" he said, and then stopped, because there was nothing else in him that was true, and about nine seconds of a man standing in a supply room on a wide road is a long time.

"**You don't want to stop at that in here,**" she said. "**It sounds like a thing you say when you've come back for something.**"

---

**And then he told her what it was for, and that was the small thing he gave up on the Friday, and it took him four seconds and it is not in any form in this city.**

"There are nine of you. One sheet of paper each, with an address on it and nothing else on it. No name, no time, no day, no condition. Nine rooms that don't know about each other."

"**Nine,**" she said. "**And you wrote them all out yourself.**"

"No. A woman four days of bus travel there and back wrote them out, and she's the one I put the ninth sheet for, and she runs a clinic with nothing on the door and she has been owed a month of decisions since the spring. And a woman of about twenty-four who does ironing on a wide road is carrying them, one at a time, and she'll hand a paper back on a table if a room says no and she won't go back in twice."

The woman of about twenty-six put the tray down and looked at the door for four seconds and then back at him.

"**Where's mine.**"

"You've got yours. It's an address and it's this room."

"**Then the time's not on it.**"

"No."

"**Right,**" she said, and picked the tray up again. "**So I've got a condition that isn't on the paper, and the paper's what people will go on, and there are about nine of those and about nine conditions and one of the nine is a supply room that has to be standing in a room at a quarter past six in the morning, and nobody in this city can find that out by reading anything.**"

---

**And she was right and he did not say so in a sentence, and what she said next is the whole of what was said in that room about it.**

"**The paper's the right shape. I'm not having a go at the paper. The paper is one address and no man in the middle of it and I couldn't do better with a pen and four minutes.**" She put the tray down again, which she did not need to do. **"But the condition is the bit that's real and the condition is on nothing, and the day somebody needs a supply room to be standing in a room at a quarter past six in the morning there'll be nobody here, and it'll be because a piece of paper was right.**"

"I know."

"**You know it because I said it four weeks ago,**" she said, **"and you wrote it down about a third of the time. This is the one you didn't write down.**"

---

**He did not ask her to put the time on anything, and that was what he gave up, and it cost him the only thing he had come for.**

A sheet of paper that carries an address and nothing else is the whole of the arrangement and it works because of what it leaves out. **If he asked her for a second sheet she would write one, and there would be ten sheets, and a person carrying ten sheets has to decide which one to hand over first on a day when a room is shut.**

He took the time of day in his head instead and put it nowhere, which is what he did with it the first time, and he did not tell her that he had done it twice, and she did not ask, and the number of conditions in this city that are on nothing went up by one at half past eleven on a Friday and stayed there.

---

**What he wanted, standing in that doorway, was for the nine pieces of paper to have been nine conditions. That is the want and it did not get said and it was the wrong shape of wish and he knew it was the wrong shape of wish while he was having it.**

Nine conditions on nine papers would have been nine rooms with a person attached to each of them, and a person attached to a room is a place things go to, and that is what this arrangement spent four months and one injury undoing. **He wanted it wrong and he wanted it for nine seconds and he did not say it and she will never know that it happened in a supply room on a wide road at half past eleven on a Friday.**

---

**And at ten past twelve a man of about twenty-six from a water office was in that corridor and said one thing in nine seconds and then went away.**

He said that from Monday next week there is something going on in nine rooms that is not on anybody's sheet and not on anybody's list, and that he is not going to say what it is, and that anybody who wants to be told to be told. **Marek asked him one question and got one word, and the word was no, and the man of about twenty-six said that was the correct answer and went back down the corridor.**

This entry does not hold a figure for what happens in those five working days, does not have a room for it, does not have a day for it, and is not going to guess at one. **A movement that publishes a gap has to say what is in it, and what is in that one is nine rooms doing something, and this file has no figure for it and the figure is not the point.**

---

Three and a half hours after half past six there was still one other person in the fourth of those four rooms. She was thirty-odd, she was by the door, and nothing whatever was put to her in all of it and nothing ever is. Her page, which carries her name at the head of it, is the eighth of eight, and it lies shut in a ring binder on a back shelf in that room and did not come out of it.

Two cards standing on end in a rail, sixty-two lines in a green-covered book, and seventy-three in a tin whose lid never came off. **No sitting fell on this Friday, no number was given out loud in this city or in any other, and this entry does not say what a sitting does when there is none of it.**

Seven things are countable in this entry and they are kept apart: a card on end in a rail of two, a form with three ruled lines, a plate of iron with four slots, a sheet that came back to a room, a docket with nine places along its back, a hand copy of a page front, and nothing else that this file has named twice.

---

**At about one he went to the bench with the back of a docket and tried to write down the about nine conditions in this city, and got four of them, and stopped.**

A set time of a quarter past six in the morning in a supply room on a wide road. That somebody should be able to say who the van is at eleven at night in a kitchen in a back yard. What happens to a Thursday at a depot with a gate onto a yard. That a room is a place and not a permission.

**He wrote the four on one side of a docket and on the other side of that docket there are nine places pencilled along the back in his own hand from about two months ago, and he turned it over and looked at the nine and then turned it back, and he did not write the other five and there is no second sheet in this city with the other five on it.**

The four are on a thing that is not a form and is in a fourth pocket and will be in that pocket when the ink has gone, and there is no way in this city of asking about four conditions at nine kitchens and four districts and three towns, and the woman of about thirty-one four days of bus travel away is the only person in this arrangement who has ever put a condition on paper, and she put hers on a scrap the size of a quarter of a sheet and it is on a bus.
