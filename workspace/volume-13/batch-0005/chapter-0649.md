# Chapter 649 — A Monday Of A Week That Carries Nothing Between One Friday And It, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Second Floor In Crown Terrace In The Evening With A Man Of About Thirty-Three In It Who Comes On That Evening Every Time, One Question Asked In About Nine Seconds By A Man In A Kitchen And Answered In About Nine Seconds By A Person Entitled, And No Offer Of Anything Made To Anybody Before The Hour Was Out

**A Monday of a week that has nothing at all in it was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a second floor in Crown Terrace in the evening. This one is four hundred people nobody has asked, nine seconds in and nine seconds out, and a yard that is still a yard on the morning this is said in a room.**

---

Five working days went by in the middle of that week and none of them is on this page and none of them has a figure, a day, a room or a name in this file. **About nine rooms did something. This entry has nothing about it and is not going to have anything about it.**

On that Monday eleven jobs carried a date against each and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and it stood in one pair of hands from two until the shutter went down.

Those four rooms off that service road are one thousand one hundred and seventy-six days, a hundred and sixty-eight weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, warm. **The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and eighty days, a hundred and sixty-eight weeks and four days, four days past them, and that rail holds two.**

The nineteenth line on the board of two nails is eight hundred and seventy-two days, a hundred and twenty-four weeks and four days, and the twelfth is one thousand and ninety-six, a hundred and fifty-six weeks and four days, and the two have been exactly four days apart since one of them was a nail and the other was a line. The separation is five hundred and fifty-six days, seventy-nine weeks and three days, shut in a form about nine weeks ago, walked every day since, no shorter.

---

**The man of about thirty-three with nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over was in that room for about an hour and a half, and he came at the time he always comes, and nobody at that table said why he was there and nobody asked.**

He has a firm, a yard in the second of the four towns, and nine crates that have been in a yard since the spring. **Eight of the nine are stacked and the ninth is not, and the ninth has not been stacked since the spring, and it stands where it was put down, and there is a rectangle of clean concrete under it that is the exact size of the bottom of it and that has not been cleaned since the day it arrived.**

He is about thirty-three. He is not owed a thing in this manuscript. **He is not a villain and he is not a rescue and nobody has emptied his yard and nobody is going to, and there is no scene coming in which nine crates go out of a gate, and the figure of that hold walks on a board in a shop in this city every working day and is a length of time and not a person.**

---

**The question was asked at half past six by a man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen and who had come straight from a shift, and it was nine seconds long, and it was the only question anybody asked in that room on that evening.**

"**Is it finished. The nine. Is that the nine rooms there is, or is that nine you picked.**"

The woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs said the answer in nine seconds and she did not stand up and she did not soften it and she did not put it in a longer sentence afterwards.

"**About four hundred people in this city still have a return that goes to one room. They have had it since the spring and some of them had it before the spring. They are not on a list because there is no list and they are not in a building because they are not in a building. Nobody has been asked and that is the whole of the reason and there is no other reason.**"

---

**And then she said the other half in the same breath, which is the half that makes the first half true, and she was not asked for it either.**

"**Nine rooms can only find the people already standing in them. A person who is not in one of those nine is not found by a piece of paper and is not found by a woman with a bag and is not found by anything anybody in this city has built in the last month. That is not a fault in the nine. That is what nine is. And there is nobody in this city who is going to be sent to ask, because there is no money for it and there is no arrangement for it and about four hundred competent people could each do it properly and not one of them has anywhere to put an objection.**"

Nobody in that room said a word for eleven seconds.

---

**And what she did not say, and did not have to say, and that this entry is not going to say for her, is that the arrangement that is standing is standing with about four hundred people out of it, and that it is slower, and that both of those are true, and that neither of them is going to be improved by anybody sitting in a room on an evening.**

**Nobody offered to do anything. Nobody said they would find out who the four hundred are. Nobody said they would ask. The woman of about fifty-two signed a form in a second floor in Crown Terrace on the Thursday of the week before last and there is no money attached to it and there is no post and there is nothing in this city that would carry a person to four hundred doors, and nobody in that room pretended otherwise and nobody in that room pretended otherwise afterwards.**

That is the incompleteness and it was published on a face in nine seconds and it was not consoled, and it is the whole of what is left of it.

---

**He wanted the answer to be a number he could write on the back of a hand.**

That is the want and it did not get said, and the number was a number, and the number was about four hundred, and **about four hundred is a figure a woman of about fifty-two has been carrying in her own head for four years and has never been able to give to anybody, and there is no column for it in a shop on a wide road and there is no line for it on a board on two nails and there is not a single piece of paper in this city with that number on it.**

He could have written it down. He has a pen in the fourth pocket of a tool roll and there are four pieces of paper in that pocket that are not his, and **he did not write four hundred on anything, and the reason is that the day he writes a number of people down in this city is the day that number is a thing a room is, and he has spent about four months refusing to be the man who makes a number into a room.**

---

**And the small thing he gave up on the Monday was that he did not say one word to the man of about thirty-three, and it took him four seconds to not say it and it was the hardest four seconds of the day.**

The man of about thirty-three was standing about nine feet away with his coat on and had heard all of it, **and Marek had a thing he could have said to him, which was that he was sorry, and he did not say it, and the reason is the same one as at a counter eight days ago, and the second time is worse than the first because the first time there had been a stranger between them and this time there was nobody.**

He is not the person who says things to people. That is on a piece of paper in a fourth pocket, and it is nine words long, and it is the only thing anybody in this city has written down about him that is not about a room.

---

**The man of about thirty-three went down that stair at about eight o'clock and nobody said anything to him, and the yard in the second of the four towns is a yard on the morning after this and the nine crates are in it and eight of them are stacked.**

The ninth is not. **He was told at a sitting in the spring that he could not ask about a tray, in four seconds, and he worked out in about four more that what he had actually been told was that he is not the person in that room who decides anything, and he has been that ever since, and nothing that has happened in this city in five months has made him anything else, and he is not owed a thing, and nothing that has happened in this city in about five months has forgiven him or repaired him or thanked him.**

---

After half past six in the fourth of those four rooms there was one other person, and she was about thirty, and she was by the door, and she was still by the door at about ten. **Nothing was put to her in three and a half hours by anybody in that building and nothing ever is.** The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it did not come out.

The rail takes two. The book with the green cover is on sixty-two lines. The tin is on seventy-three and its lid did not come off. **No sitting fell on this Monday and nothing was given out loud in this city or any other, and this entry does not say what a sitting does when there is none.**

Six objects are named on this page and no sentence in it lays any two of them together: a sheet that came back to a room, a hand copy of a page front, a form with three ruled lines in it, a docket with nine places along its reverse, a card on end in a rail of two, and a plate of iron with four slots cut into it.

---

**At about a quarter past three a man of about fifty-five came to that counter and asked for a list of rooms, and was refused in four seconds, and then said a thing that made the woman of about thirty-four put down a meter she had not finished with.**

"Which nine should I be sending to?"

"**There's no list,**" Marek said.

"**There's got to be a list. It's been six weeks. I've been to four shops this month and four shops have told me there's no list, and I started off assuming it was this one and now I'm assuming something's gone wrong with you.**"

"Nobody's sent me a list either. I made one and I gave it to a van and the van stopped on a Friday, and a man in a kitchen is standing in a room this evening with the only copy of it written on the back of his hand, and he is not going to be in this shop on Monday and he is not going to be in this shop on any Monday after that."

The man of about fifty-five looked at the counter for four seconds. **"So you're one of the four,"** he said. **"That's all right. I'd rather it was a shop than a council. I just wanted it to be somebody who'd say so.**" And he left, and he did not say what he was going to do about it, and Marek did not ask, and that is the whole of what this arrangement has done for him, which is tell a man of about fifty-five in a shop that there is no list and be one of the four reasons.

**That is about four hundred people. Not four shops.**
