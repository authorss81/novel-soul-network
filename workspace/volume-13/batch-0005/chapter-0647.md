# Chapter 647 — A Thursday, A Sheet Of Paper With Three Ruled Lines On It Filled In At A Table In Crown Terrace Over About Nine Minutes, A Number In A Box And A Date In A Box, A Name Written At The Foot Of It That Is Not His And Is Not In This City, Eleven Days And Then It Lapses, And About Nine People Who Have Stood In One Cellar Together And Do Not Know Where Any Of The Others Sends Its Paper

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a second floor in Crown Terrace from about three until half past five. This one is a form with three ruled lines on it, a number and a date, a name at the foot that is a day and a night of bus travel from here, and eleven days.**

---

Eleven jobs with a date against each on the Thursday and eleven people over the counter, the last let in at ten past five, and the day went in four blocks: bench, counter, wide road, counter, service road, and the counter was not handed over at any point.

Those four rooms off that service road are one thousand one hundred and sixty-five days, a hundred and sixty-six weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, warm. **In that rail, on end, is a card: one thousand one hundred and sixty-nine days, a hundred and sixty-seven weeks to the day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and sixty-one days, a hundred and twenty-three weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard, in the second of the four towns, is seven hundred and ninety-eight days, a hundred and fourteen weeks to the day, and those two have been a week and a day apart since one of them was a man and the other was a wall and there is nobody in this city able to say why.

The separation is five hundred and forty-five days, seventy-seven weeks and six days, ended in a form about nine weeks ago, walked every day since, no shorter, nothing put back into it.

---

**There is a cabinet in a cellar in a second district with a brass-rimmed socket in it and a card in the door of it with two printed lines on it, and about nine people in nine buildings have stood in that cellar and held their own ends against a plate at the same time for eleven minutes, and that is the whole of what is known about it.**

**Nobody in this city has ever asked the question of whether those nine know where each other send their paper, and the answer is that they do not, and they have not, and one of them keeps a supply room in a school on a wide road and one of them is four days of bus travel there and back and neither of those two has ever been in the same building as the other.**

That is what the nine pieces of paper changed and it is not a small thing and it was not said out loud in any room this week by anybody.

---

**The sheet with three ruled lines on it was filled in at ten past three and it took nine minutes and there were four people at the table while it happened.**

The top line is for what is being held. **Nobody wrote a description on it. What is on it is the address of the cabinet in that cellar in a second district, written out in full, and a person's name is not on it, because the thing is a socket in a wall and a wall does not have a person in it and putting a person on that line is the mistake this arrangement has been correcting since the spring.**

The second line has two boxes. **The date in the left-hand box is a Thursday. The number in the right-hand box is eleven, and it was said out loud once, and it is a number of days, and it is not a day number and it is not a distance and it is not a year or a month, and no page of this entry prints the day that Thursday falls on.**

Eleven days, and then it lapses. **Nobody is going to be reminded and nobody is going to be chased and no letter goes anywhere, because a letter does not go anywhere in this city, and a thing that ends because nobody renewed it is the only kind of ending in this arrangement that does not need a person to be unfair.**

The third line is a signature at the foot, and above the signature there is a name, and the name is not his.

---

**The name at the foot is a woman of about thirty-one who has a clinic with nothing written on its door four days of bus travel there and back, and it is written as an address, because that is the only way a person in this city can be named on a piece of paper and be reached.**

She is not in this city and she has not been in this city since a Thursday a fortnight ago and she has not been asked whether she will put her name there.

**She has not been asked. The scrap of paper the size of a quarter of a sheet is in a bag on a bus and about nine words on it say that nothing goes through a person, and a person who has been put on a form to be the one who stops a thing is not a thing that goes through a person, and that is the whole of the reasoning and it took four seconds and it was done by a man of about twenty-two standing at the side of a table where he has no standing.**

Somebody read the address back to him and he said it was right and did not say anything else, and **he did not have any way of asking her and there is no form in this city on which a person records that they have written a woman's name at the foot of something without her.**

---

**The fourth person at that table was the woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs, and she signed it at about twenty past three, and she did not read the two boxes out and nobody asked her to.**

The rules she signed it under are the public rules of a body with about nine chairs and no permanent staff, and the first of them is that a thing anybody can do has to be on a piece of paper with a date and a number on it, and the second is that the person who can stop it is named and is not the person who asked for it.

**Both of those are nineteen years old in the way that everything in that room is nineteen years old, and neither of them was written for this, and the woman of about fifty-two said in nine seconds that she did not write them and was not going to pretend she had.**

A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard was not asked to sign and was not asked to be a witness and was not asked for a copy. **He wrote a number in his column. It is not the number in the box and it is not the date in the box and he has never been asked and will not be.**

---

**And nine seconds before all of that, the man of about thirty-four at the counter in a shop in Lattice Ward had written the number eleven on the back of her own hand in biro, looked at it, and rubbed it out with her thumb, and written it again.**

The woman of about thirty-four is at that counter every working day and has been for four years and there is no form in this city with her name on it. She said, to nobody, at half past two, **"Eleven. That's a fortnight less three days, and there's no calendar in this arrangement, and if nobody writes a date down then eleven days is forever and forever is the one nobody's going to check.**"

**She was right and the date is in a box on a form and the form is on a table in a second floor in Crown Terrace and is not in a drawer, and anybody in this city who wants to stop it has to come up that stair and read it.**

---

**He wanted it to be his, and it was not going to be his, and he worked that out in four seconds on a Monday and had known it since the Monday.**

That is the want and it did not get said. **The small thing he gave up on the Thursday was the last thing he had a name on in this city. He has a piece of paper in a fourth pocket that says he is not the person anything comes to, and a form on a table that says the same thing in a different hand with three ruled lines on it, and about nine addresses in a bag on a bus that he carried for two days and a night and is not carrying any more. He gave up the last of it in four seconds and nobody at that table noticed him doing it and he did not tell anybody and has not.**

---

The ninth chair is hard against a wall with its back to the room. **It did not move, its mover is not named, and about six people in this city have looked at it since the spring and not one of them has ever said anything about it in a room where a decision was being taken.**

After half past six, and for three and a half hours after it, the chair by the door in the fourth of those four rooms held one other person and nobody spoke to her. **She was thirty-odd. She was still in that chair at about ten. The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight and it lies in a ring binder at the back of a shelf in that room and did not come out of it.**

Two cards in a rail, and a book with a green cover on sixty-two lines, and a tin with seventy-three in it at the end of a table in Saltmarket. **There was no sitting on this Thursday and no number was said out loud anywhere; this file does not say what one does when there is not one.**

Six objects get named on this page, and this one does not set two of them side by side: a form with three ruled lines in it, a plate of iron with four slots cut into it, a card on end in a rail of two, a hand copy of the front of a page, a sheet that came back to a room, and a docket with nine places along its reverse.

---

**At about a quarter to five a man of about fifty who keeps a gate in this city came along that street and saw him through the glass and came in, and he had come in deliberately, and he had walked nine hundred yards to do it.**

He had been up a stair in Saltmarket three weeks earlier with nothing in his hands to ask a woman of about sixty whether a thing he had been told at that gate in about the spring was written down, and she had said it was, and he had gone down that stair faster than he had come up.

"Now you've stopped it."

"**I never started it,**" Marek said. "**Eight people said yes out loud to a person standing in front of them. One of them was a woman four days of bus travel there and back and I found out in the ninth week what had happened to her and she was owed a month of somebody else's decisions and nobody in that room agreed with her, and I was one of the room.**"

"**That's not what I asked.**"

"**No,**" Marek said, "**and I'm not going to be able to answer it, and I want to say the reason out loud instead of standing here looking like a man who is about to. There's a piece of paper in a bag on this table with a woman's name at the foot of it and she has not been asked, and four seconds ago I watched a man in a kitchen and a woman with a bag of clips stand nine feet apart on a pavement for four minutes because neither of them knows the other is one of the nine. So no.**"

The man of about fifty looked at the counter for four seconds and then said, **"That's the first straight one anybody's given me about any of this,**" and went out and did not stop in the street.

**He has not believed a kitchen stopped it. He has believed since about the spring that it was not written down anywhere and was only what he was told, and that is a different belief and it is not better and nobody in this city has told him it is different.**

---

*One thousand one hundred and sixty-nine and eight hundred and ninety-four are both whole numbers of weeks, four days apart, and one of them is a card in a rail of two and the other of them is a line on a board on two nails, and neither of the two has been in a sentence with the other on any day this year.*
