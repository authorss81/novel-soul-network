# Chapter 644 — A Friday, Nine Sheets Cut From One Pad In A Supply Room On The Far Side Of A Town This Entry Does Not Describe, One Address On Each Of Them And Nothing Else, A Ninth Address That No Person In Another City Could Use, A Person Named As The One Who Is Not To Carry Them, And A Question About A Gate In This City That He Does Not Ask

**A Friday was a day and a night of bus travel short of getting home, and a supply room in a town this entry does not describe with nine sheets of paper on a table. This one is nine addresses and nothing else on nine separate pieces of paper, a door with nothing on it written down so that only somebody who has stood at it could use it, and a question not asked.**

---

He was in that town for the second night and slept about four hours and was awake for the other five, in a room over a shop with a window that faced a wall about four feet off and a second blanket on the bed that nobody had mentioned.

**The bus is a day and a night each way and the whole of it is four days there and back, and the next one leaves this city tomorrow morning, and the one after that is on the Saturday, and there is no way out of that town except one of those two.**

Those four rooms off a service road in the city he will be in on Sunday night are one thousand one hundred and fifty-nine days, a hundred and sixty-five weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, warm. **A card stands on end in that rail, and it is one thousand one hundred and sixty-three days, a hundred and sixty-six weeks and one day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on the board of two nails is eight hundred and fifty-five days, a hundred and twenty-two weeks and one day. The post at the end of that corridor is seven hundred and seven days, a hundred and one weeks to the day, and it is the only figure on that board that lands on a whole week this week besides one other, and neither of the two has anything to do with a corridor.

The separation is five hundred and thirty-nine days, seventy-seven weeks to the day, ended in a form, walked every day, not shorter.

---

**She did it between about eleven and about one on the Friday morning, at a table, with the hand truck pushed against the wall and the door shut, and there were four people in that supply room and three of them watched.**

Nine sheets cut out of a pad with a blade that was not sharp. **One address on each and nothing else: no name of a person, no time, no day, no condition, no second address, nothing on it that reaches anybody, no reference to any of the other eight, and no mark on any of the nine that would tell anybody reading one of them that there were eight more.**

A school on a wide road. A water office. A bus depot with a gate onto a yard. A council house in a street with trees on one side. A kitchen in a back yard. A room above a line in Saltmarket. A school in a town this entry does not describe. A store with a cold room in a town this entry does not describe.

**And the ninth, which is hers, and which took her eleven seconds longer than the other eight, and which is not an address at all.**

---

"**It has nothing on the door,**" she said, and put the ninth sheet flat on the table with both hands. "**So I've written the low building with the light on past the second row of shops, the door on the left of that, and there's nothing on it. Anybody who's never stood at it will not find it. That's on purpose and I'm not sorry about it, and I'll put a better line on it in four years if anybody ever stands at it again.**"

"You could write a description a stranger could use."

"**Then a stranger would walk past a room that people walk to,**" she said. **"That's what happened to about nine hundred of these a year for four years. A thing you can find is a thing that isn't finding a person. I have four people who come to that door because there is a light on in it and I have never once put a sign up, and I am not starting now to hand a stranger a good description of it in a different town.**"

He wrote it down in his own words later, on the Saturday, from memory, and he got it wrong in one place, and **she was not in this city to be asked and he has not asked anybody else whether they have it right.**

---

**And then she said the thing about him, and it was four seconds, and he did not argue with it and has not since.**

"**You're not carrying them.**"

"That's the job."

"**It's the job if you're a stop,**" she said. "**Nine pieces of paper and a man to take them is nine rooms and a road. Nine rooms and a road with a man on it is one room with a coat on. I have watched that for four years from a town four days of bus travel from where you're standing, and it is how nine of us lost a month, and you were the man on the road, and you did it in nine seconds on a Friday with a van behind you and none of it on a paper.**"

"**So who does.**"

"**Somebody with nothing to lose by being the road, and no room at the end of it, and no reason for any of the nine to be grateful to,**" she said, "**and I have one of those and she does nine sheets of ironing three days a week and there is no list of anybody in this city and there is not going to be one.**"

---

**He did not ask her the name and she did not give it, and the not asking was the small thing he gave up on the Friday.**

He wanted to be written on one of the nine. That is the want and it did not get said and it is not a small thing at all, and it is the first time in about four months that he has wanted to be an address. **A man who has spent as long as he can remember being the place a thing goes to is offered, on a Friday morning, nine pieces of paper with nine addresses on them, and one of the nine is a low building with a light on past a second row of shops, and he has stood at that door for nine minutes in the wrong weather and did not go in, and the whole of what he wanted in that room was to be the ninth line on one of those sheets.**

He did not ask to be on it. He wrote down the nine addresses in his own hand on the back of a docket that was not a docket and did not say anything about any of them, and the ninth one he has got wrong in one place, and there is no list of anybody in this city and there is not going to be one, and the woman who said that said it in four seconds and went back to a truck.

---

**And the question he did not ask was about a gate in the city he was going back to on Sunday, and it has been on him since the spring and it is nine months old.**

A man of about fifty who keeps a gate in this city was told in about the spring, by a man with a clipboard, that a kitchen stopped it. He has carried that. **He came up a stair in a room in Saltmarket three weeks ago on his own with nothing in his hands and asked a woman of about sixty whether it was written down or whether it was only what he had been told, and she said it was written down, and he said that was all he came for, and he went down that stair faster than he came up.**

The only person in this city who can correct that account is a man of twenty-two with a copy of a form folded once in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, **and he was the one who had four seconds in a corridor eleven weeks ago in which to move the fault off a woman's hand and onto a gate, and he did not, and the reason has never been printed anywhere and is not printed here, and it is that he was protecting a person and he is no longer the person who protects anybody.**

She is a day and a night of bus travel from that gate. **Nobody in either city has ever asked her what she was told, or by whom, or in which month, and he had four seconds this morning in which he could have asked her, and he did not, and he is not going to, and the reason is on the form in his pocket and is nine words long.**

---

Behind the door of that supply room there are about nine pegs and the ninth of them is still empty and has been empty since Wednesday, and the eight coats on the other eight have been worn through at the elbow for a long time and nothing on that wall has changed in four years.

The fourth of those four rooms off that service road, in the city he was not in, held one other person after half past six. She was thirty-odd and she had the chair nearest the door and she had it when the light in that stair went off. **For three and a half hours no person in that building said a word to her, and no apology is owed to her there by anybody in it, and none has ever been offered. The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight, in a ring binder on a back shelf in that room, and it has not been out of it today.**

The rail in that city takes two. The book with the green cover stands at sixty-two lines. The tin is on seventy-three and its lid never came off. **No sitting fell on this Friday in either city and no count was given out loud anywhere, and this entry does not say what a sitting does when there is none.**

Six things are named on this page and this one keeps them apart: a hand copy of the front of a page, a card on end in a rail of two, a plate of iron with four slots cut into it, a form with two boxes in it, a sheet that came back to a room, and a docket with nine places along its reverse.

---

**The man of about fifty-one came back into that room at about one o'clock, saw nine sheets of paper on a table, and stopped in the doorway for four seconds, and the woman of about nineteen with a box of staples put the box down.**

"That a lot of paper for a room that hasn't got a shelf," he said.

"**It is,**" said the woman of about thirty-one, and went on counting.

"**Where does it go.**"

"**Into nine buildings,**" she said, "**and one of them is a building on the far side of this town and I am not going to tell you which, and you have been at that desk for about nine years and you have never once asked me where my paper goes, and that is the arrangement and I have not come here to improve it.**"

He looked at the nine sheets for nine seconds and said, **"You've had that out on a table where a girl of nineteen can see it,"** and went back out, and the girl of nineteen picked the box up and carried it past the table and looked at the ninth sheet as she went, and put it down again in the same place, and said nothing.

**Nobody in that room thanked anybody and nobody apologised and four seconds of that exchange were about a shelf.**
