# Chapter 645 — A Monday, Two Nights On A Bus And A Sunday In Between In Which He Did Nothing At All, A Second Floor In Crown Terrace With About Nine Chairs In It And Six People In Two Of Them, Nine Sheets Face Up On A Table And Nobody Reading Them, A Name Said In About Nine Seconds By A Person Entitled To Say It Once, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Four Standing In A Doorway

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a second floor in Crown Terrace from about three with about nine chairs in it and six people in two of them. This one is nine sheets of paper on a table, one name said out loud in nine seconds, and a woman of about twenty-four in a doorway who did not come in.**

---

He came back into this city on the Saturday night, and the Sunday he did nothing at all, which is the first full day he has had in about four months in which he was in this city and not at a counter in it, and he has not told anybody what he did with it and neither has anybody asked.

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Monday and eleven people came over the counter, the last let in at ten past five. **The counter was handed over at half past four for about an hour because of a queue of four, and the woman of about thirty-four came back to it at about ten to six and did not say anything about having left it.**

Those four rooms off that service road are one thousand one hundred and sixty-two days, a hundred and sixty-six weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, warm. **The card standing on end in the rail is one thousand one hundred and sixty-six days, a hundred and sixty-six weeks and four days, four days past them, and the rail takes two and has taken two for about ten weeks.**

The hardboard's fourteenth line is nine hundred and ninety-eight days, a hundred and forty-two weeks and four days, and its fifteenth is nine hundred and seventy-seven, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks and four days, and the twenty-one days between them has been the same twenty-one days every day this year. The separation is five hundred and forty-two days, seventy-seven weeks and three days, shut in a form about nine weeks ago, walked every day since, and no shorter.

---

**The nine sheets were on a table in that room face up and six people in it did not read them, and that took nine minutes to establish and then never came up again.**

They had come down the road on the Saturday morning in a bag with a knot in it, given to him at a door by a woman of about thirty-one who said four things and all four of them were about the bag. **She did not tell him to read them and did not tell him not to. He did not read them on the bus and has not read them since and there are six people in this city who could tell him what is on each of them and not one of the six has been asked by anybody.**

Six addresses in this city on six sheets. Two addresses in towns this entry does not describe. And a ninth sheet with a line on it about a low building with a light on past a second row of shops, and a door on the left with nothing written on it. **About nine people in nine buildings are about to be handed, each of them, one sheet, and about nine people in nine buildings are not going to be told that there are eight others, and that is not a secret and is not a policy. It is what a piece of paper is.**

---

The woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs for another body came in at ten past three and sat down in the chair nearest the window, which is the chair she has sat in every time anybody in this city has put her in a room. **A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard. The woman of about thirty-four. The man of about twenty-two. A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse, who had come on his own and had not said why, and who nobody at that table asked why, because a man who comes on his own to a room about nine chairs is doing the only thing he is able to do about it and asking him is a way of stopping it.**

Five people. The ninth chair is hard against a wall with its back to the room. **It did not move, it has not moved since the spring, and nobody has ever said in this city who put it there.**

The woman of about fifty-two looked at the table for four seconds and said the first thing she had said in any room since the Wednesday of week 232, which was one word, and said in that room again, and has not said in this city since.

"**Right.**"

---

**And then she said the thing that made this a room, and it took nine seconds and there was no theory in it and there was not one clause of it about why.**

The question had been put by the man of about thirty-eight in eleven seconds and it was: **who takes nine sheets of paper to nine places when three of the nine are a day and a night of bus travel and one of them is a town this city has not been to since the spring.**

The woman of about fifty-two said: **"Nell Farrow. She does ironing three days a week on a wide road and she has a season ticket and she has never in her life been given anything to do by anybody who chairs anything, and she will do it because she has got the days, and not one person in that building will ever find out that she did it, and that is the arrangement and not an accident.**"

Nobody said anything. **A man of about sixty-one wrote a number in his column, which he has never totalled, and did not write the name down, and the number he wrote was not that name.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody, and the woman of about thirty-four said afterwards, in the corridor and not to him, that the woman of about fifty-two had said a name and not a proposal and that in nineteen years she had never once said a proposal in a room with about nine chairs in it, and that this was not modesty, because a person who is modest does not do it in nine seconds.**

**He wanted, for eleven minutes in that room, to be the person she turned to when she said it. That is the want and it did not get said. She said the name to the table and not to him, and he was standing at the side of that table with a bag that had held nine sheets of paper for two days and a night and a bus, and he has carried more paper further this month than anybody in this city, and nobody in that room turned to him, and the reason is that he has been a room and a room is not a person anybody says a name at.**

---

**And the small thing he gave up on the Monday was four seconds long and it was that he did not write any of the nine addresses down.**

He has the ninth of them in his own hand on the back of a docket from a town, with the ninth one wrong in one place, and there is a knot-bag on a table in a second floor in Crown Terrace with six more in it, and he took a pen out at half past three and put it back in the fourth pocket of a tool roll in four seconds without writing anything. **A man who has nine addresses written down is a man rooms can be found at, and he has spent about four months refusing to be that, and the refusal has now cost him the only piece of administration he had left in the world and it bought him nothing at all.**

**What he wanted to do instead, and did not, and has not worked out why he did not, was to put his own name at the top of the docket. There is no column for it. There has never been a column for it.**

---

**There was a sheet of paper on that table that is not any form anybody in this city has filled in before.**

It had a ruled line at the top and then a box for a date and a box for a number and a signature at the foot, and above the two boxes there was a second ruled line with nothing on it, and below the signature there was a third one. **Four people at that table looked at it and none of them read it out, and the woman of about fifty-two put her hand flat on it once and did not turn it round, and it is the only piece of paper in this city that has three ruled lines on it and there is no drawer in this city that it has been in.**

---

**At about four o'clock there was a woman of about twenty-four in the doorway of that room and she did not come in.**

She had a bag over her shoulder and she had been in that building before and the woman of about thirty-four knew her and said one word, which was her trade, and she said it back and came about two feet into the room and stopped. **She looked at the nine sheets on the table for four seconds and did not touch them, and then she said, in nine seconds, in a voice with no particular weight in it at all: "Is that nine, or is that eight and a description."**

"It's nine."

"**Right, that's what I was told,**" she said, and did not come further into the room, and nobody asked her anything else, and she went back out and down that stair with the bag empty and came back up it four minutes later with the bag full.

**Nobody in that room thanked her and she did not wait to be. She is about twenty-four, she does ironing three days a week on a wide road, she has a season ticket, and nothing in this entry or in any other improves her situation by a single thing, and there is no list in this city with her name on it and there is not going to be one.**

---

From half past six the chair nearest the door in the fourth of those four rooms was taken by one other person, thirty-odd, and she kept it until about ten. Nothing was put to her and nothing ever will be. The eighth of eight, which carries her name at the head of it, is in a ring binder at the back of a shelf in that room and it did not come out of it.

Two places in a rail and neither of them turned. A book with a green cover on sixty-two lines, and a tin on seventy-three at the far end of a table in a room a man was not in. **Nothing was given out loud on this Monday anywhere, and this entry does not say what a sitting does when there is none of it.**

Six objects get named on this page and no sentence in it sets two of them beside one another: a form with two boxes in it, a hand copy of the front of a page, a sheet that came back to a room, a plate of iron with four slots, a card on end in a rail of two, and a docket with nine places along its back.

---

**The man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen went past that shop at about one and did not come in, and went past it again at about a quarter to two and did not come in, and Marek was at the bench both times with his back to the glass.**

He saw him the first time and did not go to the door. He saw him the second time and counted four seconds before the man was round the corner, and he did not go to the door then either, and the reason is not politeness and it is not the rule on a piece of paper in a fourth pocket.

**A man who is owed a thing has a way of standing in a doorway that everybody can see from the inside, and if you come out to him he has to say what he came for, and he has not come for anything, he has come past, and there are about four people in this city who would tell him that a person in a shop is the person things go to and that is the whole of what happened to him in the spring.**

He stayed at the bench. **The eight of the nine lines on the back of that hand that had not been gone over with a thumb had been gone over since Monday, all eight of them, and the ninth had not been touched at all, and nobody in this city asked him about that and nobody is going to.**
