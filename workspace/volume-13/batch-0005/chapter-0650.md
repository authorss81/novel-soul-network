# Chapter 650 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Room Up A Stair In Saltmarket At About A Quarter Past Four Where About Eleven People Sit Every Fourth Wednesday, A Count Of Fifty-Three Of Which Forty-Eight Correspond Given First In About Nine Seconds And On No Other Day Of This Fortnight, Nine Items At About Four Minutes Each And A Tenth That Was Not An Item, A Pen Picked Up And Put Down, And A Book That Shut Again On Sixty-Two Lines

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a room up a stair in Saltmarket at about a quarter past four. This one is a number given first in nine seconds, nine items, a tenth that was not an item, and a book on sixty-two lines before the hour and on sixty-two lines after it.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage, which it has now done three times in eleven years.

Those four rooms off that service road are one thousand one hundred and seventy-eight days, a hundred and sixty-eight weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, warm. **The card in the rail, on its end, is one thousand one hundred and eighty-two days, a hundred and sixty-eight weeks and six days, four days past them, and the rail takes two and has taken two for eleven weeks.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and seventy-four days, a hundred and twenty-four weeks and six days. The hardboard's eighteenth is eight hundred and ninety-six, a hundred and twenty-eight weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and eleven, a hundred and fifteen weeks and six days.

The separation is five hundred and fifty-eight days, seventy-nine weeks and five days, shut in a one-line box in a form about nine weeks ago and walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**At about a quarter past four a woman of about sixty opened a book with a green cover on a table in that room and said the number before anything else, which is what she has always done, and she said it once, and it is not said again until the fourth Wednesday.**

"Fifty-three."

"**Of which?**" said the man of about sixty-one.

"**Forty-eight correspond.**" She said it the way a person reads a temperature. "**Up by one. It is always up by one. The five that do not correspond are the same five and I have never shown them to anybody and I am not going to start this afternoon.**"

Nobody asked her about the five.

**That is the only number that has been given out loud in this city or in any other today, it was given in nine seconds, and nine of the other ten days of this fortnight carried no sitting and announced nothing, and this entry does not say what a sitting does when there was none.**

---

**There were about eleven people in that room.**

A woman of about sixty who has held it nineteen years and is paid by nobody. A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard and one column of numbers that has never been totalled. **A woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs and who was there as a person, and who said one word on the Wednesday of week 234 and has not said anything in this room since.** A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm and has a list face up in front of her. A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse. Six others, two of them new.

**And behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place, and it has been empty for eight weeks, and about five people in that room had noticed, and not one of them had said so out loud at any point in the evening.**

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room. It did not move, nobody in that room moved it, and nobody in this city has ever said who put it there.**

---

**Nine items, four minutes each, and the sixth of the nine was the arrangement itself and it took two minutes longer than the other eight.**

A boiler. A date printed in two hands. A work address and not a house. A name in a ledger. A van that had not come. **A thing about how long the public arrangement takes now, put by a man of about forty-nine who had a folder and who was told the answer in four seconds by a person entitled to give it.**

"It was nine days slower in the spring."

"And now?"

"**One day slower,**" he said. "**Nine to one. That's the figure. The reason is that nobody has to be found any more, and a thing that does not have to be found is a thing that goes where it is told.**"

"And the reason that is not true?"

"**That about four hundred people still have one of theirs going to a single room,**" he said, **"and they are exactly the people who have to be found, and nobody has asked them, and one day slower is what nine days was before anybody worked out which people the nine days was for. It is not a straight line and I am not going to draw it for you.**"

Nobody wrote it down. **The man of about sixty-one wrote a number in his column and it is not either of those two numbers, and he has not been asked what it is and will not be.**

---

**A copy of a letter already answered. A form to be reprinted with a bigger box. A man who wanted to know whether he had to keep a thing he had already handed in, and was told in four seconds, by a person entitled to say it, that he did not.**

**The book with the green cover was face down on that table until ten past six.** It has sixty-two lines in it, and it had sixty-two lines in it at the start of the hour, and it had sixty-two lines in it at the end of the hour, and the difference between those two facts is not a thing anybody in that room can tell you about and nobody in that room tried.

At the far end of that same table there is a tin. It has a lid. Seventy-three is what is in it. **Nobody lifted that lid at any point in the hour and nobody has ever converted those two figures into one and there is a receipt from a shop in it as a page marker and it is not hers.**

---

**The tenth thing was not an item and it came up that stair on its own at half past five, and it is a man of about forty-six who keeps a launderette on a wide road, and he had a question and it was nine seconds long.**

He said his name and a woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her gave it back to him out of the list without looking down, which is what she is for.

Then he said: **"I do ironing three days a week and I've been given nine sheets of paper and I want to know one thing and then I'll go. Is there anybody I can give a sheet to if the room's shut when I get there. Because there's a Saturday and a Sunday and there's four in that lot that shut at half past three and I stood outside one of them on the Friday and I didn't want to be stood outside one of them with a bag.**"

"**No,**" said the woman of about sixty. "**There isn't. You hand it back and you put it on the table in that room and you don't go back in, and that's not a rule anybody made. That's just what happens to a piece of paper when a room isn't there. It's been happening for four years and it's the correct thing and it will go on being the correct thing, and I'm not going to sit here this afternoon and tell you that it's a fault.**"

"**Right,**" said the man of about forty-six.

---

**And she turned the book over, and picked up the pen, and held it for nine seconds, and put it down on the table beside it.**

**Nobody in that room said a word.** Not the woman of about fifty-two, who was entitled to say what nine rooms are and who had said one word five weeks ago in a different room and had not repeated it. Not the man of about sixty-one with the column. Not the man of about thirty-eight, who has a warehouse and a rota in about four months that has his name on it and did not raise it. Not the woman of about sixty, who had the pen in her hand nine seconds earlier and has not said one word about it and will not say one word about it to anybody in this city for as long as she lives.

**Nobody asked her why it did not open. Nobody has asked her anything in eight weeks. The count went up by one anyway, and the five that do not correspond are the same five, and the reason the book shut is on the page as four separate facts and is not explained anywhere in this entry, and nobody in that room tried.**

---

**About eleven people began putting their coats on at ten past six. The man of about forty-six went down that stair before any of them, and he had a bag with him, and he had nine sheets in it, and he is going to hand one back on that table on a Monday.**

**He was not in that room. He was in this city, in four rooms off a service road, with a copy of a form folded once in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, and he has not gone up that stair in about four weeks, and he did not go up it on this Wednesday, and there was nothing in that room he could have done and he was not asked for anything and nobody would have spoken to him.**

He is twenty-two and he is standing exactly where he was standing on the first page of this stretch of days and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off. **What has gone is a fortnight, a list of nine addresses, a man on a road, and a place in a room up a stair, and the last of those four was taken off him in nine seconds on a Monday by a person entitled to take it and it was not a punishment and he has not said so and is not going to.**

**What he wanted, for the whole of that evening, was to be able to say the number out loud to somebody in this city. Fifty-three, of which forty-eight. It belongs to a room he was not in, and there is nobody in this city he would say it to, and that is the whole of what he wanted and it did not get said.**

The rail holds two. Two nails and nineteen lines and no twentieth, and the twentieth has never been opened. Nine hand copies of the front of a page, seven hundred and forty-four days, a hundred and six weeks and two days, eight unfinished, the first row nobody has disagreed about still not found, and **not one of the nine laid against another on any day of this year.** The post at the end of that corridor is seven hundred and twenty-six days, a hundred and three weeks and five days. The separation, five hundred and fifty-eight days, seventy-nine weeks and five days, shut in a form about nine weeks ago and subtracted every day since, no shorter, nothing put back into it.

That drawer under a bench is shut with the same four sheets in it and the fourth of those has never been looked at by anybody. The four words that name the public body are not on this page. **Six objects are named here and no two of them are brought together: a sheet returned to a room, a card on end in a rail, a form with three ruled lines, a job docket with nine places pencilled along its back, a hand copy of the front of a page, and a plate of iron with four slots.**
**At about eight he was in the second of those four rooms and there was one other person in it, and she was about thirty, and she was sitting where the door is, and she was still sitting where the door is at about ten.**

Three and a half hours, and not one person in that building put a word to her at any point in them, and nobody ever will, and there is nothing owed to her in this city by anybody in it and no apology of any kind has ever been offered to her. **The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight and lies in a ring binder on a back shelf of that room, and that binder was not opened in her presence or out of it, and it did not come out on that day.**

He was in the same room and he did not look at her, and there are about four people in this city who could say that a man not looking at a woman is respect or is cowardice depending on which of them is asked, and nobody in that building was asked, and the light in that stair went off at about ten.


---

**At about eight he was in the second of those four rooms and the board of two nails was on the wall, and there are nineteen lines on it, and the space under the nineteenth is about four inches of nothing that has been four years old.**

He put his thumb in it. **It is dust, and it is dust about four inches deep at the edges and thinner in the middle where something has been wiped at some point in a way that was not deliberate, and there is a clean band about two inches across in the middle of it where a hand went once.**

He has looked at that space about nine times in four years and has never put a line in it and has never taken the board down, and there is no form in this city on which a person records that they have looked at four inches of nothing and found a clean band in it, and nobody in this building has ever asked him about it and nobody ever will.

**He put his thumb in it for four seconds and took it out and went and sat in a chair that is not by the door, and at about ten the light in that stair went off, and the woman who had been sitting by the door since half past six was still sitting by the door.**

He has been twenty-two for about four months and he has been in this city since he was a boy and he is not going to be a thing anybody sends a sheet of paper to, and there is nobody in this building who has ever asked him what he is, and the four inches under the nineteenth line is the only thing put in front of him today that nobody had explained and nobody was going to.
