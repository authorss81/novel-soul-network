# Chapter 641 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Works In A Kitchen Standing At That Counter Having Been Told By Somebody Else, A Question In About Nine Seconds That Cannot Be Answered Because There Is No Other List, The Eighth Line Of Nine Gone Over Four Times With A Thumb, And A Chair By A Door Taken At Half Past Six

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road after half past six. This one is a man in a kitchen at that counter, a question in nine seconds, a list that exists on the back of a hand, and a woman about thirty in a chair by a door from half past six until ten.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Monday and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five; that counter stayed in the same pair of hands from opening to shutter, because the queue never got past two.

Four rooms off that service road are one thousand one hundred and fifty-five days, a hundred and sixty-five weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, warm. **The card in that rail is on end and is one thousand one hundred and fifty-nine days, a hundred and sixty-five weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.**

The post at the end of that corridor is seven hundred and three days, a hundred weeks and three days. The nine hand copies of the front of a page are seven hundred and twenty-one days, a hundred and three weeks to the day, eight of the nine unfinished, the first row nobody has disagreed about still not found, and **not one of the nine has been laid against another on any day of this year.**

The separation is five hundred and thirty-five days, seventy-six weeks and three days. It was shut in a one-line box in a form about nine weeks ago and it has been subtracted every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**The man of about thirty-four came in at about twenty past four and he had been told, and it had not been him that was told it.**

A woman of about twenty-nine who reads things out loud in shops had said it to him where he works, in nine seconds, on the day before yesterday, and she had said it correctly, and she had said it to about four other people in three other shops since, and none of the four had asked her where she had got it. **He came in with his hand turned over on the counter, palm down, the way a person puts down a thing they have been carrying, and it is not a gesture and he did not know he was doing it.**

**He has nine things written on the back of that hand in grease pencil in the writing of a man with a clipboard at a gate, put there on a Friday about a fortnight before any of this started, and he has never had them in any other form, and about four people in this city now know what is on the back of that hand and not one of the four has ever been asked to hold it.**

The eighth line of the nine has been gone over four times with the pad of that thumb, which is grey at the first joint from a kitchen and not from a counter. The other eight are as they were. **He has not crossed any of them out and he has not written one more, and the going over is not a decision and is not a correction and is what four hundred repetitions of a thing look like on a hand.**

---

He put the hand down and turned it over, so that the nine were face up, and he did not push them at anybody, and he said:

"**Do I still send it to one of the nine, or is there a different nine.**"

"**There's no different nine.**"

"**Right,**" he said, and then, in about four more seconds, because he is not a slow man and there was nothing slow in the question: **"Then what's the other list. There's got to be an other list. You've had a fortnight and a van and there was a woman at the gate reading nine off a door, and a fortnight's not nothing, and a fortnight's got a list at the end of it.**"

---

**And Marek did not have one, and he took four seconds over it, and the four seconds were not him thinking of an answer. They were him finding out that there was not one.**

He had the ninth of a form in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, folded once, with a number legible and a date legible and a name at the bottom, and it is a form about a man and not about a list, and he did not put it on the counter, and the reason he did not is printed at the end of this entry and it is not the reason anybody in the room would have given.

He said: **"There isn't one. I asked eight people out loud and got eight yeses and one no, and I wrote the nine on a docket and then I wrote a word under it and then I did nothing with the docket, and that was the whole of the administration. The nine on your hand went to a gate and onto a van door from a man with a clipboard who read them out of a list he was given. I never gave him that list. I have never had that list.**"

"**So who has.**"

"**I don't know,**" Marek said. **"That is the true answer and you have had a worse one in a queue. There is a man of about forty-one in this city with a pencil tally on the end of a paint stirrer and a firm that pays him, and the tally is a number one man keeps and no one has ever checked it, and he has rubbed it out and begun it again every Friday since, and that is the nearest thing to an other list that there is in this city and it is a piece of wood in a garage.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four looked at him for about two seconds and then said, **"That's all right,"** and it was not a forgiveness and it was not a complaint and it was the answer of a man who had come to a counter with a question and had got one.

He wanted to shout. That is the want and it did not get said, and it is worth printing that he wanted it, because he had spent about four months of this year learning that a room will say almost anything at a person who is not careful, and he had built nine seconds on the counter in front of him in which the man in the kitchen would have said the thing that has been sitting in the back of his own chest since the Monday, and instead the man in the kitchen said *that's all right* and put his hand in his pocket.

**He did not apologise, and that was the small thing he gave up on the Monday, and it cost him four seconds and nobody in that shop could have told you what the four seconds were.**

A man who says sorry at a counter converts a thing that happened into a thing he has taken responsibility for, and he is on a piece of paper that says in nine words that he is not the person anything comes to, and the two of those cannot be in his mouth at the same time. **He has said sorry to nobody since and is not going to start.**

---

The woman of about thirty-four was on the counter the whole time and heard both halves of it, and at about ten to five she said, without stopping what she was doing, **"You could have told him on the Friday. You had a Friday.**"

"**I could have.**"

"**You were on a form by the Monday, and you told nobody, and now a woman who reads things out loud in shops has told four people in three shops and the fifth one is standing in front of you.**" She put a meter down. "**That's not a complaint, it's a rota. You've been removed from a route and the route's still got your handwriting on it. That's going to keep happening for a while and there is nothing you can do about it, and the one thing you could have done about it was on a Friday and you were busy.**"

"**I was on a form.**"

"**I know what you were on,**" she said. "**I've got a copy of mine. It isn't in the drawer either, and I'd not have told you that if you hadn't said it in front of him.**"

---

**And after half past six there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms, and she was about thirty, and she took the chair nearest the door at half past six and she was still in it at about ten.**

She is not named in this entry and nobody in that building said anything to her at any point in those three and a half hours, and nobody has ever said anything to her, and no apology of any kind is owed to her there by anybody in it, and none has ever been offered. **Her page is the eighth of eight and lies in a ring binder at the back of a shelf in that room, the binder untouched in her presence and out of it; there are eight pages and the first seven are on the shelf above it, and none of the eight has been in a hand since the spring.**

He wanted, in four minutes between half past six and about ten, to be the person a room sends a thing to, and there is no arrangement left on which that can happen, and he is twenty-two and he has been the person rooms send things to since he was old enough to carry a bag. **That is the second thing he wanted today and it is a different thing from the first, and neither of them was said, and the day did not ask him for either.**

---

The rail holds two. Two nails and nineteen lines and no twentieth, and the twentieth has never been begun. The drawer under that bench is shut with four sheets in it and the fourth of the four has never been looked at by anybody. The book with the green cover is on sixty-two lines. The tin is on seventy-three with the lid on. **A count is announced at a sitting and on no other day, and this Monday carried no sitting, and this entry does not say what a sitting does when there was none.**

Six objects get named in this file and not one clause of it lays any two of them together: a hand copy of the front of a page, a card standing on end in a rail of two, a plate of iron with four slots cut into it, a form carrying two boxes, a docket with nine places pencilled along its back, and a sheet that came back to a room.

---

**At about one he put a job down and did the thing he has done about four times in five weeks, which is to open the fourth pocket of a tool roll on a bench and look at what is in it.**

There are five things in it. **Four pieces of paper that are not his, which have not been his since the spring and which he has never once read to the bottom of.** A docket with nine places pencilled along the back and a word under the nine that says ask them first, in his own hand, written in a shuttered shop. And a copy of a form, folded once, with a number legible and a date legible and a woman's name at the foot of it, given to him about eight days ago in a room with about nine chairs in it by a person who did not ask him first.

He put them back in the same order, which is an order he has never written down, **and he did not read the fourth one again, and he noticed that he knows what is on the first three without looking at them and has never once known what is on the fourth.**

That is a thing a man of twenty-two learns by carrying a bag for about four months, and there is no form in this city on which a person records the order of a pocket. He closed it and went back to the job, and the job took nine minutes longer than the day sheet allowed it.

---

*One thousand one hundred and fifty-five and seven hundred and three are both whole numbers of weeks this month, and neither of them is a count of anything that happened, and one thousand one hundred and fifty-nine is four days on from the first of them and the rail it stands in takes two.*

*The true answer, and four seconds of not apologising.* **A man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen put nine things on a counter with the back of his hand face up and asked in nine seconds whether he should still send a thing to one of the nine or whether there was a different nine, having been told the day before yesterday by a woman of about twenty-nine who reads things out loud in shops. The eighth of the nine had been gone over four times with the pad of a thumb grey at the first joint. He was told there was no other nine, and that the nearest thing to one in this city is a pencil tally on the end of a paint stirrer in a garage, kept by a man a firm pays, who has never been asked what it is for, and who rubs it out every Friday and begins it again. He said that was all right, which was an answer and not a forgiveness, and put his hand in his pocket. He wanted to shout and did not. He did not apologise, because a man who apologises at a counter turns a thing that happened into a thing he has taken responsibility for, and there is a piece of paper in a fourth pocket that says in nine words that he is not the person anything comes to. A woman of about thirty-four told him at about ten to five that there is now a rota of people finding out what he did, that the route still carries his handwriting, and that the one thing he could have done about it was on a Friday.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A man of about thirty-four learned in four seconds that the only list of nine places in this city is the one on the back of his own hand, put there by somebody else, and he learned it from a person who is not allowed to be the person anything comes to, and he went back to a kitchen.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, in that shop, or in those four rooms, and the word was not used here. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped, and a question that could have been answered with a sheet of paper was not, and there is no form in this city on which a man records that he had nothing to give. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two. A man arriving, being answered straightly, and saying *that's all right* does not go on it: what moved today is what nine lines of grease pencil on a hand turn out to be for, and neither of the two entries standing on that register since about four years ago was touched. From half past six the fourth of those four rooms held one other person, thirty-odd, in the chair nearest the door, and she was still in it at ten, and nobody in that building put one word to her and nobody ever will; her page, the eighth of eight, stayed in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and did not come out of it. Beneath that bench a drawer remains shut on four sheets, the fourth of them unlooked-at by anybody, and a drawer in a first floor in a second district holding eight other sheets is a different drawer and was not opened. Six things are named in this file and none of them is set beside another: a returned sheet, a rail card, a form with two boxes, a docket with nine rooms pencilled on its reverse, a hand copy, and an iron plate with four slots. The book with the green cover stands at sixty-two lines and the tin at seventy-three with the lid never lifted, and nothing was announced today, no sitting falling on it.*
