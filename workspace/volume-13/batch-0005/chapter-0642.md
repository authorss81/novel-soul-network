# Chapter 642 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Number Believed About Two Hours Late And Over In Two Seconds Of A Quiet Morning, A Corner Cut Off A List In A Sleeve That Nobody Will Account For, A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Whose Name Goes In No Pocket Of His, And A Bus At About Six In The Evening Out Of A Service Road For A Town This Entry Does Not Describe

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a bus out of a service road at about six in the evening. This one is a thing believed two hours late and then over in two seconds, a corner of paper nobody will account for, and a journey taken with one question in it and no apology.**

---

Eleven jobs with a date against each and eleven people over the counter on the Wednesday, the last let in at ten past five. The bench until two, the counter from two, and the counter not handed over, because on a Wednesday the whole of it was one pair of hands and there was no queue.

Those four rooms off that service road are one thousand one hundred and fifty-seven days, a hundred and sixty-five weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, warm. **The rail holds a card on end, and that card is one thousand one hundred and sixty-one days, a hundred and sixty-five weeks and six days, four days past them, and the rail takes two and has taken two for about nine weeks.**

The nineteenth line on the board of two nails is eight hundred and fifty-three days, a hundred and twenty-one weeks and six days, and the twentieth is not begun. The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is seven hundred and ninety days, a hundred and twelve weeks and six days, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is seven hundred and sixty-three, a hundred and nine weeks to the day, and those two have been twenty-seven days apart every single day since one of them was a man and the other was a wall.

The separation is five hundred and thirty-seven days, seventy-six weeks and five days, ended in a form, walked every day since, no shorter, nothing put back.

---

**She told him on the Tuesday, in a shop on a road he did not have to be on, in nine seconds, and he did not believe her for about two hours.**

A woman of about twenty-nine who reads things out loud in shops. She said: a room up a stair in Saltmarket, a Wednesday, a book with a green cover that did not open, a pen picked up and put down, and a man of about fifty who had come up on his own and asked one thing and then gone down the stair faster than he came up. She said it in the order it happened and she said it correctly and she did not say it twice.

He did not believe her because of the pen. **A person picks a pen up for a reason and a woman who has held a room for nineteen years does not pick one up and put it down again for nothing, and the whole of his objection was that a thing which is not on paper is not a thing.**

He believed it on the Wednesday morning, on his own, in about two seconds, at about a quarter to ten, with nobody in the shop and a meter in his hand and no thought in it at all, and the reason he believed it was not the woman and was not the room and was not the pen.

**It was that she had not been asked either.**

A woman of about twenty-nine standing in a shop telling a man of about twenty-two something she had been in a room for, and nobody asking her a single question about it, and about four other people in three other shops since, and not one of the four has said back to her *were you all right*. **He has been that person for about four months. He got a woman of about sixty-one out of a shop in four seconds on a Monday nine weeks ago and the whole of what he gave her was a straight answer, and the shape of it is the same shape as this, and he was in it and he is on the other side of it now, and nobody has asked him whether he is all right and he has not asked her.**

---

**There is a corner of paper on the bench that came off a list, and it is about the size of a thumbnail, and it has a torn edge and one of the two lines of print on it is half a word long.**

The list is in a clear sleeve on the wall by the door and has been there for about four months and the corners of the sleeve have gone soft. He noticed the corner on the Monday and did not pick it up, and it is on the bench on the Wednesday morning and it was not on the bench on the Monday, and it has not been on the bench on any day this year, and there are four people in this city who could say how it got there.

He did not ask. He has not asked, and there is no form on which a person records that a corner of a list is on a bench, and he looked at it for four seconds and then he put it in the bin under the window, which is the right thing to do and which is not the same as knowing.

---

**What he wanted on the Wednesday, for most of the morning, was to be able to give the number away himself.**

Not the count, which is a number a woman of about sixty keeps and has given out loud four times in nineteen years and did not give today. **He wanted a figure of his own that he could put on a counter and hand to a person, and there is not one, and the reason there is not one is that every number in this arrangement has belonged to somebody else: a tally on a paint stirrer to a man of about forty-one, about nine hundred a year to a woman of about forty-one at a gate, a time of day to a woman of about twenty-six in a school on a wide road that he gave out loud and did not write down, and about four hundred and fifty people to nobody at all.**

He had none of it. **A man who has been the person a room sends a thing to for as long as he can remember does not have a single figure of his own by the time he is twenty-two, and this is not a thing anybody in this city has ever found out about him because there has never been a moment at a counter at which anybody wanted one.**

---

He did not write down the name of the woman of about twenty-nine, and that was the small thing he gave up, and it took him four seconds at the counter at half past eleven with a job in his hand he was not doing.

**He wrote down the day instead. The day, and the room, and the word pen.** No name, no trade, nothing that would let a person who read it put a face to it. A name in a shop is a thing a person can be found by, and he has spent about four months refusing to be that, and it turns out that refusing it does not come with a place to put the alternative, and the alternative was a piece of paper about a day, and he chose the paper.

---

At about two he was at the bench and the woman of about thirty-four put a job down in front of him and said, without any particular expression, **"You're going to ask her something.**"

"I'm going to ask her something."

"**You're not going to apologise.**"

"**No.**"

"**Good,**" she said. "**Don't. She's owed a month and an apology is nine seconds and it would be the cheapest thing anybody in this city has done this month, and she'd have to say something back, and she hasn't got the day for it.**"

---

**He got onto the bus at about six in the evening, out of a service road where he had been standing since half past five.**

It is a day and a night each way and the whole of it is four days there and back, and it goes on a Wednesday and on a Saturday, and it is the second time this year he has got onto it and the first time he has got onto it with a question in him that he had not rehearsed.

The question is not *what should be done about the fortnight*, because the fortnight is over and about four people in this city know that. **The question is that a thing with no name on it has to be answered for by somebody, and the man who had the name on it is on a piece of paper in a fourth pocket, and the only other name in this city that anybody in this city has ever taken something away from is in a town this bus goes to on a Wednesday and a Saturday, and she has been owed a month of decisions since the spring, and he has nothing to give her and is not going to pretend he has.**

He worked that out on the Wednesday morning at a bench with a job in his hand and did not write it down, and he wrote the day and the room and the word pen instead, and those three words are in a tool roll in Lattice Ward and have nothing to do with any of it.

---

After half past six there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms. She was about thirty and she was by the door and she was still by the door at about ten. **Not one person in that building put anything to her in three and a half hours and nobody ever will, and the eighth of eight is the page with her name at the head of it and it is in a ring binder at the back of a shelf in that room and did not come out of it.**

The rail holds two. The book with the green cover is on sixty-two lines. The tin is on seventy-three and the lid did not come off. **A count is announced at a sitting and on no other day, and this Wednesday was not one, and nothing was said aloud anywhere in this city or in any other, and this entry does not describe what a sitting does when there is none.**

Six articles are named on this page and no sentence in it sets any two of them beside one another: a plate of iron with four slots, a form carrying two boxes, a hand copy of the front of a page, a card on end in a rail of two, a sheet that came back to a room, and a job docket with nine places pencilled along its reverse.

---

**At about one he went into the back room and took a cardboard box off a shelf and put nine sheets of paper on the bench in the order they came out, which is the order they went in, and looked at them for four minutes.**

They are hand copies of the front of a page, and eight of the nine are unfinished, and **the first row that anybody would have disagreed about has never been found by anybody, and about four people in this city have looked at the front of a page together over about four months and have not once laid two of the nine against another.**

The seventh one has a corner torn off it. He has looked at that corner about nine times in five months and has not worked out when it happened or with what or whether the sheet it was torn from is in this city.

**He did not put two of the nine side by side. That was available to him for four seconds and it is the one thing in this shop that he is not allowed to do and has never done, and the reason is that two hands copying the same page and stopping in different places is the only evidence anybody has ever had that nine people saw something different, and there is no form in this city that a difference goes on.**

He put them back in the box in the same order and put the box on the shelf, and the ninth line on a board on two nails outside is eight hundred and fifty-three days old and has nothing to do with any of the nine copies and never has.

---

*Eight hundred and fifty-three and one thousand and seventy-seven are both six days on from a whole number of weeks and neither of them is a count of anything, and the second of those two is a line on a board on two nails in a shop and the first is a line on the same board and the two have never had anything to do with each other.*

*645. Wednesday of week 233, at ten. Eleven dated jobs, eleven names, the last let in at ten past five, the counter in one pair of hands all day and never handed over. A thing told on the Tuesday in a shop on a road he did not have to be on, believed two hours late and over in two seconds at a quarter to ten this morning for a reason that had nothing to do with the telling. Nine hand copies of the front of a page on a bench for four minutes and no two of them laid against another. A corner of paper about the size of a thumbnail in the bin under a window. A day, a room, and the word pen, written down in place of a name. A bus at about six in the evening out of a service road, a day and a night each way, four days there and back, and a question with no apology in it. Four rooms off that service road from half past six, a chair by a door taken at half past six and kept until about ten, and nobody thanked anywhere on this day at all.*

Callers: eleven. *Two seconds at a quarter to ten, and a bin under a window.* **A woman of about twenty-nine who reads things out loud in shops told a man of about twenty-two on the Tuesday that a book with a green cover in a room up a stair in Saltmarket had not opened on the Wednesday before, that a pen had been picked up and put down, and that a man of about fifty had come up a stair on his own and gone back down it faster than he had come up. He did not believe her for two hours and believed her on the Wednesday morning in two seconds with nobody in the shop, and the reason was that nobody had asked her either. He noticed a corner of paper on a bench, about the size of a thumbnail, with a torn edge and half a word of print on it, did not pick it up on the Monday, did not ask on the Wednesday, and put it in the bin under the window, which is the right thing to do and is not the same as knowing. He wanted a figure of his own to hand to a person at a counter and has not got one, because every number in this arrangement belongs to somebody else: a pencil tally to a man of about forty-one, about nine hundred a year to a woman of about forty-one at a gate, a time of day to a woman of about twenty-six in a school on a wide road that he gave out loud and did not write down, and about four hundred and fifty people to nobody at all. A woman of about thirty-four told him at about two that he was not going to apologise, and he said no, and she said good, and said an apology would be nine seconds and would be the cheapest thing anybody in this city had done that month, and that the woman would have to say something back and has not got the day for it.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen is walking about with nine things on the back of his hand and no other list, and a man of about twenty-two got on a bus at six in the evening with a question in him and no apology, and a woman of about twenty-nine has told five people a thing that five people have not asked her a single question about.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter or in those four rooms and the word was not used. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped, and an offer of an apology was turned down by the person it was made to. Six objects come named in this file and not one of them is laid against another: a sheet returned to a room, a card in a rail of two, a form with two boxes, a plate of iron with four slots, and a hand copy of a page front. A drawer under a bench stays shut on four sheets and nobody has read the fourth; the drawer in a first floor in a second district that holds eight others is not that drawer. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two and nothing here goes on it, because a man believing a true thing two hours late, and for a reason that is not the one he was given, has still changed what he got on to a bus with and what the bus was for, and the two entries on that register since about four years ago came through the day untouched. In the fourth of those four rooms from half past six there was one other person, thirty-odd, by the door until ten, and nothing was put to her, and her page, which carries her name at the head of it, is the eighth of eight, in a ring binder at the back of a shelf in that room, and it did not come out. Sixty-two lines stand in the book with the green cover and seventy-three in the tin, the lid never lifted on this Wednesday, and nothing was said aloud in this city or in any other today, because no sitting fell on it.*
