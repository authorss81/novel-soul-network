# Chapter 614 — A Friday, Eight Hundred And Fourteen Days On A Line Above A Bench And Six Hundred And Forty-Four Days On A Post At The End Of A Corridor Which Turn Out To Be Each Other, A Sheet About Four Years Old Read In About Nine Seconds Of A Face On A Landing, And A Man Who Did Not Take It Out Of The Building

**The Friday was a bench until two, a counter from two until about five, a landing in a second district from about five until about ten to six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was a sheet of paper about four years old being read in about nine seconds by two people, neither of whom said anything about it afterwards.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday and eleven customers over that counter, the last of them at about twenty past five. Bench to two, the counter from two to about five, the stair in the second district from about five, and the service road after that. **That flat's heating has now been above its winter setting for a fourth month, four names on the one tenancy are carrying the excess, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole with no one to make good to her.** Those four rooms are one thousand and ninety-six days, a hundred and fifty-six weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail on the front of that counter is one thousand one hundred days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The eighteenth line on the two nails above that bench is eight hundred and fourteen days, a hundred and sixteen weeks and two days, and the post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and forty-four days, ninety-two weeks to the day. And here is the whole of the odd thing about that pair, which has been sitting on those two numbers since Monday and which nobody in that shop has said out loud. Eight hundred and fourteen is how old that line is, and eight hundred and fourteen is also the day the post at the end of that corridor was set and has stood at the end of that corridor ever since. Six hundred and forty-four is how long that post has stood there, and six hundred and forty-four is also the day the eighteenth line was written on those two nails. In each pair one of the numbers is an age and one of them is a day a thing happened, and a figure on its own cannot tell you which of the two it is, and not one of the four is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, which this entry still declines to give days to.**

---

He went up that stair at about five on the Friday because the woman of about thirty-four had said *go on* and had then served a customer for about eleven minutes without looking up.

**The four trays were back on the shelf. The brown paper under them had lifted again in the same place. The sheet was on the table by the window, on its own, squared up with the edge of the table, and it had been squared up like that a long time ago, and it had been on that table for four days.**

**"You have not put it in the tray,"** he said.

**"No."**

**"Why not."**

**"Because I would have to have said out loud that I had put it in the tray, and there is nobody to say it to,"** she said, **"and I have not had anybody to say it to since the spring."**

---

**He read it in about nine seconds and the nine seconds were in his own face and not in anybody else's.**

It is a sheet about the size of a docket, off the back of a form, folded twice and unfolded once and folded again. **The name at the top is written in a careful flat hand that is not a doctor's and not a clerk's. The date is about four years ago. The room it names is given in full and there is nothing wrong with any part of it — a street, a building, a floor, a number, and the name of the room, which is not a number standing for a room but a word.** The boxes at the bottom have two of them ticked. **The signature at the foot of it is in a second hand, a worse hand, with a tremor in the upstroke of about four letters, and whoever signed it was not in a hurry, because the signature has a space after the surname, which nobody puts in when they are in a hurry.**

The creases have gone white and the ink on the creases is gone to nothing, and the ink in the flat middle of the sheet is still there.

---

**"Can I ask three things."**

**"Ask two,"** she said, **"and then stop, and it is not two and a half."**

**"Is the building still there."**

**"The building is there. I know because it is on the sheet and I have never once been there to look, and I am telling you what I know, and what I know is what is printed and what I have seen, and there is a difference between those two things that people in this city do not understand."**

**"Is the room still a room."**

**"I have not been. Somebody would have to go and stand in it."**

He asked the third thing and she said no before he had finished it, and it took about four seconds, and she was right.

**"No. You are not writing that room down, and I am not having it written down, and that is not mine to give and it is not yours to take, and it is a Tuesday's work and there is not a Tuesday in this week."**

---

**He wanted to know whose hand it was before he said a single other thing about it, and he did not get that, and not getting it cost him about four minutes of the best part of his week and he has not yet found out what it was going to be for.**

**What he said instead, and it was the correct thing and it was the small thing he gave up, was: he did not put his hand out for it.**

**He could have taken it. There was nobody in that room to stop him, the man with the bag was on the step and had gone, and the sheet is a sheet of paper and it weighs nothing and it was within reach of his right hand for about four seconds. He did not take it and he did not ask to take it and he did not say he would bring it back, and the reason is the same reason she gave for not carrying it down a stair on the Monday, which is that it is hers until she says otherwise, and a man who has been told that a thing is not his has no business putting it in his coat.**

**And the cost of that is that the only copy of it in the world is on a table by a window in a first floor in a second district, and if it is lost, it is lost, and there is no second one, and he will not be the person who lost it and he will not be the person who kept it and that is exactly the shape of his week.**

---

**Nothing else happened on that landing.** He did not ask her about the fourth tray, he did not ask how many sheets were in the other three, and he did not ask her to write the date down. He put it back where it had been, squared with the edge of the table, and she said **"Thank you,"** and he has thought about that one word more than he expected to, and the reason he has thought about it is that it is the first time in about four months that anybody in that room has said it to anybody.

---

**At about half past six, the four rooms off that service road. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall, and he has not been given a written thing and has not been asked for one. A woman of about thirty sat in the chair by that door for the whole evening, untouched by anybody, and her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf and it stayed in the binder and the binder stayed shut, and no one in those four rooms has ever said sorry to her for a single part of it.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is at two and this Friday did not add to it, and the case is a better one than most.** A man who reads a sheet in nine seconds and does not take it out of a room is not a correct act, because he has not done a thing at all, he has refrained from a thing, and the two on the register are the two that have been there for about four years and both of them are the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns. **The one correct thing done on that landing on the Friday was a woman of about sixty-eight saying thank you to a man who put a piece of paper back where it had been, and a person saying thank you for putting something back where it was has not changed anything, and that is precisely what that register is for, and she has not been thanked for anything else in four months and neither has he.**

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter. Nobody thanked anybody in four rooms off a service road. The shutter came down at about ten, and it comes down at about ten on every working day, and there is no Sunday inside this week and this shop does not trade on one.

**Underneath all this is a set of numbers and a shape, and it is written out once.** The man of twenty-two can do, on this fourteenth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and nothing was added to him and nothing taken off him, and having read a thing in nine seconds is not a capability. The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred days in a rail of two and it did not move. Two nails, nineteen lines, and the eighteenth of them is eight hundred and fourteen days, which is also the day a post went in at the end of a corridor in another building in this city, and the two have not been joined to one another by anybody and are not going to be. **The nine hand copies of the front of a page on that far shelf are six hundred and sixty-two days, ninety-four weeks and four days, eight of the nine still unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and nobody put two of them together on the Friday.** The separation is four hundred and seventy-six days, sixty-eight weeks to the day, ended, and the figure above is not a thing that has been restored and is not a thing that has been stopped. The drawer under that bench is shut, four sheets in it, the fourth never read, and it was not opened. Those four words naming the public body are on none of these pages. A return sheet and a card in a rail and a form with a blank on it and a plate of iron with four slots in it are four different objects and no line in this entry has put any two of them next to one another.

*617. Friday of week 224, at ten, and this entry is a bench until two, that counter from two to about five, a first floor in a second district from about five until about ten to six, and the service road after half past six, eleven dated jobs and eleven customers with the last at about twenty past five, two numbers on one wall that turn out to be each other, one sheet about four years old on a table where it has been for four days, nine seconds of a face, three questions proposed and two asked and one refused in four seconds, a sheet put back and squared to the edge of a table, one thank you, and nobody thanked at any other point in it.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Friday carried eleven jobs, each one dated, and eleven people over the counter, the last of them at about twenty past five.** The order: bench, counter, second district, service road. The rooms: one thousand and ninety-six days, a hundred and fifty-six weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, warm. The Exchange: no sitting on this day, and no count, and nothing in this entry says what a sitting does. *Nine seconds, three questions, two answers and one refusal.* **A sheet about the size of a docket came off the back of a form, folded twice and unfolded once and folded again, and was read in about nine seconds by a man of about twenty-two, and the nine seconds were in his own face and in nobody else's.** On it: a name in a careful flat hand that is not a clerk's and not a doctor's; a date about four years old; a room given in full and correct in every particular, with a street, a building, a floor, a number and the name of the room rather than a number standing in for a room; two boxes ticked; and a signature in a second and worse hand with a tremor in the upstroke of about four letters, a space after the surname, and no hurry in it anywhere. Of the two questions he got answered: the building is there, and she has never been inside the room, and what she knows is what is printed and what she has seen, and people in this city do not understand the difference between those two things. Of the third question, refused in about four seconds before it was finished: no writing that room down, because it is hers, not his to take, and it is a Tuesday's work, and there is not a Tuesday in this week. What he wanted and did not get: to know whose hand it was before he said anything else about it. What he gave up, on no form at all: he did not hold out his hand for it, did not ask to take it, and did not offer to bring it back. It is the only copy in the world and it is in a first floor in a second district, and he is not the man who has it and not the man who lost it. He put it back square with the edge of the table. She said thank you, once, and it is the first word anybody in that room has said to anybody in about four months. Nobody else was thanked anywhere on the Friday.*

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **Two numbers, one of them a line and one of them a post, turned out on a Friday to be each other, and a man read a sheet in nine seconds and put it back where it had been.** Nobody thanked anybody, once. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and a man who reads a thing and does not take it out of a room has not done an act at all, he has refrained from one, and the two on the register are the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, and the one correct thing done on that landing was somebody saying thank you for putting a piece of paper back where it was. Four objects — a return sheet, a card in a rail, a form with an empty box on it, a plate of iron with four slots in it — and nothing in this file stands two of them side by side. The drawer is shut with four sheets in it and the fourth is still not read. Sixty-one lines in that book and seventy-three in that tin, and this day carried no sitting, and a file that describes a sitting that did not happen is inventing one.*
