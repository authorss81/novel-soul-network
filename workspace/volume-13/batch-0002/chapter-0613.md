# Chapter 613 — A Thursday, A Second Floor In Crown Terrace, A Man Of About Forty-Four Under The Stair Light With A Step Ladder And A Bag Of Bulbs And No Chair And No Invitation, Nine Addresses Said Out Loud By A Woman Who Is Allowed To Say Them And Written Down By Nobody, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Did Not Offer And Cannot Say Afterwards Which Nine They Were

**The Thursday was a bench until two, a counter from two until about four, a second floor in Crown Terrace from about four until about five, the counter again until six, and the service road after that, and its shape was a man of about forty-four told in about nine seconds that he had to go to nine addresses, and the woman who said them saying them out loud and writing none of them down.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, all dated, eleven customers at that counter, the last of them at about half past four because the early part of the afternoon had been taken. **The heat in that flat is on for a fourth month above where it is set in the winter, four names are paying the difference on that one tenancy, and the fortnight a cook laid into it out of her own wages in the spring is still owed by nobody to anybody.** Those four rooms are one thousand and ninety-five days, a hundred and fifty-six weeks and three days, four of them with a fifth behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail on the front of that counter is one thousand and ninety-nine days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The sixteenth line on the two nails is eight hundred and eighty-five days, a hundred and twenty-six weeks and three days, and the hold of a man of about thirty-three in another city is seven hundred and twenty-eight days, a hundred and four weeks to the day. The first is a line on a board in a shop in this city and the second is a length of time a person has been sitting on a thing he cannot hand over, and the two of them are on one wall of one room in this entry and neither of them is a day anything happened to any heating in this city, and no sentence here is going to pretend that it is.**

---

The second floor in Crown Terrace has about nine chairs and a table and a window on the back of a building. **There were about nine people in it at about four o'clock and one chair at the end of the table was empty and nobody said why, and nobody has said why on any day since Monday, and the person who moved that chair is not named in this entry and is not going to be named anywhere.**

**And under the light on the stair there was a man of about forty-four with a step ladder folded under his arm and a canvas bag with about nine spare bulbs in it and a clipboard, and he had been in that stair for about twenty minutes before anybody noticed him, because he is in that stair every Thursday and has been for about eleven years, and the light on that landing has been out since a Friday.**

He came in off the landing in the middle of the first thing that was said and put the ladder against the wall and said his name, which was Hollis Wren, and a woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs for a body of about nine and about a hundred and forty said his name back to him and then said the rest of it.

---

**She said it in about nine seconds and she said it to him and not to the room, and the room heard all of it.**

**"There are nine places in this city that have something nobody can get back to, and I have got the address of every one of them, and I am not able to go to any of them, and nobody else in this room is able to go to any of them either, and so it is going to be you, and I am telling you that rather than asking you, because if I ask you then you have been asked, and a man who has been asked can say no, and I have not got the fortnight to be told no by a stranger."**

**"Right,"** said the man of about sixty-one with the clipboard.

**"You are not the man,"** she said, without turning round. **"You are the man,"** she said to Hollis Wren, **"because you go past four of them on a Monday and you go past three of them on a Thursday and you have a bag and a sheet and nobody has ever once asked you what you are doing in a building, and I have needed that for about three weeks and I did not know it until this afternoon."**

---

**Then she said the nine addresses out loud, and she said them fast, and she did not write them down.**

A man of about thirty-one asked her to say them again. She said them again. **He wrote four of them on the back of his hand, badly, and got two of the streets wrong, and did not say that he had got them wrong, and the man of about thirty-three with nine crates in a yard looked at what he had written for about four seconds and then looked away from it.**

Nobody else wrote anything. **Afterwards there were four separate estimates in that room of how many addresses she had said, and one of them was eleven and one of them was seven, and neither was corrected, because correcting it out loud would have meant saying them out loud a third time, and everybody in that room had understood by the second reading that the point was not to have the list in this room.**

---

**A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse said that the answer was a meeting.**

**"A meeting,"** said the man of about thirty-three, **"of nine people, in a room, once a month, which we would call a federation, and it would last about an hour and a half, and we would agree to have a chair and a secretary and a set of rules, and inside four months it would be the chair and the secretary that the nine of us were talking to, and the returns would be sent to the chair."**

**"That is not what I meant,"** said the man of about thirty-eight.

**"It is what would happen,"** said the man of about thirty-three, **"and it would happen in about four months and none of the nine of us would notice it happening, and that is the same thing as what we have now, only with a nicer address on it."**

**"It is not the same thing at all,"** said the man of about thirty-eight, and he was not the only one in that room who said so, and about five other people agreed with him in the way that people agree and then keep talking.

**Nobody agreed with anybody. That is the whole of what that floor did between about four o'clock and about five, and it was done by nine competent people who have all been wrong about different things for different reasons and none of whom is a liar.**

---

**Hollis Wren said two things in about nine minutes and neither of them was about the returns.**

**"What time do they open."**

**"Varies,"** said the woman of about fifty-two. **"The clinic doesn't open to anybody. One of the schools shuts at half three."**

**"I'll go Monday,"** he said. **"I'm in that district Mondays and Thursdays with the lights, and I'll do the rest off a Saturday, and if a place isn't there I go back."**

**And that was all he said, and he did not tell anybody why he could do it and anybody who had wanted a reason could have asked him for one all evening and about four of them nearly did and none of them did, and the reason he gave for nothing was not given because he did not have one, he had a round, and that is a completely different thing from having a reason and is worth a great deal more and nobody in that room knew it.**

---

**He wanted to be the man who went. That is the plainest sentence in this entry and it is on the page because a decision not to act is on the page in this house and a want is not a decision and has to be written down separately.**

**He is the person every one of those nine places has been sending things to for about four years, and the reason she said it to a man with a bag of bulbs is that he is the one person in this city nobody has ever thought to send anything to. If he had said anything at all in that room, at about half past four on that Thursday, the sentence would have been about him, and then the man with the ladder would have been the second person to be sent to and the whole of the Thursday would have been a different and much smaller thing.**

**So he said nothing, and about five people did not notice, and about four did, and one of those four was the man of about thirty-three and he has not mentioned it and is not going to.**

---

**The small thing he gave up, and it is on no form and nobody knew he had given it: he did not ask for the list.** He has four pieces of paper in the fourth pocket of a tool roll with four halves of four addresses on them, written in pencil by four strangers at that counter, and any one of those four would have been enough to have a go at finding a ninth room, and he wanted the nine so badly on that Thursday that his hands were cold, and he did not ask, and the cost of not asking is that he cannot say this evening or next week which nine they were, and if somebody in a kitchen four days of bus travel away asks him which day he can come, he will not be able to answer without going back up that stair and asking a woman of about fifty-two to say nine addresses out loud a third time for the benefit of a man who is not going to write them down either.

---

**Off that service road again at about half past six, in four rooms. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall with a battery charger and nobody has ever handed him a written thing and nobody has asked him for one. The chair nearest the door had a woman of about thirty in it for the whole evening, and she was not spoken to, and no question was put to her, and nobody has ever apologised to her, and her page, which is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf, was in that binder and that binder was shut and was not opened at any point in the evening.**

**The count of things done right that made no difference is two and stayed at two on the Thursday.** Nine people who have never met spent an hour failing to agree, and that is not a correct act, because there was no act; **and the one correct act in that room was a woman of about fifty-two refusing to write down nine addresses, and that is not on the register either, because the register is a register of correct things that changed nothing, and she changed what nine people were going to do with the rest of their working lives, and the change is going to be visible in about four weeks and it will be visible to exactly one of the nine, in a town four days of bus travel away, and not for a while after that.** The two that are on it are still the two that have been on it for about four years, and both are the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns.

No thanks at that counter and none on that second floor and none in four rooms off a service road. **The shutter came down at about ten on the Thursday, and there is no Sunday inside this week, and this shop does not trade on one.**

**The rest of it is the shape and the figures and they are given once.** The man of twenty-two can do, on this thirteenth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week. The card in the rail is one thousand and ninety-nine days in a rail of two and it did not move. Two nails, nineteen lines, and there has never been a twentieth. **The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and sixty-one days, ninety-four weeks and three days, eight of the nine still unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and no two of the nine were compared on this Thursday by anybody in this city.** The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and forty-three days, ninety-one weeks and six days. The separation is four hundred and seventy-five days, sixty-seven weeks and six days, ended, walked, and not to be stopped. The drawer under that bench has not been opened and there are four sheets in it, and one of the four has never been read by anybody. Those four words that name the public body are on none of these pages. A return sheet, a card in a rail, a form with an empty box on it and a plate of iron with four slots in it are four different objects and no sentence in this file has put two of them together.

*Nine seconds, nine addresses, and nobody holding a list.* **The person told: a man of about forty-four called Hollis Wren, at about ten past four, on a second floor in Crown Terrace, with about nine people inside earshot and a step ladder leaning against a wall because the light on the stair had been out since a Friday.** Whether he was asked: no, and she said so out loud in front of the whole room — she was telling him rather than asking him, so that he could not answer with a no, and about four people on that floor did not think that was right. Her reasons, in about nine seconds: a bag, a sheet, a Monday through one district and a Thursday through another, and eleven years of never once being asked what he was doing inside a building. He did not ask for the four he has not got, and the cost is that in about a month there will be exactly one person in this city who can produce a ninth address, and he has already decided he will not ask her twice. Nobody on that floor was thanked and nobody at that counter was thanked.*

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **About nine people who have never met in their lives agreed on nothing for an hour and sent a man with a bag of bulbs out to do the one thing none of them could do, and the reason the thing was done and the reason the list was not written down are the same fact.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.
