# Chapter 619 — A Monday, Four Hessian Sacks Down Four Steps On A Handcart Carried By A Man Who Was Not Asked To Open Them, A Door With A Bar On The Outside Of It And A Light That Goes Off At About Six, A Man With A Bag Of Bulbs Told No In About Four Seconds By A Man Who Had Been Told No Three Weeks Earlier, One Stamp Refused, And Two Figures That Are Each Other

**The Monday was a bench until two, a counter from two until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was four hessian sacks going down four steps on a handcart and a room with a bar on the outside of its door that the man of twenty-two did not ask to be let into.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, all dated, eleven people over that counter, the last of them at about ten past five. **The heating in that flat is above its winter setting for a fifth month and will be above it for a sixth, four names on the tenancy are paying the difference out of four different incomes, and a cook is still a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and nobody has ever told her that anybody knows about it.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and six days, a hundred and fifty-eight weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and ten days, a hundred and fifty-eight weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The ask is seven hundred and ninety-six days, a hundred and thirteen weeks and five days, and the nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and seventy-two days, ninety-six weeks to the day. The first of those is the length of time since somebody put a question to a person instead of to an institution, and the second is the length of time since nine people began making the same page out by hand, and seven hundred and ninety-six is also the day they began, and six hundred and seventy-two is also the day the ask was put. So one number in each pair is an age and the other is a day something happened, and the figure by itself cannot tell you which, and not one of the four is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and this entry has stopped saying that and started saying it, and both are the same sentence.**

---

**The man with the handcart came at about ten in the morning on the Monday.** He is the man who has been carrying a bag up that stair since he was about thirty-one, and he is the same man, and he has not changed, and he did not go up the stairs at all on the Monday, because there were four sacks and a handcart and four steps and no reason to go up.

**He did not open them. He was not asked to open them and he did not ask whether he was allowed to, and he has not been asked by anybody and is not going to be.**

**"Eleven pounds is monthly,"** he said, **"and it goes to a landlord whose name is on a bit of paper, and the door has a bar on the outside of it, and the light goes off at about six, and I have been down there with the cart before and it is dry and it is cold and it is about the size of my kitchen."**

**"Whose name."**

**"It is on the bit of paper and the bit of paper is on the door and I have not read it and I am not going to read it,"** he said, **"because it is not my name and not my bit of paper and I have been carrying things up that stair since I was thirty-one and I have never once read anything."**

---

**The light going off at about six is the part that stayed with him, and it stayed with him on the Monday and it is still with him.**

A room with paper in it, and a bar on the outside of the door, and a light on a timer, and a landlord who is somewhere else, and about eleven pounds a month. **After about six in the afternoon that room is a locked room with paper in it, and it was a room that somebody could stand in this morning, and now it is not, and the person who could stand in it was a woman of about sixty-eight and she is not there and she is not going to be, and there is nobody who can be asked about it except a man with a cart who has never read a bit of paper in his life.**

**He did not say that to anybody. There was nobody on that pavement to say it to except the man with the cart, and the man with the cart was already going down the street.**

---

**Hollis Wren was in that corridor at about ten past four on the Monday, because the light on the landing had gone on the Friday and nobody had reported it, and he had come about the light and he came out of the stair and straight into a handcart with four sacks on it going down four steps.**

**"Can I take one down."**

**"No."** It was about four seconds and it was a man of about forty-four and there was no reason in it at all.

**"I said I'd carry things."**

**"You're carrying a lamp,"** said the man of about twenty-two, **"and you'll be back in here on Thursday and you'll not want a lamp. Go on."**

**And the man with the bag of bulbs stopped for about nine seconds, which is about four seconds longer than a man being told no usually stops for, and then he said: "Right,"** and went out, and he has not mentioned it since and will not, and about six weeks afterwards he will say the whole thing out loud, in a room, with two other people in it, and neither of the other two will understand why it matters, and the man of about forty-four will not explain it to them because he does not know.

---

**The man of twenty-two did not ask to be let into that room. He did not ask on the Monday and he has not asked since and he is not going to ask.**

The cost of not asking is that he does not know whether the four sacks are dry. **He does not know it now, he will not know it for about four months, and there is no way to find out from anybody, because the woman he lied to about a stair on Friday morning is the only person in this city who knows what happened, and the landlord is a name on a bit of paper that a man of about thirty-one has decided not to read, and there is no such thing in this city as a call to a landlord, and asking the woman of about thirty-four to find out would mean telling her that he had lied to her on Friday, and he would rather not know whether it is damp down there for about four months than say that sentence out loud at about two o'clock on a Monday.**

**He wanted to be the person who is let in. That is the whole of it and it took about four seconds on a Monday afternoon and it is the second time in three weeks he has wanted that and the first time he was told no about nine seconds' worth of it by somebody with a handcart.**

---

**At about half past three, with two people in the queue, a man of about thirty-eight who sells hire equipment put a sheet on that counter and asked whether Marek would post it for him.**

**It is a return. It is off the back of a form and it is about the size of a docket and there is a room written on the back of it in a hand that is not the man's, and it has his customer's number on the front of it, and the room on the back of it is in a district he has never been to.**

**"You could put it in the box at the end of your road,"** the man said. **"It goes Monday to Friday. It's nothing. It's one stamp."**

**"No."**

**"It's a stamp."**

**"I know what it is,"** he said, **"and I have watched nine people this week describe what it is and none of them has said stamp twice. If I post it, then a person who has never met me knows that a counter in Lattice Ward will take a sheet to a room, and that is true for about a month, and then a person will bring me nine of them instead of one and none of the nine will have my name on them, and the day after that there is a person in this city who says that if you want a return to go anywhere you take it to him, and I will not be able to say no to that person because they will have been told once already that I do this."**

**"It's one stamp."**

**"It's one stamp,"** he said, **"and I would rather it was nine stamps that went nowhere than one that went through me, and I know how that sounds at half past three on a Monday and I have said it at half past three on a Monday and it sounded the same then."**

The man of about thirty-eight put it in his own coat pocket. He did not say anything else about it, he paid for a part he did not need to buy in order to have something to pay for, and he has not been in since, and Marek has not found out whether it went on the Monday or whether it is in a coat in another district and neither of those two facts is going to arrive.

**The small thing he gave up on the Monday, and it is on no form: he told that man what the reason was, all of it, in about nine seconds, to two strangers in a queue, and there is nothing in this city that says he is not allowed to.**

---

**And at about half past six, the four rooms off that service road. That north wall, that man of about fifty-one, nothing in his hands, nobody after him for it. And the chair by the door with a woman of about thirty in it for the whole evening, and in that evening she was addressed by nobody, questioned by nobody, and nobody has ever said sorry to her, and the page that is hers — the eighth of eight, in a ring binder, on a shelf — stayed where it has always stayed, and the binder stayed shut.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is two and this Monday does not touch it, and the argument is a short one.** A man carrying four sacks down four steps without opening them is not a correct act that changed nothing, because the sacks are the whole of what four trays held and he has moved them from a room a person could stand in to a room a person cannot stand in after six, and that is not nothing, and nobody who did it was asked whether it was. **The two on that register have sat on that register for about four years, and both of them belong to one woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, and she does not appear on it today either.**

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, nobody thanked the man with the cart, and nobody thanked the man with the bag of bulbs for offering. **The shutter came down at about ten on the Monday, and easier than it had come down on the Friday, because the frame had dried out over the weekend, and he did not write that down either, and it is the first thing this week that has not been written down, and the counting starts again on a Monday whether anybody wants it to or not.**

**What is left is figures and a shape, and they are given once.** The man of twenty-two can do, on this ninth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and not asking to be let into a room has changed nothing about him except the state of his hands. The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and ten days in a rail of two and it did not move. Two nails, nineteen lines, no twentieth, nothing added. **The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and seventy-two days, ninety-six weeks to the day, eight of the nine still unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and on this Monday nobody put two of the nine together, and nobody in this city has put two of the nine together on any day in this week, and the seventh of them has a corner torn on it and that is the only thing that has happened to any of the nine in about four years.** The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and fifty-four days, ninety-three weeks and three days. The separation is four hundred and eighty-six days, sixty-nine weeks and three days, ended, walked, and not a figure that anything has been put back into. That drawer is shut with four sheets in it; one of the four has never been read and was not read on this Monday. Those four words that name the public body are on no page of this one, in no register, and in no denial of themselves. A return sheet and the card standing in that rail and a form with a blank still in it and the iron plate with four slots are four objects that this city keeps apart and this page keeps apart, and nothing in it sets two of them down side by side.
