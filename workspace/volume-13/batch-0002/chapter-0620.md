# Chapter 620 — A Wednesday, About Nine Seconds Said At A Counter By A Man With A Bag Of Bulbs Who Had Been Into Eight Rooms And Had Met Nobody In Any Of Them, About Four Hundred And Fifty People Who Could Each Say One True Thing And Nine Of Them Reachable And Not One With Anywhere To Put It, And A Man Who Did Not Write It Down

**That Wednesday was a bench until two, that counter from two until about six, and four rooms off the service road from half past six, and its shape was about nine seconds at that counter from a man with a bag of bulbs who had been into eight of the nine rooms and had met nobody in any of them, and writing it down improved nothing.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, a date on every one of them, eleven people over that counter, the last at about ten past five. **The heating in that flat is above its winter setting and will be above it in a sixth month, four names on that tenancy are paying the difference and none of the four has said to another of the four that they are paying it, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and has still not been told by anybody that anybody knows.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and eight days, a hundred and fifty-eight weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and twelve days, a hundred and fifty-eight weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The fourteenth line on the two nails is nine hundred and forty-four days, a hundred and thirty-four weeks and six days, and the post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and fifty-six days, ninety-three weeks and five days. A line on a board and a piece of cast iron at the end of a passage, and the first has been there since before that shop had a shelf of finished work and the second has been at the end of that passage since before the man who put it there finished his apprenticeship, and neither of those two numbers is a day on which anything happened to the heating in this city, and there is nothing in the sentence that says that except the sentence itself.**

**Hollis Wren came in at about half past three on the Wednesday and he was in the shop for about nine minutes and he was not a customer, and about four people were in the shop while he was in it, and two of them have repeated what he said afterwards and one of them has repeated it wrong.**

**"I've been into eight of them,"** he said.

**"And."**

**"And I've not met anybody,"** he said. **"Not one. Not in a corridor, not in a doorway, not on a stair. Two of them knew about the other two. One of them has heard of the woman at the water office. That's the whole of the knowing. Nine rooms and about nine people and not one of them knows one of the other eight."**

---

**And then he said the thing, and it took about nine seconds, and he said it to a counter in a shop in Lattice Ward with about four people in the room, and he is the first person in this city to say it in that form and he does not know that and will not be told.**

**"There's about four hundred and fifty people in this city who could each say one true thing about a sheet of paper this afternoon. I've been to eight rooms in about a week and I have not found two of them in the same building. About nine of them can be reached by a person who stands in a room. And not one of them has anywhere to put it."**

**He stopped. He did not say anything else and he did not say it twice and he has not said it since and he does not intend to, because he has said it and it is said and he is a man who changes light bulbs for a living and does not have the standing to keep saying a thing.**

---

**Nobody thanked him. The woman of about thirty-four asked him what he was going to do about it and he said he was going to go back on Thursday, and she asked him what he was going to do about the four hundred and fifty, and he said he was going to go back on Thursday, and she said that was not an answer and he agreed with her and said so, and about four people in that shop heard both halves of that.**

**Nothing about his own situation changed on the Wednesday. He is on the same round, at the same rate, at the same four buildings a day, and the eleven pounds a month still goes to a landlord whose name is on a bit of paper that nobody has read, and the only thing that has happened to him in about four weeks is that he has been into eight rooms and has told two people about a bin by a door in a school and has been refused twice in about nine seconds each.**

---

**"Who was sorting them before."** He said it at the door, with his hand on it.

**"Her name is Gwen Ashby,"** said the man of twenty-two. **"She is sixty-eight. She is not paid. She was doing it since the spring and she stopped on Friday and she was right to."**

**"Right."** And the man with the bag of bulbs said that and went out, and he did not write it down, and he did not ask a single question about it, and it is not going to be the reason he does anything, and Marek said the name out loud in a shop to a stranger for the first time and the whole of what it bought was about nine seconds of a man saying *right* at a door.

---

**He is the four hundred and fiftieth person. That is not a figure, it is a place in a row, and he has got there by asking one question four times at a counter in the second week of three weeks that are now over, and the four answers he got were four different answers and every one of them was correct, and the four answers he is carrying are in his head and are staying there, and the four pieces of paper in the fourth pocket of that tool roll are four halves of four addresses written in pencil by four people he has met once, and he has not put those with these either and he is not going to.**

**The day sheet on that counter has a column. It is the fourth column along and it is headed for goods returned to us and it is for a thing that has come back to that shop, and about a month ago he wrote a docket in a sleeve on that wall for a thing that had not come back to that shop, and the woman of about thirty-four said nothing about it, and the column is not for an objection, and there is no other column, and there is no form in this city on which an objection goes, and that is not a complaint and it is a measurement, and the measurement is the same in a workshop in Lattice Ward as it is in a first floor in a second district and as it is in a room four steps below a pavement with a bar on the outside of its door.**

---

**The small thing he gave up, and it is on no form and about two people in that shop noticed it: he did not write it down.** He had a pen out by about ten past four for the day sheet and he put it back in his coat, and the four pieces of paper in the tool roll are still four pieces of paper, and there is no fifth, and there will not be a fifth. **A list of four hundred and fifty people is one place. A list of four hundred and fifty people that exists because he made it is a room. And a man who makes a room for four hundred and fifty people who can each say a true thing is a man rooms send their returns to, and he has known that since the ninth day of the first of the three weeks before these three and he is not going to un-know it on a Wednesday because somebody said it out loud and it was true.**

**The cost of that is that he will not be able to say the number again in a month. Somebody will ask him how many there are and he will have to say that he does not know and that the only person who could have told him stopped on Friday and he did not ask her, and that sentence is a sentence a woman of about sixty-eight says about herself about four times a year, and he will be saying it about somebody else, and that is the second cost of the week and it is the larger one.**

---

**At about ten past five, with the shutter half down, the woman of about thirty-four said one thing to him and it is on this page because she has not said anything like it before and she is not going to again.**

**"You are going to ask me on Monday."**

**"Probably."**

**"Do not,"** she said. **"Not because I would stop you. Because you will do it in a queue with four people stood behind you and I will have to decide in about two seconds whether to help you, and if I help you then I am a person in this who knows where they are, and if I do not then you have to go up a stair and be told what you already know by a woman who is owed you nothing, and both of those are worse than the number."**

**"So what do I do."**

**"You say you do not know,"** she said, **"and you will be believed, because you have said you did not know four times in three weeks and nobody in this shop has ever once heard you say it."**

He said **"Right,"** and put the shutter the rest of the way down, and they both stood on the wrong side of it for about a minute in the rain.
