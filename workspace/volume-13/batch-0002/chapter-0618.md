# Chapter 618 — A Friday, A Mat On A Step At The Bottom Of A Stair That Nobody Told Anybody About, Nine Seconds On A Landing In Which A Woman Of About Sixty-Eight Said She Was Stopping And Was Not Talked Out Of It By Anybody, Four Trays Emptied Onto A Table And The Sheets Not Thrown Away, And A Room Four Steps Below A Pavement With A Bar On The Door

**The Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was a woman of about sixty-eight saying in about nine seconds that she was stopping on that day, and nobody persuading her out of it, and four trays emptied onto a table by about half past five.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday, every one of them carrying a date, eleven people over that counter and the last of them at about ten past five. **The heat in that flat has been above its winter setting for a fifth month, four names on that tenancy are carrying the whole of that difference, and a cook has a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and has been told by nobody that she is owed it.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and three days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in that rail is one thousand one hundred and seven days, a hundred and fifty-eight weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The seventeenth line on the two nails is eight hundred and seventy-five days, a hundred and twenty-five weeks to the day, and the separation is four hundred and eighty-three days, sixty-nine weeks to the day. One of those is a line on a board in a shop in this city and the other is a subtraction that has been right every day for five years and describes nothing at all since a box was filled in on a first floor four weeks ago, and both of them are on this page, and neither of them is a day that anything happened to the heating of a flat in this city, and this entry will not pretend that they are.**

---

**There was a mat on the step at the bottom of that stair. It is a coir mat and it is newer than anything else in that building and it has been there since the Tuesday of that week, and nobody told him about it, and nobody has written to anybody about it, and there is no form in this city on which a mat on a step gets entered.**

**"When did that go down."**

**"Tuesday,"** said the woman of about thirty-four, at about ten past two, at that counter, and she was pricing a switch. **"And the man with the bag has been up those stairs himself twice since, and I know because I have seen him go past the window, and I did not think of it as anything and it is only since you asked that it is a thing."**

**"Right,"** he said.

**"And you've not been up there since Monday."**

**"No."**

**"Then you don't know that she's stopped,"** she said, and went and served the next one, and he stood at that counter for about eleven seconds afterwards and did not move, and there is a mark on the counter's edge where his thumb was.

---

**He went up at about half past four on the Friday, and he did not know what he was going up for, and he found out on a landing in about nine seconds with a woman holding a tray.**

**"I am stopping today."**

**"Right."**

**"The mat went on on Tuesday and he has been up himself twice and he is not going to do it a third time, and there is a Friday in it, because the bag comes on the Monday, and I am not going to be here on the Monday."**

**"What happens to it."**

**"That is the next thing and you are about eleven seconds early."**

---

**Nobody talked her out of it. He had three things available and he used none of them, and the using of none of them is on this page because it is the only decision he made all day and it cost him more than anything he has done since the spring.**

He could have said *somebody should write it down*, and a woman of about thirty-four told him in the fourth week of the last three weeks that he is the only person in this city anybody would believe while somebody else wrote it, and that if he wrote it down she would know inside about four seconds and be right, and that about a week after that she would stop. He could have said *we can pay for a room*, and he had been told at that counter, in about nine seconds, that it cannot be paid out of a till in Lattice Ward, and that if there were a fund with a name on it she would know inside about a minute that she had been the woman somebody is kind to, and that she did not ask for that either. And he could have said *stay until somebody comes*, and a man with a bag has been coming up a stair on Mondays since he was about thirty-one and has never once been asked to take it over, and asking him to take it over would have been the fifth person to ask her for something in four months.

**He said none of them. She looked at him for about four seconds and then she said, "Good,"** and went back to the trays, and the *Good* is the last word in this entry that anybody in that room said to anybody.

---

**She emptied all four of them onto the table by the window between about half past four and about ten to five, and nothing went in a bin, and nothing went down the outside of that stair, and nothing was left on the floor of that landing.**

Four hessian sacks, which came out of the bottom drawer of a press-bed in that room and which had the string on them, and the string is the string the pen on the half-landing hangs from and she took it down to cut the string off and put the pen back.

**"There is a room four steps below the pavement of that building,"** she said, at about ten to five, **"with a door on it that has a bar across it, and a light on a timer that goes off at about six, and a landlord who lets it by the month for eleven pounds, and a man with a handcart on the Monday, and I have put them in the four sacks and the four sacks are going down there in the morning."**

**"That's a room."**

**"It is a room,"** she said. **"That is the whole of what I have been able to arrange and it took me four months and it is the best thing anybody has offered me in four years and I am not going to stand here and be grateful about it, because it is better than what I had and it is worse than what it was supposed to be, and both of those are true today and neither of them is going away."**

---

**Here is the better of it. A sack of paper in a room with a door on it and a light on a timer and a name on a bill is a room. Somebody can stand in it. A sheet that is in there can be read by a person who is standing in the room it names, because the room it names is the room it is in, and the rule she has been applying since the spring without ever having been asked about it will work in there exactly as well as it has ever worked anywhere, and it will work tomorrow, and it has never worked better.**

**And here is the worse of it, and she said both halves in the same breath and about nine seconds apart and did not enjoy saying either of them. The landlord knows what is in that room, and a landlord can decline to let it, and can put a different lock on the door, and can put the rent up when the lease comes round again. The man with the handcart will carry four sacks down four steps and will not open them and has not been asked to. And from Monday, a person anywhere in this city who has sent a sheet to a room that is not there can be told, truthfully and by anybody at all, that it is with the landlord, and nobody is under any obligation to say one word more than that, and there is no longer a woman up that stair to whom a person can be sent, and the reason there is not is that a woman of about sixty-eight decided on a Friday afternoon in about nine seconds and was right.**

---

**He was not thanked for any part of it. Nobody in that room thanked anybody. She did not soften, she did not explain herself twice, and she was given nothing at the end of the afternoon, and he did not try to give her anything, and neither of those two facts is on a form.**

**The four trays are hers and they are in that room with nothing in them and she has kept the four trays, and the chip out of the near corner of the fourth one is the same chip it has had for about four years, and that is the whole of what she is taking with her.**

---

**At about half past six, the four rooms off that service road, for the last time this week. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall, and he has not been handed a written thing by anybody in about four years and nobody has asked him for one. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest that door for the whole evening, and was not spoken to, was not asked anything, and nobody has ever apologised to her, and her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf and it did not come out of that binder and the binder was not opened.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing stands at two and this Friday does not move it, and the reason is the plainest one anybody has given all week.** A woman who stops is not a correct thing that changed nothing, because a thing that changed nothing cannot end a thing that has been going on since the spring. **The two that have been on that register for about four years are the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, and they are not improved on by anything that happened on a landing this afternoon, and the woman who stopped is not going on it either, because stopping is not correct and nobody has said that it is.**

Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, nobody thanked anybody on that landing, and nobody was thanked in four rooms off a service road. **The shutter came down at about ten on the Friday, in the rain, and it took longer than it usually takes because the frame had swollen in the spring, and he noticed that and did not write it down, and it is the ninth thing in that week that has not been written down.**

**What is left is a shape and a set of figures, and they stand once and then the week is over.** The man of twenty-two can do, on this eighth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and the whole of what he did on it was three things he did not say. The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and seven days in a rail of two and it did not move. Two nails, nineteen lines, no twentieth, nothing added, ever. **The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and sixty-nine days, ninety-five weeks and four days, eight of the nine still unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and nobody put two of the nine together on this Friday or on any other day this week.** The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and fifty-one days, ninety-three weeks to the day. The separation is four hundred and eighty-three days, sixty-nine weeks to the day, ended in a form four weeks ago, walked every day since, and not stopped and not described as anything being put back. Shut is that drawer, with four sheets in it, and the fourth of those has never been read by anybody and was not read on the Friday. The four words that name the public body are on no page of this one. Three of the four articles this page holds are named in it, being a sheet, a card and a form, and the fourth is an iron plate with four slots lying under a floor in the first of the four towns, and no two of the four are the same thing and this file does not set any two of them side by side.
