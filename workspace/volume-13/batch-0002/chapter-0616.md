# Chapter 616 — A Wednesday, A Man With A Bag Of Bulbs Standing At A Counter For About Eleven Minutes With One Question That Could Not Be Answered By The Only Person In The Shop Who Had Ever Been Up That Stair, Two Schools That Disagree About Children And A Cold Store That Disagrees With Both Of Them, And A Ticket In A Coat Pocket For A Saturday That Was Not Used

**The Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter from two until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was a man with a bag of bulbs going into eight rooms that do not agree about anything and coming out of them still not agreeing, and four people learning about it afterwards and getting four of the nine rooms wrong.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, all of them carrying a date, eleven customers at that counter and nobody after about ten past five. **The heating in that flat is above the setting it is on in the winter and has been for a fifth month, four names on the tenancy are paying for that, and a fortnight of a cook's own money is still owed by her to nobody at all.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and one days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

That card standing in that rail is one thousand one hundred and five days, which is a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The fifteenth line on the two nails is nine hundred and sixteen days, a hundred and thirty weeks and six days, and the hold of a man of about thirty-three in another city is seven hundred and thirty-four days, a hundred and four weeks and six days. A line on a board and a length of time in a yard, both of them ending on the word *days*, neither of them an exact number of weeks, and neither of them a day on which anything happened to the heating in this city, and no entry in this book has ever pretended otherwise and none begins now.**

---

**Hollis Wren came into that shop at about twenty past four on the Wednesday with a lamp under his arm that he had taken out of a school and put back into the same school, and he was in there for about eleven minutes, and he was not a customer, and the reason he came in was to ask one question.**

**"You've got a shop,"** he said. **"You fix things. If I bring you a sheet of paper with a room written on it, could you tell me whether that room's the room it says it is."**

---

**He said no, in about nine seconds, and the nine seconds were correct and every part of them was his.**

**"No."**

**"You couldn't."**

**"I could, once, for about four days, and I have not been and I am not going to, and if I did it once I would do it for a second room inside a fortnight and a third inside a month, and about a year after that there'd be a man in this city who knew where nine things go, and about four hundred people would find out inside a month, and then the returns would start coming to a counter in Lattice Ward, and the day they start coming to a counter they stop being able to go anywhere, and I would be the reason."**

The man with the bag of bulbs looked at the floor for about four seconds.

**"Right,"** he said. **"That's the third person this week who's told me no in about nine seconds and I want to say something about that and then I'll go."**

**"Say it."**

**"It's not that any of you are wrong,"** he said. **"It's that all three of you said it fast, and the fast bit is doing something. I've been doing this eleven years and I go into about nine buildings a week and nobody's ever told me no to anything, and I'd honestly not worked out that I was allowed to be told."**

**"You're allowed,"** said the woman of about thirty-four from the back of the counter, without turning round, **"and I'll do it for a fiver if you want, and you can't afford it, and I'm not going to take it if you could."**

He laughed at that and put the lamp down on the counter and picked it up again and left it. **Nobody thanked him for coming in and nobody thanked him for the eleven minutes and he was not asked to stay and he did not offer.**

**"You were too quick,"** the woman of about thirty-four said, about a minute after the door shut, and she was wiping a part that did not need wiping. **"You had about four seconds where you could have said what you actually know and you spent all nine of them on the reason."**

**"I know one room with four trays in it and a woman who is not paid, and if I tell a man with a lamp that, he is a man who knows."**

**"He's a man with a lamp,"** she said. **"You've made him into a man with a lamp, and he was one before you met him, and now he's a man with a lamp who was told something in Lattice Ward, and that is worse and it is not better, and I'm not saying you were wrong, I'm saying the reasons were the only part that helped you."**

He did not have an answer to that and she did not wait for one, and about four of that answer went into the bin and the rest of it is still in the room.

---

**The rest of the Wednesday is what came back, and it came back in the wrong order, and it is here because it is what a counter in this city does with a thing nobody can file.**

**The first school.** A man of about thirty-four who runs the office there said the trouble is that nobody signs anything, and that a return with no signature on it is a return that could have been written by anybody, including the person it is about.

**The second school.** A woman of about fifty who has been in the same building for about nineteen years said the trouble is the opposite, and said it in nearly the same words, and what she said was that the children are the only people in the building who read those sheets and the adults are the only people in the building who have to answer them, and that nobody has ever put those two facts in the same sentence, and that a school is the only room in this city where that is true.

**The cold store.** A man of about thirty-one said the whole of it is a van problem, and said it four times, and was asked by the man with the ladder what would have to be true for it to stop being a van problem, and he said **"For the van not to be late,"** and about four people in a room above a line in Saltmarket heard that sentence repeated at second hand two days later and did not know who had said it.

**Nobody was right and nobody was wrong and the two schools are about nine streets apart and have never exchanged a word.**

---

**There are nine of those rooms. He has been in eight. The ninth is a clinic four days of bus travel from this city, and the address for it is in his head because a woman of about fifty-two said nine addresses out loud in a room in Crown Terrace on a Thursday and he was the only person there who was not writing, and he did not write them down either, which was the correct thing to do and which is now costing him in a way that has not finished costing him.**

**There is a ticket in the side pocket of a coat that is not his, for the Saturday, and the Saturday came and went, and the ticket is still in the coat, and he did not use it, and there is no form anywhere in this city on which a man records that he did not use a ticket.**

---

**He wanted to be the man who helps. He has wanted that for longer than any of this and he has been told in about four different rooms in about three weeks that he is the fourth person and that two of the first three were people who asked and stopped, and here was a man with a bag of bulbs who had walked into eight rooms in two days and had got nothing from this shop, and the getting of nothing was correct and he wanted it to be otherwise so much that his hand went to the fourth pocket of the tool roll twice in about four minutes and came back both times empty.**

**The small thing he gave up, and it is on no form: he did not tell him anything. Not the four halves in the tool roll, not the four trays, not the one rule, not the town with a different letterhead on it. A man who is the person a room sends its returns to cannot also be the man who tells a stranger there is a reason to keep going to rooms, because the second thing destroys the first one inside about a month and everybody thinks it improves things.** **The cost was that the man with the ladder went back into two of those nine rooms in the following month with nothing from this shop except a refusal, and a refusal is not enough to walk back into a room with, and he will not have said so to anybody, and the man of twenty-two will not find out for about four months and will not be able to do anything about it then.**

---

**Half past six, and four rooms off that service road. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall. A woman of about thirty was in the chair by that door for the whole evening and was not addressed, was not asked anything, and nobody has ever apologised to her, and her page, which is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf, was in that binder and the binder was shut and did not open and the page did not come out.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing stands at two and is not moved on this Wednesday, and the case for saying so out loud is a good one.** Refusing a man a thing he has asked for in about nine seconds is not a correct act that changed nothing, because it changes what he does next and he has said so and will act on it. The two on the register are the two that have been on it for about four years, and both are the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, and a man with a bag of bulbs going into eight rooms on a Wednesday morning is a thing that is happening, not a thing that anybody chose to do, and it is not on a list anywhere and will not be.

Nobody said thank you to anybody at that counter, and nobody said it to a man with a bag of bulbs, and the shutter came down at about ten, and this shop does not trade on a Sunday and there is no Sunday in this week.

**What is below this is a set of numbers and a shape, given once.** The man of twenty-two can do, on this sixteenth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and a refusal given to a man with a lamp under his arm has not altered one thing about him. The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and five days in a rail of two and it did not move. Two nails, nineteen lines, no twentieth, nothing added. **The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and sixty-seven days, ninety-five weeks and two days, eight of the nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and nobody compared two of them on this Wednesday or on any other day this week.** The post at the corridor end is six hundred and forty-nine days, ninety-two weeks and five days. The separation is four hundred and eighty-one days, sixty-eight weeks and five days, ended in a form a fortnight ago, walked and not stopped. The drawer under that bench is shut with four sheets in it and the fourth has never been read. Those four words naming the public body are on no page of this one. A return sheet, a card in a rail, a form with an empty box on it, and a plate of iron with four slots in it are four different objects and nothing in this entry puts two of them together.
