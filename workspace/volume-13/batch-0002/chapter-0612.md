# Chapter 612 — A Wednesday, A Room Above A Line In Saltmarket Reached By A Stair, About Eleven People, Nine Items Taken In About Four Minutes Each, One Thing Brought Up In A Bag That Was Not An Item, A Count Announced Of Fifty Of Which Forty-Five Correspond, And A Book Of Sixty-One Lines That Was Sixty-One Lines Afterwards

**The Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter from two until about four, a room above a line in Saltmarket from about four until about six, and the service road after that, and its shape was nine items taken in about four minutes each in front of about eleven people, and a thing that was not an item, and a book that did not open.**

---

The Wednesday's work was eleven jobs with a date on each and eleven customers at that counter and the last of them at about ten past five. Bench to two, the counter from two to about four, the room in Saltmarket from about four to about six, and the four service-road rooms from about half past six. **The heating in that flat is running above its winter setting and has been since the spring, four names on the one tenancy are paying for the difference, and a cook is a fortnight of her own money out of pocket with nobody to repay.** Those four rooms are one thousand and ninety-four days, a hundred and fifty-six weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card standing in the rail on that counter is one thousand and ninety-eight days, a hundred and fifty-six weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The twelfth line on those two nails, which is the oldest line on the board, is one thousand and fourteen days, a hundred and forty-four weeks and six days, and the post at the end of a corridor in a building he has been inside twice is six hundred and forty-two days, ninety-one weeks and five days. One of those is a number of days on a nail and the other is a number of days on a piece of cast iron in a passage, and neither of them is a day that anything happened to the heating of a flat in this city, and there is not going to be a sentence in this entry that pretends otherwise.**

---

The room is up a stair in a building over a line in Saltmarket, and it has been used on a Wednesday at four-week spacing for about nineteen years, and it has nine chairs round a table and a table that came with the room and a window that looks at a wall about two feet away.

**There were about eleven people in it at about four o'clock.** A woman of about sixty who holds that room has held it for about nineteen years and is not a member of anything and is paid by nobody. A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard. A woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs for another body and who was there as a person and not as a chair. A man of about thirty-three with nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over. A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm. About six others, and two of them had not been before. **And a man of about forty-nine who comes every time and who has no chair at the table and who stands behind the woman's chair, and has stood behind that chair for about eleven years, and nobody has ever explained to him that there is a spare chair and nobody has ever explained to her either.**

---

**She opened it at about four minutes past four and she said the number first, which is what she has always done, and it took about nine seconds and nobody answered it.**

**"Fifty."**

**"Of which?"** said the man of about sixty-one.

**"Forty-five correspond."** She said it the way a person reads a temperature off a wall. **"Same as it has been since the last one, up by one, and the five that do not correspond are the same five, and I have never shown the five to anybody and I am not going to start this afternoon."**

Nobody asked her about the five. **She opened the book, which is a book with a green cover and nothing written on the spine, and it has sixty-one lines in it, and she has not added to it since the last time anybody could remember, and the tin with a lid on it stood at the other end of the same table with seventy-three in it and the lid was not off it at any point in the hour and the two numbers have never been made into one number by anybody in that room and are not going to be made into one by this entry.**

---

**Nine items. About four minutes each. That is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement for about nineteen years and it works, and a man of about sixty-one writes the number of each one on a clipboard in a column and the column has about two thousand entries in it and nobody has ever totalled it up and the totalling is not on anybody's job.**

A complaint about a boiler. A dispute about two halves of a page that were not the same page. A request for a form to be reprinted bigger. A person who wanted to know whether a person in another district was still alive. Something about a cold store and a van. A request that a reply be sent to an address that is not a house. A question about a name spelled two ways. A woman who wanted to be told she had been right, and was told, by a person entitled to say it, in about four seconds, and wrote nothing down.

**And the ninth item, which took about four minutes like the others, and which was not an item.**

---

**It came up in a bag.** A woman of about fifty-two who works at a counter on a wide road had said a thing out loud to a person standing behind that counter, that person had written it on a sheet, and the sheet had gone into a bag and up a stair and onto the table face down, where the woman of about sixty turned it over.

**"I've sent it back three times,"** the woman said, reading. **"Three times to the same place and I was told each time that it had been received, and I am not here to complain about anybody, I would like to know what a return is for."**

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds. **The man of about sixty-one put his pen down, which he does not do.**

**And the woman of about sixty did not open the book, and nobody asked her why, and the reason is about four seconds long and it is not a reason anybody would have given out loud, and it is that she had been told the answer to the question at about half past three that afternoon by a man who was standing behind her chair, and what he had told her was that there is nothing to tell her, and a line in that book is for a thing she has not already been told to her face.**

---

**He had been in that room for the whole of the ninth item. The sheet was lying on the table face up for about four minutes and he had a pen in his hand because he has a pen in his hand, and he did not copy the sentence down.**

**He has been in a room in a second district three weeks now. There are four steel trays in it, one of them with a chip out of the near corner, and one of the four is older than the other three, and the woman who owns them is not paid, and a sheet that came back in a bag about four years ago is very probably in the one with the chip in it. He looked at the sentence on the table and he could have said about nine seconds that would have been true and would have changed what that room knew, and he did not say them, and the reason is not modesty and it is not the rule about not being the person rooms send things to, and the reason is that four trays are a woman's and he was told not to carry one of them and he did not, and a man told not to carry a tray is not entitled to describe what is in it.**

---

**Nobody asked her whether she was all right.** Eleven people were in that room. The whole of what happened was a thing that had already been said to her face half an hour before by a man who was standing behind her. After the ninth item the man of about sixty-one said *right*, and the woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs said *that is the same thing as last time*, and the woman of about sixty agreed with her, and eleven people began putting their coats on.

**What he wanted, and did not say, in a face, in a room, was for somebody to ask her whether she was all right, and he did not want to ask her himself, and nobody asked her, and the not-asking lasted about nine seconds and he was in all of it.**

---

**The small thing he gave up, and it is on no form: the man of about forty-nine caught him on the stair at about six o'clock, coming down, and said, in about nine seconds, in a stair about two feet wide, *have you any idea where they get put.* And he said no. And the man of about forty-nine went down the rest of the stairs without saying another word about it, and the two of them have not mentioned it since.**

**That is a small cost and it was the price of the not-saying in the room, and he paid it and would pay it again in about four seconds and cannot explain to anybody why a straight question at the top of a stair is the thing he minds most.**

---

Four rooms off that service road from about half past six. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall. **The chair nearest that door was occupied on the Wednesday evening by a woman of about thirty, and the whole of what happened to her was that the evening went past her, and nobody addressed her, nobody put a question to her, and the page with her name at the top of it, which is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf, did not come out of that binder and the binder was not opened.**

**Correct things done by people owed nothing remain at two, and a Wednesday in Saltmarket does not make a third.** Nine items taken in about four minutes each is a procedure, and a procedure is not an act, and a woman who has run a room every fourth Wednesday for about nineteen years without a wage and without a vote has not done anything today that anybody can point at. **The two that have been on the register for about four years are still the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, and neither of the two has been improved on by anything that happened up a stair in Saltmarket.**

Nobody up that stair and nobody at that counter said thank you to anybody on the Wednesday. **The shutter went down at about ten, which is what it does, and there is no Sunday inside this week and this shop does not open on one.**

**What is left is figures and a shape, and they are written once.** The man of twenty-two can do, on this twelfth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off him, and a book that did not open is not a thing that happened to him. The card in the rail is one thousand and ninety-eight days in a rail of two and it did not move. Two nails, nineteen lines, no twentieth. **The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and sixty days, ninety-four weeks and two days, eight of the nine still unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and nobody laid one of them against another on this Wednesday.** The post at the corridor end is six hundred and forty-two days, ninety-one weeks and five days. The separation is four hundred and seventy-four days, sixty-seven weeks and five days, ended in a form three weeks ago and still being walked, because a subtraction that stops when the thing it subtracts has stopped is a subtraction that has been given a favour. The drawer under that bench is shut with four sheets in it and the fourth has never been read and it was not read on the Wednesday. Those four words naming the public body appear nowhere in this file.
