# Chapter 615 — A Monday, A Landing In A Second District, Three Things Said In About Nine Seconds By A Woman Who Was Not Going To Be Thanked For Any Of Them, A Pair Of Scissors Sharpened By Somebody In That Building And Not By Her, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Did Not Go And Who Lied To One Person About Not Going

**The Monday was a bench until two, a counter from two until about four, a landing in a second district from about four until about five, the counter again until six, and the service road after that, and its shape was a woman of about sixty-eight telling him three things in about nine seconds on a landing, none of which he was going to be thanked for.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, a date on each, eleven people over that counter, the last of them at about ten past five. **The heat in that flat has been above its winter setting for a fourth month and is now costing more per week than the flat costs to run in any other month of the year, four names are carrying it between them, and a cook is a fortnight of her own money out of pocket and nobody owes it back to her.** Those four rooms are one thousand and ninety-nine days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in that rail is one thousand one hundred and three days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The nine hand copies of the front of a page on a shelf at the end of that shop are six hundred and sixty-five days, ninety-five weeks to the day, and the ask is seven hundred and eighty-nine days, a hundred and twelve weeks and five days. One of those is a shelf with eight unfinished things on it and the other is a length of time since somebody put a question to a person instead of to an institution, and they are on one page of this entry, and the first is not an age of anything in particular and the second is not a day that anything happened to the heating in this city, and this entry is not going to pretend that either of them is.**

---

He went up at about four on the Monday and the woman of about thirty-four said *go on* and did not put her head on one side this time, which she had done twice before and which he had decided not to read anything into.

**The sheet was still on the table. Her scissors were on the table with it, and the scissors had been sharpened, and she had not sharpened them, and he looked at the blades for about four seconds and she watched him looking.**

**"I did not do that,"** she said, **"and I am not going to find out who did, and there are four people in this building who could have and it is not going to be one of them tomorrow instead of it being nothing today."**

**"That's fine."**

**"I know it's fine,"** she said. **"You are the fourth one to say something is fine in here since the spring and I have stopped arguing with it, which is a thing I did not expect and have not decided to like."**

---

**Then she said the three things, and she said them in about nine seconds, standing up, in the order she had them, and she did not stop in the middle of any of them.**

**"One. The room on it is a room. It has been a room the whole time. The man with the bag knows the building and he has not been inside that room and neither have I, and that is as far as either of us goes, and it is enough for what you are about to ask me."**

**"Two. It came from a woman. It is not a person's signature at the bottom of a form their employer filled in, it is a woman writing her own hand on her own sheet, and the man with the bag has seen that hand on about nine other sheets that are not in this room, and I have looked at the two that are."**

**"Three. She lives four days of bus travel from this city and she has been told twice that a thing she sent was received, and both of those two times are on paper in this room, and she is alive, and I know she is alive because the second one is eleven days old and it came in a bag on a Monday from a town with a different letterhead on it."**

She put her hand flat on the table next to the sheet.

**"That is all three of them and I have said them in about nine seconds and I am not going to say any of them again, and I am not going to be thanked for them, and you are not going to thank me, because if you thank me then this was a favour and it was not a favour, it was a Tuesday's work and it happened to be a Monday."**

---

**He did not thank her. It is on this page because not thanking her was the hardest thing he did in four days and he did it in about four seconds and nobody in that room could have told that it had been hard.**

**He wanted to be told that she had been right. She has been right about every sheet in front of her since the spring and about the four trays and about the one rule and about the man who comes up a stair, and she has never once been told so by anybody, and a man of about thirty-four at a counter told a man of about twenty-two in the first week that four people had come to that shop ahead of him, and he is carrying that, and the woman of about sixty-eight is the person he wanted to say it to and she took the whole of the wanting off him by saying *you are not going to thank me* before he had got to it.**

---

**He could have gone. He has a Saturday and a bus goes on the Saturday and it is four days there and four days back, and there is a woman alive in a town with a different letterhead on it, and the sheet is on a table by a window in the first floor of a building four streets from that counter.**

**He did not go, and he did not say he was not going to go, and the reason took him about eleven seconds standing on that landing with his hands in his pockets and every part of it was correct.**

A man who goes and stands in the room that a return is sent to is a man rooms send their returns to. He said a version of that to a man of about twenty-nine at a counter in the second week of the three weeks that ended on that Thursday, out loud, in about nine seconds, and he meant it then and he means it now, and the difference between then and now is that then it was an argument about a clean list of four halves of four addresses and now it would be his own body in a kitchen four days of bus travel from this city. **In about a month somebody would find out that he knew. In about two months somebody in that kitchen would say so to a person at a counter, and that person would write it on a sheet, and the sheet would come up a stair, and it would go in a tray, and the woman who owns the trays would be the one who had to decide what to do about it, and there is no version of that in which he is the person who has not done it.**

**So he stayed on the Monday. And staying is the whole of what he has wanted for four weeks, and it is not a small thing and it is not going to get smaller, and he said not one word of it out loud on that landing, and she noticed none of it, because she had gone back to the trays.**

---

**From about half past six, those four rooms off that service road. The man of about fifty-one was against that north wall and nobody has given him a written thing. A woman of about thirty was in the chair by that door for the whole evening and was not spoken to, was not asked anything, and nobody has ever apologised to her, and her page, which is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf, stayed in the binder and the binder stayed shut.**

**Things done right that made no difference are at two and this Monday did not move them.** Three facts given in nine seconds to a man who had not earned them and who could not use any of them is not a correct act, because she did not perform it: she was clearing a tray she had been clearing since the spring and he was standing in the way. **The two that have been on the register for about four years are the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, and neither of them has been improved on by anything that happened on a landing this afternoon, and the woman who said the three things is not going to be on that register, because nothing she has ever done in that room has been a decision, it has been a Monday.**

No thank you at that counter and none on that landing and none in four rooms off a service road. **The shutter came down at about ten on the Monday, the same as it comes down on every day this shop trades, and there is no Sunday inside this week, and this shop does not open on one.**

**What is left here is a shape and some figures, and they stand once.** The man of twenty-two can do, on this fifteenth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and staying where he was has not made him a different man and nobody is going to thank him for it either. The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and three days in a rail of two and it did not move. Two nails, nineteen lines, no twentieth, and nothing has ever been added to that board. **The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and sixty-five days, ninety-five weeks to the day, eight of the nine still unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and no two of the nine were compared on this Monday by anybody in this city.** The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and forty-seven days, ninety-two weeks and three days. The separation is four hundred and seventy-nine days, sixty-eight weeks and three days, ended in a form four weeks ago and still being walked, and it is not shorter. The drawer under that bench stayed shut, with four sheets in it, and nobody has read the fourth of the four. The four words that name the public body are on none of these pages. A return sheet, a card in a rail, a form with an empty box on it and a plate of iron with four slots in it are four separate objects, and nothing on this page puts two of them together.

*The three things, and the not-going, and the one lie.* **On a landing at about four on the Monday a woman of about sixty-eight said three things, standing up, in about nine seconds, in the order she had them, and she did not stop in the middle of any of them: that the room named on the sheet is a room and has been one the whole time, the man with the bag knowing the building and neither of them having been inside it; that the sheet came from a woman writing her own hand on her own paper, that the man with the bag has seen that hand on about nine sheets that are not in that room, and that two of them are; and that she is four days of bus travel from this city, that she is alive, and that she has been told twice that a thing she sent was received, both times on paper in that room, the second of them eleven days old and out of a bag on a Monday from a town with a different letterhead on it. She attached one condition of her own: she would not say any of it again, and she was not going to be thanked, and a thank you would turn it into a favour, and it was not a favour but a Tuesday's work that happened to be a Monday. He did not thank her. What he wanted and did not get: to be told she had been right. What was on no form at all: that her scissors had been sharpened since the Friday by somebody in that building, and that she would not find out who. What he could have done: taken the Saturday bus, four days there and four days back. What he did instead: stayed, for a reason that took about eleven seconds and was entirely correct, and the reason is in the body above, and the cost of it is the whole of what he has wanted for four weeks. What he did with what he had been told: nothing. He wrote none of it down and said none of it. And on the Friday morning after, at that counter, when she asked him whether there had been anything new on that stair, he said there had not. Five seconds, and she does not lie to him, and he knows it, and he has not been able to put it anywhere since.*

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **Three true things were given in nine seconds to a man who could not use one of them, and a Saturday bus was not taken, and a woman of about thirty-four was told a thing that was not so, five seconds after she had asked the right question.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and three facts given to a man who could not use them is not a third instance, because she was clearing a tray and not performing anything, and both of the two that stand on that register are still the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns. The drawer is shut with four sheets in it and none was read. Sixty-one lines in that book and seventy-three in that tin, and no count was announced on this day because no sitting fell on it.*
