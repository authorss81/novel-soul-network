# Chapter 617 — A Thursday, A Second Floor In Crown Terrace, One Question Asked Of A Man With A Bag Of Bulbs And Four Answers Given About Four Different Rooms, A Woman Of About Forty-Five In A Kitchen Who Said The Only True Thing Anybody Has Said To Her And Would Not Have It Written Down, And A Bus Refused In About Nine Seconds

**The Thursday was a bench until two, a counter from two until about four, a second floor in Crown Terrace from about four until about five, the counter again until six, and the service road after that, and its shape was one question asked of a man with a bag of bulbs and four different answers given to it about four different rooms.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, every one of them dated, eleven people at that counter and the last of them at about ten past five. **The heat in that flat is on above its winter setting for a fifth month and four names on that tenancy are carrying it, and a cook is a fortnight of her own wages in the hole and is going to be a fortnight of her own wages in the hole at the end of this month as well.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and two days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and six days, a hundred and fifty-eight weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The thirteenth line on the two nails is nine hundred and seventy-three days, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is seven hundred and eight days, a hundred and one week and one day. The first is a line that has been on a board since before that shop had a second customer and the second is a man who has been sitting in the same chair in four rooms for longer than some of the people who come to that counter have been alive, and neither of the two figures is a day anything happened to any heating in this city, and the sentence that says so is going to be written out in different words at the bottom of every entry in this week and it is not a rule.**

---

The man with the bag of bulbs came up that stair on the Thursday because the light on the landing had gone again, and he was in the room for about twenty minutes and nobody asked him to stay, because that stair is in that building and a man working in a building is in the room.

**Marek asked him one question at about ten past four and it was a stupid question and he knew it was a stupid question while it was still in his mouth, and he asked it anyway because about nine people on that floor had been talking about the number of sheets for an hour and had got to nothing.**

**"Which of the nine has the most of them waiting."**

**"Depends what you mean by waiting,"** said Hollis Wren.

---

**"The water office,"** he said. **"There's a woman of about forty-six in there who's got about nine hundred of them in a cage behind her and she won't let anybody near the cage, and she says it's because the cage isn't hers, and the cage belongs to whoever the cage belongs to, and I didn't ask her the last bit because you don't."**

**"The council house,"** he said, about nine seconds later. **"Man of about sixty, been keeping them since before the spring, hasn't thrown one out, hasn't moved one, and when I asked him what the room was for he said the room is where they come back to and that's the only thing it's for and he said it like it was obvious, and I've been doing this eleven years and it wasn't obvious to me."**

**"The exchange room above the line,"** he said, a third time. **"Nineteen years. There are nine people in there and they have never thrown one out, not one, and one of them said to me that if you throw one out then somebody has made a decision and nobody in that room is allowed to make a decision, and I said that sounds like a rule and she said it isn't one."**

**"The first school,"** he said, a fourth time. **"The kids bring them in by the armful. I've seen that door. There's a bin by that door and it's full and the kids know to put them in it and nobody tells them to and nobody empties it."**

He stopped. **He had answered one question four times about four different rooms and not one of the four answers contradicted any of the other three, and there is no version of any of them that is wrong.**

---

**"Hold on,"** said a man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse, and he had a pen out and was going to write, and he put it back in his pocket. **"You've been into nine."**

**"I've been into eight."**

**"She said nine addresses."**

**"She said nine addresses,"** he agreed, **"and one of them is a clinic four days of bus travel from here and it doesn't open to anybody, and I was not going to stand outside it in a car park for four days of my week off to be the ninth."**

Nobody in that room checked, and about four of them had assumed that all nine had been entered, and nobody knew whether they were right, and the woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs said the number was eight and did not say why she was sure and the man with the bag did not say it either.

---

**The kitchen was the fifth of the eight and it is the one that Marek has gone back to three times in his own head since the Thursday and has not been able to do anything about.**

A woman of about forty-five cooks there for about nine people and has done for about eleven years. She said one thing to the man with the lamp and she said it in about nine seconds and she has said nothing else to anybody since.

**"A person who is hungry will not fill in a form in."**

**And then she said that she did not want it written down anywhere and that she had said it because he was the fourth person in about a month and the fourth one is the first one who stayed for the whole of it, and that she would like it to stay in his head and she would rather it stayed in his head than in a column on a floor in Crown Terrace, and that is a request and he has not yet decided whether he can honour it and he has not told anybody he was asked.**

**It was repeated at second hand on the Friday by a man of about twenty-nine who types for a firm, in these words: *somebody at a canteen said people are hungry and that's why it doesn't work*, and the man of twenty-two heard it and did not correct it, because correcting it would have meant explaining the kitchen, and explaining the kitchen would have meant the landing, and the landing is where four trays are.**

---

**The small thing he gave up on the Thursday, and it is on no form: he did not repeat the nine addresses.** He has four halves of four addresses written in pencil by four strangers at that counter, in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, and a man on that floor has just given four accounts of four rooms and there is a ninth address in a woman's mouth and no copy of it anywhere, and he could have asked for the four he does not have, and he did not, and the cost of not asking is that in about a month, when a man with a lamp needs a fifth address for a room in a town four days of bus travel from here, there is exactly one person in this city who can produce it, and she is the one person he has refused to ask for anything twice.

---

**And a man of about thirty-eight asked him, at about twenty past four, whether he would go.**

**"You could go,"** he said. **"You've been to that stair. You know it goes somewhere. There's a bus on the Saturday."**

**"No."**

**"Why not, you've just told us why not, you told that man with the lamp not to be the one who knows."**

**"Because if I stand in that room, then in about a month this floor will be full of people saying that the man who fixes things in Lattice Ward has been in the room at the back of the clinic, and about four hundred people in this city will find out inside a fortnight that I know where a return goes, and I will be the man they ask, and I will not be able to say no, and I have spent three weeks refusing to be that man and I am not going to start on a Saturday."**

The man of about thirty-eight said **"That is a hell of a way to live,"** and meant it kindly, and nobody in that room disagreed with the reasoning and one person in that room said the sentence was a comfortable one and was told to shut up by a man of about thirty-three, and the man of about thirty-three was right and nobody has said so since.

---

**Half past six, and four rooms off that service road, and the man of about fifty-one was against that north wall with nothing in his hands and nobody after him for it.** **The chair nearest that door had a woman of about thirty in it from about half past six until about ten, and in that time she was not spoken to, was not asked a question, and was not apologised to by anybody, and the page of hers that is filed — the eighth of eight, in a ring binder, on a shelf in the back room — did not come out of it, and the binder was not opened, and nobody has opened it in front of her at any point in this book.**

**The register of correct things that changed nothing is at two, and it is not moved by a Thursday.** Four answers to one question, none of which is wrong, is not a correct act; it is a man describing what he saw, in a room, to people who were not asking. **The two on that register are the two that were on it before any of this began, and they are the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, and she has not been improved on by a man with a bag of bulbs, and she is not going to be improved on by anybody who finds out about the thing she did about four years ago in a car park.**

Nobody at that counter thanked anybody, nobody on that second floor thanked anybody, and nobody was thanked in the four rooms off a service road. The shutter came down at about ten on the Thursday. There is no Sunday inside this week, and this shop does not trade on one.

**The shape and the figures, once, and then the day is over.** The man of twenty-two can do, on this seventeenth day, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first week, and refusing a bus in about nine seconds has made him nothing at all. The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and six days in a rail of two and it did not move. Two nails, nineteen lines, no twentieth, nothing added. **The nine hand copies of the front of a page are six hundred and sixty-eight days, ninety-five weeks and three days, eight of the nine still unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and nobody in this city put two of the nine together on this Thursday or on any other day this week.** The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and fifty days, ninety-two weeks and six days. The separation is four hundred and eighty-two days, sixty-eight weeks and six days, ended, and walked, and not to be described as anything that has been put back. Shut is that drawer; it has four sheets in it and the fourth has never been read by a person. The four words naming the public body are in none of this file, and no clause of it denies that they exist somewhere.
