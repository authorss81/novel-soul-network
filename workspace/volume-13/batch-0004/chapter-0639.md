# Chapter 639 — A Monday, A Second Floor In Crown Terrace With About Nine Chairs In It And Four People In Two Of Them, A Form With A Box For A Date And A Box For A Number And A Signature At The Bottom Of It, A Woman Of Forty-Five Who Fills In Two Boxes And Does Not Ask A Question, And About Nine Seconds In Which A Man Of Twenty-Two Does Not Explain Himself

**Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a second floor in Crown Terrace with about nine chairs in it and four people in two. This one is a form, two boxes, a signature, about nine seconds, and a man who is not asked anything.**

---

He came back into this city on the Saturday on the bus that runs twice a week, four days each way, having been away for six days on a fortnight he had asked eight people for in nine seconds each, and he had not gone to the ninth because the ninth was going to be included by a Tuesday.

**The arrangement stopped on the Sunday, at the time he gave eight people, on a piece of nothing, and it stopped without a single one of the eight objecting and without the ninth ever having been asked, and the crates went back on the Saturday afternoon in a van driven by a man of about forty-one who did not say one word about any of it and who has not said one word since.**

Those four rooms off that service road are one thousand one hundred and forty-eight days old, a hundred and sixty-four weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, warm. **The card on end in the rail is one thousand one hundred and fifty-two days, a hundred and sixty-four weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two, and the fortnight is over, and neither of those facts has anything to do with the other and the rail is exactly as it was on the Monday three weeks ago.**

---

**The room is on a second floor and it has about nine chairs in it and it has had about nine chairs in it for as long as anybody in that building can remember and nobody has ever set out fewer.**

Four people were in it at about eleven on the Monday morning. **A woman of about thirty-four who had walked out of the shop at eleven because he had told her at the counter at ten past ten that there was something on the second floor and would not say what. A woman of forty-five who is the lead repairer for a stretch of this city's heating and whose name is Sera Quill and who has been in this manuscript for eleven volumes and has been in nine seconds of every important room in it. A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard who writes numbers in one column and has never totalled the column. And a man of twenty-two.**

There is a coat over the back of one chair that has been there since the spring. **Nobody in that room will claim it and nobody has ever moved it and about four people have sat in it, one at a time, over about four months, and it is on nobody's list and it is not in this entry as anything other than a coat.**

---

**She had a form. It was a single sheet with a box for a date, a box for a number, and a signature at the bottom, and it was the ninth of a kind, and eight were in a drawer in a first floor in a second district that was not in the room and that nobody in that room opened.**

"You know what that is," she said.

"I've seen one."

"**You've seen eight.**" She turned it round. "**Mine's the ninth and it has my name in the box on the left and it has been in my bag since the spring and I have not used it once, and I am using it now, and I am not going to ask you whether you agree, because you agreed to that in a corridor about ten weeks ago in about nine seconds and you did not understand what you had agreed to and I have waited the ten weeks.**"

---

**Then she said the thing, and it was nine seconds, and it is the whole of what she said.**

"**You are off the route. Not the van — the van is a man with a job and it is not mine to take and I have not touched. The route is you. Nine rooms, one list, and a fortnight that is finished, and everything you have said out loud since a Sunday under a walkway about being the one everything comes to. All of it. As of now, and it is written down, and it is not a conversation.**"

"I'd like to say—"

"**No you wouldn't,**" said Sera Quill, and put a pen on the form.

---

**He did not say it. That is the whole of what he did and it took four seconds, and it is the second half of everything that has happened to him since the spring.**

He stood in a room with about nine chairs in it and had a sentence ready, and it was a good sentence, and it had been ready since the Saturday bus, and it was about nine seconds long and it was true. **He did not say it because she was right that he would not have said it, and because a man who argues a removal has been removed twice, and because the sentence he had ready was about him, and the thing that had happened was not about him, and there was no version of his sentence that was owed to anybody in that room.**

**Nobody thanked anybody. She filled in the box for the date and the box for the number and signed at the bottom in about nine seconds, tore the top copy off along a line that had been torn on eight other sheets, and gave him the copy and put the other one in her bag, and the two of them did not shake hands and neither of them said anything else about it.**

---

**The man of about sixty-one wrote a number in his column. He has about two thousand numbers in that column and has never totalled it and he did not totalled this one and he did not look up, and nobody has ever been able to say whether he was being kind or whether he was working.**

The woman of about thirty-four said, at the door, on the way out, in four seconds: **"That is the first thing anybody has done about any of this that was not a favour to you."** She stopped, and then said: **"You cannot tell him."**

"I know."

"**He has had nine written on the back of his hand since a Friday, put there by a man with a clipboard at a gate, and you told him under a walkway that he'd be told first, and you have told him nothing since, and now you are not allowed to tell him, and that is the actual cost of this piece of paper and it is not you.**"

---

**The crates came back on the Saturday in the same van and they went under the bench in about eleven minutes in front of him, and a man of about forty-one put them down and wiped his hands on a rag and said one thing, in about nine seconds, and it was the only thing either of them said about a fortnight.**

"**That's the fortnight,**" said Ray Topping. **"You never told me what it was for and I've never asked, and I'd not be telling you now if you hadn't been stood there looking at the crates like a man waiting to be asked a question about them.**"

"Ask it."

"**No,**" he said, and moved a crate half an inch. **"You'll answer it in nine seconds and it'll be true and then I'm a person who's been told what a fortnight's for, and right now I'm a driver on a Tuesday and a Friday, and I'd rather be that for as long as the firm lets me.**"

**He was not thanked. He is not on a list. He is given control of nothing and he is not the villain of anything and he does not soften and nothing that happened in this fortnight improved his situation by one single thing, and a man of about forty-one went back to a garage with the door open and a pencil tally rubbed out on it and nothing started.**

---

**At about a quarter to twelve on the Monday he went to the gate of a bus depot and stood at it and did not go in.**

A van was not there. The wheel on the chain was still reading the figure it had read since the spring. **A woman of about forty-one with a clipboard was out on the yard side talking to a man about a delivery time and she had her back to him for about four minutes, and he stood there for those four minutes and about four more and then went and got the bus, and the bus was due in nine minutes, and he has been sorry about a great many things in this city and has not once been sorry about a wait.**

**He did not go in because a form with a box for a date and a box for a number and a signature at the bottom of it was in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, and what it said is that he is not the man anything comes to, and a man who walks up to a gate two days after a fortnight ended without one word being said to anybody is a man who has put himself back on a road, and the whole of what he has learned since the spring is that the road is the disease.**

**She is the one person in this city who is owed an ending. She gave him a number out of a clipboard four years old that nobody has ever checked, she asked for somebody to have it, and he did not write it down, and the van that carried it has stopped, and there is no version of a Monday on which that is his to hand over.**

---

At about a quarter past twelve the corridor on that floor smelled of somebody else's dinner and two men went past him on the stairs and neither of them looked at him, and he was twenty-two years old with a form folded once in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, and it was Monday, and the thing he had lost was the only thing anybody in this city had ever asked him for.

* * *

**He did not write it down. That is the small thing he gave up on the Monday, in about four seconds, in a corridor, and there is no form anywhere in this city on which a man records that he is not allowed to speak to a person he promised a thing to.**

The copy of the form is in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, folded once, with the number legible and the date legible and her name at the bottom. **It is the fifth piece of paper in that pocket and the pocket has never been a list of anything and is not one now, and the only thing he has taken out of that pocket in a fortnight is a form that takes him off a thing.**

---

**At about half past four he went down a wide road to a covered walkway, because a man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen is finished at about half past three on a Monday and stands about there at about half past four, and there were about nine people out of the weather under that walkway and he was not one of them.**

Nobody asked him anything. He stood for about nine minutes and there was a man of about thirty-four in a doorway on the far side who looked at him twice and did not come over, and Marek did not go over either.

**What he wanted, for nine minutes under a covered walkway, was to be asked what had happened. Nobody asked him anything. That is the same want as the Monday of the second week of this fortnight in a doorway in a school on a wide road, and it has not been said twice in the same way, and it has not been said at all since.**

---

*One thousand one hundred and forty-eight is a whole number of weeks and one thousand one hundred and fifty-two is four days past it, and the second of those two numbers was in a rail this morning with a rail of two, and neither of them has moved since the Friday before last and neither of them is going to move on Monday.*

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A man of twenty-two was taken off a plan in about nine seconds by a person entitled to take him off it, it went on a form, he was not asked first, he did not argue, and the cost of not arguing is that he does not get to explain himself and finds that he does not want to.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped, and a request to be told which of nine rooms to send something to was refused in four seconds by the person it was made to. Nothing in this fortnight adds a third to a list that has stood at two since about four years ago, and a man taken off a plan in nine seconds is not on that list, because nine rooms are about to hear about it from somebody who is not him and one of them is going to hear about it from him never.
