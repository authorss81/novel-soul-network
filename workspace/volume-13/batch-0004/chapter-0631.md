# Chapter 631 — A Monday, A School On A Wide Road, A Question Put In A Doorway Of A Supply Room And Answered In About Nine Seconds With A Condition On The End Of It, A Time Of Day Given Out Loud And Not Written Down, And A Man Who Has Said He Would Do It And Has Not Done Any Of It Yet

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road after that. This one is a school on a wide road at about eleven, a supply room, a woman of about twenty-six who lets people in without asking them for anything, and one question asked in a doorway and answered in about nine seconds with a condition on the end of it.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday with a date against each and eleven people over the counter, the last of them let in at about ten past five. The order of the day: bench, counter, wide road, service road, with the counter handed over at about half past four because there was a queue of six.

The heat in that flat has been above where it goes in the winter since the spring and is above it now, and four names on that one tenancy are making the difference up between them without ever having agreed to. **A cook is a fortnight of her own money in the hole and has not been told and would not want to be told.** Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and thirty-four days, a hundred and sixty-two weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

**The card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and thirty-eight days, a hundred and sixty-two weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two and neither of them has been turned.**

The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and eighty-two days, ninety-seven weeks and three days. The nine hand copies of the front of a page are seven hundred days to the day, a hundred weeks, eight of the nine unfinished, the first row nobody has disagreed about still not found, and not one of the nine has been laid against another on any day of this year.

---

**He did it on the Monday because he said on a Sunday that he would, and because a thing said under a covered walkway on a Sunday in this city reaches about one person and a thing said on a Monday to a woman who keeps a supply room reaches a woman who keeps a supply room.**

He had written the nine down on the back of a docket in a shop with the shutter down, nine lines in pencil, in about four minutes, in his own hand. He has carried it since. **It is the fourth pocket and the fourth pocket holds four pieces of paper that are not his and a docket, and this is the first thing he has written in that pocket in six weeks and it is not a list of addresses, it is a list of rooms, and he wrote *ask them first* under it and then did not do that either, because asking them first is what he was about to do and writing it down does not make it more likely.**

---

**The school on a wide road has a side gate and a yard and a lorry on the second Monday of every month that is not a school holiday, and today the yard had nothing in it.**

He came in at the front and asked for the supply room and a girl of about seventeen walked him down a corridor with a radiator that had a blanket over it and stopped at a door and said the woman was in there and he could go in. **Nobody asked him for anything at the door. That is what a supply room is for.**

---

**She was counting out trays of clips onto a table and she did not stop counting and she did not look up, and he stood in the doorway for the four seconds it took her to finish a tray.**

"Which room," she said.

"That's not what I'm here for."

"Then it'll have to wait, because it's a supply room and if I stop in the middle of a tray I lose the count of the tray." She put the last row down. **"Right. Go on."**

---

**He had it ready. He had had it ready since about half past nine and it had been four different sentences in the bus and it was now none of the four.**

"The same question to all nine of you," he said, **"and it's a small one and you can say no in nine seconds and nobody's going to be offended and I'll write down that you said no and not why."**

"Go on."

"Would you put your returns through one man for a fortnight. Not me, necessarily. One man, one place, so that when somebody's got a question about where something went, there's one room with the answers in it instead of nine rooms with half an answer each. **A fortnight. Then it stops, and if it doesn't stop you say so and it stops.**"

---

**She thought about it for about two seconds, which was faster than he had expected and slower than he had hoped for, and then she said the thing he has thought about every day since.**

"**What time.**"

"What?"

"**What time do they come.**" She put the tray down on the table. **"Because if it's a set time, then somebody has to be here at that set time, and I'm the one who's here at that set time, and this is a supply room, and a supply room with nobody in it is a cupboard, and a cupboard is what we had before the front door was changed.**"

He had said almost exactly the same thing to about four hundred and fifty people in this city in four weeks and he had said it to himself on a Sunday, and he had not once said it to himself as a person who might be asked a question about his own arrangements.

"Yes," he said. **"That's right. You'll be here at that time."**

"I'd want to know what the time is before I said yes," she said, **"and not on the day."**

---

**He told her. He does not repeat what the time is in this entry and the woman of about thirty-four who was on that counter in the afternoon has not asked him and is not going to. He told her, and he said it once, and he did not write it on the docket, and the not-writing was the whole of a decision he made in about four seconds and it cost him something he has not been able to put a name on since.**

"Why are you not writing that down," she said, to his back, while he was going out.

"**Because you said not on the day, and if I've got it written down I don't have to remember to ask you for it again.**"

"That is a very good reason for a person who is not going to tell you anything they have not agreed to yet," she said, **"and it is a bad reason for a person who is going to make the same arrangement with eight other people, and I would like you to know that I know which one you are, and I am saying yes anyway, and you can decide for yourself what to do about that."**

---

**He stood in a yard with no lorry in it for about eleven seconds and then went back in and said thank you, which is the only time in this entry that the word is used about anybody and it was for a thing she had not agreed to and had cost her, and it went out of him before he had decided to say it and he said it out loud in a doorway and the girl of about seventeen was eleven feet away and heard all of it.**

**Nobody thanked anybody in that supply room after that. Nobody thanked him back. She went back to the trays and he left and the door shut on its own.**

---

**And what he wanted, and did not ask for, was for somebody in that school to ask him what it meant. He has been asked things about nine ends in about nine buildings in about four weeks and he has answered none of them, and the whole of what he wanted on this Monday was to be asked a question by a woman of about twenty-six about a supply room.**

There was nobody to ask him. The school went on at twenty past eleven and he was let out through a gate and nobody followed him out of it.

* * *

**He came back down the wide road on foot because the bus went every twenty minutes and the next one was in nine, and he has never once in this city regretted a nine-minute wait and has regretted a great many other things.**

There is a row of small shops on the other side of that road and a woman of about forty-five outside one of them with a cigarette she was not smoking, and she said, without turning round, **"You've asked somebody something and they said yes and you didn't like it."**

"How do you know that."

"Because it's Monday and you've got the coat open." She did turn round. **"I've been outside this shop for about nine years and I know what people look like before they go in a shop and what they look like after. Yours is the after."**

---

**He said the thing. He said all of it, standing on a pavement, to a woman he had met once, in about four minutes, which is longer than he has said anything to anybody outside a counter in about four months.**

She listened to the whole of it and did not interrupt once and at the end of it she said the useful thing, which was: **"You've told me a time of day and you've not written it down. So there's a person in this city who knows a thing you need and there's no way for you to get it back out of anybody except going and asking her again. That's not a fault. That's just the shape of it, and you'll find out the shape of it about four days from now when you want it and it's gone."**

"I know the shape of it."

"You know nine shapes of it," she said. **"You've had a bad month."**

He had, and he did not say so, and she did not press it, and neither of them said thank you, and she went inside and he stood on that pavement for about a minute and a half with a thing he had not had in about four weeks, which was a person in this city who had told him a thing he had not asked for and was owed nothing for it.

---

**And at about twenty past twelve there were two people over that counter and the third of them put a sheet of paper on it and wanted to know why it had come back to her.**

She was about sixty-one and she had brought it in a plastic sleeve and it had been in the sleeve since the spring, and it was not a return and Marek has never claimed it was one, and the two of them established that in about nine seconds because she said so before he could.

**"I only want to know if it's arrived,"** she said. **"That's all. It's got a room on it. Somebody's sent it back to me with a bit of a stamp on it and I'd like to know if it got there first."**

---

**He could have said where it was. He had a rule and the rule was good and the rule was four years old and had never once been wrong, and it would have taken him nine seconds, and it would have been a sentence about a room four steps below a pavement in a second district with a bar on the outside of the door and a light on a timer.**

He said: **"I don't know where your paper is. I know a thing about rooms and it isn't about your paper."**

She looked at him for about two seconds. **"That's the first straight answer anybody's given me about that thing in about four years,"** she said, **"and I'd have liked a worse one, and I'm not going to ask you again."**

---

**That is the third refusal of the day and the woman of about thirty-four heard the end of it from four feet away and wrote nothing down, and the thing he gave up in it was a woman of about sixty-one who was going to go away without an answer and was going to go away with a straight one, and the cost of the straight one is that she is now a person who knows he does not know, and that is a fact about him in a city of about four hundred and fifty people who can each say one true thing, and he has just made one of them.**

He could have told her. **He has not been down those four steps and was told not to go down them and did not go.**

---

**At about two he was at the bench and the woman of about thirty-four was on the counter with a meter in her hand and told him something before he had the coat off.**

"You've been to the school."

"How do you know."

"**Because you've got the face you had on the Sunday and about a fortnight ago, and it is the face of a person who has said a thing out loud and is now finding out what it costs.**" She put the meter down. "How many."

"One."

"That's a slow start and it's Monday, so that's fine."

---

**He thought about the second one for about four seconds on the bench, with a job in his hand that he was not doing, and then he did a thing he has not done in about six weeks and he wrote nothing down at all, not the number, not the room, not the time, and it is the fourth Monday in a row and it is the first one where there was nothing on the docket to look at afterwards.**

He had written the first one down. Nine lines in pencil, nine rooms, four districts and two towns, and *ask them first* underneath in the same pencil, and it is in the fourth pocket, and the fourth pocket holds four pieces of paper that are not his.

**What he gave up on the Monday was the ability to tell anybody how many he had got, and he gave it up for no reason he could put to anybody, and the reason is that a woman of about twenty-six told him in a doorway that she knew which kind of man he was, and he has not worked out what she meant by that and has not asked.**

---
