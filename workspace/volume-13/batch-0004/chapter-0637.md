# Chapter 637 — A Thursday, Four Days On A Bus To A Town This Entry Does Not Describe, A Supply Room In A School On The Far Side Of It, A Woman Of About Thirty-One Who Has Run A Clinic There For About Four Years, Nine Seconds That Agree With Nobody In The Room, A Door With Nothing Written On It That He Stood Outside And Did Not Go In, And A Return Bus That Goes On A Saturday

**Thursday was a bus from early and four days of it, and a building at the end of it with nothing written on the door. This one is a supply room, a woman of about thirty-one, about nine seconds, and four people in the room, none of whom was on her side.**

---

The bus to that town goes on a Wednesday and on a Saturday and at no other time anybody has ever booked, and he got onto the Wednesday one at about six in the morning with a bag that had nothing in it that was for anybody. **The fortnight was on its eleventh day when he stepped off it and it had three left, and he had told eight rooms in this city on eight separate days in nine seconds each that it would stop on the Sunday whatever anybody thought of it, and it was going to stop, and not one of the eight of them was in this town.**

He had the address. He has had it since the spring. **It is on the back of a man's hand in a kitchen in this city, and it is on a card in a holder in a cellar in a second district, and it is in a folder he was handed in a corridor in a first floor by a person who said do not write this down, and he wrote it down.** He had not gone. That is the whole of it, and there is no reading of this Thursday on which it was the first day he could have got there.

---

**Four days of it, and nothing that this manuscript has a figure for, and he has been on worse.**

The first day was a road with a river on it. The second was a changeover at a place with one shop and a man behind a counter who looked at him for about four seconds and then said, **"You're not from here,"** and Marek said no, and the man said **"Right,"** and gave him a cup of tea he had not asked for and did not charge him for, and neither of them said anything else. The third day was in the back of a thing with a woman of about sixty-three and two baskets, who gave up her window seat to a man of about twenty-two without being asked and without mentioning it, and slept. **The fourth was a town that came in on the road in pieces over about two hours, a chip shop, a school, a road with trees, a low building with a light in it, and then nothing much for four days of bus travel after that.**

He has not written one line of the four days down. There is a line on a day sheet in a shop in this city with his name at the top of it and four days written against it in the box for a place, and the box wants a place and what he has written in it is *away*, which is not a place and which is the only word he has put on paper about any of it.

---

**The clinic has nothing on the door. The school it borrows a room from is a long way off on the far side of that town, the supply room is where the clinic's paper lives, and one woman keeps both of those things and has for about four years.**

She was about thirty-one. She had a hand truck with three boxes on it and she was moving them when he came in, and she did not stop, and she said, **"If you're selling something, the office is down the corridor and the office does not open on a Thursday,"** and then she looked at him properly and put the truck down and said, **"You're the man from the shop in Lattice Ward."**

"That's me."

"**Then you've come about the returns, and you have come on the Wednesday bus, and the Wednesday bus is two days each way, and this is not about the returns, and I want to have said that before either of us says anything else.**"

---

**He opened his mouth to say the thing he says. He has said it to eight people in this city and it is four sentences and it has a shape and the shape is the reason it works, and he got as far as the first four words of the first sentence and then stopped in the middle of a supply room about two days of bus travel from where he learned it.**

"Would you put your—"

"No," she said. **"Not like that. Ask me the question you'd ask if you'd never asked anybody else, because that is not the question, that is a speech, and I've been here four years and a speech does me no favours.**"

---

**And he asked her a question instead, and it is the only question he has asked anybody in this city in four months that was not the same one twice, and it took him about nine seconds to arrive at it, and he arrived at it late.**

"What do you want to know about it."

"**Nothing,**" said the woman of about thirty-one. **"That's the answer and it's the first one anybody's given me. Now go on, because you'll want to ask me the other one, and I want you to have asked me both before I say anything about a month.**"

---

**He asked her the other one. He gave it to her the way he has given it to eight people and it took nine seconds and the room is four days of bus travel from the shop he learned it in.**

"Would your returns go through one man for a fortnight, so that when somebody has a question about where a thing went there is one place with the answers in it instead of nine places with half an answer each. It stops at the end of the fortnight whether anybody is happy about it or not, and I have said that part to everyone I've asked and I am saying it to you now."

She said yes in four seconds.

**And then she said what she was owed, and it took her about nine seconds, and she said it to the hand truck and not to him.**

"**A month.**"

"A month of what."

"**Of somebody else's decisions. I did not make one of them and I have been living inside every one of them, and the day your van came down that road on the Tuesday, behind a stop that had been on a door for two days, I found out that out of a crate.**"

---

**Nobody in that room agreed with her. There were four people in it and one of the four was him.**

A woman of about nineteen with a box of staples, who had her hands full, who did not look up, and who did not say a word then or afterwards. A man of about fifty-one at a desk who has been at that school about nine years and who said, **"That's the arrangement, that is,"** in the voice of a person confirming the price of milk. A woman of about thirty-one with a hand truck. And a man of twenty-two who had asked.

"That's the arrangement," said the man of about fifty-one. "**Eight of them said yes and the ninth's on a timetable. That's not a wrong thing. That's just how it gets done.**"

"**How it gets done is that nobody asks,**" she said. **"I'm not saying you did it on purpose. You didn't, did you.**"

"No."

"**Then don't stand there agreeing with me about it, because agreeing with me is not the thing I need.**"

---

**And then she said the rest of it, and it was about nine seconds, and it is the whole of what she said in this entry and there is nothing else in it.**

"**I have run this about four years and I have never had a month where somebody in another city decided what my paper did and I found out on a Tuesday from a crate. Nobody is going to give me that month back and I do not want it back. I want it said out loud, in a room, to a person who has to hear it. And you have not written one thing down since you walked in, so I am saying it to a man and not to a page, and it stops here the second you get a pen out.**"

He did not get a pen out. He had a pen in the fourth pocket of a tool roll four days of bus travel behind him and he put his hand flat on the truck instead and left it there.

---

**She asked him one question and it was the only one and it was fair.**

"Are you going to take it off the van."

"**No,**" he said, and did not give a reason, and there was one and he has never given it to anybody.

"**Right,**" said the woman of about thirty-one, and pushed the hand truck four inches, and said, **"that is the first correct thing anybody has said to me about it since Tuesday, and I would have said so if you had said yes, and now you can go and stand somewhere else.**"

She was not grateful. She was not angry. She is not on his side and she is not against him and at no point in this entry does anybody owe her anything for talking to him. **She gave him tea in a paper cup at about eleven and he stood in a supply room and drank it, and for about nine minutes neither of them said a word, and they were not nine uncomfortable minutes, and that is printed because it is the shape of the day and not because anything was settled.**

---

**About nine pegs are on a rail behind the door of that supply room. Eight of them have a coat on them that is worn through at the elbow and has been worn through for a long time. The ninth coat was new, in a bag, with the ticket still on the sleeve.**

He looked at it for about four seconds. **Nobody in that school had ever written down that a room can be nine people or one, and he had spent four months in this city learning how to tell the two apart, and the answer was on a peg behind a door two days of bus travel from the shop where he had spent the previous afternoon asking a woman at a gate to stop watching a road.**

---

**The van is in that town on a Tuesday and on a Friday, and today is a Thursday, and the bus does not go again until the Saturday.**

He worked that out at about two in the afternoon, sitting on a wall outside a shop in that town with a cup he had not finished, and it took him about four seconds and it is the only four seconds of that day he is proud of. **Tomorrow the same van comes down that road with whatever the other eight rooms have sent, and it will come down it a second time in three weeks if the arrangement were not stopping, and it is stopping, and she does not know that it is stopping, and he has about thirty hours in which to tell her and has told nobody anything.**

**He did not tell her. He has gone over it since and the reason he gives is that a man who tells a room that a thing is stopping is a man who has become a room's idea of what is stopping, and in about four months there would be a woman four days of bus travel away who expected a man of twenty-two to tell her when things stopped, and she would be right to, and he would not be able to.**

That is the small thing he gave up on the Thursday and it is on no form in this city.

---

* * *

**He walked to the clinic in the afternoon. It took about twenty minutes from the school and he did not know he was going to until he had put his coat on, and he went the whole way on foot because there is nothing in that town to get a wheelbarrow out of, and this entry does not describe the town and does not give how far anything is from anything else.**

The building is low and it is the only one on that stretch with a light in it at half past two in the afternoon in a season when the light does not go on in a building. **The door has nothing on it. Not a name, not a number, not a plate, not a bell, not a hole for anything; and he stood outside it for about nine minutes in a coat in the wrong weather for it and did not go in, and there was a light on and there was a person in there and he knew both of those things and neither of them was a reason.**

He has been told not to go down four steps to a pavement in a second district in this city. Nobody told him about this door. **He has not gone in, and the reason he has not gone in is not the rule he was given and it is not caution and it is not politeness, and it is that he worked this out on the Wednesday at a gate in about four seconds and has not had it in his mouth since: a man who walks into a room nobody asked him into is a man deciding that room's business, and there is about nine seconds between knowing a thing and being allowed to do anything about it, and the nine seconds belong to the room and not to him.**

**He wanted, for the whole of those nine minutes and for the rest of that afternoon, to be asked. That is the want and it did not get said, and the person who could have said it was behind a supply room door on the far side of that town having a conversation with a man of about fifty-one about whether the arrangement was the arrangement.**

---

*One thousand one hundred and forty-four and one thousand one hundred and forty-eight stand four days apart, and the second of the two is a whole number of weeks and the first is not, and nothing happened in that town on either day that has anything to do with either of the figures.*

*640. Thursday of week 231, at ten, and this entry opens about four days earlier on a bus and does not describe the four days because nothing happened on them that this file holds a figure for. Eleven dated jobs carried to the Friday, because the Friday is the day a van is in that town and the man of twenty-two is in that town. A bus that goes on a Wednesday and on a Saturday. A clinic with nothing on the door. A school on the far side of that town. A supply room with a hand truck, three boxes, and about nine pegs behind the door. A woman of about thirty-one who has run that clinic about four years. Nine seconds about a month. Four people in the room and not one of them on her side, the man who asked included. Nine minutes outside a door. A person in there and a light on and a man in a coat in the wrong weather for it.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven jobs carried a date each and eleven people came over that counter on the Thursday, the last let in at about half past three, and the counter sat in the same pair of hands all day and the bar went across the bottom of the shutter at ten.** In that shop, unaltered and in no order worth the name: two places in a rail; finished work standing on a shelf by the door until somebody comes for it; a list in a clear pocket with the corners gone soft; a roll of tools; the cage and the man in it with his back turned; the bench; and the shutter, with the wheel it drops on. Callers: eleven. Refusals: three, one of them a request for a list of rooms and one a man who wanted to post something to a town and wanted to be told how long it takes, which is not a thing this shop has ever had or ever will have. Those rooms: one thousand one hundred and forty-four days, a hundred and sixty-three weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, warm. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall: seven hundred and fifty days, one hundred and seven weeks and one day. The separation: five hundred and twenty-four days, seventy-four weeks and six days, ended, walked, and printed on a day this file spends two days of bus travel away. *Nine seconds, a month, and a man who would not give the same speech twice.* **A man of twenty-two took a bus two days each way to a town he has had the address of since the spring and had never gone to, and started to say the thing he says to everybody, and stopped in the middle of it in a supply room, and was told by a woman of about thirty-one that a speech does nobody a favour. He asked her a different question instead — what do you want to know about it — and she said nothing, and then he asked her the other one, in about nine seconds, with the part about stopping at the end of the fortnight said out loud, and she said yes in about four seconds and then said what she was owed: a month of somebody else's decisions, in nine seconds, to a hand truck. She had found out from a crate that had come down that road on a Tuesday behind a stop that had been on a van door for two days. A man of about nineteen with a box of staples did not look up. A man of about fifty-one at a desk said the arrangement was the arrangement and that it was not a wrong thing, and was told that how it gets done is that nobody asks. She said she was not accusing him of doing it on purpose and then told him not to stand there agreeing with her, because agreeing with her was not the thing she needed. She said nobody is going to give the month back, did not want it back, wanted it said out loud to a person who had to hear it, and told him the whole thing stopped the second he got a pen out. He did not get a pen out. He put his hand flat on the truck and left it there. She asked whether he was going to take the stop off the van. He said no and gave no reason and has never given one. She said that was the first correct thing anybody had said to her since Tuesday. Nine minutes of tea. Then nine minutes outside a door with nothing on it, in a coat, with a light on and a person inside, and he did not go in, and what he wanted for all of it was to be asked, and nobody asked him anything.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A man of twenty-two went two days of bus travel to a room he had never stood in, and was told in about nine seconds that a month had been taken out of his own decisions, and refused one offer of help in one word because he has no standing four days away, and did not go in, and was not thanked, and left on a bus that does not go again until the Saturday.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and a man going two days to say he has come is not a third instance, because he arrived after the fact and the after is the whole of the shape of it, and neither of the two that has sat on that register since about four years ago was touched by any day this month. After about half past six there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms and she was in the chair by the door and she was about thirty and she was still in that chair when the light in that stair went off, and over three and a half hours not one thing was put to her by anybody in that building, and no apology of any kind is owed to her there and none has ever been offered. The page carrying her name at the head of it, the eighth of eight, sits inside a ring binder at the back of a shelf in that room and has not been out of it on any day of this fortnight or of the one before it. That drawer under the bench did not come open; there are four sheets in it and the fourth has never been looked at. Six objects get named in this file and none is set beside another: a sheet that came back to a room, a card on end in a rail of two, a form carrying one box, a docket with nine rooms along its back, a hand copy of a page front, a plate of iron with four slots in it. Sixty-two lines in the book, seventy-three in the tin, lid never lifted, and nothing announced in either city today, because there was no sitting.
