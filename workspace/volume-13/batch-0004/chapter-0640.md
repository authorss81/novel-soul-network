# Chapter 640 — A Wednesday, A Room Up A Stair In Saltmarket Where About Eleven People Sit Every Fourth Wednesday, A Count Of Fifty-Two Of Which Forty-Seven Correspond Given First In About Nine Seconds, Nine Items At About Four Minutes Each, A Man Of About Fifty Who Came Up On His Own And Asked A Question In About Nine Seconds, A Pen Put Down And Not Picked Up, And A Woman Of About Sixty Who Was Not Asked

**Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a room up a stair in Saltmarket after that where about eleven people sit every fourth Wednesday. This one is a number given first in nine seconds, nine items, a man who came up on his own, and a pen that went on a table and stayed there.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and eleven people came over that counter, the last of them let in at about ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage, which it has now done twice in eleven years and neither of them is on a sheet anybody has kept.

Four rooms off that service road are one thousand one hundred and fifty days, a hundred and sixty-four weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, warm. **The card on end in the rail is one thousand one hundred and fifty-four days, a hundred and sixty-four weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.**

**The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is seven hundred and fifty-six days, a hundred and eight weeks to the day, and it is a whole number of weeks for the first time since the spring, and nothing on that wall has changed and nobody has said anything about it.**

---

**At four minutes past four a woman of about sixty opened a book with a green cover on a table in that room and said the number before anything else, which is what she has always done.**

"Fifty-two."

"**Of which?**" said the man of about sixty-one.

"**Forty-seven correspond.**" She said it the way a person reads a temperature. "**Up by one. It is always up by one. The five that do not correspond are the same five and I have never shown them to anybody and I am not going to start this afternoon.**"

Nobody asked her about the five.

**That is the only number announced in this city or any other today, and it was announced once, in that room, in about nine seconds, and it will not be announced again until the fourth Wednesday.**

---

**There were eleven people in that room.** A woman of about sixty who has held it about nineteen years and is paid by nobody. A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard and one column of numbers that has never been totalled. A woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs for another body and who was there as a person. **A man of about thirty-three with nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over.** A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm. Six others, two of them new.

**And behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place, and it has been empty for four weeks and about five people in that room had noticed and not one of them had said so out loud at any point in the evening, including the two who have said so to each other in a corridor on a Tuesday.**

He is four districts away in a bed and has been since the Saturday before the last of these, and nobody in that room says so, and there is no form on which a person records that they were not in a room.

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room. It did not move and nobody has ever said who put it there.**

---

**Nine items, four minutes each, and they went the way nine items have gone every fourth Wednesday for about nineteen years.**

A boiler. A date printed in two hands. A work address and not a house. A woman who wanted to know whether she was still on a list. A name in a ledger. A van that had not come. A copy of a letter already answered. A form to be reprinted with a bigger box. A man who wanted to know whether he had to keep a thing he had already handed in, and was told in four seconds, by a person entitled to say it, that he did not.

**The book with the green cover was face down on that table until half past five. It has sixty-two lines in it and it had sixty-two lines in it at the start of the hour and it had sixty-two lines in it at the end of the hour, and the difference between those two facts is not a thing anybody in that room can tell you about and nobody in that room tried.**

At the far end of that same table there is a tin. It has a lid. Seventy-three is what is in it. Nobody lifted that lid at any point and nobody has ever converted those two figures into one.

---

**The tenth thing was not an item and it came up the stair on its own, and it is the whole of this entry.**

He said his name and nobody in that room had it. He said which gate and about four people there knew which gate, and a woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm had a list face up in front of her and gave him his name back out of it without looking down, which is what she is for.

Then he said, in about nine seconds: **"About four months ago a man told me at a gate that a kitchen stopped it. He said there was a card and a column and a name went in it. I've been carrying that for about four months and I've never once asked anybody to check it, and I'd like to know now whether it's written down or whether it's just what I was told.**"

The woman of about sixty said: **"It is written down."**

"**Right,**" said the man of about fifty. **"That's all I came for.**"

And she put her hand flat on the book and turned it over, and picked up the pen, and held it for about eleven seconds, and then she put the pen down on the table beside it.

**Nobody in that room said a word. Not the man of about thirty-three with the crates, who has not been given anything this fortnight and was not given anything tonight. Not the woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs. Not the man of about sixty-one with the column. Not the woman of about sixty, who had the pen in her hand eleven seconds earlier and has not said one word about it and will not say one word about it to anybody in this city for as long as she lives.**

---

**She put the book face down again at about ten past six and nobody at that table turned it past the sixty-second line, and nobody asked her why, and that is what happened.**

About eleven people began putting their coats on. The man of about fifty went down that stair before any of them, faster than he came up, and he went out into a street and did not stop on it.

**Nobody in that room asked the woman of about sixty why it did not open. Nobody has asked her anything in four weeks. The count went up by one anyway, and the man of about sixty-one wrote a number in his column and did not write a sentence down, and has not written one down since, and was asked about it once afterwards and said *it was in the book*, and that is between him and a column nobody has ever totalled.**

---

**And the reason is not printed in this entry, and this entry is not going to guess at it, and the only thing it is willing to say is what was in the room: a man who keeps a gate, four weeks of an empty place behind a chair, a book that stayed shut at half past five, and a pen that was picked up and put down in about eleven seconds by a person entitled to pick it up.**

**A man of about thirty-three with nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over was in that room tonight and he has been in it about four times this year and he has not once been given anything.**

He was told at the Saturday sitting in the spring that he could not ask about a tray, in four seconds, and he worked out in about four more that what he had actually been told was that he was not the person in that room who decided anything, and he has been the person in that room who decides nothing ever since. **He has a yard. There are nine crates in it. He drives for a firm. He came up that stair on a Wednesday evening at about half past five because it is the evening he comes and there is nothing else to do on it, and the yard will be a yard next Wednesday, and nobody in that room said one word to him about any of it and nobody was thanked and nobody was given anything and nobody promised him anything.**

He is not a villain. He is not a man who is owed a thing in this manuscript and there is no scene coming in which a yard is emptied. **The figure of that hold walks on a board in a shop in this city every working day of this fortnight and it is a length of time and not a person, and this entry is not going to pretend otherwise on the day the book shuts.**

---

---

**He was not in that room. He was in a shop in this city with a form folded once in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, and he does not know any of it, and he will be told in about six days by a woman of about twenty-nine who reads things out loud in shops, and he will not believe her for about two hours, and the reason he will not believe her is nine seconds long and it is that a line in that book is for a thing a person has not already been told.**

He is twenty-two and he is standing exactly where he was standing on the first page of this stretch of days and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off. **What has gone is a fortnight, four crates, a route with nine lines on it, and the ability to be the man a room sends its paper to, and the last of those four was taken off him on a Monday by a person entitled to take it off him and it was not a punishment and he has not said so and is not going to.**

The rail holds two. Two nails and nineteen lines and no twentieth. Nine hand copies of the front of a page, seven hundred and sixteen days, a hundred and two weeks and two days, eight unfinished, the first row nobody has disagreed about still not found, nothing laid against anything today. The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and ninety-eight days, ninety-nine weeks and five days. **The separation, five hundred and thirty days, seventy-five weeks and five days, shut in a form about nine weeks ago and subtracted every day since, no shorter, nothing put back into it.**

That drawer under the bench is shut with the same four sheets in it and the fourth of those has never been looked at by anybody. The four words that name the public body are not on this page. **Six objects are named here and no two of them are brought together: a sheet returned to a room, a card on end in a rail, a form with an empty box, a job docket with nine places pencilled along its back, a hand copy of the front of a page, and a plate of iron with four slots.**

---

*One thousand one hundred and fifty is two days past a whole number of weeks and one thousand one hundred and fifty-four is six, and both of those numbers have moved on every day of this fortnight by exactly one, and neither of them is a count of anything that happened.*

*643. Wednesday of week 232, at ten, and eleven dated jobs with a name over that counter for each and the last of the names let in at about ten past five. A shutter down at ten that shook the cage. A stair in Saltmarket. A green book opened at four minutes past four. Fifty-two, of which forty-seven correspond, said first, in nine seconds, once. Nine items at four minutes each and a tenth that was not an item. Eleven people. A place behind a chair, empty four weeks. A chair against a wall with its back to the room. A gate, a kitchen, a card, a column, a name. Eleven seconds with a pen in a hand, then the pen on a table. The book face down at half past five and face down at the end of the hour, on sixty-two lines both times. A tin on seventy-three, lid on, all hour. Nobody thanked at any point in it.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Wednesday carried eleven jobs with a date on each and eleven callers, the last admitted at about ten past five, and about two hours of it were in a room up a stair in Saltmarket.** Four blocks: bench, counter, Saltmarket, service road. In that shop, unremarked: the bench; the cage with its occupant facing the wall; the shutter and the wheel; a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it; a list in a clear pocket; completed work on a shelf by the door; the rail, and it takes two. Callers: eleven. Refusals: three, and one of them was a request for a list of rooms. Work: eleven, three declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: eighteen pounds, exact. The heating in that flat: above its winter setting for a seventh month, four names on that one tenancy making up the difference, and a cook about a fortnight of her own wages out of pocket and owed by nobody. Those rooms: one thousand one hundred and fifty days, a hundred and sixty-four weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, warm. Rail card: one thousand one hundred and fifty-four days, a hundred and sixty-four weeks and six days, four days past the room, rail of two. The board on two nails: twentieth, none, and never begun; fourteenth, nine hundred and eighty-six, a hundred and forty weeks and seven days; seventeenth, nine hundred and twenty-two, a hundred and thirty-one weeks and five days; twelfth, one thousand and seventy, a hundred and fifty-two weeks and six days. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall: seven hundred and fifty-six days, one hundred and eight weeks to the day. The ask: eight hundred and forty days, a hundred and twenty weeks to the day, and it is the second figure in this entry to land on a whole week today. The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns: seven hundred and eighty-three days, a hundred and eleven weeks and six days. The post at the corridor end: six hundred and ninety-eight days, ninety-nine weeks and five days. The nine hand copies: seven hundred and sixteen days, a hundred and two weeks and two days, eight of nine unfinished, the first row nobody has disagreed about still not found, and not one of the nine compared with any other on any day of this year. The separation: five hundred and thirty days, seventy-five weeks and five days, ended, walked, standing. Added to that board: nothing. The number of returns: not taken, not printed, and not anybody's to take. The four words that name the public body: in none of this entry, in no register, and not in a denial of it.

*Fifty-two of which forty-seven, a pen, and eleven seconds.* **A man of about fifty who keeps a gate came up a stair in Saltmarket on his own with nothing in his hands and asked, in about nine seconds, whether the thing he had been told at that gate about four months ago — that a kitchen stopped it, and that there was a card with a column on it and a name went in — was written down anywhere or was only what he had been told. The woman of about sixty said it was written down. He said that was all he came for. She turned the book over, picked the pen up, held it for about eleven seconds, and put it down on the table beside the book. Nobody said anything. About eleven people put their coats on and the man of about fifty went down that stair faster than he came up and did not stop in the street. The book was on sixty-two lines at the start of the hour and on sixty-two lines at the end of it. The tin was on seventy-three and its lid never came off. The count went to fifty-two, of which forty-seven correspond, and it was the only number said aloud in this city or any other today, and the five that do not correspond are the same five and have never been shown to anybody. The man of about thirty-three with nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over was in that room and was given nothing tonight and has been given nothing this fortnight and was told nothing. The woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs said nothing. Nobody asked the woman of about sixty anything, and she has not said a word about it, and the man of about fifty is still going to believe that a kitchen stopped it for as long as he lives, and that kitchen is a day and a night of bus travel away and has not been told anything at all this fortnight and has had two crates go past its door.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A book shut at half past five on a fourth Wednesday in a room up a stair, and the count went up by one anyway, and nobody in that room said one word about it, and the only man in this city who could have explained it was not in it and does not know it happened.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped, and a man who was given one true sentence in about four seconds by a person entitled to give it said *right* and went down a stair. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and a book that shuts is not nothing, because eleven people changed their coats over it, and neither of the two that has stood on that register since about four years ago was touched by any day of this fortnight. One other person was in those four rooms after about half past six. **She was thirty-odd, she sat where the door is, and she was still sitting where the door is at about nine, and over those three hours not one person in that building said anything to her, and nobody ever will, and there is nothing owed to her in this city by anybody in it. What is hers stayed in its binder at the back of a shelf in that room all day and it did not come out.** That drawer under the bench is shut and there are four sheets in it and the fourth of those is unread. The ninth chair did not move and its mover is not named. The book stands at sixty-two lines and the tin at seventy-three, and neither figure is convertible into the other, and the difference between them is not a number and is not printed as one.
