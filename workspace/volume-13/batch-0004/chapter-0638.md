# Chapter 638 — A Friday, A Grey Van At The Back Of A Depot In A Town This Entry Does Not Describe, The Largest Load Of The Fortnight On It And Arriving At About Ten, A Man Of Twenty-Two A Street Away Who Arranged All Of It And Did Not Go And Look, A Ninth Line Gone Over Twice And The Only Eight That Have Not, And A Woman Of About Thirty-One On The Far Side Of That Town Who Was Not Told And Was Not Told Again

**Friday was a grey van at the back of a depot in a town this entry does not describe, and it came at about ten with the whole fortnight on it, and the man who arranged that was a street away with his hands in his pockets and did not go and look.**

---

He had a room over a shop for the night and he had it because a woman of about sixty-three had put him in it on the Thursday night without asking him a single question about what he was doing in her town, and the whole of what she said was **"There's a bed and the tap's cold,"** and he had slept about four hours in it and been awake for the other five.

**Those four days are the Friday of the fortnight's twelfth day and it has two left. He had asked eight rooms in this city that it would stop on the Sunday whatever anybody thought about it, and it is going to stop, and the only person in this city who has ever asked him whether it was going to stop is a woman of about forty-one at a gate, and she has not asked him this week.**

---

**Nobody in the nine rooms knows that anything is wrong. He has known for about four days and has not said it to anybody.**

Since the Monday, when a man of about forty-one in a garage with the door open said in nine seconds that this kind of thing works on about four people who all know each other, and that by the fourth week a person cannot get in and nobody can say why, and every one of the reasons is a room somebody has already sent something to. **That is a thing he said on the Monday of this week about a fortnight in the future and it is the Friday of the twelfth day and the van is in that town today, and Marek has known since the Monday and has told four people in a shop and a garage and a yard and a depot and not one of them is in this town.**

The saying of it would be him deciding a thing for a room he has never stood in. **He worked that out on the Wednesday at about half past two in a service road between a bench and a van and he wrote it down nowhere and he has not said it since, and it is the reason for the whole of this Friday and he is not going to give it to a reader in a nicer sentence than the one he gave himself.**

---

**The room over the shop had a window that faced a wall about four feet off, and the woman of about sixty-three had put a second blanket on the bed without being asked and had gone downstairs before he had said anything about the cold tap.**

He had got up at about half past seven and had come down and the shop was shut and there was a note on the door in block capitals that said **BACK AT ELEVEN** and nothing else, no name, no day of the week, and he had stood in a street in a town a day and a night of bus travel from home and read somebody else's handwriting to find out when he could sit down.

**He has been two days out of this city and eleven weeks in this arrangement and there is not one person in either place who owes him anything, and the only thing he has learned about himself in two days is that he is a person who reads a note on a door.**

---

**The depot has a yard and a scale in it and a man on a gate and there was a man of about twenty-six standing at the open end of a building with a clipboard he had not been given by anybody, counting boxes out loud to a woman with a manifest folder.**

He got about nine seconds of it from behind a wall and then went round the front, because a man of twenty-two who stands behind a wall listening to a count is a man doing something, and he had decided in a room over a shop at about seven in the morning that he was not going to do anything today.

"What's the ninth one."

The man of about twenty-six said, **"Mate, I'm counting what's in front of me. If it's in front of me it's on the sheet. If it's not in front of me it's not on the sheet and I don't know it exists and neither do you."** He turned a page. **"You want to know what a sheet's for, ask that bloke in the office, and he'll tell you the same thing and he'll tell you slower.**"

**And that was the entire conversation and it took nine seconds and the man of about twenty-six was right in the way the depot woman is right and the water office is right, and being right about the sheet is the whole of what anybody in this arrangement has ever managed to be, and four hundred and fifty people in this city can each say one true thing and this is a two hundred and fifty-sixth.**

---

**The van came in at about ten and it was the largest load of the fortnight and nobody has ever seen the size of it, because nobody has ever seen it and the man who has seen it is the man in it.**

Four crates on a cage, and a fifth crate on the tailgate with the back door down and a strap over it, and about eleven boxes that are not crates stacked along the nearside where a person loading by hand puts the ones that would not stack. **The depot has a yard and a scale and a man on a gate and none of the three was used, and nobody counted it, and the man in the cab is the only person on earth who knows how many of them there are and he is writing it on the end of a paint stirrer in a garage four days of bus travel from where the van is standing.**

---

**The ninth line on that nearside door has been gone over twice.**

He noticed it from the far side of the yard, standing at a wall, at about twenty past ten. The eight struck lines are thin and flat and have been there three days. **The ninth has been over the top of itself, once lightly and once hard, so that it stands off the door in a ridge you could read in a car park with the light behind it, and it is the only one of the nine anybody in that yard could read from where they were standing, and it is the only one of the nine that nobody was ever asked about.**

**That is the whole of this Friday and nobody in the nine rooms knows it and he has known it since the Monday and has not said it.**

---

**He did not go to the depot. He stood at a wall for about nine minutes and then he walked away and he was a street away before the van turned out of the yard, and the whole of that is a thing he did on purpose and has not told anybody he did on purpose.**

He could have gone. There is no rule in this city against a man of twenty-two standing at the back of a depot. **He had about four seconds in which he could have said one thing to a man of about forty-one behind the wheel of a grey van — *not today* — and four seconds is all it would have taken, because Ray Topping has never once in nine years been told to do anything by anybody and a man who has never been told would have taken a word off a man in a street.**

He did not say it. **And the reason he has never given to anybody, including the woman of about thirty-four who took that counter on a Friday in this city and was owed an honest answer to about one question a week, is that *not today* is a decision about a room, and the room is a day and a night of bus travel away, and he has never been in it, and he has no standing in it, and a man who stops a van on a Friday for a room he has never stood in is doing for that room exactly what the van was doing for it, only slower.**

---

**The van went down that road at about half past ten. The clinic is on that road and the clinic has nothing on the door and the woman who runs it was the other side of a school with about nine pegs behind a door, and a man of about fifty-one was at a desk, and a woman of about nineteen was doing something with a box of staples.**

**Nobody saw it. That is not because the van was hidden and it is not because the road is empty. It is that nobody in that town was told that a fortnight was running, and about a month ago a woman of about thirty-one had said out loud in a supply room that nobody asks, and she was right, and this is what nobody asking looks like from a road.**

---

**In the afternoon he went back down that road on purpose and stood outside the door with nothing on it for about nine minutes again, and this time somebody came out of it.**

It was a man of about sixty-two with a set of keys and a canvas bag, and he came out at about five, and he locked the door behind him, and he looked at a man of twenty-two standing on the pavement in a coat and said, **"You waiting for somebody?"**

"No."

"**Because the doctor's in on a Tuesday and a Friday and there's nobody here the rest of the time, and if you're after the doctor you're here on the wrong day, and if you're not after the doctor then you're stood outside a locked building on a Friday afternoon, and there's about nine people in this town who've told me to stop doing that.**"

He said nothing.

"**Right,**" said the man of about sixty-two, and put his bag in his hand, and said, as he went along the road, **"You'd be the fourth one this year and you're the first one that's waited.**"

**He has thought about that sentence every hour since. About four people in a town a day and a night of bus travel off have stood outside a door with nothing on it in about a year, and three of them had gone away, and he is the only one who waited, and he waited nine minutes both times and went in neither time, and he has not been able to decide whether that makes him better than the other three or just slower.**

---

---

**In this city, at about ten, the bar went across the bottom of the roller shutter, and the woman of about thirty-four did that herself, and nobody was in the shop, and the day sheet for the Friday was written up and it is the only day sheet of this fortnight that a person in this city wrote without him in the building.**

Eleven jobs with a date against each and eleven people over that counter and the last of them let in at about twenty past five. Three refusals. **Nothing escalated and nothing handed back. A woman of about fifty-eight came in at about a quarter to four with a valve seat and waited from ten past three and took it away and did not say anything and will not need to come back, which is the fourth time in five weeks that the same thing has happened to her and the second time she has said so.**

The woman of about thirty-four shut at six on her own. She did not mention on Monday that she had.

---

**What the man of twenty-two wanted on that Friday, in about nine seconds, and did not ask for, was to be told. He has been a day and a night of bus travel from the only person who could have told him anything useful, and there is a bus on the Saturday, and it is the last one, and he is going to be on it, and by the time he gets back the arrangement will have stopped without him in the room for it.**

**What he gave up, in four seconds, on a wall, at twenty past ten in the morning, was the last hour in which he could have changed one thing about the shape of this fortnight, and he has not been able to say since whether he gave it up for the right reason or whether the right reason is the one he has told himself four days running.**

---

*One thousand one hundred and forty-five is four days past the rail card and one thousand one hundred and forty-nine is the fourth of that, and a Friday is the only day in a week on which a number of this kind gets rubbed out and begun again, in a garage, in pencil, by a man who does not do it in the dark because the light is bad after four.*
