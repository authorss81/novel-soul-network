# Chapter 634 — A Friday, Eight Of Nine Rooms Asked And Seven Of Them Said Yes, A Bus That Goes Twice A Week To A Town Four Days Away, A Van That Goes There On The Tuesday Anyway, And A Word Said In About Nine Seconds By A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Has Been Building This For Four Months And Who Did Not Ask The Question

**A Friday is a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road after. This one is eight rooms asked in five days, a timetable nailed up in a bus depot, and about nine seconds in which a thing that ought to have taken a fortnight did not.**

---

Eleven jobs dated and eleven people over the counter on the Friday, the last of them at about twenty past five. The shutter went down at ten, as it does, and came up at about half past nine the same morning, and nothing about that has been different in four months.

Those four rooms are one thousand one hundred and thirty-eight days, a hundred and sixty-two weeks and four days. The card in the rail is a hundred and sixty-three weeks and one day, four days on from them. **The post at the end of that corridor is six hundred and eighty-six days, ninety-eight weeks to the day, and it lands on a whole week and the card does not, and the two of them have never been on the same kind of day in the whole of this year and that is not a thing anybody has noticed.**

---

**He had seven in nine and no paper anywhere, and on the Friday morning at about eleven he did the arithmetic of a fortnight for the first time since the spring, and it came out wrong.**

**Seven rooms, and about nine hundred returns a year between them if the depot woman's clipboard is right, and a fortnight is a hundred and four days, and about four hundred and fifty people in this city could each say one true number about their own part of that and not one of them has a way to say it out loud to a van.**

He did it in his head at a bench and got it wrong twice and then stopped, because the number he has is not a count, it is a figure a woman of about forty-one took off a clipboard once and has never had checked, and using it twice is how a person ends up being the man who knows.

---

**The eighth room is a store with a cold room in it, and it is a town, and he got there on a bus on the Thursday night and slept four hours in a bed belonging to a man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen and did not ask him for anything in the morning.**

The store said yes in about four seconds and then said, "**Only if the cold one comes with it,**" and he said yes to that too, and that was the eighth.

**The ninth is four days of bus travel away. It is a clinic in a town with a different letterhead on its paper and the woman who runs it is about thirty-one and keeps a supply room in a school on the far side of that town, and he has never been in it and has never met her, and there is no list anywhere in this city with her room on it except the back of a man's hand in a kitchen and a card in a cellar.**

---

**And here is the whole of the Friday, and it is nine seconds, and it happens in a service road at about half past two in the afternoon between a bench and a van.**

The man of about forty-one was in the grey van with the nearside door open and the list on it, and he had a fourth thing crossed off.

"There," he said. **"That's eight. I've got eight of them crossed off and one I haven't got, and the one I haven't got is a clinic and it's four days on a bus and it isn't a question I can ask on a Tuesday."**

"I know."

"Then it's eight."

**"Not for a fortnight,"** said Marek. **"It's nine. If it's eight, the depot woman is going to work that out inside a week, and she'll be right, and then the whole thing is eight rooms agreeing about eight and it isn't a route, it's a favour with a deadline on it."**

---

**The man of about forty-one did not answer for about four seconds and then said the sentence that decided the afternoon, and he said it as a fact about a timetable and not as an offer.**

"**The van's in that town on the Tuesday and the Friday. It goes to the back of the depot and it takes about forty minutes and it comes back with four hundred kilos on it and it has done for about nine years and nobody has ever asked me to change where it goes.**"

"You could ask the woman."

"**I could put a box on the Tuesday for the clinic and she'd not know for a fortnight and by then it's a habit.**" He put his hand flat on the door again. **"That's not me deciding. That's just what a Tuesday does."**

---

**And Marek said the word. It is one word and it took him about nine seconds to get to it, and he has gone over those nine seconds roughly four hundred times since and has never once been able to make it shorter.**

"**Right.**"

---

**That is the injury. It is not a wound and it is not a device and nobody in this city knows it has happened and the man who did it was in a hurry and was right about both halves of what he said.**

**Eight rooms said yes out loud to a person standing in front of them. The ninth is going to have a box put on a van on a Tuesday in a fortnight by a man who was not asked, by a man who said one word in about nine seconds, and the woman who runs that clinic is four days of bus travel away and is owed a fortnight of that decision, and by the time any of it reaches her it will have been made.**

He wrote nothing down. He did not tell the depot woman. He did not tell the man of about thirty-four in the kitchen who has all nine on the back of his hand and who told a person under a covered walkway on a Sunday that he would be told first.

**Nobody thanked anybody, and nobody was lied to, and nobody in this city has been given a false sentence by anybody in this entry.**

---

**What he gave up on the Friday, in about four seconds, for no reason he has been able to name, was the sentence he had been carrying since Sunday under a covered walkway: that he was going to ask all nine of them the same thing. He is going to be able to say he asked nine. He asked eight. Nobody in this city can check and one man in a kitchen can, and that man has not asked, and will ask, and Marek knows the week he will ask in.**

---

* * *

**The timetable was on a wall at the depot and he read it standing up, and it took him about four minutes, and he read it the way a man reads a thing he has already decided to agree with.**

It is a printed sheet with a plastic sleeve over it and a thumbprint in the corner of the sleeve, and there are eleven times on it and eight of them are Tuesdays and Fridays, and one of the two is marked with a small pen cross that somebody put there years ago for a reason nobody now on earth can give you.

**The clinic is not on that timetable. The clinic is four days away. What is on that timetable is a road, and a stop, and a depot at the end of it, and the road is where the van turns and the stop is where people get off.**

"You can read that for as long as you like," said the woman of about forty-one from behind her, **"and I want to say now, while you're reading it, that the cross on the Friday is not mine and I have asked twice and been told it is not anybody's."**

"Who put it there."

"**Somebody with a pen and about nine seconds.**" She let that sit. **"You'd be surprised how much of this city is that."**

---

**He got on the bus at about half past three on the Thursday and it was the one that goes to a town, and the fare was a single coin and a half coin and he gave her two and she did not give him the change and he did not ask, and there is no form in this city for a coin.**

Four hours. A man of about sixty got on at a stop with a folding frame and talked to him for about nine minutes about a boiler and then apologised for talking to him, and Marek said it was all right, and it was the only conversation he has had with a stranger in this city in four months that was not about a return.

**At about half past nine he knocked on a door in that town and a man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen opened it and had a bed made up in a front room and did not say one word about the fact that a stranger was in his house.**

"You've come about the returns."

"Yes."

"Then you've come on a bus for it," said the man of about thirty-four, **"which is the most anyone has ever done about a return in this city, and I want to say that and I don't want anything for it."**

---

**They went to the store in the morning and the woman behind the counter there said yes in about four seconds and then said, *only if the cold one comes with it*, and he said yes to that, and she said, *that's not a thing I needed you to agree to, that's a thing I needed to be able to say out loud to somebody.***

**And in a café on that town's high street at about one, over two teas neither of them ordered, he worked out the thing he had worked out wrong at a bench the day before, which is that eight rooms is not eight rooms. It is eight rooms that have been asked. There are about nine hundred returns a year in this arrangement now, on a figure nobody has checked, and every one of them comes out of a building where a person is standing when it leaves, and every one of them arrives in a place where nobody is standing when it gets there.**

He said that out loud to a man of about thirty-four in a café and the man of about thirty-four looked at him and said, "**Then you've not saved nine hundred things. You've moved the standing-up to the other end.**"

---

**He wrote that down. It is on the back of the docket in the fourth pocket, in pencil, in his own hand, and it is four words and a full stop, and it is the only thing he has written in four months that he got from somebody else, and the man of about thirty-four has not been told that it was written down and has not asked to see it and would not know it if it were read out.**

**He read it back to himself twice in that café and it did not improve on the second reading, and a man of about thirty-four watched him read his own handwriting twice and said nothing, and the not-saying was the correct thing and cost that man about four seconds of wanting to say something useful.**

---

*Seventeen days and a hundred and four of them in the second half of that, and nine hundred returns a year that have never been counted by anybody, and a number a man in a van puts on a door in grease pencil and rubs out with a thumb when it stops being true.*

*637. Friday of week 230, at ten. Eleven jobs with a date on each, eleven people at that counter, the last at about twenty past five, the shutter down at ten and up at half past nine. Eight rooms asked across five days, seven of them yes and one of them a no with a reason. A store with a cold room in a town, on a bus, on a Thursday night, and four hours of somebody else's bed. A service road at half past two. A van, a nearside door, nine lines in grease pencil with a fourth of them struck out. A timetable for a town four days away that goes twice a week. A box on a Tuesday. And one word, in about nine seconds, from a man who has been saying one word at a time to about four hundred and fifty people in this city for four months.*

*Conditions and docket.* **A date against eleven jobs on the Friday and eleven people over the counter, the last of them let in at about twenty past five, and the counter was not handed over all day.** In that shop, unchanged and unremarked: the long bench; the cage and the man in it with his back to the room; the shutter on its wheel; tools in a roll; a schedule in a clear pocket; finished work on a shelf by the door; and two places in a rail at the end of it. Callers: eleven. Refusals: three, and one was a request for a number at which a person could be reached, which is not a thing this shop has and has never had. Work: eleven, three declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: seventeen pounds and then six, nothing out. The heating in that flat: above the winter's for a fifth month, four names on that tenancy between them making good, a fortnight of a cook's own wages in the hole and owed by nobody. Sixty-two lines in the book, seventy-three on the tin, lid down all Friday. **A count is announced at a sitting and on no other day; no sitting fell on this Friday, and this entry does not say what one does.** That drawer is shut with four sheets in it and the fourth has never been read by anybody. Not one of the four words that name the public body appears on this page. Five articles are spoken of and none is set beside another: a returned sheet; a card on end in a rail; a form with a box nothing has been written in; a hand copy of a page front; and a bar of iron with four slots.

*One word, and a Tuesday, and a box.* **Seven of eight rooms said yes across five days, and the eighth is a store with a cold room in a town that said yes in about four seconds and then asked for the cold one as well, and got it. The ninth is four days of bus travel away and Marek has never been inside it and has never met the woman who runs it, who is about thirty-one and keeps a supply room on the far side of that town, and the only record of her anywhere in this city is on the back of a man's hand and on a card in a cellar in a second district.** At about half past two on the Friday, in a service road, between a bench and a grey van, the man of about forty-one said that the van is in that town on a Tuesday and on a Friday, takes forty minutes at the back of a depot, and has been doing so for about nine years, and that a box put on the Tuesday for the clinic would be a fortnight before the woman knew anything and a habit by then, and that this is not him deciding, it is what a Tuesday does. Marek said one word, and it took him about nine seconds to get to it, and the word was *right*. Eight rooms agreed out loud to a person standing in front of them. The ninth is going to be included by a timetable. Nobody in this city knows it has happened. The depot woman has not been told and the man of about thirty-four in the kitchen who has all nine written on the back of his hand has not been told and will ask, and Marek knows which week he will ask in.

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A man of twenty-two said *right* in about nine seconds to a fact about a bus timetable, and the whole of what that costs lands four days of bus travel away in about three weeks, on a person who has not been asked and has not been told and is not on any list in this city.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped, and one offer of help was made on a Friday morning by a man of about fifty-eight who was refused in about four seconds by the person it was made to. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and folding a room into an arrangement by saying one word is not a third instance, because it changed what a van does on a Tuesday in a town four days away. What is on that register has not moved in about four years and both of its instances belong to one woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns, doing a thing she has done for four years without being asked to do anything different. A woman of about thirty sat in the chair nearest the door from about half past six and nobody said one word to her, and the page that belongs to her, the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf in the back, did not come out of it. The fourth sheet in that drawer has still not been read. **Sixty-two and seventy-three. No sitting on this Friday, and nothing in this entry about what a sitting does.**
