# Chapter 633 — A Thursday, Four Rooms Asked In Four Hours And Two Of Them Said Yes Before He Had Finished The Sentence, A Depot With A Gate Onto A Yard And A Woman Of About Forty-One Who Asked Him A Question About A Number Instead Of Answering One, A Man Of About Forty-One In A Van Who Did Not Get Out Of It, And A Nearside Door With A List On It In His Own Hand

**A Thursday is a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road after. This one is four buildings in four districts in six hours, a depot with a gate open onto a yard, and a van parked at the kerb with the engine off and a man in it who did not get out.**

---

Eleven dated jobs on the Thursday and eleven over the counter, the last of them at about half past four. Bench, counter, four districts, counter, service road.

Four of those rooms are one thousand one hundred and thirty-seven days old, a hundred and sixty-two weeks and three days, and the rail card is four days past them. The post at that corridor end is six hundred and eighty-five days, ninety-seven weeks and six days. **The ninth line on the two nails is eight hundred and thirty-three days, a hundred and nineteen weeks to the day, and it lands on a whole week this week and did not last week, and that is a fact about a subtraction and about nothing else.**

---

**He asked four and got three.**

He had three names in his head and none of them on paper and he worked down the list in the order the rooms sit in the city rather than the order anybody would choose, and the order is not a good one, because the water office closes its counter at three and he got there at two.

---

**The water office said yes before he had finished the sentence, and the young man of about twenty-four who had brought nine sheets down a stair in a coat lining put the card down and said the reason in about nine seconds.**

"Because the nine's already in a coat. **If you want one room instead of nine I'll have brought it here in the first place and I'd have taken about a fortnight off my own time doing it, and I don't want to go on doing that.**"

"That's a yes."

"That's a yes and it's a reason and don't tell anybody the reason, because about four people in that building will offer to do it for nothing and none of them will do it." He did not say it crossly. **He said it the way a person says a thing they have already said to themselves.**"You'd have had me either way."

"Yes."

"Then don't do the face."

---

**The depot was worse and better, and it is the only one of the six rooms asked so far that put a question to Marek before it agreed to anything, and the question was about a number, and he could not answer it, and he said so in about four seconds, and that is the entire reason the depot is in this entry.**

There is a yard behind it and the gate is up and a woman of about forty-one was on the edge of it with a clipboard and a man in orange overalls behind her.

"Who's the one man."

"That's me."

"**Then what's the point.**" She did not raise her voice and she was not being difficult and she had the same face the woman of about twenty-six had on Monday. **"There's nine rooms. If they all put their paper through a person, that person's a room. And I've been telling people for four years that the answer's nine doors and not one door, and now you're telling me it's one door with nine addresses on it."**

"That's exactly what I'm telling you."

"Right." She wrote something on the clipboard, about nine seconds' worth. **"Then how many come through my gate on a Tuesday, in a fortnight, roughly. Not a number for the paper. A number I'd have to tell a man on a lorry."**

---

**He did not have it. He had never counted anything in his life, not in this city and not anywhere, and the whole of the arrangement he is building in a fortnight is paper he has not seen the size of.**

"I don't know."

"**You don't know.**"

"No."

"You've asked four rooms and you're on your fourth and you don't know how many sheets come through a gate on a Tuesday."

"**I know how many rooms there are. I don't know how many returns they send, and neither does anybody in this city, and about four hundred and fifty people in it could each tell you their own true number and if you put them all in one place you'd get something nobody has ever written down.**"

---

**The woman of about forty-one looked at him for about four seconds and then said yes.**

"Yes," she said. **"And I'll tell you the number I've got, which is two crates and about nine hundred a year across all nine of us, and I've never had that checked, and you can have it and I would like somebody to have it.**"

---

**He did not write it down. That is the small thing he gave up on the Thursday and it is on no form and nobody in the depot knows it happened, and about nine hundred a year is a figure a woman counted once from a clipboard in about a fortnight four years ago and has carried since the way a person carries a number they have never been asked to prove.**

**He wrote a two on the back of his hand. The two is the only figure he is carrying on Thursday that is not a count of rooms.**

---

**The kitchen in the back yard said yes and then said the condition, and the condition was a person and not a time, and he had not been ready for it.**

The woman of about forty-five was at the end of a long table with eleven plates on it and a pot of something hot, and she answered while she was serving, and did not stop serving.

"Yes. **But I need to know who the one man is at eleven at night, because I have eleven people eating here at eleven at night and if a van's coming through to drop paper off then somebody in this kitchen sees a van.**"

"It's me."

"You're in a shop in Lattice Ward at eleven at night?"

"No," he said, and it came out faster than it meant to.

"Then it's not you." She put a spoon down. **"It's a van and a driver and I've got eleven people who eat off that table and one of them is fourteen and none of them signed up for a van.**"

---

**He had no answer to that in nine seconds and he had no answer to it in nine minutes, and what he actually said was a thing he had never said in a kitchen to anybody.**

"What's the alternative."

"**The alternative is it comes on the same van that comes on a Tuesday, which it does, because that's the one that comes.**" She said it without heat. **"You've been in about nine buildings in four weeks and you've been asking people to give you a fortnight and nobody's asked you what you've done about the van.**"

"Nobody's asked me."

"Right," said the woman of about forty-five. **"Well, I'm asking. I'm not saying no. I'm saying there's a fourteen-year-old."**

---

**He said yes to that as well and it is the fourth yes and there is no form anywhere in this city on which four yeses are written down, and there are four hands in this city that know it and no piece of paper in any of them.**

---

**And at about a quarter to five he passed a van.**

It was a plain van, grey, with a firm's name on the side in letters about the size of a thumbnail, and the engine was off, and there was a man of about forty-one in the driver's seat with a coat open and a hand on the wheel and no radio on and nothing at all in the cab.

**The man in the van had a list on his nearside door written in grease pencil in his own hand, and there were nine lines on it, and eight of them were crossed out.**

---

**Marek stood on the pavement and looked at it for about eleven seconds. The man in the van looked back at him and did not wind the window down and did not open the door, and after a moment he put his hand flat on the list, which is a thing a person does when they are about to be asked something.**

"You've been round in four days," the man said, through the glass.

"Yes."

"That'll be four loads and not one, and you can't afford one load, that's the whole of what I know about it." He did not ask who Marek was.

**"Where does it go."**

"I don't know yet."

"Then don't tell me," said the man of about forty-one, **"and don't tell anybody I've got a list, because I write that on a door and it's got eight things crossed off it and if the wrong person sees that door I'm finished and it's got nothing to do with you."**

---

**He did not say thank you. He stood on that pavement and said, "About nine hundred a year across nine rooms," and the man in the van said, "That's about a load and a bit," and Marek said, "It's never been counted," and the man of about forty-one said, "**Nothing's ever been counted. That's not a complaint. That's why I've got a door.**"**

---

*The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is seven hundred and seventy days, a hundred and ten weeks to the day, and a man of about fifty-two is still sitting in that yard on the same terms, and neither of those two facts has anything to do with a van in a second district on a Thursday afternoon.*

---

* * *

**The room above the line in Saltmarket is the sixth of the nine and he did not get to it on the Thursday, and the reason he gives is that the counter closes at six and the counter is where he is from four, and the reason he actually gives, to the woman of about thirty-four at about six, is different and shorter.**

"It's the one I know least about."

"You've met everybody else this week."

"I've been in all of them. I don't know the ninth one." He put the tool wrap down on the bench. **"That's not the same and I want it written down somewhere that those two are not the same, and there isn't anywhere, so I'm saying it to you."**

She said, "**Right,**" and did not write it down either, and neither of them said thank you, and that was the end of it at about six and he had four rooms off that service road to sit in for two and a half hours.

---

**The woman of about thirty sat in the chair nearest the door from half past six, the way she does. He did not look at her, because looking has been on the list of things he is not going to do to a person for about four months, and it is the only rule he has kept perfectly.**

**The fourteenth line on the two nails is nine hundred and seventy-three days, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks to the day, and the fifteenth is nine hundred and fifty-two, a hundred and thirty-six weeks to the day, and the two of them are twenty-one apart on a board where nobody has ever written the difference down, and that difference is a free check and it is clean today and it will be clean tomorrow and it is the only number in this city that anybody checks and nobody reads.**

*636. Thursday of week 230, at ten. Eleven jobs carry a date against each and eleven people come over that counter, the last at about half past four. Between two and six this is a bench, then a counter, then four buildings in four districts, then that counter again. A water office that shuts its counter at three. A yard, and a gate standing open onto it, and a woman with a clipboard. Eleven plates on a long table. A plain grey van at a kerb with the engine off and a man in it who stays in it. Nine lines in grease pencil along a nearside door and eight of them struck through. A two, in biro, on the back of a left hand.

*Conditions and docket.* **The Thursday carried eleven dated jobs and eleven callers, the last let in at about half past four, and about four of the eleven were in and out of the door while Marek was in a vehicle.** What is in that shop: the working bench; the cage, with its occupant still facing the wall; the shutter and the wheel it runs down on; tools in a roll; a list in a clear pocket, soft at the corners; completed work standing on a shelf by the door; the rail, and it holds two. Callers: eleven. Refusals: three, and one of the three was an offer of help that was declined by the person it was made to. Work: eleven, three declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: fourteen pounds, and a shilling back as change given wrong and corrected without a word. The heating in that flat: above the winter's for a fifth month, four names on the tenancy making up the difference between them, a cook two weeks of her own wages in the hole and owed nothing by anybody. The rooms: one thousand one hundred and thirty-seven days, a hundred and sixty-two weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, warm. Rail card: one thousand one hundred and forty-one days, a hundred and sixty-three weeks to the day, four days past the room, rail of two. The hold: seven hundred and seventy days, a hundred and ten weeks to the day. The ask: eight hundred and twenty-seven days, a hundred and eighteen weeks and one day. The board on two nails, in the order it is read by nobody: thirteenth, one thousand and eight, a hundred and forty-four weeks to the day; nineteenth, eight hundred and thirty-three, a hundred and nineteen weeks to the day; sixteenth, nine hundred and twenty-seven, a hundred and thirty-two weeks and three days; twelfth, one thousand and fifty-seven, a hundred and fifty-one weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against the north wall: seven hundred and forty-three days, a hundred and six weeks and one day. The seventeenth line: nine hundred and nine, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks and six days. The post at the corridor end: six hundred and eighty-five days, ninety-seven weeks and six days. The fourteenth: nine hundred and seventy-three, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks to the day. The fifteen: nine hundred and fifty-two, a hundred and thirty-six weeks to the day. The nine hand copies: seven hundred and three days, a hundred weeks and three days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found, and not one of the nine set against another. The eighteenth: eight hundred and fifty-five, a hundred and twenty-two weeks and one day. The separation: five hundred and seventeen days, seventy-three weeks and six days, ended, and walked, and standing. Sixty-two lines in that book and seventy-three on that tin and the lid stayed down. **A count is announced at a sitting and on no other day, and no sitting fell on this Thursday, and this entry does not say what a sitting does.** That drawer is shut with four sheets in it and nobody has read the fourth. None of the four words naming the public body is on this page. Five objects get mentioned in this file and this file never puts two of them together: a sheet that came back, a card on end in a rail, a form with one box left empty, a hand copy of a page front, and an iron plate with four slots.

*Three yeses in six hours and one question that could not be answered.* **The water office agreed before the sentence was finished, with a reason that the young man of about twenty-four asked not to be repeated, which is that he had been carrying nine sheets about in a coat lining for six weeks on his own time and would rather bring the lot to one room than do it again. A depot with a gate up onto a yard asked how many sheets come through it on a Tuesday, in a fortnight, roughly, not a number for the paper but a number for a man on a lorry, and Marek said he did not know, in four seconds, and the reason he did not know is that the number has never been taken by anybody, and the woman of about forty-one gave him one anyway and asked for somebody to have it, and it is about nine hundred a year across the nine and it is a figure from a clipboard in about a fortnight four years ago and he did not write it down. A kitchen in a back yard with eleven plates on a table said yes and then said the condition, which was not a time but a person: somebody has to be able to say who the van is at eleven at night, because there is a fourteen-year-old eating at that table, and the answer is the van that already comes on a Tuesday, and the man who asked the question was the only person in the four rooms who asked Marek what he had done about anything. At a quarter to five a man of about forty-one was in a grey van at a kerb with the engine off and nine lines in grease pencil on his nearside door and eight of them crossed, and he did not get out, and he did not say who he was, and he said that if the wrong person saw that door he would be finished, and that had nothing to do with Marek.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **Six rooms have now been asked. Four have said yes, one has refused with a reason that was free, and one has not been reached, and none of the four yeses is on a piece of paper in this city.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped, and an offer of help was made on a Thursday morning and declined by the person it was made to. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and saying yes in nine seconds to a question you have been asked four hundred and fifty times is not a third instance, because it moved a fortnight for a depot, a kitchen and a water office, and the two on that register are still the same woman of about fifty-two in the first of the four towns. A woman of about thirty sat in the chair nearest that door from about half past six and nobody spoke to her, and the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a back shelf did not come out of it. The fourth sheet in that drawer has still not been read. **Sixty-two and seventy-three, unchanged, lid down, and nothing was said aloud in this file about what a sitting does because there was not one.**
