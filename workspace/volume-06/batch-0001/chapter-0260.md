# Chapter 260 — A Line Is Not A Rule

The fourteenth line went on the hardboard on the Thursday evening of week ninety-one in the hand of Talia Venn, who is twenty-four, who named the cost out loud before she made the mark, and the cost was that her name is now on a surface in a building with a plate on the outside of it, and that she has said no to being findable three times in a term and a half, and about nine people heard the whole of it and nobody thanked anybody, and the heating was on its fifty-ninth day and nobody had paid for it and nobody was going to.

---

Eleven on the docket and ten in date. The stair fire door in Nine Bridges came up for the eleventh time in about a month and was attended and reset and is expected back, and the basement in an unnamed district was reported for the ninth time at about four inches, and he went, and it had gone down again on its own, and he wrote that it had.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its fifty-ninth day and it is a hundred and sixty-four days old.

---

There were about nine people in at about half past seven and about eleven by nine, which is what that room is on a Thursday evening, and the shutter came down at about ten and the day ended on a service road.

The board over the door carried four words in chalk in the hand of a woman of about sixty-eight, and above them a grey ghost of four in a sign-writer's hand that has been there since a Friday in week eighty-seven and cannot be got off it, and nobody in that room has ever tried twice.

Underneath it, on two nails, a piece of hardboard the size of a tea tray carried thirteen lines in seven hands and eight names, and the first of the thirteen was a cook's and was still the first, and the thirteenth was a first-year of nineteen's and was about five weeks old and was an opt-out, and the hardboard is not the board over the door and is not the wall list in its plastic sleeve in a repair shop four districts away, and there are three objects and none of them has ever been merged with either of the others.

Talia Venn had been in the room for about two hours and had not sat down once.

---

She stood up at about nine o'clock and she did not ask whether she could and nobody said that she could.

"**I am going to write a line on that and I have to say the cost out loud before I do it, because that is what happens here, and I have been in this room long enough to know that and I have watched it happen four times.**"

"**Then say it.**"

"**It is that my name is going to be on a surface in a building that has a plate on the outside of it with a telephone number on it and no name, and that a plate with a number on it is how about eleven things have got to this door in about a term, and that in about four years somebody is going to stand in a room and photograph that hardboard and my handwriting will be on it and I will not be able to take it off. I have said no to being findable three times in a term and a half. This is not one of the three.**"

"**Say the other part.**"

"**Say what.**"

"**Say what you wrote down, because you have written it down already, I can see that you have.**"

---

"**I wrote it last night and I did not tell anybody I had written it, because the moment I tell somebody it is a thing somebody else knows, and I have been the thing somebody else knows for a term and a half and I am tired of it.**"

She read it out.

"**I am not going to be the person who is asked about this room.**"

A first-year of nineteen, from the end chair with her boots off, said:

"**That is a line.**"

"**I know it is a line.**"

"**Then say the other thing out loud, because in about four years there is going to be somebody in this room who has not been here and they are going to read that and think it is a rule, and it is not, and the only reason they will think it is a rule is that nobody said the difference. A rule has got a number and a date on it and it hangs on a wall in a building with a lot of paper in it, and any competent person in this city can pick it up on a Monday and use it without asking anybody, and that one has been in force for eleven years and nobody has ever withdrawn it. That has two hands and one of them is yours. Yours is a line somebody wrote on a piece of hardboard, and it costs you the thing you just said, and it does not bind one person including you, and you can take it off with a thumb in front of nine people, and it is evidence of nothing in any proceeding, there being none.**"

Talia Venn said: "**I did not know that on Wednesday. I thought they were the same thing and I have been in this room a term and a half and I thought they were the same thing, and I want it entered that I did not know that on Wednesday.**"

"**Nobody is entering anything.**"

"**Then I want it said, and it has been said, and that will do.**"

---

A cook of twenty-seven said, to a sink, without turning round:

"**Nine words is a lot to pay for a piece of hardboard.**"

"**It is fourteen,**" said a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, from the fourth chair.

"**It is fourteen and it is on a wall instead of in a head, and that is the whole trade, and I have been making it for about fourteen weeks with a kettle.**"

Nobody improved on the cost. Nobody said that the cost was worth it and nobody said that it was not, and she asked for that and was not asked twice.

She wrote it on the hardboard in about nine seconds with a pen off the counter and she did not write her name, and about four people looked at where her name would have gone.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag, who had come in at about half past eight on his way somewhere and was still there at nine, read it from the other side of the room and said nothing at all, and he has said he does not have a comment on lines and has never been asked twice.

---

Nobody signed it. Nobody was asked to. He did not come to the board at all, which is the first time in a term and a half that a line has gone on that hardboard and he has not been within four feet of it, and he was at the counter with a docket the whole time, and about four people noticed, and he did not explain it and was not asked.

At about half past nine a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven put a cup of tea in front of her and said nothing at all, and about four people in that room wanted very much to ask her why she comes, and nobody did, and nobody apologised to her either, because an apology would have been a question and it would still be one.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and the figure is on the back of a woman's own card, and it is not a rate.

The shutter came down at about ten. The room was cold. The heating was on its fifty-ninth day and had been on its fifty-ninth day since seven in the morning on the Monday of week eighty-three, and there is no fund and no collection and no subscription and no grant and no list and no minute and no notice and no appeal, and none of them was proposed on that evening by anybody, and the fortnight at the start of it is not paid for by anybody in this book and is not going to be.

He said one thing out loud on a service road at about ten, standing up, in the wet, to nobody, and then wrote it down, and the two are the same sentence.

*263. Thursday of week 91, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one line in fourteen words, one cost, and fifty-nine days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and ten in date. A stair fire door in Nine Bridges reported for the eleventh time in about a month, attended, reset, expected back. The basement in an unnamed district reported for the ninth time at about four inches, attended, and found to have fallen again with nothing done to it; the entry records the fall and attributes it to nothing. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on its fifty-ninth day off. The room is a hundred and sixty-four days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and sixty-eight days in the rail and is a third object and is not in that room. The twelve lines on the hardboard are eighty-four days old and the thirteenth is thirty-five.**

**The fourteenth line is zero days old, which is a difference and not an inclusive count, and nobody in that room has any idea how old anything is.**

*The count, before and after.* **Thirteen lines in seven hands and eight names became fourteen lines in eight hands and nine names at about nine o'clock on the Thursday evening. No line was rubbed off in this ten-day run and no line has been rubbed off since the eleventh week, when a cook of twenty-seven took off a line of her own with her own thumb in front of about nine people. The first line is a cook's and remains the first line. The hardboard is on two nails, is the size of a tea tray, carries no date, no number, no reference and nothing at the top, and is not the board over the door, which carries four words in chalk in the hand of a woman of about sixty-eight and one unremovable grey ghost of four in a sign-writer's hand, and is not the wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward. Three objects. None has been merged with either of the others in any of the ten days of this run.**

*What was written, and what it is.* **Fourteen words: that she is not going to be the person who is asked about this room. Written in about nine seconds with a pen off the counter, in her own hand, unsigned, with a space left where a name would go and about four people looking at it. She had written it the previous night and told nobody, on the stated ground that telling somebody makes it a thing somebody else knows. It was not read out before it was written, which is the first time in this case that a line has gone on that board without its words being said first, and the reason is in the chapter.**

*The cost, in her words, before the mark.* **That her name would then be on a surface in a building with a plate on the outside of it carrying a telephone number and no name; that such a plate is how about eleven things reached that door in about a term; that in about four years somebody may stand in a room and photograph that hardboard and her handwriting will be on it and she cannot take it off; and that she has said no to being findable three times in a term and a half and that this was not one of the three.** Nobody improved on the cost, nobody said it was worth it, nobody said it was not, and she asked that it not be improved on and was not asked twice. **Nobody was thanked. A cook of twenty-seven priced it from a sink at a kettle: fourteen words is a lot to pay for a piece of hardboard, and the trade is that it is on a wall instead of in a head. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag, in from half past eight and still there at nine, read it from the other side of the room, said nothing, and has said he has no comment on lines and has not been asked twice. Nobody improved on the cost and nobody said it was worth it.**

*A line and a rule, which are two different objects, and which this room has been arguing about for a term and a half.* **Stated on the page by a first-year of nineteen, with the boots off, in about nine seconds: that what was written is a line, and that in about four years a person who has not been in the room will read it and take it for a rule, and that the only reason they will is that nobody said the difference. She then gave the whole of the difference without being asked and it is the standing definition of this room:**

**a rule has a number and a date and hangs on a wall in a building with a great deal of paper in it, and any competent person may pick it up on a Monday and use it without asking anybody, and that one has been in force eleven years and has never been withdrawn; a line on a hardboard costs its writer something said out loud, binds nobody including its writer, can be lifted with a thumb in front of nine people, and is evidence of nothing in any proceeding, there being none.**

**Talia Venn then said that she did not know the difference on Wednesday, that she had believed for a term and a half that the two were the same thing, and asked that it be said rather than entered, and was told that it had been said and that would do. The word rule is on the page in both senses in the same minute and neither sense was explained by the narrator.**

*What he did not do.* **He did not go to the board. He was at the counter with a docket for the whole of it and did not come within four feet of the hardboard, which is the first time in a term and a half that a line has gone on it and he has not been near it; about four people noticed and he did not explain it and was not asked. He did not sign it and was not asked to and nobody asked anybody to sign anything.**

**Nobody in that room was thanked for anything by anybody, including the two correct actions of the evening, which are a refusal given on Wednesday in a corridor by a person who has nothing to do with that hearing, and a line written on Thursday that changes nothing outside the building.***

*The people the room did not ask.* **A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door from about two until the shutter came down, with a cup of tea put in front of her and no words, and is described in this entry by a count and not by a name, an age, a district or an occupation, as she has been for about nineteen weeks. Nobody asked her anything. Nobody apologised to her. About four people wanted to and did not.**

**She is the person the fortnight of unpaid heating was paid for and she is still in that chair, and the room is still open, and it is still cold, and none of the fourteen lines on that hardboard is about her and she did not ask for one.**

*State and boundaries.* **The heating is on its fifty-ninth day off, unpaid, and is not resolved, and no fund, collection, subscription, grant, list, minute, notice, report, form, leaflet, registration or appeal exists or was proposed.**

**The only new object made by anybody in this case in ten days is one line of handwriting on an existing hardboard, and one reply on somebody else's notepaper that left the building in a stranger's bag in the previous entry and is not a page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution. The ring binder holds eight items, was not opened, and had no page read and no page removed. No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by him; a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception. The condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased. No panel and no marker. Nine keys needed and two existing, count not stated. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank. No message has arrived from anybody. A hearing is set for the Monday of week ninety-two and is not held in this run. The standing question is not stated, carries no number, and no rule about who may be asked was invented. Entry 263.**
