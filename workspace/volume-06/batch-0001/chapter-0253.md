# Chapter 253 — Three Buildings

On the Thursday morning of week eighty-nine a man of thirty-four who buys training for a company said out loud, in three buildings and in about eleven minutes in total, a sentence he had heard said in a room off a service road, and he was thanked in one of the three and ignored in one of the three and asked to leave the third, and the sentence is the first line on a hardboard on two nails and it is a cook's, and none of the four people in that room who heard him say it had agreed to be quoted.

---

Ten on the docket and nine in date, and the standing water in that unnamed basement was reported for the sixth time in about four weeks and had gone down two inches since the last attendance without anybody doing anything to it, which he wrote down.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its forty-fifth day and the gas ring was on.

---

He came in at about eleven and he had been in that room twice before and asked for nothing in either, and he gave his name at the door, which nobody in that room had ever done before and which about four people noticed.

"**Tobias Vahl. I buy training. I am not going to be here long and I am not asking you for anything, I am here because somebody in this building said that somebody who goes to a talk about training in the lower wards should come and stand in this room once.**"

He was about thirty-four. He had a coat that had been on a train that morning and a piece of paper folded in it that he did not take out for about twenty minutes.

---

"**I am going to say a sentence and I am going to tell you first that none of you said it to me to be said again and that I am saying it anyway, and I have thought about that for about nine days and I have decided that not saying it is worse and that is a decision about me and not about you.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**The room will not keep anything it cannot take back.**"

He said it the way a person reads a line off a wall, which is to say without changing it.

"**I have said that in three buildings this month. I have said it to about thirty people in total. I was thanked in the first one by a woman who meant it. Nobody in the second one heard it, and I could tell, and I said it anyway. I was asked to leave the third one by a man who was polite about it and who used the word *scope* twice.**"

"**Which of those was the difficult one,**" said a woman of twenty-four who was in for a thing that had to do with a care-link and had not been offered one.

"**The second. The second is the one I keep thinking about. In the first one I had a person. In the third one I had a wall. In the second one I had about eleven people and every one of them was waiting for somebody else to move, and I stood there and said a true sentence and nothing happened, and I have been not-enjoying it for about nine days.**"

"**It is a true sentence.**"

"**It is the truest sentence in this city and I have watched it not do a single thing for about nine days.**"

---

"**What did the woman who meant it do with it.**"

"**She wrote it on a whiteboard in a room with about nine chairs in it and did not attribute it and said out loud that she had done that.**"

A first-year of nineteen said, from the end chair:

"**That is worse than photographing it. Photographing it makes a picture. A whiteboard makes it a thing that is in the building, and in about four years somebody is going to write a sentence under it and the two will be read together and neither of them will be about a cook.**"

"**It is a woman who has been on about eleven hundred leaflets,**" said the man of thirty-four, "**and I have read four of the leaflets and I did not know that before today, and I still do not think a whiteboard is the same as a photograph.**"

"**It is not the same. It is worse and it is smaller. That is all.**"

---

The man of twenty-two said: "**You have carried it out of the room.**"

"**I have.**"

"**Now about nine people in three buildings know a man of twenty-two has a view about names, and none of them know which of us said it, and none of it is on anything.**"

"**It is on a whiteboard.**"

"**It is on a whiteboard in a building none of us has been inside and there is no date on it and no version and the whole of the sentence is a cook's and she would not want it out of that room and she cannot stop it and she has never once been asked.**"

The man of thirty-four folded his piece of paper in half, then in half again, and did not open it.

"**Then I have made a woman I have never met responsible for about thirty people, and I have done it because I did not want to be the person saying it,**" he said. "**I would like to be told I am wrong and I do not think I am going to be.**"

Nobody told him he was wrong.

---

"**There is a card,**" he said, at about twenty past twelve. "**I am not going to read it out. It is a company's and I bought it and it is in a pack of about four hundred in a storeroom in Crown Terrace and if I read it out in here then it is in this room.**"

"**Then describe it.**"

"**I can describe it. It has a starting thing you hold. It has steps. It has a place where you let go. It has a line about what it must not touch, printed in a box. And it has nothing at all in it about how you decide you are finished.**"

A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, who was in at the back with a page of his own in the ring binder, said: "**Do not describe it any further.**"

"**I am not going to.**"

"**I am going to say why and I have said why twice in this city and both times in this room. I will not go and look at it. Not because of you. Because if I go and look at it then in about four years there is a list of buildings and the buildings are the ones I went into, and a list of buildings is a list of people, and I have said that in this room before and you have all heard it.**"

"**You have never given a reason for the eleven.**"

"**No. I have given a reason for not going. The eleven are a different sentence and I do not have one and I am not going to make one up in a room on a Thursday.**"

---

The woman of twenty-four was there for a second hour and at about half past two she said the half of it that was hers, and she said it to the room and not to him.

"**A sentence in a room is nine people. A sentence outside a room is nobody's. Those are the two things there are and neither of them is a third thing and you cannot build a third thing out of the two.**"

"**I have been saying that sentence in about nine rooms for a term and a half and it has never once been outside one of them.**"

"**Because you say it in rooms. If you said it in a hall it would be nine hundred people and it would be a slogan in about a fortnight and the cook would be a woman with a whiteboard.**"

"**That is the best argument anybody has made in this room and I am not going to pretend I have an answer to it.**"

"**You have got a different answer and it is the one you have had since the spring before the one before last, which is that a room is a room and a sentence is a sentence and a city is a thing that happens to other people.**"

"**That is not an argument, that is a position.**"

"**It is a position and it has kept about nine people out of this manuscript's own trouble, and I am not going to argue with it in a room on a Thursday.**"

The shutter came down at about ten and it was still cold.

He wrote one thing on a service road in the dark.

*256. Thursday of week 89, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one sentence in three buildings, one card nobody read out, and a refusal.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Ten on the docket and nine in date. The basement is at about twenty inches and was reported for the sixth time inside about four weeks and has fallen two inches since the last attendance with nothing done to it; the entry records the fall and attributes it to nothing. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on its forty-fifth day off and the gas ring was on because a cook put it on. The room is a hundred and fifty days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is fourteen days in the rail. The twelve lines on the hardboard are seventy days old and the thirteenth is twenty-one. Eight items in the ring binder, unchanged, and the eighth was added on the Monday and has not been read.**

*The man of thirty-four.* **Named at the door, which nobody in that room had done before, and about four people noticed it. He buys training for a company, has attended twice and asked for nothing on either occasion, and came on a Thursday because a person in his own building had told him that a person who goes to a talk about training in the lower wards should stand in that room once. He said beforehand that nobody in the room had asked him to repeat anything and that he had decided in about nine days that not repeating it was worse, and that the decision was about him and not about the room. He has been named in this case for about nine days and had no name until today.**

*What the sentence did in three buildings.* **The first line on the hardboard, a cook's, read without alteration, said to about thirty people across three buildings in about a month. In the first building a woman who meant it wrote it on a whiteboard with about nine chairs in the room, uncredited, and said out loud that she had done so. In the second building about eleven people heard it and every one of them was waiting for somebody else to move, and the speaker identified that as the difficult occasion. In the third building a man used the word *scope* twice and asked him to leave. He identified the first as the occasion he was worst at, and named the whiteboard as the thing he had made a woman he had never met responsible for, and said he did not expect to be told he was wrong and did not think he would be. Nobody told him he was wrong.**

*The whiteboard, argued about by two people.* **A first-year of nineteen said a whiteboard is worse than a photograph and smaller, because a photograph makes a picture and a whiteboard makes a thing that is in the building, and that in about four years a sentence will be written under it and the two will be read together and neither will be about a cook. The man of thirty-four answered that a woman who is on about eleven hundred leaflets and who has not knowingly agreed to be quoted is a different problem from a photograph of a counter, and that he had not known about the leaflets before today. A woman of twenty-four put the whole of it in about thirty words: that a sentence in a room is nine people, a sentence outside a room is nobody's, that those are the two available things, and that a third cannot be built out of the two. She stated that the argument as held by the man of twenty-two is a position and not an argument, that it has kept about nine people out of trouble, and that she was not going to argue it in a room on a Thursday.**

*The card, described and then stopped.* **A company card, bought by the speaker, held in a pack of about four hundred in a storeroom in Crown Terrace. He declined to read it in that room on the ground that reading it there would put it there. He then described it: a starting thing held, steps, a place where the holder lets go, a printed box saying what the practice must not touch, and nothing whatever about how a person decides they are finished. **A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts instructed him to stop, and gave his standing reason for the second time in this case, which is that if he goes and looks then in about four years there is a list of buildings and a list of buildings is a list of people. He added that the eleven buildings are a different sentence, that he has no reason for the eleven, and that he will not make one up in a room on a Thursday.**

*The cost, which is not on anything.* **About nine people in three buildings now know that a man of twenty-two holds a view about names, and none of them know which of the people in that room said the sentence, and the sentence itself is a cook's and has been carried out of a room she has never been asked about, and she is owed nothing and is not to be asked.**

*Boundaries.* **No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution was created by anybody in this case; the whiteboard in the first building is another company's and another building's and is not ours. No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by anybody. Nine keys needed and two existing, not stated aloud. The standing question was not stated in the room and carries no number. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, and no message has arrived. Entry 256.**
