# Chapter 255 — Four Parts And Not Five

On the Monday morning of week ninety a clerk of forty-four put a company card on a counter in a district office and read it out loud, and the man of twenty-two turned it over twice and found that two of the five things on it were printed in a different typeface from the other three, and the date on the revision was eleven years old, and nobody had withdrawn anything, and the whole of that is a piece of paper and not a conspiracy.

---

Ten on the docket and nine in date, and the standing water in that basement was down to about sixteen inches and had been reported for the seventh time and nobody had put a stopcock on anything.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its forty-ninth day.

---

Aurelia Sopp sent for the person who answers her questions, which is a job nobody has the title for, and the person who came was a woman of forty-four called Noor Vaszary, and she has been a count in this case since a term and a half ago and nobody in this room knew her name and she said it at the counter, in the ordinary way, and about four people heard it and one of them wrote it down in a notebook that is not the book and is not in that room.

She brought a plastic wallet. Inside it were about nine cards and a folded sheet of paper with a list on it of which printing each of them was.

"**These are ours,**" she said. "**Not mine. Ours. They come in a pack of about four hundred and they go out to about eleven sites and the sites are not always ours.**"

"**Some of them are a company's.**"

"**Most of them are. The one on the top is not.**"

---

She put it on the counter and turned it round so it faced the other way, which is a thing people do in this office.

"**Read me what it has on it.**"

The man of twenty-two read it. Everybody in the room could have read it and two of them did not want to.

"**It has a starting thing in it. It has steps. It has a place where you let go. It has a box that says what the practice must not touch. And it says at the bottom that there is an aftertaste and that it is normal.**"

"**That is five things.**"

"**That is five things. That is every part of a practice, named, on one side of a card about the size of a playing card.**"

"**And what is not on it.**"

"**What is not on it is how a person knows they are finished.**"

He said: "**Do not say that too quickly, because if you say it too quickly everybody in the room starts looking for who put it there.**"

"**I have not said it quickly. I have been carrying it since about a term and a half and I brought it today because a person of sixty asked me a question in this building and I am the person who answers her questions.**"

---

Then he turned the card over.

He did it because a card is a card and it is the one thing in this case he is better at than anybody else, and he has said in about four rooms that he is a person who looks at objects, and there is no version of that being a technique.

"**Give me that pack.**"

She gave him the pack. He took out four cards and laid them face down in a row and put the fifth one beside them and then turned all five over.

"**Same card. Five printings. The starting thing and the steps and the let-go are the same typeface in all five. The box about what the practice must not touch is the same typeface in four of them.**"

"**And the fifth.**"

"**The fifth has that box in a different typeface and a half-point larger, and it is the one you have given me, and it has been in circulation for about eleven years.**"

"**How do you know eleven years.**"

"**I do not. I know that is the printing on it. The date is on your list.**"

She turned the folded sheet round. There was a revision column. The revision in the box about the boundary was dated eleven years ago and the four before it were older than that and the list had no note of any kind beside any of them, and there was no line anywhere on it that said withdrawn.

---

Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds and then Aurelia Sopp said:

"**So somebody added the line about what it must not touch, eleven years ago, and did not add the beat that enforces it.**"

"**That is what the card says.**"

"**That is not a question and I want it minuted that I did not ask it, because in about four weeks somebody is going to say that an officer of this district asked whether a company was hiding something, and I did not, and this is a card with two typefaces on it.**"

"**Nobody is taking minutes in this office.**"

"**I am aware. I have said it out loud so that nobody can afterwards say I had not.**"

---

The man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was in the room at about half past two and was told about it in about four sentences by a woman of twenty-four and did not ask a question and said, at the end:

"**I have already told that room I will not go and look. Do not spend the sentence twice.**"

"**Nobody is spending it twice.**"

"**I am not talking to you. I am talking to the next person who asks me, and there will be one, and I would like it on the record that the answer was given before the question.**"

A first-year of nineteen, who has been in that room for a term and a half, said the thing that is still the position and did not improve it and was not thanked:

"**We are not going to write that down anywhere, and if somebody writes it down it is a record of a card and a card is a company's, and a record of a card says who read the card.**"

"**Somebody has to write it down,**" said the woman of thirty-four who buys training, who was in for the afternoon and had not been in the room that morning. "**I have nine hundred of them.**"

"**Then you write it down, and it is yours, and you will be findable, and I am not going to stop you and I am not going to help you.**"

"**I would rather be findable than be the ninth person in a row who is not.**"

---

The clerk of forty-four said, at about four o'clock, while packing the wallet:

"**There is a thing I have not said and I am going to say it now so that it is in a room and not in a corridor.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I worked in Crown Terrace for about two years, a long time ago, in an office that had nothing to do with this, and I left and I came here and I have never worked on a district again until about four years ago.**"

"**Do you remember which floor.**"

"**No.**"

"**Would you remember the name of the room if somebody said it.**"

"**I would remember the name of the room. I would not remember anything else. There were about nine people in it and I typed for all of them and I was not important and neither was the typing.**"

She put the wallet in her bag.

"**That is all. I am telling you because in about four years somebody is going to find out that a clerk in this district used to type in a building about two hundred metres from where I work, and I would rather it came from me on a Monday.**"

He wrote one thing, on a service road, in the wet, at about ten.

*258. Monday of week 90, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one card, one typeface, and forty-nine days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Ten on the docket and nine in date. The basement in an unnamed district is at about sixteen inches, down from about twenty-two at the last attendance and about nineteen at the one before, reported for the seventh time in about four weeks; no stopcock, valve or inlet has been fitted by anybody, and the entry records the fall and attributes it to nothing. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on its forty-ninth day off. The room is a hundred and fifty-four days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and fifty-eight days in the rail and is not in that room. The twelve lines on the hardboard are seventy-four days old and the thirteenth is twenty-five. Eight items in the ring binder, unread.**

*The card, and what it names and does not name.* **Brought by a clerk of forty-four who was named at the counter in a district practice standards office and who is the person who answers the officer's questions; the plastic wallet held nine cards and a folded sheet listing printings. The card names all five parts of a practice: a starting hold, ordered steps, a point of release, a printed box stating what the practice must not touch, and a statement that an aftertaste is normal.** It contains no beat that performs the boundary, and no instruction by which a practitioner determines that they are finished. **The clerk stated that she has held this since about a term and a half and produced it because a person of sixty asked her a question in that building, and that she is the person who answers that officer's questions and not the officer's instrument.**

*The five printings, and the two typefaces.* **Five copies of the same card were examined. The hold, the steps and the release are set in one typeface in all five. The box about what the practice must not touch is set in the same typeface in four of them and, in the fifth, in a different typeface at half a point larger. The folding sheet carries a revision column; the revision that changed that box is dated eleven years and the four earlier printings are older, and no line of that sheet carries any note of withdrawal, amendment, reason or reviewer. The revision is a fact about a typeface and a column and is not evidence of a decision by any person, and the district officer said so unprompted in the room and asked that it be noted that she did not ask a question about concealment.**

*What was said and refused in the room.* **A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, told the finding in four sentences, asked nothing, and restated his standing refusal to go and look, on the ground already given twice, adding that the sentence should not be spent twice and that the answer was given before the question. A first-year of nineteen stated the room's unchanged position: that nothing is to be written down, that a record of a card is a record of a card and not of a practice, and that a record says who read the card; and that if a person with about nine hundred of them wishes to write it, the record is theirs and that person is findable, and that the room will neither stop them nor help them. A man of thirty-four who buys training answered that he would rather be findable than be the ninth in a row who is not, and holds no standing in that room and was not thanked.**

*The disclosure made in the room and not in a corridor.* **The clerk of forty-four stated, unprompted, before leaving, that she worked in Crown Terrace for about two years a long time ago, in an office unconnected to this one; that she has not worked on a district since until about four years ago; that she does not remember the floor; that she would remember the name of a room if it were said to her and nothing else; that there were about nine people in it; that she typed for all of them; and that she was not important and neither was the typing. She gave the reason for saying it in the room: that in about four years somebody will find out that a clerk in this district used to type in a building about two hundred metres from where she works, and she would rather it came from her on a Monday. No name was asked for and none was given. No room was named in this chapter and no floor and no date.**
