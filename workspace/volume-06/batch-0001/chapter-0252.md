# Chapter 252 — The Box In Pencil

On the Wednesday morning of week eighty-nine a woman of sixty whose job it is to ask questions asked a woman of twenty-four, in a room in District 3 with a wall binder of printed standards in it, whether a person can take a share back without a form, and was told yes, and was told that that is not a thing anybody in this city has ever had to do, and read a date out loud from a document that is still in force, and the man of twenty-two was in the corridor for the last four minutes of it and not in the room for the first fifty.

---

The docket was a stair fire door in Nine Bridges that he did not attend, and a bus in Nine Bridges that did not need him, and about nine inches of standing water against the same wall as last week in a basement in a district whose name is not in this book, reported for the fifth time, and he wrote in the description box that the fault is not the pump and that he is not going to write where he thinks the water came from.

The word was at nine and ten and the heating in that room was on its forty-fourth day.

---

The office is on the ground floor with the window painted out at the bottom and about four rooms' worth of paper on a counter in four stacks, and a wall binder with about nine printed things in it standing open on a nail, each one with a reference and a date, and the dates are the interesting part.

Roisin Keogh works there and has worked there for the district, and she read a letter for a man in a car park in the eighth week of a term two years ago and telephoned the woman who was going to read it on the Friday to say what was in it, and he has not forgotten that and has not used it.

The woman of sixty is Aurelia Sopp. She has been a practice standards officer in District 3 for nineteen years and her job is to ask questions, and she is going to be asked in the course of this volume whether that is a post, and the answer is that it is a job.

She had a page in front of her with four fields on it and one of the fields was a tick, and she turned it round so that the other person could read it, and the other person did not look at it.

---

"**I am going to ask you the whole thing and then I am going to ask you a smaller one, and I want the whole one first because the small one only makes sense after it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Can a person take a share back without a form.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**In this city.**"

"**In this city, yes, and in about four buildings, and in one of those four by hand, and the hand is a clerk's, and it is a box ruled in pencil with three words over it in her own writing.**"

Aurelia Sopp wrote one word down and then put the pen down.

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**About forty-one people in one district can be brought out of a thing that was closed on a Tuesday about twenty-two weeks ago. The page exists because somebody put a form in about four years ago. It cannot be closed from the outside. There is no job in this city whose work it is to telephone those people. About nine of them were telephoned before anything happened to them. One of the nine did not happen, and that one is a man of fifty-one and he was safe, and the woman who rang him afterwards found out that she could have rung him on the Friday and did not, because the number in front of her was a man's list of buildings and not a page.**"

"**And the box.**"

"**The box is the only one in this city for the person who took a share off. It has one name in it. It has no reference number, it is not on a system, it is not a form, and it may not take a second name, and nobody in this city has ever asked the clerk for it and she is fifty-three and has nineteen years behind her and does not want it.**"

"**There is a procedure and no post.**"

"**There is a procedure, which is a page and a pencil, and no post, which is a woman who is not allowed to be a post.**"

---

"**I am going to say the figures and you are going to stop me if I have them wrong.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Four hundred and one replies went into one company's general enquiries box in about four weeks, of which about nine of the first forty-one got the company answer, which means there are about three hundred and sixty letters in a building in this city that a man in that building has not read and that a clerk of forty-four has read. Nine hundred doors in four buildings carry a number off a different line of the same company's list. A room in a corridor says two-fourteen and is two-oh-seven, and about four hundred leaflets in a rack on that floor are all printed two-oh-seven, and the ticket for it was closed with the right number written on it in the right typeface by a man doing the job properly.**"

"**That is a fault report that was answered correctly.**"

"**That is a fault report that was answered correctly and it changed nothing, and the plate is still on the wall, and the room is still on a shelf, and the ticket is closed.**"

Aurelia Sopp did not write that down. She looked at the four fields for about four seconds.

"**What is the number on the standard for a card that does not perform its release.**"

---

"**I do not have the reference in my head and I am not going to read a reference out loud in a room.**"

"**Then tell me what it says.**"

"**It says a practice card is not to be issued unless the card carries a beat that performs the boundary. It has been in that binder since before I came here. It has a date on it, and the date is when it was last looked at properly, and the date is eleven years old.**"

"**Is it withdrawn.**"

"**It is not withdrawn. Nobody withdrew it. Nobody has withdrawn anything in that binder in my time and I would know, because withdrawing one is a thing you write on it and nobody has written on any of them.**"

She turned the chair about a quarter of a turn and said: "**That is the whole of it. That is the thing your campaign is against, and it is not a conspiracy and there is nobody in it to catch. It is a rule, in a binder, on a wall, that eleven competent people in this district invoke every year, correctly, and it stops a card going out that would leave a learner with a boundary written on it and nothing that performs it.**"

"**And it is right.**"

"**It is right. It is completely right. I would be worried if it were not.**"

---

The door was open about nine inches and the man of twenty-two was in the corridor and had been for about four minutes, and he had heard the last part of it and not the rest, and Aurelia Sopp turned and saw him and did not ask him to come in and did not ask him to go away.

"**You have a room,**" she said.

"**I have been in a room.**"

"**Have you ever seen one of these.**"

"**No.**"

"**Would you know if somebody put one in front of you.**"

"**No.**"

"**That is the most useful thing anybody in this building has said this morning and you did not know you were saying it.**"

He said: "**There is a thing on a wall where I have been, and it is a hardboard on two nails, and it has thirteen lines on it, and the first one is a cook's, and every one of them was written by the person it is about, and it is not a form, and it is not enforceable against anybody who did not write it, and any of them can be rubbed off with a thumb.**"

"**Then I have no use for it and I am not going to pretend otherwise.**"

"**You could photograph it.**"

"**Somebody did, in a prospectus, about four hundred people have read it, and I am not going to do it again.**"

---

Then she asked the smaller one, and it took about nine seconds, and it was the thing she had wanted the whole thing for.

"**Are you going to say this again.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**Why.**"

"**Because I want it, and wanting it is not a reason, and I have known that since about two minutes ago and I am saying it anyway because I would rather be told than have it noticed later.**"

"**You are the first person in nineteen years to have said that sentence to me in this room,**" said Aurelia Sopp, "**and about nine of the last eleven said no and gave a reason, and two of those nine reasons were better than yours.**"

The man of twenty-two said: "**She is not the only one of them who is going to say it.**"

"**No. She is not. I am going to have to find out whether the other one says it in the same room, and I would rather find that out from you than from a chair.**"

He wrote one thing, on a service road, at about ten, in the wet.

*255. Wednesday of week 89, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is four fields, one date, and a binder on a wall.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date, the first of this volume and the first time in about a term that the number of jobs in date has equalled the number on the list. The bus in Nine Bridges did not require attendance and the standing water in a basement in an unnamed district was reported for the fifth time and is at about twenty-two inches, and the entry records that the fault is not the pump and declines to state where the water is thought to come from. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on its forty-fourth day off. The room is a hundred and forty-nine days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is thirteen days in the rail and is a third object. The twelve lines on the hardboard are sixty-nine days old and the thirteenth is twenty.**

*The room in District 3.* **A ground-floor office with about four rooms' worth of paper in four stacks on a counter, a window painted out at the bottom, and a wall binder standing open on a nail holding about nine printed standards, each carrying a reference and a date. A standards officer of District 3 named in this manuscript since the eighth week of a term two years ago read a letter for a man in a car park and telephoned the woman who was to read it on the Friday in advance of the reading; the man has not forgotten it and did not use it today. The person whose job it is to ask the questions is a practice standards officer of District 3, sixty, nineteen years in the office. **The standard on a practice card is PS-3-11(c), dated eleven years, and it requires a practice card to carry a beat that performs the boundary before it is issued. It is not withdrawn. No reference was read aloud in the room and the reading of it is a thing this entry holds and the room does not.**

*The whole question and the four figures.* **Asked whether a person can take a share back without a form, and answered yes, in this city and in about four buildings, one of them by hand, by a clerk of fifty-three with nineteen years behind her, in a box ruled in pencil with three words over it in her own writing, holding one name, carrying no reference, not on a system, and not to be a post. The page of about forty-one people in one district cannot be closed from the outside; about nine were telephoned before anything happened; one did not happen and he was safe. Four hundred and one replies in a company's general enquiries box, of which about nine of the first forty-one received the company answer, leaving about three hundred and sixty unread in that building and read by a clerk of forty-four. About nine hundred doors in four buildings carry a number off a different line of the same company's list. A wall plate in one corridor reads two-fourteen over a room that is two-oh-seven, with about four hundred leaflets printed two-oh-seven in the rack on that floor, and the fault ticket was closed with the correct number written in the building's own typeface by a man doing the job properly, and it changed nothing.**

*What the officer said about the rule.* **That the standard is right, that she would be worried if it were not, that it is invoked about eleven times a year by competent people who are owed nothing, and that it stops a card going out that would leave a learner with a boundary printed on it and nothing that performs it. That it has not been withdrawn and that nothing in the binder has been withdrawn in her time, because withdrawing one is a thing a person writes on it. That there is no conspirator in it. That the question of who may invoke it is therefore a question about about four hundred people and not about one, and she said that without being asked for it.**

*What the man of twenty-two said and did not say.* **He was in the corridor for the last four minutes and did not enter the room. He said that he has been in a room; that he has never seen one of those standards; and that he would not know if one was put in front of him. He described the hardboard on two nails in thirteen lines, the first of them a cook's, each written by the person it is about, not enforceable against anybody who did not write it, and removable with a thumb. He was told there was no use for it. He said that somebody had already photographed a thing in a room for a prospectus read by about four hundred people and that he was not going to do it again. **He did not say the standing question out loud and no number is attached to it.**

*The smaller question, and the answer given to it.* **Asked whether she was going to say all of it again. She said yes. Asked why, she said because she wants it, that wanting it is not a reason, that she has known that for about two minutes, and that she would rather be told than have it noticed later. The officer recorded this as the first such statement in nineteen years in that room, against about eleven refusals of which about nine gave a reason and two of those were better. She was told she is not the only one who will say it, and was told that the officer intends to find out whether the other one says it in the same room, and would rather be told by her than find out from a chair.**

*Boundaries.* **No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution was created by anybody in this case; the binder on the wall and the four fields on the officer's page are both other people's and are older than this case. No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by anybody. Eight items in a ring binder in another district, unchanged. Nine keys needed and two existing, count not stated. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, and no message has arrived from anybody. Entry 255.**
