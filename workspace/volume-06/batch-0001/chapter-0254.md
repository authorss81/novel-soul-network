# Chapter 254 — The Word He Did Not Say

On the Friday of week eighty-nine the man of twenty-two stood in the back of nine folding chairs in a company training room in Crown Terrace and watched a woman of thirty-six finish a practice the only way anybody in that building finishes it, and a woman of about twenty-two could not finish it at all, and the word that made it work was said by a person whose job it was to say it, and he was in the room, and he was the only other person in the room who could have said it, and he did not, and the reason took him about nine seconds afterwards and he gave it out loud on a service road.

---

Grade three, twenty to eight, on a card in date. Eleven on the Friday list for the week and about four of them not on the list, and the number eleven is on the back of a woman's own card in a flat in Nine Bridges and is on no form anywhere and is not a rate, and about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard.

The stair fire door in Nine Bridges was the fourth job and was attended at about half past two, and the closer was reset again for the fifth time in about a fortnight and will be back.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its forty-sixth day and it is a hundred and fifty-one days old.

---

The training room is off a corridor on a floor with about nine other rooms on it and there is a laminated sheet on a post by the door with nine words on it and no name and no date and no company and no version, and he has seen that sheet in about nine other places in this city and it is the same nine words in all of them.

Nine folding chairs. Nine people. A woman of thirty-six with a company lanyard and a card of her own on the table in front of her, and a man of fifty-one in the second row with a bad wrist who came in on a Friday and is not on the list.

He was there to confirm scope, which is the whole of his job on a grade three Friday, and his name is on the list for the week in a system, and his exception is recorded and renewed for the term in a box that says *No reason given by holder*.

He has confirmed about nine hundred of these. He has never once been asked to teach one.

---

The practice is a load-hold with a boundary-release at the end of it, and the boundary-release is the beat that confirms the agreed scope, refuses further sharing, and hands the choice to act back to the person doing it.

The card in front of each of the nine people has that beat on it. It is printed in a box, in the same typeface as the box about what the practice must not touch, and it is a good box, and the words in it are the words a person would use.

The woman of thirty-six ran the practice through four times in about twenty minutes, and at the end of each one she said one word and stopped, and the nine people stopped, and the man of twenty-two understood in about nine seconds that the word was not *finished* and was not *release* and was not any of the words on the card.

He asked her, at the end, in the corridor, what the word was for.

"**To confirm the release.**"

"**That is what the card says it is for.**"

"**It is, and the card is not the room. The card is what somebody wrote down and the room is what people actually do, and in about four years a person is going to be sitting in a chair somewhere and they are going to be waiting, and I am the person they are waiting for, and I would like somebody to be able to tell me why that is a good idea.**"

"**Do you think it is a good idea.**"

"**I think it is very good at not going wrong and I think that is not the same and I am thirty-six and I have been doing it for nine years.**"

---

On the last run the woman of about twenty-two in the front row could not do it.

She did the hold. She got to the end of it. She stopped, and she looked at the card, and the card has the beat on it, and she waited.

Somebody at the back counted about eleven seconds. He counted them twice.

Then the woman of thirty-six said the word and the woman of about twenty-two finished and everybody in the room went back to the start.

The man of twenty-two did not say the word. He was in the fourth row against the wall and he had the word in his mouth and it was one syllable and he could have said it at any point in the eleven seconds and about four people in that room would have been glad.

He did not.

---

The man of fifty-one with the bad wrist said, on the way out, to nobody:

"**She is going to go home tonight and practise it and she is going to wait, and she is going to do that about four times, and the fourth time is going to be in a stairwell on her own and there is going to be nobody there, and then it is going to work without her noticing and that is the good outcome and it is the bad outcome and it is the same thing.**"

"**You have written that down somewhere.**"

"**I have written a version of it down at home for a company and I have been told that is not a field. And the field is blank and the person who licensed it is not named, and I have been trying for about two years to find out who they were and nobody will tell me, and I have stopped asking, and I have not stopped noticing.**"

Nobody thanked the man of twenty-two for not saying the word and nobody said anything about it in the corridor either.

---

At about half past four on a service road with the shutter behind him he said the reason out loud, standing up, to nobody, and it took him about nine seconds to find and it is not a sentence he had.

"**Because I am the man in the lanyard.**"

He said it again, to check it.

"**Nine people a week, about eleven, seven on the rota, four not on it. That is me. That is not a figure about somebody else. In about nine minutes in a room in Crown Terrace I was the only other person in that building who could have made that woman of twenty-two finish, and I did not, because I do not know what happens to a person who finishes it on her own and does not know she has done something she should not have been able to do.**"

He wrote it down afterwards and it is in the entry below and it is the same sentence.

---

At about nine the shutter came down and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door with a bag on her lap and had been there since about half past two, and a cook of twenty-seven put a cup of tea in front of her and said nothing at all, which is now what happens every day and has been for forty-six days.

He wrote one thing on a service road in the dark.

*257. Friday of week 89, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one release, one word, and forty-six days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Grade three from twenty to eight, on a card in date, with the exception for the term recorded and renewed in a system in a box that says *No reason given by holder*. Eleven on the Friday list for the week and about four of the eleven not on it. The city's standing weekly figure is unaltered: about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, the number is on the back of a woman's own card, it is on no form, and it is not a rate. Four jobs attended, of which the fourth is a stair fire door in Nine Bridges reset for the fifth time in about a fortnight and expected back. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on its forty-sixth day off and unpaid. The room is a hundred and fifty-one days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is fifteen days in the rail and is a third object and is not in that room. The twelve lines on the hardboard are seventy-one days old and the thirteenth is twenty-two.**

*The room and the laminated sheet.* **A company training room off a corridor on a floor with about nine other rooms on it, nine folding chairs, nine people, a laminated sheet on a post by the door carrying nine words with no name, no date, no company and no version, identical to a sheet seen in about nine other places in this city and not once explained by anybody. The instructor is a woman of thirty-six with a company lanyard and her own card on the table, nine years in the job. A man of fifty-one with a bad wrist attended and is not on the list.**

*What the practice is and what the card says.* **A load-hold with a boundary-release at the end, and the boundary-release is the beat that confirms the agreed scope, refuses further sharing and hands the choice to act back to the practitioner. The beat is printed on all nine cards in a box, in the same typeface as the printed box about what the practice must not touch, and the wording is correct. The instructor ran it through four times in about twenty minutes, ending each run with one spoken word, and the word is not *finished*, not *release*, and is not any word on the card. Asked in a corridor what the word is for, she answered: to confirm the release, and that the card is what somebody wrote down while the room is what people do, and that in about four years somebody will be sitting in a chair waiting, and she is the person they wait for, and she would like to be able to say why that is a good idea. Asked whether he thought it was a good idea, she said she thought it very good at not going wrong and that this is not the same, and that she is thirty-six and has done it for nine years.**

*Eleven seconds, and what happened in them.* **A woman of about twenty-two in the front row completed the hold, reached the release, stopped, consulted a card that carries the beat, and waited. Two counts of about eleven seconds were taken, one by the man of twenty-two and one by a person at the back. The instructor then said the word and the practitioner finished and the room restarted. The man of twenty-two was in the fourth row against the wall, had the word available, could have produced it at any point in those eleven seconds, and did not. Nobody in the room or the corridor remarked on it and nobody thanked him for it and he was not thanked and is owed nothing.**

*What a man of fifty-one said on the way out.* **Unprompted and to nobody: that the practitioner will go home and practise and will wait, and will do so about four times, and the fourth will be in a stairwell alone with nobody there, and that it will then work without her noticing; and that this is simultaneously the good outcome and the bad outcome. He added that he has written a version of this at home for a company and has been told it is not a field; that the field is blank and the upstream licensor is not named; that he has been trying for about two years to find out who they were; and that he has stopped asking and has not stopped noticing.**

*What the man of twenty-two said on a service road, twice, standing up, to nobody.* **That he is the man in the lanyard. That about eleven a week are, seven on the rota and four not on it, and that this is a figure about him and not about another person. That in about nine minutes in a room in Crown Terrace he was the only other person in that building who could have made the practitioner finish, and did not, and that the reason is that he does not know what happens to a person who finishes it alone and does not know she has done something she should not have been able to do. He said it twice, to check it. He is not thanked and nobody is angry with him and nobody knew to be.**

*Boundaries.* **No teaching, demonstration, assessment or assistance by him: he confirmed scope, which is the whole of what a grade three Friday is, and he did not say the word. A room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception, and the condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased in this chapter. No relay, no staging, no comparison. No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet or constitution was created. Eight items in a ring binder in another district, unread and unchanged. Nine keys needed and two existing, count not stated. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. No message has arrived from anybody. Entry 257.**
