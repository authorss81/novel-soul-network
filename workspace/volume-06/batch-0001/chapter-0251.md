# Chapter 251 — Folded Once

The eighth thing in a ring binder went in on the Monday morning of week eighty-nine without anybody asking for it, and the room was on its forty-second day with the heating off, and about nine people watched a woman of about thirty walk to a shelf and open a binder and put one folded page into it and close it again, and about four of them wanted very much to know what was written on it and none of them found out, and he wanted to know more than any of them and did not read it.

---

Ten on the docket and nine in date, and the tenth is a stair fire door in Nine Bridges that has come up seven times in about a month with a stamped number in the description box, and he has adjusted that closer four times and gone to the frame twice and found it as he has described it every time, and there is no fault in it that he can put in a box.

The word was at nine and ten all morning and it has not been told to anybody in six volumes.

The room was a hundred and forty-seven days old. It was cold. The gas ring was on because a cook of twenty-seven had put it on at about half past eight and had put it on for about fourteen weeks before that at the same time on the same mornings and had told nobody either time.

---

There were about eleven people in and out between half past nine and half past two, and three of the four who used to come in at that hour for the temperature had not been in the room since the Monday the supply went off, and no person in that room said that out loud, and there is no instrument in there by which it could be established.

A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip was in at about eleven and read the board over the door, which carried four words in chalk in a woman's ordinary hand and above them a grey ghost of four that has been there since the Friday of week eighty-seven and cannot be got off, and he offered no comment on either, and he was in for about an hour and a half and did not have a cup of tea, and nobody noticed that either.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in at about one and said the number on the door of a building he was in on Monday, and said the number on the door of the building next to it, and said that both of those numbers are on a list that is not the list they should be on and that he has said so in writing twice and has been thanked once for it by a woman he does not work for, and that the thanking is worse than the not doing.

---

At about half past two a woman of about thirty stood up.

She has been in that room on every working day since the Friday of week sixty-nine. She was in the chair nearest the door and her bag was on her lap and it was in her lap for most of the morning and then it was on the counter and then it was in her hand.

She took a folded page out of it. It was folded once, across, and it was the size of half a sheet of typing paper and it had been folded and unfolded and folded again because the corners had gone soft.

She walked to the shelf. She took the ring binder off it. She opened it — and about four people in that room could see, from where they were sitting, that there were things in it, and had been able to for about three weeks — and she put the page in at the back, and she shut it, and she put it back on the shelf, and she sat down.

She said one sentence and it was not said to the room.

"**That is mine, and I have not shown it to anybody, and I am not going to, and it is going in there, and you can all carry on.**"

Nobody asked her a question. It is the third thing she has done in that room in nineteen weeks and the other two were sentences and this one is a sentence and the difference is that two of the three were said to people.

---

He wanted to read it.

He did not say that he wanted to read it. He stood at the counter for about four seconds with his hand about eleven inches from the shelf, and a first-year of nineteen, who had been sitting on the end chair with her boots off, said:

"**Reading it makes you the person who read it.**"

That is nine words. He did not argue with it and he did not improve on it and he has not improved on it since.

"**Manners are not why,**" she said, without looking up. "**It is that four of us were watching you and if you do it then you are a person who reads other people's pages, and that is a thing you can do again.**"

"**I know.**"

"**I know you know. I have watched you know it twice and then not do it and I am not going to keep saying it.**"

He put his hands in his pockets, which he does not do, and said: "**What do I do with it.**"

"**Nothing. It is the eighth thing. It is in a ring binder on a shelf. It has a page in it that somebody wrote and nobody read out and that is a thing that exists in this city now.**"

---

It holds eight things, and it cannot be emptied, and nobody who wrote in it can be found in it, and a man of about thirty-one who had never been in the room read the whole of it standing up for about nine minutes a fortnight ago and put it back on the shelf and said one sentence on his way out that four people heard and nobody wrote down.

**Anybody may take it off that shelf and read it. If you are the person who wrote one of the things in it you may take that page out, and nobody will stop you, and nobody will be told.**

Nobody has taken a page out. There are eight things in it and it was seven on Friday.

---

At about a quarter to four a cook of twenty-seven came out of the back and washed a mug that did not need washing.

Somebody said, not unkindly, that it is the heat, and that there is a form for the heat, and that a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket said so in this room in the first week of the fortnight and was right that there is.

"**I have asked three times,**" she said, to the sink. "**Three. I am not going to ask a fourth and I want that written on something if anybody in this city ever writes anything down.**"

Nobody wrote anything down.

"**That is not a refusal either. I want that on the same thing if anybody writes it. It is not a refusal, it is a Tuesday I am not going to spend again.**"

Then she said: "**And that is the fourth time I have said a thing of that shape and I have said I would not say it a fifth, and I have just counted, so one of the two of us is wrong and I would rather it was me.**"

A man of thirty-four who works nights was in at about five, on the standing Tuesday at about ten that she had offered him in the first week of the cold and had refused thanks for in advance. He had a sheet of about nine addresses in his coat and he did not take it out, and he said he was not going to talk about the door list again, and that the last time he talked about the door list he had talked about it for about four minutes and had come away with the sense that he had done something, and that he had not.

"**You have not,**" said somebody.

"**No. I have not. I am saying it once so that the rest of you can stop waiting for me to.**"

The shutter came down at about ten and the room was cold and the board over the door carried four words in chalk and one grey ghost.

He wrote one thing, standing up, on a service road, at about ten, with the shutter cold behind him.

*254. Monday of week 89, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one folded page, one number, and forty-two days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Ten on the docket and nine in date. The tenth is a stair fire door in Nine Bridges, the seventh report of the same fault in about a month, and it has not been attended today; the description box carries a stamped number that is not the room number, the room has never had its own number on the door, and the closer has been reset four times and the frame checked twice and found as described each time. The rate held at nine and ten across the whole day and has not been spoken in front of another person in six volumes. The heating in that room is on its forty-second day off and unpaid and is not resolvable by any document and none is proposed. The room is a hundred and forty-seven days old, counted from the Monday it opened. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and fifty-one days in the rail, which is a third object and is not in this room and nobody here could produce it. The city's weekly figure stands unaltered, is not a rate, and is on the back of a woman's own card.**

*The page, and the shape of the room.* **At about half past two a woman of about thirty, who has attended on every working day since the Friday of week sixty-nine and who is asked nothing by anybody, took a folded page from a bag on her lap, carried it to the shelf, opened the ring binder, placed it at the back, closed the binder and replaced it on the shelf. Nine words and no more were said by her, addressed to the room and not to any person in it. No question was asked of her by anybody, no name was used, no age or district or occupation was stated, and no person in that room described her afterwards except by a count. She has now done three things in that room in nineteen weeks, of which two were sentences said to people and this one is a sentence said to a shelf.**

*The ninth words, and what they were not.* **A first-year of nineteen said, from the end chair with her boots off, that reading the page would make him the person who read it — nine words, counted, not improved on by anybody. She then gave the reason unprompted, and the reason is not manners: four people were watching him, and his doing it would make him a person who reads other people's pages, and that is repeatable. He asked what he is to do with it and was told: nothing. The standing answer about who may be asked about the binder was not given, was not asked for, and carries no number, and the binder did not acquire a rule. Nothing was written down.**

*What the binder now holds.* **Eight items, in eight hands or claimed as eight hands, in a ring binder on a shelf. It is not a book, not a system, not a register, and cannot be emptied. Any person may take it off the shelf and read it standing up. Any person who wrote one of the eight may take that page out and nobody will stop them and nobody will be told. No page has been taken out. The seventh item went in in an earlier movement; the eighth is the folded page, and it is unread by nine people including the man who wrote this entry.**

*Two correct actions and a refusal, none of which changed anything.* **A cook of twenty-seven washed a mug that did not need washing, stated that she has asked about the heating three times and will not ask a fourth time, that this is not a refusal, and that it is a Tuesday she is not going to spend again; and then counted aloud that this is the fourth time she has made a statement of this shape in this manuscript's own hearing, against a promise she made not to make a fifth, and said she would rather she was the one wrong. No document, fund, collection, subscription, list, notice or appeal exists and none was proposed by anybody. A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip attended for about an hour and a half, declined tea, read the board and the ghost above it, and offered no comment on either. A man of thirty-four who works nights, on a standing Tuesday offered in the first week of the cold with thanks refused in advance, declined to produce a sheet of about nine addresses and said once that he was not going to talk about the door list again, on the stated ground that the previous occasion of about four minutes had left him with a sense of having done something he had not done.**

*State and boundaries.* **Four words in chalk and one unremovable grey ghost on the board over a door. Thirteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, the first twelve of them sixty-seven days old and the thirteenth eighteen. Eight items in a ring binder, one of them added this morning by a person who was not asked and who has not been thanked. Nine chairs. The counter bare. Nine keys are needed and two exist and the count was not said aloud in this room today and was not needed. No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet or constitution was created. No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by anybody. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, no message has arrived from anybody, and the master is in the fourth pocket of a tool roll in another building with its fifth line blank. The standing question is untouched, is unnumbered, and is about a different object than it was a fortnight ago. Entry 254.**
