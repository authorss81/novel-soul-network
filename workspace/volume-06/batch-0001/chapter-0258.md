# Chapter 258 — Nine Seconds, And Then A Letter

A company telephoned the number on the plate on the Monday morning of week ninety-one, asked for about forty minutes, and said it would bring two people, and a first-year of nineteen said no in about nine seconds, and the whole of the cost of the no fell on the one person in that room who can be named to a stranger, and he wrote it out in a hand of his own at the counter and put it in an envelope and gave it away, and nobody in that room thanked anybody.

---

Ten on the docket and nine in date, and the standing water in that basement was reported for the eighth time in about four weeks and had come down to about nine inches without anybody having done anything to it, which he wrote down and attributed to nothing.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its fifty-sixth day and the gas ring was on and it is a hundred and sixty-one days old.

---

The call came in at about half past nine to a number on a plate on the outside of a shuttered unit, and the number belongs to a letting agent and not to anybody in that room, and the person who answered it said *hold on* and put it through, and that is how a plate on the outside of that unit has been working all term, and nobody in that building chose it.

The woman gave her name and her company before she gave the reason, which is a thing a person does when the reason is going to sound bad.

She was about forty-three and she had a company lanyard on, of a kind that the room had seen before on about nine different people over a term and a half. She asked for about forty minutes and then said she would bring two people and not four, and she said the reason before anybody asked her for it, which is not a thing anybody had done in that room for about four months and which about four people had got used to.

She said: "**My company supplies about eleven of those sites. On Wednesday somebody in a district office pulled about nine hundred of our cards out of use in about four days on the strength of a piece of paper on a wall, and it was the correct thing to do and I am not here about that. I am here because on Friday my employer asked me whether there is anything in that room that is a problem, and I have to write an answer, and I cannot write an answer about a thing I have not been in.**"

"That is a reasonable request."

"**It is entirely reasonable and I would make it. That is why I am nervous.**"

---

She came at about half past two on the Monday with one other person, who is not named, because she was in that room for about eleven minutes and asked one question, and what the question was is nobody else's business and certainly not the room's.

The woman of forty-three stood in the middle of the floor, in the gap between the counter and the hardboard, where about four people a week stand because there are nine chairs and about eleven people, and she looked at the board over the door for about nine seconds, at the four words in chalk and the grey ghost of four above them that cannot be got off, and she did not ask what either of them said.

She said, unprompted: "**I am not going to pretend I came to look at those. I have a photograph of that counter on a piece of paper in a bag in a car and I have looked at it more than I have looked at my own, and I know that is a filthy thing to say to a room.**"

A cook of twenty-seven said: "**It is a filthy thing to say and it is the first true thing anybody has said at that door in about four months, so go on.**"

---

A first-year of nineteen said no.

It took about nine seconds and she gave the ground, and the ground is not manners and was never manners.

"**No. And the reason is that a room which lets a company in is a room that gets quoted, and a room that gets quoted is a room that gets asked about, and then in about four years somebody is going to stand in that doorway and say the room agreed to something, and there is no way in this city to make them take it back out of the sentence.**"

"You are saying a company is a problem."

"**I am saying a company is a company and it is not a problem and it did nothing wrong, and that is exactly why it cannot be in here. If a thing in here is a problem then the people in here are the problem, and if a thing in here is not a problem then the company has no business in here and it has lost an afternoon. Both of those are the same answer and it took me nine seconds because I have had about a term to think about it.**"

The woman of forty-three said: "**That is the cleanest refusal anybody has given me and I want to say that I do not think it is right.**"

"**Then you are in the right building and I would like to know the rest.**"

---

Then nobody could work out who was going to answer the letter, and it took about four minutes, and those four minutes are the whole of what happened that afternoon.

She put her card on the counter and said that she would take a written answer away with her, and that she would not be able to tell her employer that a room had thought about it and said no if there were nothing to take with her.

Then there was about nine seconds of nobody speaking.

There is no name over the door. There is no committee, no minute, no officer and no post, and every one of those facts has been true for a term and a half and none of them is a complaint. A refusal that is written down is not a refusal, it is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper has to have a person at the bottom of it, and in that room there were about eleven people and one of them had a name that anybody outside that building could use.

A cook of twenty-seven said that it was not going to be her.
A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts said that it was not going to be him, in four words, and did not give a reason, and was not asked for one.
A first-year of nineteen said the position, which is hers and which she gave in this room on the Monday of last week before anybody had said they wanted a thing written down: whoever writes it, it is theirs, and that person is findable, and the room will neither stop them nor help them.

She said: "**I am not writing it. I have got my name on about eleven hundred pieces of paper and I am the one person in here who knows exactly what that does to a person, and I am not doing it to somebody else to save myself an argument.**"

---

So he wrote it.

She left the sheet of her company's notepaper on the counter, and it was her company's, and it had her company's mark in the corner, and he wrote about four sentences on it at the counter standing up, and it took about four minutes, and he signed it at the bottom, and it is the only thing in that building that carries his name and that he wrote.

Then he read it back to the room before it went in the envelope, which nobody had asked him to do and which he did because a thing you are going to be asked about for four years ought to have been said in front of the people it is about.

"**Read it,**" said the cook of twenty-seven.

"**It says no, it says thank you for asking, it says we are not able to say why, and it says that if your company wants to be useful in any of this then it can go and find the about nine people in those sites who are not on anybody's rota and put somebody in with them. That is the whole of it.**"

"**Then that is a good letter and it is the worst thing that has happened in this room in about four months, and both of those are about the signature.**"

---

The woman of forty-three took it and put it in a bag with her own card, and then asked whether there was anything she could be given instead, and was given four figures, out loud, once, in about nine minutes, by Talia Venn standing up with her hands behind her back: about nine people on no rota across four of the eleven sites, two of whom have never been asked whether they want to be there, about nine hundred doors in four buildings carrying a number off a different line of the same list, and about nine hundred cards that came out of use in about four days.

The woman of forty-three wrote the four figures on the back of her company's own card, in her own hand, and said she was not going to give the room a copy, because the room does not keep things and she understood that about four minutes after she came in.

She said: "**This does not help me. What I have to write is that I have seen it.**"

"**No. It does not.**"

"**Then I am going to write it, and I am going to say the room was right, and I am going to be asked in about four months why I went, and I am going to have to say that because it was correct, and I am not going to be thanked for that by anybody, and I would like it recorded that I chose to write it myself instead of letting the man above me write it about me, and that is the whole of my part.**"

Nobody thanked her. She is owed nothing and she is not a villain and her company sends nothing further this term.

---

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wet, at about ten, and he said most of it out loud first, standing up, to nobody, which he has done twice this term.

*261. Monday of week 91, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one refusal, four minutes at a counter, and fifty-six days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Ten on the docket and nine in date. The basement in an unnamed district stands at about nine inches, down from about sixteen at the third attendance, reported for the eighth time in about four weeks; no stopcock, valve, inlet or person has been fitted by anybody, and the entry records the fall and attributes it to nothing. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on its fifty-sixth day off, unpaid. The room is a hundred and sixty-one days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and sixty-five days in the rail and is a third object and is not in that room. The twelve lines on the hardboard are eighty-one days old and the thirteenth is thirty-two. Eight items in the ring binder, unread and unchanged.**

*The call, and who made it.* **Incoming to a number on a plate on the outside of a shuttered unit, which belongs to a letting agent and to nobody in that room, at about half past nine on the Monday; the agent answered, said *hold on*, and put it through, which is the seventh such arrival in about a term. A woman of about forty-three, with a company lanyard and the company named before the reason, asked for about forty minutes and reduced it to about twenty-two and one other person. Her stated reason: her company supplies about eleven of the sites in question; on Wednesday about nine hundred of that company's cards came out of use on the authority of a printed standard invoked by a clerk of forty-four who is owed nothing; the withdrawal was correct; and her employer has asked her whether the room is a problem, and she cannot answer that about a room she has not been in. She stated before being asked that she considered the request entirely reasonable and that she was nervous on account of its reasonableness. She said in the room, unprompted, that she has looked at a photograph of that counter in a bag in a car more often than she has looked at her own, and called that a filthy thing to say to a room, and was told by a cook of twenty-seven that it was the first true thing anybody had said at that door in about four months.**

*The nine seconds, and the ground.* **A first-year of nineteen refused the visit. The ground given was that a room which admits a company becomes a room that can be quoted, that a room which can be quoted becomes a room that is asked about, and that in about four years a person would stand in that doorway and say the room agreed to something, with no mechanism in this city for taking it back out of a sentence. She stated that the refusal cost about nine seconds because she has had about a term to think about it, and she stated that the company is not the problem and did nothing wrong, which is the reason it cannot be admitted. The visitor said she thought the refusal was correct and did not think it was right, and was told to say the rest, and said the rest. The visitor was not thanked and was not argued with.**

*Four minutes at a counter, which is the whole of the cost.* **The visitor said she would take a written answer away and could not report a refusal she had not in her hand. There is no name over the door, no committee, no minute, no officer and no post, and a written refusal requires a name at the bottom of it. About eleven people were present. A cook of twenty-seven declined in one sentence. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts declined in four words and gave no reason and was not asked for one. A first-year of nineteen stated the standing position, that whoever writes it owns it and is findable and will be neither stopped nor helped, and then declined to write it, giving as her reason that she is the one person in that room who knows precisely what her own handwriting on about eleven hundred pieces of paper does to a person and will not do it to somebody else to save herself an argument.**

**The man of twenty-two wrote about four sentences on the company's own notepaper, standing up at the counter, in about four minutes, and signed it, and read it aloud to the room before it was sealed, which nobody requested. The refusal is the only object in that building bearing his name that he wrote, and it is now in a company in another district, and it cannot be taken back because he wrote it, and the first line on that hardboard, that the room will not keep anything it cannot take back, does not apply to it and he did not claim that it does.**

*What the visitor was given, and what it was worth.* **Four figures, out loud, once, in about nine minutes, by Talia Venn, twenty-four, standing with her hands behind her back: about nine people on no rota across four of the eleven sites, two of whom have never been asked whether they want to be there; about nine hundred doors in four buildings carrying a number off a different line of the same company's list; about nine hundred cards withdrawn in about four days; and the standing weekly figure, which is not a rate and is on no form. The visitor wrote the four figures on the back of her own company's card in her own hand, declined to leave a copy with the room, and said she understood within about four minutes that the room does not keep things. She then stated that none of it helps her, that what she must write is that she has seen the room, and named the price of that herself: a file note recording that the approach was declined, which within her own employer is a thing she can be found about, and a stated preference for writing it herself rather than have it written about her by the person above her. She was thanked by nobody and is owed nothing and is not a villain, and her company sends nothing further this term.**

*State and boundaries.* **The only object made by anybody in this case this week is a reply on somebody else's notepaper, which is a correspondence and not a page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution, and which went out of the building in a stranger's bag. The ring binder did not take it, holds eight items, has not been opened and has had no page read or removed. Four words in chalk and one unremovable grey ghost over a door; thirteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, the first a cook's and still the first. Nine chairs, a bare counter, a kettle, a plug socket that works and a bell-push that does not ring. A woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door, described by a count and not by a name, an age or a district, in the room since about two, not asked anything, and about four people wanted to.**

**No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by him, and the reason he may put his name to a piece of paper is that it is paper and not a practice. No panel and no marker. Nine keys needed and two existing, count not stated; the answer to the question on the back of a docket was given aloud in this room three weeks ago and is not repeated here and is not written down anywhere. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, and no message has arrived from anybody. Entry 261.**
