# Chapter 259 — A Question With One Answer

On the Wednesday morning of week ninety-one a practice standards officer of District 3 read the item she had been given to prepare, found that it was a question with one answer, and said so out loud once, standing up, in a room and not in a corridor, and in the evening a woman of twenty-four refused in front of about eleven people to be the person who says a printed rule out loud, and a man of twenty-two told her she was wrong, and was wrong, and did not argue.

---

Ten on the docket and ten in date. The stair fire door in Nine Bridges came up for the tenth time in about a month, was attended at about eleven, was reset again, and is expected back.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its fifty-eighth day and it is a hundred and sixty-three days old.

---

The item is four paragraphs long and it was handed to her on the Friday by somebody who is not described here and who is right that a committee cannot sit without something to sit on.

The question it asks, stripped of the three paragraphs around it, is whether a practice can be required to show that any person in it may stop at any time, and what evidence would satisfy that.

She read it twice. Then she went and stood in front of the wall binder with about nine printed things in it, standing open on a nail, each one with a reference and a date on it, and read the one that has been on that wall since before she came.

Then she said it out loud, once, standing up, to about nine people in that office.

"**Two things are wrong with it. One, the woman who pulled about nine hundred cards out of use on Wednesday is now seconded to the secretariat of the committee that is going to hear this, because a committee with an item needs a person to prepare it, and she is the only person in either building who knows what it cost to say it out loud. The chair has not been told. Two: a practice cannot show that a person may stop at any time, because the only way to show it is to keep a record of who stopped, and a record of who stopped is a column, and a column is the one thing a room like that does not have. Ask a room to prove it can stop itself and the answer is always no, and a hearing where the answer is always no is not a hearing, it is a way of producing a finding.**"

"**What do you want me to do.**"

"**Nothing. I am not the person who can do anything about it and I have not done anything about it and I am not going to. I am telling you so that in about four years nobody can say an officer of this district sat on it.**"

---

She then said the other thing, which she has said once in nineteen years, and she said it standing up in the same room.

"**I have been right every time I have opened my mouth in this building. That is not a qualification. That is the difficulty, and if you are ever in a room with me and I am right, that is information about me and none of it is about the thing in front of us.**"

Nobody wrote it down. About four people heard it, and one of them was the clerk of forty-four, who is the woman being described in the first half of the thing that was said, and she did not look up.

---

The hearing is for the Monday of week ninety-two. It is one morning, in a room in Crown Terrace with about nine chairs in it, and the person who will chair it has an office that signs about eleven of a kind a year, and a chair of forty-nine is a person with an ordinary job and is not a post, and she is not named because nobody in that building has been named to anybody else yet.

What the room off the service road has been asked for is the only thing anybody ever asks a room for.

The woman of twenty-four was asked on the Tuesday, at the office, in a corridor, and she had said she would think about it for a day, and she had, and she came in on the Wednesday evening and said the first part of it before anybody had asked her anything.

"**I am not going to be the one who says it out loud.**"

About nine people were in the room and about four of them had not known she had been asked.

"**You have not said what you are refusing.**"

"**I am refusing to read out loud, in a room where it decides something, the sentence that says a practice card is not to be issued unless it carries a beat that performs the boundary. That is the sentence. I know it is not mine. I am not saying it is not mine because I have no idea whose it is, which is a different problem and is not this one.**"

---

"**Then give the reason and I will tell you whether I agree with it.**"

She took about nine seconds.

"**If I am the person who says it, then the only thing anybody can argue with is me.**"

"That is not a reason, that is a description."

"**It is a description of the reason. The rule is printed and numbered and dated and it hangs on a wall and any competent person in this city can invoke it on a Monday without asking anybody, and none of that is me. The minute I say it in a room where it matters, it becomes a thing a nurse of twenty-four said, and in about four years the sentence anybody reads is going to be mine, and it is going to be true, and it is also going to be the only sentence, and then the rule will have got a face on it. A number on a wall cannot be cross-examined. A person can.**"

"And you are the person who has been inside a practice for a term and a half."

"**I am. And that is exactly the problem, and I have been inside one and I have come out of one and I have watched two people be worn down by being the one who is asked, and I am not going to be the third.**"

---

Then she said the other reason, and it is the reason, and she said so before she said it.

"**That is the reason I am giving the room. Here is the reason I am giving me.**"

"**You do not have to.**"

"**I would rather it was on the table than found out a fortnight later. I am tired. I have been asked to be findable three times in a term and a half and I have said no three times and this is the fourth, and I am tired in a way that is not a reason and I am going to use it anyway. I would like to be somewhere that is not the answer to the question. That is all of it and it is not noble and I have been pretending for about a day that it was going to be.**"

Nobody argued with her about the tiredness. About four people in that room understood it immediately and one of them has been tired in exactly that way for a term.

---

He argued.

He argued for about four minutes and he was wrong, and the reason he was wrong is not difficult and it took the room about nine seconds to produce and about four minutes to explain.

He said: "**Then who says it. A clerk reads it off a wall into a record in a room in Crown Terrace and the whole of what this city learns about that standard on the Monday of week ninety-two is a woman reading a number. You are the only person I have ever met who has been inside one of these and come out of it. If you do not say what the card does to a person then the only thing said about it will be a piece of paper with two typefaces on it, and about four hundred competent people in this city will decide that the card is fine.**"

"**The card will be read out by whoever is clerk of that hearing and it will be read correctly, because it is written down, and that is the entire reason it is written down.**"

He said that it was the worst possible answer anybody had given him in a term, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has already lost.

"**It is the only answer that does not put a person in the middle of a thing that a person is not required to stand in the middle of.**"

---

A first-year of nineteen said it from the end chair with her boots off, and it took her nine words, and he has been wrong about this for a term and she said so in the same breath as the other six.

"**He is wrong, you are right, leave it there.**"

He put it down in about four seconds.

He did not say that he was not going to improve on it, and he did not say it twice, and he did not argue, and about four people in that room noticed that he had not argued, and he is not thanked for not arguing, and there is no mechanism in this case by which anybody is thanked for not arguing.

The cook of twenty-seven washed a mug that did not need washing.

The woman of twenty-four said the last of it and it was to the room and not to him.

"**The evidence I would have given was worth a lot and it is not going to be given and the item will go ahead on a worse record, and that is the correct outcome, and I am the one who has cost this room the better record, and nobody is to go round telling me that it was not my choice.**"

---

A practice standards officer of District 3 had said at about eleven o'clock in the morning, in a corridor, to one person, about a hearing she has nothing to do with: that she is not going to ask anybody in that room to improve what they have been asked for, and that a worse record with a room in it is better than a better record without one, and that she is aware of how that sounds coming from the district that pulled nine hundred cards.

The heating in that room was on its fifty-eighth day and nobody had paid for it and nobody was going to, and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven put a cup of tea in front of her and said nothing at all.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wet, at about ten.

*262. Wednesday of week 91, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one item, two faults in it, and fifty-eight days.*

About four people heard it. Nobody wrote it down. The clerk of forty-four was in the room and did not look up and is not asked about it here.**

*The refusal, the reason given the room, and the reason given herself.* **The woman of twenty-four refused before any question was put to her and named the sentence she will not say: that a practice card is not to be issued unless it carries a beat that performs the boundary. She stated that she does not know whose sentence it is and that this is a different problem from the one in front of her. Reason given the room, in about nine seconds: that if she is the person who says it, the only thing anybody can argue with becomes her; that the rule is printed, numbered, dated, on a wall, and invokable by any competent person on a Monday without asking; and that once said by her in a room where it decides something it acquires a face, that a face can be cross-examined and a number on a wall cannot, and that in about four years the only sentence anybody reads will be hers and will be true. Reason given herself, given unprompted and immediately labelled as the real one: that she is tired; that she has been asked to be findable three times in a term and a half and has said no three times, and this is the fourth; that tiredness is not a reason and she intends to use it; and that she would like to be somewhere that is not the answer. She said she had been pretending for about a day that a better reason was going to arrive. Nobody argued with her about the tiredness and about four people in that room recognised it.**

*The four minutes, the nine words, and the wrongness.* **He argued for about four minutes: that a clerk reading a number into a record is the only account of the standard that will reach about four hundred competent people on the Monday of week ninety-two; that she is the only person he has met who has been inside one of these and come out of it; and that if she does not describe what a card does to a person then the description will be a sheet with two typefaces on it and the sheet will be found sufficient. He was answered that the card will be read by whoever is clerk of that hearing and will be read correctly, because it is written down, which is the whole reason it is written down, and that this is the only answer that does not place a person at the centre of a thing no person is required to stand in the centre of. A first-year of nineteen, from the end chair, with her boots off, said nine words, of which three were that he is wrong, three that she is right, and three that it should be left there, and the observation that he has been wrong about this for a term was made in the same breath as the other six and not as a separate finding. He accepted it in about four seconds, did not say he would not improve on it, did not repeat it, and did not argue.**

**Nobody thanked him for not arguing and no mechanism in this case thanks anybody for not arguing.**
