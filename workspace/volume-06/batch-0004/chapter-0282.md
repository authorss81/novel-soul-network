# Chapter 282 — She Is Still Seconded

On the Thursday of week ninety-eight, in a building in Crown Terrace, a clerk of forty-four told a man of twenty-two what is written down about a morning he was in, and told him that the only thing keeping a fault on that record is that nobody in the building knows she is in it, and at a lift he worked out what her secondment has made her and did not say it and was not asked, and in the same evening a woman of thirty-four found a sign gone and did not put it back up and told nobody, and neither of them changed anything and neither of them was thanked.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date, which is the first time in about a fortnight that the two have been level, and four jobs attended and none of them reported twice.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its hundred and eighth day and it is two hundred and thirteen days old.

---

He was in that building for a job that took about four minutes and she was coming out of a room on the floor below it at about half past four with a folder under her arm.

She had not been told he was going to be in the building. She has not been told a great many things and the reason she has not been told them is that she is the one who would have to say them.

"**I have got about eleven minutes before somebody comes down that passage and then I cannot say any of this.**"

"**Go on.**"

---

What is written down, she said, is four things.

The hearing is recorded as having taken place. The chair's reason for not deciding is recorded. The nine sentences are recorded, in her own voice, as hers, because she asked for that and was given it in about four seconds. And at the foot of it there is a line that says the item was noted, with a date, and nothing else.

"**That is the whole of what is on it. There is no page of that anywhere you could get at, and I could not get at it if I tried, and I would not take it out of that room in my coat.**"

"**And there is no name of mine on it.**"

"**No.**"

"**I was in that room for about nine minutes and I answered two questions.**"

"**The chair does not take names. She has never taken a name off a thing in nineteen years and I have watched her refuse one.**"

---

He stood with his back to a wall in a passage in a building in Crown Terrace and had about nine seconds to do the arithmetic in and it took him about four.

He has been findable at a door since the spring before the one before last, and about four hundred people in this case can find him, and in about four years somebody is going to add all of it up and write the sum down. He has said that to himself in about nine different rooms.

A record in a secretariat is the one surface in this city he cannot get his name off, and it is the one surface in this city he has never wanted his name on.

"**That is the best thing anybody has done for me in about four years and it was an accident and it was not about you.**"

"**I know it was not about me. That is why I said it.**"

Nobody thanked her. She is owed nothing by anybody in this case and she said so out loud, in the passage, in about nine words, and then she said the other half of it, which is that a room cannot ask to be heard again and a person has to, and that she has said that in a different building and it did not help and she is saying it again anyway.

---

"**Say the part about the fault.**"

"**The second fault is on it. It went on on a Friday in the week before last and it has not moved and nothing has come off it, and the reason it has not moved is that nobody in this building has been told that I am in this room.**"

"**You checked.**"

"**I checked this morning. That is the third time. First week, last week, and this morning.**"

"**And the chair.**"

"**The chair asked me nine questions yesterday in the passage outside that room and about nine of them were about the item and not one of them was about where I am, and I have decided that is not an accident, and I am not going to decide what it is instead.**"

Nobody in that passage said anything for about nine seconds, and the passage was not empty, and a person went past both of them and neither of them stopped talking.

---

The lift is at the end of it and it is a small one and it takes four people.

He noticed the thing at the lift and did not raise it and was not asked about it, and the reason he did not raise it is not manners and is written down further on.

What he noticed is this. He has seen her go down in that building about four times and go up about none. The secondment is a route and not a room. It is four days a week and about a term. She did not apply for it, she cannot end it, and while it lasts she is findable at a building on four days a week, and the only reason anybody can find her there is a set of stairs and a door that somebody else unlocked, and she has known that since the first week and checked it three times, and she is the one person in this case who has made herself findable on purpose in order to do a thing correctly, and there is no version of that in which it is a favour to her.

He did not say it. He was not asked. He wrote it down at about ten that night on a service road, and the reason he wrote it down is that a finding is only a finding when it is written down, and that this one is about a person who is owed nothing and it is going to sit on him for about four years.

---

At about half past five the same evening a woman of thirty-four stood on a landing in Nine Bridges at the top of about four flights and looked at a door she has been past about nine times this year and found that the sign was off it.

There is nothing on the glass. There is a mark where a fixing was and the paint around it has not gone back, and the door is a door.

She did not put anything back. She did not knock, and she did not go down and ask anybody on that landing, and she did not write it down anywhere, and she did not telephone the person who asked her to look.

She stood on that landing for about four minutes and then she went down.

What she said to nobody, on the stairs, is the whole of what she said that evening and it is in this entry because nobody else has it.

"**Somebody can be asked to do that and somebody did it, and I am not going to find out who, and if I did find out who then in about four years that person would have a person coming to their door about a sign, and I have watched what that does to people and I am not going to be the one who starts it.**"

Nobody thanked her. She is owed nothing and she was not thanked and nobody in this case knows she went up those stairs.

---

At about half past six in a cold room off a service road there were about nine people and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door with a bag on her lap and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven put the ring on at about half past eight and said nothing to anybody, which is the seventh time in about a fortnight.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.

*285. Thursday of week 98, at about ten on a service road in the wind, and this entry is one passage, one lift, four flights, and a hundred and eight days.*

He wrote it down at about ten and it is not in anybody's mouth.**

*A door with nothing on it, and a landing.* **A woman of thirty-four, who has checked about nine hundred doors in four buildings on her own time, unpaid, for about four years, went up about four flights at about half past five on the Thursday to a door she has been past about nine times this year, and the sign was off it. It changed nothing and it is not to be referred to in any room.**
