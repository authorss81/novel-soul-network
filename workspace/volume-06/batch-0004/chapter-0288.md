# Chapter 288 — He Asked For The Last One

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred was the fourteenth sitting of that Exchange, four weeks to the day after the thirteenth, and the book did not open, and the reason is the holder's and is not a new one and is nineteen years old, and the telephone count moved from forty-nine to fifty-two and was announced for the nineteenth time, fourteen of those being the fourteen Wednesdays this book holds and five predating it on a sheet not shown to anybody, and the ninth chair was against the wall and was not moved back.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Four jobs attended, and the stair fire door in Nine Bridges was not among them and has not been chased.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in the room off the service road is on and it is two hundred and twenty-six days old.

---

The room is above the line in a building in Saltmarket and the holder of the room says so at the start of every sitting, and the shutters are up, and the light in it is the flat light of a room that has been on the same side of a street since before anybody in it was working.

Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in. The ninth stands against the wall facing away from the room and has been where it is since the holder of it was fifteen and nobody has asked her about it in nineteen years and nobody is going to.

There is a table, a tin that has never had a lock on it, and a book with four columns and forty-seven lines in it. The last line went in a hundred and twenty-six days ago and has not been read since.

---

The man of about forty-four has come four times now. He came once and asked for something. He came twice and asked where a reason had come from and was refused before he was told what he had asked. He came three times and said nothing at all.

On the fourth he asked for the last line to be read out.

He said it plainly, in about nine words, and he had it ready, and it was not a good question and it was a fair one.

"**I have been four times and I have never heard what is in it. I do not want to know who said it. I want to know that somebody said it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**No.**"

"**You have not said what I asked.**"

"**I have said what you asked. You have asked me to read a line out to nine people and the answer is no, and I am not going to give you a reason that you can use, and I am not going to give you a reason that I have not got.**"

---

Then she gave the reason, to the room and not to him, and it took about four minutes, and about nine people in that room had never heard any part of it, and nobody thanked her and nobody asked a second question and nobody asked which line.

That a line goes into that book when a person says it to her face, and that this is the only thing that book has ever held and the only thing it is for.

That reading a line out to a room is the other thing, and that in nineteen years she has let about nine of them be heard in that room, and that every one of the nine has come back out of that room wrong.

That the ninth one came back out of it in a building in Crown Terrace, by a person doing their job, in about four months, in a sentence that had four words in it that were not the four words.

And that she has never been told which line it was and has not asked and is not going to.

Somebody said, after a while, that it might have been nothing.

"**It was nothing and that is the entire finding. A line came out of this room and went into a room where a person was asking a question, and what arrived there was four words that were not the four words, and there were about nine people in that room and not one of them has ever been in this building.**"

The young man of twenty-one, who has been coming since he was seventeen and who keeps a count he has never given her, said one sentence.

"**Then somebody was in that room from this room, and I do not know who, and I am not going to say a name in here, and I have never said one in nineteen years and I am not starting on a Wednesday.**"

Nobody asked him. She gave the answer about furniture, which is a chair is a chair, and it was against the wall at the end of the morning and it was not moved back and its mover is not named here and is not to be named by anybody.

---

The count came at about ten past ten, unasked, as it comes, with the arithmetic done in the head about four minutes earlier and refused in the room.

"**Forty-nine to fifty-two. Three in four weeks. The telephone is ahead of the book and it was ahead of the book four weeks ago and it was behind it before that, and I am telling you the direction and not the difference, and I did the difference in my head about four minutes ago and I am not going to do it in front of nine people.**"

"**You have announced that count how many times.**"

"**Nineteen. Fourteen of those are the fourteen Wednesdays this book holds, and five predate it, and there is a sheet with the five on it that I have not shown anybody and that I am not going to.**"

"**And nobody converts anything.**"

"**Nobody in this room converts a call into a line or a line into a sitting or any of the three into any of the others, and I have asked for that about nine times in nineteen years and I have been given it every time and I have never once been given it by anybody who needed it.**"

---

At about half past six in a warm room off a service road there were about nine people and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven put the ring on at about half past eight and said nothing at all.

Talia Venn was in that room for about an hour and said one sentence, and her name went to nobody in it, and the sentence was about a book and not about a person.

"**A book nobody can read is the only kind of book that can hold a person's own words for nineteen years, and in about four years somebody is going to ask her to put it in a system, and the system is going to be the correct thing to do, and she is going to say no, and she is going to be the only person in the room who has understood what the thing is for.**"

Nobody improved on it and nobody asked her a second question and she did not volunteer a second thing and she did not say her name to anybody in that room.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.
