# Chapter 290 — The Seventeenth Line

The Friday of week one hundred was the third Friday rota of this run of days and it ran one part short of two, and a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip was in that room and was not the person who says the word and does not hold a card, and he said so out loud, and a notice came from a landlord nine days after he put the heating on, and there are four names on the lease and four names now cost more, and in the evening a clerk of forty-four said the name of her own building out loud in a room and wrote the seventeenth line on a hardboard on two nails in seventeen words and signed it, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Two jobs attended in a district this case has not been in and neither was the stair fire door in Nine Bridges, which has not been reported since the Monday.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on. The room is two hundred and twenty-eight days old.

---

The instructor was not there. She is a woman of thirty-six with a lanyard and a card on a table in an office nobody in this case has been inside, and she was not there, and there is no rota in this city that says who stands in that room when she is not, because a rota that said that would be a second rota and there is one already and it is the Friday one and about four of the eleven on it are not on it.

A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip was there. He has been in a cold room off a service road on and off for about a term and has said two sentences in it in about a term and one of them was in the spring of this year and it was the correct one and he said he would not say it again.

He did not say anything on the Friday until about twenty past seven.

Then he said, in front of about nine people, that he was not the person who says the word, that he does not hold a card, that he is not on the list, and that he would like it written down that he is not the person who says the word.

"**Then somebody who is should do it.**"

"**I do not know anybody who is. You do not either. That is not a fault in this room and it is a fault in about nine rooms, and I have been in about four of them, and I am not going to be the one who stands up in one and does a thing I have not been asked to do.**"

The second part did not run. About nine people did a hold and about nine people finished and about nine people did a second thing that was not the second thing, and nobody said the word, and the room came apart at about half past seven.

Nobody thanked the man of fifty-eight. Nobody asked him a second question. He went home and has not been in that building since.

---

The notice came at about eleven in the morning and it is a landlord's and it is not ours and no page of it is in this file.

It is about the rent of one unit on one street. It is dated nine days after a man went round four units on a Saturday and opened a valve in the last one, and it is signed by a managing agent and not by the man who did the Saturday.

There are four names on the lease. There have been four names on the lease since the Monday of a week in the spring before last and each of the four said out loud, before signing, what it cost, and nobody has asked any of the four to say it again.

A first-year of nineteen did the sum nobody asked for, out loud, standing up, in about nine words.

"**Four names on a lease is the smallest number of names that can hold a room and it is the largest number that can be got rid of, and a landlord holding four names on a sheet gets four chances a year to find out which of them is still there, and none of the four of us has said one word about that and I have just said it twice.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**No. The rest of it is about four people, and two of them would be in this room, and neither of them has asked me anything.**"

Nobody thanked her. She is owed nothing. She holds no post, is not a deputy, is not a mechanism, is on about eleven hundred leaflets with her own name at the bottom of them by her own choice, and is the only nineteen-year-old in this case.

---

She came in at about half past six on the Friday and there were about nine people in that room and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two and was not asked anything by anybody, including the woman who had just come in.

About four people in that room had been in it for the temperature of a day room next door that has been shut since the Monday before last, and a valve came back on the Saturday before that, and about four of them came back and one of them has been coming since the Friday of week sixty-nine and has not been asked why.

The clerk of forty-four stood up at about half past seven.

She is seconded to a secretariat in a building in Crown Terrace and has been for about a term and a half, and she is not known to be in it by anybody in that building, and the only thing keeping a fault on a record is that, and a secondment cannot be ended by anybody in this case and she has checked it three times.

She said the name of her own building out loud. She said it to about nine people in a room off a service road who had never heard that building mentioned in that room and about four of whom have never been in it.

Then she said the cost, out loud, before the mark, in about eleven seconds, and nobody improved on it.

"**I have been findable at that building for about a term and a half because somebody was sensible in the spring, and in about four years there are going to be about four people who can find me there, and every one of them is going to want something, and I am forty-four and I have a job and I am not going to be one of them.**"

Somebody said that a person with an ordinary job is not a post.

"**No. A person with an ordinary job is not a post and I have known that for about four years and I have never once been asked to be one and I am not going to be one, and the reason nobody has asked me is that I am a clerk and clerks are cheap and everybody knows I am not going to say anything.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody said the reason was good. Nobody asked her whether she was sure, and nobody has asked her about it since.

She took a pen off the counter and wrote it on the hardboard on two nails, which is not the board over the door and is not the wall list in its plastic sleeve in a repair shop in Lattice Ward and is not a sheet of about two hundred and forty lines on a wall in a building in Crown Terrace, and there are five of those and none of them is this.

She wrote it in about nine seconds and signed it, which is the first time a name has gone on that board next to a line in about a term.

*Nobody made me findable. I have an ordinary job in a building you have never been in.*

Seventeen words. The wall is seventeen lines in eleven hands and twelve names. No line has been removed and none has been rubbed off and the first line is a cook's and is still the first line.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the dark, at about ten.
