# Chapter 286 — She Handed It Back

The Friday of week ninety-nine was the second Friday rota of this run of days and it ran in a company training room at the end of a passage in Crown Terrace with about nine people in it and a laminated sheet on a post, and a woman of thirty-one who had come once handed her card back at the end and said she did not want to be on the list, and the woman of thirty-six with the lanyard took it and did not argue and said out loud in front of about nine people that the woman was right to give it back, and nobody thanked the woman of thirty-six and nobody agreed with her and the rota ran.

---

Grade three, twenty to eight, on a card in date, the exception recorded and renewed for the term in a system in a box that says *No reason given by holder*. Eleven on the Friday list for the week and about four of the eleven not on it.

The word was at nine and ten and was not said out loud to anybody. The heating in that room had come back and it is two hundred and twenty-one days old, and the figure of how long it had been off is not an interval any more and is not in this entry.

---

She came at about half past five and it was her second time and not her first, because she had been in that room in the spring before last and had not been since and nobody asked her about that either.

The card she handed back was her own. It had her name on it and a week on it and nothing on the back of it, which is a company thing and not ours and predates this case by about four years.

"**I do not want to be on this.**"

The woman of thirty-six with the lanyard did not ask why, and did not ask her to stay, and did not say anything at all for about four seconds, which is the length of time it takes a competent person to decide that a thing which has been offered is not going to be improved on by a person asking about it.

"**Then give it to me and do not come back, and I am not going to say one word to try to keep you.**"

Somebody said that everybody on the list needed it.

"**Everybody on the list needed it in the spring. I have not asked one of them in four months and I am not going to start by asking her, and if I do that then the asking is the service and in about four years every one of them will have a card because somebody made them feel they ought to.**"

---

Then she said the part, out loud, in front of about nine people, and it is the whole of what she gave that evening.

"**She is right to hand it back and I am not going to stand here and pretend she is not. I have never once asked anybody in this room what they are waiting for and a woman of twenty-three asked it in here in about four weeks and I heard the question and I did not hear the answer, and this is the same thing one week later with a person standing at the door holding a card.**"

Nobody in that room agreed with her. Nobody in that room disagreed with her. Nobody thanked her. A man of about twenty-one near the door said, to the floor, that she had made a person leave.

"**I did not make anybody leave anything. I gave her a lanyard's worth of authority at about half past six and she worked out inside about four minutes what that was worth, and I would rather that happened in this room than at about two in the morning somewhere else.**"

Nobody asked her a second question about it and she did not offer a second thing and she said the word at the end of every run the same as she always has.

---

On the last run the woman of about thirty-four in the second row, four years on that list, reached the end of the hold and stopped and finished, and there was a gap.

About four seconds. Everybody in that room heard it and about four of them noticed and nobody said anything, and about four of them have been not saying something about it for about five Fridays.

He was in the fourth row against the wall with the word available and did not say it. He was not asked to and is not to be, and afterwards he could not have told anybody which of the two Fridays had the gap and which had not from the inside, and he did not ask.

The man of about forty-four was not in that building and is not to be asked a third time.

---

At about half past nine in a room off a service road there were about nine people and the room was warm for the second time in about four months, and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door with a cup of tea in front of her and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and had said nothing to anybody all evening, which is the ninth time in about a fortnight.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard.

That is a fact and it is not a rate and it is on the back of a woman's own card in a flat in Nine Bridges and it is on no form, and it has not been said out loud in that room or in that training room or in this one, and he has not said it to anybody in about four years and is not going to start at about half past nine on a Friday because a woman handed a card back at a door.

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody rang anybody. The room is not going to be rung about a card, and it is not going to be rung about the second week in a row that the word arrived early or late, and there is nobody in that room to whom either of those things could be reported.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the dark, at about ten.
