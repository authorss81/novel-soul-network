# Chapter 287 — She Would Not Stock It

The Monday of week one hundred was the afternoon a man of forty-one came back to four feet of shop in the Quiet Quarter with a product, having said a long time ago that he was owed nothing further, and the product is good and the reasoning behind it is better than the product, and a printer of fifty-eight read both and said no, and gave the half of the no that nobody asked for, and a hundred and forty-one pounds in an envelope under that till did not move, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

Ten on the docket and nine in date, and a lamp ballast in a stairwell in Lattice Ward was attended at about two and was not a fault and the description box says so.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in the room off the service road is on and it is two hundred and twenty-four days old.

---

The shop is four feet wide and has been for about nineteen years, and there is a counter, a press, a till, a sign over the door that the woman who owns the shop paid for out of her own money in her first year, and a queue of about four hundred lines in a folder.

Under the till there is an envelope with a hundred and forty-one pounds in it and no name on it and no address on it. It was on a coat hook in the spring and it is under the till now because the hook was above the damp, and it cannot be sent back, and there is no mechanism in this city that can move it, and that includes the money.

She has looked at it about nine times. She has not opened it and opening it is a decision, and the man who put it there is not in that shop and is not in this entry and has not been in that shop since the spring.

---

He was Vey. He gave his name, which nobody wrote down, and he kept his coat on, and he came at about half past two on the Monday afternoon of a week in which he had already been told nothing at all about that room.

He put nothing on the counter. He took a folded card out of an inside pocket and put it flat, and it is a company's and it is not ours, and it is a card the size of a playing card.

"**A standing monthly arrangement. A card for the inside of a door, with a number on it that somebody in that building answers, and once a month a person of ours comes and rings it and writes down who answered and asks that person whether they knew they were going to be rung.**"

The printer of fifty-eight read it once and turned it over and there was nothing on the back of it.

"**It works.**"

"**It is meant to. It is a good product and I did not come here to argue that it is a good product, because it is, and a shop that says otherwise about a good product is a shop that has stopped selling things.**"

---

Then he said why, and the why took about nine minutes, and the man of twenty-two was in that shop for all of it and is a customer of that counter and has been for about four years and was not asked anything and said nothing.

The reasoning, in his order, and the reader can check every part of it against about four years of this case:

That the whole of what goes wrong in a small building is that a telephone is on a wall and nobody is on the other end of it, and that this is not a technique problem and was never a technique problem, and that a person who is on a rota for a thing they already do is the cheapest version of not failing that exists and costs nothing to invent.

That the rota it would use is not a new one. It is the people already going into that building — the cleaners, the keyholders, the person who locks up — and the product asks for a name next to a number and not for anybody to become anything.

That the beat that ends a practice is a spoken word said by a person whose job is to say it, and that a word spoken by whoever happens to be in the building at the time is not that, and that a company which has printed the beat on about nine hundred cards and been told to take them out of use is not the right seller of a card with a beat on it, and that he is not arguing with that and is not going to argue with that.

That the only thing his product asks a person to do is answer a telephone, and that a person who answers a telephone on a rota is not a practitioner, is not a holder, is not a post, and can stop on a Friday by going home.

And the last one, which he gave as an aside and not as a claim.

"**I am not selling this to anybody who has a room. I am selling it to about four hundred premises that have a person in them on a Tuesday and nobody on a Saturday, and I have worked out that those are the ones that cannot get anybody back.**"

---

She listened to all of it and did not interrupt and she asked two questions and both of them were about the product.

"**What happens to the person who is on the rota when they stop coming in.**"

"**They stop being on it. The card comes down at the next visit and nobody comes to take it down and it is left there.**"

"**And who is at the end of the number in about four months when that person has gone and the building has a new keyholder and nobody has rung it since.**"

He did not answer that one.

He answered the other one, which she had not asked, and he answered it in one sentence and it is the sentence she came out of that shop carrying and she has not put it down anywhere.

"**I do not know and I am not going to tell you, and the reason I am not going to tell you is that the moment I tell you there are about four hundred people in this city who can ring it, and then it is a name with a number under it instead of a number with a name under it, and I have spent about four years watching that happen.**"

---

Then she said no, and she said it before he had finished, and she gave the reason in about forty words, and it was hers, and she said where it was not from.

"**I am not going to use your line about a person who finishes a practice on her own. That is not my line and I have not got a line of my own and I am not borrowing somebody else's off a wall, and I have said one thing in four years and it is four words long and it is about printing and not about this.**"

"**Then what is the reason.**"

"**The reasoning is better than the product and I have been in about four rooms in two years and I have never once seen the better reasoning work on a person, and I am not going to be the counter that a thing like this is sold across.**"

"**You are not going to be anything. It is a card and it goes in a shop or it does not.**"

"**In about four years the only evidence anybody in this city has that anybody ever thought about a person who has not come back is a shop in the Quiet Quarter with a card on the top of it, and there is a name over that door and I paid for it out of my own money in my first year, and I would be that shop. I am fifty-eight. I have been the place a thing was bought from for nineteen years and I know exactly what that is and I know it before anybody tells me.**"

---

Then she gave the half of it that nobody asked for, and it took about eleven seconds, and he did not interrupt her and he did not improve on it and he is not going to.

"**And I would like it noticed that a no from a shop is not the right answer either. It goes in your book as a shop. You will have about four of those and every one of them will be a woman who is careful, and in about four years you will have a page of careful women and no page of anything, and that is the same shape as the four hundred lines in that folder and I know it because I printed them.**"

"**Then I have two things.**"

"**You have one thing and one no and the no is mine and I am not going to be a data point in a book with a shop in it.**"

He said that was fair, in about four seconds, and did not say anything else about it, and nobody thanked either of them, and the man of twenty-two who had been in that shop for all nine minutes was not asked for an opinion and gave none and has never given one in that shop and is not going to.

The card went back into an inside pocket. Nothing was left on that counter. The hundred and forty-one pounds under the till did not move and was not opened and was not mentioned by anybody in that shop on that afternoon.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.
