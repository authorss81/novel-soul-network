# Chapter 281 — It Is Four Sentences

On the Wednesday morning of week ninety-eight, in a room in Crown Terrace with about nine chairs in it, a chair of forty-nine put an item back on a table because its own record said something, a clerk of forty-four said the name of a printed rule out loud for the first time in front of anybody, a practice standards officer of sixty read nine sentences into the record, and a man of twenty-two described a room from a chair and was not standing in front of anything. What came out of the morning was nine sentences and not a verdict. Nobody thanked anybody.

---

Ten on the docket and nine in date, and the stair fire door in Nine Bridges was not reported since the Monday and has not been chased, and the entry records that and attributes it to nothing.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its hundred and seventh day and it is two hundred and twelve days old.

---

The room is on a floor with a corridor outside it and about nine chairs with metal frames in it, and the chairs have been in service longer than anybody working in that building.

The item has now been on that table three times. The first time it was four paragraphs long and had a heading on it with a word that means a meeting. The second time it was the same four paragraphs with a second fault written on the record of it, which is a fault nobody raised out loud because nobody in that room could raise it without saying how it got there.

The third time is this morning, and the only thing anybody says about why it is back is that it is back because of what is written on it.

That is a complete answer. It is also, in a way that nobody in that room remarks on, the whole of the case.

---

The chair of forty-nine has an ordinary coat and a folder and a pen, and her name is Ilse Nordahl, and it was said out loud in a district office a fortnight ago by a man with a bad hip who was not talking to her.

She is a person with an ordinary job. She signs about eleven of a kind a year. She is not a post and nothing that happened in that room this morning required her to be one.

"**You have it because of what is on the sheet.**"

"**I have it because of what is on the sheet.**"

"**Then say what is on the sheet, and do not say who put it there.**"

"**What is on the sheet is that the item asks a question with one answer, and that the room answered it, and that the answer was that it cannot.**"

Nobody in that room looked at anybody. About four people in it knew about a set of stairs and about four of those four were not going to say so and the chair had not asked and was not going to.

---

The clerk of forty-four was in the room and she is the woman who pulled about nine hundred practice cards out of use in about four days, correctly, and who has been seconded to the secretariat of that committee for about a term, and who nobody in the building she works in has been told is in it.

She was asked, at about twenty past ten, whether the practice the item was about fell under anything printed.

"**It falls under the printed rule. It is called the Crown Clause. It is on a wall in a binder in a district office in District 3, it has a number on it and a date, and the date is about eleven years ago.**"

"**Has it been withdrawn.**"

"**No. Nothing in that binder has been withdrawn in nineteen years, because withdrawing one is a thing a person writes on it, and I have worked beside that binder for about a term and a half and I have not seen anybody write on anything in it.**"

"**And what does it say.**"

"**That a practice card has to carry the beat that performs the boundary before it goes out, and that about nine hundred of them did not, and that is why the cards went.**"

She said the name once. It was said out loud in a room in Crown Terrace for the first time and it was said by a competent person doing her job, and about four people in that room had never heard it and about four had and were not going to say where.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody asked her about the secondment. Nobody in that room knew there was a secondment.

---

Aurelia Sopp was in the room again and she was not on anybody's list, and her job is to ask the questions, and she has been right every time she has opened her mouth in that building for nineteen years and being right has done one small thing so far, and she has said out loud that she is not going to be thanked for it.

Talia Venn gave evidence for about eleven minutes about a practice she has been inside of for a term and a half. She described the beat in which a person knows a practice is finished. She described that it is not a feeling and not a decision and cannot be taught by being told, and that in about four years she has watched about nine people discover it, and that two of the nine were told afterwards by somebody that they had got lucky.

She did not read the sentence out of the binder. She was asked about it outside that room, afterwards, by a man of twenty-two, and gave the same answer she gave six weeks ago, which is that she said what a card is for and not what a district says about a card, and that those are two sentences and one of them is hers.

Nobody thanked her. Her name went to nobody in that room.

---

Then he was asked, and he answered from a chair with his hands on his knees, and he was not standing in front of anything and he taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nobody.

He described the room. Four rooms and a back one off a service road in the Lower Wards, a board over a door with four words in chalk in the hand of a woman of about sixty-eight and a grey ghost of four above them that came off in a thumb and will not come off any further, a hardboard on two nails with sixteen lines on it in ten hands, a ring binder on a shelf with eight things in it that has been opened twice in a term and a half, nine chairs, a counter with nothing on it, a kettle, a socket that works, a bell-push that does not ring by itself, and a plate outside with a letting agent's telephone number on it and no name on it.

"**And about four hundred people in this case could find that door.**"

"**And one of them is you.**"

"**And one of them is me and that has been true since the spring before the one before last.**"

"**Then that is the answer to the item.**"

"**It is the answer to one question in it. There are about four others and I have got nine minutes and I would rather spend them on the other four.**"

Nobody improved on that and the chair said go on and he did.

---

What the morning produced was said at about ten to twelve, and it was nine sentences, and it was read out by the practice standards officer of sixty into a record that belongs to a committee and is not ours, and it is the whole of what that morning produced, and it is not a verdict.

She said it once, to the room, at a pace that was not slow, and she did not ask anybody to check it and she did not look at anybody while she was saying it.

Afterwards the chair of forty-nine said that she was not going to decide the item on that morning, and gave the reason in one sentence, and the reason is true and it is the reason a competent person gives: that the item turns on whether a record of who stopped exists, that no such record has been produced by anybody in nineteen years, and that a room cannot be asked to produce one and cannot be expected to and does not have one.

"**So the item stands.**"

"**The item stands open and it will be on the list again and nobody in this room expects anybody to be able to answer it, and I am not going to pretend to a thing I have not got.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody said the nine sentences were good. About four people in that room had wanted the item closed and about four had wanted it open and neither of those four groups said so.

---

At about half past six there were about nine people in a cold room off a service road and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door with a cup of tea in front of her and had been since about two, and nobody in that room knew what had happened in a building in Crown Terrace and nobody was told and nobody will be.

A cook of twenty-seven had said nothing all day. She has said five sentences of a shape in two volumes and has said she will not say a sixth, and about four people in this case are keeping the count and nobody in that room is.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.

*284. Wednesday of week 98, at about ten on a service road in the wind, and this entry is one item, one name, nine sentences, and a hundred and seven days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Ten jobs and one short of it in date, and the item was at that table for the third time in about four months. Nothing was chased: an address in Nine Bridges has now gone unreported since the Monday and this entry does not say why. The basement in an unnamed district was not reported. The second fault in the item is therefore still on a record, in a secretariat, in a building, and the only thing keeping it there is unchanged and is not named in this entry because nobody in that room named it. She said that it does, that the printed rule has a name and that the name is a person with a job could say out loud, that it is on a wall in a binder in a district office in District 3, that it has a number and a date and the date is about eleven years ago, that nothing in that binder has been withdrawn in nineteen years because withdrawing one is a thing a person writes on it, and that she has not seen anybody write on anything in it in about a term and a half. She then said what it requires: that a practice card carry the beat that performs the boundary before it goes out, that about nine hundred did not, and that this is why the cards came out of use.  The name was said out loud in a room for the first time, it is written once in the entry above and is in no document of this case, and about four people in that room had never heard it and about four had. *Nine sentences, read into a record that is not ours.* **That a room cannot be asked to prove that it can stop itself, because the only evidence of a person stopping is a record of who stopped, and that is a column, and about eleven thousand premises do not have one; that nobody can produce that column and nobody is going to build it; that what a room can do is name a person who is not coming, say out loud what has already been done about it, say how long that is going to be, and then go and do the next thing that does not need the person; and that this is all it is, that it is four sentences, that none of them is about consent, and that all of them are about arithmetic.   These are the version that goes into the record, they are the ones a practice standards officer of sixty wrote, they are carried here whole because a load book that carries nine sentences cannot get them wrong, and they are not a document of this case and are not on anything of ours. The record belongs to a committee in a building in Crown Terrace and no page of it is in this file or in that room. She said them once, to the room, at a pace that was not slow, and checked nothing and looked at nobody.**

The item stands open. She said that it will be on the list again and that nobody in that room expects anybody to be able to answer it, which is a thing a competent person says. Nobody thanked her, nobody said the nine sentences were good, and about four people in that room had wanted the item closed and about four had wanted it open and neither of the two groups said so.
