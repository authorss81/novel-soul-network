# Chapter 280 — She Did Not Take It Down

The Monday of week ninety-eight was the day a man of about thirty-eight asked a woman of about sixty-eight, in front of about nine people, to take down a sign on a door that is not true, and she did not take it down, and it is the first time in about a term that anybody in that room has watched a person decline to do a thing they were asked to do, and she wrote the sixteenth line on a hardboard on two nails in seventeen words, unsigned, and the cost of it was the opposite of the cost of the fifteenth, and nobody thanked her.

---

Eleven on the docket and ten in date, and the stair fire door in Nine Bridges was attended at about two and reset for about the ninth time in a month.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its hundred and fifth day and it is two hundred and ten days old.

---

He has a district jacket and he visits addresses and he has been doing it for about six years, and on the Friday before he had been at a door on a second floor in a building in a district this case has not been in, and the door has a sign on it, and the sign names a firm that has not been there since about a year ago, and a woman on the ground floor told him twice that nobody lived there.

He has been back to that door twice. Both times a neighbour told him nobody lived there. The sign is on the door.

He is owed nothing, he is not a villain, and nobody in that room has ever seen him before.

---

He put it in front of her at about half past four, in front of about nine people, and he did it in the plainest words anybody in that room has heard in about a term.

"**That sign is not true. I go to doors for a job and I have been three times and three times I have been told there is nobody there, and if I put that in a report it is somebody else's problem at about half past four in the afternoon, and I would rather somebody who can take a sign down took it down.**"

A first-year of nineteen said: "**She is sixty-eight and she is here and you are right.**"

He said: "**I know she is. I have been waiting about four weeks to be in a room with somebody who can.**"

---

Nobody improved on his reason. It is a good reason and it is a real one and about four people in that room thought it was the right thing to do, and nobody said so, and one person said it and she has not been asked again.

A cook of twenty-seven said, to a sink, without turning round: "**Then it is a sign about a person and not a sign about a firm, and it is the same four words either way.**"

He said: "**It is not the same thing. The firm is not there. The person is.**"

"**Nobody in this room knows that. You do not know that. It is a thing a person on a landing said three times.**"

---

The woman of sixty-eight stood up at about ten to five.

She is the person who took a thumb to four words in a sign-writer's hand eight weeks ago and said out loud what it had cost her to become that, and who wrote four words in chalk on a glass door in Nine Bridges a fortnight ago for a woman of thirty-four who stood in it and did not telephone.

She did not say she would do it and she did not say she would not. She asked one question, and it was the only question she asked, and it took her about four seconds to ask it and about nine seconds to hear the answer to.

"**Who is behind that door.**"

---

"**I do not know.**"

"**You have been three times.**"

"**I have been three times and I have been told three times by people on that landing and by a shop on the ground floor, and one of the three said the name of the firm that was on the sign, which means he read the sign, and the other two did not give me a name at all.**"

"**Then you do not know.**"

"I do not know."

---

"**No.**"

"**That is fair. I am asking you to take down a sign that says a firm is not there. It is not me that is asking. It is me that is asking because I cannot get anybody else to, and about four years ago somebody who could would have taken it down in about four minutes and gone away, and you have never had anybody in a room with you before this year.**"

"**No.**"

"**I have not given you a reason yet.**"

"**You have not, and you are not going to, and that is not manners and I am not being careful with you. I do not know who that is. If I take that sign down then I have said something about that door, and if I leave it up then I have said nothing about it, and I am not going to say something about a door in a building in a district none of us has been in because a man doing a job on a Friday was told three times by people on a landing that there was nobody there.**"

---

"**And if somebody in that flat is old and cannot get down the stairs.**"

"**Then there is a person on that landing who knows it, and it is not me, and it is not a sign. I have been putting words on doors for about nine years and every one of the four I have taken down was put up by somebody who could get down the stairs, and I never once took one down for the person it was about. I have thought about that a great deal since the spring and I have not changed my mind and I am not going to today in front of nine people.**"

He sat down. He did not argue. Nobody in that room argued with her and nobody said she was wrong and nobody said she was right, and she was not thanked, and about four people in that room wanted to say something and about four of them did not, and the entry records the not saying and does not attribute it.

---

Then she went to the hardboard on two nails, which is not the board over the door and is not the wall list in its plastic sleeve in a repair shop in Lattice Ward and is not a sheet of about two hundred and forty lines on a wall in a building in Crown Terrace, and there are four of those objects and this is the third of them and it has fifteen lines in nine hands and ten names.

She said the cost out loud before she made the mark, standing in front of it, and it took her about eleven seconds, and nobody improved on it.

"**Eight weeks ago I said the cost of becoming a person who can take a sign down and it was that in about four years somebody would stand in a doorway and ask me to put a different one up, and it was eight weeks, and a fortnight ago somebody did, and I have wanted that for about eight weeks and did not know it.**"

"**This is the other half of it.**"

"**This is the other half of it. That cost me the last thing I wanted. This one is going to cost me the only thing I have, and the only thing I have is that somebody can ask me and I can say no, and in about four years there are going to be about four people in this city who know I can take a sign down, and all four of them are going to be told that I did not take one down, and every one of them is going to decide that I could not.**"

---

"**You could.**"

"**I could and I did not, and you are going to go away and say the other half of that to about nine people and about four of them are going to hear it, and I am sixty-eight and I have a piece of chalk in a coat and there is not one form in this city for being a person who is asked and says no.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And I am not going to take it down tomorrow either, and nobody is going to ask me again, and that is the last thing I am going to say about it in this room.**"

---

She wrote it in about nine seconds with a pen off the counter and did not write her name, and nobody looked for a place where one would have gone, because there has never been one on that board and there is not going to be one.

She wrote, in her own hand: *I take signs down. I did not take that one down, because nobody behind it asked me.*

Seventeen words. It is a line and not a rule and it binds nobody including her, and it is evidence of nothing in any proceeding, there being none, and it is not enforceable against a man in a district jacket or against anybody else.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody said the reason was good. Nobody asked her whether she was sure, and nobody has asked her about it in the fortnight since, and about four people in this case have thought about it and about four of them have not said anything.

---

At about half past six Talia Venn was in that room and had been for about an hour and said one sentence, and the sentence was about the sign and not about the woman, and nobody thanked her for it either, and she is named here and by nobody in that room and her name went to nobody in it.

"**A sign that is not true is still a sign, and a sign is the only thing on a door that anybody in that building can read from the bottom of a staircase, and I am not saying she is right. I am saying that in about four years the only record of that door will be a word on a piece of paper somewhere, and a piece of paper is the thing this whole case is against, and I have been in rooms with about nine people in them for a term and a half and every one of us would have taken it down.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody asked her a fifth question and she did not volunteer a sixth and she did not say her name to anybody in that room.

---

The man of thirty-eight went at about half past six. Nobody asked him to write it down and he did not write it down, and nobody told him it had been dealt with, and nobody told him it had not, and the sign is still on that door and the firm has still not been there for about a year.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.
