# Chapter 275 — Eleven Minutes

The Thursday morning of week ninety-six was a morning in a basement in Nine Bridges where a machine that has been stuck for about four years was made to work, and eleven minutes of a tape made fourteen years ago in a room in a building in Crown Terrace were heard once by two people and by a man who opened the door and does not know why he opened it, and a woman of fifty-three on that tape said the name of the rule about herself, and nobody has been found, and nobody went looking, and the recording is not a message from anybody and is not going to be treated as one.

---

Eleven on the docket and nine in date, and the stair fire door in Nine Bridges was attended at about seven and reset and is expected back, and the standing water in a basement in an unnamed district was not reported, and none of this is in the entry.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its ninety-fourth day and it is a hundred and ninety-nine days old.

---

He had been at it for about two weeks.

The thing he wanted was a name, and the name was not in the binder of printed standards on the wall of a district office in District 3, and it was not in the record of the item, and it was not in a document of any kind that anybody in this case holds or has ever been shown.

It was on a shelf in a room in Crown Terrace, in a box of drafts, and beside the box of drafts in that room was a shelf of tapes, and the tapes are the reason the rule is a rule and not a rumour: somebody read it out loud, in that building, fourteen years ago, to a person who was doing their job.

That room closed about four years ago. Its paper went into boxes and its tapes went down into a basement in Nine Bridges, and the machine down there has been stuck for about four years.

---

He could not open the door.

Not because of anything anybody did to him. Because a basement in Nine Bridges is a building four hundred people in this case could find and he is the only one of them who is findable at it, and being findable at a building and being known to the man who has the key to that building are two different things and only one of them is true about him.

He telephoned the number on a plate at about seven in the morning.

"**I am going to ask you to do something and I have thought about it for about two days and I want you to know that before I ask, because I have not asked anybody in this case for anything since a Friday in the spring before the one before last.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I need a door opened. A basement in Nine Bridges, a machine in it, and a man who looks after the building. That is all I am going to say to you and I am not going to say anything else about it now or afterwards, and I am not going to telephone you about it again.**"

Nobody in that building was told why the door was open. The man who looks after it was asked whether the machine worked, and said no, and then said yes, and did not ask what it was for, and was not asked.

---

The machine is a recorder. It ran for eleven minutes and then stopped and did not start again, and about four people were in the basement for the whole of it, and one of them had opened the door and had not been told why, and two of them had a reason, and the fourth was a woman of forty-four who had been a typist in that room fourteen years ago and who is seconded to a secretariat in another building and who has never once been asked about a tape in her life.

He had asked her. She came in about twenty minutes and she was late and she did not apologise for being late.

"There is a tape of that room on a shelf in that room and I typed for that room for about two years," she said, "and I know which one it is and I know roughly where it is, and I have not been in that building since about the spring."

"You have never listened to it."

"**No.**"

"**In fourteen years.**"

"**No. And I am going to tell you the reason now, once, and I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to be asked for a longer version of it, and then I am going to stand in a basement and listen to a tape with a man I have known for a term.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I was the typist. I was not the audience.**"

Nine words. She did not improve on them then or afterwards and nobody in that basement improved on them, and she has never said them in a room, and she was not thanked, and she is owed nothing by anybody in this case.

---

The tape starts with a chair moving. There is a hum in it that is not the machine in the room and is the machine in the basement, and the whole of it is thinner than the voice in the room and about four minutes of it is two people not saying anything.

It was made in a room in Crown Terrace fourteen years ago. The person asking is doing their job. She is fifty-three on the tape and there is no name on it and the name arrives eleven seconds in, because the person doing their job asked for it.

"Your name for the record."

"Iona Sorn."

Then, after a pause of about four seconds:

"Whose procedure is this."

"Mine. I drafted it. It is called the Crown Clause and it is mine and I have never denied writing it and I am not going to now, and I would like it noted that I have not come here to be asked that and that I have answered it anyway because you are going to ask it in about nine minutes and I would rather it was on this."

---

"Then tell me what it is for."

"It is for a building in which nobody has answered a door for two days. That is the whole of the condition and it is not a long one and I have never wanted it longer. In that situation the correct procedure is the last approved sequence, run as a standing response, and it runs while the person is still on their way back, and everybody who is competent in this city knows it and about four hundred of them could pick it up on a Monday without asking anybody."

"And if nobody will run it."

"Then somebody has decided that a pump runs until a person comes back. That is not a neutral thing to decide. I have watched that decision get made about four times in fourteen years and I have never once watched anybody make it about a person they knew."

"**You are deciding that about a stranger and you will never have to look at them.**"

---

"How often has it been run."

"Nine times."

"In fourteen years."

"Nine times. That is the figure in the file and the figure in the file is the figure in the file."

Nobody in that basement corrected her. There is no figure on the table in that basement and the figure she has given is wrong by a very long way, and this entry does not put the right one here, because the right one is about four hundred competent people in a city, nobody in this case can produce it, and nothing in this case is going to try.

---

"Did you want it used."

"That is not my question."

"It is mine."

"It is not yours. It is mine, and I have answered it, and I will now tell you the true answer and you may write it down and it will do you no good at all. Nobody who drafted it wanted it used. That is not a complaint and it is not a defence. It is used. I have watched it be used and I did not stop it and I could not have stopped it, because a thing that can be picked up on a Monday by anybody competent is not a thing that its author can pick up."

---

"Then who wrote these questions."

"Somebody read my submission in about nine minutes. You can check the date on the file. Whoever wrote these questions did not read what I sent, and I have been in this building about eleven minutes and I have been asked four questions and three of them were about whether I would say a thing again, and I would like to go back to work."

Then the chair moved, and there were about four seconds, and the tape ran on for another two minutes and there is nothing usable in it.

---

And then the thing came, in the space between one sentence and the next one, about two feet from a man in a cold basement, and it was not a voice and it was not anybody.

* * *

> A person who has not come back is not a person who refused.
> You are deciding for them, and you will not be there.

---

Nothing followed it.

He had heard it six times in three volumes and it had never once told him anything, and this was the seventh and it was the first one in a basement and it was the first one that agreed with a person.

It agreed with her about the pump. It agreed with her about the four seconds on a Friday and about a room in a cold building where nobody had come back yet and about a woman of about thirty in a chair nearest a door who has never been asked anything. It said the same sentence she had said fourteen years ago to a person doing their job, in a worse room, in front of nobody, and it did not tell him a single thing he did not already have, and it did not name her, and it was not enough.

He looked at it and put it away, and the reason he looked at it is that it was there.

---

The two of them stood in that basement for about nine minutes afterwards and did not discuss the tape, and the man of thirty-four who had opened the door went back to his shift at about half past six, and nobody asked him what was in it and he has not asked and did not say.

Nothing was copied. Nothing was written down by the man of thirty-four, who has never written anything down about anything in this case. A transcript exists of about four passages and it is a transcript, and it is not a page, and it is not in the ring binder on a shelf in a room off a service road, and it is not in the book above the line in Saltmarket, and it is not going into either of them.

She was not found. Nobody looked for her. Nothing arrived from her and nothing will, and a recording of a room in which a person was asked a question is not a message and is not going to be written as one by anybody in this case.

---

He said the part that was his, on a service road, in the wind, at about ten, standing up.

"**For about four years I have been the person you can find at a door and I have wanted that not to be true, and this morning I used it to get a basement open, and I used it in about nine seconds, and I did not once think about it, and I would like it written down somewhere that I did not once think about it.**"

"**You have written it down.**"

"**On a service road, at about ten, to nobody, standing up. That is not somewhere. I have been doing this since the spring before the one before last and I have got about four years of it and it has never once been anywhere.**"

He wrote one thing on that service road.
