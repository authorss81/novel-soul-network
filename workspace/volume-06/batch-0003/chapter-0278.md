# Chapter 278 — No

On the Wednesday of week ninety-seven, at about half past four, in a corridor outside a room in a building in Crown Terrace, a practice standards officer of sixty asked a man of twenty-two to run the procedure once, in a room, with the people in it, and he said no in about nine seconds, and nobody thanked him and nobody was angry, and about an hour later she read him nine sentences in that corridor that are going into a record instead of anything of his, and they are better than what he had, and he found nothing in them to argue with, and that is the whole of what the refusal cost him.

---

Eleven on the docket and ten in date, and he was two streets away from his own building at about half past three and did not go into it.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its hundredth day and it is two hundred and five days old.

---

She had come out of the room and stood in the corridor and not gone back in, and he was on his way past, and neither of them arranged it and neither of them will say they did.

"**Take ninety seconds.**"

He stopped.

"**Say what it is.**"

"**I want you to do it once. In a room. With the people in it.**"

---

He said no in about nine seconds and he said it standing up and he did not apologise for it and he did not explain it in the first nine seconds, and about four people came out of that room behind her over the following two minutes and went past both of them and about nine of them did not look at either.

She said: "**Say the reason.**"

"**I do not know what happens to a person who finishes a practice on her own and does not know that she has done something she should not have been able to do.**"

"That is about a word in a Friday room in Crown Terrace."

"**It is the only thing I have got that is not about me.**"

---

"**That is not the reason.**"

"**It is the reason I have.**"

"Then I am not going to argue with you about it, " she said, " and I want it noticed that I did not argue with you about it, because in about four years somebody will say I did, and I will not be able to tell them that I did not."

She had been right every time she had opened her mouth in that building for nineteen years and she had had to hold it back on purpose twice. This was not one of those. This was the first time in nineteen years that being right in that building had cost her something, and the something was that she was asking a man to do a thing and he had said no, and she was going to have to be the person who said so in a room.

---

"**Then you will say it to the room.**"

"I will say it to the room in the morning."

"**With my name in it.**"

"**With your name in it. Not mine. You have just refused to be the person who says a printed rule out loud in a room in front of about nine people, and I am not going to let you have the easier half of that by refusing a different thing to me on a Wednesday afternoon.**"

Nobody thanked him for that either.

---

She went back in and came out again at about six, and stood in the corridor, and he was still there, because he had a thing at about seven in another building and had not said so.

"**I am going to say what I am going to say and you are going to hear it, and you are not going to be able to stop any of it, and I am doing it on purpose.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**A room cannot be asked to prove that it can stop itself, because the only evidence of a person stopping is a record of who stopped, and that is a column, and about eleven thousand premises do not have one. Nobody can produce that column and nobody is going to build it. What a room can do is name a person who is not coming, and say out loud what has already been done about it, and say how long that is going to be, and then go and do the next thing that does not need the person. That is all it is. It is four sentences and none of them is about consent and all of them are about arithmetic.**"

---

She said about nine sentences in all, in a corridor, at about six in the evening, with the door of that room shut and about nine people behind it, and she did not ask him a single question and she did not check whether she was getting it right.

The man of twenty-two listened to all nine and found nothing in them to argue with. That is the finding and it is not a compliment. He had a version of it himself, and it had been in his head since the spring before the one before last, and it was about a person who can be found at a door and about a card with a beat on it, and every one of her nine sentences was about something that already existed and none of them was about anything he had to be for.

He stood in that corridor for about four minutes afterwards and said:

"**That is better than what I had.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**I am not going to pretend it is not.**"

"**I did not expect you to. I want you to understand what I am doing, because I am doing it on purpose and I have done nothing else on purpose in this building in nineteen years: I am taking a thing off you and putting my name on it, and the reason is not that yours is worse. The reason is that a thing in a record with a man's name on it can be argued with in about four years by about nine people, and a thing in a record with my name on it can only be argued with by whoever is standing in the room at the time, and that is me, and I am sixty and I am not going to be able to do that much longer.**"

---

"**You could have told me that before you asked.**"

"**I could not. If I had told you that before I asked then I would have been asking you to come and be the person who shows a room, and that is a different thing and it would have worked. I asked for the other thing because I am allowed to ask for the other thing and because you are the only person in this city I can ask and because I was tired, and I have already told a room in about a term that tiredness is not a reason and that I intended to use it.**"

He did not say anything to that. There was nothing on the page to say to it with.

---

She went back into the room at about ten to six and he stood in that corridor for another four minutes and then went to a building two streets away and did a job that took about an hour.

At about half past six in a cold room off a service road there were about nine people and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door with a cup of tea in front of her and had been since about two, and nobody in that room knew what had happened in a building in Crown Terrace and nobody was told and nobody will be.

A cook of twenty-seven had said nothing all day, which is the sixth time in about a fortnight and she has said five sentences of that shape in two volumes and is not going to say a sixth, and about four people in this case are keeping the count and nobody in that room is.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.
