# Chapter 274 — One Line

The thirteenth sitting of that Exchange fell on the Wednesday morning of week ninety-six, four weeks to the day after the twelfth, and the book opened, and one line went in, and the telephone count moved from forty-seven to forty-nine, and the book stands at forty-seven lines, and the reason it was shut at the twelfth sitting is nineteen years old and is not hers to give, and nobody in that room asked her for it and about four of them wanted to.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date, which is the first time in about a fortnight that the two have been level.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its ninety-third day and it is a hundred and ninety-eight days old.

---

The room is above the line in a building in Saltmarket and the holder of the room says so at the start of every sitting, and the shutters are up, and the light in it is the flat light of a room that has been on the same side of a street since before anybody in it was working.

Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in. The ninth stands against the wall facing away from the room. There is a table, a tin that has never had a lock on it, and a book with four columns and forty-six lines in it, and it is a hundred and twenty-two days since the last line went in.

Nine people. The young man of twenty-one who has been coming since he was seventeen. The young woman of twenty-one who has been coming since she was fifteen, who put the ninth chair where it is, who has never been asked to move it and has never been named in that room and who is not asked anything this morning, and who is not the first-year of nineteen in another building, and there is one nineteen-year-old in this case and it is the other one.

Nobody wrote down that she was there. Nobody writes it down.

---

The first thing that happened was that a woman of twenty-six sat down at about a quarter to ten and said that she had telephoned eleven days ago and had not been rung back and had come anyway, and that she wanted to say a thing out loud to the holder of the book and not to the room.

Bea Nunn said: "**Say it to me then.**"

Nobody else heard it. It took her about four minutes and there is not one word of it in this entry, because the holder of the book wrote it down in the third column of a four-column book on a Wednesday morning and nobody read it and nobody has read it since, and it is in this case only as a line, and a line is a line.

---

What she said, in the room's hearing afterwards and in about eleven words, was that a man had answered a telephone on a plate off a service road in the Lower Wards at about half past five on a Friday and had told her where the dry ground was, and that he had not asked her a single question, and that in about four years she is going to remember that it happened for about four years and then she is not going to remember it at all.

The holder of the book wrote the line in about nine seconds and did not read it back and did not summarise it and did not thank her.

---

A young man of twenty-one said, to the room and not to her:

"**You have been telephoned and not rung back before. I know because I keep a count and I have never told you what the count is.**"

"I know you keep it."

"**I am not going to tell you and you are not going to ask, and I have got a second thing and I have never said it out loud, and I am saying it now because there is a person in the room.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**About four of the people who ring that number are not ringing for a technique and about four of them are ringing because they are somewhere with nothing and it is dark. Nobody has ever asked any of them which, and I have wanted somebody to ask for about two years and I have not asked, because if I ask then I am the one who has decided that matters, and I have not got the standing to decide that matters.**"

Nobody said anything to that and the holder of the book said the thing she says about furniture, and a chair is a chair, and it was against the wall at the end of the morning and it was not moved back and its mover is not named here and has not been named in nineteen years and has not been in that room since her holder was fifteen.

---

The count came at about ten past ten, unasked, as it comes.

"**Forty-seven to forty-nine. Two in four weeks, and I am telling you the direction and not the arithmetic, and I did the arithmetic in my head about four minutes ago and I am not going to do it again in front of nine people.**"

"Give us the rest of it."

"**Forty-nine calls and forty-seven lines. The book is one line ahead of the telephone and it was one line behind four weeks ago, and it has not been ahead in four years and I have not looked into that and I am not going to, and I would like nobody in this room to convert those two numbers into each other or into a number of sittings.**"

"**That is the second time you have said the direction.**"

"**It is the second time it has.**"

"How many times have you announced the count."

"**Eighteen. Thirteen of those are the thirteen Wednesdays this book holds and five predate it, and there is a sheet of paper with the five on it that I have not shown anybody and that I am not going to.**"

---

Then a man of about forty-four, who has come twice and asked for something once, said the thing that about four other people in that room had been carrying since the last sitting and had not said.

"**Last time you gave us a new reason for not opening it and it was the best thing anybody has said in this room in about four years, and I have been carrying it about four weeks, and I want to know where it came from.**"

Bea Nunn put two fingers on the cover of the book and left them there.

"**No.**"

"**You have not even said what I asked.**"

"**I have said what you asked. You have asked me for the reason and the answer is no, and the reason I am not going to give you is the thing you should have worked out in about four seconds, which is that it is not mine.**"

---

She said the next part to the room and not to him, and it is the whole of what she gave, and about nine minutes passed before anybody said anything.

"**I was twenty-nine. I was not working anywhere anybody recognised. Somebody said one sentence to me about a book in a room and I have carried it every day since, and it is not a sentence I wrote and it is not a sentence I chose and I could not give it away if I tried, and the only person in this city who could say it out loud with any right to is not findable and has not been for nineteen years, and that is why it is not going in a building in Crown Terrace, and that is why it is not in a book, and that is why it is in me and it comes out of me the way it comes out, and if I put it on a page then in about four years there will be a rule about who may be heard in a room and a rule about who may be heard is a rule about who may be found.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody asked who the person was. Nobody asked a second question. The young man of twenty-one closed his mouth, which he had opened about four seconds earlier, and did not say what was in it, and it was not about four words and nobody in that room ever found out what was in it.

A man of twenty-two in that room had been about eleven minutes and had worked out the same thing about four seconds before she said it, and did not put his hand up, and has never been told that he worked it out, and worked it out on a stair afterwards instead, in about nine seconds, and gave the reason out loud on a service road that night and not in that room.

"**Because a person is not a mechanism, and a book is not a library, and if a nineteen-year-old sentence is the thing nineteen years of people have relied on then the worst thing anybody can do to it is print it and hang it on a wall where about four hundred competent people can pick it up on a Monday.**"

---

At about half past six in the cold room off a service road there were about nine people and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven put a cup of tea in front of her and said nothing at all, and a first-year of nineteen said one sentence about the afternoon, which was that a person of twenty-six came eleven miles to say four minutes to one woman in a room above a line in Saltmarket and that nobody heard a word of it and that the whole of the day is that one line going in.

Nobody thanked the holder of the book. She is owed nothing by anybody in this case. She is not carried anywhere, she is not on a stage, she is not described as a resource, and she is not going to be asked again.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.
