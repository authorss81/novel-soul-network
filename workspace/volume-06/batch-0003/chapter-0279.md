# Chapter 279 — Did You Want To

On the Thursday of week ninety-seven, at about half past four in the afternoon, in a cold room off a service road, a woman of twenty-four worked out an arithmetic that nobody had given her, asked a question that was smaller than the one she wanted, and was told, and nothing was decided and nothing was resolved and nobody was thanked and there is no scene here and there was none.

---

Ten on the docket and ten in date, and the stair fire door in Nine Bridges was not reported and has not been chased.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its hundred and first day and it is two hundred and six days old.

---

She had been in that room for about an hour and a half and had said about nine sentences in it, all of them about other people, and she has a way of arriving at a thing by counting what is in a room rather than what is said in it. She is Talia Venn and nobody in that room said her name and she did not give it to anybody.

There were about nine people in that room at about half past four on the Monday. There were seven of them now, and one of the seven was asleep, and nobody had gone anywhere that anybody would have noticed, and nobody had left early, and nobody had said a word about it.

"**You were not in here on Monday afternoon. You were somewhere with a great deal of paper in it and about nine people who wanted something, and you have not said one word about Wednesday, and I have not asked you about Wednesday, and I am not going to ask you what it was.**"

"**No.**"

"**I am going to ask you something smaller than that.**"

---

"**Did you want to.**"

It took him longer than she took.

"**I want you to be careful what that question is. I have had four days with that question and I have not got an answer and I have got about nine words, and if you ask me the bigger one I will give you the nine words, and the nine words are about a Friday room in another building and about a woman of twenty-two who could not finish a practice on her own, and you have heard that one and it is not the one you are asking about.**"

"**Then I am asking the small one.**"

---

He told her. It took about four minutes and it was not a speech and he did not arrange it into one, and the whole of it was: a person of sixty had asked him in a building in Crown Terrace on Wednesday afternoon to run the procedure once, in a room, with the people in it, and he had said no in about nine seconds, and the reason he had given her was the one she had just said she had heard, and it was not the reason, and she had not argued with him about it, and she was going to be the person who said in a room the next morning that he had said no.

He did not tell her the nine sentences and she did not ask for them and neither of them referred to them.

He did not say that the refusal had cost him something. She worked that out and it took her about two seconds and she did not say it either, and about nine words of it was that he had said no and had not said why, and the whole of the rest of it happened outside a room in a building in Crown Terrace and would stay there.

---

She said one thing and it was about him and not about the two of them and it is the only sentence either of them said that anybody could have called a conclusion.

"**You have been the person a room can ask for about four years and you have never once wanted to be the person a room can ask, and I have watched you build a whole case out of not wanting it, and I do not think that case is what you think it is.**"

"**Then what is it.**"

"**It is that you have wanted one person to ask you for something and to keep on asking, and there has not been one, and there is now, and she is sixty and she will be sixty-one and she does not want you.**"

He did not answer that. He has not answered it in any way to anybody, and about four people in this case have worked out that there was a Thursday and none of them has said so.

---

Nobody in that room knew what had happened. Nobody asked him about Wednesday and he did not bring it up again, and about four people noticed that he was unusually quiet and about four of them decided he was tired, and he was tired, and that was not the reason and nobody in that room was misled by it for longer than the evening.

A cook of twenty-seven put the ring on at about half past eight, which she does on every working morning and has done for about twenty-eight weeks, and put a cup of tea in front of a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door and said nothing at all, and about four people in that room wanted to ask her why she comes and nobody did and nobody apologised to her, because an apology would have been a question and it would still have been one.

A first-year of nineteen said one thing at about half past seven that was not about any of it and that four people wrote down afterwards and nobody acted on.

"**There is no post in this city whose business it is to ring a person, and there are about four rooms full of people in this city every year who could hold that job and none of them do, and every one of the four of us has said in this room in the last two years that somebody should.**"

Nobody said who. Nobody said it again. She did not improve on it and she has not improved on it since.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.
