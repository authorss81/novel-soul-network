# Chapter 272 — Four Seconds, Again

The Friday of week ninety-five was a Friday rota, and it ran in a company training room at the end of a passage on a floor with about nine other rooms on it, with eleven people on the week's list and about four of them not on it, and the cards have been out of use for about five weeks and the room still ends on a spoken word, and a man of about forty-four who has been on that list for about nine years offered the reason for the four seconds for the third time in three weeks and this time one person took him up on it, and neither of them has the reason, and nobody thanked either of them and nobody has said out loud what the figure is this year.

---

Grade three, twenty to eight, on a card in date, with the exception for the term recorded and renewed in a system in a box that says *No reason given by holder*. Eleven on the Friday list for the week and about four of the eleven not on it.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its eighty-eighth day and it is a hundred and ninety-three days old.

---

The room has nine folding chairs in it and a laminated sheet on a post by the door with nine words on it and no name and no date and no company and no version, and he has seen that sheet in about nine other places in this city and it is the same nine words in all of them.

Nine people, and two against the wall, and a woman of thirty-six with a company lanyard and a card of her own on the table in front of her, and a man of fifty-one in the second row with a bad wrist who came in on a Friday and is not on the list, and a young man of twenty-one who is near the door and is on the list and is not the man of fifty-one and is not a young woman of twenty-one in a room in another district who has never been named.

He was there to confirm scope, which is the whole of his job on a grade three Friday, and his name is on the list for the week in a system, and there is a stamped number in a box in that system that is not his room number, because the room has never had a number on the door.

He has confirmed about nine hundred of these. He has never once been asked to teach one.

---

The practice is a load-hold with a boundary-release at the end of it, and the boundary-release is the beat that confirms the agreed scope, refuses further sharing, and hands the choice to act back to the person doing it.

Every card that company printed has that beat on it. There are none in the room. They came out of use on a Wednesday four days after the twelfth sitting of an Exchange in Saltmarket and went into about four hundred in a bag in a storeroom, and nobody in this city has any use for that fact this evening.

She runs the practice through four times in about twenty minutes. At the end of each one she says one word and stops, and the nine people stop, and the word is not on anything anybody in that room is holding, because nobody in that room is holding anything.

---

On the last run the woman of about thirty-four in the second row got to the end of the hold and stopped and finished, and the gap was about four seconds.

She has been on that list for about four years. She was in the second row on the Friday of week ninety-two and on the Friday of week ninety-three and she is in it now, and that is three consecutive Fridays and nobody in the room has ever asked her to account for any of it.

The instructor did not say anything. The whole room went back to the start. The man of twenty-two was in the fourth row against the wall with the word available and did not say it and was not asked to and is not going to be.

---

He caught the man of about forty-four on the stairs afterwards, which nobody arranged.

The man of about forty-four has been on that list for about nine years. He made the offer on the Friday of week ninety-three and again on the Friday of week ninety-four, in a doorway, on the grounds that in about four years somebody is going to write down that a room of nine people got faster when the cards came out of use, and that it will be true and wrong at the same time.

"**You have told me twice that the reason is available.**"

"I have."

"**Then I am asking for it, and I have thought about asking for about two days and I am aware that I am the person in the lanyard and that asking a stranger for a fact is a thing I do about eleven times a week for money.**"

"**You are.**"

"**Then here it is anyway. I want it and I would rather have it from you than work it out wrong in a book.**"

---

The man of about forty-four was quiet for about nine seconds.

"I do not have it," he said. "I have got as far as you have and no further, and I have been getting there for about three Fridays, and I want that noted because I offered it twice as though I had it."

"**What have you got.**"

"That the gap was not there before and it is there now and I have not been in this room often enough to say what before means. You are, or you were, and you are the only person I know who has counted them before, and I have not asked you because you would have had to tell me whether you had."

"**I did not tell you.**"

"No."

---

He said the rest of it on the stairs and not in the room and not in the doorway.

"**Then we are two people, and neither of us has it, and in about four years somebody is going to be right and it is going to be neither of us, and I would rather that than one of us being right about a thing we made up on a Friday night.**"

"**That is the first useful sentence anybody has said to me about it.**"

"**It is not useful. It is a smaller number of people being wrong. Do not go home and think about it.**"

Nobody thanked either of them. Nobody said the exchange had improved anything, and the rota finished at about nine and the shutter in another district came down at about ten and nobody in this case has ever been thanked on a Friday.

---

At about half past nine in the cold room off a service road there were about eleven people and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door with a bag on her lap and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven put a cup of tea in front of her and said nothing at all, which is what has happened on every working day for eighty-eight days.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the dark, at about ten.

The same gap on the Friday of week ninety-two, on the Friday of week ninety-three, and on this one, and about nine times in between the instructor had finished a run the same way with nothing said about it by anybody. The instructor said nothing. The room restarted. The man of twenty-two was in the fourth row against the wall with the word available, did not say it, was not asked to, and is not to be. The gap was not measured by an instrument and is the same figure because three people have said it is the same figure.**

*Two people on a staircase, and neither of them has it.* **A man of about forty-four, nine years on that list, offered the reason twice in about three weeks in a doorway, and this time the man of twenty-two asked for it, having thought about asking for about two days, and having said out loud that he is the person in the lanyard and that asking a stranger for a fact is a thing he does about eleven times a week for money. The answer was that the man of about forty-four does not have it either, that he has got as far as the man of twenty-two has, and that he wants that noted because he offered it twice as though he had it. What he has: that the gap was not there before and it is there now, and that he has not been in that room often enough to say what *before* means, and that he has not asked the man of twenty-two because the answer would have been whether the man of twenty-two had counted, and the man of twenty-two had not told him. The man of twenty-two gave the position, on the stairs and not in the room: that there are now two people who have it and neither is right, and that in about four years somebody will be and it will be neither of them, and that he would rather have that than one of them being right about a thing they made up on a Friday night. Called that by the other man a smaller number of people being wrong, and told not to go home and think about it. Nobody thanked either of them.**
