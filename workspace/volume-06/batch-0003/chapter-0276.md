# Chapter 276 — Nobody Asked Her For a Number

The Friday of week ninety-six was a Friday rota and it was the second of the two in about a fortnight, and it ran in a company training room at the end of a passage with nine folding chairs in it and eleven people on the week's list and about four of them not on it, and a woman of twenty-three who came for the first time asked a practical question whose answer is a number, and the number was not said out loud to anybody, and on the last run the gap of about four seconds was not there, and nobody in that room said so, and nobody thanked anybody at the end of it.

---

Grade three, twenty to eight, on a card in date, with the exception for the term recorded and renewed in a system in a box that says *No reason given by holder*. Eleven on the Friday list for the week and about four of the eleven not on it.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its ninety-fifth day and it is two hundred days old.

---

She was twenty-three and she was in the front row and she had a card of her own with her name on it and a plastic sleeve, and the sleeve was the only new object anybody in that room had brought into it in about a term.

She had worked out on a bus that she did not know what she was being taught and had decided to be the person who asked.

She asked it at about half past six in the evening, in front of nine people, in one sentence, and it was the right question.

"**When somebody on this list finishes and the room does not say anything, what are they waiting for.**"

---

The woman of thirty-six said it in about nine seconds and she was not a person who gives long answers.

"**For the instructor to say the word.**"

"**Right, so it is not me.**"

"It is never you. It is me. I have a company lanyard and a card on that table and I say it every time, and if I am ill then the room does not run, and that is the arrangement and it is in a book in an office I have never been inside."

Somebody at the back said, "**That is the first time anybody has said that in a room.**"

"**It is not a secret, it is a rota, and nobody has ever asked anybody in one of these rooms what a person is waiting for, and the answer is a woman of thirty-six with a lanyard, and I have never once been asked whether I would rather it was a card.**"

---

The man of twenty-two was in the fourth row against the wall and had the whole of the answer in about four seconds, and did not say it, and was not asked to, and did not say it afterwards either, and gave the reason on a service road at about ten and not in the room.

"**Because if I say there are about eleven of them a week and seven on a list and four not on it, then every one of those about eleven people becomes a figure, and the only person in this case who can be found at that door is me, and in about four years somebody is going to add it up and write the sum down. It is on the back of a card in a drawer in a flat in Nine Bridges and it is on no form in this city and I am not going to be the reason it stops being that.**"

"**You have thought about that a lot.**"

"**I have thought about it since the spring before the one before last, and I have never said it, and saying it in that room at about half past six on a Friday to nine people and a woman of twenty-three is the worst version of saying it there is.**"

---

The woman of twenty-three did not get a number and she did not ask a second time, and she was not the first person in that room to be told that a number is on somebody's own card, and she was not the second, and nobody explained that to her because nobody in that room explains anything to anybody.

What she said instead, about half a minute later, to nobody, was: "**Then it is you. You are the one I am waiting for and you are the one who is waiting too, and that is a strange thing to be and I would rather be waiting for a card.**"

The woman of thirty-six said: "**Yes.**"

Nobody said anything else about it. Nobody thanked the woman of twenty-three for asking. Nobody said that the question had been a good one, and the instructor did not say it again, and nobody in that room has asked her a second question about anything.

---

On the last run the woman of about thirty-four in the second row, four years on that list, reached the end of the hold and stopped and finished, and there was no gap.

Not a short one. There was nothing. The instructor said the word and the room went back to the start and the whole of it was one beat, and about four people in that room noticed, and nobody in that room said anything about it, which is not a sentence anybody in that room can be credited with.

He was in the fourth row with the word available and he did not say it. He was not asked to and is not to be.

Afterwards he could not work out whether there had been a gap of a second and a half and he had missed it, or whether there had been none, and he did not ask, and the man of about forty-four was not on that list and was not in that building, and the man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was not there and has not been asked and is not to be.

He wrote the not knowing down. That is the whole of what he did about it.

---

At about half past nine in the cold room off a service road there were about nine people and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and had not said anything to anybody all evening, which is the fifth time in about a term and she has said four sentences of that shape and not a fifth and has said that she has said four.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the dark, at about ten.
