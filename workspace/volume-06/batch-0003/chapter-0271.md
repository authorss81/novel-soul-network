# Chapter 271 — Four Years Early

The Monday morning of week ninety-five was a morning in a district office in District 3, where a fault went onto a record it had been off for a fortnight, and an afternoon in a doorway in Nine Bridges, where a woman of about sixty-eight who can take a sign down was asked to put a different one up eight weeks after she said that somebody would. The fault was on the record by about eleven and nothing whatever had happened by four. The four words went up in about four minutes. Nobody thanked anybody for either.

---

Ten on the docket and ten in date, and the stair fire door in Nine Bridges came up for the twelfth time in about a month, was attended at about eleven, and was reset again, and there is still no fault in the frame that will go in a box.

The word was at nine and ten and did not reach eleven. The heating in that room was on its eighty-fourth day and it is a hundred and eighty-nine days old.

---

The office has a counter, about nine rooms' worth of paper in it, a wall binder of printed standards with numbers and dates on them, and a person of sixty whose job it is to ask the questions.

Aurelia Sopp was at her desk with the item open in front of her and it was the second time in about a fortnight that it had come back to her, and the first time she had gone and asked a person to come and say what happens on the other side of her table, and been refused in ten words.

She was not working on it. She was working on four other things and she had the item open because it is the item she opens when she is not working on it, which is most days.

---

What came back at about ten past ten was not the item. It was a woman of forty-four with a secondment and a folder and the particular expression of a person who has found a way to do something and is about to be told it does not count.

Noor Vaszary put one sheet down on the counter, face up, and it had a heading and a paragraph under the heading and at the bottom of the paragraph a line that said the item was noted, with a date, and nothing else.

"The second fault is on it," she said. "It has been on it since Friday. I put it on it on Friday."

Aurelia Sopp did not pick it up.

"Say the sentence again so that I know what is on the sheet and not what you have been saying in my office for a week."

"It says that the only evidence of a person stopping is a record of who stopped, that a record of who stopped is a column, and that the item as drafted asks a room to prove that it can stop itself, which is a question with one answer, and that the room has answered it correctly and the answer is that it cannot."

"That is what you told me in a room in Crown Terrace with about nine people in it."

"That is what I told you in a room in Crown Terrace with about nine people in it."

"Whose hand is that."

"Mine."

---

"You understand what you have done."

"I understand what I have written."

"Those are different sentences and you have known the difference for nineteen years, so I am going to ask you the next one once. How is it possible that you can put a fault in an item's record at all."

"I am seconded to the secretariat of the committee that heard the item."

"And the secretariat keeps the record."

"The secretariat keeps everything anybody in that building says, including the things people say to me, and Ilse Nordahl's office signs about eleven of a kind a year, and the record of that kind is a line in a book in that room, and I am four metres from that book on a Tuesday."

---

Somebody at the end of the counter said, "**That is the whole of it and you have just said it out loud in an office.**"

It was a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip who has been in that room on and off for about a term and has said almost nothing in it in a term.

"**Which part.**"

"**The part where a fault gets onto a record because of a set of stairs.**"

Nobody improved on that and nobody asked him a second question, and he did not say anything else that afternoon, and there is no mechanism in this case by which anybody is asked a second question by a man with a bad hip.

---

Noor Vaszary said the part that was hers, unprompted, and said it once.

"I want it noticed that if anybody finds out that I am not entitled to note that item then the note comes off, and the reason it is on there now is that nobody in that building has been told that I am in it, and I am not going to be the person who tells them, because I like the job and because a secondment cannot be ended by anybody in this case and I have checked."

"You have checked."

"Twice. In the first week and last week."

"Nobody thanked you."

"I did not do it to be thanked and I would like it noticed that I did it because a room cannot ask to be heard again and a person has to, and that I am the person, and that this is a thing about a set of stairs."

---

Aurelia Sopp looked at the sheet for about nine seconds and then said: "It is on the record."

"It is on the record."

"And in about four years it will be a thing that was said rather than a thing that was found, and there is no difference between those two on a page, and that is the whole of what my job is."

"**Then it has done nothing.**"

"**It has done one thing and it is a small one and I am not going to be the person who says it was nothing, because it is on there in a hand, and in about four years somebody will be able to say that a person wrote it down, and about nine of them will not be able to say who.**"

She closed the folder.

"I have been right every time I have opened my mouth in that building for nineteen years. Being right has never once done anything. It is on the record. I am not going to be thanked for it and I would like nobody to try."

---

A man of twenty-two was in the outer part of that office at about half past ten and he heard the last of it and not the first of it, and he had about eleven minutes and he did not say a word.

He could have said one sentence of about nine words and it would have gone straight onto a second sheet, and he did not, and when he was asked about it afterwards, on a service road, standing up, in the wind, he gave the reason and the reason was not manners.

"Because she said she had checked twice, in the first week and the last week, and a person who has checked twice is not somebody who is going to be caught by accident."

He said it again, to check it.

"**I have been findable since the spring before the one before last and this is the first time I have been glad of it and I do not know what to do with that, so I did not use it.**"

---

At about half past two a woman of about thirty-four stood in a doorway in Nine Bridges with a coat on and a piece of paper in a pocket she did not take out.

The doorway is on the ground floor of a building in a street about four minutes' walk from a room with a counter in it, and there is glass in the lower half of the door and a sign inside the glass, and the sign says a firm that has not been in that building since about the spring.

She has checked about nine hundred doors in four buildings in this city against a list that is reissued four times a year and never corrected, on her own time, for about four years, unpaid, and asked twice in writing and been told the list is a guide. She is Petula Fenn and she is not in anybody's plan and she is owed nothing by anybody in this case.

She had been past that doorway about nine times this year and had never gone in, and she had never gone in because there was nothing behind the door to go in for, and she had worked that out about four times and had told herself each time that a sign is a sign.

She had not telephoned. She had not written. She had come, and she had stood in the doorway, and she had waited in it for about four minutes, and the reason she had come in person was that she had worked out while she was on the stairs that if she telephoned then the person on the other end would have nine hours to decide whether they were the sort of person who took signs down, and she was not going to give anybody nine hours to decide that.

---

The woman of sixty-eight came at about four o'clock and she had a coat on and there was chalk in the pocket of it, and she is the person who can take a sign down, and it is eight weeks since she did that in a room off a service road and said out loud what it had cost her to become it.

She was told there was a doorway. She did not ask who had told her and nobody said.

Petula Fenn said what she wanted written, before she asked for it, because she had known for about two hours that she would be asked.

"**I want you to put a different one up, and I am telling you what I want on it before I ask, because I know what you are going to ask me and I have thought about it on a staircase and I would rather not waste the four minutes.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**The door is on the ground floor of a building in Nine Bridges and the sign says a firm that has not been here since the spring, and I have been past it about nine times this year and every time I have read it and every time I have thought there is nobody in here, and about four times I have gone past it in the dark.**"

The woman of sixty-eight looked at the sign for about eleven seconds.

"**Who is behind that door.**"

"**I do not know. I asked a woman on the second floor and she said nobody, and she has been here thirty-one years, and I think she means nobody as a way of saying do not ask.**"

"And if there is somebody behind it."

"Then the sign is wrong and it has been wrong since the spring and about nine people a month read it."

---

She wrote it on the inside of the glass in chalk, over the top of the old one, without taking the old one down, in about four minutes, in an ordinary hand, and the old firm is still under hers and cannot be got off a glass door either.

She wrote: *Nobody answers. Ring again.*

Four words. She keeps to four, and when somebody asks her about that later she will say that four is what a person reads in the rain, and that the first four were an invitation and these are a report, and that a report is a thing that goes out of date, and that she is the one who will be asked why it says what it says.

---

And then she said the part that is the cost, and she said it standing in the doorway with the chalk already back in her pocket, and it took her about eleven seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her and there is no mechanism in this case by which anybody is thanked for standing in a doorway.

"**In about four years, on a Friday, in a room with about nine people in it, I said that somebody would stand in a doorway and ask me to put a different one up and that I would be the one who knew where the chalk was.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**It has been eight weeks. I was wrong about the four years and I would like it noticed that I was wrong about the four years and that I am not going to complain about it, and that I have not said anybody should have waited longer.**"

"It was not four years for me, " said Petula Fenn. " It was about two hours on a staircase, and I would like it noticed that I worked out the two hours and not the four years, and I am not going to complain about that either, and I am not going to be one of about nine people in this city who says a person of sixty-eight took too long."

She did not look at her.

"**And the other part. I am sixty-eight and I have taken one sign down and put this one up, and there is now a woman of thirty-four in this city who knows that I can be asked, and in about four years there will be about four of them, and every one of them will find me, and I have wanted that for about eight weeks and I did not know that I had wanted it.**"

---

He was not in that doorway. He heard both parts of it in the evening, in a cold room off a service road, from a woman of thirty-four who was owed nothing and had come in about half past five and had said all of it once and had not said it twice.

A cook of twenty-seven put the ring on at about half past eight and has done for about twenty-six weeks and told nobody either time, and the heating in that room was on its eighty-fourth day, and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door with a bag on her lap and had been since about two, and about four people in that room wanted to ask her why she comes and nobody did and nobody apologised to her, because an apology would have been a question and it would still have been one.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.

*274. Monday of week 95, at about ten on a service road in the wind, and this entry is one fault, one record, one doorway, and eighty-four days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Ten on the docket and ten in date. A stair fire door in Nine Bridges, twelfth report of the same fault in about a month, attended at about eleven, reset, expected back; the description box carries a stamped number that is not the room number, the room has never had its own number on the door, and nothing in the frame supports a fault. The rate held at nine and ten across the whole day and reached eleven on no hour of it, and no person in this case would have noticed. The heating in the room off the service road is on its eighty-fourth day off, unpaid, and unresolved, and no fund, collection, subscription, grant, list, minute, notice, form, leaflet, registration or appeal exists anywhere near it. The second produced no action at all and one thing in the way of paper: on the Friday before, at about a quarter past ten, a clerk of forty-four put a second fault on the record of that item. The fault is that a room cannot be asked to prove that it can stop itself, because the only evidence of a person stopping is a record of who stopped, that a record of who stopped is a column, and that the item as drafted asks a question with one answer. One sheet, a heading, a paragraph, and a line at the foot saying the item was noted, with a date and nothing else. The record is a committee's and is not ours; no page of it is in this file and none will be.**

A chair of forty-nine whose office signs about eleven of a kind a year is called Ilse Nordahl; she has an ordinary job, is not a post, and her name was said out loud in a district office and not written down. A secondment of about a term's standing. One fault, one sheet, one paragraph, one line at the foot, one date, one hand, and the hand is not the officer of sixty's. Sources of the fault's being on the record: one, and it is a person standing in a room she is seconded into; the only thing keeping the note where it is is that nobody in that building has been told she is in it. In chalk, in about four minutes, in an ordinary hand, by a woman of sixty-eight who has chalk in her coat, laid over the old firm's name and not over the top of it.
