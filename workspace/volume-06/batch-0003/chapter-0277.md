# Chapter 277 — Who Is Going To Be Standing There

The Monday of week ninety-seven was the day a room stopped asking whether a printed rule is right and started asking who is going to be standing next to a person who has not come back yet, and a woman of about thirty-four rang the number on a plate at about half past four and asked whether anybody knew where a man was, and the answer the room arrived at was a person on a rota with a card in date, and no rota was made and no post was invented and no list was written and nobody was thanked for the answer.

---

Ten on the docket and nine in date, and the stair fire door in Nine Bridges was not reported and has not been chased, and the standing water in a basement in an unnamed district was not reported either.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its ninety-eighth day and it is two hundred and three days old.

---

The telephone on the plate rang at about half past four on the Monday afternoon and a woman of about thirty-four said that she had come back to a landing in a block in Nine Bridges at about ten past four and found a man sitting on the bottom step outside a flat, and that he had asked her whether anybody knew where a man who works nights was, and that she had said no, and that he had said it was all right, and that she had come to the room because she had been in it about a term ago and it was the only place in this city she knew.

He had not said a name. She did not know one and said so twice.

Nobody in that room was rude to her. About nine people in that room knew the shape of what she was describing and about four of them looked at each other and about five did not.

---

A first-year of nineteen said: "**How long.**"

"**He said four. He said he finishes at five and he would be in at five and he would rather somebody was in his building.**"

"**And it is not five.**"

"**It is not five and I am not going to sit on a step in a block in Nine Bridges until five, and I want somebody to know he is on that step and I do not want to be the person who has to make that decision alone on a Monday afternoon, and that is the whole of why I rang.**"

Nobody asked her to sit down and nobody asked her anything else and she left at about twenty to five and nobody thanked her and she is owed nothing.

---

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in that room and had been since about two, and he said the thing that changed the shape of the afternoon, and it took him about four seconds.

"**Then the answer to that is a person on a rota with a card in date, and there is a rota and it is the Friday one and it has been running for years, and I do not know anybody on it.**"

Somebody said, "**How can you not know that.**"

"**Because it is in a system in a company in another district and it is a list of about nine names a week and a card on a wall, and I have been in four hundred buildings a person cannot get out of and nine hundred they can and I have never once seen it, and neither has anybody in this room, and that is not a failure of that rota. That is what a rota is.**"

---

A man of thirty-four came in at about ten past seven and had been on since one and had a torch on the counter and did not pick it up, and about four people in that room had been not worrying about him for about two and a half hours, and one of them is the man of twenty-two and he was the one who had done most of the not worrying.

He was told about the call after he had been in for about four minutes. Nobody made a joke about it. Nobody said anything about the fact that he is the only person in this case who can be found at a door.

"**A man on a step at ten past four, waiting for me, because nobody in that building knows I exist until I am standing in it.**"

Nobody said yes to that and nobody said no to it.

"**I wrote a line about that eight days ago. It is on that board. It has got my handwriting nowhere on it and there is a space where a name would go and about four people in this room know what it says.**"

"**It says you are the one who can be found at a door.**"

"**It says that. And in about four years it is going to say it in a photograph of that board, and in about four years a man is going to be standing on a step in nine hundred buildings instead of one, and none of them will be him, because he will be the one who is findable, which is a completely different thing, and this city has about one of those and I am it.**"

---

Then Talia Venn, who is twenty-four and who had been in that room for about two hours and had not said anything, said the sentence that is the whole of the change, and it was one sentence, and she said it to the room and not to him, and she did not give her name to anybody in that room and about nine people in it already had it.

"**The question is not whether the rule is right. The question is who is going to be standing next to a person who has not come back yet, and everybody in this room has been asking the first question for about four years because the first question has an answer and the second one has a person in it.**"

Nobody thanked her for that. Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her a fifth question, because there has never been a fifth, and she did not volunteer a sixth, and she is owed nothing and holds no position in any of it and has not asked for one.

---

A cook of twenty-seven came out of the back and said the other half, and it was about nine words and she has said four sentences of that shape in two volumes and has said she would not say a fifth and has now said five and did not notice.

"**It is a rota, then. Not a man.**"

Nobody said that was not what had been said. About four people in that room wanted to say that it was, and about four of them had a reason, and none of the four reasons was about a rota and all four of them were about a person.

---

A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts said one thing and it was the last thing anybody said about it.

"**I am on a rota. It is a Tuesday. It has been a Tuesday for about nine years and nobody has ever asked me to be on it and nobody has ever rung to say thank you, and I get into about four hundred buildings a person cannot get out of and I do not bring anybody out of them, and if the person who wrote the rota in that system is honest with himself then the rota is the answer and the rota has been the answer for about nine years and none of us has ever stood next to it.**"

"**You have never said that before.**"

"**No. I have said the other half for about four years, which is the list, and the list is a different sentence and I do have a reason for that one and this is not it.**"

---

A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip was in the corner and said the one practical thing anybody said, and it is the whole of what a person in this case can do with a rota in about four minutes.

"**Then somebody has to have a piece of paper with nine names and a date on it and a room on it, and that is about nine lines, and none of you are going to write it, and I want it said out loud that none of you are going to write it.**"

"**Nobody is going to write it.**"

"**I have been in this room about a term and I have seen about nine people get their hands towards a sheet of paper and about nine of them did not, and I am the one who says it out loud, and I am not going to say it again.**"

Nobody thanked him. He was not thanked then and he was not thanked on the Friday a fortnight before, in a district office in another district, for the other thing he said, and he has not been thanked in about a term and a half and does not expect to be.

---

At about nine the shutter came down and the man of twenty-two went out to a service road and stood up in the wind and worked out, in about nine seconds, the thing he had not worked out in about six volumes.

A rota is a promise made in advance by a person who cannot be reached. He has spent four years telling about nine rooms that being the person who can be found at a door is the trap and not the answer, and he was right about that and wrong about one thing, and the thing he was wrong about was that he thought being findable was the only way a person ends up next to somebody who has not come back. A man on a Tuesday for about nine years is next to nobody. A woman of thirty-six with a lanyard on a Friday is next to about nine people a week. A cook at a sink in a cold room at two in the morning is next to whoever walks in.

He wrote that down. He did not improve on it and he did not say it to anybody and he has not said it to anybody since.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.
