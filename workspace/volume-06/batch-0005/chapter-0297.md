# Chapter 297 — He Said It Out Loud

The Friday of week one hundred and two was the second Friday rota of the last ten days and it ran in a company training room at the end of a passage in Crown Terrace with nine folding chairs in it, and the cards have been out of use for about fifteen weeks, and nothing at all was reported to that room between about ten o'clock on the Thursday morning and about seven o'clock on the Friday evening, and a man of twenty-two said out loud, in front of about nine people, the sentence he has been carrying since the spring before the one before last, and nothing was done about it, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Three jobs attended, one in a district this case has not been in, and nothing was reported by anybody in three districts between about ten o'clock on the Thursday morning and about seven o'clock on the Friday evening, which is the first clear day anybody in this case has had since the Monday.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on and it costs more. The room is two hundred and forty-three days old.

---

The thing stopped on its own and nobody in that room was told anything about it, because nobody in that room knew anything about it.

A man of thirty-four who buys training for a company had gone back to work on the Thursday afternoon. A woman of thirty-four who had been going to about nine buildings in a day had gone back to about four hundred doors on her own time on the Friday. A first-year of nineteen had gone to a college. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts had gone to nine buildings and found that nine lifts came up when they were called.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in that training room on the Friday at about half past five, on his way to a stair door in a second district which he opened at about eleven the next morning, and he said in a doorway, to nobody, that he did not know why it had stopped, and that he had a stair door to do on Monday, and that those two facts had nothing to do with each other.

Nobody asked him why. He is owed nothing and nobody has ever asked him anything twice.

---

Eleven on the week's list and about four of the eleven not on it, which is the standing figure. Four runs, faster, with nothing in anybody's hands, all four ending on a spoken word that has never been on a card and never will be. He was in the fourth row against the wall.

Somebody said, at about half past six, in about nine people, that the number of people who had come to that room in the last two Fridays was twice what it had been in the spring, and that something about a Friday had changed and that somebody ought to write down what it was.

The instructor of thirty-six with the lanyard said no to that in about four seconds, and she said it the way she says everything, which is once.

"**Nobody is writing down what it was, because what it was is that about eleven people came, and about eleven people is not a finding, and the day we have a finding about a Friday is the day this room becomes a thing that can be quoted.**"

"**You do not think it is a thing worth finding out about.**"

"**I think it is a thing worth finding out about and I think finding out about it is how about four hundred people in about four years are going to be told that they have to come on a Friday at six o'clock, and I would like to be wrong about that and I have no way of finding out whether I am.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody improved on her. She said the word at the end of every run the same as she always has.

---

And then, at about twenty to seven, before the last run, with about nine people in that room and the door shut and a young man of twenty-one near the door who had not said anything for two hours, he said it out loud.

He had it in about four seconds, as he has on every Friday of the year for about four years, and it was about eleven words and it took him about eleven seconds and he said it to a company training room and not to a person.

"**About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I have been that man in about nine buildings for four years and I have never once been in the room, and I have said that number in a room about four hundred times and I have said it to about four people who are not in this room, and it is on the back of a card in a drawer in a flat in Nine Bridges and it is on no form in this city, and I am not going to put it on one.**"

"**You have said it to about four of us.**"

"**I have said it to one of you, in this city, and she is not in this room.**"

"**Then that is twice.**"

"**That is twice, and one of the two was in a room off a service road about a fortnight ago, and she told me what to do with it, and I did not ask her to, and I am not going to tell anybody what she told me.**"

"**Why are you saying it here.**"

"**Because I have been in about four of these rooms since the spring, and on the Friday of a week in the winter I found out that I am the four seconds, and on the Thursday I watched four people run four things that I did not teach them, and this morning nothing happened anywhere, and there is one sentence in my head that has been true for four years and this is the room it has been waiting for and I have been saving it for about four years and I have just spent it.**"

"**And now what.**"

"**And now it is in a room, and about four of you have heard me say it before, and about four of you have not, and none of you is going to do anything about it, and I would like that to be on the record as my expectation rather than as a criticism.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody wrote it down. Nobody acted on it inside about four seconds, inside about four minutes, or inside about four days. The card with the number on the back of it is in a drawer in a flat in Nine Bridges and it is not going to be on a form and he has said so out loud in a room, which is the first thing in six years that has changed about it and the change is that about nine people now know he thinks it should be on one.

That is the whole of it and it is not a fix and it was never going to be a fix, and he said so himself before anybody could have said it for him, and about four people in that room noticed that he had said it first and nobody said anything about that either.

---

On the last run a woman of thirty-four in the second row, four years on that list, reached the end of a hold and stopped and finished and there was no gap at all, and the room came apart at about half past seven.

Somebody said, at the door, that being on that list now made a person part of something.

"**A person is not a post and a list is not a room. The difference is that a room you can leave at seven o'clock and a list you have to explain at a counter.**"

"**You have to explain it now.**"

"**I did not sign anything. There is a card in a box on a table and it is the company's, and it has been there about four years, and I have never once asked anybody in this room what they are waiting for.**"

"**And you still have not.**"

"**And I still have not, and if somebody here wants to ask it then somebody here has to want to ask it, and I am not going to make it easier by being right about it.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody asked it. She has not been asked it twice and is not to be.

---

At about half past nine in a room off a service road there were about nine people and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard. The figure is not a rate. It is on the back of a woman's own card and it is on no form, and it was said out loud once in a public room in the six years this case has been running, on a Friday, by the man it is about, and nothing came of it and nobody was thanked and the number is not smaller this morning than it was on the Thursday.

The shutter came down at about ten.

---

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the dark, at about ten, standing up.

*300. Friday of week 102, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one rota, one sentence, and no change.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; three jobs attended, one of them in a district this case has not been in; nothing reported by anybody in three districts between about ten o'clock on the Thursday morning and about seven o'clock on the Friday evening, which is the first clear day anybody in this case has had since the Monday. The rate held at nine and ten and reached eleven on no hour of it. The heating in the room off the service road is on and the days-off series is closed and no figure of days is carried forward. The room is two hundred and forty-three days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is two hundred and forty-seven days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that training room. The twelve lines on the hardboard are a hundred and sixty-three days old, the thirteen a hundred and fourteen, the fourteen seventy-nine, the fifteen fifty-eight, the sixteen thirty-three, and the seventeen thirteen. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread and unchanged. The wall is seventeen lines in eleven hands and twelve names, the first line a cook's and still the first, no line removed in this run of days and none rubbed off.**

*An inventory, kept short on purpose.* **Nothing was reported for about a day and a half and nobody in this case knows why. Four people went back to what they do: a man of thirty-four who buys training to his own company; a woman of thirty-four to about four hundred doors on her own time, unpaid; a first-year of nineteen to a college; a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts to nine buildings, where nine lifts came up when they were called. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag said in a doorway that he did not know why it stopped, that he had a stair door on Monday, and that those two facts had nothing to do with each other, and nobody asked him why. The reason is not stated in this entry. It is not known, and this run of days does not say it, and the not saying is the whole of what the Monday and the Thursday and this Friday add up to.**

*The rota, which is a rota.* **A company training room at the end of a passage in Crown Terrace. Nine folding chairs, a laminated sheet on a post belonging to the company and about four years old, a card in a box on a table belonging to the company. Eleven on the week's list, about four of the eleven not on it, cards out of use for about fifteen weeks, four runs, all four ending on a spoken word never on a card. A woman of thirty-six with a lanyard, present for all four, who refused in about four seconds the proposal that somebody write down why about twice as many people had come to that room in the last two Fridays, on the ground that about eleven people is not a finding and that the day this room has a finding about a Friday is the day it becomes a thing that can be quoted; who said she thinks it is worth finding out about; and who said she would like to be wrong about that and has no way of finding out whether she is. Nobody thanked her, nobody improved on her, and she has not been asked about it since. On the last run there was no gap at all and the room came apart at about half past seven.**

*The sentence, and what it did.* **Said out loud once, in a public room, in front of about nine people, at about twenty to seven, before the last run, to a room and not to a person, in the words he used, which were that *About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard.* He had it in about four seconds, as he has on every Friday for about four years, and it took about eleven seconds. He then said that he has been that man in about nine buildings for four years and has never once been in the room, that he has said that number in a room about four hundred times and to about four people who are not in that room, that the figure is on the back of a card in a drawer in a flat in Nine Bridges and is on no form in this city, and that he is not going to put it on one. Asked how many had heard it in that room, he said one, in this city, and that she is not in that room; told that this made it twice, he agreed, said that the other was in a room off a service road about a fortnight ago, that she told him what to do with it, that he did not ask her to, and that he is not going to tell anybody what she told him. Asked why he was saying it in that room, he gave four reasons: that he has been in about four of those rooms since the spring; that on a Friday in the winter he found out that he is the four seconds; that on the Thursday he watched four people run four things he did not teach them; and that nothing happened anywhere that morning. Asked what comes next, he said that about nine people now know he thinks it should be on a form, and that he wanted that to be his expectation and not a criticism. Nobody thanked him, nobody wrote it down, nobody acted on it in about four seconds, in about four minutes or in about four days, and the figure is not smaller this morning than it was on the Thursday. This is the whole of the use the figure gets in this run of days and it is not a fix, and it is the first time in six years the number has been said out loud in a room, and a reader is entitled to know that hearing it is not the same as it having done anything.**

*A person is not a post.* **Asked in a doorway at about half past seven by a person leaving, whether being on that list now makes a person part of something, the instructor of thirty-six said that a person is not a post, that a list is not a room, that the difference is that a room can be left at seven o'clock and a list has to be explained at a counter, that she did not sign anything and that the card in the box on the table is the company's and about four years old; and that she has never once asked anybody in that room what they are waiting for. Asked whether she still has not, she said that she has not, that if somebody in that room wants to ask it then somebody in that room has to want to ask it, and that she is not going to make it easier for them by being right about it. Nobody asked it. Nobody thanked her. A young man of twenty-one near the door had not spoken for two hours and did not speak after this and is not the woman of twenty-one in another room in another district and is not the man of fifty-one with a bad wrist and is a third person.**

*The shape of a Friday night.* **About nine people in a warm room off a service road at about half past nine, with a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door from about two until ten, described by a count, not asked anything, not thanked, not apologised to, and still the person a fortnight of unpaid heating was paid for. A cook of twenty-seven at a sink, silent. No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution was created by anybody in this case, and the one new object in this run of days is a line of handwriting on an existing hardboard that has not been written yet. No panel and no marker. No relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded; the machine in a basement in a building in Nine Bridges is a recording machine that has been stuck for about four years and nobody went near it. The plate in a service cupboard on a landing in Nine Bridges is not explained, not removed and not blamed on anybody, and the man of thirty-four who found it is not named here and is not to be named. No message arrived from anybody. No authorship identified by anybody. He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception. Nine keys are needed and two exist, said here and in no room; the first cannot be asked back by anybody, the second is in the pocket of a woman of thirty-one, and the answer to the question on the back of a docket is on nothing. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. The master's fifth line is still blank in the fourth pocket of a tool roll and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in. Six surfaces — the board over a door, the hardboard, the wall list in its plastic sleeve, a sheet of about two hundred and forty lines in a building in Crown Terrace, a day room next door that is shut, and a door in Nine Bridges with nothing on it — and none merged with another here. The standing question is not stated here, carries no number, and no rule about who may be asked was invented. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 300.**
