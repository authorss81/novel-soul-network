# Chapter 293 — Settled For One Friday

The Friday of week one hundred and one was the second Friday rota in a fortnight and it ran in a company training room at the end of a passage in Crown Terrace with nine folding chairs in it and a laminated sheet on a post, and the cards have been out of use for about twelve weeks, and there was a gap of about four seconds on the last run, and the reason for it was worked out at about half past eight by one man and told to nobody, and it was not the reason anybody in that room would have wanted.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. A job in a district this case has not been in was attended at about eleven and one address in Nine Bridges was not attended and has not been chased.

The word was at nine and ten, and at ten a minute by the time the room came apart. The heating came back on in the winter and it costs more, and the room it is in is two hundred and thirty-five days old.

---

Eleven on the week's list and about four of the eleven not on it, which is the standing figure and has been the standing figure for about a term. About nine hundred of that company's cards came out of use in the spring across about eleven sites in about four days, correctly, and they went into a bag in a storeroom and there is nothing to hand out and there has been nothing to hand out for about twelve weeks.

The room ends on a spoken word. It has ended on that word about four hundred times and the word is not on anything and is never going to be, and there is no card in that room and no box on the table and nothing in anybody's hands.

---

He was in the fourth row against the wall with the word available and about four people in that room knew that, and nobody had said it out loud and nobody was going to.

On the first three runs it did not come up, because it does not come up on a run where everybody finishes when they mean to.

On the last run the woman of about thirty-four in the second row, four years on that list, reached the end of the hold and stopped and finished, and there was a gap.

About four seconds. Everybody in that room heard it. About four of them noticed. Nobody said anything, and about four of them have now been not saying something about it for six Fridays.

"**You have been not saying something about it for six Fridays.**"

"That is a different sentence and you have both of them."

"**I know which one I have.**"

---

He worked it out at about half past eight, outside, on a step, with his coat on, and he said it out loud to a step, which is a thing he has done about four thousand times.

"**The gap is me.**"

Not hers. Not the fourth row's. His.

He sat on that step and did the arithmetic about four times and it came out the same way each time, and there is no other arithmetic available for it because there are only two people in that room who could have filled that gap and one of them said the word and it was not early and it was not late, and the other one did not say it.

---

He did not tell the instructor of thirty-six. He did not tell anybody in that room. He did not tell the man of about forty-four, who was not on the list and came anyway, and who has offered the reason for the gap three times in three weeks and in three weeks before that, and who was not asked for it and was not to be asked for it a third time.

He did not say it on a service road that night either, and that was the first time in about four years he had not said a thing on a service road, and he noticed himself not doing it and did not go back and do it.

What he wrote down instead was this, and it is in the entry below in his own words, and it is the whole of what came of the six Fridays:

*It is me. It is not the room and it is not her and it is not the cards and I have been the four seconds since about the spring before last. If I say the word on a Friday I stop it being the thing about a man in a lanyard, and then it is a thing about a word, and I have spent four years being the reason it is a thing about a man in a lanyard. I do not know whether that is a finding or an excuse and I have been sitting on this step about eleven minutes and I am not going to find out tonight.*

---

The instructor said one thing out loud to that room at about twenty past eight, and she said it to the room and not to him, and about nine people heard it and nobody thanked her.

"**Nobody in here is a post. I am not a post. I have a lanyard and a card and about eleven years of a Friday, and if this room stops on a Friday because I am not in it then this room was never running. It was standing.**"

"Then what was it doing."

"**It was doing what it does, which is four things in about nine minutes, and the four things do not need me and I would like that noticed before somebody in this building gives me a title.**"

Not one of them thanked her and not one of them wrote a word of it down. She said the word at the end of every run the same as she always has, and she has never once asked anybody in that room what they are waiting for, and a woman of twenty-three asked it in there in about four weeks and she heard the question and did not hear the answer.

---

The man of about forty-four was in that room for the whole of it and is not on the list and has not been for about a year, and he has been on it for about nine years before that.

He was asked nothing. He gave nothing. He did the whole run, and about eleven minutes before the end he sat down on a chair by the door and stayed there until the room came apart, and he has done that three times now and nobody has ever asked him what the chair is for.

Nobody asked him. Nobody has ever asked him.

---

Half past nine in a room off a service road, and about nine people in it, and the room warm. A woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door with a cup of tea in front of her and in it since about two. A cook of twenty-seven at the sink who had said nothing to anybody all evening, which was the twelfth time in about a fortnight.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard. The figure is on the back of a woman's own card in a flat in Nine Bridges, it is on no form, it is not a rate, and it is not on the laminated sheet on the post in a company training room and never has been.

Nothing was done about it on that Friday. Nothing is going to be done about it by anybody in that room, and the man who has the figure in his head in about four seconds on every Friday of the year did not say it, and has not said it out loud in a room in six years, and said it once to one person in the winter and cannot get it back.

The shutter came down at about ten and the last person through it put a chair up on the table.

---

He wrote one thing on a service road that night, standing up, in the dark, at about ten, and he had not eaten and did not notice that either.

*296. Friday of week 101, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one gap, one step, and one Friday.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; one job in a district this case has not been in attended at about eleven; one address in Nine Bridges not attended, unreported and not chased, and the not chasing is attributed to nothing. The rate held at nine and ten across the whole day and reached eleven on no hour of it. The heating in the room off the service road is on, restored by a landlord, and the days-off series is closed and no figure of days is carried forward. The room is two hundred and thirty-five days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is two hundred and thirty-nine days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a company training room. The twelve lines on the hardboard are a hundred and fifty-five days old, the thirteen a hundred and six, the fourteen seventy-one, the fifteen fifty, the sixteen twenty-five, and the seventeen seven. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread and unchanged. The wall is seventeen lines in eleven hands and twelve names, the first line a cook's and is still the first, and no line has been taken off that board and none has been rubbed at.**

*The cards, and the sheet on the post.* **About nine hundred of that company's cards came out of use across about eleven sites in about four days in the spring, correctly, on a printed standard read off a wall binder by a clerk of forty-four, and they are in a bag in a storeroom. They have now been out of use for about twelve weeks. Four runs were done on the Friday, faster, with nothing in anybody's hands, and all four ended on a spoken word that has never been on a card. Eleven on the week's list and about four of the eleven not on it, which is the standing figure for about a term. A laminated sheet on a post in that room is the company's, is about four years old, predates this case, and carries none of this entry's material; the figure of about eleven a week is on the back of a woman's own card in a drawer in a flat in Nine Bridges and is on no form and is not a rate.**

*The gap, and the one Friday it was settled on.* **About four seconds, on the last run only, after the woman of about thirty-four in the second row, four years on that list, reached the end of a hold and stopped and finished. Every person in the room heard it, about four noticed, and nobody said anything, and about four have now been not saying something about it for six Fridays. Worked out at about half past eight by one man on a step outside: the length of the gap is the length of a person deciding not to say a word, the two people in that room who could have filled it are the one who said the word and said it neither early nor late, and the one in the fourth row against the wall who did not say it. The reason is him. It was told to nobody, not to the instructor, not to the room, not to the man of about forty-four, and not on a service road, and that last is the first time in about four years he has not said a thing on a service road. Whether that is a finding or an excuse was not settled on the step and was not settled afterwards. The question of the four seconds has been open for about four months and is closed as of this entry for one Friday, and a later week is not to re-open it and is not to call it solved, and the man of about forty-four is still short of a reason and is not to be asked for one again. Counts: one gap, one step, about eleven minutes, one sentence written down and not said, and zero thanks.**

*The instructor of thirty-six, and the word post.* **Present for all four runs and said the word at the end of every one of them, as she has for about eleven years. Out loud, to the room, at about twenty past eight, and to nobody in particular: that nobody in that room is a post, that she is not a post, that she has a lanyard and a card and about eleven years of a Friday, and that if the room stops on a Friday because she is not in it then the room was never running and was only standing. Asked what it was doing, she said it was doing what it does, which is four things in about nine minutes, and that the four things do not need her, and that she would like that noticed before somebody in that building gives her a title. Nobody thanked her, nobody wrote it down, and nobody has given her a title. She has never once asked anybody in that room what they are waiting for, and a woman of twenty-three did ask it once, on her first attendance, in about four weeks, and she heard the question and did not hear the answer. A person with an ordinary job is not a post and a lanyard is not a post and neither is the man in it.**

*The man of about forty-four, who was asked nothing and gave nothing.* **Present for the whole of it. Nine years on that list, not on it for about a year, and one of the four of the eleven who is not on it. Did the whole run and then sat on a chair by the door from about eleven minutes before the end until the room came apart, which is the third time he has done that and which nobody in that room has ever asked him about. He offered the reason for the gap three times in three weeks before this and was not offered it back and is not to be asked a third time by anybody, including the man who worked it out. Nobody thanked him, nobody asked him a question, and he gave nothing, and giving nothing is what he has done three times.**

*The shape of the evening.* **About nine people in a warm room off a service road at about half past nine, with a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door from about two until ten, described by a count, not asked anything, not thanked, not apologised to, and still the person a fortnight of unpaid heating was paid for. A cook of twenty-seven at a sink who has said five sentences of one shape and has said she will not say a sixth, and who said nothing at all for the twelfth time in about a fortnight. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag not present. One stair light in Nine Bridges not attended. The heating is on and costs more and the number of names that now cost more is four, and the fortnight that a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and unresolved and was not mentioned in that room and is not to be mentioned in a later one. No fund, collection, subscription, grant, list, minute, notice, leaflet, registration, appeal or form was proposed on that evening by anybody and none exists near it.**

*Boundaries.* **No panel and no marker. No relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded, and the machine in a basement in a building in Nine Bridges is a recording machine stuck for about four years that nobody went near. No message from anybody, and no arrival of any kind. No authorship of anything identified by anybody, and the one identification this case holds was made by the person it concerns and by nobody else. He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing on that Friday, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception; the condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased. Nine keys are needed and two exist, said in this entry and in no room; the first cannot be asked back by anybody and the second is in the pocket of a woman of thirty-one, and the answer to the question on the back of a docket is on nothing. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. The master's fifth line is still blank and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in. The board over a door, the hardboard on two nails, a wall list in its plastic sleeve in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, a sheet of about two hundred and forty lines on a wall in a building in Crown Terrace, a day room next door that is shut, and a door in Nine Bridges with nothing on it are six objects and none has been merged with another here. The standing question is not stated in this entry, carries no number, and no rule about who may be asked was invented; the question the gap raises is about four seconds and not about a person. Entry 296.**
