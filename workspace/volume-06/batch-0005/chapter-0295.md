# Chapter 295 — She Described The Room

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and two was the morning about nine people sat in a room in a building in Crown Terrace and a woman of twenty-four described, in her own words, a converted shop unit off a service road that has a plate on the outside of it carrying a telephone number and no name, and a man of twenty-two sat in that room and did not speak and is findable about four things, and a temporary consent standard was adopted in about four minutes and no page of it exists, and an audit was ordered that is nine people in rooms with a list, and a printed rule was not destroyed, and nobody thanked anybody.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on and it costs more. The room is two hundred and forty days old.

The room was on a floor with about nine other rooms on it and it was not a chamber and nothing in it had ever been on a platform. Nine chairs, a table, a window that does not open, a radiator older than anybody in that room, and a jug of water filled at about eight by somebody whose job it is to fill jugs of water.

Ilse Nordahl is forty-nine and her office signs about eleven of a kind a year, and she had an ordinary coat on and a folder and no badge, and a person with an ordinary coat is not a post.

She opened at nine and said four things, and this is the whole of what she said before anybody else got a word in.

"**This is not a hearing. Nothing here is going to be decided and I am not going to allow anybody in this room to pretend that it will be. We are going to hear one thing and we are going to adopt one thing and we are going to order one thing, and the one thing we are going to order is the least interesting of the three.**"

Somebody asked what the one thing was.

"**That is why we have to get through the other two first.**"

---

The woman of twenty-four went first, and she had asked to go first, and the reason she gave for asking was about eleven seconds long and nobody improved on it.

"**I have been inside a practice for a term and a half. I am the only person in this room who has been inside one. If I go second, then everything anybody says about it is a description of a description, and by about four o'clock this afternoon we will be four removes from anybody who has done it.**"

Then she described the room, in her own words, for about eleven minutes, and she did not read anything.

She described the shutter, about nine chairs of which two lean, a counter with nothing on it, a kettle, a plug socket that works, and a bell-push by the door that does not ring by itself. She said that a room with a bell-push is a room with a bell-push and not a building.

She described the hardboard on two nails, the size of a tea tray, with seventeen lines on it in eleven hands and twelve names, and said that the first line is a cook's and is still the first line and that nobody has ever taken one off it.

She described the plate outside, which carries a letting agent's telephone number and no name at all, and she said what it has been used for.

"**About eleven times in about a term, people arrived at that door having read the number off the outside of it, and about four of them had something they wanted to leave, and about four of them wanted to know whether they could leave something, and about four of them wanted to leave and be told afterwards that they had, and there is no version of that door that is not a door somebody can knock on.**"

Somebody asked her what a room like that was for, and she said the rest of it, and it is the part that went in, and nobody thanked her for any of it.

"**It is for one person at a time to say out loud what they are not going to do, and for one other person to hear it and not write it down. That is the whole of it. There are seventeen lines on that wall and every one of them was written by somebody who had to say the cost of it out loud first, and not one of the seventeen is enforceable against anybody who did not write it, and any one of them can be lifted with a thumb in front of nine people. I have been inside a practice for a term and a half and I have never once been asked to show anybody my own card, and the only person who ever asked me about it asked me in a passage in about nine seconds, and those two things are one sentence and not an accident.**"

Then she said the thing she had come to say, in about nine seconds, and the six words in the middle of it are the reason a campaign for a right has to end up in a piece of paper, and she said them once and did not say them again.

"**If you want a right to take your share back, you have to be able to say it in words that a stranger can put on a form, and the words a stranger can put on a form are patient identifiers, if available, and I have never in my life been in a room where the words that matter were patient identifiers, if available, and every single one of us has been in about four of those rooms, and a form with those words on it is a correct form and it is the only reason I am standing here, and it is going to be the only reason anybody can say this out loud in about four years.**"

Nobody said anything. A person of sixty whose job is to ask questions looked at her for a while and then at a printed slip on the table and did not say what she was thinking.

---

A man of twenty-two sat at the far end of the table with his hands on his knees and had not said a word.

A first-year of nineteen was in that room because a room of this kind is the only room in this city a person of nineteen can be in without anybody making a thing of it, and she had been in it for about twenty minutes, and she said nine words to him at about half past ten, across a table, in front of about nine people, and it was nine words.

"**Not one person in that room can stop you.**"

He did not answer it. Nobody answered it. She did not improve on it then and she has not improved on it since, and he did not ask her to say it again and nobody else asked either.

He had been asked, in a passage outside a room in an earlier run of days, to run a procedure once in a room with the people in it, and he had said no in about nine seconds, and that is closed. This morning he was in a room and he was not speaking, and that is all it was. It is not the condition and it is not a refusal, nobody in that room described it as either, and nobody in that room knew there was anything to describe.

Then the chair of forty-nine asked the only question she asked all morning, and it was a small one, and she put it to the room and not to him.

"**Nobody has heard from this man. How many people in this city could have told me where he was at four o'clock yesterday afternoon.**"

The clerk of forty-four answered it, and she has been in that secretariat on the floor below for about a term and a half and nobody in that building has been told she is in it, and she answered in about nine seconds and it was a count and not an argument.

"**Four. A plate on the outside of a unit off a service road with a telephone number on it and no name, which is a letting agent's and has been used about eleven times in a term. A stair fire door on a docket with a wrong number in a box in a box in a building in Nine Bridges, the eighth time in about a month. Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**Thank you.**"

"**You are welcome. I did not enjoy it and you did not ask me to.**"

The chair did not improve on it and did not turn it into a second question. She wrote nothing down and said she was not going to, and said that a person who is findable four ways is a person a room has to be careful about, and that the way to be careful about such a person is not to stop him being findable, and did not say what the way was, and was not asked.

The Continuity Office was there in a coat that has never been in a workshop, and its man is fifty-eight, and he has emergency authority and he kept it and nobody took it off him and he did not give it up. He said four sentences and every one of them was a sentence a competent person says.

"**The authority stays. I am not here to give it up and nobody in this room has the power to take it, and I would like the four people in this room who have been hoping for nine days to hear that from a person whose job it is rather than from a rumour.**"

Somebody said that was the wrong thing to say.

"**It is the only true thing anybody is going to say this morning and it is not the wrong thing, it is the first thing. I am right about one other thing and I am going to say that one too and then I am going to sit down.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**A standard adopted as temporary, with no date on it, is a standard that is in force until somebody with a stamp puts a date on it. That has happened twice in this city in nineteen years. The first temporary thing outlasted the permanent thing it replaced by about six years and nobody noticed until it was over.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked him for a source. Nobody thanked him. A first-year of nineteen wrote it on the back of her own hand and then did not do anything with it, and the chair of forty-nine looked at her and did not ask to see it.

He sat down and did not speak again for an hour and about ten minutes, and he was not thanked when it was over, and he agreed to nothing, and he was not defeated and not turned, and nobody in this case has asked him to be anything.

The temporary consent standard was adopted at about twenty past eleven in about four minutes and it exists in about nine heads and nowhere else.

It is that a person may end their part of a practice at any point, without a form and without giving a reason, and that the only record of it is that somebody who was in the room knows it, and that nothing about it goes into any system of any kind. She said the whole of it in about nine seconds and the chair of forty-nine adopted it in about four minutes and asked three questions, all of them smaller than the one before.

Nobody signed anything. There is no copy, no form, no minute, no register, and no page anywhere in this city that records that this happened.

"**Say that back to me.**"

"**There is no copy of it.**"

"**There is no copy of it and in about four years somebody is going to ask me for a copy and I am going to say that I do not have one, and that is going to be the correct thing to do and it is also going to be a thing about nine of us have to do on our own.**"

"**That is what temporary means, and about four of the nine of us have been in this city long enough to know that temporary has meant permanent twice.**"

Nobody thanked her. She did not thank him. She said his name to nobody in that room, and he has not spoken.
