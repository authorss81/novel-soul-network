# Chapter 291 — The Practical Half

The Monday morning of week one hundred and one was the day a man of twenty-two worked out that the only thing he had to put in front of a room in Crown Terrace was a set of about nine things that about nine other people had already done, and that he was not one of the nine, and a first-year of nineteen gave him the reason for that in nine words she had given him before in a colder room and did not improve on it, and he asked for the practical half and was given it, and the list is on nothing at all except one line in a book a man keeps against himself on a service road, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Two jobs were in a district this case has not been in, and a stair fire door in Nine Bridges came up for the eighth time in about a month and was attended and expected back, and a man with a clipboard in a building in Crown Terrace asked him, on the Monday morning, whether he was from the company, and he said that he was not, and the man wrote that down.

The word was at nine and ten and got to eleven on no hour of it. The heating in that room has been on since a landlord put it there and it costs more than it did, and the room itself is two hundred and thirty-one days old, and neither figure is about the other.

---

The question had come to the room a fortnight before, on the telephone, from a person of sixty whose job is to ask questions, and she had asked it plainly: whether there was anything in that room that could be put in front of a body on a Wednesday morning, and whether the person she was asking would be the person who said it.

He had said that he would find out and that he would not be the person who said it, and she had said that was the answer she expected and had not sounded disappointed, and neither of them had written anything down, because she does not take minutes and he does not keep a page of what people say to him.

What she wanted, when the room answered, was a thing that was not his.

He had known that on the Monday morning at about eight, standing at a counter in a building in Crown Terrace with a docket in his pocket, and he had known what it was by about half past eleven, and he had spent the rest of the day not writing it down on purpose, and the not writing was a decision and not a difficulty.

---

He did the list in his head on the bus and again in a stairwell and again at about half past four in a room off a service road with his coat still on, and about nine people were in it by half past six and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two.

There were about nine things. He knew all nine and he knew who had done each of them and in which order, and he had not said any of them out loud.

Somebody asked him what he was doing.

"Standing about."

"You have been standing about for an hour and you have not had a cup of tea."

"I have been deciding whether to say a thing."

"Say it or do not. You are the worst person in this city at the middle of that."

He said the shape of it, and not the nine.

"**There is a set of things. It is about nine of them. Every one of them was done by somebody else and I watched and I was not in it. That is the whole of what I have.**"

Somebody said that was not an answer.

"**It is not an answer. It is the reason there is going to be one.**"

---

Then the first-year of nineteen, who was at the counter with a cloth and had been listening for about an hour, said nine words to him across nine feet of floor.

"**Nobody should have to find you to stop me.**"

She had said it to him about four months ago, in that room, on a Monday, in the cold, and it had taken a piece of the only thing he owns off him and gone and washed up. Nobody in that room said anything now. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink with her back to the room and did not turn round.

"You have said that before."

"I have."

"Do you want a better one."

"No."

She did not improve on it. Nobody improved on it. He stood there and let about nine people watch him not have an answer, and that is the second time in about four months and it went the same way.

---

He asked her for the practical half, and she came round the counter and stood in front of him, which she had done once.

"**What do I do with it in that room.**"

"You say nine things."

"And I am standing at the back."

"**You are standing at the back and you are not standing in front of anything and there are about nine people in that room and one of them has a job that is asking questions and she has already told you she does not expect you to be the one who says it.**"

"**And if somebody in that room asks me a question.**"

"**Then you say the name of the person whose work it is and not your own, and if it is one of the nine then you say whose, and if it is not one of the nine then you say that it is not one of the nine and you will go and find out whose it is and you will come back with it. That is all. It takes about eleven minutes and you will be the only person in that room who has an answer to a question nobody else in there has an answer to.**"

He waited for the rest of it, because there is always a rest of it with her.

"That is the practical half."

"That is the practical half."

"**I have never once been asked for the personal half and I am not going to start.**"

No thank went to her from anybody in that room and she did not wait for one.

---

Then a clerk of forty-four, who had been in that room for about twenty minutes and had not sat down, said the true thing about a list of nine, out loud, to about nine people, and nobody thanked her for it either, and it changed nothing.

"**It is the only kind of procedure that can be carried into a room by a man who is not standing in front of anything, and it is also the only kind that cannot be checked. Nobody in that building is going to be able to ask a single person in this room whether you have done it properly, because the nine of them are not in this room and about four of them would not tell you if you did.**"

"**That is a reason not to do it.**"

"**It is a reason to do it and to be wrong in a room instead of right in a stairwell. I have been wrong in a room in a building in Crown Terrace and I would do it again and I did not enjoy it.**"

"**You were right.**"

"**I was right and it cost nine hundred cards and about four days of a man's leave and one secondment that nobody in my own building knows about. Being right is not the cheap part.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her a second question. She went and sat down at the far end of the counter and has not been asked about it.

---

He did not say the nine out loud to anybody that night, and that was the whole of what he did with them, and in the morning he could have said all nine from memory in about four minutes standing up, and he did not, and he has not since.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag came in at half past eight on his way somewhere, read the hardboard from the other side of the room, said nothing at all, and has said he has no comment on lines and has not been asked twice.

At about half past nine the woman of about thirty was still in the chair nearest the door with a cup of tea in front of her and nobody in that room had asked her anything since about two and about four of them had wanted to.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard. The figure is on the back of a woman's own card in a drawer in a flat in Nine Bridges, it is on no form, and it is not a rate, and nothing was done about it on the Monday and nothing is going to be done about it by anybody in that room.

The shutter came down at about ten and the day ended on a service road in the dark.

---

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the dark, at about ten, standing up, and it was the only page in the case that had his name nowhere on it.

*294. Monday of week 101, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one list, nine hands, and one practical half.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; two jobs in a district this case has not been in; a stair fire door in Nine Bridges attended for the eighth time in about a month and expected back; a man with a clipboard in a building in Crown Terrace asked whether he was from the company and was told he was not. The basement in an unnamed district was not reported. The rate held at nine and ten and reached eleven on no hour of it. The heating in the room off the service road is on, restored by a landlord, and the series of days it was off closed a while back and is not an interval and no figure of days is carried forward. The room is two hundred and thirty-one days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is two hundred and thirty-five days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room, is not in a company training room and is not in a building in Crown Terrace. The twelve lines on the hardboard are a hundred and fifty-one days old, the thirteen a hundred and two, the fourteen sixty-seven, the fifteen forty-six, the sixteen twenty-one, and the seventeen three, which is a difference and not an inclusive count. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread and unchanged. The wall is seventeen lines in eleven hands and twelve names, the first line a cook's and still the first, and no line has been removed since the Monday and none has been rubbed off.**

*What is in the list, and who is not in it.* **About nine items, assembled in a head, on a bus and in a stairwell and standing up in that room with his coat on, and not spoken to any person on any of the days this entry covers. The record of it is this paragraph and there is no other record of it in this city. Sources, by hand, and the hands are: a cook of twenty-seven, who put a hot plate and a chair where a person can be found; a woman of sixty-one, who has run a room above a line for nineteen years and has said one rule out loud in it, a great many times, and has let nobody write it down; a first-year of nineteen, who is the only nineteen-year-old in this case and who gave nine words in the cold and has now given the same nine again; a woman of about sixty-eight, who writes four words in chalk and who declined once to take a sign down; a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, who decided that a list of buildings is a list of people and that a page in a ring binder is the most he will be on; a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip, who said out loud that he is not the person who says the word and would like it written down; a woman of thirty-six with a lanyard, who took a card back at a door and said in public that the person was right; a clerk of forty-four, who put nine sentences into a record because a room cannot ask to be heard again; and a woman of twenty-four, who wrote a line in fourteen words after naming what it would cost her, and who is not on a telephone. Nine hands. He is not one of the nine and was not asked to be and did not offer, and the reason is in nine words in the room and is in this entry once and is not improved on.**

*Why it is not on anything.* **No page, no minute, no form, no leaflet, no register, no notice, no report and no constitution, and the list is not in the ring binder, is not on the hardboard, is not in the book above a line in Saltmarket and is not in a bag in a car. Offered on the Monday evening by a person in that room and refused by the man who assembled it, on the ground stated once and not argued: that the only copy of it is in one head, and that a thing in one head cannot be asked of him by anybody afterwards, and that a list with nine names on it would be nine people who can be found about a list. The first-year of nineteen said nothing about it and was not asked, and a rule about who may be asked was not invented and no number is attached to one. Counts: one list, nine hands, one refusal to write it down, and zero thanks.**

*The practical half, and the nine words.* **Asked for by him and given by her in about eleven seconds standing in front of him, on the ground that a person standing at the back of a room with a list of other people's work in his head has exactly one thing to do with it. Subjects of what she said, this entry carrying the subjects and not the sentences: that he says nine things; that he is at the back and not in front of anything; that about nine people are in that room and one of them has a job that is asking questions and has already said she does not expect him to be the one who says it; that if he is asked a question he says whose work it is and not his own; that if it is one of the nine he says whose; and that if it is not one of the nine he says so, goes and finds out, and comes back with it. Asked for the rest of it and told there is no rest of it and that she has never once been asked for the personal half and is not going to start. Nobody thanked her. He asked for no better version of the nine words and was given none, and nobody in that room improved on them, and about four people in it had heard the nine words before.**

*One correct sentence from a clerk, and what it cost.* **A clerk of forty-four, seconded to a secretariat in a building in Crown Terrace that nobody in her own building has been told she is in, said out loud in about eleven seconds that a set of about nine things other people did is the only kind of procedure that can be carried into a room by a person who is not in front of anything, and the only kind that cannot be checked, and that about four of the nine would not confirm it if she did; and then that being right is not the cheap part, and that being right cost about nine hundred cards and four days of a man's leave and one secondment. Nobody thanked her, nobody asked her a second question, nobody improved on either half, and she sat down at the far end of the counter and has not been asked about it. This is a correct action by a person who is owed nothing and it changed nothing. It is one of at least four since the Monday, and the one that costs somebody else something is the same speech: about nine hundred cards were somebody else's cards and about four days of leave were a man's and the secondment is hers and nobody in her own building knows, and all three of those are still being paid by other people and none of them was paid back.**

*The shape of a Monday evening, and who was in it.* **About nine people in a warm room off a service road from about half past six, and about nine at about half past eight, and a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door from about two until the shutter came down at ten, described by a count, not asked anything by anybody, not thanked and not apologised to, and still the person a fortnight of unpaid heating was paid for. A cook of twenty-seven at a sink who said nothing at all. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag in at half past eight who read the hardboard from the far side and said nothing, as he does. A stair fire door in Nine Bridges attended. A man with a clipboard told he was not from the company. A first-year of nineteen at a counter. Nine heads, one of them full, eight of them not told what is in it, and the man who assembled it said the shape of it out loud once and the nine of it never.**

*What Monday did not hold, item by item.* **No panel and no marker, and the one this case has is spent and is not repeated. No relay staged, nothing run, nothing forwarded, and the machine in a basement in a building in Nine Bridges is a recording machine that has been stuck for about four years and nobody went near it. No message from anybody and nothing at all arrived from anybody, and the man of thirty-four who works nights was not in any of these days, was not named, and has not been asked about anything. No authorship of a technique bundle identified and no authorship of a printed rule identified again, the one identification in this case having been made once by the person it concerns and by nobody else. A man of twenty-two taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing and said no to nothing, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception. Nine keys are needed and two exist; the count is not said aloud in any room; the first cannot be asked back by anybody and the second is in the pocket of a woman of thirty-one; the answer to the question on the back of a docket is on nothing. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. The master's fifth line is still blank in the fourth pocket of a tool roll and nobody has asked him to fill it in. The board over a door, the hardboard on two nails, a wall list in its plastic sleeve in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, a sheet of about two hundred and forty lines on a wall in a building in Crown Terrace, a day room next door that is shut, and a door in Nine Bridges with nothing on it are six objects and none of them has been merged with another in a sentence. The standing question is not stated here, carries no number, and no rule about who may be asked was invented. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 294.**
