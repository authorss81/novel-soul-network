# Chapter 300 — The Sixteenth Sitting

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and eight was the sixteenth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and it was four weeks to the day after the fifteenth, and nothing at all happened in the four weeks between them, and the one thing that did happen in that span happened in a room on a Wednesday, and the book did not open, and the reason it did not open is the first in nineteen years that has been about the room and not about a person, and a woman of sixty-one wrote the eighteenth line on a hardboard on two nails in another street in nineteen words and signed it, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Four jobs attended, one in a district this case has not been in, and nothing has been reported in three districts since the Monday of a week in the spring, and the eleven buildings are still eleven and nobody has asked about them since the Thursday.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room is on and it costs more. The room is two hundred and eighty-two days old.

---

The shutters were up in a room above a line in a building in Saltmarket and the holder of the room said so at the start, as she says at the start of every sitting, and nine chairs were in a curve with the two ends turned in and the ninth was against the wall facing away from the room and it stayed where it was.

There is a table, a tin that has never had a lock on it, and a book with four columns and forty-eight lines in it. The last line had gone in four weeks earlier.

Nobody had come in the first eleven minutes, and at about a quarter past, the man of about forty-four came in for the sixth time and sat down, and said nothing for about nine minutes, and then asked one question.

"**Are you going to open it.**"

"**No.**"

"**You opened it last time.**"

"**I did, and you were in the room, and you asked me afterwards whether a person was all right, and that is the first question anybody has asked in that room in about two years that was about a person instead of about a book.**"

"**And today.**"

"**Today I am not opening it, and I am going to give you the reason, and I have never given that reason in this room, and I am aware that it is nineteen years old and that I have been asked for it in nineteen years and that I have not given it, and I am giving it now because the reason has changed and the old one will not do any more.**"

---

She gave it in about four minutes to the room and not to him.

That in nineteen years the book has opened four times, and that every one of the four times it opened, a person was standing in front of her and said a thing to her, and that what went in was what that person said.

That the thing she would have to write this morning is not that. It is a fact about a room, and it came out of a room on a Wednesday four weeks ago, and it is a true fact and she believes it, and it is not something a caller said to her, and a book of what a caller said cannot hold it.

That if she writes it, then in about four years there is a line in that book that nobody rang, and anybody who reads that book in a building in Crown Terrace will think a person telephoned, and there is no correcting it afterwards, because you cannot unsay a thing that nine people in a room have agreed happened.

"**So the fact is just lost.**"

"**The fact is not lost. The fact is on a hardboard in another street in nineteen words with my name under it, and it is not lost and it is not in here.**"

"**That is the first time in nineteen years you have said that about anything.**"

"**It is.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her a second question and nobody asked which fact, and the man of about forty-four did not ask and has not asked since.

---

The count came at about ten past ten, unasked, as it comes.

"**Fifty-four to fifty-seven. Three in four weeks. The telephone is ahead of the book, it was ahead four weeks ago, and it was behind the book before that, and I am telling you the direction and not the difference, and I am not going to look into it in front of nine people and I did the other thing about four minutes ago and I am not going to do that either.**"

"**You have announced that count how many times.**"

"**Twenty-one. Sixteen of those are the sixteen Wednesdays this book holds, and five predate it, and there is a sheet with the five on it that I have not shown anybody in nineteen years and am not going to on a Wednesday.**"

"**And nobody converts anything.**"

"**Nobody in this room converts a call into a line or a line into a sitting or any of the three into either of the others, and I have asked for that about nine times in nineteen years and I have been given it every time and I have never once been given it by anybody who needed it, and I have said it today for the last time and if anybody in this room needs it after today they can do without me.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody thanked her. The ninth chair was against the wall at the close and was not moved back, and its mover is not named, and the answer about furniture was given for the ninth time and was not confirmed, because confirming it is a minute and she does not take minutes.

---

At about half past eleven she said one more thing, and it was unasked, and nobody had asked her for anything all morning.

"**I am sixty-one and I have run this in a room above a line for nineteen years and I have never once been on a list, and I am not going to be on one in about four years either, and when somebody comes with a system I am going to say no, and I would like about four of the nine of you to have said no to me about something in the last fortnight so that when it happens I know what it sounds like.**"

Nobody said anything to that either. A young man of twenty-one, who has been coming since he was seventeen and who keeps a count he has never given her, said one word, which was that he had, and she said that one was enough, and he has never said another.

---

He went into a repair shop in Lattice Ward at about four in the afternoon on the way to a service road, because it is on the way and because he has been going in for about a term without a reason he has examined.

A bench, a parts cage, a shutter, a lamp, a counter with a rail of card slots in it and about ten cards in it in nine years, and a wall list in its plastic sleeve that is not the wall, and has never been the wall, and is not going to be the wall.

A tool roll was in a drawer where it had been since the spring before the one before last. He did not open the drawer for about nine minutes and then he opened it and the roll was in it and the fourth pocket was where the fourth pocket has been, and he did not take it out.

There are five lines on it. Four of them are full. The fifth one is blank, and it has been blank for about a term and a half, and nobody in five of these books has ever asked him to fill it in, and he has never decided anything about it, and he did not decide anything about it at about four in the afternoon on a Wednesday, and nobody in that shop asked him about it and nobody in that shop knows it is blank.

---

At about half past six in a room off a service road there were about nine people and the room was warm.

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all, which was the eighteenth time in about a fortnight, and a man of fifty-one with a tool bag read the hardboard from the far side of the room and said nothing, as he does.

At about half past seven a woman of sixty-one came in off the street, on her own, having walked from a room above a line in Saltmarket, which is about nine miles, and which nobody in that room has ever asked her to come from.

Nobody knew who she was. A cook of twenty-seven looked at her for about four seconds and then looked at the counter. Somebody said that the door was open and she did not have to knock, and she agreed that she did not, and came in.

She stood in front of the hardboard for about a minute and a half and read all eighteen lines on it, slowly, and about four people in that room watched her do it and nobody said anything.

Then she said the cost out loud, to a room of about nine people who do not know her, and nobody improved on it and nobody asked her a name.

"**I have said the same thing out loud in that room for nineteen years and not one person has ever written it down, and in about four years about four people in this city are going to be able to find a woman of sixty-one at a building with a plate on the outside of it, and every one of them is going to want the reason, and I have never had it in writing to give anybody and I cannot start now, because it is going to be on a wall in another street with my name under it and I am the one who put it there.**"

She wrote it on the hardboard in about nine seconds with a pen off the counter and signed it.

*I have said the same thing out loud for nineteen years and I have never once been written down.*

Nineteen words. The wall is eighteen lines in twelve hands and thirteen names. No line has ever been removed and none has been rubbed off, and the first line is a cook's and is still the first line, and the newest is a woman of sixty-one's and it is signed and it is about being written down.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody asked her name. Nobody said that she was welcome, and about four people in that room wanted very much to ask her to stay and did not, and one of them has wanted to ask a stranger to stay in that room for about four years.

Talia Venn was in that room for about an hour and said one thing in it, and her name went to nobody in it.

"**That is the only line on that wall that anybody has ever had to pay for who was not already in the room. Every other one of the eighteen cost somebody who came in on a Thursday and was owed nothing and chose it, and that one cost a woman who had to walk nine miles to be in a room where nobody knew her, and she did that on purpose, and I am not going to be the one who tells her she should not have.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her a second question. She volunteered no second thing.

---

The heating in that room is on and it costs more. A landlord put it on in a Saturday in about four minutes in a building he has never been inside, and a notice came nine days later signed by a managing agent and not by him, and there are four names on the lease and four names now cost more, and each of the four named the cost out loud before signing in a week in the spring before last, and nobody has asked any of the four to name it again.

The fortnight that a cook paid for is not paid for by anybody and is not going to be, and no fund, collection, subscription, grant, list, minute, notice, leaflet, registration, appeal or form exists anywhere near it, and none has been proposed by anybody in about six weeks, and nothing that has happened in a room in Crown Terrace has paid for any of it and nothing was ever going to.

The shutter came down at about ten.

---

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the dark, at about ten, standing up.

He wrote a second thing on the same service road, four minutes later, which is the only thing he has ever written twice, and the second thing is the only question in this case that has been carried forward unanswered on purpose.

*Can a city of this size survive a cascade without the single response a woman said on a recording fourteen years ago that it needs. He has the answer to a question he has been asking for about four years and he does not have this one, and the two of them are not the same question, and the first one did not help, and he does not know what the second one is for and he is not going to spend the winter finding out by asking people to be findable.*

*303. Wednesday of week 108, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one book, one line, and one question.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; four jobs attended, one of them in a district this case has not been in; nothing reported in three districts since the Monday of a week in the spring; the eleven buildings are still eleven and nobody has asked about them since the Thursday. Four weeks between this Wednesday and the last with nothing in them, and the one thing that happened in that span happened in a room on a Wednesday four weeks ago. The rate held at nine and ten and reached eleven on no hour of it. The heating in the room off the service road is on and the days-off series is closed and no figure of days is carried forward. The room is two hundred and eighty-two days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is two hundred and eighty-six days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The twelve lines on the hardboard are two hundred and two days old, the thirteen a hundred and fifty-three, the fourteen a hundred and eighteen, the fifteen ninety-seven, the sixteen seventy-two, and the seventeen fifty-two. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread and unchanged. The wall was seventeen lines in eleven hands and twelve names at the start of this Wednesday and is eighteen lines in twelve hands and thirteen names at the close of it.**

*The sixteenth sitting, and the book shut.* **The sixteenth, four weeks to the day after the fifteenth, in a room above a line, on a Wednesday. Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in; the ninth against the wall facing away from the room and not moved back at the close, its mover not named, and the answer about furniture given for the ninth time and not confirmed. A tin with never a lock on it. A four-column book at forty-eight lines, the last of them four weeks old. Present: the holder, sixty-one; a man of about forty-four, sixth attendance; the young man of twenty-one in attendance since seventeen, who keeps a count; a young woman of twenty-one in attendance since fifteen, unnamed, unasked, given no reason, and not the first-year of nineteen of another building; and about five others not described here. The book did not open and was not touched. The reason, given to the room and not to the man of about forty-four, in about four minutes, and given because it had changed: that in nineteen years the book has opened four times and every one of the four times a person was standing in front of her and said a thing to her; that what she would have to write this morning is not that but a fact about a room that came out of a room on a Wednesday four weeks ago; that she believes it and it is true; and that a book of what a caller said cannot hold it. Given the consequence, in the same four minutes: that if she writes it then in about four years there is a line in that book that nobody rang, that anybody reading it in a building in Crown Terrace will think a person telephoned, and that there is no correcting it, because you cannot unsay a thing that nine people in a room have agreed happened. She added, unasked, that the fact is not lost and is not in there, and that it is on a hardboard in another street in nineteen words with her name under it. This is the first time in nineteen years that the reason the book has not opened has been about a room rather than about a person, and it is not to be improved on in any later run of days.**

*The figures at the close, and the four that are not to be added up.* **The telephone moved from fifty-four to fifty-seven, three in four weeks; the book stayed at forty-eight and no line went in. Stated: that the telephone is ahead of the book, that it was ahead four weeks ago, and that it was behind the book before that; and the direction is given and the difference is not, and the holder said that she is not going to look into it in front of nine people. The count has now been announced twenty-one times, sixteen of those being the sixteen Wednesdays this book holds, and five predating it on a separate sheet she has not shown anybody in nineteen years and is not going to on a Wednesday. She gave the no-conversion rule for about the ninth time in nineteen years, said she had been given it every time and had never once been given it by anybody who needed it, and said that she had said it that morning for the last time. Four figures, and they are not to be converted into one another or into a number of sittings by any writer or any character: forty-eight, fifty-four, fifty-seven, and twenty-one.**

*One more thing, unasked for.* **At about half past eleven, unasked, the holder said that she is sixty-one, that she has run that room for nineteen years, that she has never once been on a list and is not going to be on one in about four years, that when somebody comes with a system she is going to say no, and that she would like about four of the nine people in that room to have said no to her about something in the last fortnight so that when it happens she knows what it sounds like. One young man of twenty-one said the single word that he had. She said one was enough. He has never said another and has not been asked for one. Nobody thanked her and nobody improved on it.**

*Nine miles, eighteen lines, and the eighteenth.* **A woman of sixty-one, the holder of the Exchange, entered a converted shop unit off a service road at about half past seven on the same Wednesday, on foot, having walked about nine miles, unasked by anybody and uncarried by anybody, on a day when nobody in that room knew she existed. She was let in by a cook of twenty-seven, who looked at her for about four seconds and then at the counter. She read all seventeen lines on the hardboard slowly for about a minute and a half in front of about nine people and nobody said anything. She named the cost out loud before the mark, to a room that did not know her, and nobody improved on it and nobody asked her a name: that she has said the same thing out loud in her own room for nineteen years and not one person has ever written it down; that in about four years about four people in this city will be able to find a woman of sixty-one at a building with a plate on the outside of it; that every one of them will want the reason; that she has never had it in writing to give anybody; and that she cannot start now, because it is going to be on a wall in another street with her name under it and she is the one who put it there. Nineteen words, in about nine seconds, with a pen off the counter, signed. The wall is eighteen lines in twelve hands and thirteen names. No line has been removed in nineteen years and none has been rubbed off, the first line is a cook's and is still the first line, and no line will be. About four people in that room wanted to ask a stranger to stay and did not, and one of them has wanted to for about four years. Nobody thanked her, nobody asked her name, and nobody said she was welcome.**

*What the evening did not touch.* **The heating is on and it costs more, and the number of names that now cost more is four, and each of the four named its cost out loud before signing in a week in the spring before last, and nobody has been asked to name it again. The fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody, unresolved, and is not to be resolved by anything this case makes. Nine keys are needed and two exist; the first cannot be asked back by anybody, has not been fixed in about six weeks, and nothing was invented to fix it; the second is in the pocket of a woman of thirty-one, who has not been asked to be carried anywhere since she said no and is not to be asked; and the answer to the question on the back of a docket is on nothing. The word is uncorrected at nine and ten, was said out loud once to one person about a fortnight ago, is not going to be said to anybody else, and is not reported. The master's fifth line is still blank, is in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, is the cheapest object in this case and the one that has cost the most, and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in; he did not take it out of the drawer on the Wednesday and decided nothing about it. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. No message arrived from anybody. No authorship identified by anybody, and the one identification was made once by the person it concerns. No relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded; the machine in a basement in a building in Nine Bridges is a recording machine that has been stuck for about four years; the plate in a service cupboard on a landing in Nine Bridges is not explained, not removed and not blamed on anybody, and the man of thirty-four who found it is not named here.
