# Chapter 300 — The Sixteenth Sitting

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and eight was the sixteenth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and it was four weeks to the day after the fifteenth, and nothing at all happened in the four weeks between them, and the one thing that did happen in that span happened in a room on a Wednesday, and the book did not open, and the reason it did not open is the first in nineteen years that has been about the room and not about a person, and a woman of sixty-one wrote the eighteenth line on a hardboard on two nails in another street in nineteen words and signed it, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Four jobs attended, one in a district this case has not been in, and nothing has been reported in three districts for about six weeks, which is longer than any other gap anybody in this case has had, and the eleven buildings are still eleven and nobody has asked about them since the Thursday.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating is on and costs more and the room is two hundred and eighty-two days old, which is the oldest figure in this entry and is not the most recent thing that happened in it.

---

The shutters were up in a room above a line in a building in Saltmarket and the holder of the room said so at the start, as she says at the start of every sitting, and nine chairs were in a curve with the two ends turned in and the ninth was against the wall facing away from the room and it stayed where it was.

There is a table, a tin that has never had a lock on it, and a book with four columns and forty-eight lines in it. The last line had gone in four weeks earlier.

Nobody had come in the first eleven minutes, and at about a quarter past, the man of about forty-four came in for the sixth time and sat down, and said nothing for about nine minutes, and then asked one question.

"**Are you going to open it.**"

"**No.**"

"**You opened it last time.**"

"**I did, and you were in the room, and you asked me afterwards whether a person was all right, and that is the first question anybody has asked in that room in about two years that was about a person instead of about a book.**"

"**And today.**"

"**Today I am not opening it, and I am going to give you the reason, and I have never given that reason in this room, and I am aware that it is nineteen years old and that I have been asked for it in nineteen years and that I have not given it, and I am giving it now because the reason has changed and the old one will not do any more.**"

---

She gave it in about four minutes to the room and not to him.

That in nineteen years the book has opened four times, and that every one of the four times it opened, a person was standing in front of her and said a thing to her, and that what went in was what that person said.

That the thing she would have to write this morning is not that. It is a fact about a room, and it came out of a room on a Wednesday four weeks ago, and it is a true fact and she believes it, and it is not something a caller said to her, and a book of what a caller said cannot hold it.

That if she writes it, then in about four years there is a line in that book that nobody rang, and anybody who reads that book in a building in Crown Terrace will think a person telephoned, and there is no correcting it afterwards, because you cannot unsay a thing that nine people in a room have agreed happened.

"**So the fact is just lost.**"

"**The fact is not lost. The fact is on a hardboard in another street in nineteen words with my name under it, and it is not lost and it is not in here.**"

"**That is the first time in nineteen years you have said that about anything.**"

"**It is.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her a second question and nobody asked which fact, and the man of about forty-four did not ask and has not asked since.

---

The count came at about ten past ten, unasked, as it comes.

"**Fifty-four to fifty-seven. Three in four weeks. The telephone is ahead of the book, it was ahead four weeks ago, and it was behind the book before that, and I am telling you the direction and not the difference, and I am not going to look into it in front of nine people and I did the other thing about four minutes ago and I am not going to do that either.**"

"**You have announced that count how many times.**"

"**Twenty-one. Sixteen of those are the sixteen Wednesdays this book holds, and five predate it, and there is a sheet with the five on it that I have not shown anybody in nineteen years and am not going to on a Wednesday.**"

"**And nobody converts anything.**"

"**Nobody in this room converts a call into a line or a line into a sitting or any of the three into either of the others, and I have asked for that about nine times in nineteen years and I have been given it every time and I have never once been given it by anybody who needed it, and I have said it today for the last time and if anybody in this room needs it after today they can do without me.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody thanked her. The ninth chair was against the wall at the close and was not moved back, and its mover is not named, and the answer about furniture was given for the ninth time and was not confirmed, because confirming it is a minute and she does not take minutes.

---

Nothing was said in that room about either of the two things that were decided in a room in Crown Terrace in the fortnight before the fourteenth Wednesday, and both of them are still true, and neither has been asked about since.

The printed rule is still named. A slip in a binder in a district office restricts how it may be used and does not withdraw it, about four hundred competent people in this city can still invoke it, that number is not a conspiracy and is not on a list and is not reducible, and nobody in this case can produce it.

A Continuity Office man of fifty-eight still holds an emergency authority. He agreed to nothing in that room, he was not defeated, he was not turned, and he is right about one thing that nobody improved on, which is that a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it runs until somebody with a stamp puts a date on it.

---

At about half past eleven she said one more thing, and it was unasked, and nobody had asked her for anything all morning.

"**I am sixty-one and I have run this in a room above a line for nineteen years and I have never once been on a list, and I am not going to be on one in about four years either, and when somebody comes with a system I am going to say no, and I would like about four of the nine of you to have said no to me about something in the last fortnight so that when it happens I know what it sounds like.**"

"And on a Friday."

"**I have not opened it on a Friday in nineteen years and I am not going to start, and that is not a rule and nobody made it one. It is a habit, and a habit is the only thing in this room that has never once needed a decision, and there are about four people in this city who could tell you the day of the week I do things on and not one of them has ever asked me.**"

Nobody said anything to that either. A young man of twenty-one, who has been coming since he was seventeen and who keeps a count he has never given her, said one word, which was that he had, and she said that one was enough, and he has never said another.

---

He went into a repair shop in Lattice Ward at about four in the afternoon on the way to a service road, because it is on the way and because he has been going in for about a term without a reason he has examined.

A bench, a parts cage, a shutter, a lamp, a counter with a rail of card slots in it and about ten cards in it in nine years, and a wall list in its plastic sleeve that is not the wall, and has never been the wall, and is not going to be the wall.

A tool roll was in a drawer where it had been since the spring before the one before last. He did not open the drawer for about nine minutes and then he opened it and the roll was in it and the fourth pocket was where the fourth pocket has been, and he did not take it out.

There are five lines on it. Four of them are full. The fifth one is blank, and it has been blank for about a term and a half, and nobody in this case has ever asked him to fill it in, and he has never decided anything about it, and he did not decide anything about it at about four in the afternoon on a Wednesday, and nobody in that shop asked him about it and nobody in that shop knows it is blank.

---

Half past six in a room off a service road that was warm, with about nine people in it, and the room had not changed in ten days and the room was two hundred and eighty-two days old.

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all, which was the eighteenth time in about a fortnight, and a man of fifty-one with a tool bag read the hardboard from the far side of the room and said nothing, as he does.

At about half past seven a woman of sixty-one came in off the street, on her own, having walked from a room above a line in Saltmarket, which is about nine miles, and which nobody in that room has ever asked her to come from.

Nobody knew who she was. A cook of twenty-seven looked at her for about four seconds and then looked at the counter. Somebody said that the door was open and she did not have to knock, and she agreed that she did not, and came in.

She stood in front of the hardboard for about a minute and a half and read all seventeen lines on it, slowly, and about four people in that room watched her do it and nobody said anything.

Then she said the cost out loud, to a room of about nine people who do not know her, and nobody improved on it and nobody asked her a name.

"**I have said the same thing out loud in that room for nineteen years and not one person has ever written it down, and in about four years about four people in this city are going to be able to find a woman of sixty-one at a building with a plate on the outside of it, and every one of them is going to want the reason, and I have never had it in writing to give anybody and I cannot start now, because it is going to be on a wall in another street with my name under it and I am the one who put it there.**"

She wrote it on the hardboard in about nine seconds with a pen off the counter and signed it.

*I have said the same thing out loud for nineteen years and I have never once been written down.*

Nineteen words. The wall is eighteen lines in twelve hands and thirteen names. No line has ever been removed and none has been rubbed off, and the first line is a cook's and is still the first line, and the newest is a woman of sixty-one's and it is signed and it is about being written down.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody asked her name. Nobody said that she was welcome, and about four people in that room wanted very much to ask her to stay and did not, and one of them has wanted to ask a stranger to stay in that room for about four years.

Talia Venn was in that room for about an hour and said one thing in it, and her name went to nobody in it.

"**That is the only line on that wall that anybody has ever had to pay for who was not already in the room. Every other one of the eighteen cost somebody who came in on a Thursday and was owed nothing and chose it, and that one cost a woman who had to walk nine miles to be in a room where nobody knew her, and she did that on purpose, and I am not going to be the one who tells her she should not have.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her a second question. She volunteered no second thing.

---

The heating in that room is on and it costs more. A landlord went round four units on a Saturday and put it on in this one in about four minutes, in a building he has never been inside, and nobody here asked for it and nobody here paid for it and nobody here knew until about eleven that morning. The notice about the valve is a different object and is about nine days old: it was signed by a managing agent and not by the landlord, it was dated nine days after the man who opened the valve, and nobody has acted on it and it is not a request for money from anybody. There are four names on the lease and four names now cost more, and each of the four named the cost out loud before signing in a week in the spring before last, and nobody has asked any of the four to name it again.

The fortnight that a cook paid for is not paid for by anybody and is not going to be, and no fund, collection, subscription, grant, list, minute, leaflet, registration, appeal or form has been proposed by anybody in about six weeks, and nothing has been put near it, and nothing that has happened in a room in Crown Terrace has paid for any of it and nothing was ever going to.

The shutter came down at about ten, and about nine people had been in that room since half past six and one of them had walked nine miles to it.

---

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the dark, at about ten, standing up, and then four minutes later he wrote a second, which is the only thing he has ever written twice.

He wrote a second thing on the same service road, four minutes later, which is the only thing he has ever written twice, and the second thing is the only question in this case that has been carried forward unanswered on purpose.

*Can a city of this size survive a cascade without the single response a woman said on a recording fourteen years ago that it needs. He has the answer to a question he has been asking for about four years and he does not have this one, and the two of them are not the same question, and the first one did not help, and he does not know what the second one is for and he is not going to spend the winter finding out by asking people to be findable.*

*303. Wednesday of week 108, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one book, one line, and one question.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; four jobs attended, one of them in a district this case has not been in; nothing reported in three districts for about six weeks, the longest gap in this case; the eleven buildings are still eleven and nobody has asked about them since the Thursday. Four weeks between this Wednesday and the last with nothing in them, and the one thing that happened in that span happened in a room on a Wednesday four weeks ago. The rate held at nine and ten and reached eleven on no hour of it. The heating in the room off the service road is on and the days-off series is closed and no figure of days is carried forward. The room is two hundred and eighty-two days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is two hundred and eighty-six days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The twelve lines on the hardboard are two hundred and two days old, the thirteen a hundred and fifty-three, the fourteen a hundred and eighteen, the fifteen ninety-seven, the sixteen seventy-two, and the seventeen fifty-two. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread and unchanged. The wall was seventeen lines in eleven hands and twelve names at the start of this Wednesday and is eighteen lines in twelve hands and thirteen names at the close of it.**

*The sixteenth sitting, and the book shut.* **Number: sixteenth. Gap since the last: four weeks, to the day. Day: a Wednesday. Room: above a line, shutters up, the holder announcing it at the start as she does at the start of every sitting. Furniture: nine chairs in a curve, both ends turned in, the ninth against the wall with its back to the room and not moved back at the close; mover not named; the answer about furniture given for the ninth time and unconfirmed, because confirming is a minute. Tin: never locked. Book: four columns, forty-eight lines, last line four weeks old. Present: the holder, sixty-one; a man of about forty-four, sixth attendance; a young man of twenty-one, in attendance since seventeen, keeping a count; a young woman of twenty-one, in attendance since fifteen, unnamed, unasked, given no reason, and not the first-year of nineteen of another building; about five others undescribed. Book: not opened, not touched. Reason, to the room and not to the man of forty-four, about four minutes, given because it had changed: openings in nineteen years, four; at every one, a person standing in front of the holder and speaking. What the morning offered was not that, but a fact about a room, out of a room, four weeks earlier, believed and true. Consequence, same four minutes: a line nobody rang, read four years hence in a building in Crown Terrace by a reader who will take it as a call, and no correction available, since a thing nine people in a room have agreed happened cannot be unagreed. Added unasked: the fact is not lost, and is not in the book. First such reason in nineteen years: about a room rather than a person. Not to be improved on in any later week.**

*The two things nobody in that room raised, and both of them are still true.* **One: the printed rule. Named out loud in a district office a fortnight before the fourteenth Wednesday, restricted by a slip in a binder that belongs to whoever maintains that binder, and not withdrawn, not repealed, not destroyed. About four hundred competent people in this city can still invoke it. That number is not a conspiracy, is not on a list, is not reducible, and cannot be produced by anybody in this case; a person of sixty in a district office has been trying for about a term and has said so aloud and has not been thanked for failing to. Two: the Continuity Office. Emergency authority retained, unchanged, held by a man of fifty-eight who agreed to nothing in a room in Crown Terrace, was not defeated, was not turned, and is right about one thing, which is that a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it runs until somebody with a stamp puts a date on it. Neither fact was raised at this sitting, neither was asked about, and neither was on a list, because there is no list, and no page of either is in this file or in that room.**

*The figures at the close, and the four that are not to be added up.* **The telephone moved from fifty-four to fifty-seven, three in four weeks; the book stayed at forty-eight and no line went in. Stated: that the telephone is ahead of the book, that it was ahead four weeks ago, and that it was behind the book before that; and the direction is given and the difference is not, and the holder said that she is not going to look into it in front of nine people. The count has now been announced twenty-one times, sixteen of those being the sixteen Wednesdays this book holds, and five predating it on a separate sheet she has not shown anybody in nineteen years and is not going to on a Wednesday. She gave the no-conversion rule for about the ninth time in nineteen years, said she had been given it every time and had never once been given it by anybody who needed it, and said that she had said it that morning for the last time. Four figures, and they are not to be converted into one another or into a number of sittings by anybody in this case, on any surface, at any point:  forty-eight, fifty-four, fifty-seven, and twenty-one.**

*One more thing, unasked for, and the day of the week.* **At about half past eleven, unasked, the holder said that she is sixty-one, that she has run that room for nineteen years, that she has never once been on a list and is not going to be on one in about four years, that when somebody comes with a system she is going to say no, and that she would like about four of the nine people in that room to have said no to her about something in the last fortnight so that when it happens she knows what it sounds like. One young man of twenty-one said the single word that he had. She said one was enough. He has never said another and has not been asked for one. Asked, afterwards, about a Friday, she said that in nineteen years she has not opened that book on a Friday and is not going to start; that this is not a rule and nobody made it one; that it is a habit; that a habit is the only thing in that room that has never needed a decision; and that about four people in this city could tell you the day of the week she does things on and not one of them has ever asked her. Nobody thanked her and nobody improved on either half of it.**

*Nine miles, eighteen lines, and the eighteenth.* **Person: the holder of the Exchange, sixty-one, whose name is on nothing and who had never been in this room before this evening. Distance walked: about nine miles. Time of arrival: about half past seven, on the same Wednesday, the same day as the sitting she had come from. Carried by: nobody. Asked by: nobody. Known at the door by: nobody; a cook of twenty-seven looked at her for about four seconds and then at the counter and said the door was open. Read before writing: seventeen lines, at walking pace, about a minute and a half, in front of about nine people, no comment. Cost named before the mark, aloud, to a room that did not know her, in four items: the same statement spoken aloud in her own room for nineteen years with no written instance; about four people in this city able in about four years to find a woman of sixty-one at a building with a plate on the outside of it; every one of them wanting the reason; no written form of the reason in her possession. The reason she gave for going ahead anyway is on the wall and not here. Mark: one, nineteen words, about nine seconds, a pen off the counter, signed. Wall after: eighteen lines, twelve hands, thirteen names. Lines removed: none. Lines rubbed off: none. First line: a cook's. Age of the newest before this evening: fifty-two days. Consequence for her, stated by her and accepted by nobody: she has put herself on a surface in a form four people will be able to use. Refused: a name, a welcome, a thank you, and about four invitations to stay, one of them from somebody who has wanted to make that invitation for about four years.**

*What the evening did not touch.* **The heating is on and it costs more, and the number of names that now cost more is four, and each of the four named its cost out loud before signing in a week in the spring before last, and nobody has been asked to name it again. The fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody, unresolved, and is not to be resolved by anything this case makes. Nine keys are needed and two exist; the first cannot be asked back by anybody, has not been fixed in about six weeks, and nothing was invented to fix it; the second is in the pocket of a woman of thirty-one, who has not been asked to be carried anywhere since she said no and is not to be asked; and the answer to the question on the back of a docket is on nothing. The word is uncorrected at nine and ten, was said out loud once to one person about eight weeks ago, is not going to be said to anybody else, and is not reported. The master's fifth line is still blank, is in the fourth pocket of a tool roll, is the cheapest object in this case and the one that has cost the most, and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in; he did not take it out of the drawer on the Wednesday and decided nothing about it. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. No message arrived from anybody. No authorship identified by anybody, and the one identification was made once by the person it concerns. No relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded; the machine in a basement in a building in Nine Bridges is a recording machine that has been stuck for about four years; the plate in a service cupboard on a landing in Nine Bridges is not explained, not removed and not blamed on anybody, and the man of thirty-four who found it is not named here. He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception. No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution was created by anybody in this case, and the only object this case has made in the days this entry covers is one line of handwriting and one signature on an existing hardboard. Six surfaces and none merged with another here. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door from about two until ten, described by a count, not asked anything, not thanked and not apologised to. The standing question is not stated here, carries no number, and no rule about who may be asked was invented. The question written on the service road four minutes before this entry was opened is not answered here and is not to be answered here, and nobody in this case has an answer to it.
