# Chapter 270 — The Last Thing He Wanted

The Thursday of week ninety-four was the small hours of that night, in a cold room off a service road, and a man of thirty-four who works nights wrote the fifteenth line on a hardboard on two nails in fourteen words, unsigned, with a space left where a name would go, and he named the cost out loud before he made the mark, and the cost was that he is the only person in this case who can now be found at a door, and that is the last thing he has wanted for about four years. Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him whether he was sure and nobody improved on any part of it, and the heating in that room was on its eightieth day and nobody has paid for it and nobody is going to.

---

Eleven on the docket and ten in date, and a job he did not go to and did not reassign and did not tell anybody he had not gone to.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its eightieth day and it is a hundred and eighty-five days old.

---

About half past one in the morning, and about five people, and the shutter half up.

A cook of twenty-seven puts the ring on at about half past eight in the morning on every working day and has done for about twenty-two weeks, and puts the same ring on in the small hours for whoever is in that room at two, which is about four people a week, and she has never been thanked for it and has said that if anybody does she will ask them to stop.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in and out of that room between one and two. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was on his way to work and had a box of cups in his hand, which he carried out of that building in four trips in an earlier movement and which he has never been thanked for. A first-year of nineteen was in the end chair with her boots off because it is the warm end and because the shutter is half up and there is a lamp.

The man of thirty-four who works nights had been in since about one and had a torch on the counter and had not picked it up.

---

He said it before he did it, standing in front of the hardboard, and it is the whole reason the room has that object on it and it is not manners and it was not improved on.

"**The cost is that I am the only person in this case who can now be found at a door, and that is the last thing I have wanted for about four years, and I have wanted it not to be true the entire time, and I know that is a stupid thing to want and I am not going to improve on it.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I telephoned the number on a plate at ten past five on the Friday morning six days ago and you wrote it down, and I told you not to write it down, and you were right to write it down, and I would have told you to write it down if I had thought about it for four seconds instead of none.**"

"**Say the part about the door.**"

"**I am the only person in this case who can be found at a door. I work nights. I know which four hundred buildings in this city have a landing on them and which nine hundred of them have a door that somebody put a number on that is off a list somewhere. There is nothing else in this case that a person could describe. A first-year of nineteen is on about eleven hundred pieces of paper and knows it. A man of fifty-one has a company and a tool bag. A cook is a cook. You are a name on a piece of paper in a company in another district and that is the only reason anybody can get at you at all.**"

"**And now you.**"

"**And now me. I am a man who works nights in Nine Bridges. That is four words and it took me about four years to become them and they are not going to get less true because I would rather they were not.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody said that the reason was good. Nobody improved on the cost and nobody argued with any part of it and every word of it was understood in that room and one of the people understanding it has been findable since the spring before the one before last.

---

Then he said the other thing, and it is why the cost is real and not a cost anybody has to imagine.

"**A plate. A fourth landing in Nine Bridges, a service cupboard, a number that is the right number for that block, and four things in a box on the face of it and no fifth. Nothing wrong with it. Not scratched, not loose, not warm. It was not there in the spring and I was in that cupboard in the spring.**"

"**You have not been back in.**"

"**I have not been back in. Nobody has removed it. Nobody has explained it. Nobody has been blamed for it and there is nobody in this case it could be blamed on, and I am not going to be the man who says that there is a person behind it, because I do not know that and I have not looked and I am not going to look.**"

"**Then what makes the cost real.**"

"**That it is there. That in about four years somebody will go into that cupboard, and they will find it, and it will be on somebody's file, and the only person in this city who knows it exists is a man who can be found at a door.**"

---

He wrote it in about nine seconds with a pen off the counter and he did not write his name, and two people looked at where his name would have gone, and he had been in that room about four times.

A first-year of nineteen said, from the end chair, with her boots off: "**That is a line.**"

"**I know it is a line.**"

"**Then say the other thing, because in about four years somebody is going to read that and take it for a rule, and it is not, and the only reason they will is that nobody has said the difference.**"

"**The difference has been said on that board four times and by about nine people and none of us is you.**"

"**Then I will say it once more and then nobody is to say it again this year. A rule has a number and a date and hangs on a wall in a building with a great deal of paper in it, and any competent person may pick it up on a Monday. A line costs the person who wrote it something they have to say out loud, binds nobody including the person who wrote it, comes off with a thumb in front of nine people, and is evidence of nothing in any proceeding, there being none.**"

He put the pen down.

"**That is four times now.**"

"**That is four times.**"

---

Nobody signed it and nobody was asked to. Nobody was asked whether he was sure. Nobody was thanked, and there is no mechanism in this case by which anybody is thanked for standing in front of a hardboard at half past two in the morning and saying out loud what a thing has cost him.

He said one more thing on his way out at about ten to two, with the torch, and it is the only forward thing anybody said in that room in four days.

"**I am not going back into that cupboard. Nobody is going. That is not a plan, that is four words at ten to two in the morning, and in about four years it will be nine hundred doors and a person who knows about one of them, and I would like somebody in this city to write down before then that not going is a decision and not a thing that lasts.**"

"**Nobody is going to write that down.**"

"**No. I know. That is why I said it to a room and not to a man.**"

---

At about two a woman of about thirty came into that room and sat in the chair nearest the door and a cook of twenty-seven put a cup of tea in front of her and said nothing at all, and two people in that room wanted to ask her why she comes, and nobody did, and nobody apologised to her, because an apology would have been a question and it would still have been one.

The heating in that room was on its eightieth day and had been on its eightieth day since seven in the morning on the Monday of week eighty-three, and there is no fund and no collection and no subscription and no grant and no list and no minute and no notice and no appeal, and none of them was proposed that night by anybody, and the fortnight at the start of all of it is not paid for by anybody in this case and is not going to be.

He wrote one thing on a service road, at about ten past two in the morning, in the wet, standing up, and then he wrote it down, and the two are the same sentence.

*273. Thursday of week 94, at about ten past two in the morning on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one line in fourteen words, one cost, and eighty days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and ten in date. One job was not attended, not reassigned, and not reported, and the entry records the not going and attributes it to nothing. The rate held at nine and ten all through the movement and reached eleven on no day of it, and no person in this case would have noticed if it had. The heating in the room off the service road is on its eightieth day off, unpaid, and is not resolved, and no fund, collection, subscription, grant, list, minute, notice, report, form, leaflet, registration or appeal exists or was proposed in this run. The room is a hundred and eighty-five days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and eighty-nine days in the rail and is a third object and is not in that room. Four figures are now carried for the wall and all four are live: the twelve lines are a hundred and five days old, the thirteenth is fifty-six, the fourteenth is twenty-one, and the fifteenth is zero, which is a difference and not an inclusive count.**

*The cost, in the words of the man it cost.* **Stated standing in front of the hardboard at about half past one in the morning, before any mark, and not improved on by anybody: that he is now the only person in this case who can be found at a door; that this is the last thing he has wanted for about four years; that he has wanted it not to be true the entire time; and that he knows that is a stupid thing to want and will not improve on it. Mechanism, given by him and not softened: the call at about ten past five on the Friday morning six days earlier, the refusal to have it written down, and his own statement that he would rather be written down than asked twice. His account of why he is findable and the others are not: night work, four hundred buildings with a landing on them, nine hundred doors carrying a number off a list somewhere, a first-year of nineteen on about eleven hundred pieces of paper who knows it, a man of fifty-one with a company and a tool bag, a cook who is a cook, and a man of twenty-two reachable only through a company in another district. Named twice, not improved. Nobody thanked him, nobody said the reason was good, nobody argued, and nobody asked afterwards whether he was sure.**

*The plate, and why the cost is not an imagined one.* **Restated from memory in that room six days after: fourth landing, Nine Bridges, service cupboard with no lock, the block's own number on it, four items in a box and no fifth, nothing wrong with it in any respect, absent in the spring, finder present in the spring. Not removed, not explained, not blamed on anybody — his own words were that there is nobody in this case it could be blamed on, and that he is not going to be the man who says there is a person behind it, because he does not know that and has not looked and will not look. His reason the cost is real: the plate is there; in about four years somebody will go into that cupboard, find it, and it will be on a file; and the only person in this city who knows it exists is findable at a door. He has not been back and has not been asked to go.**

*The line.* **Fourteen words, written in about nine seconds with a pen off the counter, unsigned, with a space left where a name would have gone and two people looking at it: that he is the one of them who can be found at a door. He had been in that room about four times. The wall is now fifteen lines in nine hands and ten names. No line has been removed since the eleventh week, when a cook of twenty-seven took a line of her own off with her own thumb in front of about nine people, and the first line is a cook's and is still the first line. A first-year of nineteen said from the end chair that it is a line and not a rule, and gave the difference whole and in full for the second time in a term, and was told by the man who had written it that the difference has been said on that board four times and by about nine people and that she is none of them, and that she may say it once more and that then nobody is to say it again this year. The count is four and it was agreed as four. Nobody signed it and nobody was asked to.**

*What was said on the way out, at about ten to two.* **With a torch, to the room, by the man who had just written the line: he is not going back into that cupboard, nobody is going, and that this is not a plan but four words at ten to two in the morning; that in about four years it will be about nine hundred doors and one person who knows about one of them; and that somebody ought to write down before then that not going is a decision and not a thing that lasts. Told that nobody is going to write it down, he said he knows, and that this is why he said it to a room and not to a man. Nobody thanked him, and there is no mechanism in this case that thanks anybody for standing in front of a hardboard at half past two in the morning and saying out loud what a thing has cost him.**
