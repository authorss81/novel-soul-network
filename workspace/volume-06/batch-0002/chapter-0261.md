# Chapter 261 — A Room Cannot Come Back

The Monday morning of week ninety-two was one morning in a room in Crown Terrace with about nine chairs in it, and a chair of forty-nine whose office signs about eleven of a kind a year asked seven questions and every one of them was smaller than the one before it, and a woman of twenty-four gave evidence about a practice she had been inside of for a term and a half, and a man of twenty-two described a room and was in the room and was not standing in front of anything, and what came out of it was one thing, and the thing was not a verdict, and the woman who pulled about nine hundred cards out of use on a Wednesday is still seconded to the secretariat of the committee that heard the item and the chair has still not been told.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. The stair fire door in Nine Bridges has not been reported since the Thursday and has not been chased, and the entry records that it has not been chased and does not say why.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its sixty-third day and it is a hundred and sixty-eight days old.

---

The room is on a floor with a corridor outside it and about nine chairs in it, and the chairs have metal frames and fabric seats and have been in service longer than anybody working in that building.

At the head of the table there is a woman of forty-nine with a folder, a pen and an ordinary coat, and she is not a post, and nothing that happened in that room on that morning required her to be one, and about four people noticed the second half of that and none of them said it.

She read the item. It is four paragraphs long and it was prepared for her by somebody who is not described here and who is right that a committee cannot sit without something to sit on.

The question under the four paragraphs is whether a practice can be required to show that any person in it may stop at any time, and what evidence would satisfy that.

---

The first person she spoke to was the clerk of forty-four, who is the woman who pulled the cards, and who is now seconded to the secretariat of the committee that is sitting, because a committee with an item needs a person to prepare it, and she is the only person in either building who knows what it cost to say a number out loud in a room.

The chair of forty-nine said: "**You prepared this.**"

"**I did.**"

"**Who told you what to prepare.**"

"**Nobody. It came to me with a heading on it.**"

"**What is on the heading.**"

"**A word that means a meeting.**"

"Then the meeting is the item and not the question. Go on."

Nobody thanked her for that and nobody in that room had any mechanism by which to.

---

Aurelia Sopp, who is sixty and whose job it is to ask the questions, was in the room and was not on the list of anybody who had prepared anything, and she was asked by the chair of forty-nine what evidence would satisfy the item, and she answered it in one sentence that she had already given in a corridor a fortnight before.

"**A record of who stopped. That is the only evidence there is and it is a column, and a column is the one thing a room of that kind does not have.**"

The chair of forty-nine said: "**Who would keep the column.**"

"**Nobody, in about eleven thousand premises, and I have asked three of them and one of them said the word budget out loud before I finished the sentence.**"

"**So the answer to my question is no.**"

"**The answer to your question is no.**"

"**Then I have what I came for.**"

Nobody in that room wrote it down. The woman who keeps the record for that committee wrote down what she had been paid to write down, and that record is a committee's and is not ours, and nobody in a room off a service road will ever see a page of it.

---

The chair of forty-nine asked four questions in the whole of that morning and every one of them was smaller than the one before it, and about four people in the room understood by the third that she was not going to be asked a question she could answer by being right.

He asked her, in the corridor outside, at about eleven, whether she was going to do anything about the second of the two faults in the item.

She said: "**I found it a fortnight ago and I said it once and I did nothing about it and I am not going to, and that is not a defence of the item, it is the position, and if you want the other half of it then here it is: I was right in there this morning and being right would have cost that woman something, and I have been right every time I have opened my mouth in this building for nineteen years, and this is the first time that being right was a thing I had to hold back on purpose.**"

She is not thanked for holding it back. There is no mechanism in this case by which anybody is thanked for holding anything back.

---

Talia Venn gave evidence for about eleven minutes and it was the strongest evidence in the room.

She described a practice she has been inside of for a term and a half. She described what happens at the end of it. She described a beat in which a person knows they are finished, and how it is not a feeling and not a decision and not anything anybody can be taught by being told, and how in about four years of attending those rooms she has watched about nine people discover it, and how two of the nine were told by somebody afterwards that they had got lucky.

She did not read out the sentence on the wall binder. She was asked, in a corridor afterwards, by a man of twenty-two, whether she had said it.

"**I said what a card is for. I did not say what a district says about a card. Those are two sentences and one of them is mine.**"

He did not thank her, and he was not thanked in that room for anything, and about four people in it understood that the strongest thing said that morning had just gone into a folder that somebody else keeps.

---

Then he was asked, and he answered from a chair with his hands on his knees.

"**Describe the room.**"

"**It is four rooms and a back one off a service road in the Lower Wards. There is a board over the door with four words in chalk and above it a grey ghost of four more that came off in a thumb and will not come off any further. Underneath that on two nails there is a hardboard the size of a tea tray with fourteen lines on it in eight hands. There is a ring binder on a shelf with eight things in it and it has been opened twice in a term and a half and one of those times was by a man who put it back. There are nine chairs, a counter with nothing on it, a kettle, a socket that works, and a bell-push that does not ring by itself. Outside on the wall there is a plate with a letting agent's telephone number on it and no name on it, and about four hundred people in this case could find that door and the only one of them who is findable at it is me.**"

"**And what does the room do.**"

"**It has a hardboard on it that says what it will not do, and it is not enforced against anybody who did not write it, and one of the fourteen lines on it is a young woman of nineteen saying she is not going to be the person who is asked about it, and she is right.**"

He was not standing in front of anything. He was not asked to show anybody anything, and he showed nothing, and he did not teach, demonstrate, assess or assist anybody in that room or after it.

---

The one thing the morning produced was said at about eleven twenty-five, and it was nine words, and the chair of forty-nine said it to the room and not to any person in it.

"**A room cannot ask to be heard again. A person has to.**"

Nobody in that room thanked her for it. Nobody argued with it. About four people wrote it down afterwards in notebooks that are not the book and are not in that building, and one of those four is a woman of twenty-four and one is a man of twenty-two, and neither of them has ever read the other's.

She did not say what follows from it. She did not say that an item comes back only when a person asks for it, and she did not say that the person who would have to ask is the person already standing in a building two hundred metres away on a secondment that nobody in that room was told about.

He noticed that at about the lift and he did not say it, and about four people noticed him noticing, and it was not raised.

---

At about half past six there were about nine people in a cold room off a service road and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven put a cup of tea in front of her and said nothing at all, which is what happens on every working day and has happened for sixty-three days.

A first-year of nineteen said, to nobody, that the finding about the column had been made a fortnight ago by the one person in that building whose job is to ask questions, and that it had been right, and that the item had gone ahead anyway, and that this was the correct order of those two things and that nobody was going to be asked about it.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wet, at about ten.

*264. Monday of week 92, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one item, four smaller questions, and sixty-three days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date, the first time in about a month that the two have been level. The stair fire door in Nine Bridges has not been reported since the Thursday of last week; it has not been chased and the entry records that it has not been chased and attributes the not chasing to nothing. The standing water in a basement in an unnamed district was not reported this week and stands at whatever it stands at. The rate held at nine and ten and did not reach eleven and nobody in that room would have noticed if it had. The heating in the room off the service road is on its sixty-third day off, unpaid, and is not resolved and nothing in this entry resolves it. The room is a hundred and sixty-eight days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and seventy-two days in the rail and is a third object and is not in that room. The twelve lines on the hardboard are eighty-eight days old, the thirteenth is thirty-nine, and the fourteenth is four.**

*What the item was.* **Four paragraphs prepared for a hearing by a person not described here. Beneath them: whether a practice can be required to show that any person in it may stop at any time, and what evidence would satisfy that. Heard on one morning in a room in Crown Terrace with about nine chairs in it, before a woman of forty-nine whose office signs about eleven of a kind a year. A woman of forty-nine with an ordinary coat is a person with an ordinary job and is not a post, and nothing in the morning required her to be one. The record of the hearing is the committee's, is not ours, and no page of it is in this file or in that building.**

*The seven questions.* **Three of them asked of the clerk of forty-four, who is seconded to the secretariat of the committee that heard the item and who is owed nothing by anybody in this case: whether she prepared it, who told her what to prepare, what was on the heading, and what the heading meant. The third answer was that the meeting is the item and not the question, given unprompted, and the chair accepted it and said go on. The secondment was not disclosed to the chair at any point in the morning and cannot be ended by anybody in this case, and the man of twenty-two noticed the shape of it at a lift and did not raise it and was not asked to.**

*The second fault, raised by the person who found it.* **A practice standards officer of sixty, whose job it is to ask the questions, was in the room and was on nobody's list, and was asked what evidence would satisfy the item and gave the answer she had already given standing up in a corridor a fortnight before: that the only evidence is a record of who stopped, that a record of who stopped is a column, and that about eleven thousand premises have no column and three were asked and one of them said the word budget out loud first. She was told that this was the answer to the chair's question and the chair said she had what she came for. In the corridor afterwards she said that she found the fault a fortnight ago, said it once, did nothing about it, is not going to, and that being right in that room would have cost the clerk of forty-four something, and that this is the first time in nineteen years she has had to hold being right back on purpose. She was not thanked for it and there is no mechanism here that thanks anybody for holding anything back.**

*Eleven minutes of evidence.* **Given by Talia Venn, twenty-four, who has been inside a practice for a term and a half. She described the beat in which a person knows a practice is finished, that it is not a feeling and not a decision and cannot be taught by being told, that in about four years about nine people have been seen to discover it, and that two of the nine were told afterwards by somebody that they had got lucky. She did not read the sentence on the wall binder and was asked in a corridor whether she had, and answered that she said what a card is for and did not say what a district says about a card, and that those are two sentences and one of them is hers. The strongest evidence in the room went into a folder that somebody else keeps. She was not thanked and she is owed nothing.**

*What the man of twenty-two did, from a chair.* **He was asked to describe the room and described it in about nine sentences: four rooms and a back one off a service road, a board over a door with four words in chalk and an unremovable grey ghost of four above them, a hardboard on two nails with fourteen lines in eight hands, a ring binder on a shelf holding eight things and opened twice in a term and a half, nine chairs, a bare counter, a kettle, a working socket, a bell-push that does not ring, a plate outside with a telephone number and no name, and about four hundred people in the case who could find the door, of whom one is him. He was then asked what the room does and answered that it has a hardboard saying what it will not do, that the hardboard is not enforceable against anybody who did not write it, and that one of the fourteen lines is a first-year of nineteen saying she will not be the person asked about it and that she is right. He was not standing in front of anything and he taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nobody.**

*What the morning produced, and it is not a verdict.* **Nine words, said at about eleven twenty-five by the chair of forty-nine, to the room and not to any person in it: that a room cannot ask to be heard again and a person has to. It is owed to nobody and it is on nobody, and the record that holds it is the committee's. Nobody thanked her, nobody argued with it, and about four people wrote it down in notebooks that are not the book and are not in that building, of whom one is the woman of twenty-four and one is the man of twenty-two and neither has read the other's. She did not say what follows from it and did not say that the item returns only on a person's asking, and the person who would have to ask is in a building two hundred metres away on a secondment the chair was not told about.**

*State and boundaries.* **About nine people in a cold room and a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door from about two, described by a count and not by a name, an age, a district or an occupation, not asked anything, not thanked, and about four people wanted to. Fourteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, the first a cook's and still the first. Four words in chalk and one unremovable grey ghost over a door. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened on that day. No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution was created by anybody in this case; the four paragraphs and the record of the hearing are a committee's and are not ours. No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by him, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception, and the condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased. No panel and no marker. Nine keys needed and two existing, count not stated. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. No message has arrived from anybody. The standing question is not stated here and carries no number. Entry 264.**
