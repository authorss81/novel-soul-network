# Chapter 269 — A Page Nobody Has Read

The Wednesday of week ninety-four was the afternoon a clerk of fifty-three read a page of about forty-one people out loud, once, in about nine minutes, in a back room above a counter on the ground floor of a building in Bower Street, with about nine people in it, because a page nobody has read is a page nobody can close, and she was thanked by nobody and owed nothing, and a box ruled in pencil under a fourth field on that counter with one name in it and no number on it was not emptied and took no second name and was not given a reference and was not moved onto a system, and no district adopted it.

---

Eleven on the docket, and the stair fire door in Nine Bridges was attended at about eleven and reset for the eleventh time in about a fortnight and is expected back, and the standing water in the basement in an unnamed district was not reported.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its seventy-ninth day and it is a hundred and eighty-four days old.

---

The counter is on the ground floor and it has about two hundred forms on it in four stacks and a rubber band on each stack, and the stacks go back into a cupboard at the end of the day and come out in the morning, and there is a bell and there is a sign saying the bell is not a service.

Behind the counter there is a door and behind the door there is a room with a table in it, a window that does not open, and about nine chairs, and a woman of fifty-three has worked that counter for nineteen years and has been asked about four things in that time and has answered all four.

She had the page in the third stack. It came in with about two hundred others about four years ago off a van, and it is a single sheet with a column of about forty-one names on it, and the names were on the same list on the same day, and the list belongs to somebody else and is not described here.

About nine of the forty-one were telephoned before whatever happened, happened. One of the forty-one was not, and he was safe. The other thirty-one nobody has heard from and nobody is going to.

She had never read it out. She had looked at it, on and off, for about four years, in the ordinary way a person looks at a sheet in a stack.

---

"**I am going to read this out and I want to say before I start that it is not going to do anything, and I would like four people in this room to notice that I know that.**"

"**Then why do it.**"

"**Because a page nobody has read is a page nobody can close.**"

Nobody thanked her for that and she is owed nothing by anybody in this case and she has nineteen years of a counter behind her and no post and no title and a bell that is not a service.

It took about nine minutes. She read the names, in the order they were on the sheet, at the speed of somebody reading a list, and she did not do anything to her voice at any point, and she did not stop at any of them, and she did not go back over any of them, and she did not say anything between any two of them.

About nine people were in that room. A man of twenty-two. Talia Venn, who is twenty-four. A first-year of nineteen. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts. A woman of about thirty-four with a district jacket who is the one person in that room who telephoned somebody on that page, once, and has not been asked about it and is not going to be. And about three others who are not described here.

Nobody in that room said a word between about forty-one names.

---

When she finished, she put the sheet down on the table with the writing side up, which is the only time in about four years that anybody has put that sheet down on a table rather than back into a stack.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag said: "**Nine minutes and nobody in this room has a different fact about any of them than they had before you started.**"

"**No.**"

"**Then what has changed.**"

"**Nothing. That is the answer and I have known it was the answer since about halfway down the list, and I read the other half anyway, and I would like it noticed that I read the other half anyway.**"

Nobody improved on that either. Nobody asked her to do it again, and she did not offer, and it was read out once, and it will not be read out again in this case, and that room understood that a page nobody has read is a page nobody can close and that a page somebody has read is a page nobody can close either, and that the difference is about her and not about them.

---

Then a man of about forty-nine from a district office, who had come to that counter about eleven weeks ago on a matter of his own and who had asked a question in the corridor on the way in, said:

"**Could that not be recorded somewhere. Properly. With a reference.**"

"**No.**"

"**It is forty-one people. About nine of them were rung. One was not and did not happen, and thirty-one others were not, and that is the single most useful thing anybody in this city has said in about four years. There is a district that could—**"

"**There is a district that could adopt it and it would be gone in about nine weeks and it would be right, and that is what is wrong with it. There is a box on this counter ruled in pencil under the fourth field with one name in it and no number on it, and it is the only one of its kind in this city, and it is not a system, and it cannot be emptied, and it is not going anywhere. If a page of forty-one goes into a system then thirty-one people have been on a list that a person with a budget can look at, and in about four years there will be about four hundred of them, and every one of them will have been entered by somebody kind.**"

"**You are refusing a district on principle.**"

"**I am refusing it because it works. If it did not work I would not be able to do it and I would be doing it.**"

Nobody in that room said so. About four people in that room had not known that and about four of them wrote nothing down, and one of them is a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts and he has said twice that a list of buildings is a list of people and he did not say it and was not asked and is not to be asked.

---

The box is on that counter. It is on the top page of the third stack, or it was in about four years ago when a woman of thirty-eight gave a name to a stranger in about four seconds and then asked for it back, and it cannot be taken out, because the page cannot be taken out of a stack and the stack cannot be taken out of the counter and a hole in a stack is a thing somebody asks about.

It was not emptied. It took no second name and was not offered one and was not asked for one. It was not given a reference number and nobody asked for a reference number. It was not moved onto a system and nothing about it was entered anywhere. No district has adopted it, and none has asked, and about four people in that room who work for districts did not mention it.

Nobody thanked the clerk of fifty-three. She is owed nothing by anybody in this case, she has been asked about four things in nineteen years, she was not asked a fifth today, and the one question she was asked in a corridor at about two o'clock was asked by a man of twenty-two and was about something else entirely and took about nine seconds and is not in this entry.

---

At about seven in the cold room off the service road there were about nine people and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven put a cup of tea in front of her and said nothing at all, which is what has happened on every working day for seventy-nine days.

Nobody in that room said anything about the page. About four people in it wanted to and did not, and a first-year of nineteen said one sentence about it at about half past seven, and the sentence was that a woman of fifty-three read forty-one names out loud for nine minutes in a room with nine people in it and that is the whole of what happened today, and that nobody is going to make it into a thing.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wet, at about ten, and it took longer than any of them because about nine of the names are not short.

*272. Wednesday of week 94, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one page, forty-one names, nine minutes, and seventy-nine days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket. The stair fire door in Nine Bridges was attended at about eleven, was reset for the eleventh time in about a fortnight, and is expected back; the description box carries a stamped number and nothing in the frame supports a fault. The standing water in a basement in an unnamed district was not reported this week. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in the room off the service road is on its seventy-ninth day off, unpaid, and unresolved. The room is a hundred and eighty-four days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and eighty-eight days in the rail and is a third object and is not in that building in Bower Street. The twelve lines on the hardboard are a hundred and four days old, the thirteenth is fifty-five, and the fourteenth is twenty. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened and unchanged.**

*The counter and the back room.* **A counter on the ground floor of a building in Bower Street, about two hundred forms in four stacks with a band on each, taken into a cupboard at the end of the day and brought out in the morning, a bell that is not a service, and behind a door a room with a table, a window that does not open and about nine chairs. A woman of fifty-three has worked that counter for nineteen years, has been asked about four things in that time, and has answered all four. She was not asked a fifth.**

*The page.* **One sheet out of the third stack, in the counter since about four years ago, with a column of about forty-one names on it, all of the same list on the same day, the list being another party's and not described here. About nine of the forty-one were telephoned before whatever happened; one of the forty-one was not and was safe; the remaining thirty-one have not been heard from and will not be. The holder had looked at the sheet on and off for about four years and had never read it out to anybody.**

*The reading.* **Given once, at about half past two, in about nine minutes, to about nine people in that back room: about forty-one names, in the order on the sheet, at the speed of a person reading a list, with nothing done to the voice, no stop at any of them, no return to any of them, and no words between any two of them. She stated before beginning that it was not going to do anything and that about four people should notice she knew that, and gave the reason in one sentence: that a page nobody has read is a page nobody can close. About nine people were present: the man of twenty-two, a woman of twenty-four, a first-year of nineteen, a man of fifty-one with a tool bag, a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, a woman of about thirty-four with a district jacket who made the one call that page produced in an earlier movement and has not been asked about it and is not to be, and about three others not described here. Nobody spoke between the names. The sheet was then put down on the table writing side up, which is the only time in about four years it has been put on a table rather than back in a stack. It was read once and will not be read again in this case.**

*What it did not do, stated by two people.* **A man of fifty-one said that nine minutes produced in that room not one new fact about any of the forty-one, and was told yes, and that the holder had known that from about halfway down the list and read the remaining half anyway, and asked that it be noticed that she read the other half anyway. A page nobody has read is a page nobody can close and a page somebody has read is a page nobody can close either, and the difference is about her and not about the room. Nobody improved on either statement and nobody asked her to read it again and she did not offer.**

*The request that was refused.* **A man of about forty-nine from a district office asked, having come to that counter on a matter of his own and having asked one question in the corridor on the way in, whether the page could be recorded properly with a reference, and observed that about nine were rung, that one was not and did not happen, and that thirty-one others were not, and that this is the most useful fact anybody in this city has produced in about four years. He was refused. The ground given: that there is a district that could adopt it, that it would be gone in about nine weeks, that it would be right, and that this is what is wrong with it; that the box on that counter is ruled in pencil under a fourth field, holds one name, carries no number, is not a system, cannot be emptied and is not going anywhere; and that if a page of forty-one went into a system then thirty-one people would have been on a list a person with a budget can look at, and that in about four years there would be about four hundred of them, every one entered by somebody kind. Pressed as to whether she was refusing a district on principle, she answered that she is refusing it because it works, and that if it did not work she would not be able to do it and would be doing it. Nobody in the room agreed with her out loud. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts said nothing and was not asked and is not to be asked.**

*The box in pencil.* **On that counter, ruled under the fourth field, one name in it, no reference number, not on a system, not movable, because the page cannot come out of the stack and the stack cannot come out of the counter and a hole in a stack is a thing somebody asks about. On that day it was not emptied, it took no second name and was neither offered nor asked for one, it was not given a reference number and none was requested, it was not moved onto a system and nothing about it was entered anywhere, and no district adopted it or asked to. Nobody thanked the woman of fifty-three, who is owed nothing, who has been asked about four things in nineteen years, and who was not asked a fifth.**

*State and boundaries.* **About nine people in a cold room and a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door from about two, described by a count, not asked anything, not thanked, and people there wanted to and did not; a first-year of nineteen said one sentence about the afternoon, that a woman of fifty-three read forty-one names out loud for nine minutes in a room with nine people in it, that this is the whole of the day, and that nobody is going to make it into a thing. Fourteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, the first a cook's and still the first. Four words in chalk and one unremovable grey ghost over a door. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened. No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution was created by anybody in this case; the sheet of about forty-one names, the four stacks, the list behind the sheet and the district's own business are other parties' and predate this case, and the pencil box is not a page, a register or a form and was not treated as one. No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by him, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception, and the condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased. No panel and no marker. Nine keys needed and two existing, count not stated. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. No message has arrived from anybody. The standing question is not stated here and carries no number. Entry 272.**
