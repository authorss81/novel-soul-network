# Chapter 268 — Four Hundred Hands

The Monday of week ninety-four was the day about four hundred people in about nine buildings in this city were found to have written down four words they had heard somebody say, in four years, in a different hand every time, about two hundred and forty of them on one sheet of paper about the size of a piece of kitchen paper in a service corridor in Crown Terrace, and nobody had been looking for it, and a cook of twenty-seven who is not known to have written any of them was standing in the same room while it was said, and nobody told her, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

Ten on the docket and ten in date, and a call in Nine Bridges was closed by somebody else before he got there, and he was not told that it had been reassigned and he did not ask.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its seventy-seventh day and it is a hundred and eighty-two days old.

---

He was in Crown Terrace on the Monday morning for a door closer on the ninth floor of a building he has worked in four times, and the closer was fine, and he was waiting for a part, and there is a service corridor on that floor with a ledge along it at about waist height where a person can put a cup down.

There is about two hundred and forty lines of pencil on a sheet of paper about the size of a piece of kitchen paper, and the sheet is held to the wall at the top corner with a piece of tape about the size of a stamp, and it has been there long enough that the paper has gone the colour of the wall behind it.

Every line is the same four words. He counted them twice, in about four minutes, and got two hundred and thirty-eight, and then a woman of about thirty-four who was going the other way up the corridor counted them with him and got two hundred and forty.

Every line is in a different hand. He is a man who puts a number on about nine hundred doors a year and he knows what two hundred and forty different people's handwriting looks like, and he said that out loud on the stair, once, and did not say it again.

Going back four years, in the nine buildings he has worked in, there are about four hundred lines of the same four words: about two hundred and forty on that sheet, about ninety on a landing on the fourth in a second building, about forty on a wall by a bin store in a third, and the rest on about nine notices in as many more and in about four ledgers in four offices, in a different hand every time.

Nobody made that sheet. Nobody has ever had the intention of making that sheet. It began because a person wrote four words on a wall in that corridor, and a second person came along with nothing to write on and put them on the ledge, and the third person found the sheet and put two lines on it, and there has been a ledge in that corridor for four years.

---

He said it out loud in the cold room off the service road at about half past six, in about nine seconds, standing up, to about nine people.

"**About four hundred lines in four years in about nine buildings, and about two hundred and forty of them on one sheet of paper the size of a piece of kitchen paper, and every one of them in a different hand, and not one person in four years has been told they were allowed to write it down.**"

Nobody thanked him. He did not ask to be thanked and he said afterwards that he had been saying numbers in about four rooms for about four years and this is the first one he has said that has grown since he last said it.

Nobody wrote it down. He did not take a photograph of the sheet. He said he had not taken a photograph of it and that he had wanted to, at about half past ten that morning, for about four seconds, and that he had put the phone back in his pocket because the corridor had a camera in it and he does not put things in buildings where he has been asked to leave a name.

---

A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink in that room and had been since about half past four.

The four words are hers. They are the first line on a hardboard on two nails in that room and they have been the first line since the Thursday of week seventy, and she wrote them about a term and a half ago and has never repeated them and said at the time that she would not say the shape of a thing a fifth time and has not said it a fifth time since.

She was not told. Nobody told her, nobody asked her, and the whole of that room wanted very much to tell her and did not, and one of them was asked by a man of fifty-one with a tool bag at about ten past seven why not, and the answer came from a first-year of nineteen from the end chair with her boots off, and it was ten words and it was not manners.

"**They are hers. Four hundred lines is a bigger surface.**"

A cook of twenty-seven, without turning round, said: "**Say the rest of it, because that is half of it.**"

"**You wrote four words on a hardboard in a room that has a plate on the outside of it with a telephone number on it and no name, and you have been telling this room for a term that you cannot be findable, and in about four years you are going to be the author of four hundred lines in about nine buildings and there is no plate on any of them and no way to take any of it off any of them, and the only person who can prevent that is you, and the way you prevent it is by never being told, and the only way you can be never told is if everybody in this room keeps it, and that is why nine people just said nothing at the same time and I have never seen nine people say nothing at the same time before.**"

Nobody improved on it. She did not say that it was the right call. She washed a mug that did not need washing, which is what she does.

---

Then the other thing, and it is a man and not a sheet, and it is the reason nobody is going to be able to do anything with any of this.

"**The oldest hand on that sheet is in the second column and about nine lines down and two people in this room have seen it before and neither of them is going to say so, and the man who wrote it was given an answer by a stranger in this building about four years ago, and he was sixty-one, and he has gone quiet, and there is a room of about forty people in Crown Terrace that knows he still has nine words of his own and there is no way anybody in this case can hand him four words, because he cannot be found, and he is not going to be found this term.**"

"**Who is he.**"

"**He is not going to be said out loud in this room by anybody, and that is not manners, and I will give you the ground instead: a man of sixty-one who was given an answer by a stranger and has gone quiet is the only person in this case who is owed something, and the only way to pay him is to arrive, and there is no mechanism in this city for arriving at a person, and that is the whole of it.**"

He was not given the four words. Nothing has been sent to him and nothing is going to be. Nobody in that room rang him, wrote to him, went to any building he has been in, or asked anybody who knows him to do any of those things, and one person in that room thought about doing one of those things and did not.

A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was in that room and said nothing at all about it, and nobody asked him, and somebody in that room thought about what he has said twice about lists of buildings being lists of people and did not say it out loud, and he has not been asked and is not to be asked.

---

At about ten a cook of twenty-seven put the ring on at about half past eight as she has done for about twenty-two weeks, and about nine people were still in the room, and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and nobody asked her anything and nobody apologised to her.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wet, at about ten, and the rain got into the first line and he wrote it again inside the door.

*271. Monday of week 94, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one sheet, about two hundred and forty hands, and seventy-seven days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Ten on the docket and ten in date. A call in Nine Bridges was closed by another person before his arrival; he was not told of the reassignment and did not ask, and the entry records that he did not ask and attributes it to nothing. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in the room off the service road is on its seventy-seventh day off, unpaid, and unresolved. The room is a hundred and eighty-two days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and eighty-six days in the rail and is a third object and is not in that room. The twelve lines on the hardboard are a hundred and two days old, the thirteenth is fifty-three, and the fourteenth is eighteen. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened and unchanged.**

*What was found, and by accident.* **A man of fifty-one with a tool bag, on a job in Crown Terrace, waiting for a part for a door closer that was sound. A ledge runs that corridor at about waist height. A sheet about the size of a piece of kitchen paper is taped to the wall at the top corner, aged to the colour of the wall behind it. Two counts of the lines on it, in about four minutes, by him and by a woman of about thirty-four going the other way: two hundred and thirty-eight and two hundred and forty. Every line identical; every hand different. Across the nine buildings he has worked in, over four years, about four hundred lines of the same four words: two hundred and forty on the sheet in one corridor, about ninety on a fourth-floor landing in a second building, about forty by a bin store in a third, the rest across about nine notices in as many more and four ledgers in four offices. No person made the sheet and none ever intended to. It began with one person writing on a wall, a second with nothing to write on and a ledge to write on, and a ledge that has been there for four years. Nobody had been looking for it. He did not photograph it, said he had wanted to for about four seconds, and gave as the reason that the corridor has a camera in it and that he does not put things in buildings where he has been asked to leave a name.**

*Whose four words, and why nobody said anything.* **The first line of the hardboard, since the Thursday of week seventy, in a cook of twenty-seven's hand. She was at a sink from about half past four and was not told, not asked and not thanked; everybody in that room wanted to tell her and did not. Asked why, a first-year of nineteen gave ten words and then, on being pressed for the rest of it, the whole ground in her own order: the authorship is hers; four hundred lines is a larger surface than a hardboard in a building with a number and no name on it; she has told that room for a term that she cannot be findable; in about four years she would be the author of about four hundred lines in about nine buildings with no plate on any of them and no way to take one off any of them; only she can prevent that; the only way she is prevented from being told is if everybody in the room keeps it; and this is why nine people said nothing at the same time. Nobody improved on it. She does not know. She washed a mug that did not need washing.**

*The oldest hand, and the one person who is owed something.* **That the oldest hand on the sheet is in the second column, about nine lines down, was said in that room, together with the statement that two people present have seen that hand before and neither is going to say so. The man who wrote it was given an answer by a stranger in that building about four years ago, was sixty-one, and has gone quiet. A room of about forty people in Crown Terrace knows that he still has nine words of his own. Nobody in this case can hand him four words, because he cannot be found, and there is no mechanism in this city for arriving at a person; that is the whole of the reason and it is not manners. He was not named, he was given nothing, and no person in this case is going to give him anything. Nobody in that room rang him, wrote to him, went to a building he has been in, or asked anybody who knows him to do any of those four things, and one person thought about doing one and did not. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was present, said nothing about it, was not asked, and is not to be asked.**

*State and boundaries.* **About nine people in a cold room and a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door from about two, described by a count, not asked anything, not thanked, not apologised to, and not the subject of a question. Fourteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, the first a cook's and still the first. Four words in chalk and one unremovable grey ghost over a door, in a different building and a different object, and the sheet in a service corridor in Crown Terrace is a fourth surface and is not either of them. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened. No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution was created by anybody in this case; the sheet in the corridor, the four ledgers and the notices are other buildings' and predate this case, and the sheet is not a list that anybody made. No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by him, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception, and the condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased. No panel and no marker. Nine keys needed and two existing, count not stated. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. No message has arrived from anybody. The standing question is not stated here and carries no number. Entry 271.**
