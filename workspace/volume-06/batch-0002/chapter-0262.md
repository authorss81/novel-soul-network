# Chapter 262 — A Hearing Is A Book

The twelfth sitting of that Exchange fell on the Wednesday morning of week ninety-two, four weeks to the day after the eleventh, and the book was not opened, and the reason was hers and it was new, and the telephone count moved from forty-four to forty-seven and she gave the direction out loud once and did not look into it, and the ninth chair was against the wall where a young woman of twenty-one put it about four years ago, and nobody moved it back and nobody asked her why it is there.

---

The stair fire door in Nine Bridges was reported on the Monday and attended at about eleven and reset for the eighth time in about a fortnight, and the frame was as he had described it, and there is no fault in it that will go in a box.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its sixty-fifth day and it is a hundred and seventy days old.

---

The room is above the line in a building in Saltmarket and the holder of the room says so at the start of every sitting, and the shutters are up and the light in it is the flat light of a room that has been on the same side of a street since before anybody in it was working.

Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in. The ninth stands against the wall facing away from the room. There is a table, a tin that has never had a lock on it, and a book with four columns and forty-six lines in it, and it is a hundred and four days since the last line went in.

Eight people. The young man of twenty-one who has been coming since he was seventeen. The young woman of twenty-one who has been coming since she was fifteen, who put the ninth chair where it is, who has never been asked to move it and has never been named in that room and who is not asked anything this morning.

Nobody wrote down that she was there. Nobody writes it down.

---

A man of about forty-four, who has come twice and has not asked for anything, said it at about eleven minutes in, and he said it as a favour and not as a demand.

"**We should put the hearing in the book. Somebody in this room was in that room on Monday. If it is not written down then in about four years it is a thing three of us remember.**"

Bea Nunn put two fingers on the cover and left them there.

"**No. And the reason is not the one I have given you at every sitting since you started coming.**"

"**You have never given me a reason. You have given the rule.**"

"**A hearing is a book. And a book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call.**"

She took about four seconds over the rest of it.

"**Not one person in that room on Monday telephoned me. None of them telephoned this desk, none of them came to this room, and none of them has ever been in this room. If we write Monday down then the thing that goes in this book is not what anybody said, it is what we made of a morning, and this book has never once in nineteen years held what anybody made of a morning.**"

"**Then what does it hold.**"

"**What one person said to me in this room with their face in front of mine and nobody else listening.**"

Nobody thanked her for it. It is the first time in nineteen years that the reason has been about the room and not about a person, and about four people in that room heard the difference, and the man of forty-four said nothing, and the young man of twenty-one said nothing, and the young woman of twenty-one said nothing at all.

---

The count came at about ten past ten, unasked, as it comes.

"**Forty-four to forty-seven. Three in four weeks, and I am telling you the direction and not the arithmetic, and I did the arithmetic in my head about four minutes ago and I am not going to do it again in front of eight people.**"

"Give us the rest of it."

"**Forty-seven calls and forty-six lines. The book was two ahead of the telephone four weeks ago and the telephone is one ahead of the book now, and the gap has changed its sign, and I have not looked into it, and I am not going to, and I would like nobody in this room to convert those two numbers into each other or into a number of sittings.**"

"**That is the first time you have said the sign changed.**"

"**That is the first time it has.**"

"**How many times have you announced the count.**"

"**Seventeen. Twelve of those are the twelve Wednesdays this book holds and five predate it, and there is a sheet of paper with the five on it that I have not shown anybody and that I am not going to.**"

---

Then the young man of twenty-one said, to the room and not to her, that the ninth chair had been moved again before he got there, and that he was not reporting it, he was saying it out loud because there was a person in the room.

She said: "**A chair is a chair, and anybody who wants to move a chair in this room may move a chair in this room, and that is the whole of what I have to say about furniture.**"

"**That is the fifth time you have said it and I am keeping count and you have never confirmed the count and I have never been asked to stop keeping it.**"

"**Then don't stop. I am not going to confirm it, because confirming it is a minute, and I do not take minutes, and that is the one thing in this room I have never been tempted about.**"

The chair was against the wall at the end of the morning. It was not moved back. The woman who put it there was in the room for about eleven minutes and was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not given a reason by anybody, and her reason is not in this book either, and it is not in any of the four columns.

---

There was one more thing and it came at the end, and it is in the entry below because it is the kind of thing that gets said once in a room and then never again.

One of the three calls was from a person who has not come to that room and who was not expected to.

"**I did not ring him back.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because if I ring him and ask whether he is coming on Wednesday then the thing he tells me is the answer to my question and not his account, and I would not be writing it down if he said no, and I would be writing it down if he said yes, and either way it would be the first line in this book in nineteen years that I put in because I asked somebody a question about it.**"

Nobody in that room asked her to ring him. She did not say that she would ring him. He did not come.

---

At about half past six in the cold room off the service road there were about eleven people, and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and had been since about two, and a cook of twenty-seven put a cup of tea in front of her and said nothing at all, which has happened on every working day for sixty-five days.

Talia Venn was in for about an hour and said four sentences and all four of them were about Monday, and one of them was that a woman of sixty-one in Saltmarket had just refused to write a morning down because nobody in it had telephoned her, and that this is the same reason she has been refusing for nineteen years, and that hearing it in a different building on a Monday is what took.

Nobody thanked her for the Monday. Nobody thanked anybody for anything. About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and the figure is on the back of a woman's own card, and it is not a rate.

He wrote one thing on a service road, in the wind, at about ten.

*265. Wednesday of week 92, at about ten on a service road in the wind, and this entry is one unopened book, one sign that changed, and sixty-five days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The stair fire door in Nine Bridges was reported on the Monday, attended at about eleven, reset for the eighth time in about a fortnight, and found as described; the description box carries a stamped number and nothing in the frame supports a fault, and the entry records the finding and attributes it to nothing. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in the room off the service road is on its sixty-fifth day off, unpaid, and no fund, collection, subscription, grant, list, minute, notice or appeal exists or was proposed. The room is a hundred and seventy days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and seventy-four days in the rail and is a third object and is not in that room and is not in Saltmarket either. The twelve lines on the hardboard are ninety days old, the thirteenth is forty-one, and the fourteenth is six.**

*The sitting and the room.* **The twelfth, on the Wednesday of week ninety-two, four weeks to the day after the eleventh, in a room its holder states is above the line. Nine chairs in a curve with the ends turned in, the ninth against the wall facing away. A tin that has never had a lock. A four-column book at forty-six lines, the last line a hundred and four days old. Eight people present. A young man of twenty-one in attendance since seventeen kept a count and was not to be stopped. A young woman of twenty-one in attendance since fifteen put the ninth chair where it stands, has never been asked to move it, has never been named in that room, and was not asked a question and was given no reason and is not the first-year of nineteen in the other building; there is one nineteen-year-old in this case and it is the other one.**

*The item and the reason, which is new.* **A man of about forty-four asked that the Monday hearing be entered. He was refused, and the rule was given in its usual form and then withdrawn and replaced, and the replacement is the first reason given in nineteen years that is about the room rather than about a person: that a hearing is a book, and a book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call; that not one person in that hearing had telephoned this desk or attended this room; and that what would have gone in was not what anybody said but what eight people made of a morning, which this book has never once held. The book holds what one person said to the holder with their face in front of her and nobody else listening. The book was not opened. Nobody thanked her.**

*The figures, and the sign.* **Telephone calls from forty-four to forty-seven, three in four weeks, announced unasked at about ten past ten, with the arithmetic done in the head about four minutes earlier and refused in the room. Stated: that the book stood two lines ahead of the telephone four weeks ago and that the telephone is one line ahead of the book now, that the gap has changed its sign, that she has not looked into it and will not, and that no person present may convert the two figures into one another or into a number of sittings. The count has now been announced seventeen times in total, twelve of those being the twelve Wednesdays this book holds and five predating it on a separate sheet not shown to anybody. The book did not open and is at forty-six lines, and no line went in, because no line goes in except where a caller says the words to her face.**

*The call not returned.* **One of the three is from a person not in attendance and not expected. She did not ring him back, and gave the reason: that a question asked by her would make any answer a reply to her question rather than an account, and that she would not enter the first line in nineteen years that she had entered because she had asked somebody about it. Nobody in the room asked her to ring him, she did not say she would, and he did not come.**

*The ninth chair.* **Reported moved again before the sitting by a person not in attendance, in the same words as at the two previous sittings, by a young man of twenty-one who said he was not reporting it and was saying it because there was a person in the room. The holder's answer, given for the fifth time: that a chair is a chair and that anybody may move a chair in that room. He kept his count; she did not confirm it, on the standing ground that confirming it is a minute and that she does not take minutes. The chair was against the wall at the close. It was not moved back, its mover is not named here, and no reason for it exists in that room or in this entry.**

No panel and no marker. Nine keys needed and two existing, count not stated. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. No message has arrived from anybody. The standing question is not stated here and carries no number. Entry 265.**
