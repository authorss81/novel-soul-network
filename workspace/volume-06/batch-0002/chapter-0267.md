# Chapter 267 — The Back Of His Own Hand

The second Friday of this rotation ran in a company training room off a corridor in Crown Terrace with nothing to hand out, and at ten past five that morning the telephone on a plate on the outside of a shuttered unit in the Lower Wards rang and was answered, and the man of thirty-four who works nights said about nine sentences about a plate in a service cupboard in Nine Bridges that was not there in the spring, and what is wrong with it is that nothing is wrong with it, and he did not touch it, and he wrote on the back of his own hand that he had not touched it, and nobody removed it and nobody blamed anybody.

---

The stair fire door in Nine Bridges was attended at about half past two on the Thursday, reset for the tenth time in about a fortnight, and is expected back, and the standing water in a basement in an unnamed district was not reported.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in that room was on its seventy-fourth day and it is a hundred and seventy-nine days old.

---

He has been finding things on that shift for about nine years and has never once told anybody what he found. He works nights, and about four nights a week he is in a building in Nine Bridges between one and five in the morning with a torch and a key ring that belongs to a company.

The plate is on a landing in a residential block, inside a service cupboard whose door does not lock and has not locked since before he started, and it is a plate. It is a rectangle with a number on it and four things printed in a box on the face of it: a starting thing you hold, steps, a place where you let go, and a line about what the practice must not touch.

The fifth thing is not on it. There is no beat on it and no instruction of any kind by which a person decides they are finished.

Nothing is wrong with it. The number on it is the number on the list for that block. The print is the same print as about nine hundred others. It is not scratched, it is not warm, it is not loose, and it is not in a place where plates go. It was not there in the spring and he has been in that cupboard in the spring and in the autumn and in the winter.

He looked at it for about four minutes. He did not touch it, he did not open the box behind it, he did not take a photograph of it, he did not write on the wall next to it, and he did not report it, because reporting it is a thing a person does on a Monday to a person in an office and he is not going to be the person who made about four people in a room off a service road look at a plate.

---

He rang the number on the plate at about ten past five and the man of twenty-two answered it, having come off a job at about half past four.

"**It is in a service cupboard on the fourth landing at Nine Bridges and it was not there in the spring and I want you to know that before I say anything else about it, because that is the part that matters and the rest of it is a plate.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**A plate. A number on it that is the right number for that block. Four things in a box on the face of it. A starting thing you hold, steps, a place where you let go, and a line about what the practice must not touch. That is four. There is no fifth. I do not know what the fifth one is and I have never known and I have never asked.**"

"**Have you touched it.**"

"**No. I have not touched it and I did not open the box behind it and I did not take a picture of it, and I have not reported it, and I am not going to report it, and I am telling you instead and you can do what you want with it.**"

"**Why me.**"

"**Because you are the only person I know who has ever said no to a thing in a room instead of yes, and because I am not going to say this twice and I am not going to say it on a piece of paper, and if you write it down I will know, and that is the whole of the arrangement.**"

"**Then I am not going to write it down.**"

"**You are going to write it down. That is what you are for. I have known you for about a term and I know what you do with a thing at about ten at night. Do not tell me you are not, because I would rather be written down than be asked twice.**"

Nobody asked him twice. That is the entire extent of it.

---

He was asked, at about ten past five, what the fifth thing was.

He did not say. He has not said to anybody in this case, in a room or in a corridor, and the reason he gave was that telling a person who works nights what the fifth part of a practice is would make him the second person in this case who has been told, and the first one is a first-year of nineteen who has about eleven hundred leaflets with her own name at the bottom of them, and neither of them owes the other anything, and the man of thirty-four works nights and is owed nothing by either of them.

What was recorded instead, in a book kept by one man against himself, is that there is a fifth thing and that it is not on the plate and that nobody in this case has offered to explain it to anybody.

---

The plate is not removed. It is not explained. It is not blamed on anybody, and there is nobody in this case it could be blamed on, and the man of thirty-four who found it has not gone back into that cupboard since and has not been asked whether he is sure.

---

The rota ran in the evening, in nine folding chairs, with eleven people in them and two against the wall and a laminated sheet on a post by the door with nine words on it and no name and no date and no company and no version.

The woman of about thirty-four was on that list for the first time in about four years. She was in the second row. She did not ask anything at all.

On the last run she got to the end of the hold, stopped, and finished, in about four seconds, and the whole room went back to the start, and the instructor did not say anything and nobody in that room said anything and about four people in it were thinking about it, and the man of twenty-two, who was in the fourth row against the wall with the word available, did not say it and was not asked to and will not be asked to.

Nobody thanked anybody. The instructor said at the end of the evening the sentence she says at the end of every one of these, and about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and the figure is on the back of a woman's own card, and it is not a rate.

---

At about ten, on a service road, in the dark, he wrote it down.

He wrote down that a plate was found, and that a man who works nights did not touch it, and that a plate is a plate and nothing is wrong with it, and that the man who found it told him not to and he wrote it down anyway, and that a record of that kind is the only kind there is.

He did not write down the number of the block, and he did not write down the landing, and he did not write the man's hours in it, and about four people who would read that book in four years would not be able to find the plate from it.

He did not write any of that down either. That is the whole of what happened on the Friday of week ninety-three.

*270. Friday of week 93, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one plate, one refusal, and seventy-four days.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Grade three, twenty to eight, on a card in date, the exception recorded and renewed for the term in a system in a box that says *No reason given by holder*. Eleven on the week's list, about four of them not on it. The stair fire door in Nine Bridges was attended at about half past two on the Thursday, reset for the tenth time in about a fortnight, expected back. The basement in an unnamed district was not reported. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in the room off the service road is on its seventy-fourth day off, unpaid, and unresolved. The room is a hundred and seventy-nine days old; a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a hundred and eighty-three days in the rail and is a third object and is not in that room. The twelve lines on the hardboard are ninety-nine days old, the thirteenth is fifty, and the fourteenth is fifteen. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened. The city's standing weekly figure is unaltered: about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, the number is on the back of a woman's own card, it is on no form, and it is not a rate.**

*The plate.* **Found on a night shift by a man of thirty-four who works nights, on the fourth landing of a residential block in Nine Bridges, inside a service cupboard whose door does not lock. It is a plate. It carries the number that the list for that block carries, and four things in a box on the face of it: a starting thing that is held, steps, a place where the holder lets go, and a line about what the practice must not touch. The fifth is absent and there is no instruction on the plate of any kind by which a person determines that they are finished. It was not there in the spring; the finder has been in that cupboard in the spring, the autumn and the winter. It is not scratched, warm, loose, or in a place where plates go. Nothing is wrong with it, and that is the finding, and the whole of the finding, and no person in this case has been able to say what is wrong with it.**

*What was not done to it.* **It was not touched. The box behind it was not opened. No photograph of it was taken. Nothing was written on the wall beside it. It was not reported to anybody in an office, on the stated ground that a man who works nights at one in the morning is not going to be the person who made about four people in a room off a service road look at a plate. He has not been back into that cupboard and has not been asked whether he is sure, and the plate was not removed, not explained, and is not blamed on anybody, there being nobody in this case it could be blamed on.**

*The call, at about ten past five on the Friday morning.* **Incoming to the number on the plate, answered by the man of twenty-two at about half past four off a job. About nine sentences. He gave the fact that mattered first, which is the spring, and then the object, and then the reason for the call: that the man of twenty-two is the only person he knows who has ever refused a thing in a room rather than agreed to one; that he would not say it twice and would not put it on paper; and that if it were written down he would know, which is the whole of the arrangement. Told it would not be written down, he answered that it would be, that this is what the man of twenty-two does at about ten at night, and that he would rather be written down than asked twice. Nobody has ever asked him anything in this case and that is the entire extent of it.**

*The fifth thing, and who was not told.* **He was asked what the fifth thing was and he did not say. The ground given: that explaining the fifth part of a practice to a person who works nights would make him the second person in this case who has been told, the first being a first-year of nineteen with about eleven hundred leaflets carrying her own name at the bottom of them by her own choice; that neither of them owes the other anything; and that the man of thirty-four works nights and is owed nothing by either of them. What is on the page is that there is a fifth thing, that it is not on the plate, and that nobody in this case has offered to explain it to anybody, including the person who found it and including the man who wrote this.**

*The rota, and the four seconds again.* **Nine folding chairs, eleven people, two against the wall, the laminated sheet with nine words and no name, date, company or version. A woman of about thirty-four was on the list for the first time in about four years, was in the second row, and asked nothing at all. On the last run she reached the end of the hold, stopped, and finished, in about four seconds, and the room restarted. The instructor said nothing. Nobody in that room said anything. About four people in it were thinking about it. The man of twenty-two was in the fourth row against the wall with the word available, did not say it, and was not asked to and will not be asked to. Nobody thanked anybody.**

*What was written down, and what was not.* **Four items went into that book at about ten on a service road: a plate was found, a man who works nights did not touch it, nothing about the plate is wrong, and it was written down after he asked that it not be, the recorded reason being that a record of that kind is the only kind there is. Three items were withheld by the same hand: the number of the block, the landing, and the finder's hours. Nothing else about the call was entered, and the entry is the whole of the Friday of week ninety-three.**

*State and boundaries.* **Nine chairs and a cold room. Fourteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, the first a cook's and still the first. Four words in chalk and one unremovable grey ghost over a door. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened. No page, minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet, registration or constitution was created by anybody in this case; the plate, the list it matches and the nine hundred of them are not ours and were not there before this case. No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by him, and in particular no explanation of the fifth part of a practice to anybody, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception, and the condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased. No panel and no marker. Nine keys needed and two existing, count not stated. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. No message has arrived from anybody. The standing question is not stated here and carries no number. Entry 270.**
