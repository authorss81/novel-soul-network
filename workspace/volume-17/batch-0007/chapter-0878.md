# Chapter 878 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room In The Evening Where A Man Who Is On No Body In This City Was Asked About A Thing Four Hundred People Do Every Day And Gave Nine Words To A Face, And Nobody In That Room Beat Him And Nobody In That Room Agreed With Him And The Sheet With Both Of The Things On It Went In A Drawer

**A bench until two and a counter until about six, and then a room in the evening with about nine people in it, and one sentence said out loud in about nine seconds by a man who is on no body in this city, and about nine people in that room who each had a reason to want that sentence not to have been said and did not use it.**

---

That Monday brought ten of them to that counter, and ten dates went onto the sheet, the tenth of them at about ten to five, and the shutter was down at about ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road have stood for one thousand six hundred and ten days, and that is two hundred and thirty weeks to the day. Of the nineteen ruled lines on the board on two nails, the sixteenth has been ruled for one thousand four hundred days, two hundred weeks to the day. Copied out in nine hands, the front of one page has been in those nine hands for one thousand one hundred and seventy-six days, a hundred and sixty-eight weeks to the day, and no two of those nine have been brought together.**

**No figure was said in any room in this city on that Monday and none was announced anywhere, and there is a Wednesday of every fourth week sixteen days ahead of it that carries one.**

---

**The reason was on a sheet in a room off a service road from about half past six on that Monday, and about nine people were in that room, and the sheet had been in that room for six days and had been read four times.**

A man of about thirty-three who writes this city's wording had brought it to that room on that Monday because about nine people had asked him to bring it, and he brought it and read out the reason again, and it took him about nine seconds.

Four hundred people do this thing with their hands every day and nobody in this city has ever asked them whether it is safe.

**Nobody in that room said the reason had got weaker in six days and nobody in that room said it had got stronger, and about four people in that room have said since that they expected it to have got weaker and did not.**

---

**And about half an hour later the other question went into a face in front of about nine people, once, and the man it went to was the one who has been over there and done it twice.**

A woman of about thirty-eight asked him, standing, in about nine seconds, what a body that cannot reach four hundred people has to say about a thing those four hundred people do with their hands.

He is not on that body and he is not on any body in this city. He has no post and no vote and no minute and he was asked because he has done the thing twice with his own hands.

He said nine words, and he said them in about nine seconds, and he said them into her face and not into the room.

He said: nobody over there has ever asked us about it.

---

**And about nine people in that room heard all nine of those words and the argument that came with them, and it was made properly, and it stood.**

He said that the four hundred have been doing it for longer than this city has records and that every one of them has been asked about it by somebody who does it themselves or by nobody.

He said in about nine seconds that a person who does a thing is not the person who gets to decide whether it is safe, and that this is the whole of what he came to say and that he has said it twice before.

**Nobody in that room beat it and nobody in that room agreed with it, and the second of those two things is not a smaller thing than the first.**

A man of about fifty-two said afterwards, in about nine seconds, that he had wanted it beaten for about nine days, and that there was nothing in it to beat, and that a person who does a thing saying that he is not the person to ask about it is not making an argument, he is telling you where you are standing.

**Nobody in that room said that was an answer and nobody said it was not an answer, and about four people in that room have said since that they do not know which of those two it was.**

---

**The sheet in that room has the reason on one side of it. It has the nine words on the other, in the hand of a woman of about thirty-four, written at about half past eight on that Monday, and she asked him whether she could write them down and he said yes in about four seconds.**

Nobody in that room wanted a copy and nobody in that room asked for one and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted a copy and did not ask.

The sheet went into a drawer in that room at about nine, with the two sides facing each other, and it has not been taken out.

**Nobody in this city has been told what is written on both sides of that sheet, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have told somebody and did not.**

---

**And the man who said the nine words is on no body in this city and nobody thanked him that night and nobody in this city has thanked him since.**

He has said since that he did not expect to be thanked and that he had made himself say it in front of about nine people because otherwise it would have stayed a thing he thought, and that thinking a thing is not the same as saying it out loud in a room.

**Nobody in that room said he had been brave and nobody said he had been made a fool of, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted to say one of those and did not.**

He went on doing it after that Monday. Nobody asked him to stop and nobody said he should go on, and about nine people in that room have each said since that they could have argued for either and argued for neither.

---

**He was in that room and nothing at all was asked of him and nothing was put in front of him, and what he said at about twenty past eight was about the drawer.**

He said that the drawer that sheet went into has a runner on it that has been oiled about four years ago and has not been oiled since, and that a thing put into a drawer that runs is a thing that will be found eventually by somebody who has no idea what it is.

**Nobody in that room asked him who that somebody would be, nobody there came back with a second question, and he said afterwards that he had been in that room about four weeks before he noticed the runner.**

---

**And that Monday is not on the register, and the reason it is not on the register is on this page and not in anybody's mouth.**

The register of correct acts that changed nothing stood at four when that room filled and stands at four now. The thing done in that room was correct and true, it cost a man of about thirty-four nothing at all, and it changed nothing whatever.

**Nobody in this city counts that register and about four people in this city have said since that a true sentence in a room is not an act, and that this is why the figure has not moved in about nine years.**

---

**The remainder of that Monday went into a fourth district, and it was about two hours of it and four jobs.**

**A handrail on a gangway in the same yard had been made up from two lengths of different diameter with a sleeve over the join, and the sleeve had not been fixed at the top.**

He took the sleeve off, made one length out of the two, and left the gangway with one rail on it.

**Forty-four pounds. He told the man that a sleeve over a join in a handrail is a handrail with a place to come apart, and that about four gangways in that yard have rails like that, and that one of them has been reported as a rail that moves for about four years.**

---

**A bearing in the same building had been fitted with the grease nipple turned into a corner, where it could not be reached without taking something else off first.**

He moved the nipple to where a thumb goes, and greased it.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a nipple in a corner is a nipple that is not greased, and that about four bearings in that building are nippleed into corners, and that one of them has been reported as a bearing that runs hot for about four years.**

---

**A gate in the same yard had a closing stop fitted with a screw in a slotted hole, so that the gate could be pushed past its own stop by anybody leaning on it.**

He packed the hole, fitted a bolt in the middle of it, and proved the gate shut.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a stop in a slotted hole is a suggestion to anybody in a hurry, that about four gates in that yard have stops like that, and that one of them has been a gate that will not stay shut for about four years.**

---

**A light on a machine in the same place had been earthed to its own switch plate, so that the earth depended on which switch was pressed.**

He took the earth off the plate and put it on the frame, and proved the fitting.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that an earth on a switch plate is an earth a person can press away, and that about four fittings in that building are earthed that way, and that one of them has been reported as a light that hums when it is on for about four years.**

**The sum of those four bills was a hundred and ten pounds on that Monday, exact.**

**At half past six a woman of about thirty was behind the shut door of the fourth of those four rooms, and the eighth of eight lay on a shelf at the back of that room at three hundred and ninety-nine days.**

**About that page: nobody in this city has asked her anything, nobody has asked her anything on any of the days of this stretch, and nobody is going to. What is on it is not given here.**
