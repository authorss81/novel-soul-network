# Chapter 875 — A Wednesday Of Every Fourth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And Then A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where About Nine People Were Present When A Number Was Said Once In A Woman's Face And Where A Sheet Of Nine Things Was Read Out In The Order The Forms Were Printed And Came Back Off The Table With The Space At The Foot Of It Empty

**The sixty-third sitting was held in the room on that first floor on that Wednesday evening and about nine people were in it, and a number was said once into the face of that room, and about an hour and a half of the day before that had been a counter. What was said after the number was not said by anybody who had been waiting about nine days to say it.**

---

That Wednesday's sheet carried twelve names by the time the day was over, and a date against every one of them, the twelfth of those dates taken at about twenty to five, and by about ten that shutter was down.

**The nineteenth of the ruled lines is the last one with anything on it and the eighteenth of the two has been ruled on the board on two nails for one thousand three hundred and sixteen days, which is a hundred and eighty-eight weeks to the day. One thousand two hundred and four days is how long a man of about fifty-one has stood on the north side of that room, a hundred and seventy-two weeks to the day. The sum still owed on the open page in front of him is one thousand two hundred and eighty-eight days, a hundred and eighty-four weeks to the day.**

---

**And that Wednesday came round on its four weeks, the same as every one of the three before it, and nobody in this city has said a figure out loud in a room in between, and the count goes up by one whether that book opens or not.**

By about half past six about nine people were up that stair and the last of them came in at about seven. The room was warm, the window had been open since the spring, and about four of that nine had come before the rest of them.

**The ninth chair in that room is hard against a wall with its back to everything that happens in it, and it did not move on that Wednesday, and on no page of this stretch of days is the person who would move it named.**

---

**A sheet of nine things came in a plain envelope, and nobody opened it until about ten past six, and then the woman at the head of that room read out the nine lines a man had brought her. She took them in the order they were printed in and she left the last one until last.**

The ninth of the nine is the one a person goes to when they want to say no. It has held that place for about nine years, and about four people in that room said on that Wednesday that they would put it first if anybody let them.

**Nobody in that room let anybody, nobody in that room has let anybody since, and the ninth was read out last.**

A man of about thirty-four who owns four vehicles said in about nine seconds that the ninth one is the one every person in that room thinks about first, and the man of about thirty-three said in about four seconds that nobody has ever let them, and did not finish it, and did not finish it later.

**About four people wrote nothing at all while those nine lines were being read and nobody in that room asked them why.**

---

**And then at about half past six the count was said once, into the face of the room and in about nine seconds, and that is the way it is said and it has been said that way for about nineteen years.**

She said: sixty-eight, of which sixty-three correspond.

She said it in about nine seconds and she did not say it twice and she did not explain it and she did not say what either of the two figures was for.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and then a man of about thirty-four said nothing at all, which is what a man in that room has done at every one of these, and the woman at the head of that room said nothing back.**

**The book in the green binding stood at sixty-seven lines when that room opened and it stood at sixty-seven lines when the shutter came down at about ten, and the tin beside it was not opened that evening and was on seventy-three.**

---

**A man of about thirty-eight asked her after about four minutes what the second figure counts, and she answered him in one sentence and did not answer it twice.**

She said that five of the things are older than that book and are not in it, and that they are written on a sheet she has never shown anybody, and that he was the first person to ask her in about nineteen years.

**Nobody in that room asked to see the sheet and nobody in that room has asked since, and about four people in that room have said since that they would rather she had not been asked at all.**

---

**And the thing that did not happen on that Wednesday was that anybody's hand went into anybody's bag.**

A woman of about thirty-four was in that room with a sheet folded in four in the inside pocket of her bag, and about nine people in that room knew she had it, and she did not take it out, and nobody asked her to.

She has said since that she had it in her hand under the table for about half an hour and that she kept thinking about the box at the bottom of a form, and that a sentence does not go in a box that size.

**Nobody in that room knew what she was thinking about and nobody in that room has asked her since, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have liked to know.**

---

**And at about twenty to seven a woman of about thirty-eight put one question to the man of about thirty-four who has been over there and done it twice, and he gave her an answer in about nine seconds.**

She asked whether this body is going to put something in the box at the bottom of the form.

He said: I do not know and I will not guess.

**Nobody in that room put a second question to him about it, nobody there has asked since, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted to know more of him and did not ask.**

The sheet went back into the plain envelope at about ten to eight with the space at the foot of it empty, and about nine people watched that happen and nobody said a word about it.

---

**He sat in that room for that evening, nothing was asked of him and nothing was put in front of him to sign, and he is not the person who signs anything in this stretch of days.**

He said afterwards, at about half past seven, that the book on that table has a spine that has been repaired twice with a length of tape and once with a piece of cloth, and that the last repair is under the third line from the bottom where nobody puts a thumb.

**Nobody in that room asked him which repair was the older of the three and he said he did not know either, and that he had been in that room for about four weeks before he noticed.**

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was the counter until about six and then about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A lamp on a gantry in the same yard had been left on through about nine daylight hours every week, because nobody had found the switch.**

He found the switch, which was behind a plate that had been painted over, and put a label on it.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a switch behind a painted plate is a switch nobody switches off, and that about four lamps in that yard are behind plates, and that one of them has been reported as a light that is on all day for about four years.**

---

**A belt on a fan in the same building had been fitted an inch too long, so that it was riding about a quarter of an inch below the pulley and had been getting warmer for about four years.**

He took the belt off, fitted one of the length that belongs there, and proved the fan on its own guard.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a belt below a pulley is a belt being dragged, and that about four belts in that building have been fitted long, and that one of them has been reported as a fan that smells hot for about four years.**

---

**A fire door on a stair in the same place had a wedge under it about two inches deep, put there by somebody who wanted it to stay open.**

He took the wedge out, closed the door, and left it on its closer.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a wedge under a fire door is a door that has been argued with once and won, and that about four fire doors in that building have something under them, and that one of them has been reported as a door that will not shut for about four years.**

---

**An earth on a machine in the same yard had been made on a length of water pipe that somebody else runs.**

He took it off the pipe, ran it back to the machine frame, and proved it.

**Eighteen pounds. He told the man that an earth on somebody else's pipe is an earth somebody else can turn off, and that about four machines in that yard are earthed on pipework, and that one of them has been reported as a fault nobody could find for about four years.**

**That Wednesday came to fifty-five pounds, exact, which is the whole of that Wednesday.**

**By half past six a woman of about thirty had her door shut on the fourth of those four rooms, and on the back shelf behind her the eighth of eight had been lying for three hundred and eighty-seven days.**

**Nobody in this city has put a question to her about that page, nobody has put one to her on any day of this stretch, and nobody is going to. What is written on it is not given here.**
