# Chapter 879 — A Monday, A Counter Until About Six And About Two Hours Of It, And A Form That Went Out Of That Counter On That Monday Carrying Two Lines About What This Building Is And What It Is Not And One Word In A Box At The Foot Of It Where A Date Should Be, And A Woman Of About Forty-Four Who Came To That Counter To Say Nine Words To A Man About A Fact Of Hers He Had Used In A Room She Was Not In

**A form went out of a counter in a second district on that Monday with two lines printed at the top of it that have never been at the top of anything this city sends, and at about half past three a woman came through that door and said nine words to a man about a thing he had said in a room she was not in. Nobody was thanked and nobody was forgiven.**

---

Nine were written down on that Monday, and nine dates were set against them, the ninth of those at about ten to five, and the shutter of that shop came down at about ten.

**Those four units have stood behind that service road for one thousand six hundred and seventeen days, which comes to two hundred and thirty-one weeks to the day. Line sixteen of those nineteen has been ruled for one thousand four hundred and seven days, which is two hundred and one weeks to the day. The front of one page in nine hands has been there for one thousand one hundred and eighty-three days, a hundred and sixty-nine weeks to the day, and no two of the nine have been laid beside any other.**

**Nothing was said in that building on that Monday that was a number, no count was announced anywhere, and there is a Wednesday of every fourth week nine days in front of it that carries one.**

---

**The form went out of that counter on that Monday with two lines printed at the top of it that have never been at the top of anything this city has sent anywhere.**

The first line says what that building is: a room in this city that keeps a book and says a number out loud four times a year in the same faces.

The second line says what that building is not: anybody who can tell you whether it is safe to go on doing a thing you already do.

**Nobody in that shop printed a third line and nobody in that shop has asked for one, and about four people who have read the form have said since that two lines is more than anything in this city has ever put at the top of anything.**

The form went out to every caller on that Monday and every one of them was handed it by a person who carried it out of that shop and put it into that hand, and about nine people in this city have one and about four of them have read the two lines at the top.

---

**And the box at the foot of it has one word in it, and the word is not a date, and the word is the one that has been in that box for about nine years.**

Nobody printed it on that Monday in a second place. Nobody asked for it to be printed anywhere, and the printers put one word in a box about the width of a thumb and that is all that happened to it.

**Nobody in that shop has said the word out loud to a customer and nobody in that shop has said it to anybody, and the word is not given on this page.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that the word has been the answer to that box since before anybody in this city had a question to put in it, and that nobody has ever asked what it is an answer to.

**Nobody in that shop answered her and nobody in that shop has answered her since.**

---

**And nobody banned the practice that week and nobody endorsed it, and about nine people in this city have said since that the second of those two is the harder of them and about four have said since that it was the first.**

A woman of about thirty-eight read out, at about half past one, that this body would not be asking about the practice again, and that it would not be saying anything about it either, and that the form was going out in the meantime.

She said in about nine seconds that a thing this body cannot do anything about should not keep being on the list of things this body does.

**Nobody in that shop argued with her and nobody in that shop agreed with her, and about four people in that shop have said since that they would rather it had stayed on the list.**

The man of about thirty-four who has been over there and done it twice was in that shop on that Monday, and nobody asked him about the practice and he did not raise it himself.

He said in about nine seconds that a thing being off a list is not the same as a thing being stopped, and that he knows that better than anybody in that shop.

**Nobody in that shop asked him what the difference is worth and he said he did not know, and that he had done the thing twice and could not say.**

---

**And at about half past three a woman of about forty-four came into that shop and did not trade, and stood at that counter, and nobody asked her what she wanted.**

She has been at that counter for about nine weeks of weekdays and about nine years in all, and she had a sheet folded in four in her bag the whole time and she did not take it out.

She said nine words to the man of about thirty-three, who was at that counter by chance and who had come in about nine minutes before she did, and she said them to his face and not to the shop.

She said: you told a room my week without asking me.

---

**Nobody in that shop said anything at all for about half a minute, and she used it.**

She said in about nine seconds that she is not going to be thanked for telling him, and that anybody who thanks her for it has made it a thing she did for them.

She said in about nine seconds that an apology would be a way of putting it back on her, and that she would be carrying it after he had finished being sorry.

**Both of those sentences are hers. He did not say either of them and he did not say the first of them twice and he did not answer the second of them.**

He did not apologise. He has said since that she told him not to and that he had not been going to, and that those are two different reasons and that the one he has is the one that came first.

**Nobody in that shop asked him to say anything and nobody in that shop has asked him since, and nobody in this city has thanked her for saying it.**

---

**And the nine words went into no form and into no file and were not read out again in that shop, and the sheet in her bag stayed in her bag, and both of those things are on the record here.**

A woman of about thirty-eight asked afterwards whether the two of them were going to do anything about it, and she said no in about four seconds, and then said in about nine seconds that there is no procedure for this either.

**Nobody in that shop asked her what there is, and there is not one, and about four people in that shop have said since that they would have liked there to be.**

---

**He came to that counter at about five, was asked nothing whatever, and the one thing he said while he was there was about the two lines.**

He said that the two lines are printed at the top and not in the middle, and that a person reads the top of a thing about nine times and reads the middle about nine times, and that whoever set that type up chose the top without anybody asking them to.

**Nobody in that shop knew who set the type up and nobody came back with a second question about it, and he said afterwards that the person is probably not in the building.**

He put his name to nothing in this stretch of days and nothing went out of that shop on that Monday carrying it, and there is nothing on that form that wants a name.

---

**What was left of that Monday went into about two hours of work in a fourth district.**

**A stop valve on a tank in the same yard had been painted the same colour as the pipework either side of it, so that about four people in that plant room had walked past it.**

He repainted it, put a tag on it, and left the handwheel where it was.

**Thirty-eight pounds. He told the man that a valve painted like a pipe is a valve nobody will close, and that about four valves in that plant room are painted like pipework, and that one of them has been reported as a valve that is off for about four years.**

---

**A tray in the same building had been cut round a bracket instead of being notched, so that the tray had to be lifted about nine millimetres to get the bracket in.**

He cut a proper notch and left the tray with no lift on it.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a tray cut round a bracket is a tray fitted over a bracket, and that about four trays in that building have been cut that way, and that one of them has been reported as a tray that is never quite in for about four years.**

---

**A washer on a bolt in the same place had been put under the head and over a painted surface, so that it had been holding paint rather than metal for about four years.**

He cleaned the surface back to metal, moved the washer onto bare steel, and refitted the bolt.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a washer over paint is a washer sitting on a room-temperature surface, and that about four bolts in that building were done that way, and that one of them has been reported as a bolt that moves for about four years.**

---

**A pipe in the same yard had been lagged with a length of insulation that stopped about nine millimetres before the joint, so that a band about as wide as a finger was doing the insulating.**

He took that band off, put in a full length of the same thickness, and re-banded the joint.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a band across an insulation joint is a joint that is insulated as well as a band, and that about four joints in that run are banded that way, and that one of them has been reported as a cold joint for about four years.**

**Those four bills, added, come to a hundred and fourteen pounds on that Monday, exact.**

**At half past six the fourth of those four rooms was shut, there was a woman of about thirty behind that door, and on the back shelf the eighth of eight lay at four hundred and six days.**

**Nobody in this city has asked that page anything and nobody ever will. What is on it is not given here, and the binder stayed shut on that Monday.**

*882.
Monday of week 299, at ten. That is the one hundred and eighty-fourth day of this stretch of days. Nine names and nine dates went onto that Monday, the last of the dates at about ten to five.
**There was no sitting on that Monday, and nothing was counted aloud in any room in this city on it. A form went out of that counter with two lines printed at the top of it, one saying what that building is and one saying what it is not.
The practice was not banned that week and was not endorsed, and this body will not ask about it again. The box at the foot of the form carries one word and it is not a date, and nobody said the word and nobody has asked what it answers.
A woman of about forty-four came to that counter at about half past three and said nine words to a man about a fact of hers that he had used in a room she was not in. He did not apologise, at her instruction, and she said in advance that she is not going to be thanked for telling him.
He was at that counter, nobody put anything to him, and he said one thing about two lines being printed at the top of a form. That Monday was worth a hundred and fourteen pounds, exact.**

*Conditions of the close.* **The green-bound book in the room upstairs stands at sixty-seven lines and the tin beside it at seventy-three, and no number on this page has been got out of setting one of those against the other.
That ninth chair is against a wall in the first floor with its back turned to everything that room is used for. It did not move on that Monday and no page of this stretch of days names anybody who moves it.
Under a building in a first district there is a room, and it was without a light in it at about eleven on that Monday and it is without one at this hour, and no request for a light has ever come from anybody in this city.
The register of correct acts that changed nothing stood at four when that Monday began and stands at four at the end of it, and nobody counts it here any more. A form with two lines on it that nobody has answered is not entered on it.

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are set down in the line that follows, one to a sentence, and no sentence after that line brings any two of them together. A strip of paper cut narrow with one word on it. A doorway held open on a card creased once. The front of one page copied out by hand nine times. The tin with its lid shut. A cage down one side of a counter. The shutter. The board on two nails with nineteen lines on it. The book in its green cover. The rail that takes two. The binder standing on a shelf at the back.**
The form that came back on a bus is not in those ten, and a sheet folded in four in a woman's bag is not an eleventh. The sheet that came back that way is no relation whatever to a board on two nails, nor to a printed sheet held to one by two drawing pins.
A dated rule stands over a matter that has nothing to do with this page, and the records behind it are open to anybody who comes and asks.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Monday. A woman said nine words to a man in a shop and he did not apologise because she had asked him not to, and nobody in this city has thanked her for saying them.
