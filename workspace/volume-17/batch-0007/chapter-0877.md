# Chapter 877 — A Tuesday, A Counter Until About Six, And A Room In The Evening Where About Nine People Put A Reason On A Form That Is True And Is About Four Hundred Pairs Of Hands On The Other Side Of A Border And Is Not A Thing This Room Is Allowed To Trade, And Where A Man Of Twenty-Two Was The Only Person Present Who Could Have Made The Trade Attractive And Refused It In About Four Seconds For A Reason About His Own Hands

**A counter until about six and then a room with about nine people in it was the shape of that Tuesday. The reason that room was given is true, and about nine people in it could see that it was true and could also see that it could not be given away, and one man of twenty-two refused to be used in it.**

---

On that Tuesday nine of them were written into that day's book with a date beside each, and the ninth of those dates went in at about ten to five, and about ten the shutter came down.

**The card standing on end in the two-place rail at that first door has been on end in it one thousand six hundred and eight days, two hundred and twenty-nine weeks and five days. The sixteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on the board on two nails has been ruled for one thousand three hundred and ninety-four days, a hundred and ninety-nine weeks and one day. The front of one page, written out by nine hands, has been in those nine hands for one thousand one hundred and seventy days, a hundred and sixty-seven weeks and one day, and no two of those nine have been set one against another.**

**And nothing was counted in any room in this city on that Tuesday and nothing was announced anywhere, and there is no Wednesday of every fourth week inside that day and there was not one the day before it either.**

**There is no whole number of weeks in any figure on this page. Every interval here falls on a day, and not one of them falls on a week, and no other day of this stretch of days carries a page on which that is true of all of it.**

---

**The form that came back on a bus has a box at the foot of it about the width of a thumb, and the room on that Tuesday evening had about nine people in it and one question on the table about the box.**

A man of about fifty-two who keeps a register had worked out what the body is able to put in that box. He said that it can put a date, and that a date would be a lie, because nobody in this city knows when the thing started.

He said in about nine seconds that the box can also be left blank, and that a blank box is a thing four hundred people will read as the same sentence as a date.

**Nobody in that room said what the third thing was and about four people in that room had looked for it for about nine days.**

---

**And the reason came out of a man of about thirty-three who writes this city's wording, at about ten past seven, and he had it written on a sheet in his own hand, and it is true.**

Four hundred people do this thing with their hands every day of their lives and nobody in this city has ever asked them whether it is safe, and a body that cannot say whether it is safe ought to ask them to stop until somebody can say so.

He said in about nine seconds that that is what goes in the box, and that the word that goes in it is not a date and is not a promise and is not a finding.

**Nobody in that room said he had the box too small, nobody in that room has said so since, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have raised it and did not.**

---

**And then about half an hour later the reason stopped being a reason and became a price, and nobody in that room had decided to do that.**

A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that the one thing this city has that four hundred people do not have is the only thing: somebody who can say what a set of people who have done a thing for years know about their own hands, and that this room has one of those in it.

He said that if the reason went in the box and this room got that, then four hundred people would be told something by this city instead of being told nothing by this city.

**Nobody in that room said it was a good bargain and nobody in that room said it was a bad one, and about four people in that room have said since that they thought it was one and about four have said since that they thought it was the other.**

---

**And the one person in that room who could have made that bargain hold was a man of twenty-two, and he said no in about four seconds, and the reason he gave was about his own hands.**

He said: you would need steady hands and mine are not.

He said it to the man of about thirty-eight and not to the room, and he had said it in about four seconds, and nobody in that room had finished asking him.

**Nobody in that room asked him how often his hands are not that and nobody there has put it to him since, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have asked and did not.**

He has said since that it is about four times in eleven years and that each of the four takes about two hours, and that he had told that room the half of it that concerns him and not the half that concerns the four hundred.

**Nobody in that room said what the other half was and nobody in that room has worked it out at any time since.**

---

**And that is the part of that Tuesday this page cannot tidy up.**

Afterwards, on a stair in a second district with a door closer on the step, he stood for about nine minutes trying to work out whether he had refused it for the reason he had said out loud or for one he had not, and he has said since that he could not do it and that he went back up.

He has said that the reason might have been about his hands and might have been that a man of twenty-two in a room of nine is not a thing that four hundred people should be told about, and that he does not know which of the two was in front.

**Nobody in that building asked him. Nobody in that room asked him. Nobody in this city has asked him, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have asked him and did not.**

**Nobody has answered the question for him, and this page does not answer it either.**

---

**And about nine seconds after he had said no, the room did the other thing, and it did it without anybody voting on it.**

The man of about thirty-three folded his sheet and said that the reason does not need anything in exchange and that he was going to put it in the box on the form exactly as it stood, with nothing traded for it and nothing gained by it.

Nobody in that room argued with him and nobody in that room agreed with him, and about four people in that room said afterwards that they had thought the reason was worthless without something behind it.

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that the reason is true whether or not anybody here ever hears whether it is true, and that a true thing handed over does not stop being true on the way.

**Nobody in that room found anywhere to put that, and nobody came back with a second question about it.**

The man of about twenty-two was still in that room when it was said and was asked nothing about it, and he said nothing, and he stayed until about nine.

---

**After that room emptied the balance of that Tuesday was about two hours in a fourth district, and there were four jobs in it.**

**A cable gland on a motor in the same yard had been fitted with a plate smaller than the hole it was meant to close, so that about four millimetres of the cable had been outside it.**

He took the gland off, cut the hole to the size the plate is meant for, and refitted it and proved it.

**Fifty-five pounds. He told the man that a gland plate that does not cover its hole is a gland that has been fitted by eye, and that about four glands in that yard were fitted that way, and that one of them has been reported as a motor that trips for about four years.**

---

**A ladder in the same building had its feet level on a floor that was not, so that it stood about a quarter of an inch out and everybody who used it had learned to step over the low side.**

He packed the foot that was high down and proved the ladder level.

**Eighteen pounds. He told the man that a ladder out of level is a ladder everybody walks round, that about four ladders in that building stand on floors that have settled, and that one of them has been a ladder that rocks for about four years.**

---

**A terminal block in the same place had two conductors under one clamp, so that the second of them had no clamp of its own and had been carrying about half its load through the first.**

He split them, gave the second its own clamp, and proved the panel.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that two conductors under one clamp is a conductor with no connection, that about four blocks in that panel have been doubled up that way, and that one of them has been an intermittent fault for about four years.**

---

**A float switch in the same yard had its cable laid in a tray where a door closes across it, so that about nine openings a day went against it.**

He moved the cable out of the way and left it where a hand could see it.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a cable in a door is a cable somebody will cut one morning, and that about four runs in that yard cross a door line, and that one of them has been reported as a fault that comes and goes for about four years.**

**A hundred and eight pounds is what those four came to on that Tuesday, exact.**

**By half past six the fourth of those four rooms was shut, and the woman of about thirty was still behind that door, and the eighth of eight sat on the shelf at the back of it at three hundred and ninety-three days.**

**Nobody in this city has ever put one question to her about that page, nobody put one to her on that Tuesday, and nobody ever will. What is on that page is not given here.**
