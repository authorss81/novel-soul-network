# Chapter 874 — A Monday After Two Days On Which Nothing In This City Was Written Down At All, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Where About Nine People Heard A Draft Read Out That Had Been Written Over The Weekend At A Counter In A Second District And Could Not Find Anything To Put It On

**A Thursday and a Friday went by in the middle of this stretch of days with nothing written down in this city on either of them, and then the Monday came round and about nine people were in a room with a piece of paper on a table, and what was on that piece of paper was good, and about nine people knew it was good, and nobody found anywhere to put it.**

---

Eight of them came through that counter on the Monday and were written down, and eight dates went in against them, the eighth of them at about ten to five, and the shutter came down at about ten that Monday.

**Those four converted units behind that service road have stood for one thousand five hundred and ninety-six days, which comes to two hundred and twenty-eight weeks to the day. One thousand three hundred and eighty-six days, a hundred and ninety-eight weeks to the day, is what line sixteen of those nineteen has been ruled for on that board. The front of one page copied out in nine hands has been in those nine hands for one thousand one hundred and sixty-two days, which is a hundred and sixty-six weeks to the day, and nobody has laid two of those nine side by side.**

**And nothing was said in any room in this city on that Monday that was said in a number, and no count was announced, and the Wednesday of every fourth week that carries one was two days off.**

---

**A man of about thirty-three who writes the wording on this city's forms had drafted something over the weekend and brought it in on that Monday in his own hand.**

He put it face up on the table and read it out once, in about nine seconds, and the room did not say anything for about half a minute afterwards.

The sentence is this: nothing in this room will be done to anybody before they have been told what this room is.

**Nobody in that room argued with it and about four people in that room said afterwards that they had read it twice and it was still the best sentence anybody had ever heard about this city in a room.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that it is the first sentence anybody in this city has written down that a person could hold somebody to, and that she has wanted one of those for about nine years.

**Nobody in that room asked her what she would do with it, and nobody in that room has put that question to her since.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-two, who keeps a register at a counter in a second district, found the first thing wrong with it inside about four minutes, and he found it by asking where it goes.**

He said that the four hundred people it is written for are not in the room, that nobody in the room can reach them, and that a sentence told to a room is a sentence told to the wrong people.

He said in about nine seconds that it is a true sentence and that a true sentence with nowhere to go is the same thing this city has about nine of already.

**Nobody in that room said he was wrong and nobody in that room said he was being careful, and about four people in that room have said since that they thought he had been careful and about four have said since that they thought he had been frightened.**

A man of about thirty-three said in about nine seconds that there is one thing in this city that carries a sentence to somebody who is not in the room, and that it is a form, and that he had brought the sentence here instead of onto one.

**Nobody in that room asked him to put it on one, nobody there has asked him to since, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have asked him to and did not.**

---

**And then the reason came out of the sheet itself, and it came out because somebody read the bottom of it out loud.**

The box at the bottom of every form this city sends is about the width of a thumb, and there is one word in it, and the word has been in that box since before anybody in this city thought to ask for a date.

**A thumb is what a person puts on a line when they are not writing their name, and that has been the use of that box for about nine years, and nobody in that room on that Monday had ever asked what else it is for.**

A man of about twenty-two, who had been asked nothing, said in about nine seconds that the sentence cannot go in that box, and that a box that size is not a place a sentence can be put, and that the sentence is longer than the box and was never going to be that length.

**Nobody in that room said he had been wrong and nobody in that room came back with a second question, and about four people in that room have said since that they would not have thought about the size of the box at all.**

---

**And two people in that room spent that evening on things that were not the sentence, and neither of the two of them did anything else.**

Dessa Kwan, who cooks and cares for people part of the week and has never let anybody turn a list of hers into a standard, had the hot water on and about four chairs brought in from the corridor, and wrote a list of what that room needed and did not read it out.

Asha Reed, who has organised a group that studies a thing in public before, had made about nine copies of that draft and had put them in about nine hands and had not asked anybody to do anything with one.

**Nobody in that room thanked either of them and nobody in that room has thanked either of them since, and neither of them asked to be thanked and neither of them said anything about what the sentence was for.**

---

**And then a man of about thirty-three said the other thing that evening, and he said it to the room and not to anybody in it, and it was about a woman who was not in that room.**

He said that this city has exactly one trade that opens on nothing but a Saturday and a Sunday, that the person who stands at that counter on a weekday is a woman of about forty-four, and that the person who stands behind it is a man of about forty-seven, and that that is the shape of the practice on this side of the water.

He said in about nine seconds that it is the clearest fact anybody had got hold of about how the thing reaches people, and that it came from a counter book on a weekday and not from over there.

**Nobody in that room asked him how he knew the shape of that counter, and nobody in that room asked him on whose authority he had read a day's work off it.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said afterwards, in about nine seconds, that she had wanted to ask and had not, and that nobody else in that room had asked either.

---

**Nobody in this city has told the woman of about forty-four what was said about her counter on that Monday, and this page is not going to.**

---

**He came up with the rest of them at about half past six and nobody put anything to him, and he said two things that evening, which is one more than he has said in about nine days.**

He said that the sentence has nobody in it who can be asked a question back, and that a thing you can be asked a question about is a thing somebody has to answer.

He said that the sentence as written has a room in it and nothing else, and that a room is not a thing anybody over a border has ever been in.

**Nobody in that room argued with him about either and nobody in that room came back with a third question, and about four people in that room have said since that they have thought about which of those two sentences was the one that mattered.**

He put his name to nothing in this stretch of days and carried nothing out of that room on that Monday, and the draft was copied out about nine times and not one of the nine went anywhere.

---

**The rest of that Monday, after that room emptied, was about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A handrail on a landing in the same building had been fitted with brackets that were drilled into the plaster and not into the brick, and about four landings in that building were like it.**

He took the brackets off, cut new ones longer, and fixed them into the brick where a hand goes.

**Forty-four pounds. He told the man that a bracket drilled into plaster is a bracket waiting for a fall, and that about four landings in that building have theirs in plaster, and that one of them has been reported as a rail that came away for about four years.**

---

**An isolator on a board in the same workshop had a label on it that named a different circuit from the one it was on.**

He took the label off, made a new one, and fixed it so that it says what the thing is.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a label that names the wrong circuit is a label somebody has had to think about before switching on, and that about four boards in that workshop have labels that are wrong, and that one of them has been reported as a wrong circuit for about four years.**

---

**A cable tray in the same yard had been supported on a piece of angle that had been cut about a quarter of an inch too long, so that the tray sat on the fixings rather than on the tray.**

He packed the angle out, cut it back, and put the tray down on the tray.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a tray sitting on its own fixings is a tray about four millimetres from being a tray on the floor, and that about four supports in that yard have been cut long, and that one of them has been reported as a tray that moved for about four years.**

---

**A hose on a machine in the same building had been clamped over a joint that somebody expected to move, so that the clamp had carried about half the movement on its own.**

He took the clamp off, fitted it past the joint, and left the joint free.

**Eighteen pounds. He told the man that a clamp over a joint is a joint that has been made a pipe, and that about four hoses in that building are clamped over their joints, and that one of them has been reported as a leak that comes and goes for about four years.**

**Add those four together and the answer is a hundred and seven pounds, exact, and that Monday is the whole of it.**

**At half past six a shut door on the fourth of those four rooms had a woman of about thirty behind it, and the eighth of eight was on the shelf at the back of that room at three hundred and eighty-five days.**

**That page has not been read by anybody in this city and nobody is going to read it. What is on it is not given here.**

---

*877.
Monday of week 296, at ten. That is the one hundred and seventy-ninth day of this stretch of days. Eight names and eight dates went onto that Monday, the last of the dates at about ten to five.
**No sitting was held anywhere on that Monday and no number was said in any room in this city. Two days had gone by before it with nothing written on them anywhere.
A man of about thirty-three who writes this city's wording read out a draft he had made over the weekend. About four people in that room said it was the best sentence anybody had written in a room in this city in about nine years.
A man of about fifty-two found in about four minutes that it had nowhere to go, and a man of about thirty-four read the bottom of a form out loud and a box that takes a thumb turned out to be the only place in this city a sentence can be put.
He was in that room, was asked nothing, and said two things, one of them about a sentence with nobody in it who can be asked a question back. That Monday was worth a hundred and seven pounds, exact.**

*Conditions of the close.* **That book in its green cover, on the table upstairs, is at sixty-seven lines, and the tin next to it is at seventy-three. No figure on this page was got from the other by measurement.
The ninth chair is against a wall in that first floor with its back to everything done in the room, and it did not move on that Monday, and nothing in this stretch of days names whoever moves it.
In a first district there is a room under a building, and nothing has been burning in it at about eleven on that Monday or at this hour, and nobody in this city has ever asked for a light in it.
The register of correct acts with no consequence stood at four when that Monday began and stands at four at the end of it, and no person in this city is keeping that figure. A sentence that is the best one anybody has heard and has nowhere to go is not on it.

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named in the sentence underneath, in an order of their own, and no line further down this page puts any two of them together. A card creased once and holding a doorway open. The front of one page, copied out nine times. The rail that takes two. The tin with its lid shut. The cage down the side of a counter. A strip of paper cut to width with one word on it. The board on two nails. The binder on a back shelf. The shutter. The book with the green cover.**

A form that came back on a bus is not in that list of ten and neither is a ninth copy of a sentence. A person carrying a thing back on a bus has as little to do with the board as with the printed sheet somebody pinned to one with two drawing pins.
A dated rule stands over a matter of its own entirely, and any person who wants the records behind it is welcome to come and read them.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Monday. The best sentence anybody in this city has written in a room in about nine years was read out once, was copied nine times, and went nowhere, and nobody has been thanked for any part of that.
