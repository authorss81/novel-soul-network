# Chapter 871 — A Wednesday Which Is Not One Of The Four, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where About Nine People Put A Sheet With Two Sentences On The Back Of It On A Table And Stood Looking At It And Then Took It Off The Table Again, And Nobody In This City Has Written Down What Is Done With A Piece Of Paper Once Somebody Has Written On It

**About nine people were in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket on that Wednesday evening, and about two hours of the day before that had been a counter with people standing at it. No figure was said in that room on that Wednesday, because a Wednesday of every fourth week is when a figure is said there and that Wednesday was not one of the four.**

---

Nine callers were written down on that Wednesday, and a date was written against every one of them, and the ninth of those dates was taken at about ten to five, and at about ten the shutter was down.

**Nineteen ruled lines are on the board on two nails, and one thousand three hundred and nine days is what the eighteenth of them carries, which is a hundred and eighty-seven weeks to the day. He has been on the north side of that room for one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven days, a hundred and seventy-one weeks to the day, and nobody in that room has said a word about how long that is. The sum owed on the open page in front of him stands at one thousand two hundred and eighty-one days, a hundred and eighty-three weeks to the day.**

**The chair at the head of that room is not the ninth chair, which is hard against the wall. Nothing has stood behind the chair at the head of that room for longer than about nine people in this city can say, and it stood empty on that Wednesday, and no figure is put against it on this page.**

---

**And nothing was counted in that room that evening and nothing was announced anywhere in this city on that Wednesday, and the last figure said in that room was said there three Wednesdays before this one.**

About nine people came up between about half past six and about seven. The window had been open since the spring and the room smelled of warm paper and dust, and there was a chair at the head of the table with nothing behind it at all.

**Nobody in that room said anything about that chair and nobody in that room has said anything about it since, and the ninth chair in the corner with its back to the whole of the room did not move on that Wednesday.**

A woman of about thirty-four came in at about ten past six and did not sit down, and took a sheet out of the inside pocket of her bag and put it flat on the table with the writing facing up.

She said in about nine seconds that there are two sentences on the back of that sheet and that they are in her hand, and that she wrote them in that room on a Friday about nine days ago after a man who is on no body in this city had said them out loud in a face.

**Nobody in that room asked to keep a copy, nobody in that room has asked since, and about four people in that room have said afterwards that they wanted a copy and did not ask.**

About four of them stood and read it. It took a person about forty seconds to read two sentences and it took the room about ninety seconds to do nothing at all afterwards.

**Nobody in that room agreed with it and nobody in that room disagreed with it, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted it for about nine days and had not dared to want it in writing.**

---

**And then the question that the evening turned on was asked by a woman of about thirty-eight, and it was not a hard one, and it took her about nine seconds to ask.**

She asked what the sheet was for.

The woman of about thirty-four said that it is a piece of paper with two sentences on it and that it is hers, and that she said on the Friday it was going to stay in her bag.

A man of about thirty-three who writes the wording on this city's forms said in about nine seconds that every form this city sends anywhere has a space on it for what a person is signing, and that there is no space on any of them for what a body is saying, and that a body does not sign anything anyway.

**Nobody in that room asked him where a sentence like that would go instead and nobody in that room has asked him since.**

The man of about fifty-two keeps a register at a counter in a second district, and he said nine words about it, and he said them to the man of about thirty-three and not to the room.

He said: this city has nowhere to put a written page.

**Nobody in that room argued with him and nobody in that room said he was wrong, and about four people in that room have said since that they thought he was being careful and about four have said since that they thought he was frightened.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-eight who works on the second floor of that same building said the other half of it, and his half was about a drawer.**

He said that a thing goes into a drawer in that building or it goes into a bag, and that about nine things a year go into drawers in that building and nobody has ever taken one out again.

He said that there is no form in this city for a sentence nobody asked for, and that there is no form in this city for a sentence that was asked for either, and that the difference between those two is not something anybody has ever printed.

**Nobody in that room found a place to put that and nobody came back with a second question about it.**

At about twenty past seven the woman of about thirty-four folded the sheet once and then twice more and put it back into the inside pocket of her bag, and the table where it had been was bare for the rest of that evening.

**Nobody in that room said that it had been better on the table and nobody said that it had been better in the bag, and about four people in that room have said since that they would rather it had stayed out and about four have said since that they would rather it had gone back.**

That is the whole of what happened to that piece of paper on that Wednesday. It came into a room, it lay on a table for about ten minutes, four people read it, and it went out again in the bag of the woman who had brought it.

**Nobody in this city has been told about that sheet and about four people in that room have said since that they would tell somebody and did not.**

---

**A man of twenty-two came up that stair with the rest of them at about half past six, was asked nothing at all, and said one thing at about half past seven, and the thing he said was about a step.**

He said that the second stair up to that floor has a tread that sits about a quarter of an inch lower than the eleven above it, and that about nine years of people going up to that room have put their weight on the outside edge of it and not once have said so.

**Nobody in that room knew what to do with that and nobody standing on it that night said anything about it, and about four people in that room have said since that the step had been under all of them for about nine years.**

He signed nothing in this stretch of days and he carried nothing out of that room, and there is nothing in this city that wants his name on a page.

---

**Once that counter had shut on the Wednesday, the rest of the day went into about two hours of work in a fourth district.**

**A conduit strap on a run in the same workshop had been screwed to the tray and not to the building, and about four of them along that run had been done the same way.**

He took the straps off, drilled them into the tray where the tray is fixed, and strapped the run where it went across a beam.

**Forty-four pounds. He told the man that a strap screwed to the tray is a strap holding up the tray, and that about four runs in that workshop were held up by their own fittings, and that one of them has been reported as a tray that has come down for about four years.**

---

**A stopcock on a riser in the same building had its gland packed with hemp and a nut on top of the hemp, so that turning it meant turning the packing.**

He cut the hemp out, packed it properly, and refitted the nut so that the handle turns the stopcock and not the stuffing.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a gland packed under a nut is a gland that has been packed too many times, and that about four risers in that building carry a nut sitting on top of their packing, and that one of them was reported as a stopcock that would not shut for about four years.**

---

**An earth on a socket in the same yard had been made off a length of scaffold tube that had been in the ground for about four years and had moved since.**

He took the earth off the tube, ran it back to the box it belongs on, and proved the socket with an instrument.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that an earth on a scaffold tube is an earth that goes away when somebody knocks the scaffold down, and that about four sockets in that yard are made off that way, and that one of them has been reported as a socket that shocks in wet weather for about four years.**

---

**A door on a store in the same place had its hinges packed with a wedge and a screw, and the door had been opening on its own frame for about four years.**

He took the packing out, packed the hinge leaves to the jamb with a proper packer, and left the door closing on the frame it was made for.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a hinge packed with a wedge is a hinge that has been told which way to go, and that about four doors in that building were hung that way, and that one of them has been reported as a door that will not stay shut for about four years.**

**The four of those jobs priced one after another came to a hundred and twenty pounds on that Wednesday, exact.**

**At half past six the fourth of those four rooms had its door shut and a woman of about thirty behind it, and on the shelf at the back of that room the eighth of eight stood at three hundred and eighty days.**

**Nobody in this city has put one question to her about that page, and nobody ever is going to. What is on that page is not given here.**

*874.
Wednesday of week 295, at ten. That is the one hundred and seventy-sixth day of this stretch of days. Nine names and nine dates went onto that Wednesday, the last of the dates at about ten to five.
**No sitting was held in that first floor on that Wednesday and no figure was said in it. *Conditions of the close.* **The book lying on the table in the room upstairs is on sixty-seven lines. The tin beside it is on seventy-three. This page has not arrived at either of those figures by setting one against the other.
The ninth chair in that first floor is hard against a wall with its back turned to everything that happens in that room. It has not shifted on that Wednesday, and nothing in this stretch of days names the person who shifts it.
Underneath a building in a first district there is a room, and at about eleven on that Wednesday there was nothing lit in it, and there is nothing lit in it at this hour, and nobody in this city has ever asked for a light there.
The count of things done correctly that changed nothing was four when that room filled and is four now, and nobody in this city keeps a figure of it. A sheet of paper lying on a table for ten minutes is not entered on that count.

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Named in the sentence underneath, ten objects, and no line further down this page brings two of them together. A narrow slip of paper with a single word on it, cut down one side of a sheet. A book with a green cover. A doorway kept open by a card creased once across it. The board on two nails. Nine hands' copies of the front of one page. The shutter. The tin with its lid down. The cage down one side of a counter. The rail that holds two. The binder standing on the shelf at the back.**

The form that came back on a bus is none of those ten. It is as little the board on two nails as it is the printed sheet that went up on a board by two drawing pins, and a sheet with two sentences on the back of it in a woman's bag is not an eleventh thing.
A dated rule stands over a separate matter entirely, and whoever wants the records behind it is welcome to come and look at them.
Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Wednesday. A woman put two sentences on a table and took them off it again, and nobody in that room asked her to leave them there and nobody has since.
