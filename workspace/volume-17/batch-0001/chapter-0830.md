# Chapter 830 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And About Nine Hours Of A Counter With Thirteen Callers On It And One Of Them Was A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Said Out Loud That He Had Been Over There And Had Done It Himself Twice And That It Worked And That He Does Not Know If It Is Safe And That He Is The Only Person In This City Who Does Not Know

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and nine of the hours of it were a counter with thirteen callers on it, and one of the thirteen was a man of about thirty-four who said out loud that he had been over there and had done it himself, twice, and that it worked, and that he does not know if it is safe.**

---

Nine names went onto that Friday's sheet with nine dates, the last of them taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten, the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**Those four rooms stand at one thousand five hundred and thirty days, two hundred and eighteen weeks and four days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is four days further on at one thousand five hundred and thirty-four, two hundred and nineteen weeks and one day, and the rail takes two.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand three hundred and two days, one hundred and eighty-six weeks to the day. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and seventy-eight, one hundred and fifty-four weeks to the day. The separation is nine hundred and ten days, one hundred and thirty weeks to the day.**

---

**And thirteen people came to that counter on that Friday and the thirteenth name was taken at about twenty to five, and one of the thirteen said the thing that the whole of this movement has been standing in front of since about eight days ago.**

He is about thirty-four. He works on the second floor of a building that lets out four rooms in a second district and he has been in that stair every weekday since before the spring. He is the man who asked the woman of about thirty-four, on the Tuesday, whether the man at the bus stop had said his name.

He said, in about nine seconds, standing at that counter, that he has been over there. He said that he has done it himself, twice, on a Sunday and on a Wednesday, about eleven years ago, and that it worked both times, and that he was not asked anything by anybody at the time and did not know he was supposed to be.

He said that he has thought about it every week since about eight days ago, which is about a week, and that he has not been able to work out whether it is safe, and that he has looked for somebody in this city to ask four times now and has not found them.

**He said, in about four seconds, that he is the only person in this city who has done it and cannot say whether it is safe, and that about four hundred people do it every day and have never once thought about the question, and that this is the whole of the thing and he has been carrying it since about a week.**

---

**Marek did not say a sentence. About four seconds passed and he did not say one, and the man of about thirty-four said that he had come in to say that out loud and not to be answered, and that he knew he would not be answered and came anyway.**

A woman of about thirty-eight who was in that shop said in about nine seconds that he had come in to be the man in the room again, and he said no, and she said that she knew that and that she said it out loud because about four people had said it out loud about other people this week and nobody had said it about him.

**He said she was right and that he had not worked that out, and that he was the man in the room on the Tuesday in a fourth district and had not been the man in the room since, and that about four people had said he was still the man in the room and he had not believed any of them until she said it.**

Nobody in that shop was thanked for anything on that Friday and nobody in that shop was asked to be a member of anything.

---

**A man of about fifty-two who works in a third district was the eleventh caller and asked one question, which was whether there was a room somewhere in this city that he could walk into and be told the answer.**

Marek said no in about four seconds, and gave a reason that was about the room and not about the man.

The man of about fifty-two said that he had been told no in four seconds four times in about nine days and had stopped minding, and that what he wanted was not an answer and was a room, and that a room with no answer in it would have been about nine minutes of his life better than a counter.

He left. He did not pay and was not asked to and has not been back, and nobody has written down that he came in.

**Nobody in that shop said his name and nobody in that shop asked after him and about two people in that shop have said since that they would have liked to have written his name in the pad by the till and did not.**

---

**And the rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where a length of handrail had been fitted with the brackets set into the plaster rather than into the structure.**

He took the rail off, filled the eight holes, found the wall plate behind with a detector, and fixed the brackets through the plaster into that.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a bracket in plaster is a bracket in about nine millimetres of board and that four rails in that building are fixed that way and that one of them comes off the wall about four inches in a year with a coat on it.**

---

**And a set of stairs in the same building had about eleven treads with nosings that had been cut back to fit a carpet.**

He took nine of the eleven up, put a new nosing on each with the same section, and relaid them.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a tread nosing cut back for a carpet is a tread that has nothing left to take a heel and that four flights in that building have been cut and that one of them has a tread worn about nine millimetres through in the middle.**

---

**And a door in the same building had been hung on three hinges.**

He took the door off, took the top hinge out, packed the hinge stile to square, fitted a fourth hinge, and refitted the door.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a door on three hinges sags about nine millimetres in about four years and that four doors in that building are on three and that one of them has a latch that has to be lifted to be shut.**

---

**And a landing handrail on the same stairs had been painted where a hand went and not anywhere else.**

He rubbed the rail back where the paint had built on it, took the paint off the underside with a strip, and left the rest.

**Eleven pounds. He told the man that a rail painted only where a hand goes is a rail somebody has decided is not a surface, and four rails on that stair have been painted in patches and that one of them has a patch about nine inches long in the middle of nowhere.**

---

**In the fourth of those four rooms a woman of about thirty has been at her chair from half past six with the door shut behind her, and for three hundred and nineteen days the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her. It is not read. She has not been asked about it and is not going to be.**
