# Chapter 826 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And About Four Hours Of A Room Off A Service Road Where About Nine People Sat Down And About Four Of Them Had Come To Ask The Same Question And Nobody In That Room Was Allowed To Answer It Because Nobody In That Room Was A Member Of Anything

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in the fourth of four rooms off a service road, where about nine people sat down and about four of them had come to ask the same question and nobody in that room was allowed to answer it, because nobody in that room was a member of anything.**

---

Nine names and nine dates were on that Monday's sheet when it was filled in, the last of the nine at about twenty to five. At about ten the shutter came down, and it is that hour on every day of this stretch of days.

**Those four rooms stand at one thousand five hundred and twenty-six days, two hundred and eighteen weeks to the day, and the card on end in the rail by that door is four days further on at one thousand five hundred and thirty, two hundred and eighteen weeks and four days, and the rail takes two.**

**The sixteenth line on the board of two nails is one thousand three hundred and sixteen days, one hundred and eighty-eight weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand and ninety-two, one hundred and fifty-six weeks to the day, one corner torn, eight unfinished, and not two compared on this day or on any day of this stretch.**

---

**And about nine people sat down in that fourth room from half past six and about four of them had come to ask the same question and about four of the other five had come because somebody they know had come.**

The question was whether anybody in this city could find out whether the thing those four hundred people do over there is safe, and about four of them asked it out loud in about nine minutes, and nobody in that room answered it.

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that she had asked it in a first floor on a Tuesday the week before and had not been answered, and that she had come back because a woman of about twenty-nine had told her that asking twice was allowed.

A man of about fifty-two said in four seconds that asking twice is allowed and asking four hundred times is not, and that the room is the same size either way.

Nobody in that room asked him what the fourth time would be and nobody in that room said the word *four hundred* out loud in that room on that Monday.

---

**And a woman of about thirty at the fourth desk in that room had her door shut and was not in that room, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and fifteen days and has not been read.**

**Nobody has asked her one question about it, nobody has apologised to her for anything, and nobody is going to, and this volume does not open the binder and does not name her and does not say what is on the page behind it.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who works in a fourth district asked, in about nine seconds, the only question anybody in that room asked that was about a person rather than about a practice.**

He asked whether anybody in this city had asked the four hundred themselves.

A man of about fifty-two said that no.

The man of about thirty-four said that he had thought about that on a bus for two days and that it had not occurred to him until he got in that room, and that it was the first thing anybody had said to him in about nine years that he had not thought of before he heard it.

**Nobody in that room asked him what to do about it and nobody in that room said they would, and about two people in that room have said since that they wanted to and that there was nothing they could do with a Saturday.**

---

**And about an hour of that Monday evening was in that room and the other half hour was in the corridor outside it, where four people stood and did not go in either, and one of the four was a woman of about twenty-four with a key to a room of her own and not to his.**

She said in about nine seconds that a body that has a procedure for telling its own people the truth about what they are leaving has not got a procedure for telling anybody else anything, and that the two are not the same work and that the second one is harder and that about four people had spent nine years finding out the first one worked.

She said that she is not going to say what to do, because she is not a member of anything either, and because the room she works in can look into anybody who sits on it including him and that this is not the place to say it.

**Nobody in that corridor said that back to her and about four people in that corridor have said since that they wanted to and that she was right not to say it.**

He had four sentences ready and did not say them and has said since that about two of the four were about a decision he made in a different city a long time ago, and that she did not ask because she was not going to, and that a person who does not ask does not get the answer and she had worked that out a long time before that corridor.

---

**And the rest of that Monday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where a length of a wall had been taken out to get a machine through and had been filled with block and plaster that had not cured before the shop opened.**

He took the loose plaster off, cut the block back to sound on both sides, refitted it with the opening narrowed by about two inches, packed the joint with a foam bead, and skimmed it twice.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a machine in and out of a building in a morning means about nine weeks of drying whatever is put back, and that four openings in that building have block and plaster in them and that one of them has a corner that is still soft enough to take a thumbnail.**

---

**And a length of skirting in the same building had been nailed to a joint that moves, and the joint moves about four millimetres across a year.**

He took the run off, filled the old fixings, fitted a scribed batten across six joints and fixed through those, and refitted the skirting onto the batten.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a skirting on a moving joint is a skirting that opens a gap at one end every year, and that four runs in that building are fixed to joints and that one of them has a gap at the corner about a leaf thick.**

---

**And a length of capping on a window in the same building had been painted onto the frames instead of onto the masonry, and had come away in one piece with about four feet of frame moulding stuck to it.**

He scraped the paint back to bare timber, cut the capping off, bedded a new one on the masonry head and refitted the frames onto it.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a capping on a frame is a capping that takes the frame with it, and that four windows in that building are capped that way and that one of them has a head that is now about four inches under the opening it came out of.**

---

**And a floor hatch in a corridor had been fixed with four screws into chipboard.**

He took the hatch up, put a frame round the opening into the joists, and refitted the hatch onto the frame.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a hatch screwed into chipboard is a hatch that carries nobody's weight and that four hatches in that building are fixed that way and that one of them has a corner about an inch down.**

---

**A woman of about thirty sits at her chair from half past six in the fourth of those four rooms, the door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and fifteen days. Nobody has asked her one question about that page and nobody is going to.**
