# Chapter 827 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And About Two Hours Of A Depot On A Road In A Fourth District Where A Man Of About Twenty-Two Was Asked To Say Out Loud Whether He Thought It Was Safe And Said No In About Four Seconds With A Reason That Was About His Own Hands And Not About Four Hundred People

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a depot on a road in a fourth district, where about nine people had come to hear a man of twenty-two say out loud whether he thought something was safe, and he said no in about four seconds, with a reason that was about his own hands.**

---

Nine names went on that Tuesday's sheet and nine dates went with them, the last taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten, which is the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**Those four rooms stand at one thousand five hundred and twenty-seven days, two hundred and eighteen weeks and one day, and the card on end in the rail by that door is four days further on at one thousand five hundred and thirty-one, two hundred and eighteen weeks and five days, and the rail takes two.**

**Line thirteen is one thousand three hundred and ninety-eight days, one hundred and ninety-nine weeks and five days. Line fourteen is one thousand three hundred and sixty-three, one hundred and ninety-four weeks and five days. What is still owed is one thousand two hundred and seventeen, one hundred and seventy-three weeks and six days.**

---

**And about nine people were in that depot from about half past six and about four of them had asked him to say something out loud, and he said no in about four seconds.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that four people in this city have now said the same thing to four different rooms, and that if a man who is not a member of anything says it out loud then a thing that has been said four times becomes a thing anybody can point at.

He said no in about four seconds, and his reason was that he has a condition that means his hands are not right for about two hours four times in eleven years, and that a person who cannot hold a release steady for about two hours in about eleven years is not the person a room should be told is the answer, whatever else he is.

**Nobody in that depot said that was not an answer to the question that was asked. About four people in that depot said afterwards that it was not an answer to the question, and about four of them said afterwards that it was the only answer anybody had given in about nine weeks that had a person in it instead of a policy.**

He has said since that he wanted to have said the other thing and that the other thing was in his mouth for about four seconds, and that it was a sentence about four hundred people and he does not know four hundred people.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who owns four vehicles and has been in four volumes of this city asked him, in about nine seconds, whether he would say yes if nobody wrote it down.**

He said no in about four seconds and gave the reason about his hands again, and about two people in that depot said that they had heard the same reason twice in about nine seconds and that it had not sounded like a decision made for the room.

He has said since that it was a decision made for the room, and that the difference between a decision made for a room and a decision made for himself is about nine years and that he could not tell you which one it was on that Tuesday and has not worked it out since.

**Nobody in that depot asked him which one it was and about four people in that depot have said since that they wanted to and that they were afraid of the answer and about four of them have said that they were not.**

---

**And the rest of that Tuesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a fourth district, where a set of tram boards had been stacked on a pallet against a wall with a gap of about two inches behind them, and about nine feet of water had come down that wall in about two winters.**

He pulled the stack out, took the bottom four boards out, cut the wall's render back to sound above the water line, filled the line with a mortar mix, and restacked the boards on a batten.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that boards stacked on a floor two inches off a wall are boards against a wet wall, and that four stacks of boards in that depot are stacked that way and that one of them has nine feet of green at the bottom.**

---

**And a length of conduit in the same depot had been saddled over a cable tray that somebody had later cut down about two inches.**

He took the conduit down, took the saddles off, put a new piece of tray under the run to bring it back up, and refitted the saddles.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a saddle on a tray that has been cut down is a saddle holding about two inches less than it holds on, and that four runs in that depot are on cut-down trays and that one of them has two cables in it that are not in any tray at all.**

---

**And a set of points at the end of that run had been greased in the spring with a compound that has to be worked out of with a solvent before anything else goes on.**

He took the grease off both blades with a solvent and a rag, cleaned the throat out, and re-greased it with the thixotropic the depot keeps.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a points mechanism greased in the spring with the wrong compound is a points mechanism that is about nine weeks out of date, and that four sets in that depot are due and that one of them has a blade that closes about four seconds late.**

---

**And a lamp standard at the end of a run had a base plate with four bolts and about two of them loose.**

He dug out the base, cut new holding-down bolts, and grouted the base back into fresh concrete.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a lamp standard with two of four bolts loose is a lamp standard on two bolts, and that four standards on that run have loose baseplates and that one of them has a column that has moved about two inches at the foot.**

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and sixteen days and has not been read, and nobody has asked her one question about it and nobody is going to.**
