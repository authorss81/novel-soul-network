# Chapter 823 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where The Room Was Open And About Nine People Were In It And No Number Was Said Because A Number Is Said At A Sitting And This Wednesday Was Not One

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where the room was open from half past six and about nine people were in it and about four of them stayed the hour. No number was said that evening, and nobody in that room used a word for that, and the tin on the same table did not move.**

---

Nine names and nine dates went onto that Wednesday's sheet, the last of the dates entered at about twenty to five. At about ten the shutter came down, and at about ten is the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**Line eighteen is one thousand two hundred and thirty-nine days, one hundred and seventy-seven weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand one hundred and twenty-seven, one hundred and sixty-one weeks to the day. What is still owed is one thousand two hundred and eleven, one hundred and seventy-three weeks to the day.**

**Behind the chair of the woman of about sixty a place has stood empty since a week in the spring, and nobody in that room asked her about it on that Wednesday or on any day of this stretch, and about four people in that room have said since that they had wanted to ask her about it for about nine weeks and had not turned the wanting into a question.**

The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to everything else in the room. It did not move on that Wednesday, and on no page of this stretch of days is the person who moves it named.

Nobody in that room put the question, and in about fifty-seven weeks it has not been put.

---

**And no number was said, and this is the second time in this manuscript that a reader can be shown a Wednesday in that room with nothing in it, and the first time was in the volume before this one and it said a different thing.**

A number is said at a sitting. A sitting is a Wednesday of every fourth week. This was not one of those Wednesdays, and the room was open, and about nine people came up that stair, and nobody said a number, and nobody in that room said why.

A man of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that he has been in that room on a Wednesday that was not a sitting twice before and that both times he counted the people and both times he got a different number, and that he has stopped doing it.

Nobody asked him what the two numbers were and he did not say.

---

**And the woman of about sixty said the thing that is the whole of what happened in that room that evening, and she said it in about nine seconds, and she said it before anybody asked her anything.**

She said that she has been in that room nineteen years and that nobody in this city has ever told her that anything outside this city is either good or bad, and that she has not asked, and that she has not wanted to ask, and that she wants it written down that she did not want to ask, because in about nine years somebody will want to know what she knew and she will not have it.

**About four people in that room said afterwards that they had not thought about the outside of this city in about nine years and that she was right and that not thinking about it had been the arrangement everybody made.**

Nobody in that room asked her why she had not wanted to ask. Nobody in that room has asked her.

**A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that this was the most useful thing anybody had said in that room in about nine years and that he was not going to say why, and that about four people in that room looked at him and about four did not.**

He has said since that he was not going to say why because the reason is that nobody wanted it, and that if he had said that out loud then about nine people in that room would have wanted it, and that the one thing this city has never done is admit in a room that it wants something about the outside of itself.

**Nobody in that room agreed with him and nobody in that room asked him to say it anyway and about four people in that room have said since that they would have.**

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four asked the one question nobody else in that room was going to ask, in about nine seconds, which was whether anybody in that room thought that the four hundred people over there had ever been asked anything at all, by anybody, ever.**

A man of about fifty-two said no.

A man of about thirty-four said that about nine people in this city were part of something once and were not in it now and had not been asked anything, and that this is the second time in about nine years that anybody in a room has asked a question and got an answer about people who are not in the room.

**Nobody in that room asked him to name the nine and nobody in that room named them and about four people in that room have said since that they have wanted to for about nine years.**

---

**And a man of about fifty-two was given the job that evening of finding out how long *as it has been* is, and he did it in about nine seconds and put the pen down without writing anything.**

He said that he does not know who he would ask, and that the person who carried the form back on the bus was not asked by anybody, and that if he asks the woman of about thirty-four then she will say the word again and he will write the word down again and that is not an answer to anything.

He said that a number one man keeps is a different object from a number people have taken, and that nobody has taken this one, and that he is not going to be the first.

**Nobody thanked him for refusing the job and nobody in that room asked him to take it again and about two people in that room have said since that they had not realised until he put the pen down that somebody would eventually have to do it.**

**And the man of about forty-four said one more thing at the end of those nine minutes, in about four seconds, which was that he was asked to read them out and not asked to believe them, and that this is a thing this city has never asked anybody to do before and that he would like it noticed that it was asked.**

A woman of about twenty-nine said in four seconds that she had noticed.

Nobody in that room asked her to say that again and nobody in that room said what she had noticed.

---

**And about half an hour of that Wednesday was in the corridor outside that room, and four people stood in it and did not go in either, and one of the four was a man of twenty-two.**

He has said since that he came up that stair at about half past six for no reason he could give afterwards, and that he stood in that corridor for about thirty minutes and could hear nine people breathing and a chair moving and nothing else.

He said that he had been in that corridor four times since the last volume closed and that on all four of them he had had something in his mouth and had not said it, and that on this Wednesday he had had nothing and had still not said it.

**Nobody in that corridor said a number and nobody in that corridor asked anybody else to say one.**

---

**And the rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where a shop's awning had been put up on four bracket bolts through a fascia that had been replaced and not the bolts.**

He took the whole awning down, filled the four old holes, cut four lengths of the size of the rafter tail and let them into the plumb of each rafter, and refitted the awning through those into sound timber.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a bolt in a replaced fascia is a bolt in about nine years of weather, and that four awnings in that district are on replaced fascias and that one of them has a bracket that has pulled about an inch clear of the wall.**

---

**And a handrail on the stairs of the same building had been fixed through the same kind of replaced trim with four plugs.**

He took the rail off, filled the four plugs, found the rail ends behind the trim with a detector, and fixed the rail through the trim into those.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a rail fixed into a plug is a rail fixed into a hole somebody filled, and that four rails in that building are on plugs and that one of them moves about a quarter of an inch at the bottom bracket when you put a hand on it.**

---

**And a length of flooring in a back room had been laid on an underlay that was about nine years old and had gone to powder in one place.**

He lifted eleven boards in that place, took out the powder, put a new hard strip under the line of the joists, and relaid the same boards on it, taking up about a quarter of an inch of the thickness so that the same boards went back at the same height.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that an underlay goes to powder under a leak and the leak is above the boards and not under them, and that four floors in that building are on that underlay and that one of them has eleven boards that click when you walk across.**

---

**And a set of hooks on a door had been screwed into the face of the door and not into the frame stile.**

He took all four hooks out, filled the holes, fitted two new ones through the frame stile on the hinge side, and left the other two where they were because they were holding nothing.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a coat hook on the face of a door works until somebody hangs a wet coat on it, and that four doors in that building have hooks in the face and that one of them has a door that is out of square because of it.**

---

**Behind the shut door of the fourth of those four rooms a woman of about thirty has been at her chair since half past six, and the eighth of eight stands on the back shelf behind her, three hundred and ten days old and unread. She has not been asked one question about it and is not going to be.**
