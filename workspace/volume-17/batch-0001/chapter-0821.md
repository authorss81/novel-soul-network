# Chapter 821 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Half An Hour Of A Stair In A Second District With A Door Closer Screwed To The Top Of A Step, A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Who Put A Form On A Table About Nine Feet Away And Said Nothing To The Man On The Floor, And Nine Pounds For A Part

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and about half an hour of it in a stair in a second district with a door closer in his hand. Nine jobs were dated for the day on a board by the till. A form went onto a table in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket at about eleven while he was on the step.**

---

Nine names went on that Monday's sheet and nine dates went with them, the last taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten. The cage in the round-the-side had been going about four times in the fortnight and the van was not needed.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand five hundred and nineteen days, two hundred and seventeen weeks to the day, and the card on end in the rail by that door is four days further on at one thousand five hundred and twenty-three, two hundred and seventeen weeks and four days, and the rail takes two.**

**The nineteenth line on the board of two nails is one thousand two hundred and fifteen days, one hundred and seventy-three weeks and four days. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand one hundred and twenty-five, one hundred and sixty weeks and five days. What is still owed is one thousand two hundred and nine, one hundred and seventy-two weeks and five days.**

The separation is at eight hundred and ninety-nine days, one hundred and twenty-eight weeks and three days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old.

---

**The stair is the top of a stair and it belongs to a building that lets out four rooms on a first floor, and the door that slams is the one at the bottom of it, and it has been slamming since before the spring because the closer on top of it is set to shut it as fast as the door will bear.**

He had it off in his bag before he went and he had the new one in a bag as well, and the adjustment screw on the old one was down as far as it will go and somebody had put a piece of tape round it to keep it there.

**About nine people came up that stair between ten and half past eleven and about four of them were going to the rooms and about four of the other five were not going anywhere in particular, which is what a stair in that building is at that hour.**

He had the old unit off and the back plate out and the dust in his hands and his knees on the step when the door at the bottom of it went and did not slam, because the closer was on the bench beside him in pieces.

---

**And the form was already on the table when he got back, and it had been on the table for about four minutes, and about four people had stood at the other side of that table and read it and gone back down the stair without saying anything to anybody.**

It is about the size of a folded sheet of writing paper. There is a heading at the top of it with no body under the heading, and a ruled line about two thirds of the way down with two words over it, and a box at the bottom, and **in the box there is one word and no date, and the word is *as it has been*.**

Nobody in this city has written that word in a box on a form. **A woman of about thirty-four put it there on a Monday morning at about eleven, having come back on the bus the day before, and she is not the author of it and she did not choose the word.**

He read it three times standing at the counter when he got in.

He wanted to ask her what the form was for. **He had a sentence ready in about two seconds and he did not say it, and the reason he did not say it, which he worked out on the Thursday and not at the time, was that she had not asked him what he was doing, and a person who is not asked does not ask.** That is the second time in about four years and he has noticed that it is a habit and not a virtue.

---

**The building had paid him before she came up, in an envelope, at the door, by a man of about fifty-eight who said nothing beyond the amount and did not ask him to sign anything.**

He was on the step again by about half past eleven with the new unit bolted to the top of the door and the tape off the old screw and the old unit in the bag with the tape still round it, because he does not throw away a piece of tape that somebody had put there on purpose.

**Nobody in that building said one word to him about the form, and nobody in that building read it out loud to him, and about four people read it and about five did not.**

He was back at the counter at about half past one and the shop had gone on without him and had not stopped.

---

**About nine jobs went out on that Monday and about nine came in and nothing escalated and nothing was handed back, and the till came up about four pounds out and he counted the tray twice.**

A woman of about thirty-eight brought in a bracket for a gate and wanted it the same afternoon because the gate was open on a lane and the lane was a bus route.

"**That's a bracket,**" he said. "**It'll be Thursday.**"

She said Thursday and it was Thursday and she came back on Thursday and it went on.

**He charged about nine pounds for it and the bracket cost him about two and he did not tell her that and did not charge her for the ten minutes it took him to explain that a gate bracket that has been open on a bus route for a fortnight is a bracket somebody has already been told to replace and has not.**

---

---

**About four of the people who read that form are not the people it is about, and that is the first thing about it that is worth knowing and nobody has printed it anywhere.**

The people who read it are the people who were in that first floor at eleven on a Monday. The form is about four hundred people who are not in this city, and not one of the four who read it is one of the four hundred.

He knows that because he asked one of them, a man of about thirty-eight who works on the second floor and has been in that stair every weekday since before the spring, and the man said he has never been over there in his life and never been asked to be.

**That man bought a washer on the Thursday and said nothing about the form then either.**

---

**The rest of that Monday was nine jobs and a counter and a till that does not add up, and he did all nine and charged for all nine.**

A man of about sixty came in with a lamp he had brought back because he had brought it back before.

"**That is a lamp,**" Marek said. "**You have had that lamp before.**"

"**Twice,**" the man said. "**It is not the lamp, it is the flex.**"

"**Then it is the flex,**" Marek said, and it was the flex, and it cost about four pounds, and the man said it had cost him eleven pounds the first time at a shop on a wide road, and Marek did not ask which shop and did not say anything about it and has thought about the shop on a wide road perhaps four times since.

A woman of about forty-one brought a bag of door handles she had taken off a door in one piece instead of eleven.

A boy of about fifteen brought a bicycle chain that fitted the bicycle and had been fitted by somebody who did not know what it was for.

**About four of the nine jobs on that Monday were jobs somebody had brought back, and that is not a bad day and it is not a good day and it is about what a counter is.**

---

**At about four o'clock a woman of about twenty-six came in and asked for a man and he said she had him and she said no, she had wanted to ask him something, and he said then ask him, and she stood at the counter for about nine seconds and then said it was a stupid question and went out.**

He thought about her for the rest of that week. **She had come about the form, and she had wanted to ask a man who fixes things whether the thing in the box at the bottom of that piece of paper was a real answer or a way of refusing to write one, and she had decided in nine seconds that it was a stupid question.**

He did not know whether it was one and he has not found out and has not looked, and about nine people in this city have seen her come out of that shop and about four of them thought nothing of it at all.

---

---

**The last job of that Monday was a gate at about a quarter to five and it is the one he thinks about.**

A man of about forty-three wanted it shut by dark and it cannot be shut by dark because the post is in and the hole has to be filled, and Marek said so in about nine seconds and then stayed for about forty minutes and did it badly and quickly.

They filled the bottom eleven inches with something that came out of a bucket and they put the gate on and it shut and it is shut now and about two of the four hundred yards in that district are shut on a Monday.

**He charged about thirty-one pounds for the Monday in total, and the gate was most of it, and the gate is the only thing on that day anybody will still be able to see in about four years.**

---

**At about ten he went up a stair near Civic Spine, about nine doors along from the tram stop, and about four minutes after him a woman of about twenty-four came up the same stair and went to a door about nine metres from his and shut it.**

They had rice and a stew that was three days old because that is what was in the pot, and bread, and there was no second thing.

She asked him what he had done on the day and he told her about the stair and the closer and the tape and the man of about fifty-eight with the envelope, and he did not tell her about the form, **because he did not want to say the word *practice* at a table on a Monday night and have it be the thing they talked about.**

She said the gate was a stupid gate to have shut and he said it was, and she said the post was in and he said it was.

**Nobody said anything else about the form that night and neither of them has mentioned it since and it has been on that table for six days.**

---

---

**There is a second form in that building and it went up in the autumn before the spring and nobody has taken it down.**

It is about a room that is to be shut for one day a week and it is the one that is still on the board at the building that receives the returns with a heading and no body.

The woman of about thirty-four put hers down about nine feet from a man on the floor and nobody in this city has ever asked her why she chose that table and not another one.

---

**He wanted, all of that afternoon, for one person to come in and ask him what was in the box.**

Nobody came in and asked him what was in the box. Nobody came in and asked him about anything that was not on the ticket.

---

**What it cost was about two pounds and about four minutes of arguing with himself at about half past two, and the two pounds are the whole of it and he did not tell her that and told nobody, and the four minutes are not a figure anybody could use.**

**There are four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six on that Monday a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, in her chair, with the shut door of that room behind her.**

The eighth of eight is her page and it has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for three hundred and eight days. It did not come out on that Monday and nobody asked her one question about it and nobody is going to.

---

**He wanted the woman who put the form down to have asked him for something, and she did not, and the wanting lasted about two days and then stopped being a wanting and became a thing he kept looking for.**

He looked for it on the Tuesday and on the Wednesday and on the Friday and it did not come.
