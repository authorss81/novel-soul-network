# Chapter 822 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where About Nine People Went Up A Stair To Look At One Word In A Box And About Four Of Them Read It Out Loud And A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Asked The Only Question Anybody Asked That Day And Nobody In That Room Answered Her

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and about an hour of it in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people went up a stair to look at one word in a box and about four of them read it out loud to each other and about two of those four asked a question and got no answer.**

---

That Tuesday's sheet took nine names and carried nine dates against them, the last of the nine entered at about twenty to five. The shutter went down at about ten, and that is the hour it goes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand five hundred and twenty days, two hundred and seventeen weeks and one day, and the card on end in the rail by that door is four days further on at one thousand five hundred and twenty-four, two hundred and seventeen weeks and five days, and the rail takes two.**

**The nineteenth line on the board of two nails is one thousand two hundred and sixteen days, one hundred and seventy-three weeks and five days. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand one hundred and twenty-six, one hundred and sixty weeks and six days. What is still owed is one thousand two hundred and ten, one hundred and seventy-two weeks and six days.**

The separation is at nine hundred days, one hundred and twenty-eight weeks and four days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old.

---

**The form was still on that table on the Tuesday, and nobody had moved it, and nobody had put anything on it, and about nine people came up that stair between half past five and a quarter to seven and about four of them stood at that table for longer than they stood at anything else in that room.**

**It is about the size of a folded sheet of writing paper. A heading sits at the top of it with nothing under that heading, and rather more than halfway down there is a ruled line with two words over it, and at the foot of the sheet there is a box, and one word stands in that box where a date would go on anything else in this city.**

The four who read it out loud read the same four things and they read them in four different orders. About four minutes of that room was spent on the heading and about nine minutes in total was spent on the whole of it, and the woman of about sixty let it happen and did not sit down.

---

**And a woman of about twenty-nine asked the only question anybody asked in that room on that Tuesday, and she asked it in about nine seconds, and she asked it to the room and not to the woman of about thirty-four.**

She asked whether anybody in this city knew whether the thing those four hundred people do over there is safe.

Nobody in that room answered her.

A man of about fifty-two said in about nine seconds that nobody in that room knew and that four of them had said so out loud in the last nine minutes by reading a form, and a man of about thirty-eight said that a question with four hundred people on the other side of it is not a question a first floor can answer, and the woman of about twenty-nine said that is exactly the question she was asking and that she would like to know what to tell her.

**Nobody told her and nobody wrote her anything down and she did not ask twice, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted to answer her and could not think of one sentence that would not have been a lie.**

A man of about twenty-four said in about nine seconds that she could be told that nobody knows, which is not the same as being told that it is safe, and that the two have been running together in this city for about nine years and that most of the damage on that Tuesday was done by somebody not saying which one they meant.

The woman of about twenty-nine said in four seconds that she would write *nobody knows* down if anybody would let her write it down.

**Nobody in that room agreed to let her and nobody in that room said why not, and about four people in that room have said since that they did not think anybody wanted to be the first person to put that in writing on a form and that this is why the box on the form has one word in it and no date.**

---

**And a woman of about fifty-two who keeps a register at a counter in a second district said the thing that made the rest of that Tuesday's evening make sense, and she said it in about nine seconds and not to the woman of about twenty-nine.**

She said that a question a room cannot answer is not a failure of the room, it is a question about somebody who is not in the room, and that the somebody is about four hundred people and there is no procedure anywhere in this city for putting a question to a person who has not agreed to be asked.

The woman of about twenty-nine said that she had not agreed to be asked either and had come anyway.

The woman of about fifty-two said that she knows, and that everybody in that room does something without agreeing to be asked, and that the whole of what went wrong in this city about nine years ago was that everybody did and nobody wrote down which four hundred it was.

**Nobody in that room wrote down which four hundred it was and nobody in that room said the four hundred out loud in that room on that Tuesday.**

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four said the only thing anybody said that evening that was about the form and not about the four hundred people.**

She said that she did not write it and that she carried it back on the bus on the Sunday because a man in a town two days of bus travel away gave it to her at a stop with a bus timetable on the wall and told her to put it in front of somebody in this city who dealt with things, and that she asked him three questions on the bus and he answered two of them.

**She said the two answers were that it is done every day and that everybody there does it, and that she asked him how long and he said he did not know and that it had not occurred to him that anybody would want to know.**

Nobody in that room said the word back to her and nobody in that room asked her the name of the town.

---

**A man of about thirty-four who works on the second floor of that building asked her one question, which was whether the man at the bus stop had said his name, and she said he had, and he wrote it on the back of the form in pencil and did not say it out loud and about four people in that room have seen the back of that form and two of them remember a name on it.**

**That name is on no page of this movement after this one and he has not asked her for it again and she has not offered it.**

---

**And about half an hour of that Tuesday was a counter until about six, and about two hours of it were a shop's back door in a second district, where a length of worktop had been fitted with screws into a wall that is a partition and not a wall.**

He took the whole run down, found the studs behind the plaster with a detector and moved every fixing onto them, filled the four holes that had been in the plaster, and refitted the run.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a screw into a partition is a screw into about nine millimetres of board and a bag of plaster and that four runs of worktop in that shop were fixed that way and that one of them has a drawer front that has come away from the carcass at the corner.**

He was asked, by the man who owned the shop, whether it was worth taking the other three down now, and he said that was three more hours and a different day and that the man should have a second man in on a Tuesday and did not have a second man in on a Tuesday.

---

**And the man who owned that shop wanted to know on that Tuesday why he was being told about three other runs of worktop he had not asked about, and it took about nine seconds and Marek gave the reason in about nine seconds and it was the whole of the reason.**

He said that a run fixed into a partition is a run that comes off a wall on a wet Tuesday about nine years after it went on, and that when it comes off it comes off with about nine millimetres of board still in the screw, and that the man who finds it is not the man who fitted it and is usually in a hurry.

He said that he tells people about the other three because he will not be here in nine years and the man will, and that this is the only free thing he does and that the man need not buy anything on a Tuesday for it.

The man said that he had been in that shop for about eleven years and that nobody had ever told him anything about the other three, and that he did not want to know.

**He was told anyway, in about nine seconds, which runs of worktop were fixed into a partition and which four they were, and the man wrote two of the four on the back of his hand and did not write the other two and did not say why.**

---

**And a window in the same shop had been painted shut, and it was the second time in about nine years that any window in that building has been painted shut.**

He broke the seal with a plastic wedge and a piece of card, cleaned the rebate out with a wire brush, and repacked the sash with a length of tapered wood shim rather than with paper.

**Eleven pounds. He told the man that a painted window is a window somebody has decided is not a window, and that four windows in that building have been painted and that nobody in that building has a list of which four.**

---

**And a length of drain pipe on the back of that shop had been let into the same wall joint as the worktop was, and had been packed with a strip of rag.**

He took the pipe off the wall, cut a proper hole with a rotary cutout, fitted a slotted drain bracket round the pipe so the back cannot sit against the wall, refitted it, and ran a length of sealant round the joint where the wall had been cut into.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a pipe let into a wall joint is a pipe let into a crack, and that four downpipes on that lane are in cracks and that one of them has been packed with rag and that the rag is wet and will stay wet.**

---

**And a handrail on the stairs of that building had been fitted with brackets set out from the wall far enough that the rail could be turned a quarter of a turn in its own fixing.**

He took the rail off, packed each bracket back so the rail stands square to the stair, refitted it, and ran it along its whole length to be sure.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a rail that can be turned in its own brackets has been fixed to the wall and not to the stair, and that four rails in that building are like it and that one of them has loose brackets on both sides.**

---

**In the fourth of those four rooms a woman of about thirty sits at her chair from half past six with the door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and nine days without anybody reading it. Nobody has put one question to her about it. Nobody is going to.**
