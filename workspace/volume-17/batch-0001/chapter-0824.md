# Chapter 824 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Turned A Form Round On The Table With Her Hand Flat On The Top Edge Before Anybody In That Room Had Spoken So That Four People Saw The Box Before Anybody Saw The Form

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where a woman of about thirty-four turned a form round on the table with her hand flat on the top edge before anybody had spoken, so that four people were looking at the box in the bottom of it before anybody had looked at the form.**

---

That Thursday's sheet carried nine names with nine dates beside them, the last of those dates taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten, the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**The card in the rail is one thousand five hundred and twenty-six days, two hundred and eighteen weeks to the day, and it is four days ahead of the four rooms off that service road, and the rail takes two.**

**Behind the chair of the woman of about sixty a place has stood empty since a week in the spring, and it is four hundred days old on that Thursday, which is fifty-seven weeks and one day, and it is set down here and on no other page of this stretch of days.**

The ninth chair is against that wall with its back to the whole of the room and it did not move on that Thursday, and the person who moves it is named on nothing anybody in this city holds.

Nobody in that room asked her about the place, and in about fifty-seven weeks nobody has.

---

**And she turned it round with her hand flat on the top edge, and nobody in that room had spoken, and about eleven seconds passed between the hand going down and the first person in that room saying anything at all.**

**It took about nine minutes for the four of them to get to the box. It took about four minutes for the two of them who had not read it on the Monday to read it, and neither of those two read the heading, and both of them read the box, and one of them has said since that she did not notice the two words over the ruled line until somebody said them out loud four days afterwards.**

The woman of about thirty-four said nothing while that was happening. She has said since that she did it because a form lying the other way up on a table is a form nobody reads from the top.

---

**And the register of correct acts that changed nothing was read out in that room on that Thursday, all four of them, by a man of about forty-four, and he read them because he was asked to and not because he offered to.**

Nobody in that room counted them. **He did not say *one*, *two*, *three* or *four*, and nobody in that room has worked out since why not, and about two people in that room have said that it is because the last volume's close found out that nobody in this city counts it and that the counting was the wrong part.**

The four of them took about nine minutes.

**A man of about fifty-two said in about nine seconds afterwards that reading them out loud in a room is a fifth one and that it changes nothing and that nobody should write it down, and the man of about forty-four said that he was not going to write it down and that he had been asked to read them out and had read them out.**

Nobody in that room wrote it down and nobody in that room has referred to it again on any day since.

---

**The four, in the order he gave them, were a man who carried nine beds up a stair on a Sunday and told nobody; a woman who left a form on a counter with the box ticked before anybody asked her whether the box was hers; a man who found a broken handrail, reported it to a landlord, was told it was not that landlord's, and reported it again to a different landlord on the same day; and a woman of about fifty-three who said a thing to a room of nine people and was not asked about it and has not been asked about it since.**

**What the fourth cost, in that room, in about nine seconds, by the man of about forty-four, is that the woman of about fifty-three is on a first floor under a hold and about nine people in that room know that and about nine people in this city know the name of the floor and not one of them has said it to her.**

**Nobody in that room said her name and nobody in that room asked whether she had been asked anything and nobody is going to, and this volume does not say whether anybody has and does not resolve it.**

---

**And a man of about fifty-two said one more thing that evening, in about nine seconds, to the room and not to the woman of about thirty-four, and it is the thing the movement is about.**

He said that a form with a word in the box where a date should be is not a form about a practice, it is a form about a person, and that whoever filled it in was not asked what the practice is, and that the only two people who know anything about it are a man at a bus stop two days of bus travel away and a woman who carried it back on a Sunday, and that neither of them is a member of anything and neither of them has been asked.

A woman of about twenty-nine said in four seconds that she had asked the man at the bus stop three questions and that she had told him so on the Tuesday.

The man of about fifty-two said that she had told the room that and not him, and that he had been in that room and had not heard it, and that she was right and he was not going to ask her to say it again.

**Nobody in that room asked her to say it again and nobody in that room said the three questions out loud and about two people in that room have said since that they would have written them down if they had heard them.**

**A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that there is a version of this room where the three questions are on the wall at the top of it where anybody coming up the stair can read them, and that about four people in that room have thought about it since and that none of them has said it in that room again.**

He said that the reason nobody has said it is that a wall in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket is read by about nine people a day and that about four hundred people over a border are not in this city and cannot read it.

**Nobody in that room asked him how many of the nine a day would go and do the thing differently, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have answered and did not.**

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four said one thing about herself on that Thursday, in about nine seconds, and nobody asked her for it and nobody has asked her since.**

She said that she works in a second district and that she has four hundred people on a form she did not write and that she has been asked by nobody to explain the form and that she has told nobody what it is, and that she does not know what it is, and that this is the fifth day she has said that in a room and that it stops being a sentence this weekend.

A man of about thirty-four said in four seconds that he had written a name on the back of it on the Tuesday and that he would take it off if she wanted.

**She said no in about four seconds, and said that a name on the back of a form is the only thing on it that anybody can be asked about, and that nobody has asked, and that she would rather it stayed.**

---

**And the rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district, where four downpipes on one side of a lane were discharging into a channel that had been built with a fall the wrong way.**

He took the topmost fourteen inches off the bottom of each of the four and re-screwed them, which moved every outlet four inches up and put the fall back.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that four downpipes on that side of that lane have been discharging into a channel that drains towards the channel, and that one of them has been running against the fall since it was built and that the wall behind it is wet to about two feet.**

---

**And a length of guttering on the same building had been fixed with a bracket through a soffit that had been replaced and not the bracket.**

He took the guttering off, filled the old holes, cut four lengths of the size of the rafter tail, let them into the plumb of the rafters, and refitted the gutter through those.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a bracket in a replaced soffit is a bracket in about nine years of weather, and that four runs of guttering in that lane are on replaced soffits and that one of them has come down on a wet Tuesday and put a bucket in a doorway.**

---

**And a length of paving in a yard in the same district had been laid on a fall of about one in eighty towards a doorway, which is a fall, and the door has been hard to open in the wet for about two years.**

He lifted eleven flags, took out the bedding sand under them, re-graded the base to about one in sixty away from the door, and relaid the same flags.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a fall towards a door is a fall and that four yards in that district are laid to their doors and that one of them is laid to its drain and holds about four gallons a week.**

---

**And a manhole cover in the same lane had been dished by about two inches in the middle by a delivery, and the lane's low point is four feet from it.**

He lifted the cover, packed the frame up on three shims on the high side, and set the cover back on its original seating.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a cover dished by a wheel is a low point about four feet away, and that four covers in that lane are dished and that one of them has standing water in it after about nine minutes of rain.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six with the door shut, and on the back shelf behind her the eighth of eight has been for three hundred and eleven days. It has not been read. No question about it has been put to her, and none is going to be.**
