# Chapter 825 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And About Four Hours Of A Hall In A Fourth District Where About Four Hundred People Came To Look At A Sheet On A Table And Nobody Said Anything To The Woman Who Stood Behind It

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a hall in a fourth district where about four hundred people came, one at a time and in twos, to look at a sheet on a table, and nobody said anything to the woman who stood behind it and she said almost nothing herself.**

---

Nine names went on that Friday's sheet and each of them took a date, the ninth at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten, which is where it goes down on every day of this stretch of days.

**Line seventeen is one thousand two hundred and ninety-five days, one hundred and eighty-five weeks to the day. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and seventy-one, one hundred and fifty-three weeks to the day. The separation is nine hundred and three days, one hundred and twenty-nine weeks to the day.**

---

**And the hall was open from about eleven until about seven and nobody turned anybody away and about four hundred people came through the door of it on that Friday, and the count is a number one man kept on a pad by the door and not a number anybody has taken.**

**It is on no form of any body in this city. It is not a membership. It is not the number of doors anything is at. It is not printed in the load book of that Friday and it is not in that Friday's body, and the man who kept it has said that he did it because a hall that cannot say how many people came through its door is a hall that has to be argued about afterwards.**

A woman of about thirty-four stood behind the table with the form on it for about seven hours of that Friday and she did not sit down at any point and she has said that she had not expected the table to be the hard part.

---

**And about four of the four hundred said something to her and about the rest said nothing, and she has said since that the four are the four she can remember and that she does not think the other three hundred and ninety-six said nothing, only that she could not hear them.**

A man of about sixty said in about nine seconds that he has done it over there for about thirty years and that it takes about eleven minutes and that he has never once been asked by anybody in this city whether it was safe.

A woman of about thirty-eight said in four seconds that she has done it for about nine years and that her mother did it and that nobody over there has ever asked her either.

A man of about twenty-nine said that he wanted to know whether the word in the box was a fact or a politeness and that he had walked two days of bus travel to ask and that he was sorry to have come.

She said in about four seconds that she was not the person to ask and that she carried it back on a bus on a Sunday and that she had asked the man at the stop three questions on the bus and he had answered two of them.

The man of about twenty-nine said that she had said that in a room on the Tuesday and he said that he had not been in that room, and she said that she knew and that she was saying it again anyway because he had come two days of bus travel.

**Nobody in that hall said the third question out loud and about nine people in that hall have said since that they would have given anything to hear it.**

---

**And a woman of about fifty-two who keeps a register at a counter in a second district came to that hall on that Friday, on her day off, and she did not go to the table, and she stood about nine feet away from it for about forty minutes.**

She has said since that she came because somebody in that hall would eventually ask her whether the four hundred on the form and the four hundred through the door are the same four hundred, and that nobody has asked her yet and that she would rather have the question early.

A woman of about thirty-four said in about four seconds that the two numbers are not the same four hundred and that there are people who came through that door who came for the hall.

The woman of about fifty-two said that she knew, and that she had not asked, and that the reason she came was to be standing near it.

**Nobody in that hall asked either of them anything else and nobody thanked the woman of about thirty-four for being there all day and she says she was not thanked and did not expect to be and that the day was long.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who works on the second floor of the building in Saltmarket came to that hall at about half past four on that Friday and stood at the table for about nine minutes and read the whole of it and asked one question.**

He asked whether the four words over the ruled line were the same four words everywhere it was done.

The woman of about thirty-four said that she did not know and that she had not asked and that the man at the bus stop had answered two of three and that the ruled line was not one of his answers.

**He did not write anything down on that Friday and about four people in that hall have said since that they wanted to see him write it down and that he did not, and he has said that he did not because he does not know the answer and would have had to leave a gap.**

---

**And the rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where a fire door on a stair had a closer on it that had been set to slam so hard that the latch could not be turned from the landing side without putting a hand round the edge of the door.**

He took the closer off, took the pin out of the spindle, turned the body about a quarter of a turn on its back plate, and refitted it.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a closer set to slam is a door that has to be hit to be opened and that four closers on that stair are set that way and that one of them has a strike plate worn to about a third of its thickness.**

---

**And a second fire door on the same stair had a closer that had been wired so that the arm could not swing at all.**

He took the wire off, cut about four millimetres out of the arm so that the swing came back to normal, and re-secured it with a proper split pin.

**Eleven pounds. He told the man that a wired arm is a door that does not close from anywhere above it, and that four doors on that stair have arms wired or pinned and that one of them is a door a man has to shout to be let through.**

---

**And a pair of hooks had been put into a fire door with two screws each and into a masonry wall with one each.**

He put a third screw into each of the four hooks, went into the wall and not the frame where the hook was in the wall, and refitted them.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a hook in a fire door is a hook somebody will pull in a hurry, and that four sets in that building have two screws and one of those four has pulled about half an inch out of the frame.**

---

**And a length of nosing had been fitted over a nosing, on a stair, in three places.**

He took the whole run off, took the top nosing back to sound, refitted the run onto the base, and let the nosing sit on it the way it was meant to.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a nosing over a nosing is a nosing with two thicknesses and one fixing line, and that four runs of nosing in that building are doubled and that one of them has a tread about four millimetres low where it has worn through.**

---

**From half past six the fourth of those four rooms holds a woman of about thirty at her chair, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has lain on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and twelve days without being read. Nobody has asked her about it once. Nobody is going to.**

---
