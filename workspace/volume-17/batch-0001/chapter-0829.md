# Chapter 829 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And About Four Hours Of A Room Over A Shop In A Second District Where About Four People Compared Four Words On A Form With Four Words Somebody Else Wrote Down In About Nine Years And The Two Sets Of Words Were Not The Same And Nobody In That Room Said Which One Was Right

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a room over a shop in a second district, where about four people compared four words on a form with four words somebody else had written down in about nine years, and the two sets of words were not the same, and nobody in that room said which one was right.**

---

That Thursday's sheet had nine names on it and nine dates with them, the last of the dates taken at about twenty to five. At about ten the shutter came down, and that is the hour on every day of this stretch of days.

**Those four rooms stand at one thousand five hundred and twenty-nine days, two hundred and eighteen weeks and three days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is four days further on at one thousand five hundred and thirty-three, two hundred and nineteen weeks to the day, and the rail takes two.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand three hundred and one days, one hundred and eighty-five weeks and six days. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and seventy-seven, one hundred and fifty-three weeks and six days. The separation is nine hundred and nine days, one hundred and twenty-nine weeks and six days.**

---

**And the four words over that ruled line are not the four words that are written down in about three places in this city where somebody wrote them by accident, and about four people compared the two sets on that Thursday in about nine minutes.**

**Nobody in that room said which set was right. A man of about fifty-two said in about nine seconds that nobody in that room knew what the four words meant on either side and that about four people had spent nine minutes proving that two documents in the same city about the same thing do not use the same words, and that this is not the same as saying one of them is wrong.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in four seconds that the four words on the form are the four words the man at the bus stop used, and that she wrote them down herself on the Sunday on the back of a receipt, and that she is not going to be asked which is correct because she does not know.

Nobody in that room asked her.

---

**And the other set of four words came out of a drawer in a second district in a building that receives the returns, in about nine minutes, by a man of about fifty-two who has the key and has not opened that drawer since before the spring.**

He has said since that he opened it because a woman asked him a question about four words and he knew there were two versions and had never put them side by side, and that putting them side by side took about nine minutes and that after about nine minutes he wanted to shut the drawer.

**Nobody in that room said that he should not have opened it and nobody in that room has asked to see the other four words and about two people in that room have said since that they would have liked to and did not ask.**

He said that the four words in that drawer are in a hand about nine years old and are not in a hand anybody in this city recognises and that he is not going to say whose hand it is on the ground that he does not know and that he has looked at it about four times.

---

**And a woman of about twenty-nine asked in that room on that Thursday whether the two sets of four words could both be right, and a man of about thirty-eight said no, and she said why, and he gave a reason in about nine seconds.**

His reason was that if both sets were right then whoever wrote either one of them could have written anything, and that a practice that four hundred people do every day is not a thing anybody can write down loosely and still be doing.

She said that she had not asked whether either of them was written loosely, and that she had asked whether two people can use different words for the same four steps, and that a bus timetable in this city has two names for about half its stops and that the buses still run.

**Nobody in that room agreed with her and nobody in that room disagreed with her and about two people in that room have said since that they have thought about the two-names-on-a-timetable thing for about nine days and have not worked out whether it is the same.**

---

**And the rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where a set of shutters on a shop front had been re-hung with the barrels on the wrong side of the stiles.**

He took all eight off, reversed the barrel on each, put them back on with new screws into the old holes filled, and re-hung them.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a shutter barrel on the wrong side is a shutter that does not close over its own weight and that four sets of shutters in that district are hung that way and that one of them has a bottom rail that has dropped about nine millimetres out of square.**

---

**And a length of fascia in the same shop had been fixed with screws into the face of a rafter instead of into a rafter end.**

He took it off, filled the old holes, cut four lengths to the rafter depth, let them into the plumb of the rafters, and refitted the fascia through those.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a fascia fixed into the face of a rafter is fixed into about nine millimetres of softwood and that four fascias in that district are like that and that one of them has pulled about half an inch away at its left end.**

---

**And a canopy bracket in the same shop had been bolted to a fascia that was not over anything.**

He took the bracket off, found the nearest thing above it that was actually carrying load, and refitted the bracket with four new bolts into that.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a canopy bracket on a fascia that carries nothing is a bracket carrying a canopy off four screws and about four inches of board, and that four brackets in that district are on that kind of fascia and that one of them has a crack about nine inches long beside the fixings.**

---

**And a length of rainwater pipe in the same shop had been run behind a downpipe so that two pipes were in the same space.**

He took the pipe out, moved it four inches clear, put a bracket through the wall behind it and refitted it.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that two pipes in one space are two pipes that cannot be replaced and that four runs in that lane are doubled up and that one of them has a length of pipe about four feet long inside another one.**

---

**Since half past six a woman of about thirty has been at her chair in the fourth of those four rooms with her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on that back shelf behind her for three hundred and eighteen days. Nobody has put a question to her about it. Nobody means to.**
