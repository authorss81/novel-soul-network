# Chapter 828 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where About Nine People Came Up A Stair And About Two Of Them Came To Find Out Whether A Man Of Twenty-Two Had Been Right To Say No And About Two Of Them Came To Tell Him That He Had Said It Wrong

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people came up a stair and about two of them came to find out whether a man of twenty-two had been right to say no, and about two of them came to tell him that he had said it the wrong way round.**

---

Nine names went on that Wednesday's sheet and nine dates went with them, the last taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten, which is the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**Those four rooms stand at one thousand five hundred and twenty-eight days, two hundred and eighteen weeks and two days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is four days further on at one thousand five hundred and thirty-two, two hundred and eighteen weeks and six days, and the rail takes two.**

**Line eighteen is one thousand two hundred and forty-six days, one hundred and seventy-eight weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand one hundred and thirty-four, one hundred and sixty-two weeks to the day. What is still owed is one thousand two hundred and eighteen, one hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day.**

---

**And no number was said in that room on that Wednesday, for the reason it is never said on a Wednesday that is not a sitting, and about nine people came up that stair between half past six and about seven.**

About two of them had come to find out whether a man of twenty-two had been right to say no on the Tuesday. About two of them had come to tell him that he had said it the wrong way round. About four of them had come because somebody they work with had come.

A man of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that he had come to tell him he had said it the wrong way round, and that wrong way round meant that a man who cannot hold a release steady for about two hours in eleven years is exactly the man who should say whether four hundred people are safe, and that the room had asked the wrong man the wrong question and he had given the wrong answer to it.

**About four people in that room said that this was the argument everybody had been waiting for and that nobody in that room agreed with it and one person in that room agreed with all of it.**

He said in about four seconds that he had not said it the wrong way round, that he had said the thing that was true about himself, and that if four hundred people over a border are going to be made safe by a rule then somebody in nine years will find a way to apply that rule to somebody he cannot hold a release steady for, and that he knows that because he has spent about eleven years being the person that rule would have been applied to.

Nobody in that room said that he had said that on the Tuesday. About four people in that room have said since that they were not there on the Tuesday and that they believe him and that they cannot check it.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-eight said the other half of it in about nine seconds and she said it to the room and not to him.**

She said that a man who cannot hold a release steady is not disqualified from saying a thing out loud, he is disqualified from being the only one who says it, and that the Tuesday had been the second time in about nine days that one person had been asked to stand in a room and be the answer, and that she had not put him there and neither had anybody else and that everybody had been quietly waiting for somebody to.

**Nobody in that room said she was talking about somebody in particular and about four people in that room have said since that she was and that she did not name anybody on purpose.**

He said in four seconds that she was right and that he had not worked out until she said it that that was what had happened, and that he had spent about four seconds on the Tuesday with a different sentence and that the different sentence had been about four hundred people and had been the better answer and would have been the answer everybody wanted.

---

**And about half an hour of that Wednesday evening was in the corridor outside that room, where four people stood and did not go in either, and one of the four was a man of twenty-two.**

He has said since that he stood in that corridor for about half an hour and worked out that a sentence he had been carrying since a Sunday in another volume, about a decision he made in a different city a long time ago, is the reason he is the man people ask, and that it is not a reason anybody chose.

**Nobody in that corridor said that to him and about two people in that corridor have said since that they have worked out the same thing about somebody else and have not said it.**

---

**And the rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district, where a length of trench cover had been replaced with a plate of a different thickness and the frame had been packed with a hardwood strip at one end only.**

He lifted the plate, took the strip out, packed the frame all round with a lead wedge and a mastic strip, and refitted the plate.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a frame packed at one end has about nine millimetres of movement at the other, and four covers in that lane are packed that way and that one of them has a plate that rocks about four millimetres at the far end when a wheel goes over it.**

---

**And a length of gully grating in the same lane had been bedded on tarmac rather than on the frame.**

He took the grating out, cut the tarmac back to sound all round, rebased it, and set the grating on a lead strip.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a grating bedded on tarmac carries no load into the frame and that four gratings in that lane are bedded that way and that one of them has a bar that is broken about four inches from the frame.**

---

**And a length of kerb in the same lane had been laid on the tarmac shoulder rather than on the road base.**

He took eleven feet of it out, cut the shoulder back to the road base, and relaid the same kerb on a base course.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a kerb on a tarmac shoulder settles about four inches in about two winters, and that four lengths in that lane are laid on shoulders and that one of them has dropped about two inches and opened a gap at its joint.**

---

**And a drain cover in the same lane had been propped open with a piece of timber and left like that for about nine weeks.**

He took the timber out, closed the cover, and put a bar through its handle and the frame so that it could not be propped without a key.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that an open cover propped with a bit of wood is an open hole in a footway, and four covers in that lane have been propped and that one of them has been propped about nine times.**

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and seventeen days and has not been read, and nobody has asked her one question about it and nobody is going to.**
