# Chapter 849 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room In A Second District With About Nine People In It And A Form On A Table About The Size Of A Folded Sheet Of Writing Paper, And A Woman Was Asked To Sign Something About Four Hundred People Who Are Not In This City And Refused In About Four Seconds And The Reason Was About A Signature And Not About The Border, And Nobody In That Room Agreed And Nobody Disagreed

**The refusal took about four seconds and it was said in a face, in a room in a second district, in front of about nine people, and the reason was about a signature and not about the border. Nobody in that room agreed with her and nobody disagreed.**

---

The book for that Thursday took eleven names and eleven dates, the last of the dates at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**The card on end in the rail beside that door is one thousand five hundred and sixty-one days, two hundred and twenty-three weeks to the day, and the four rooms it runs ahead of stand at one thousand five hundred and fifty-seven, two hundred and twenty-two weeks and three days, and the rail takes two.**

**Line twelve is one thousand four hundred and seventy-seven days, two hundred and eleven weeks to the day. Line thirteen is one thousand four hundred and twenty-eight, two hundred and four weeks to the day. Line fourteen is one thousand three hundred and ninety-three, a hundred and ninety-nine weeks to the day. Line fifteen is one thousand three hundred and seventy-two, a hundred and ninety-six weeks to the day.**

**Line nineteen, the last line anybody uses, is one thousand two hundred and fifty-three days, a hundred and seventy-nine weeks to the day. The hold on nine crates and the floor they stand on is one thousand one hundred and ninety, a hundred and seventy weeks to the day.**

---

**And the form on that table is about the size of a folded sheet of writing paper and it has a box at the bottom of it, and the box wants one signature.**

Above the box there are four words and under the four words there is a space about the width of a thumb, and the width of a thumb will take a signature laid down and will not take a name written out in full.

Above the four words, in the middle of the sheet, there is a heading that wants a number.

**About nine people were in that room and about four of them had wanted that box to be filled in for about nine days and about four had not dared to want it.**

---

**And the woman asked was the woman of about thirty-four who has carried one of those sheets back on a bus from a town two days of travel away, and she was asked at about ten past seven.**

The asking was done by a man of about thirty-four who owns four vehicles and he asked her in about nine seconds and he asked her in front of about nine people because he had said in advance that he would ask her in front of them.

He said that she is the only person in this city who has been over there and come back, and that the sheet cannot be signed by anybody who has not, and that this is the only reason he asked her.

She said no in about four seconds.

---

**And the reason she gave was about four seconds long and it was about a signature and not about the border.**

She said that a signature is a person saying they stand behind a thing, and that she has never spoken to one of the four hundred people and does not know one of their names, and that her name is not the one being described.

**She said it in four seconds and she did not add to it and about nine people in that room heard all of it.**

A man of about thirty-eight said in about four seconds that she is not refusing because they are over there.

She said, in four seconds, that she had not been asked about that.

---

**And nobody in that room agreed with her and nobody in that room disagreed with her, and about four people in that room had wanted the signature and about four had not dared to.**

A man of about fifty-two said in about nine seconds that a signature is worth what the person signing it knows, and that a signature from somebody who has spoken to nobody is worth nothing, and that this is a thing about the four seconds and not about the border.

**Nobody in that room said whether that made the refusal correct and nobody in that room has said since.**

A woman of about thirty-nine said in about nine seconds that she wanted the box filled in and had wanted it since the Wednesday, and that she had not said so and was saying so now, and that she is not going to argue with a woman who has been asked to put her name under four hundred people she has never met.

**About four people in that room said afterwards that they had wanted it too and about four said afterwards that they had not dared to, and neither group said anything to the other.**

---

**And the man of twenty-two was in that room and was not asked anything and did not say anything for about the whole of it.**

He is not the person who signs anything in this stretch of days and was not the person that sheet was put up to, and he has said since that he worked out during the four seconds that the whole difficulty is a space the width of a thumb.

He said that a space the width of a thumb will take a signature and will not take a name written out, and that this city built about four thousand of those spaces in the spring and filled in none of them.

**Nobody in that room asked him how he knew and he did not say.**

A man of about thirty-four who works on the second floor of a building in Saltmarket said in about nine seconds that he has been one of the four and that he has never told anybody that he does not know whether the thing is safe, and that this room is the first one he has been in where that could be said out loud without anybody answering it.

**Nobody in that room answered it and nobody in that room has answered it since.**

---

**And the rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a fourth district.**

**A loading ramp in the same building had been packed up with steel shims at the low end where it meets the yard, and had worked loose so that a loaded trolley could get a wheel under it.**

He took the shims out, dug the low end back to sound, and laid the whole ramp on a fresh fall with the high end packed.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a ramp packed on shims is a ramp that moves at the joint, and that about four ramps in that yard have been shimmed up over about nine years, and that one of them has a lip at the low end that a trolley wheel can get under and a man cannot see.**

---

**A door on a stair in the same building had a hold-open magnet fitted to the wrong leaf, so that the door it was supposed to hold open was on the other side of the stair.**

He took the magnet off that leaf, drilled and plugged the two holes, and fitted it to the leaf it was meant for.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a hold-open on the wrong leaf is a door nobody is holding open, and that about four magnets in that building are on the wrong leaf, and that one of them has been on the wrong side for about four years and has been reported about nine times as a door that will not shut.**

---

**A length of rail in the same yard had about a third of its chairs fitted the wrong way round, so that the joint at each of them worked about nine millimetres loose under a loaded wagon.**

He took that third up, turned the chairs, packed the fastenings properly, and brought the joint to torque.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a chair the wrong way round is a joint that has to be hammered to close, and that about four lengths in that yard have been laid by three gangs, and that one of them has about a third of its chairs turned and a gang has been blamed for it twice.**

---

**A stack of sheets in the same building had been standing on a slab that ponds at one corner, and about four of the sheets had gone soft along their bottom edges.**

She took the stack down, cut the four affected sheets off, shimmed the run up about an inch onto a bearer, and stacked the sheets back on that.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a sheet on a slab that ponds is a sheet that becomes a floor for whatever goes on it next, and that four stacks in that building stand on that kind of slab, and that one of them has a bearer that has sunk about nine millimetres and has been described as a bad floor for about four years.**

**That Thursday came to a hundred and two pounds, exact, and it is the four of them together.**

**From half past six there has been a woman of about thirty at her chair in the fourth of those four rooms with her door shut, and on the back shelf behind her the eighth of eight has lain for three hundred and forty-six days.**

**Nobody has asked her one question about it. Nobody is going to. What is on that page is not given here.**
