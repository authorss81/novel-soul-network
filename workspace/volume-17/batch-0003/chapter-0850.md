# Chapter 850 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Counter And About Two Hours In A Room Off A Service Road Where About Nine People Were Sitting Down For The First Time In About Nine Days, And The Movement Closed On The Fact That Three Places Describe A Practice That Four Hundred People Do Every Day And That Not One Of The Three Can Be Read By Anybody Who Does It, And On A Decision A Man Of About Thirty-Four Made About Next Tuesday And Not About The Four Hundred

**A counter and about four hours on that Friday, and a room off a service road in the evening where about nine people sat down for the first time in about nine days. A man of about thirty-four decided something about next Tuesday and it was about a wall map.**

**And the whole of what that room did in about two hours was establish one thing, and the thing is an absence and not a person.**

Three places in this city describe what four hundred people do over a border every day. A wages book with a column. A margin in a gate day book. A laundry book kept to count a Thursday.

**Not one of the three can be read by anybody who does it. Every word in all three of them is in this city's language and none of the three is in the language of a person who does the thing, and not one of the four hundred has ever seen any of them.**

A man of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that this city has three descriptions of a thing done by four hundred people a day, and that the four hundred have none, and that the three have been there for about nine years.

**Nobody in that room said what should be done about it and nobody in that room has said since, and about four people in that room have said afterwards that they wanted somebody else to say it first.**

**And about four people in that room had come to give something and about four had come to say that nothing had changed.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that she has not said anything to anybody over there and that nobody has asked her to and that she is not going to be the one who starts it on the strength of a laundry book.

**Nobody in that room asked her whether she would and nobody in that room has asked her since.**

A man of about fifty-two said in about nine seconds that the man who wrote it in a margin asked him how long the thing takes, and that he answered him, and that the answer is in a book in a fourth district and has been for about nine years and that he has thought about it every working day since about four days ago.

A man of about thirty-eight said that this is a thing about answerings and not about descriptions, and that a man who asked a question and got an answer wrote the answer down and that is the whole of how the three of them happened.

**Nobody in that room said that is why and nobody in that room has said since.**

---

**And the decision was made at about twenty to seven and it took the man who made it about nine seconds to say and it was about next Tuesday.**

The man of about thirty-four who owns four vehicles said that his office writes every run against a place and that the sheet his vans carry has a box on it for a place name, and that the box has a name in it that a man copied off a map.

He said that on Tuesday he is going to go to the office that keeps the map and take it down off the wall and put it in a drawer, and that he is going to write on the sheet that the run finishes at a town and not at a stretch of country, and that he is doing it because a run is written against a place.

**Nobody in that room argued with him and nobody in that room asked him why, and about two people in that room have said since that they did not know anybody had noticed the map.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in about four seconds that a man of about thirty-one keeps that map and has looked at it every working day since a Monday and has not taken it down and nobody has asked him to.

**Nobody in that room asked whether the man knew about the decision and nobody in that room has asked him, and the man of about thirty-four said he did not know and would go on a Tuesday.**

**And the rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district.**

**A length of skirting on a floor had been jammed under a floor finish that had been laid over it, and had lifted about four millimetres along the whole run.**

He took the run off, took up about four feet of the finish at the junction, rebedded the skirting, and laid the finish back down.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that skirting under a floor finish is skirting that has been buried twice, and that about four runs in that building were buried when the floor was done, and that one of them has been lifted about four millimetres along nine feet and has been described as a floor that moves.**

**A door frame in the same building had a head that had been packed with foam and had dropped about four millimetres on one side, so that the door was harder to shut at that corner than at the other.**

He took the foam out, packed the head on mortar, and refitted the door to the frame.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a head packed with foam is a head that takes a nail for about four years and then takes nothing at all, and that about four heads in that building are packed that way, and that one of them has a door that has been planed at the top corner by somebody who decided the frame was the problem.**

**A trench cover frame in a lane had been packed with a timber offcut that had gone soft and let the frame drop about nine millimetres at one corner.**

She lifted the plate, took the offcut out, dug the pocket out to the bottom, and rebedded the frame all round on a lead wedge.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a frame packed on timber is a frame packed on something that has a season, and that about four covers in that lane have been packed on offcuts, and that one of them has a frame that has dropped at one corner and rocks every time a wheel comes over the far end.**

**A door in the same building had a lock case let into a rail that moves about four millimetres across a year, and the latch had worn into a curve.**

He took the case out, filled the old opening, fitted a new case with the spindle set back into the solid behind, and refitted the door.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a lock case let into a moving rail is a case that moves with the door it is meant to shut, and that about four doors in that building have their latches in the rail, and that one of them has a strike plate that has been filed to fit a latch that had already gone.**

**Those four came to ninety-five pounds on that Friday between them, exact.**
