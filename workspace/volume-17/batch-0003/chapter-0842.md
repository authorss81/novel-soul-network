# Chapter 842 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And Three Places In One Day Where The Practice Was Written Down By Accident, The Third Of Which Is A Laundry Book Kept By A Woman Who Was Counting Socks, And A Man Who Went To Look At All Three And Refused In About Four Seconds To Have Any Of Them Photographed, And The Reason Was About The Sending And Not About The Ink

**Three places, three districts and about nine hours were the shape of that Tuesday, and the third of the three turned out to be a book a woman keeps to count what comes in on a Thursday. A man who went to look at all three said no in about four seconds to having any of them copied out.**

---

That Tuesday's book carries nine names with a date against each, the ninth of the dates taken at about twenty to five, and the shutter went down at about ten.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand five hundred and forty-eight days, two hundred and twenty-one weeks and one day, and the card on end in the rail beside that door is one thousand five hundred and fifty-two, two hundred and twenty-one weeks and five days, and the rail takes two.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand three hundred and twenty days, a hundred and eighty-eight weeks and four days. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and ninety-six, a hundred and fifty-six weeks and four days. The separation is nine hundred and twenty-eight days, a hundred and thirty-two weeks and four days.**

---

**And the three places are a wages book in a fourth district, a day book at a works gate in a second district, and a laundry book in a first district, and all three of them are about nine years old.**

The wages book is kept by a man of about sixty who has kept it since before the spring four years ago and who writes what a man is paid in a column and has never had a column for anything else.

The day book is kept at a gate where about nine men clock in and out and where a man of about thirty-four wrote in the margin, in about nine seconds, the four words, because a man asked him how long the thing took and he wrote it down rather than say it twice.

**Neither of those two men knows that what he wrote is the only description of it anybody in this city can point to, and neither has been told, and about four people who were in that works gate have since worked out that they were in a room with a record and did not know.**

---

**And the third of the three is a laundry book, and the woman who keeps it kept it because she was counting.**

Her name is on the front of it in ink and the ink has not run and it is about nine years old. On about nine lines of it, in a column headed with a day, she has written four words and against each of the four words a number of between about eleven and about thirty.

She kept it because she was counting what came in on a Thursday. The four words are the name she gave to a thing people brought in and she wanted to know whether it was the same thing every Thursday, and she wrote it in her own book because she had not been asked to write it anywhere and there was nowhere else to put it.

**Nobody in this city has told that woman what she wrote and nobody in this city has asked her, and about four people who were told about the book in the last week have said since that they wanted to go and tell her and have not.**

She said, in about nine seconds, that she does the same book now and that this year's is on the shelf behind the counter and that nobody has asked to see it.

**Nobody has asked to see it.**

---

**And the man who went to look at all three is a man of about fifty-two who keeps a register at a counter in a second district, and he went on one Tuesday because about four people had told him about nine years ago that he had wanted to know.**

He got to the works gate first and read the margin standing up, and he read the wages book on a table in a canteen with a man of about sixty sitting opposite him and not knowing why he had come.

**And then he went to the first district on a bus and asked for the laundry and was told which book and was handed the book.**

He has said since that he stood in that shop for about nine minutes and did not open it at the counter and carried it to the end of the counter and opened it there, and that he read about nine lines and put it back and thanked nobody and was thanked by nobody.

---

**And the refusal was asked for at about half past three in a corridor in that first district by a man of about twenty-six who had come up behind him, and it took about four seconds.**

The man of about twenty-six said that if a person wrote those four words down in a book of their own then the four words were about nine years old in a hand nobody in this city recognises, and that this was the only description of the thing that existed anywhere, and that it should be copied out and printed.

**The man of about fifty-two said no in about four seconds, and the reason was about the sending and not about the ink.**

He said that a thing written down by accident in about nine years in a hand nobody recognises is the only description there is and that copying it out and printing it would make it a description this city had made, and that there is nobody over there to say whether it is the one.

**Nobody in that corridor argued with him and nobody in that corridor disagreed with him, and about four people who were in that shop have said since that they thought he was being careful and about four have said since that they thought he was frightened.**

A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that both of those are the same thing said by two people who would rather not be the one who was wrong.

---

**And the rest of that Tuesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district.**

**A padlock on a cupboard door in a lock-up had been painted into the frame about nine years ago and the key had been thrown away.**

He came off the escutcheon, cleaned the paint out of the keeper with a chisel and a wire brush, rebated the door edge a hair, and refitted the lock on a fresh keep.

**Six pounds. He told the man that a padlock painted into a frame is a lock that has been declared not to open, and that about four cupboards in that row are painted that way, and that one of them has a door that has been planed at the corner to make a lock go back into.**

---

**A sash window in the same building had a cord that had been replaced with a length of fencing wire.**

He took the sash out, cut the wire out, refitted new cords on the original pulleys, and put the weights back.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that wire on a sash is wire that takes the whole weight on one edge, and that about four sashes in that street are corded with wire, and that one of them has a bottom rail that has dropped about half an inch at the corner nearest the wall.**

---

**A threshold bar at a shop entrance had been set so that it fell the wrong way and let a cart's wheel up onto the footpath.**

He took the bar out, turned it over, and bedded it again on a fall towards the channel.

**Seven pounds. He told the man that a threshold set uphill is a bar that a loaded wheel uses as a step, and that about four bars on that street are laid that way, and that one of them has been shimmed up at one end by a builder and has not been level since.**

---

**A name plate on a door at the back of that shop was loose on two screws and moved when the door was worked.**

He filled the two slots, fitted a third screw into a fresh hole into the frame behind, and packed the slot so the plate sat flat.

**Four pounds. He told the man that a plate loose on a door is a plate that reads as a door that is not worth answering, and that about four plates on that street are fixed to two screws into a moving rail, and that one of them has a strike hole that has been elongated about nine millimetres by a hinge that has been worked on too hard.**

**That Tuesday came to twenty-six pounds, exact, and that is what those four came to between them.**

**A woman of about thirty has been at her chair in that fourth room since half past six with the door shut, and behind her the eighth of eight has stood on a shelf for three hundred and thirty-seven days.**

**She has not been asked about it once. She is not going to be. This page leaves it alone.**
