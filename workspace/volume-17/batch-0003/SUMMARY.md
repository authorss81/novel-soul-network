## 2. The movement's spine, and where each of its three things is

**The omission, and the fact that there is no person behind it.** The practice is described in three places in this city and in no place on purpose. **Each of the three was written by somebody doing something else**: a man wrote an answer in a margin because he was asked how long the thing takes; a man wrote it in a wages book in the column he had; a woman wrote it in a laundry book because she was counting what came in on a Thursday. **Not one of the three knows what they wrote and not one has been told. **The sentence that makes the movement, and it is about an absent procedure and not about a broken rule.** Chapter 843, in about nine seconds, to the room: *there is no place in this city where it is written down on purpose, and this is not because anybody decided there should not be one.* **Chapter 846 states the shape of the absence in the same register and in the same day:** there is no form in this city for finding out that there is a thing to hold. **The refusal, and what it was actually about.** Chapter 849. **It was about a signature and not about the border**, and the reason is specific, practical and four seconds long: *a signature is a person saying they stand behind a thing, and I have never spoken to one of the four hundred people and does not know one of their names, and her name is not the one being described.* **She is the only person in this city who has been over a border and come back, and the reason she was asked is that nobody else could be.** Nobody in that room agreed with her and nobody disagreed, and about four of them had wanted that box filled in for about nine days and about four had not dared to want it.

**And what is not on any page of this movement:** the four words, the number of people who do it, any correction to the woman of about thirty-four, any name over a border, any word at all about whether the practice the four hundred kept after their district left worked.
