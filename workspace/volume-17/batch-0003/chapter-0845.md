# Chapter 845 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, About Four Hours Of It At A Counter, And A Man Of About Sixty-One Came In With A Form Of This Body's Own And Asked In About Nine Seconds Whether His Name Was On It And It Was Not And The Man At The Counter Could Not Say Why And The Reason Is About Four Words On A Line That Has Been There Since Before The Spring

**About four hours at a counter on that Friday, and about sixteen callers, and one of them came in at about eleven with a form of this body's own and asked in about nine seconds whether his name was on it. Nobody in that shop offered him an explanation and nobody asked him to leave it.**

---

Sixteen callers' names and sixteen dates went onto that Friday's sheet, the sixteenth date at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Line seventeen is one thousand three hundred and twenty-three days, a hundred and eighty-nine weeks to the day. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and ninety-nine days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks to the day. The separation is nine hundred and thirty-one days, a hundred and thirty-three weeks to the day.**

---

**And he came in at about eleven with a form in a plastic sleeve and he put it on that counter and asked the question before he had taken his coat off.**

He asked whether his name was on it.

He said that it is a form of this body's own, that he filled in about four sections of it at a counter in a second district in the spring, and that it came back to him in the autumn, and that his name is not on it.

**Nobody at that counter said anything for about four seconds and then the man at the counter said that he would look, and he did look, for about nine minutes.**

The name is not on it. His section is on it and it is filled in in his own hand and the date is in his own hand, and the name is not on it anywhere on the page.

**Nobody in that shop can say why and about four people in that shop have said since that they have looked at the same form type and cannot find a reason.**

---

**And the reason is about four words on a line, and the four words have been there since before the spring.**

The form has a line near the top of a section and on that line, printed, are four words. Under the line there is a space about two inches deep and the space is where a name goes.

The four words are the words for what a person is being asked to confirm. On this form type they have been printed since before the spring and they have not been changed since and about four thousand of these forms went out in the spring.

**Nobody in that shop can say what the four words mean, and about four people who have seen the form type have said that they read it as an instruction and not as a title.**

A woman of about thirty-four who works at that counter said in about nine seconds that the line reads as a question and that nobody has ever answered it, and that a space under a question gets nothing put in it by a person filling in a form in about nine seconds.

---

**And the man of about sixty-one said the thing that made the rest of that morning, and it took him about nine seconds.**

He said that he has been in this city for about nine years and has filled in about nine forms of that type in about nine years, and that his name has been on four of them and not on five of them, and that he does not know what the five had in common.

He said that the four it is on, it is on because there is a box with a printed label over it, and that the five it is not on, it is not on because there is a line with four words on it and no box under it.

**Nobody at that counter asked him which is which and nobody at that counter has asked him since, and about two people in that shop have said since that they have gone through their own and cannot tell.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-four who was at that counter said in about four seconds that he has been one of the four for years and that he has never worked out what the four words are for either.**

He said that he assumed it was a heading and that a heading with a space under it is a place for a name and that he has never put one there because there was nothing to put.

**Nobody at that counter asked him what he would have put there and nobody at that counter has asked him since.**

A woman of about twenty-six said in about four seconds that she has a form of that type at home in a drawer and that she has had it for about four years and has never sent it in.

She said that she was not going to be asked about that and that she had said it anyway.

**Nobody in that shop asked her why and nobody in that shop has asked her why since.**

---

**And the rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a fourth district.**

**A stack of nine stillages in a building had been standing on a floor that holds water in one corner, and about four of them had gone soft along their bottom boards.**

He took the stack down, cut the four affected boards out, shimmed the whole run up about an inch onto a bearer, and stacked them back on that.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a stillage on a floor that holds water is a floor for whatever gets stacked on it next, and that four stacks in that building stand on that kind of floor, and that one of them has a bearer that has sunk about nine millimetres into the slab.**

---

**A fire door on a stair in the same building had a wedge that had been painted over and no longer dropped into the frame it sits in.**

He cleaned the paint out of both faces of the frame and both faces of the wedge, and worked it through about nine times.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a fire wedge painted shut is a door that stays as the last person left it, and that about four fire doors in that building have wedges painted, and that one of them has a frame that has been filled and painted over twice without anybody opening the door to see.**

---

**A point rodding run in the same yard had come up with about two rods missing out of the whole length, and the two that were there had been pushed back without being looked at.**

He ran the whole length, found the two, withdrew and cleared them, and put the length back together with new joints where it had been broken.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a rodding run with rods missing is a run that was never rodded, only pushed, and that about four runs in that yard are worked that way, and that one of them has come up clear three times in about four years without anybody ever having opened a valve.**

---

**A racking bay in the same building had one upright packed out with an offcut of timber about nine millimetres thick instead of being shimmed so that the beam sat on metal.**

He unpacked the bay, shimmed the upright properly with steel at four points, and packed the beams back on.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a racking upright packed on timber is a bay that carries its load on a piece of offcut, and that about four bays in that building have been packed that way, and that one of them has a beam that has dropped about four millimetres at one end and taken about half an inch out of true across nine feet.**

**That Friday came to a hundred and two pounds, exact, which is the four of them added together.**

**That evening, at about half past six, a woman of about thirty took her chair in the fourth of those four rooms and shut the door, and the eighth of eight lay on the shelf behind her at three hundred and forty days.**

**Nobody has asked her about it once, in about nine years, and nobody is going to start now.**
