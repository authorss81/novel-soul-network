# Chapter 844 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Counter In A Second District With About Fourteen Callers On It Where A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Asked How A Person Who Has Not Agreed To Be Asked Gets Written Down, And Nobody Answered Her And The Not-Answering Took The Shape Of A Form And A Field On It And The Field Cannot Be Filled In With A Person Who Is Not There To Be Asked

**A Thursday's shape was a counter in a second district with about fourteen callers on it and a question asked there at about half past four that nobody answered. The question was how a person who has not agreed to be asked gets written down, and the not-answering had a shape.**

**And she asked it at about half past four at that counter and it took her about nine seconds and nobody said anything for about nine seconds afterwards.**

She asked how a person who has not agreed to be asked gets written down.

She said that she has a form in her bag with a field on it and that the field wants a person's name, and that she has looked at that field for about four years, and that she cannot find anybody who has been asked and agreed, because there is no box on that form for a person who was not asked.

**Nobody at that counter answered her and nobody at that counter has answered her since, and about four people who were at that counter that afternoon have said afterwards that they had an answer ready and did not give it.**

A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that the form is a form and the field is a field and that is the whole of it, and that this is how forms are.

She said that she had not asked how forms are.

---

**And what the not-answering turned out to be about was not a refusal and was not a rule, and it took about nine minutes of that counter to establish.**

The form is one this city sends to places that hold records. On it there is a box for the name of a person who has confirmed something. **There is no second box on that form, and there has never been one, and the reason is not that anybody took one off.**

A woman of about twenty-six who works at that counter said in about nine seconds that people ask about the field the way they ask about a missing button, and that the field is not missing, and that the form was made for a case where somebody had been asked and had said yes, and that this counter does a different case about nine times a week.

She said that in the other case a person is written on a line in a book with a date against it, and that nobody asks whether they agreed, and that they have never been asked, and that about nine thousand people in this city are on lines in books in that state.

**Nobody at that counter said anything about that and nobody has gone back to it since, and the woman of about twenty-nine said afterwards that she had asked a different question and had got a different answer and had not known she was being answered.**

---

**And then the woman of about twenty-nine said the thing that made the rest of that counter's day, and she said it in about nine seconds and she said it to the form.**

She said that she is not asking for the four hundred people over a border to be asked, and that she has not been asked to ask for anything and is not asking anybody for anything.

She said that she is asking about a form in her own bag with a field on it, and that about four people she has spoken to in about four years have been written down on a field like that one and did not know it.

**Nobody at that counter asked her who the four people are and nobody at that counter has asked her since, and about two people there have said afterwards that they wanted to and did not.**

A man of about twenty-six said in about four seconds that he had looked at her form and that he did not know what she wanted done with it, and that he had looked at it for about nine seconds and had not found anything wrong with it.

She said that she had not brought it to be looked at and that she had brought it because she did not know who else to ask.

**And the rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district.**

**A casement in the same building had its restrictor wired open so that the window could not be shut and could not be left where it was.**

He cut the wire off, replaced the restrictor on the original plate, and put the window back on two catches.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a window wired open is a window that has been decided to stay as somebody last left it, and that about four windows in that building have been wired, and that one of them opens onto a landing where about four people have to pass every hour of the day.**

---

**A length of architrave on the same floor had been nailed across a joint that moves about four millimetres across a year, and the head had opened about nine millimetres at one end.**

He took the run off, filled the old fixings, fitted a scribed backbatten across four joints, and refitted the architrave onto that.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that an architrave across a moving joint is a joint that has been given a name and a frame, and that four runs of architrave on that floor are fixed across joints, and that one of them has an end that has been packed with a card to close a gap and has gone soft.**

---

**A sill in the same building had been bedded on a bead of silicone instead of onto its own rebate, and had dropped about nine millimetres at the outer end.**

He took the sill off, cut the silicone out with a blade, rebedded it on the original rebate, and packed the back edge with mortar.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a sill bedded on silicone is a sill held on something that moves when the weather does, and that about four sills in that building were bedded that way, and that one of them has a frame that has had to be packed and unpacked about twice in about nine years.**

---

**A gully grate at the end of a lane had been bedded on a mortar plug that had cracked through and let the frame sit about nine millimetres low.**

He lifted the grate, dug the plug out, rebased the frame onto a fresh bed, and refitted the grate to the fall.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a gully bedded on a plug is a gully with no fall behind it, and that about four gullies in that lane are bedded that way, and that one of them has a frame that has settled about half an inch and takes the whole load as a rattle on every lorry.**

**That Thursday came to ninety-five pounds, exact, and it is the four of them together and not the first of them.**

**Since half past six a woman of about thirty has been sitting at her chair in the fourth of those rooms with the door shut behind her, and the eighth of eight has been on that back shelf for three hundred and thirty-nine days.**
