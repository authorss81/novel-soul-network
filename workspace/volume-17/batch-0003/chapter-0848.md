# Chapter 848 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Building In A Second District That Receives The Returns, Where A Landlord Holds Paper And Not People And Somebody Asked Him In About Nine Seconds Whether A Document That Describes Four Hundred People Can Be Held On File By A Landlord, And He Said Yes And Then Said The Thing That Made The Room, Which Is That This Is The Correct Arrangement And It Is Also The Reason Nobody Can Be Found

**A Wednesday went to a building in a second district that receives the returns, where about nine people stood in a corridor with a document and a landlord who is a landlord of nothing else said the thing about a piece of paper that nobody in that corridor told him he was not allowed to say.**

---

Nine names and nine dates were written up for that Wednesday, the ninth date taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten.

**Line eighteen is one thousand two hundred and seventy-four days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against the north wall is one thousand one hundred and sixty-two days, a hundred and sixty-six weeks to the day. What is still owed is one thousand two hundred and forty-six days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks to the day.**

---

**And the document is a single sheet and it is about the size of a folded sheet of writing paper and it has a box at the bottom of it with one word in the box.**

The word in the box is *as it has been*, and it has been on the bottom of that form since before the spring, and about four of these have come back on a bus in about nine days carried by the person who chose to carry them.

**Nobody in that corridor could say who is supposed to hold them and nobody in that corridor has been told, and about four people in that corridor have said since that they have asked twice.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that she has brought one and that she has been told twice to bring it to that building and that the man at the counter there took her name and the date and nothing else.

---

**And a man of about forty-four who is a landlord of that building and not of anything else asked the question, and he asked it himself, and it took him about nine seconds to ask it and about four to answer it.**

He said that a landlord holds paper and not people, and that a document that describes four hundred people can be held on file by a landlord, and that he has four such things on a shelf in a room at the back and has had them for about nine days.

**Nobody in that corridor said that was the wrong answer and nobody in that corridor has said since.**

He said that he is a landlord and that his job is a building and a rent and a set of keys, and that nobody asked him whether he understood what is on the paper and that he has read it twice because he had nothing else to do on a Wednesday.

---

**And then he said the thing that made that corridor, and he said it in about nine seconds and he said it to the wall.**

He said that this is the correct arrangement, and that a landlord holding paper and not people is the correct arrangement and has always been the correct arrangement, and that the reason nobody can be found is the same reason the paper is safe.

He said that a piece of paper about four hundred people cannot be asked anything, and that as long as the paper is with a landlord nobody has to be told anything, and that this city has been doing this correctly for about nine years and that is not a thing anybody decided.

**Nobody in that corridor told him he was not allowed to say that and about four people in that corridor said afterwards that they had wanted to.**

A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that that is the answer to the question nobody has asked, and that the question is how somebody over a border gets told what this city holds on their behalf.

**Nobody in that corridor answered him and nobody in that corridor has answered him since.**

---

**And about four people in that corridor had brought something and about four had not, and neither group said so.**

A man of about thirty-four said he had brought a note about a vehicle and that the note had nothing on it about any of this and that he had brought it because he was in the building.

A woman of about thirty-nine said in about nine seconds that she had come to ask whether the shelf at the back was where a document like that went, and that she had asked in two corridors and got told no twice and yes once.

**Nobody in that corridor told her which of the three answers to believe and she did not ask again.**

The landlord said that the shelf at the back is where the four of them are and that she can look at them if she wants to and that he would go back to his rent.

**Nobody in that corridor asked him whether he minded and nobody in that corridor has asked him since, and about two people in that corridor have said since that they would have liked to and did not.**

---

**And the rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district.**

**A curtain rail in a shop had been fixed into a lintel that had been cut when the opening was made wider.**

He took the rail off, filled the old holes, fitted a length of back-box across the cut, and hung the rail off that.

**Six pounds. He told the man that a rail into a cut lintel is a rail into about four inches of nothing and then into whatever is behind it, and that about four rails in that shop are on cut lintels, and that one of them carries a curtain that has been taken down and put back up four times because it will not stay.**

---

**A door closer on the back of that shop had an arm that had been ground so that the swing stopped about four inches before the catch.**

He came off the arm, put a fresh one on the same body and plate, and set the speed so the latch lifts cleanly.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that an arm ground back is a door that has been made to fail quietly, and that about four closers in that yard have had their arms altered, and that one of them has a plate that has been drilled about nine millimetres over from where it was and does not close square.**

---

**A spring pocket in that shop's front door had been packed with a tile spacer by a fitter and the door had been hung on it.**

He pulled the spring out, dug the spacer out, bedded the spring on its own grease in a pocket taken out to size, and refitted the door.

**Seven pounds. He told the man that a spring pocket packed out with a spacer is a spring sitting on something that was chosen for thickness and not for load, and that about four doors on that street have been hung that way, and that one of them has a door that has dropped onto its frame about half an inch every time it has been let go.**

---

**A length of guttering on a parapet in that district had its outlets taken into the wall face instead of out over the path, so that everything it caught went down the brickwork.**

He took the outlets out, cut new ones through the coping, and rebedded the hoppers on that.

**Four pounds. He told the man that an outlet taken into a wall is a drain that empties a roof into a wall, and that about four lengths of guttering on those parapets are taken in that way, and that one of them has nine feet of staining down the face and has been looked at as damp by about four people.**

**That Wednesday came to twenty-six pounds, exact, which is the four of them added together.**

**From half past six there has been a woman of about thirty at her chair in the fourth of those four rooms, her door shut behind her, and on the back shelf the eighth of eight has lain for three hundred and forty-five days.**

**Not one person in this city has asked her a question about it and not one is going to, and this page does not say what is written there.**
