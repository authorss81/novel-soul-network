# Chapter 847 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Corridor And A Stair In A Second District Where Two People Who Have A Key To A Room Of Their Own Stood About Four Feet Apart For About Nine Minutes, And She Asked Him What Happens To A Person Who Cannot Sign Anything About Themselves, And He Gave Her An Answer That Was About The Question And Not About Either Of Them, And Neither Of Them Said Another Word

**The shape of that Tuesday was nine minutes of a stair in a second district and about two hours of repair work, and the nine minutes were one question asked, one answer given, and then nothing said by either of them for about nine minutes.**

---

Thirteen names went onto that Tuesday's book with a date against each of them, the thirteenth taken at about twenty to five. At about ten the shutter came down.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand five hundred and fifty-five days, two hundred and twenty-two weeks and one day, and the card on end in the rail beside that door is one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine, two hundred and twenty-two weeks and five days, and the rail takes two.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand three hundred and twenty-seven days, a hundred and eighty-nine weeks and four days. The post at that corridor end is one thousand one hundred and three, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and four days. The separation is nine hundred and thirty-five days, a hundred and thirty-three weeks and four days.**

---

**And the question was asked at about four minutes past seven on that stair and it took her about nine seconds and him about nine seconds and neither of them took longer than that.**

She asked what happens to a person who cannot sign anything about themselves.

**She did not say who she meant and he did not ask, and about nine other people went past the door at the end of that corridor while the two of them were on it.**

He said that a person who cannot sign anything about themselves is the only person in this city who cannot be put on a form with a name in the box, and that this is not a protection anybody gave them and that they cannot get a form that will take their name whatever they do.

He said that about four thousand of these forms went out in the spring and that a person who cannot sign is not on any of them and never has been, and that the four words on the line above the box are the reason, and that a person who cannot sign is the exact shape of the hole the four words make.

**He said it to the stair and not to her, and he said it in about nine seconds and then he said nothing else about it for about nine minutes.**

---

**And then neither of them said another word, and about nine minutes of that stair went past with the two of them on it.**

She has said since that she asked a question and got an answer about forms and that she had asked about a person, and that she has worked out since that she got the better answer and has not decided whether to say so.

He has said since that the answer was true and that a man who cannot hold a release steady for about two hours in eleven years is the person least entitled to be asked what happens to a person who cannot sign, and that he was not asked.

**He said that this is the third time she has declined to ask him that and that he still has not worked out whether that is a courtesy or a decision.**

She said that it is a decision and that she made it a long time before any of these three.

---

**And there was a sentence he had ready and did not say, and the file has it because the not-saying took about four seconds and both of them were standing still.**

The sentence was about a man in another city a long time ago who made himself unable to sign anything about himself on purpose, on a day, in about four seconds, and who was not asked then either.

He had it ready and he did not say it because she had not asked about a man, and because the answer he had given her was about the shape of a hole in a form and did not need anything added to it.

**Nobody on that stair asked him what the sentence was and he did not say it, and about two people who went past the door at the end of that corridor have said since that they thought one of the two of them was about to say something and neither did.**

---

**And the rest of that Tuesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district.**

**A pair of floor springs at a shop entrance had both been set so that the second one fought the first and the door arrived on the frame at a slant.**

He came off both bodies, reset them to their own grease with about a quarter inch of lift on the second, and set the first to close before it.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that two springs fighting each other is a door that closes on whatever it lands on, and that about four entrances on that street are sprung in pairs set by two different men, and that one of them has a strike plate that has been packed out about four millimetres to make the door shut at all.**

---

**A door in the same building had a floor spring that had been dropped onto a tile after the floor had been laid over it, and the leaf was riding about nine millimetres high.**

He lifted the door, took the spring out, bedded it on the screed below, and refitted the door to the spring.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a spring laid on top of a floor is a spring whose top face is a tile edge, and that about four doors in that building are hung on springs over finished floors, and that one of them has a leaf that has been taken off and packed down twice to close a gap nobody could find.**

---

**A length of guttering on the same building had a joint bedded on cement instead of on the seal it was designed to be bedded on, and had been weeping about four inches behind the outlet for about four winters.**

He took the length down, cut the cement out, rebedded it on its own seal, and refitted the brackets onto the rafter ends.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a gutter joint bedded on cement is a joint with no movement in it, and that about four lengths of guttering on that building are bedded that way, and that one of them has a fascia behind it about nine millimetres soft where the water has been going for four winters.**

---

**A gate latch at the back of that building had been packed with grease by a man who could not work it, and had worked loose from the staple.**

He cleaned the staple, packed the eye properly, and put the gate back on its jamb.

**Four pounds. He told the man that a staple packed with grease is a staple that has been given a coat instead of a bearing, and that about four gates on that yard have been packed at the staple, and that one of them has worked loose twice in about four years and has been propped on a wedge both times.**

**That Tuesday came to seventy-five pounds, exact, and it is those four added together and not one of them.**

**At about nine metres along that corridor a woman of about thirty was at her chair in the fourth of those four rooms with the door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and forty-four days.**

**Nobody has asked her one question about it and nobody is going to, and this page gives nothing of what is on it.**
