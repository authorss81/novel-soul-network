# Chapter 846 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In The Lower Wards Where A Service Group Meets And The Three Places Were Read Out Together For The First Time In One Room, And About Four People Worked Out That All Three Were Written About Nine Years Ago By Three People Who Had No Idea They Were Agreeing, And Nobody In That Room Was Told And Nobody In That Room Would Have Been Able To Do Anything About It, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Was In That Room And Did Not Say Anything About The Word

**About nine hours went to reading three books out loud in a room off a road in the Lower Wards, and about four people in that room worked out while it was being read that all three had been written about nine years ago by three people doing something else.**

---

That Monday's sheet took nine names and nine dates, the last of the dates at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand five hundred and fifty-four days, two hundred and twenty-two weeks to the day, and the card on end in the rail beside that door is one thousand five hundred and fifty-eight, two hundred and twenty-two weeks and four days, and the rail takes two.**

**Line sixteen is one thousand three hundred and forty-four days, a hundred and ninety-two weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand one hundred and twenty, a hundred and sixty weeks to the day, one corner torn, eight unfinished, and not two compared.**

---

**And the three were read out in about nine minutes and they were read out by three different people and the fourth words were never said.**

The wages book was read by the man of about fifty-two, who had stood at that canteen table and read it before. He read the four lines he had read before and said the four words twice each and did not say them to anybody in particular.

The day book at the works gate was read by a man of about thirty-four who has worked at that gate for about nine years and who said he had not looked at the margin since he wrote it.

**Nobody in that room said that he had written it and he said so himself, in about four seconds, and nobody made anything of it.**

The laundry book was read by a woman of about thirty-four who had been in the shop in the first district on the Tuesday and who had asked the woman who keeps it for the book and been refused in about four seconds and had been given it on the Saturday.

She read nine lines and gave the numbers against each of the four words out loud and they were between about eleven and about thirty and she said so in about four seconds.

---

**And about four people in that room worked out the same thing at the same moment and about four of them did not.**

The three of them are about nine years old. That is not a coincidence anybody chose and about four people in that room put their hands on the table at about the same moment.

The man who wrote it in a margin was asked how long the thing takes and wrote down the answer rather than say it twice. The man who wrote it in a wages book was counting what a man was paid and wrote it in the column he had. The woman who wrote it in a laundry book was counting what came in on a Thursday.

**Each of the three was doing something else. Not one of the three was writing a record of anything and not one of the three knew the other two existed.**

A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that this is what a thing looks like when about four thousand people do it every day and nobody is paid to write it down, and that the only places it got written down are places where somebody was already writing.

**Nobody in that room said how many people do it and nobody in that room has said since, and about two people in that room have worked out what the number is and have not said it.**

---

**And about four people in that room noticed the woman of about thirty-four had said nothing about the word and about four of them noticed that she had been in the shop where the third of the three is kept.**

She was in that room for about an hour and she did not say the word once. She listened to all three and she asked about the numbers and she asked whether the laundry book for this year is on the shelf behind the counter, and nobody in that room asked her why she has not said anything.

**Nobody in that room asked her and nobody in that room has asked her since, and about four people in that room have said afterwards that they wanted to and that she looked like somebody who had already decided.**

A man of about fifty-two said to her, in about nine seconds, that she is the only person in this room who has been over a border and that he is not going to ask her anything, and that he wanted her to know that he was not going to.

She said, in about four seconds, that she had not expected to be asked and that she was not.

---

**And nobody in that room could have done anything about it, and about four people in that room worked that out while the reading was going on.**

There is no procedure. There is no form that a person in this city fills in to say that a thing was written down by accident three times and should be written down on purpose. **The card that exists says what this city will hold for about nine weeks and that it will not ask them anything, and it is not a form about this.**

A woman of about thirty-nine said in about nine seconds that the finding is that there is a form for holding a thing and no form for finding out that there is a thing to hold, and that this city built the first one.

**Nobody in that room agreed with her and nobody in that room disagreed with her.**

---

**And the rest of that Monday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district.**

**A handrail bracket on a stair in the same building had a coach screw that had split the render behind it and was working out of the wall about nine millimetres.**

He took the rail off, filled the hole, moved the bracket in by two courses, and fixed it back.

**Eleven pounds. He told the man that a screw that has split its render has been working in a hole about twice the size it needs, and that about four brackets on that stair are fixed that way, and that one of them has a rail end that stands about an inch away from the wall where it meets the return.**

---

**A flight of stairs in the same building had its top nosing replaced in a section that did not match the other eleven.**

He took the wrong nosing up, made a new one to the section of the eleven, and relaid it.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a nosing in the wrong section is a lip that catches a toe on the way down and a heel on the way up, and that four flights in that building have had one tread replaced at a time over about nine years, and that one of them now has three nosings in three sections and nobody has noticed for about four of those years.**

---

**A sash window in the same building had its meeting rail planed out so that the sashes pass each other instead of closing on a rebate.**

He fitted a new meeting rail to the section of the frame, packed it, and let the sashes back onto it.

**Eleven pounds. He told the man that a meeting rail planed away is a window that cannot shut in two places at once, and that about four sashes in that building have been altered that way, and that one of them has a gap at the corner that has been packed with a strip of card about twice.**

---

**A handrail on the same floor had been painted the whole length in one colour over two, so that the part a hand goes on could not be told from the part that is only a rail.**

He rubbed the rail back along its whole length, took the built paint off the underside with a strip, and left the rest of it alone.

**Seven pounds. He told the man that a rail painted in one colour over two is a rail that has been decided to be one surface, and that about four rails on that floor have been painted over, and that one of them has about four feet of the old colour still showing under the new at the bottom of the post.**

**That Monday came to fifty-five pounds, exact, and that is the four of them together.**

**There was a woman of about thirty at her chair in the fourth of those rooms from half past six with the door shut, and behind her on the shelf the eighth of eight stood at three hundred and forty-three days.**

**No question about that page has ever been put to her and none is going to be, and what is written there does not appear on this page.**
