# Chapter 833 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where The Room Was Open And About Nine People Came Up The Stair And About Four Of Them Had Come About The Name, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Said In About Nine Seconds That She Has Never Used It And Will Keep Using The Town Name, And Nobody In That Room Argued With Her

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where the room was open from half past six and about nine people came up the stair and about four had come about a name. No number was said in that room, and about four people in it had wanted to argue with a woman and did not.**

---

Nine names went onto that Wednesday's sheet and nine dates went with them, the last of the nine taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Line eighteen is one thousand two hundred and fifty-three days, a hundred and seventy-nine weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand one hundred and forty-one days, a hundred and sixty-three weeks to the day. What is still owed is one thousand two hundred and twenty-five, a hundred and seventy-five weeks to the day.**

---

**And the four people who came about the name had all of them come from the same nine men in a room over a shop, and not one of them had come about anything else.**

A man of about thirty-eight, who is not one of the nine, said in about nine seconds that the name had been on four clipboards by the Friday and that by the Wednesday it was on nine, and that nobody had put it anywhere except those clipboards and mouths.

Nobody in that room asked him to say where the mouths were.

A woman of about twenty-six said that she had heard it at a bus stop on a Monday from a man she did not know who was talking to another man, and that neither of the two men knew where it came from, and that she had checked, because she had stood there for four minutes.

**Nobody in that room thanked her for checking and about two people in it have said since that they would have liked to and did not say it to her.**

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four said the thing that the rest of that hour was about, and she said it in about nine seconds and she said it before she had sat down.**

She said that she has never used the new name once, that she has not used it this week and did not use it on the Monday, and that she is not going to start.

She said that the man at the bus stop used the town name, and that when she asked him three questions on the bus he gave her two answers and neither of them had the new one in it.

**She said that nobody over there has ever used the new name at all, in about nine years, and that she is not being difficult and that she knows how that sounds.**

A man of about fifty-two said in four seconds that he knew exactly how it sounded and that he was not going to tell her.

She said she had not asked him to.

**Nobody in that room argued with her. About four people in that room said afterwards that they had wanted to, and that they had wanted to for about nine minutes, and that not one of them could say what they would have said.**

---

**A man of about thirty-four who works on the second floor of that building asked her one question, and it was not a question about the name.**

He asked whether the man at the bus stop had said the new name at any point, on the bus or before she got on it or after she got off it.

She said no, in about four seconds, and said that she had gone back and looked at the timetable on the wall where he had handed it to her and that the new name was not on it.

He said that the timetable was about nine years old.

**She said she knew how old it was and that a timetable that old is the only one anybody has, and that a person who wanted to be asked about what a place is called would be asking about a wall.**

Nobody in that room said anything about the wall.

---

**And a man of about forty-four, who asked to be recorded as having said the following, said that both of the two names are in use in this city and that the region has more than one name in this city and that a region having a name in a city is not the same as a region having a name.**

He said it in about nine seconds and then said that he was not going to be able to say anything else about it, and that this was not modesty.

**Nobody in that room asked him why and about four people in that room have said since that they have worked out why, and that it is that answering the second half would need somebody to have been there.**

A man of about fifty-two said in four seconds that the man of about forty-four had said the whole of the thing and had said it twice.

The man of about forty-four said that he had, and that he had done it on purpose, and that he did not have a better sentence.

---

**And about half an hour of that Wednesday evening was the corridor outside that room, where four people stood and did not go in either, and one of the four was a man of twenty-two.**

He has said since that he stood there for about half an hour and worked out that four people in that room had wanted to argue with a woman who had the better of it, and that wanting to argue with somebody who has the better of it is the most ordinary thing in this city and takes about four seconds to start.

**Nobody in that corridor said a number and nobody in it said why not.**

He also said, in about nine seconds, to nobody, that the man of about forty-four had done the thing he would have done if he had had four years and a room.

He said that he would not have done it in about four seconds and that this was the whole difference between them and that it was about four years long.

---

**And the rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district, where a set of about nine feet of handrail on a landing had been painted where a hand went and nowhere else.**

He rubbed the paint back off the top of the rail with a strip and left the rest of it, and the landing was brighter under the paint than it was on it.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a rail painted only where a hand goes is a rail somebody has decided is not a surface, and that four rails on that stair have been painted in patches, and that one of them has a patch about nine inches long in the middle of nowhere.**

---

**And a shopfront shutter in the same street had been re-hung with the barrels on the wrong side of the stiles, so that it came down and stopped about four inches above the sill.**

He took it off, reversed the barrel on it, filled the old holes, and hung it back so that it closed on its own weight.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a shutter that stops four inches above its own sill is a shutter that has hung on the wrong side since the day it went up, and that four shutters in that street are hung that way, and that one of them has a bottom rail that has dropped about nine millimetres out of square.**

---

**And a rainwater pipe on the same building had been run behind a downpipe, so that two pipes were inside the same hole in the wall.**

He took it out, cut a fresh hole about four inches clear, fitted a slotted bracket so that the back could not sit against the masonry, and refitted it.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that two pipes in one hole are two pipes that cannot be replaced without breaking the wall, and that four runs in that street are doubled, and that one of them has about four feet of pipe inside another one.**

---

**And a length of fascia on the same building had been fixed with screws into the face of a rafter instead of into the end of one.**

He filled the eight holes, cut four lengths to the depth of a rafter tail, let them into the plumb of the rafters, and refitted the fascia through those into timber that carries something.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a screw in the face of a rafter is a screw in about nine millimetres of board and a bag of plaster, and that four fascias in that street are fixed that way, and that one of them has pulled about half an inch away at one end.**

---

**Since half past six a woman of about thirty has been at her chair in the fourth of those four rooms with her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and twenty-four days. Nobody has put one question to her about it and nobody is going to.**
