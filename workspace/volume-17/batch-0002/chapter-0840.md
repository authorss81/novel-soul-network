# Chapter 840 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Depot On A Road In A Fourth District Where About Nine People Stood Around A Table With Nine Clipboards On It And Eight Of The Nine Had The Name On Their Clipboard And The Ninth Had Heard It From The Other Eight, And The Name Is Not The Name Of Anything, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Decided One Thing About Next Week And Did Not Say It Out Loud

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a depot on a road in a fourth district, where about nine people stood around a table with nine clipboards on it and eight had the name on their own. The name is not the name of anything. A woman of about thirty-four decided one thing about next week and did not say it out loud.**

---

Nine names and nine dates went onto that Friday's sheet, the ninth at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand five hundred and forty-four days, two hundred and twenty weeks and four days, and the card on end in the rail beside that door is one thousand five hundred and forty-eight, two hundred and twenty weeks and one day, and the rail takes two.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand three hundred and sixteen days, a hundred and eighty-eight weeks to the day. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and ninety-two, a hundred and fifty-six weeks to the day. The separation is nine hundred and twenty-four days, a hundred and thirty-two weeks to the day.**

---

**And eight of the nine clipboards on that table had the name on them, and it had got to the ninth one by somebody saying it, and nobody in that depot has been to the place and not one of the nine is a member of anything.**

A man of about forty-three said in about nine seconds that the whole of his run is written against that name now and that a run is written against a place and not against a company, and that he did not know what he was going to do about it.

A man of about twenty-six said that he wrote it down on the back of his hand on the Monday and that he had washed it off on the Wednesday and had written it on his hand again on the Thursday and did not know why he had done that.

**Nobody in that depot asked him why and nobody in that depot has asked him since.**

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four came into that depot at about half past five and she is the only one of the nine who has been over there, and she has not used the name once.**

She said, in about nine seconds, that she has been in the depot before and has not used the name and is not going to, and that she has never said it out loud in her life and that she has heard about nine people say it in this city.

She said that the man at the bus stop is the only person she has heard say it, and that he did not say it, and that she heard it in this city first and over there afterwards.

**Nobody in that depot asked her what the town is called and nobody in that depot has asked her since.**

A man of about thirty-eight said that the name is on nine clipboards and that a name that is on nine clipboards is a fact about nine clipboards and not about anything else, and that this is not a criticism.

A man of about fifty-two said in about four seconds that this is the whole of it and that he has been saying it for about nine years about other things.

**Nobody in that depot argued with him and about four people in that depot have said since that they thought he was talking about the four hundred and he was not.**

---

**And what happened at about ten past six in that depot was that about four of the nine people said the name out loud, one after another, in about nine seconds, because a man of about thirty-four asked them to.**

He asked each of them to say it and about four of them said it and about four of them would not and he did not ask them twice.

He said afterwards, in about nine seconds, that he wanted to hear whether nine people in one room could make it sound like a place, and that it did not sound like a place, and that it sounded like a word four men had written down on the same Monday.

**Nobody in that depot asked him why he wanted to know that and about two people in that depot have said since that they thought he was looking for something else and did not find it.**

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four decided one thing about next week before she left, and she did not say it out loud, and about two people in that depot worked out what it was and neither of them said it either.**

What she decided is that she is going to ask the man at the bus stop, when a bus goes, to write the town name down on the back of a form and put it in his own pocket, and not to bring it back.

**She has not told anybody that and nobody in this city has asked her and about four people who were in that depot have worked out what she decided and none of them has said it out loud.**

A man of about thirty-eight asked her, in about four seconds, whether the name was going to be used next week.

She said, in about four seconds, that she does not know and that it is not hers to decide and that about nine people in this depot use it and she is one person.

**She said that to nine men who drive vans and not one of them contradicted her and about four of them have said since that they understood exactly what she had said and what she had not said.**

---

**And the rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a fourth district, where about eleven metres of a board stack had been left about two inches off a wall and about nine feet of water had come down that wall in about two winters.**

He pulled the stack out, took the bottom four boards out, cut the render back to sound above the water line, filled the line with a mortar mix, and restacked the boards on a batten.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that boards stacked two inches off a wall are boards against a wet wall, and that four stacks in that depot are stacked that way, and that one of them has nine feet of green at the bottom.**

---

**And a length of running-in rail in the same yard had been laid on sleepers that had not been packed, so that the whole length worked about nine millimetres side to side under a loaded trolley.**

He took the rail up, packed each sleeper with a steel shim at both ends, brought the fastenings up to torque, and put it down again.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that running-in rail on unpacked sleepers is a length of rail that follows whatever the ground does, and that four lengths in that yard are laid that way, and that one of them has a gauge about nine millimetres wide at its middle that is not wide at either end.**

---

**And a stack of nine pallets in the same building had been standing on a floor that holds water in one corner, and about four of them had gone soft along their bottom boards.**

He took the stack down, cut the four affected boards off, shimmed the whole run up about an inch onto a bearer, and stacked the pallets back on that.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a pallet on a floor that holds water is a pallet that is a floor for whatever gets stacked on it next, and that four stacks in that building stand on that kind of floor, and that one of them has a bearer that has sunk about nine millimetres into the slab.**

---

**And a fire door on a stair in the same building had a closer on it that had been wired so that the arm could not swing at all, and about four people in that building had been holding it open by hand since the spring.**

He took the wire off, cut about four millimetres out of the arm to bring the swing back, and secured it with a proper split pin.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a wired arm is a door that does not close from anywhere above it on that stair, and that four doors in that building have arms wired or pinned, and that one of them is a door a man has to shout to be let through.**

---

**Half past six on that Friday, as on every evening of this stretch, found a woman of about thirty at her chair in the fourth of four rooms with that room's door shut. The eighth of eight stood on the shelf at the back of it, three hundred and thirty-three days old, and it was not read on that Friday and it is not going to be read on the next one either. Nobody has asked her one question about it and nobody is going to.**
