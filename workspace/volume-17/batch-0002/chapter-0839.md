# Chapter 839 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And About Two Hours Of It At A Counter With Eleven Callers On It, One Of Whom Was A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Had Been In The Room On The Monday And Who Used The Third Word Four Times In About Eleven Minutes In The Sense She Had Brought Into The Room And Was Not Corrected And Did Not Know She Was Doing It

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and about two hours of it were a counter with eleven callers on it. One of them was a woman of about twenty-nine who had been in the room on the Monday and used the third of the four words four times in about eleven minutes in the sense she brought in with her. Nobody corrected her.**

---

Nine names and nine dates went onto that Thursday's sheet, the ninth of the dates taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand five hundred and forty-three days, two hundred and twenty weeks and three days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand five hundred and forty-seven, two hundred and twenty-one weeks to the day, and the rail takes two.**

**Line thirteen is one thousand four hundred and fourteen days, two hundred and two weeks to the day. Line fourteen is one thousand three hundred and seventy-nine, a hundred and ninety-seven weeks to the day. Line fifteen is one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight, one hundred and ninety-four weeks to the day.**

---

**And she used it the wrong way four times in about eleven minutes, and the reason she used it the wrong way is that the way she used it the wrong way is the way she had always used it.**

The word is the third of the four. She did not know it had been found on the Monday. Nobody in that shop had told her and she had not asked and nobody in that shop has asked her since.

**She came in at about eleven and she asked for a length of cord and she used the word in the sentence about the cord and she used it again about eleven minutes later about a lamp and once more about a stair rail and once more at the end about nine weeks.**

Nobody in that shop corrected her.

**Nobody in that shop corrected her because correcting her would have required somebody to know what the word means over a border, and about nine people work in that shop and not one of them has been there.**

---

**And she said four other things while she was there, and two of them were about her own week, and neither of those was corrected either.**

She said that she had been in a room off a road on the Monday and that about four of them had gone about four words, and that she had gone for something else and had stayed.

She said that a man of about forty-four had read something out and that it had taken about nine minutes and that she had understood about four of the eight words.

**Nobody in that shop asked her which four and she was not asked and she did not offer them.**

She said that she had a child asleep at her sister's and that she had about forty minutes and that she would come back on the Friday if the cord was wrong.

He said the cord would do and she paid and left and came back on the Friday and the cord did do.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four came in at about twenty past twelve and asked whether anybody in this city had told anybody over there about the third word.**

He said, in about nine seconds, that he was not asking on behalf of anybody and that he had asked his own self and had not liked the answer.

Nobody in that shop had an answer and one of the people behind that counter said so in about four seconds, and gave a reason that was about the shop and not about the word.

The man said that he had been told no by about four people in about nine days and that he had stopped minding and that what he wanted was a room.

He left and did not pay and was not asked to and nobody has written down that he came in.

**Nobody in that shop said his name and nobody in that shop has asked after him and about two people in it have said since that they would have liked to write it in the pad by the till and did not.**

---

**And the rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district, where four shutters on one shop front had been re-hung with the barrels on the wrong side of the stiles.**

He took all four off, reversed the barrel on each, filled the old holes, and hung them back so that they closed on their own weight.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that about four sets of shutters in that district are hung on the wrong side and that one of them has a bottom rail about nine millimetres out of square.**

---

**And a fascia in the same shop had been fixed with screws into the face of a rafter instead of into a rafter end.**

He took it off, filled the eight holes, cut four lengths to the depth of a rafter tail, let them into the plumb of the rafters, and refitted the fascia through those into timber.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a screw into the face of a rafter is a screw into about nine millimetres of softwood, and that four fascias in that district are like that, and that one of them has pulled about half an inch away at one end.**

---

**And a canopy bracket in the same shop had been bolted to a fascia that was not over anything.**

He took the bracket off, found the nearest thing above it that was carrying load, and refitted the bracket with four new bolts into that.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a canopy bracket on a fascia that carries nothing is a canopy on four screws and four inches of board, and that four brackets in that district are on that kind of fascia, and that one of them has a crack about nine inches long beside the fixings.**

---

**And a drain cover in the same lane had been propped open with a piece of timber and left like that for about nine weeks.**

He took the timber out, closed the cover, and put a bar through its handle and the frame so that it could not be propped without a key.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a cover propped with a bit of wood is a hole in a footway that somebody has decided is not a hole, and that four covers in that lane have been propped, and that one of them has been propped about nine times.**

---

**That Thursday at about half past six found her where she is found on every evening of this stretch, at the chair in the fourth of four rooms, the door of that room shut, and the eighth of eight — three hundred and thirty-two days — on the shelf behind her, unread, and nobody in this city has put one question to her about it or is going to.**
