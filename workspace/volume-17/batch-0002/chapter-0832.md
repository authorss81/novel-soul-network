# Chapter 832 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, About Two Hours Of It In A First District At The Front Of A Shop Where Nine Doors Were Hung On Three Hinges Each, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Eight Who Came In And Did Not Ask Him Anything About The Form And Was Told About Three Other Things Anyway

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and about two hours of it were the front of a shop in a first district where nine doors had been hung on three hinges each. A woman of about thirty-eight came in at about half past three and asked him about a closer, and he did the work and told her about three other things she had not asked about.**

---

The four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand five hundred and thirty-four days, two hundred and nineteen weeks and one day. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand five hundred and thirty-eight, two hundred and nineteen weeks and five days, and the rail takes two.

**The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand one hundred days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and one day, one corner torn, eight unfinished, and not two compared on this day or any day of this stretch. The separation is nine hundred and fourteen days, a hundred and thirty weeks and four days.**

---

**And nine doors on the front of that shop were all hung the same way, which is on three hinges, and he had been asked to look at one of them and had said in about nine seconds that all nine of them were the same and that he was going to do all nine because doing one on its own would have been a lie told to a man with a shop.**

The man who owned the shop said that he had asked about one door.

He said he had not been asked about eight doors.

**Nobody thanked him for the other eight and nobody has thanked him since, and the man who owns that shop has said that he found out about them in about four minutes and did not put up any argument about it.**

He charged twenty-six pounds for the nine doors and it was nine pounds for the hinges and about seventeen for the labour, and he told him that.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-eight came in at about half past three with a closer in her hand that she had been told about by somebody else, and she did not ask Marek anything about the form and she has said that she has not thought about the form since the Wednesday.**

He took the closer off the door it came from, which was not hers, and looked at it.

The pin had been bent so that the arm could not swing more than about an inch, and somebody had put a washer over the spindle to hold it that way, and the washer was the wrong size and had been cut with a hacksaw.

**He said, in about nine seconds, that whoever had done that had wanted the door to stay open and had not wanted to ask the man who owns the building.**

He said he was not going to say that about the man who owns the building, because he did not know that, and he said the washer was the wrong size and that cutting a washer with a hacksaw is what a person does on a Sunday.

---

**And he did the work and then he told her about three other things, and this is the part of that Tuesday that the man of twenty-two would not have done and did.**

The first was a hinge on the same door that was not the same as the other two because it was a different maker and about a sixteenth of an inch thinner.

The second was a threshold at that shop's back door that had been painted shut and that he said had been painted shut by somebody who had decided it was not a threshold.

The third was a length of gutter on the front that was discharging into the same channel as the shop next door, which meant that in about nine years one of the two of them would be blamed for the other's water.

**She said she had not asked him about any of the three and he said he knew and told her the second one anyway, in about nine seconds, with the reason.**

The reason was that a painted threshold is a threshold somebody has decided is not a surface, and a person who finds it in about nine years will not know why, and he said he will not be here in nine years and the man will.

She said that she had come about a closer and that she had got a closer and about four other things, and that she was not complaining, and that she had not asked.

**He said he knew that too, and did not stop, and has said since that he did not stop because she had not said anything that made him stop, and that she was not the first person to have said that to him and that he has thought about it for about four days.**

---

**And a man of about forty-three came in at about four and asked him to have a look at a gate, and the gate had been shut by him on a Monday about nine weeks ago and he wanted it looked at, and Marek said in four seconds that it was a gate and he had shut it and it was holding.**

The man said he had not slept well.

Marek said that about nine gates in that district were holding after being shut badly and that one of them was holding because the post had been set in by a builder who put a stone in the hole and packed around it, and that this one was not that one.

**Nobody in that shop asked which of the nine he meant and the man did not ask and has not been back since and nobody has written down that he came in.**

---

**And the rest of that Tuesday was a counter until about six with about eleven callers on it and about nine of them talked about the weather and about two of them did not.**

A woman of about twenty-nine brought back a kettle she had bought from that shop in the winter before last and wanted to know why it had a mark on the bottom.

**He said the mark was a mould line and that a kettle without a mould line was not a kettle anybody wanted, and that he had told her so when she bought it, and that she had not believed him, and that he was not going to be sorry about it.**

She said she had not asked him to be sorry either and she left with the kettle, paid nothing, and was not asked to.

A man of about fifty-six wanted a length of cord and asked whether a length would do and Marek said that for what he was doing a length would not do and told him what would, and the man took what would.

---

**Since half past six the fourth of those four rooms has held a woman of about thirty at her chair, her own door shut behind her. On the shelf at the back of that room the eighth of eight has now lain three hundred and twenty-three days. It has not been read, it will not be read this week, no one has put a question to her about it, and no one is going to.**

---
