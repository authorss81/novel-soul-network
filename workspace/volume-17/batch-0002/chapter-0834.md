# Chapter 834 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Over A Shop In A Second District Where Nine People Were Standing Up Because There Were Not Enough Chairs, And A Man Of About Thirty-Four Read Two Lines Off A Card Aloud So That A Man Of About Twenty-Eight Could Hear Them, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Nine Wrote One Word On The Paper Underneath Them And Did Not Say What It Was

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a room over a shop in a second district where about nine people were standing up for want of chairs. A man of about thirty-four read two lines off a card out loud twice, and a woman of about thirty-nine wrote one word on the paper under it and said nothing.**

---

That Thursday's sheet carried nine names and nine dates, the last of the nine taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**The card in the rail by that door is one thousand five hundred and forty days, two hundred and twenty weeks to the day, and it is four days ahead of the four rooms, which stand at one thousand five hundred and thirty-six. The rail takes two.**

**Line twelve is one thousand four hundred and fifty-six days, two hundred and eight weeks to the day. Line thirteen is one thousand four hundred and seven, two hundred and one weeks to the day. Line fourteen is one thousand three hundred and seventy-two, a hundred and ninety-six weeks to the day.**

---

**And the two lines on the card are these.**

*For about nine weeks this city will hold a version of what four hundred people do over there, and will say plainly to anybody who asks what it is holding and what it is not holding.*
*It will not ask them anything.*

**They are about nine weeks and about a version and they are not about anybody's mind and they do not say what anybody ought to think, and about four people in that room read them as a promise and about four read them as a refusal, and both of those four are misreading it and neither of them has been told.**

A man of about fifty-two said in about nine seconds that the second line is the one that matters and that the first line is the one anybody will repeat.

Nobody in that room asked him why and he did not say.

---

**And the man of about thirty-four read them out twice, once from the card and once from memory, and he did it because the man of about twenty-eight could not hear from the back of that room.**

He said that he had been asked to make sure everybody in the room heard it and that he was not going to shout it.

The man of about twenty-eight said, in about four seconds, that he had heard it and that it was the first thing anybody had read aloud to him in about nine years that had said what a thing would not do.

**Nobody in that room asked him what he had been expecting instead and about two people in that room have said since that they would have liked to and did not ask.**

A woman of about twenty-six said that about four people had come on the Wednesday because of the name and that none of them had come for the card, and that this was the first week in about nine years that a thing had been read out in that room that four people had come for.

Nobody in that room agreed with her and nobody disagreed.

---

**And the cost of it is in the card, and the card says it in about nine words, and the cost is money and about nine weeks of somebody's time, and nobody in that room offered any of it.**

A man of about thirty-four read the money off the card and read the time off the card, and the money is about nine hundred pounds and the time is about nine weeks of one person's working life, and the name of that person is on the card and is not repeated here because he is not in this room and has not been asked.

A man of about fifty-two said in about nine seconds that nine hundred pounds is about four months of what nine of them earn between them and that about four of them have known that for years.

Nobody in that room said whether they had it.

**He was not asked to say and he was not asked twice, and about two people in that room have said since that they wanted to ask him and did not.**

---

**And nobody in that room offered anything, and that is a thing about the room and not about the people in it.**

About nine people were in it and about four of them had come about the name and about four had come to find out whether the cost was real and about four had come because somebody they work with had come.

**Nobody said they would put in some of it. Nobody said they would put in all of it. Nobody said they would find somebody who could.**

A man of about thirty-four said, in about nine seconds, that the card does not ask anybody for anything and that this is the first document he has read in about nine years that asks for nothing and says what it will not do.

**Nobody in that room said that to him and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted to and that they have not said it.**

---

**And a woman of about thirty-nine wrote one word on the paper under the card, at about nine minutes past seven, and she did not say what it was and nobody in that room asked her.**

She asked for the paper and a pen and wrote one word on the bottom half of it, below the two lines, in about four seconds, and put the pen down.

**The word is *unasked*, and it is on this page once and this page does not explain it.**

A man of about thirty-four said that he had not asked for anything to be written down and that she had written it anyway.

She said, in about four seconds, that she had not written it for him, and that she had written it because the second line on that card is the only one in about nine years that has said what a thing will not do, and that somebody ought to have it in writing.

**Nobody in that room thanked her for it and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted to and did not say it to her.**

Nobody asked her where she got the word from. Nobody asked her why that word and not another one.

---

**And a man of about twenty-eight asked, in about nine seconds, the question that about four people in that room had been carrying since the Wednesday.**

He asked whether the nine weeks includes anybody over there, and whether anybody in this city had asked anybody over there anything at all, ever, about the thing the card is about.

**A man of about fifty-two said no, and said it in four seconds, and said that there is no procedure in this city for putting a question to somebody who has not agreed to be asked.**

Nobody in that room said anything else about it.

The man of about twenty-eight said that he had not expected that and that he had thought about nine people in about nine years who had all assumed somebody had asked.

**Nobody in that room told him he was the only one who had thought that and about four people in that room have said since that he was not.**

---

**And the rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where about nine metres of shelving in a storeroom had been put up with brackets at the ends and nothing at the middle.**

He took the whole run off the wall, let an upright into each end and one into the middle, fixed all three into the floor, braced the back across them, and hung the run off the three.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a nine-metre run carried at two ends is a cantilever for as long as nobody leans on it, and that four runs in that building are carried that way, and that one of them has an upright that has begun to tear the plaster out where it meets the wall.**

---

**And a set of stairs in the same building had a handrail fixed through replaced trim with four plugs in it.**

He took the rail off, filled the four plugs, found the rail ends behind the trim with a detector, and fixed the rail through the trim into those.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a rail fixed into a plug is a rail fixed into a hole somebody filled, and that four rails in that building are on plugs, and that one of them moves about a quarter of an inch at the bottom bracket when a hand goes on it.**

---

**And a length of guttering on the same building had been fixed through a soffit that had been replaced and not the bracket.**

He filled the four holes, cut four lengths to the depth of the rafter tail, let them into the plumb of the rafters, and refitted the gutter through those.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a bracket into a replaced soffit is a bracket into about nine years of weather, and that four runs of guttering in that district are on replaced soffits, and that one of them came down on a wet Tuesday and put a bucket in a doorway.**

---

**And a set of hooks on a door in the same building had been screwed into the face of the door and not into the frame stile.**

He took four hooks out, filled the holes, and put two of them through the frame stile on the hinge side, and left the other two where they were because they were carrying nothing.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a coat hook in the face of a door works until somebody hangs a wet coat on it, and that four doors in that building have hooks in the face, and that one of them has a door out of square because of it.**

---

**From half past six there has been a woman of about thirty at her chair in the fourth of those four rooms with the door shut behind her, and the eighth of eight has lain on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and twenty-five days. Nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**
