# Chapter 836 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In The Lower Wards Where A Service Group Meets And About Nine People Were In It And About Four Of Them Had Come About The Four Words, And A Man Of About Forty-Four Read Both Sets Out And Found The One That Meant The Opposite And Said He Was Not Going To Be The One To Say It To Anybody Over A Border

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a room off a road in the Lower Wards where a service group meets, with about nine people in it and about four of them for the four words. A man of about forty-four read both sets out loud and found that one of the four means the opposite.**

---

Nine names and nine dates went onto that Monday's sheet, the last of the nine at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four rooms stand at one thousand five hundred and forty days, two hundred and twenty weeks to the day, and the card on end in the rail beside that door is one thousand five hundred and forty-four, two hundred and twenty weeks and four days, and the rail takes two.**

**Line sixteen is one thousand three hundred and thirty days, a hundred and ninety weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand one hundred and six, one hundred and fifty-eight weeks to the day, one corner torn, eight unfinished, and not two compared.**

---

**And a man of about forty-four read both sets of four words out loud in that room on that Monday, in about nine minutes, and about four of the people in that room had come for it.**

The first set is the four over the ruled line on the form, and he read them as they are written, in the order they are written, which is not the order the man at the bus stop used them in.

The second set is the four that are written down in about three places in this city where somebody wrote them down by accident, in a hand about nine years old that nobody in this city recognises.

**He read them both twice and did not say which one held until about the seventh minute, and what he said at the seventh minute was that he had found what he had been looking for and that it was not what he had been looking for.**

---

**And the word is the third of the four, and it means the opposite, and he said so in about nine seconds and did not say anything else about it for about nine minutes.**

He said that the third word of the first set and the third word of the second set look the same to anybody who is not careful and do not mean the same, and that the second one means the other thing, and that there is no reading of the first one that makes it mean what the second one means.

A woman of about twenty-nine said in about four seconds that she had looked at those two words on the Wednesday and had thought they were the same word written twice.

He said yes, and said that she was not careless, and that they are printed the same and that one of them has been printed the same as the other by accident, and that about four hundred people do the thing that word is about every day.

**Nobody in that room said the number out loud and nobody in that room said why not.**

---

**And he said he was not going to be the one to say it to anybody over there, and he said it in about nine seconds, and he said it to the room and not to the woman of about thirty-four.**

She was not in that room. He said that she was not in that room and that this was one of about nine reasons.

He said that a man who finds out that one of the four words on a form means the opposite is the person who wants to be the one who told four hundred people, and that about nine years ago somebody in this city let that happen to a man and nobody has worked out since what it cost.

**Nobody in that room asked him which man and nobody in that room named one, and about four people in that room have said since that they had wanted to and did not.**

A man of about fifty-two said in about four seconds that the woman of about thirty-four carried that form back on a bus and that she would be the natural person to be told, and that this is not a reason.

The man of about forty-four said it was not a reason and that the first reason was that she would be asked to carry an answer back on a bus and that this city has already done that once.

**Nobody in that room agreed with him and nobody in that room disagreed with him, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted to and that they have not worked out whether agreeing would be better.**

---

**And a man of about fifty-two said the thing that made the rest of the hour, in about nine seconds, and it was not about the word.**

He said that a word which means the opposite on one side of a border cannot be corrected in this city, because the correct meaning is not in this city and is not on anything in this city, and that the only place it exists is in about nine people's heads in a stretch of country nine days away on a bus.

He said that about four people have those words in their heads and that none of them has been asked and that two of them do not know they have them.

Nobody in that room asked him how he knows which two.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four was not in that room and the room did not send anybody to her, and about four people in that room said afterwards that they had wanted somebody to.**

A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that she had asked a question once and been told that nobody knew, and that telling her the answer now would be better than not telling her and worse than not telling her at the same time.

He said he could not make that work and that he had been trying since the Wednesday.

**Nobody in that room made it work and nobody in that room has worked it out since, and about two people in that room have said that they have stopped trying, which is not the same as working it out.**

---

**And the rest of that Monday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a fourth district, where about eleven metres of roofing ladder hooks had been set into a fascia that had been replaced and not the hooks.**

He took all eleven hooks off, found the old fixing holes in a fascia that was no longer there, filled them, and set the hooks into the ends of the rafters where they had been when the roof was framed.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a hook in a replaced fascia is a hook in about nine years of weather, and that four runs of hooks in that district are in replaced fascias, and that one of them has let a ladder go in about four winters.**

---

**And a pair of points at the end of a run in the same place had been greased with a compound that has to be worked out of with a solvent before anything else goes on.**

He took the grease off both blades with a solvent and a rag, cleaned the throat out, and re-greased it with the compound that place keeps.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a points mechanism greased with the wrong compound is about nine weeks out of date, and that four sets on that run are due, and that one of them has a blade that closes about four seconds late.**

---

**And a length of conduit in the same place had been saddled over a cable tray that somebody had later cut down about two inches.**

He took the conduit down, took the saddles off, put a new piece of tray under the run to bring it back up, and refitted the saddles onto that.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a saddle on a tray that has been cut down is a saddle holding about two inches less than it holds on, and that four runs there are on cut-down trays, and that one of them has two cables in it that are not in any tray at all.**

---

**And a lamp standard at the end of that run had a base plate with four bolts and about two of them loose.**

He dug the base out, cut new holding-down bolts, and grouted the base back into fresh concrete.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a lamp standard on two of four bolts is a lamp standard on two bolts, and that four standards on that run have loose base plates, and that one of them has a column that has moved about two inches at the foot.**

---

**At about half past six that Wednesday she was at her chair in the fourth of the four rooms and the door of that room was shut, and behind her on a shelf stood the eighth of eight at three hundred and twenty-nine days. Nobody in this city has asked that woman a single question about that page and nobody is going to, and this page does not say what is on it.**
