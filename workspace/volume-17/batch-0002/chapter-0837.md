# Chapter 837 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Corridor And A Stair In A Second District Where Two People Who Have A Key To A Room Of Their Own Each Stood At About Four Feet Apart For About Nine Minutes, And She Asked One Question About The Four Words And About Who Is Going To Be Asked, And He Gave Her An Answer That Was About The Question And Not About Either Of Them, And Neither Of Them Said Another Word

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and it finished in a corridor and on a stair in a second district, where two people who have a key to a room of their own stood about four feet apart for about nine minutes. She asked one question. He answered it. Neither of them said another word.**

**And the question was asked at about four minutes past seven in that corridor and it took her about nine seconds to ask it and him about nine seconds to answer it.**

She asked which of the four words was the one that meant the opposite, and who was going to be told that it did, and whether anybody had decided that yet.

He said that a man of about forty-four read two sets of four out loud in a room off a road in the Lower Wards on the Monday and that the third word is the one, and that the man of about forty-four said he was not going to be the one to say it to anybody over a border.

She said that she had not asked who was going to say it over a border. She said she had asked who in this city was going to be told.

**He said, in about nine seconds, that nobody, as far as he knows, and that he knows of about four people and that the four are the ones who have already said it in a room.**

She said, in four seconds, that that is not an answer about anybody.

He said that it was the answer he had.

---

**And then neither of them said another word, and about nine minutes of that corridor went past with the two of them in it and about nine other people going past the door at the end of it.**

She has said since that she asked the question and got an answer that told her a thing she could have got from anybody and that she had wanted something else and had not asked for it and that this is the arrangement and has been for about nine years.

He has said since that the answer was true, and that a man who cannot hold a release steady for about two hours in eleven years is not the person a room should be told is the answer, and that she did not ask him about his hands and that she has never once asked him about his hands.

**He said that this is the second time she has declined to ask him that and that he has never worked out whether that is a courtesy or a decision.**

She has said that it is a decision and that she made it a long time before that corridor and that he has been told that already.

---

**And there is one more thing in that corridor that is not about either of them and that both of them heard.**

A man of about thirty-four came up that stair at about seven with a piece of paper in his hand and asked a woman of about twenty-nine at the door whether anybody had told them about the word, and she said no, and he said that he had not either and that he had been looking since the Wednesday.

**Nobody in that corridor told him anything and he went back down it and about four people who were passing said afterwards that they had wanted to stop him.**

He did not know the word. He did not know there were two sets. He had been looking since the Wednesday because a woman of about thirty-four had said in a room that nobody over there has ever used the new name, and he had gone back to the bus stop at the end of the road and asked the man who works there.

**The man at the bus stop told him that he did not have a third set and that the woman was the only person who had come back with anything.**

Nobody in that corridor said that out loud and about two people who were passing have said since that they did not know whether he was told it here or not.

**And the rest of that Tuesday was a counter until about six with about thirteen callers on it and about two hours of work in a first district, where a door in the same building had been hung on three hinges and had sagged about nine millimetres.**

He took the door off, took the top hinge out, packed the hinge stile to square, fitted a fourth hinge, and refitted the door.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a door on three hinges sags about nine millimetres in about four years, and that four doors in that building are on three, and that one of them has a latch that has to be lifted to be shut.**

---

**And a length of skirting on the same floor had been nailed to a joint that moves about four millimetres across a year.**

He took the run off, filled the old fixings, fitted a scribed batten across six joints, and refitted the skirting onto the batten.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a skirting on a moving joint opens a gap at one end every year, and that four runs in that building are fixed to joints, and that one of them has a gap at the corner about a leaf thick.**

---

**And a capping on a window in the same building had been painted onto the frames instead of onto the masonry and had come away with about four feet of frame moulding stuck to it.**

He scraped the paint back to bare timber, took the capping off, bedded a new one on the masonry head, and refitted the frames onto it.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a capping on a frame takes the frame with it, and that four windows in that building are capped that way, and that one of them has a head that is about four inches under the opening it came out of.**

---

**And a run of trench cover in a lane in the same district had been replaced with a plate of a different thickness and the frame had been packed with a hardwood strip at one end only.**

He lifted the plate, took the strip out, packed the frame all round with a lead wedge and a mastic strip, and refitted the plate.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a frame packed at one end has about nine millimetres of movement at the other, and that four covers in that lane are packed that way, and that one of them rocks about four millimetres at the far end when a wheel goes over it.**

---

**And at about nine metres along that corridor a woman of about thirty was at her chair in the fourth of those four rooms with her door shut behind her, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and thirty days. No one has asked her one question about it and no one is going to.**
