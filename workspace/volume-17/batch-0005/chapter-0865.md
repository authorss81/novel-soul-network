# Chapter 865 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Service Road Where About Nine People Came To Find Out What Was Supposed To Happen Next Because A Trade Four Doors Along From A Book Was Told On Sunday That It Was Going To Be Asked About A Thing It Does, And Where The Man Of About Forty-Seven Said One Sentence About What He Would Do And It Was Not What That Room Wanted

**About nine people went into one of those four rooms on that Monday evening expecting there to be a next thing to do, and there was not one, and the evening went on for about two hours without becoming anything else. About two hours of the day were a counter.**

---

Ten names, and ten dates against them, went onto the sheet for that Monday, the last of the ten put in at about ten to five, and that day's shutter came down at about ten.

**The four converted units off that service road, one of them the warm one, are one thousand five hundred and eighty-two days, two hundred and twenty-six weeks to the day. The sixteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on the board on two nails is one thousand three hundred and seventy-two days, a hundred and ninety-six weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of the front of one page stand at one thousand one hundred and forty-eight days, a hundred and sixty-four weeks to the day, and no two of the nine have been compared.**

---

**And no number was said in that room on that Monday and none has been said in it since the last Wednesday of every fourth week, because there is no Wednesday of every fourth week inside this stretch of days and there is not going to be one before that room is asked something.**

A woman of about thirty-four who is in that room said in about nine seconds that the last time somebody said a number in that room she wrote it down, and that since then nobody has said one, and that she does not know whether that is because nobody has anything to say or because there is no longer anybody who says it.

**Nobody in that room said which of those two it was and about four people in that room have said since that they would rather somebody had answered her.**

---

**He came in at about half past five and he was let in and he did not come up the stair, because he has never once come up that stair and nobody in that room expected him to.**

A man of about forty-seven with a folded sheet in his inside pocket, on which there is a road written and no name, stood at the back of that room and listened to about four people talk for about half an hour about what this city is supposed to do next.

**Nobody in that room said the word procedure and nobody in that room has said it since, because there is no procedure in this city for what happens after a person is told, and about nine people learned that in that room in about half an hour.**

---

**At about ten past six he said one sentence, and it took him about nine seconds, and it was not what that room had been building towards all evening.**

He said: I am opening on Sunday as I always open.

What he said about that took another twenty seconds and came down to a man who stops trading on a Sunday because a room in a fourth district has a question having already given them the answer, and nobody in that room was in a position to argue with a sentence that brief.

**Nobody in that room said he was being careful and nobody in that room said he was being stupid, and about four people in that room have said afterwards that they wanted him to say something else and have not said what the something else was.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that she had wanted him to say he would stop until somebody came and explained it to him, and the man of about forty-seven said in about nine seconds that nobody in this city is coming, and that this is a fact and not a complaint.

**Nobody in that room agreed with her and nobody in that room agreed with him, and he did not stay and he did not leave early, and about four people in that room have said that those two things were the whole of the evening.**

---

About nine people in that room had come with something they had written, and about four of them read theirs out, and one of the four read a sentence about a bus and about how long the bus takes, and nobody in that room asked her anything about it afterwards.

**Nobody at that table said the four sentences were the same as each other and nobody said they were different, and the sheets went into about four different pockets.**

**Nobody thanked him for standing at the back of that room and nobody forgave him for the sentence, and nobody in that room has asked him a second time whether he has changed his mind, and the folded sheet stayed in his inside pocket the whole of that evening.**

He was asked, by a woman of about forty-four who works for him, at about twenty to seven, whether she is to keep working the counter through the week while this is going on, and he said in about nine seconds that she is, and that he had not thought about it, and that this was the first thing anybody had asked him about that he had not had an answer ready for.

**Nobody in that room asked her anything else and nobody in that room has asked her since, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted to ask her and did not.**

---

For about half an hour before he spoke, that room did the thing it does, which is to make a list.

A woman of about thirty-eight wrote four lines on the back of a sheet and read three of them out and then stopped, and said in about nine seconds that the fourth is what happens next and that nobody in this city has an answer to it, and that writing it down makes it look like a task.

A man of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that there are about nine people in this city who could write the fourth line and that about nine of them are in that room, and that nobody has written it because a person who writes it is then the person who has to do it.

**Nobody at that table said that was cowardice and nobody said it was correct, and the sheet went into somebody's pocket with nothing under the last line.**

**A man of twenty-two was in that room, was asked nothing at all, and said nothing until about half past seven, when he said one thing about a fitting.**

He said that the chair the man of about forty-seven has been leaning on for about half an hour has one leg a little less than the other three and that the whole room has been tilting very slightly towards him for half an hour and that nobody noticed.

**Nobody in that room had noticed and nobody in that room asked him a second question, and he has said since that a chair that leans is the only thing in that room anybody was sitting on the truth about.**

He signs nothing in these days, and on that Monday it was not to him that the folded sheet was put.

---

**Once that counter shut on the Monday there were about two hours of the day still to run, and they were in a fourth district.**

**A ladder in a goods store in the same place had been standing on about nine inches of packing, so that its feet had been off the floor for about four years and every pair of hands that used it had been standing higher than the floor.**

Down it came, the packing came out from under all four feet, it went onto the floor, and the feet were checked against a straight edge.

**Thirty-eight pounds. He told the man that a ladder on packing is a ladder that has been quietly cut down, and that about four ladders in that store have been, and that one of them has been reported as a ladder that has thrown somebody for about four years.**

---

**A slingshot on the same yard had been made up with a shackle in place of the eye, so that the sling could roll on the load at the moment it was asked to hold it.**

Out came the shackle, an eye went in, and the sling was proved with the load it takes.

**Thirty-eight pounds. He told the man that a shackle in a slingshot is a roller in the one place a roller must not be, and that about four slingshots on that yard have one, and that one of them has been reported as a load that came off for about four years.**

---

**A tap on a machine in the same building had its gland nut done up with the wrong washer, so that it had been weeping into the tray below it and the tray had been emptied every week by somebody who assumed it was the machine.**

He took the washer out, put the one that belongs there, and dressed the thread with a new washer and a nut.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a weeping gland is a machine that looks wet, and that about four taps in that building have one, and that one of them has been reported as a machine that needs looking at for about four years.**

---

**A handrail bracket in the same yard had been fixed with two screws into the edge of a steel section and none into its web, so that the rail moved about half an inch at that bracket every time anybody put a hand on it.**

He took it out, moved the fixings into the web, and refitted the bracket.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that two screws in an edge is a bracket that is being held by the screws and not by the steel, and that about four brackets on that rail are like that, and that one of them has been reported as a loose rail for about four years.**

**The four prices on that Monday added together are a hundred and twenty-one pounds, exact.**

**Behind the shut door of the fourth of those four rooms was a woman of about thirty who had not come out since half past six, and the shelf at the back of that room was holding the eighth of eight at three hundred and seventy-one days.**

**Nobody in this city has asked her about it and nobody is going to. What is written on that page is not given here.**
