# Chapter 867 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Upstairs Where Nobody Said A Number Because It Was Not One Of The Four Wednesdays This City Counts On, And Where A Man Of About Thirty-Eight Asked One Question About Three Books In Three Places And Nobody In That Room Could Answer It And Nobody In That Room Said The Word

**Three books in three buildings in three districts carry a description of four hundred people who live in another region, and on that Wednesday evening somebody put a question about that to about nine people in a room upstairs. About two hours of the day were a counter.**

---

Eight names went onto that Wednesday's book, a date against every one of them, and the last of those dates at about ten to five, and that shutter went down at about ten.

**The eighteenth of the nineteen ruled lines, ruled across on a board on two nails, is one thousand three hundred and two days, a hundred and eighty-six weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against the wall at the north end is one thousand one hundred and ninety days, a hundred and seventy weeks to the day. What is still owed on the page lying open in front of him is one thousand two hundred and seventy-four days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks to the day.**

---

**That Wednesday was not one of the four this city counts on, and so no number was said in that room and none was announced anywhere on that day, and about four people who came up that stair had not realised there was a Wednesday that a number was kept for.**

A woman of about thirty-four who is in that room said in about nine seconds that she had assumed it was every Wednesday and that she has been wrong for about nine years, and that nobody ever corrected her because nobody ever said anything.

**Nobody in that room said whether it was a week out or nine years out, and about four people in that room have said since that it does not matter which and they mind anyway.**

---

**Those three books are on three shelves, in three districts, and every one of them was written on purpose by nobody, and every one of them is about nine years old.**

One is in a laundry on a road in a first district and it is the owner's own book and she keeps it behind her own counter. One is in the minute book of a service group in a second district and it is written across a ruled line in the middle of a page about something else. One is in a district office in a fourth district and it is in a return that nobody has queried in about four years.

**Nobody in that room on that Wednesday had been in all three buildings and about four people in that room had been in one of them, and none of the three of those four had told anybody else what was in the one they had been in.**

---

**A man of about thirty-eight who works on the second floor of the building in Saltmarket asked the question at about half past seven, and it took him about nine seconds to get to it.**

He asked what happens to a description of four hundred people that is written down in a city which is not their own.

**Nobody in that room answered him and nobody in that room has answered him since, and about four people in that room have said afterwards that they had an answer and that it was not one they were willing to say out loud in that room.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that it is a description, and that descriptions go in files, and that a file in this city does not know what it is describing. The man of about thirty-eight said that is exactly what he is asking about.

---

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that the fourth district return is the only one of the three that anybody could find again in about four years, and that the other two have been put in with about nine other papers by about four different people.

**Nobody in that room asked her how a description of four hundred people ends up in with about nine other papers and nobody in that room has asked her since.**

**And a man of about fifty-two, who keeps a register no one has ever asked to see, was in that room and was asked nothing and said nothing for about an hour.**

He said one thing at about ten past eight. He said that the answer to that question in this city is that nobody has ever had to give one, because nobody has ever asked, and that the two of those have been sitting next to each other for about nine years.

**Nobody in that room asked him a second question and nobody in that room has asked him since, and about four people in that room have said that it is the only time anybody has said anything about that in that room in about nine years.**

---

**And there was a word that about four people in that room had in their mouths that evening, and not one of them said it, and it is not written down on this page either.**

A woman of about thirty-one said in about nine seconds that there is a word for a thing like that, and then stopped, and said in about nine seconds that there are about four words depending on which department you ask, and that nobody in this city has ever had to pick one because nobody has ever had to write one.

**Nobody in that room asked her which four, and nobody in that room has asked her since, and about four people in that room have said that she came nearer to it than anybody has come in about nine years.**

---

A woman of about thirty-one said in about nine seconds that she has been in one of those three buildings and that what is written in it is four lines long and that she has read it about nine times and has never once been able to say out loud what the four lines mean.

**Nobody in that room asked her which building and nobody in that room has asked her since, and about four people in that room have said since that they have been inside one of the other two.**

**A man of twenty-two was in that room and was asked nothing at all and said nothing until about half past eight, and what he said was about a fitting.**

He said that the third chair in from the door on that side has been through a machine that has left about four scratches in the varnish of the front left leg, and that the scratches are about nine years old and are under the varnish and not on it.

**Nobody in that room knew what that meant and nobody in that room asked him a second question, and he has said since that scratches under varnish are the only kind anybody keeps.**

He is nobody's signatory in this stretch of days, and nothing at all on that Wednesday was put up to him.

---

**After the counter shut on that Wednesday the afternoon was about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A drainage gully cover in a yard in the same place had been bedded on its own bedding which had been put in dry, so that it had settled about two inches and stopped sitting level with the frame.**

The cover came up, the dry bedding came out, it went back down on something that holds, and the cover was levelled on the frame.

**Thirty-eight pounds. He told the man that a cover bedded in dry stuff settles and stays settled, and that about four covers in that yard are bedded that way, and that one of them has been reported as a hole in a yard for about four years.**

---

**A stop on a pipe in the same yard had been fitted with a washer on the wrong side and had been dripping into a bucket which had been emptied every week for about four years.**

He took the stop off, put the washer on the correct side, dressed the joint, and left the bucket where it is.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a bucket under a stop is a stop that has been reported as a leak and never been reported as a stop, and that about four stops in that yard have one, and that one of them has been reported as a dripping pipe for about four years.**

---

**A catch on a window in the same building had been screwed to the frame about nine millimetres proud, so that the sash had to be forced down on the catch to shut it.**

He took the catch off, packed it back, and refitted it so the sash shuts on its own weight.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a catch set proud is a catch that has been taught to be forced, and that about four catches in that building are like that, and that one of them has been reported as a window that will not shut for about four years.**

---

**A bolt on a gate in the same place had its nut on the wrong side of the hinge plate, so that the plate had been pressed hard against the frame and the bolt had been run up to hold the plate in.**

He took the nut off, moved it to the other side, and packed the plate back off the frame.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a nut on the wrong side is a bolt doing a plate's job, and that about four gates on that yard have one, and that one of them has been reported as a gate that binds for about four years.**

**No more than the four of them, and no less: a hundred and nine pounds, exact, on that Wednesday.**

**Behind the door of the fourth of those four rooms, shut at half past six, a woman of about thirty had been sitting since, and the back shelf of that room was carrying the eighth of eight at three hundred and seventy-three days.**

**No one in this city has asked her about that page and nobody is going to. What is on it is not given here.**
