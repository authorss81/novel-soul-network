# Chapter 869 — A Friday, A Room In The First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket With About Nine People In It, And A Question Put To A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Has Been Over There And Done It Twice And Who Is On Nothing And Entitled To Nothing, And An Answer Of Nine Words And Then Another Nine Words That Everybody In That Room Had Been Afraid Of And That Is True

**About nine people were in one room on that Friday evening and somebody in it had arranged for a question to be asked, and the question was put to a man who is on no body in this city. The rest of the day was a counter and about two hours of work.**

---

Nine callers, and nine dates against them, were on that Friday's sheet, the last of the nine at about ten to five, and that Friday's shutter came down at about ten.

**The seventeenth of the nineteen ruled lines on the board on two nails is one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight days, a hundred and ninety-four weeks to the day. The post standing at the end of that corridor is one thousand one hundred and thirty-four days, a hundred and sixty-two weeks to the day. The separation, which is one line inside one box in a room off a road, is nine hundred and sixty-six days, a hundred and thirty-eight weeks to the day.**

---

**And no number was said in that room on that Friday and none was announced anywhere on that day, because the Wednesday this city counts on is not for another week and a day, and about four people in that room had been counting the days to a different thing for about nine.**

The room was the first floor above a line in Saltmarket, four doors along from the trade that opens on nothing but a Saturday and a Sunday. Nine people came up between about half past six and about seven.

**Nobody in that room had told anybody what was going to be asked, and about four people in that room had known since a Wednesday about nine days before, and about four more had worked it out and had not been told.**

---

**A man of about thirty-three who writes the wording on this city's forms asked it, and he had the paper in front of him and he did not read it.**

He asked the man of about thirty-four who has been over there and done it twice what this body owes four hundred people it does not govern.

**He said it in about nine seconds and he said it in the man's face, and about nine people in that room heard every word of it and not one of them looked at the paper afterwards.**

He is not on that body. He is not on any body in this city. He has no post and no vote and no minute and he was asked because he has been in a room with about nine people in it at two in the morning about twice, and that is the whole of what he has.

---

**And the man of about thirty-four said nine words, and he said them in about nine seconds, and he said them in the asker's face.**

He said: we owe four hundred what we owe our own.

**Nobody in that room said anything at all for about half a minute, and about four people in that room have said since that they had wanted that sentence for about nine days and had not dared to want it.**

---

**And then he said nine more, in about nine seconds, and this is the half of it that everybody in that room had been afraid of and that is also true.**

He said: it does not get to say what is true.

**Nobody in that room argued with him and nobody in that room agreed with him, and a woman of about thirty-eight said afterwards, in about nine seconds, that she had wanted to argue with it and had worked out in about four days that there was nothing to argue against.**

The man of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that he was not saying it should be kept going and was not saying it should stop, and that a person who has done a thing for nine years without anybody asking is not the person who decides whether it is safe.

A woman of about thirty-one said in about nine seconds that the whole of what she has heard in about nine years of this work is that nobody over there is asked anything, and that she had assumed the fault was that people over there are not asked, and that she has stopped assuming that.

**Nobody in that room asked her what she is assuming instead, and nobody in that room has asked her since.**

**Nobody in that room asked him who is, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have asked him and did not.**

---

**And then about nine people in that room did the thing they have apparently never done in about nine years, which was to write the sentence down.**

A woman of about thirty-four who is in that room wrote it on the back of a sheet and did not put it in a file, and said in about nine seconds that it is going to stay in her bag, and that a thing in a file in that building has never once come out of that building.

**Nobody in that room took the sheet off her and nobody in that room asked to keep a copy, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted a copy and did not ask.**

---

A woman of about thirty-eight said at about ten to eight that the sentence cannot go on anything in that building, because the building's forms have a place for what a person is signing and no place for what a body is saying.

**Nobody in that room asked her where it could go and nobody in that room has asked her since, and about four people in that room have said that they would have asked and did not.**

**Nobody in that room thanked the man of about thirty-four and nobody in that room has thanked him since, and nobody was forgiven by anybody on that Friday, because there was nothing anybody had done that was being forgiven.**

He was asked at about twenty past seven whether he would put his name to it, and he said no in about four seconds, and said that a man who does the thing has no standing to say what is owed by people who do not do it.

**Nobody in that room argued with him about that either and nobody in that room has raised it since.**

---

The woman of about thirty-four who wrote it folded the sheet once and then twice more and put it into the inside pocket of her bag, and nobody in that room saw her do it and nobody in that room has asked her about it since.

She said in about nine seconds, at the door, that she is not going to hand it to anybody, and that a thing on a table in that building comes out of that building about four times in ten years.

**Nobody at that door said that was a good instinct and nobody said it was a bad one, and she went down the stair with her hand flat on the pocket.**

**A man of twenty-two was in that room, was asked nothing at all, and said nothing until about twenty to eight.**

He said that the fourth door along that first floor from the one they are standing in has a closer on it that was adjusted so that it closes hard, and that it was adjusted by somebody who wanted it to shut and not by anybody who wanted it to be kind.

**Nobody in that room found anywhere to put that, and nobody in that room came back with a second question about it.**

Nothing was signed by him in this stretch of days, and on that Friday no sheet was held up to him, and there is nothing in that room that wants a name on it.

---

**What was left of that Friday was a counter and about two hours of work in a fourth district.**

**A stopcock on a tank in the same place had been wired into place with the body of it below the outlet, so that it trapped about nine inches of water at the bottom of the tank and that water could not be taken out of it.**

The stopcock came off and went back with its body above the outlet, and the tank was drained and proved empty.

**Thirty-eight pounds. He told the man that a stopcock below an outlet traps whatever has been in there for four years, and that about four tanks on that site are fitted that way, and that one of them has been reported as a tank that cannot be emptied for about four years.**

---

**A ladder on the roof in the same yard had its top extension worn through on one side, so that the rungs went round in a hand.**

The extension came off and two worn rungs came out of it and rungs made for it went in.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a rung worn round in a hand is a rung that will go at the moment somebody leans on it, and that about four extensions on that roof have worn rungs, and that one of them has been reported as a ladder that bends for about four years.**

---

**A bolt on a handrail in the same place had been put in with a nut that was a size too small, and had been done up until the thread started to shear rather than until the nut was tight.**

He took it out, put a nut of the size that belongs there, and refitted the bolt.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a nut a size too small is not tight, it is a thread being destroyed, and that about four bolts on that rail are like that, and that one of them has been reported as a loose rail for about four years.**

---

**A wall light in a passage in the same building had been earthed off the conduit instead of the box, so that the earth depended on a length of conduit that has been painted for about four years.**

He cut it off the conduit, earthed it on the box, and proved the fitting.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that an earth off a painted conduit is an earth that a painter turns off, and that about four lights in that passage are done that way, and that one of them has been reported as a light that is a nuisance to touch for about four years.**

**Ninety-seven pounds, exact, is what that Friday came to across those four.**

**At half past six on that Friday the fourth of those four rooms had its door shut and a woman of about thirty behind it, and the eighth of eight lay at the back of that room at three hundred and seventy-five days.**

**Nobody in this city has asked her one question about that page and nobody is going to. What is on that page is not given here.**
