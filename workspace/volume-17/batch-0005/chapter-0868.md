# Chapter 868 — A Thursday, Four Hours At A Counter, And A Man Who Said He Had Been In Three Rooms Like That One And Who Was Leo Marr And Who Was Given Nothing To Do About It, And The Ninth Of Nine Lines Still Being The Last One Read Because Nobody Has Ever Been Able To Change The Order Of Things Once The Forms Have Gone Out

**Four hours of that Thursday were at one counter where nine lines get read out in a fixed order, and a man who had been in three rooms like that one stood up in the middle of it. The rest of the day was in a fourth district.**

---

Eleven names, and eleven dates, went onto the sheet for that Thursday, the eleventh of the dates written at about ten to five, and at about ten the shutter of that Thursday came down.

**The card that stands on end in a rail which takes two is one thousand five hundred and eighty-nine days, two hundred and twenty-seven weeks to the day. The twelfth of the nineteen ruled lines on the board on two nails is one thousand five hundred and five days, two hundred and fifteen weeks to the day. The thirteenth is one thousand four hundred and fifty-six days, two hundred and eight weeks to the day. The fourteenth is one thousand four hundred and twenty-one days, two hundred and three weeks to the day.**

**The fifteenth is one thousand four hundred days, two hundred weeks to the day. The nineteenth and the last of those lines is one thousand two hundred and eighty-one days, a hundred and eighty-three weeks to the day. The hold on nine crates and on the floor they stand on is one thousand two hundred and eighteen days, a hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day.**

---

**And no number was said in that room on that Thursday and none has been said in it since the last Wednesday this city counts on, which is not for another week.**

Nine lines are read out in a room off a service road. They were read in the order the forms they were written on were printed, and nobody chose that order, and nobody has been able to change it since because the forms have gone out and a printed order cannot be reprinted without a new number on it.

**The ninth of those nine is still the last one read, and it has been last on every day it has been read, and nobody in that room has been able to say what it is about, because nobody has ever read anything after it.**

---

**A man who said he had been in three rooms of that kind, and who had never once been in that one before that Thursday, stood up at about twelve and said so out loud before he said anything else.**

His name is Leo Marr. He has been in a room like that one in a second district and in a room like that one in a fourth district and he has been sent a thing to look at twice without being asked to look at it, and he said all of that in about nine seconds.

**Nobody at that counter asked him about either of the other two rooms and about four people at that counter have said since that they wanted to and did not.**

A man of about thirty-three who writes this city's wording said in about nine seconds that there is nothing on any sheet for a person who has been in three of those rooms, because the sheet is for a person who is being asked, and being in the room is not being asked.

**Nobody at that counter said that was a cruel thing to do and nobody said it was a decent one, and Leo Marr was not asked to do anything on that Thursday and did not do anything on that Thursday.**

---

**And at about half past two somebody raised the order, and it was raised in about nine seconds, and nobody in that room argued about it.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said, in about nine words: read the ninth second and not last of all.

What she said about that took another twenty seconds and came down to the second of the nine being about what a person is agreeing to stay inside of, and there being no sense in asking a person about that before anybody has told them what is going to be read at the end.

**The seven words were read out last on that Thursday exactly as they were read out on the Monday about nine days before, and about four people at that counter had written the seven down the first time and did not write them again.

**Nobody at that counter said that not writing them again was an improvement and nobody said it was a surrender, and the sheet with the first week's seven words on it went into a drawer somewhere in that building.**

About four people at that counter said the order should be different. Nobody at that counter said the order should not be different. Nobody at that counter has said the order should not be different since, and about four people at that counter have said afterwards that they expected somebody to.**

---

**And nobody reopened anything, and in particular nobody went back over what was said on the Wednesday about four days before this one, and nobody quoted it, and nobody in that room asked whether it had been decided.**

A man of about fifty-two said in about nine seconds that the order is the order and that the forms went out in that order and that this city does not have a way of making a thing untrue by wishing it had gone out in a different one.

**Nobody at that counter said that was the last word on it and nobody said it was not the last word on it, and the ninth of the nine was read last again at about half past three.**

Leo Marr was in that room when the ninth was read and said afterwards that he had been in three rooms and that in two of them it had been read fourth and in one of them it had not been read at all.

A man of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that he has been in two of the rooms Leo Marr has been in and that in one of them the ninth of the nine was read second, and that the two people in that room had argued about it for about half an hour afterwards.

**Nobody at that counter said which room and nobody at that counter has asked him since, and about four people at that counter have said that they would have asked and did not.**

**Nobody in that room asked him which of the two orders is better and nobody in that room has asked him since, and about four people in that room have said that this is the fifth time in about nine years that anybody has told the truth about an order and changed nothing by it.**

---

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that the ninth of the nine is read last for the same reason a postscript is read last, which is that it was added after everything else and nobody redrew the page.

**Nobody at that counter said that was why and nobody said that was not why, and the ninth of the nine was read last again at about half past three with the same seven words in front of it.**

**A man of twenty-two was at that counter from about three and was asked nothing at all and said nothing until about four.**

He said that the fourth drawer down in that counter has a runner in it that has been oiled, and that every other drawer in that counter has a runner that has not, and that there is no reason anybody would know that except by opening them.

**Nobody at that counter knew what to do with that and nobody at that counter asked him a second question, and he has said since that an oiled drawer in a counter of un-oiled drawers is the only order in that room that anybody chose.**

He signs nothing in these days and on that Thursday no part of what was said in that room was for him to sign.

---

**The rest of that Thursday, after about four hours at that counter, was about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A mast on a yard crane in the same place had a limit switch on it that had been shimmed out about nine millimetres so that the crane could reach further than it is meant to, and the shim had been there about four years.**

He took the shim out, re-set the switch, and brought the crane down to its stop and back up about nine times.

**Forty-four pounds. He told the man that a limit switch with a shim in it is not a limit switch, and that about four cranes on that yard have been shimmed, and that one of them has been reported as a crane that reaches too far for about four years.**

---

**A hook block on the same yard had its safety catch bent back and held by four inches of wire, so that the catch could not close and had been carried like that for about four years.**

He took the catch back, put in a new pin, and proved it with a test weight.

**Thirty-eight pounds. He told the man that a catch held open by wire is a catch that has been described as fitted for about four years, and that about four hook blocks on that yard have one, and that one of them has been reported as a hook that will not hold for about four years.**

---

**A sling tag in the same yard had been written on with a pen that has gone, so that the load weight on it cannot be read at all, and the tag had been in that state for about four years.**

He took the tag down, worked out what the load was from the crane's own record, and wrote a fresh tag.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that an unreadable tag is not a record of a load, and that about four tags in that yard have gone, and that one of them has been reported as a lift that cannot be proved for about four years.**

---

**A grease line in the same building had been run through a bracket with no clearance, so that the line was rubbing on the bracket every time the arm moved.**

He took the line off, took the bracket back, ran the line with clearance, and put a restraint on it.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a grease line through a bracket is a line that wears through in a year and gets oiled for four, and that about four lines in that building are run like that, and that one of them has been reported as a machine that needs greasing every week for about four years.**

**A hundred and twenty-seven pounds, exact, is the figure those four arrived at on that Thursday.**

**The fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six and a woman of about thirty had not come out from behind that door since, and the eighth of eight stood on a shelf in that room at three hundred and seventy-four days.**

**Nobody has asked her about that and nobody is going to. What is written on it is not given here.**
