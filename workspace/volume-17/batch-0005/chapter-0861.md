# Chapter 861 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Upstairs In The Evening Where A Standing Body Sat Down With One Person Light And Said Nothing At All, And Where A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Owns Four Vehicles Took Nine Seconds To Say That A Man Of About Forty-Seven Is Going To Be Asked About A Practice In About Nine Days And That Nobody Is Going To Tell Him

**A standing body with about eight people on it met on that Wednesday evening, and about nine people came up a stair afterwards and sat on benches. A man of about thirty-four who owns four vehicles was in that room. About two hours of the day were a counter with callers standing at it.**

---

Eight callers came to that counter on that Wednesday and eight callers went onto the sheet with a date against each, the last of those dates entered at about twenty to five, and at about ten that shutter came down.

**The eighteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on the board on two nails is one thousand two hundred and ninety-five days, a hundred and eighty-five weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against the wall at the north end is one thousand one hundred and eighty-three days, a hundred and sixty-nine weeks to the day. What is still owed on the page lying open in front of him is one thousand two hundred and sixty-seven days, a hundred and eighty-one weeks to the day.**

**The chair the woman of about sixty-one sits in is not the ninth chair, which is hard against the wall. The place behind her chair has stood empty for longer than anybody in that room has been on a standing body, it stood empty on that Wednesday, and this page puts no figure against it.**

About four people in that room had not come for the fourth column. They had come because the room is where the notices are and the notices have not changed since before the spring, and one of those four said in about nine seconds that she comes for the company and that the company is nine people.

**Nobody at that table said that the room would stop being a room if the notices stopped changing, and nobody said it would not.**

The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back turned to the whole of the room. **It did not move on that Wednesday, and on no page of this stretch of days is the person who would move it named.**

---

**A number is said in that room on a Wednesday of every fourth week and on no other day at all, and that Wednesday was not one of those four, so nothing was counted and nobody up that stair went away having expected to be given a figure.**

About nine people came up between about half past six and about eight. About four of them had come about a piece of paper. The rest came because it is a Wednesday.

**And the piece of paper was not read out and nothing was put on a table in that room on that evening, and the reason is that a body of eight had met on the Wednesday before, had done nothing whatever for about two hours, and nobody in this city has ever written down whether eight may meet at all.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that the last of those sheets has four columns in it and that the fourth column has a line for a name in it and that on the Wednesday before nobody put a name in that column, including the person whose name it would have been.

She brought the sheet out of a folder and put it flat on the table and turned it round so that the empty column faced the room, and she said in about nine seconds that she had brought it out on purpose and that she was not going to fill it in and that she would like somebody else to.

A man of about twenty-nine said in about nine seconds that there is no version of this where he is the one to fill it in, because a group that keeps devices running is not allowed to decide things, and that a person who cannot decide a thing cannot put a name against it either.

**Nobody at that table said that the fourth column should be filled in by somebody with more standing and nobody said it should be filled in by nobody, and the sheet stayed flat on that table with the column facing the room for about forty minutes and then went back into the folder.**

**Nobody at that table said whether the fourth column should have been filled in and nobody said it should not have been, and about four people at that table have said since that they could not sleep on the Wednesday before and have not been able to say what about.**

---

**And at about ten past seven a man of about thirty-four who owns four vehicles put his hand flat on the table and said nine words, and they were about a man he has never spoken to.**

He said: he is going to be asked about the practice.

What he said about that took another thirty seconds and named a man of about forty-seven who owns the only trade in this city that opens on nothing but a Saturday and a Sunday, and a week of days from now, and the absence of any way in this city to hand a person a piece of news before it happens.

**Nobody in that room asked him how he knew, and he said in about nine seconds that he knew because he carries to that road four mornings a week and that on two of those mornings the man is standing in his own doorway at about seven with the lights already on.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that a person who is told a week beforehand can do something about it, and the man of about thirty-four said that that is exactly the thing nobody in this city is going to do, because there is no procedure for telling anybody.

**Nobody in that room said the word procedure out loud twice and about four people in that room have said since that it is the first sentence anybody has offered them about a person rather than about a form.**

A woman of about thirty-one said in about nine seconds that she has been trying for about nine years to find out how a person gets found in this city and that the only method anybody has ever shown her is a postal address, and that about nine people in this city have no postal address that anybody here can use.

**Nobody at that table said how many of the nine and nobody at that table said what a person does about it, and the woman of about thirty-one said afterwards that she has said the same thing in four rooms and has been in a hurry every time.**

He said that he is not going to be the one who tells him, and that he has driven past that road four mornings a week for about nine years, and that a haulier is the last person a shopkeeper believes.

---

**Nobody in that room said the man of about forty-seven should be told, and about four people in that room said privately that he should be, and about four said there is no sheet in this city that a person can be handed which says what is going to happen to them.**

A man of about thirty-three who writes the wording on this city's forms said in about nine seconds that there are nine things somebody has to do before anybody in this city is asked anything, and that the ninth of the nine is to find the person, and that nobody has printed a way of finding a person who is only in his own shop on two days of the week.

**Nobody at that table asked him to finish the nine and he did not finish the nine, and about four people at that table have said that they would rather he had finished them.**

---

**A man of twenty-two was in that room and was asked nothing and said nothing until about half past seven, and what he said then was about a bench.**

He said that the bench at the end of that room has a bracket at the back of it that carries nothing at all and that has been carrying nothing since it was made, and that about four people in this city have decided that a bracket is for a box.

**Nobody in that room knew what to do with that and nobody in that room asked him a second question about it.**

Nothing was signed by him in this stretch of days and no sheet on that Wednesday was held up to him, and he has said since that a bracket with nothing on it is the first object in about nine years that nobody has taken away.

---

**What was left of that Wednesday, once the counter had shut, went into about two hours of work in a fourth district.**

**A cable tray on a wall in a workshop in the same place had been run with the lid off its fixings, so that the tray was carrying its own weight on two screws and one of the screws had started to come out of the wall.**

He made good the fixings, refitted the lid, and strapped the run where it crossed a hot pipe.

**Forty-four pounds. He told the man that a tray with no lid on its fixings is a tray that is being held up by luck, and that about four runs of tray in that workshop were left that way, and that one of them has been called a tray that has come down for about four years.**

---

**A stopcock on a riser in the same building had been painted over twice, so that the paint had built up around the handle and the handle could not be seen to be there at all.**

He scraped the paint back off the gland, cleaned the thread, and marked the pipe with a band so the next person can find it.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a stopcock under paint is a stopcock that gets broken in an emergency, and that about four risers in that building carry paint over theirs, and that one of them was reported as a stopcock that was not there for about four years.**

---

**An earth strap on a machine in the same yard had been made off the mounting bracket instead of the frame, so that the bracket was the earth and the bracket had been painted.**

He took the strap off the bracket, made it off clean metal on the frame, and cleaned the paint back off the seat.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a strap on painted steel is a strap that stops working when the paint is wet, and that about four machines in that yard are earthed off a bracket, and that one of them has been reported as a machine that hums in wet weather for about four years.**

---

**A door on a store in the same place had its latch keeper mounted on the frame and not on the door, so that the door had to be pulled hard to shut and had been pulled hard for about nine years.**

He moved the keeper on to the door, refitted the latch to it, and plugged the two old holes.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a keeper on the wrong side is a door that teaches everybody in the building to shut it badly, and that about four doors in that building are hung like it, and that one of them has been reported as a door that will not shut for about four years.**

**The four jobs priced out one after another came to a hundred and twenty pounds on that Wednesday, exact.**

**At half past six the door of the fourth of those four rooms was shut and a woman of about thirty was behind it, and on the shelf at the back of that room the eighth of eight stood at three hundred and sixty-six days.**

**Nobody in this city has put one question to her about that page, and nobody ever will. What is on that page is not given here.**
