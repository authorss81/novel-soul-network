# Chapter 863 — A Friday, Four Hours At A Counter Where A Man Of About Fifty-Two Was Asked Whether He Would Like To Be Told A Week In Advance What He Thought Of A Thing He Had Already Been Asked About, And Said No In About Four Seconds, And Nobody At That Counter Told Him That The Body Which Would Have Asked Him Is Now One Person Under Strength

**Four hours of that Friday were spent at one counter where one man keeps a register nobody has ever asked to see, and the only thing anybody wanted out of it was a yes or a no. About two hours of the rest of the day were in a fourth district.**

---

Seven names and seven dates went onto the sheet for that Friday, the last of the seven put down at about ten to five, and the shutter was down at about ten.

**The seventeenth of the nineteen ruled lines on the board by the counter is one thousand three hundred and fifty-one days, a hundred and ninety-three weeks to the day. The post standing at the end of that corridor is one thousand one hundred and twenty-seven days, a hundred and sixty-one weeks to the day. The separation, which is one line in one box in a room off a road, is nine hundred and fifty-nine days, a hundred and thirty-seven weeks to the day.**

---

**And a number was said in one room in this city about nine days ago and has not been said since, and none was said on that Friday, because this city says its number on a Wednesday of every fourth week and a Friday is not that day.**

A man of about fifty-two kept a register at that counter. He has kept it for about four years and nobody in this city has ever asked to see it and it is not on the board on two nails and it is not in the green-covered book.

**He has been asked about the practice once already and he answered it in about nine seconds in a room with about nine people in it, and about four of them heard it, and it has not been put to him since and he has not offered it again.**

---

**At about half past eleven on that Friday a woman of about thirty-eight came to that counter and asked him a question she had clearly rehearsed on the way down the stairs, and it took him about four seconds to answer it.**

She asked whether he would like to be told a week in advance what he thought about the practice, so that he could think about it properly before anybody came.

**He said no in about four seconds and the reason he gave was about the difference between being told and being prepared for, and he did not put those two things next to each other in a sentence and nobody at that counter wrote it down.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that those were two words and not a reason, and he said in about nine seconds that they are the whole of the reason and that a man who is handed a week is still a man being handed something.

**Nobody at that counter agreed with him and nobody at that counter disagreed with him, and the register stayed shut for the rest of that day and about nine people traded over it.**

---

She had come to that counter with the question written on a card and she put the card face down on the counter before she asked it, which is a thing people do when they want to be able to say afterwards that they did not have it written.

**Nobody at that counter asked her to turn the card over and nobody has asked her since, and the card went back into her handbag unturned.**

**And then she asked him a second thing, which she had not rehearsed, and he gave her an answer to that one too.**

She asked whether he would still say the same in a week, and he said, in about nine words: I was asked once and I answered once.

What he said about that took another twenty seconds and came down to the once having been in a room with about nine people in it, and to the answer being on the record already, and to him not intending to give it a second time.

**Nobody at that counter asked him which room and nobody at that counter has asked him since, and about four people in that building have said since that a man who has already said a thing once is harder to reach than a man who has never said it at all.**

---

**What nobody at that counter told him, and what nobody has explained to him since, is that the body which would have been the one to ask him is one person under strength.**

A woman of about thirty-eight who keeps no minutes of anything looked at her own sheet while she was saying it and did not read a line of it out, and afterwards said in about nine seconds that she could not have told him even if she had read it out, because she does not know what eight people are allowed to do.

A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that the body met on the Wednesday with one chair at it and did nothing for about two hours, and that he was the one who sat in it, and that he did not know until afterwards that it was allowed.

**Nobody at that counter asked him how he found out that it was allowed, and nobody in that building has asked him since.**

**Nobody at that counter asked her, and nobody in that building has asked her since, and about four people in that building have said since that a body one person under a decision is not a thing anybody can explain anything to.**

---

**A man of twenty-two was at that counter at about half past three and was asked nothing and said nothing at all until about four.**

He said that the third shelf from the bottom of the rack behind that counter has been planed at some point in its life, because the front edge of it is dead flat where everything else on it is rounded.

**Nobody at that counter knew what that meant and nobody at that counter asked him a second question, and he has said since that a planed shelf edge is a shelf that has been let down at one time for something long and put back.**

No signature of his appears in this stretch of days and nothing on that Friday was put in front of him to sign, and there is no form at that counter that wants him.

---

**The rest of that Friday, after about four hours at that counter, was about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A terminal on a control board in a plant room in the same place had its gland plate refitted with the cable gland loose in it, so that the cable moved about nine millimetres every time the cabinet was shut.**

He re-made the entry, tightened the gland onto the sheath, and left about four millimetres of slack inside the plate.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a gland with nothing to grip is a gland that has been dressed up and not done, and that about four cabinets in that plant room were dressed that way, and that one of them has been reported as a machine that will not hold a setting for about four years.**

---

**A speed restraint on a conveyor in the same building had been bypassed with a link at some point, so that the belt could be run with the gate open and had been run that way.**

He took the link out, proved the restraint, and put the cover back on it with a seal through it.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a bypassed restraint is not a slow machine and it is a machine with nothing stopping it, and that about four conveyors in that building were altered that way, and that one of them has been reported as a belt that hurt somebody for about four years.**

---

**An earth bar in a switchboard in the same yard had two of its lugs painted and two of them bare, so that the bar was making its earth through two bolts and not through four.**

He stripped the two painted lugs back to metal, cleaned the bar, and proved the bar with an instrument.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a bar that is painted under two lugs is a bar with half an earth, and that about four boards on that yard were painted through like that, and that one of them has been reported as a machine that trips in rain for about four years.**

---

**A lamp holder in a store in the same place had been fitted with a ring that was a shade too large, so that the lamp rested on the shoulder of the holder and not on its bottom contact.**

He fitted a ring that belongs to that holder, put the lamp back on its contact, and tried the switch twice.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a lamp on the shoulder is a lamp that has been in the holder about four years without ever making contact, and that about four holders in that store were fitted that way, and that one of them has been reported as a light that does not work for about four years.**

**A hundred and seven pounds, exact, is the sum of the four prices on that Friday.**

**The fourth of those four rooms had its door shut at half past six and a woman of about thirty was in it, and behind her the eighth of eight had lain since three hundred and sixty-eight days.**

**Nobody in this city has put anything to her about that page, and nobody will. What is written on that page is not given here.**
