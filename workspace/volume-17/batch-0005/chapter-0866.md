# Chapter 866 — A Tuesday, A Counter Until About Six And About Two Hours At It, And The Only Sentence That Came Out Of That Counter All Day Was Nine Words Long And Was About The Difference Between Being Told A Thing And Being Prepared For A Thing, And Nobody At That Counter Has Found A Box To Put It In, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Was Standing There And Was Asked Nothing And Said One Thing About A Washer

**A counter in a first district on that Tuesday, about two hours of it, and one question put to a man who has done the thing over a border twice, and a nine-word sentence afterwards that about four people in that city have since tried to write down. The rest of the day was in a fourth district.**

---

That Tuesday's sheet took nine names and nine dates, the ninth of the dates entered at about ten to five, and at about ten that shutter was shut.

**The card that stands in the rail at that first door, and the rail takes two, is one thousand five hundred and eighty-seven days, two hundred and twenty-six weeks and five days. The eighteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on the board on two nails is one thousand three hundred and one days, a hundred and eighty-five weeks and six days. The post standing at the end of that corridor is one thousand one hundred and thirty-one days, a hundred and sixty-one weeks and four days.**

---

**And no number was said in that room and none was said anywhere else on that Tuesday, and the Wednesday of every fourth week that would have carried one is not for another week and a day.**

Nobody has asked the people who work at that counter why they count a figure at all, and about four of them have said that it is not the figure they want and that it is the only one anybody has ever offered them.

---

**A woman of about thirty-eight asked the man of about thirty-four who has been over there and done it twice whether being told a week in advance would have made any difference the first time, and it took him about nine seconds to answer her and the answer was no.**

He said that he was told on a Friday, and that the person who told him was being kind, and that nobody asked him whether he would rather not have been told at all.

**Nobody at that counter asked him what he would rather have had instead and about four people at that counter have said since that they would have asked him and did not.**

He said in about nine seconds that he went in on the Saturday at about two in the morning into a room with about nine in it, and that his hands were no use to him whatever for about two hours that night, and that he did not ask anybody for anything for the whole of those two hours, and that people have been telling him for about nine years that this was a thing about him.

**He said in about nine seconds that it was not a thing about him and that nobody in that room had been asked whether they would prepare anybody, and that a man nobody has prepared does not know that there is anything to be prepared for.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-three who writes the wording on this city's forms said nine words, and nobody at that counter wrote them down at the time and about four of them have since.**

He said: told in advance is not the same as prepared.

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that the difference between the two is about nine hours of sleep and something to eat, and that nobody in this city has ever offered either of those to anybody.

**Nobody at that counter said whether that was the difference anybody meant, and the man of about thirty-four who has been over there said in about nine seconds that it is the only one anybody over there has ever offered him either.**

**Nobody at that counter took it up and nobody at that counter put it down, and the man of about thirty-four who has been over there said in about nine seconds that this was the first sentence anybody had said to him in about nine years that fitted the thing.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that if it fitted the thing then somebody should put it on something, and the man of about thirty-three said he had looked for about four days and that there is no box on anything this city sends out for the difference between two things that sound the same.

---

**And what they did instead, at about half past three, is the ordinary end of an afternoon in that building.**

He took a sheet and wrote the nine words at the top of it and then sat and looked at it, and about nine minutes later he wrote one word underneath them, which was *prepared*, and then he crossed it out, and then he put the sheet in a drawer with about nine other sheets in it.

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that a drawer is the only place in this city where a sentence like that has ever been kept, and that about nine of them are in drawers in about four buildings.

**Nobody at that counter asked how many of the nine she meant and nobody at that counter has asked since.**

**Nobody at that counter asked to see the drawer and nobody has asked since, and about four people at that counter have said that the drawer is the closest thing this city has to a place for that sentence.**

---

A woman of about thirty-eight asked him, at about twenty past four, whether those nine words would have helped him in a room over there, and he said in about nine seconds that nobody over there has ever asked him anything, so that the difference does not arise on that side of the border.

**Nobody at that counter asked him what the difference would be if it did arise, and about four people at that counter have said since that they would have asked him and did not.**

**A man of twenty-two was at that counter from about four and was asked nothing at all and said one thing, and that one thing was the whole of what he had.**

He said that a washer that fits four ways will do one job properly and three badly, and that he can tell anybody in that room which of the four is the proper one in about nine seconds, and that he cannot tell anybody how to pick it, and that there is no way of writing the picking down so that a person could do it without him.

**Nobody at that counter asked him a second question about it and nobody at that counter has asked him one since.**

Nothing in this stretch of days is signed by him and on that Tuesday he was not the one any sheet was read to, and what he said at that counter is everything he has ever had to say about anything.

---

**The rest of that Tuesday, after about two hours at that counter, was about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A curtain rail in a hall in the same place had been fitted with the wrong length of rail, so that one end of the curtain had about four inches of travel and the other end had none at all.**

He took the rail off, took about four inches out of it, re-centred it, and refitted both carriers.

**Forty-four pounds. He told the man that a rail that is the wrong length is a curtain that is gathered on one side and jammed on the other, and that about four rails in that hall are like that, and that one of them has been reported as a curtain that will not shut for about four years.**

---

**A gasket on a pump in the same building had been made up in a material that does not take that fluid, so that it had gone soft and started to weep inside the pump casing.**

He took the old one out, cleaned the faces, fitted a gasket in a material that takes it, and ran the pump and looked at it again.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a soft gasket is a gasket that was fitted because it was the one on the shelf, and that about four pumps in that building have been done that way, and that one of them has been reported as a pump that needs packing for about four years.**

---

**A tray under a machine in the same yard had been set about nine inches off the low point of the floor, so that whatever came out of that machine went past the tray and on to the floor.**

He took the tray off, set it where the low point is, and levelled it on the floor.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a tray off the low point is a tray that catches the day somebody looks, and that about four trays on that yard are set like that, and that one of them has been reported as a machine that leaks for about four years.**

---

**A washer on a bolt in the same place had been fitted hard against the face of the plate with no room behind it, so that the plate could only ever have gone on one way and it had gone on the other.**

He took the washer out, moved it behind the plate, and refitted the bolt with about nine threads showing.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a washer hard against the face is a plate that has been assembled backwards and been made to work, and that about four bolts on that yard are like that, and that one of them has been reported as a plate that works loose for about four years.**

**Those four came to a hundred and ten pounds on that Tuesday, exact.**

**Half past six on that Tuesday found the fourth of those four rooms shut with a woman of about thirty behind the door, and the eighth of eight lying on a shelf behind her at three hundred and seventy-two days.**

**Nobody in this city has ever put a question to her about that page, and nobody is going to. What is on that page is not given here.**
