# Chapter 864 — The One Sunday Of These Days, A First Floor Over A Line In Saltmarket Four Doors Along From The Room With The Book In It, And About Nine People Standing At A Counter From About Noon Until The Shutter Came Down At About Two, And The Whole Of It Was That One Trade In This City Which Opens On Nothing But A Saturday And A Sunday Was Told A Week Before It Is Going To Be Asked About The Thing It Does

**On the only Sunday of these days a trade on a first floor in Saltmarket stopped taking orders at about noon and about nine people stayed on that first floor until the shutter came down at about two. What was left of that Sunday was a counter and about two hours of work in a yard.**

---

Four names went onto the sheet for that Sunday, four dates with them, the last of the four taken at about half past one, and at about two that shutter came down.

**Those four converted shop units off that service road are one thousand five hundred and eighty-one days, two hundred and twenty-five weeks and six days. The fifteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on the board on two nails is one thousand three hundred and ninety-six days, a hundred and ninety-nine weeks and three days. The front of one page written out in nine hands stands at one thousand one hundred and forty-seven days, a hundred and sixty-three weeks and six days, and not two of the nine have been laid beside each other.**

---

**And no number was said in any room in this city on that Sunday and no count was announced anywhere, because this city says its number on a Wednesday of every fourth week and there is no Wednesday inside that Sunday.**

A woman of about thirty-four who has been over a border and came back was in the first of those four rooms and said in about nine seconds that this city shuts on a Sunday and that four hundred people do not, and that this is a fact about shutters and not about anybody.

**Nobody in that room said anything back and nobody in that room has said anything about it since.**

---

**The trade is on a first floor over a line in Saltmarket, four doors along from the room the green-covered book stands in, and the man who owns it is about forty-seven and he has been about nine years.**

He came in at about nine that Sunday and traded until about noon the way he trades every Sunday, and at about noon he shut the inner door and stood at the front of the counter with about nine people.

**Nobody in that room had asked to be there. Four of the nine came because they trade there and five of them came because a woman of about forty-four who works at that counter told them on the weekday that there would be something said.**

---

**What was said, at about five past twelve, took about nine seconds, and it was said by a man of about thirty-three who writes the wording on this city's forms, and it was not a question.**

He said that this room is going to be asked, in a week, about the thing the people on that first floor do, and that the asking is going to come from a body one person under what it was, and that nobody in this city is going to tell the man of about forty-seven which day.

**Nobody in that room asked which day it is going to be and nobody in that room has asked since, and the man of about forty-seven said in about nine seconds that he has known for about nine days.**

---

**He said in about nine seconds that he does not intend to stop, and that a man who closes on the Saturday because of something said on the Saturday has told whoever said it whatever they wanted to hear.**

**Nobody in that room said whether that was pride and nobody said whether it was fear, and about four people in that room have said since that they did not need to know.**

And then the man of about forty-seven said the one thing he wanted said, and he said it once, and nobody in that room argued with him about it.

He said that if it goes down on anything then it goes down as the road and not as his name.

**Nobody in that room asked him why and about four people in that room have said since that they would have asked him why and did not.**

A woman of about forty-four took a sheet out of her bag, wrote the road on it, did not write his name on it, and put it in his hand at about half past twelve, and nobody in that room said one word about the doing of it.

The five who had come because she told them stood at the back and about four of them left before he put the shutter down, and one of those four said in about nine seconds at the door that this is a thing you do to a man and not a thing you do for him.

**Nobody in that room said that was a reason to have done it differently and nobody said it was not.**

**Nobody thanked her for writing it and nobody thanked him for taking it, and neither of them said anything at all when it was done.**

---

**And that was the whole of it. Nothing was asked of the man of about forty-seven on that first floor and he answered nothing, and nobody admitted anything to anybody, and nothing was forgiven by anybody, because there was nothing that anybody had done.**

He asked one question before he went back behind the counter, and he asked it of the man of about thirty-three rather than of the room, and it took about nine seconds.

He asked whether anybody in this city is going to come and stand here on the day, and the man of about thirty-three said in about nine seconds that no, and that the room has never sent anybody anywhere, and that this is not a thing it does.

**Nobody in that room said that ought to change and nobody in that room said that it should not, and the man of about forty-seven nodded and went back behind the counter.**

He went back behind the counter at about twenty to one and traded for about forty minutes and then put the shutter down at about two.

**Nobody in this city has told him what the answer is going to be and nobody has told him what the answer is going to be asked with, and about four people on that first floor have said since that they do not know whether they have helped him or frightened him.**

---

**After that shutter came down, the rest of that Sunday was about two hours at a counter and about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A gate spring on a loading bay in the same place had been set so heavy that the gate had been lifted off its top hinge twice, and both times it had been put back with the spring turned down.**

He took the spring off, turned it to the weight the gate actually wants, greased the pin, and worked the gate through about nine times.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a spring turned down to save an arm is a spring that gets lifted off, and that about four bay gates on that yard have one turned down, and that one of them has been reported as a gate that will not stay on for about four years.**

---

**A ratchet strap on a load in the same yard had been round a box with nothing to stop the box working out, so that the strap went slack the moment the van moved.**

He re-routed the strap round the frame of the box, took up the slack, and proved it with a pull.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a strap round a box instead of round its frame is a strap that has never been tested, and that about four loads on that yard are done that way, and that one of them has come apart on a road for about four years.**

---

**A shackle on a lifting eye in the same place had been made up with a screw pin rather than a bolt, and the screw pin had backed out about half a turn.**

He took it out, put a bolt through it, put the split pin back in, and proved the eye with the load it takes.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a screw pin in a lifting eye is a pin that walks out under load, and that about four eyes on that yard have one, and that one of them has been reported as a lift that came down for about four years.**

---

**A pallet in a store in the same building had four broken stringers under it and was being used with about two sheets on top of it and no weight on it at all.**

He took the load off it, broke the pallet down, and put a sound one in the same place.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a pallet with four stringers gone is a pallet that looks like a pallet, and that about four stacks in that store are on that kind, and that one of them has been reported as a floor that has moved for about four years.**

**Eighty pounds, exact, is what the four of those came to.**

**At half past six on that Sunday the fourth of those four rooms was shut and a woman of about thirty was sitting behind that door, and the eighth of eight was at the back of that room at three hundred and seventy days.**

**No one has asked her anything about it and nobody is going to. What is on that page is not given here.**
