# Chapter 862 — A Thursday, A Counter Where Orders Were Taken For A Trade That Opens On Nothing But A Weekend, And A Woman Of About Forty-Four Who Works At That Counter And Who Has To Decide On Her Own Whether To Carry A Thing Along A Corridor A Week Before The Day It Matters, And A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Has Been Over There And Done It Twice Standing At That Counter Not Being Able To Say Whether It Is Safe

**On that Thursday the only counter in this city that takes orders for a trade which does not open until the morning was taking orders as usual, and one of the people taking them had a week in front of her and had nobody to ask. About two hours of the day were a bench and a counter.**

---

Nine names went onto that Thursday's sheet, and a date against every one of them, the ninth of those dates written at about ten to five, and by about ten the shutter was shut.

**The card standing on end beside that first door, in a rail that takes two, is one thousand five hundred and eighty-two days, two hundred and twenty-six weeks to the day. The twelfth of the nineteen ruled lines on the board on two nails is one thousand four hundred and ninety-eight days, two hundred and fourteen weeks to the day. The thirteenth is one thousand four hundred and forty-nine days, two hundred and seven weeks to the day. The fourteenth is one thousand four hundred and fourteen days, two hundred and two weeks to the day.**

**The fifteenth is one thousand three hundred and ninety-three days, a hundred and ninety-nine weeks to the day. The nineteenth and last of those lines is one thousand two hundred and seventy-four days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks to the day. The hold on nine crates and on the floor they stand on is one thousand two hundred and eleven days, a hundred and seventy-three weeks to the day.**

---

**And nothing was said out loud on that Thursday about what anybody in this city is going to do, and nobody went up a stair that evening, because a Wednesday of every fourth week is when this city says a number and that Thursday was not one of those days.**

The trade is on a first floor over a line in Saltmarket. From Monday to Friday the door is shut and the lights are off and the only thing that happens on that first floor is a shutter and a counter and an order book. On the Saturday and the Sunday the lights are on from about seven and the man who owns it is standing behind the counter himself.

**Nobody in this city can find him on any other day of the week and nobody in this city has tried very hard, because a shopkeeper who is only in his shop at a weekend is a person whose weekend has a different name from everybody else's.**

---

**A woman of about forty-four has worked at that counter for about nine years and she is the only person there on a weekday, and on that Thursday she had a decision in her and no procedure for it.**

She said in about nine seconds that a man of about thirty-four came in on the Wednesday and told her, in a doorway, that her employer was going to be asked about the thing he does, and that nobody in this city is going to tell him, and that she has to decide by herself whether to say it to him.

**Nobody at that counter asked her what she was going to do and about four people who came in through that door over the rest of that day have said since that they would have asked her and did not.**

She said in about nine seconds that a person told a thing in a doorway finds out about it afterwards.

She said that if she says it at the counter on the Saturday morning then he will have about nine customers behind her and a person in a doorway on a Wednesday is a person who has got the whole of the weekend to sit with it.

**Nobody at that counter said that was a good arrangement and nobody said it was a bad one, and the order book stayed open and about nine people traded that Thursday and one of the nine was told nothing at all.**

---

**And then she said the other half of it, which was about a piece of paper, and it took her about nine seconds.**

She said that if it goes down on anything then it goes down on a sheet and the sheet goes into his hand, and that a thing nobody is holding is a thing that did not happen.

**A man of about thirty-four who has been over there and done it twice was standing at that counter at about half past three and heard all of it, and he said in about nine seconds that he has been told a thing like that before.**

He said that he was told it once, on a Friday, by a person who was being kind, and that he went on doing it on the Saturday and the Sunday and the Monday and for about two years after that.

**Nobody at that counter asked him whether it was safe and he said in about nine seconds that he cannot say whether it is safe, and that he has been asked that by about four people and that he has given about four different answers and that none of the four was a guess.**

---

**And about four people in that city had already worked out the same thing about him and had not said it, and one of those four was the woman of about forty-four.**

She said in about nine seconds that she is not going to ask him again, and that she had worked out from the way he stands at that counter that he is somebody who has been told and gone on doing it.

**Nobody at that counter said that was a reason to say nothing to her employer, and nobody said it was a reason to say something, and the sheet she had in her bag stayed in her bag for the rest of that Thursday.**

She said afterwards, at about half past four, that she had decided to write it down and put it in his hand and that she is not going to be talked out of it, and that she is also not going to be thanked for it, and that a person who does a thing because of what follows it afterwards is not doing the thing.

At about half past four she took the sheet out of her bag and looked at it and put it back, and a woman of about thirty-eight who was in that shop at the time said in about nine seconds that she had done the same thing four times in about nine days.

**Nobody in that shop asked the woman of about forty-four what the sheet said and nobody in that shop has asked her since, and about four people in that shop have said afterwards that they would have asked and did not.**

About nine people traded at that counter that Thursday and about four of them asked her the same question, which is whether the lights are on at the weekend, and she said yes to all four of them in the same tone.

**Nobody in that shop said anything about what is going to be asked and nobody in that shop has been told, and the shop shut at about six the way it shuts on every weekday.**

**Nobody in this city has thanked her and nobody in this city is going to.**

---

**A man of twenty-two was at that counter at about four and was asked nothing at all and said one thing about a fitting.**

He said that the eye of the hinge on that door has been packed with about two coats of paint at some point, so that the door now hangs a very small amount lower on the latch side than it did, and that about four years ago it hung true.

**Nobody at that counter knew what to make of that, and nobody behind it came back with a second question.**

He signs nothing in this stretch of days, and on that Thursday no sheet was put up to him, and he has said since that a hinge painted shut holds the door at whatever height the last coat of paint decided.

---

**The rest of that Thursday, after about two hours at the counter, was about two hours in a fourth district.**

**Two joints on a duct run on the roof of a works in the same place had been made up with a sealant that went chalky, and both of them were pulling apart at the corners.**

He cut both out, took the sheet back to bright metal, banded them properly and smoke-checked the run.

**Thirty-eight pounds. He told the man that a chalky joint on a roof is a joint that lets go in the first hard week, and that about four runs on that roof are sealed like that, and that one of them has been reported as a ceiling that stains for about four years.**

---

**A bracket under a shelf in a store in the same yard had been welded to a length of angle iron instead of to the upright, so that the shelf moved with the angle and not with the frame.**

He cut the bracket off, ground both faces, welded it to the upright, and packed the angle so it cannot move on its own any more.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a bracket on the wrong member is a shelf that has been moving for years and everybody has blamed the shelf, and that about four brackets in that store are like that, and that one of them has been reported as a shelf that has been levelled twice for about four years.**

---

**A bolt on a gate in the same place had been fitted with the washer on the wrong side of the plate, so that the head stood proud and could be caught by anything going past.**

He took the bolt out, turned it over, refitted it with the washer under the head, and cut the burr off the plate.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a proud bolt head is a bolt head that gets knocked off by a lorry wing, and that about four gates on that yard have one, and that one of them has been reported as a gate that will not stay shut for about four years.**

---

**A pull cord on a roller blind in the same building had lost about nine inches of its length over about nine years, so that the blind stopped about nine inches above the sill it was supposed to reach.**

He took the blind down, unhooked the cord, let about nine inches back through the clutch, and rehung it.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a cord that stops before its stop is a cord somebody has tied off to save reaching, and that about four blinds in that building have been done, and that one of them has been reported as a blind that does not close for about four years.**

**What those four came to on that Thursday was ninety-two pounds, exact.**

**Behind the shut door of the fourth of those four rooms a woman of about thirty had been sitting since half past six, and the eighth of eight was lying on a shelf at the back of that room at three hundred and sixty-seven days.**

**Nobody in this city has ever asked her a thing about that page, and nobody is going to. What is on that page is not given here.**
