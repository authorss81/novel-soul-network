# Chapter 858 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six And About Two Hours Of That Counter, And A Woman Of About Sixty-One Who Came In At About Half Past Four With A Card About The Size Of A Playing Card In Her Handbag And Who Put It On The Zinc And Said Nine Words, And Who Was Not Thanked And Was Not Asked To Stay, And Who Said That She Is Not Going Because She Was Not Asked To Go

**The woman who said about nine words standing up in a room on the Wednesday came to a counter on that Friday at about half past four and put a card on the zinc and left it there, and said nine more words on the way out. About two hours of that day were that counter. Nobody thanked her and nobody asked her to stay.**

---

Nine names and nine dates on that Friday's sheet, the ninth of them taken at about ten to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The seventeenth of the nineteen lines on the board on two nails is one thousand three hundred and forty-four days, a hundred and ninety-two weeks to the day. The post at the end of the corridor is one thousand one hundred and twenty days, a hundred and sixty weeks to the day. The separation, one line in one box and about sixteen months old, is nine hundred and fifty-two days, a hundred and thirty-six weeks to the day.**

---

**And she had been in that shop once, about four years ago, to have a door closer looked at, and nobody in the shop had seen her since, and about four of the people who work there recognised her and about five did not.**

The bench took him until two. There were about nine callers and the first eight of them came in before four.

**She came in at about half past four with her handbag on her arm and did not sit down, and she put a card on the zinc about the size of a playing card, and the card had pencil marks on one side of it and nothing at all on the other.**

She said that this is hers and that it has been hers for about nine years, and that it is not minutes and it is not a record of anything, and that she has never shown it to anybody and that she is showing it now because she is going.

**Nobody at that counter asked what the marks were and nobody at that counter asked to count them, and about four people in that shop have said since that they wanted to and did not.**

A woman of about thirty-eight who works at that counter said, in about nine seconds, that the marks are in a hand and that a hand like that is not anybody's handwriting to do with, and that she would not know where a person would keep such a thing.

---

**And the woman of about sixty-one said one more thing about the card, and it took about nine seconds, and it was longer than nine words and it was about herself.**

She said that in about nine years nobody has ever asked her whether a room is going to be there next week, and that she had to be the one who knew, and that a person cannot be asked a question and cannot answer it either, and that the card is what she did instead of asking.

She said that there is no form in this city for asking a person that, and that she has looked, and that about four people in this city have looked with her and did not find one.

**Nobody at that counter said anything about that and nobody at that counter said whether it was a fault in this city or a fault in her, and she did not ask them to.**

A man of about thirty-three, who was in the shop at about five for a length of cable and had not come about any of it, said in about nine seconds that a card with a mark on it for every week is a thing somebody made so that a room could not be quietly stopped, and that he is not saying that was wrong.

**The woman of about sixty-one looked at him for about nine seconds and said nothing, and about four people in that shop have said since that she was waiting for him to say the second half of it, and he did not.**

---

**And at about twenty to five she said nine words on her way to the door, and nobody at that counter said anything back.**

She said: I am not going. Nobody asked me to go.

She said it to the room rather than to anybody in it and she said it while she was already moving, and then she went out into the street and shut the door behind her with her hand.

**Nobody at that counter said she should come back, and nobody at that counter said they were sorry, and nobody said one word to her after the nine words and about four people in that shop have said since that they would have said something and did not have it.**

The card is still on the zinc. It is on the docket under the counter with the pencil marks uppermost, and it has not been put in a book, and the docket under that counter is not a book and is not kept by anybody.

---

**And the standing body, which is not the same thing as a sitting and meets on Wednesdays whatever the fourth week does, has one chair at it that nobody has moved and nobody has filled, and the man of about thirty-three has not been asked to take it and has not offered.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that a body of nine that loses one is a body of eight, and that an eight is not a quorum and that nobody in this city has written down what a quorum is for a standing body, and that this is the fourth thing in about nine days that this city has no form for.

The man of about thirty-three said in about nine seconds that a body of eight can still meet and did meet on the Wednesday, and that it did not do anything, and that he was not going to write that down either.

**Nobody in that shop said whether an eight was allowed to meet and nobody has said since.**

---

**A man of twenty-two was at that counter for the whole of the afternoon and was not asked anything and did not say one word to the woman of about sixty-one, and he has said since that he worked out during the nine words that he could have asked her something and would not have been one of the four she came to see.**

He said that she came to that counter to put a card down and not to be asked anything by a man of twenty-two, and that about four people in this city have spent about nine years learning what a person looks like when they are about to be asked something, and that he knows what it looks like and did not do it.

**Nobody in that shop knew what he was talking about and nobody asked him.**

---

**And the rest of that Friday was about two hours of work in a fourth district.**

**A roller on a wash-down line in the same yard had run with a groove worn down one side of it only, so that the water came off it in a fan on one side and the other side stayed wet.**

He took the roller out, turned it in its journal, faced the groove all round with a hard material, and bedded it back in.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a roller grooved on one side is a roller that has been turning the same way for about four years, and that about four rollers in that yard are running without being turned, and that one of them has been reported as a line that washes unevenly for about four years and has had the brushes changed twice.**

---

**A sump in the same place had a bar put across the top of it and left there, and about nine inches of silt had built up behind it.**

He took the bar out, rodded the sump through, dug the silt out by hand, and left the grating clear and fitted.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a bar across a sump is a dam that somebody will blame on the weather, and that about four sumps in that yard have had a bar or a pallet put across them, and that one of them has been reported as a drain that has backed up twice in about four years.**

---

**A step at a doorway in the same building had a nosing and a grating set with the grating running the wrong way across it, so that a foot went between two bars and found the edge.**

He took the grating up, turned it a quarter, re-bedded it on fresh clips, and made the nosing flush with the frame.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a grating across a step that runs the wrong way is a step that catches a heel, and that about four doorways in that building are laid that way, and that one of them has been reported as a slippery step for about four years and has had a mat put over it.**

---

**A tap in a washroom in the same place had a spindle of the wrong length in it, so that it had to be wound almost fully to shut and then it leaked.**

He took the tap apart, found a spindle about a quarter of an inch too long, and put in the one that belongs to it with new washers.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a spindle a quarter too long is a tap that will not close and then will drip, and that about four taps in that building have had a spindle put in by somebody in a hurry, and that one of them has been reported as a dripping tap for about four years and has had the head packed twice.**

**That Friday came to ninety-five pounds, exact, which is the whole of the day and not a penny of it.**

**At half past six the fourth of those four rooms had its door shut and the woman of about thirty behind it, and the eighth of eight lay on the back shelf at three hundred and sixty-one days.**

**No question about that page has ever been put to her by anybody in this city, and none is going to be.**
