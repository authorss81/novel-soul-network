# Chapter 857 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Hired First Floor Over A Warehouse In A Second District Where About Nine People Turned The Nine Things Into Nine Things Somebody Has To Do, And Where A Man Of About Thirty-Three Asked About Each Of Them In Turn And Got An Answer About A Different One Four Times, And Where Nobody In That Room Said The Thing That Was True, And Where A Man Who Is Under A Review He Agreed To Without Reading It Asked For His In Writing

**Nine things somebody has to do were written out on that Thursday in a hired first floor over a warehouse, and about nine people spent an hour on them one at a time. A man under a review he agreed to without reading it was handed one of them and asked for it in writing with a date on it. Nobody thanked anybody in that room all afternoon.**

---

Nine names and nine dates on that Thursday's sheet, the last of those dates a little after four, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The card standing on end in the two-place rail beside the door of the first of the four rooms is one thousand five hundred and seventy-five days, two hundred and twenty-five weeks to the day. The twelfth of the nineteen lines on the board on two nails is one thousand four hundred and ninety-one days, two hundred and thirteen weeks to the day. The thirteenth is one thousand four hundred and forty-two days, two hundred and six weeks to the day.**

**The fourteenth is one thousand four hundred and seven days, two hundred and one weeks to the day. The fifteenth is one thousand three hundred and eighty-six days, a hundred and ninety-eight weeks to the day. The nineteenth, which is the last line anybody uses, is one thousand two hundred and sixty-seven days, a hundred and eighty-one weeks to the day. The hold on nine crates and on the floor beneath them is one thousand two hundred and four days, a hundred and seventy-two weeks to the day.**

---

**And the room was hired for an hour on the Thursday afternoon, and about nine people came, and one of the nine was a man who is under a review he agreed to without reading it and has not asked what the review says.**

The nine things somebody has to do were written out on a sheet of paper by a man of about thirty-three in about nine minutes, and the sheet has nine lines on it and no heading and no date on it.

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that a list of things somebody has to do is a different object from a list of things that is true, and that the first one can be done on a Thursday and the second one cannot be done at all.

The man of about thirty-three said that was the first sentence anybody had said to him in about nine days that he had written down, and that he had not put it on the sheet.

---

**And he went down the nine, and about four people in that room answered him, and four times out of nine the answer was about a different one.**

The first is to print the nine sentences on the top of a sheet, and a woman of about thirty-six said in about nine seconds that that is done and was done on the Monday before last and there is a sheet with nine lines on it in a first district.

The second is to get a copy of that sheet in front of a person in this city, and about four people in that room said that this is the one that has been happening for about nine days without anybody deciding to do it.

**The third is to get a copy of it to a town two days of bus travel away, and he asked, in about nine seconds, who pays for the bus, and about four people in that room answered him about the printing.**

The fourth is to find somebody over a border who would read it out loud, and he asked who that person would be, and a man of about thirty-four who owns four vehicles said in about nine seconds that there is no such person in this city and has not been since before the spring, and that he has looked.

The fifth is to ask the three people who wrote it down by accident what they wrote, and a woman of about thirty-one said in about nine seconds that that can be done on a foot and takes an afternoon, and that about four people in this city would not be glad to be asked.

The sixth is to put a name and a room into the ninth line in place of a landlord's, and he asked which room, and nobody in that room said a word for about nine seconds, and a man of about thirty-one said at last that there is no room in this city that anybody would put on a form for that.

**The seventh is to get the nine forms reprinted in one order, and a man of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that it is a plate, and that a new plate is about nine months, and that the man who sets it is not going to be asked to hurry by anybody in this city.**

The eighth is to find out whether a name written out in full will go in the space at the foot, and a woman of about thirty-five who had been in three rooms said in about nine seconds that she had done it at home three times with a scrap of paper and that the answer is no, and that she is the only person in this city who has done it and that nobody asked her to.

The ninth is to write down who asked for the nine and when, and that one took about nine seconds and was done.

---

**And four times he got an answer about a different thing, and he asked each of the four a second question, and the second question was always longer, and after the fourth one he stopped asking.**

A woman of about thirty-one said afterwards that she watched him stop, and that it was at about half past four, and that he put the pen down on the ninth line and did not pick it up again that afternoon.

He said afterwards that he had four answers that were about something else and that he had understood all four of them, and that he had understood them about nine minutes before he had understood that he had four.

**Nobody in that room said which four they were and nobody in that room has said since, and the sheet of nine went into an envelope at about half past four and the envelope is not in a book.**

---

**A man of about forty-six who is under a review he agreed to without reading it had the second one, which is to get a copy of that sheet in front of a person in this city, and he asked for it in writing.**

He said in about nine seconds that he will do the second one, and that he wants it in writing with a date on it, and that he has been in post about four years on a review he agreed to without reading it and has not asked what the review says and is not going to start now.

He said in about nine seconds that the difference between the two is that the review was agreed to in a room with a form in it and this was agreed to in a corridor with a sheet of paper, and that he can tell the difference between those two things and has about nine years of practice at it.

**Nobody in that room said that was a reason to do it and nobody said it was a reason not to, and a woman of about thirty-eight wrote his request on a card and dated it and gave it to him, and nobody thanked her.**

He said one more thing, which was that he is not resigning from anything and that nobody in this room should write down that he is, and that he has heard people write things down about other people in about nine days and that he is on one of those pages himself.

---

**And a man of twenty-two was in that room and was not asked anything and said nothing until about half past four, and what he said then was about the four answers.**

He said that four times out of nine he got told something true about a different question, and that this is what happens at a counter every day of his working life, and that about four times in nine a person comes in with one fault and is told about another one because the other one is in the book.

**Nobody in that room said that was a fault in the counter and nobody in that room said it was a fault in the person, and he said it was a fault in the order of the book and nobody answered him.**

He is not the person who signs anything in this stretch of days, and the card with the date on it was not signed by him and was not put in front of him.

---

**And the rest of that Thursday was about two hours of work in a fourth district.**

**A set of weights in a test house had been left off a beam for about four years, so that everything weighed on that beam was a little heavier than it should have been and nobody had noticed because it had always been that way.**

He had the set down, checked each one against the reference set, found two that were out, and had them re-adjusted and put back.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a weight left off a beam is a beam that is wrong every day for about four years, and that about four beams in that house have sets that are never taken down, and that one of them has been out twice and has been blamed on a machine.**

---

**A sluice gate in a yard in the same district had a spindle packed solid with grease and had been stiff since before the spring.**

He took the stuffing box apart, cleaned it out, packed the spindle with the material that belongs in it, and worked the gate through about nine times with a bar before he let it stand.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that grease packed into a spindle is grease that has turned to a paste, and that about four gates in that yard have their spindles packed all over, and that one of them has been reported as a stiff gate for about four years and has been forced with a bar twice.**

---

**A stop valve in a plant room in the same place had been fitted with the handwheel on the discharge side, so that shutting it off put the pressure on the seal instead of taking it off.**

He took the valve out, turned it end for end, refitted it, and left the old stud holes plugged.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a stop valve the wrong way round is a valve that tightens when a person shuts it, and that about four valves in that plant room are fitted that way, and that one of them has been reported as a valve that will not close for about four years and has had the gland packed twice.**

---

**A conveyor drive in the same building had a belt tracked to one side and running on the roller edge, and had been adjusted twice by moving a tail roller instead of the drive.**

He took the belt off, squared the pulleys, set the tail roller back where it belonged, and ran the belt half an hour to bed in.

**Thirty-eight pounds. He told the man that a belt on the roller edge is a belt that has been tracked twice in the wrong place, and that about four drives in that building have been adjusted at the tail, and that one of them has had a belt replaced twice in about four years and has been reported as a drive that will not hold a line.**

**That Thursday came to a hundred and nineteen pounds, exact, which is the whole of that Thursday.**

**She was behind a shut door in the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, with the eighth of eight lying on the shelf behind her at three hundred and sixty days.**

**No one has asked her one question about that page and nobody is going to ask her one.**
