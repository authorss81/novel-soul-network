# Chapter 854 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six And About Four Hours Of Callers Standing At That Counter, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Five Who Has Sat In Three Rooms Like The One Above A Line In Saltmarket And Who Says So, And Who Has Written The Width Of A Box On The Back Of Her Own Hand In Pencil, And A Clerk Who Writes It On The Back Of A Docket Because Nobody In This City Has Ever Measured It

**The last room was the Wednesday before this, the Thursday in between was a day nothing in that building opened to anybody, and this Friday had about nine callers at a counter, one of them from three rooms like the one over that line. Nothing in that shop had measured anything in about nine years.**

---

Nine names and nine dates went on that Friday's sheet, the ninth of the dates at about ten to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The seventeenth line of the board on two nails is one thousand three hundred and thirty-seven days, a hundred and ninety-one weeks to the day. The post at the end of the corridor is one thousand one hundred and thirteen days, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks to the day. The separation, which is one line in one box and has been for about sixteen months, is nine hundred and forty-five days, a hundred and thirty-five weeks to the day.**

---

**And there was no room on the Thursday between the last one and this one, and nobody in that building has said why, and about four people who use those stairs have said privately that a Thursday is a day the people who come up them do not come up.**

This was the first evening anybody in that building had had since the Wednesday. The woman of about thirty was at her chair in the fourth of the four rooms off that service road at half past six with the door shut, and the eighth of eight stood on the shelf behind her at three hundred and fifty-four days.

He was at the counter from two until about six. Nine callers came in, eight of them carrying something with them, and one of them not.

---

**The one who did not was a woman of about thirty-five, and she sat on the stool at the end of the counter at about half past three and put a folded sheet of paper on the zinc and said she had not come about the paper.**

She said she has been in three rooms over about nine days, and that in all three of them there was a sheet on a table, and that in all three of them the sheet was about the size of a folded sheet of writing paper, and that at the bottom of it there was a box.

**She said the box was the same width in all three, and that she has put her own name out flat on a scrap at home in each of three weeks and could not get it in.**

She said that on the Sunday before last she wrote that width on the back of her own hand in pencil, and that she was showing it to him and not to the room, and that it is about the width of a thumb and a bit more than a thumb.

**Nobody at that counter asked her which three rooms, and about four people in that shop have said since that they wanted to and did not.**

---

**A man of about thirty-eight who was waiting on a part asked her, in about four seconds, why a person measures a box.**

She said, in about nine seconds, that she signs about nine things a year and signs them in whatever room she happens to be standing in, and that nobody has ever told her how much room the thing has, and that a person who is going to put their name to something is entitled to know the size of the space before putting it there.

**The man of about thirty-eight said that was the first sensible thing he had heard anybody say in about nine days, and then went back to his part. Nobody at that counter agreed with her and nobody disagreed.**

A woman of about twenty-six who works at that counter said in about nine seconds that the box is not big and never was, and that everybody who signs anything in this city signs in a space that size, and that the space is the same on the supply notes and the work contracts and the school places, and that she does not think anybody chose it.

The woman of about thirty-five said that she does not think anybody chose it either, and that this is why she is here with a pencil mark on her hand and not with a complaint.

---

**And the clerk wrote it down, and nobody asked him to, and he has said since that it took about nine seconds and that he did it because the shop keeps a docket under the counter and a docket is a thing a person can be shown afterwards.**

What he wrote was this: the width of the box at the foot of that sheet, the width of a thumb and a little over, and the number of rooms in which it had been seen to be that, which is three.

**That is the first time anybody in this city has measured that box and written the measurement down. It is in pencil on the back of a docket and it is not in a book and it is not on a form.**

The woman of about thirty-five said in about nine seconds that she would like it to stay on the docket and not go anywhere else, and the clerk said it would stay on the docket.

She said good, and then said in about nine seconds that she is not going to be one of the people who says this in a room, because she has been in three rooms and each of the three was about nine people and about four of them had something at stake, and that she is not one of the four and would like it noticed.

**Nobody at that counter asked her which four she meant, and nobody asked her anything else.**

---

**And at about five a man of about thirty-three came in for a length of cable and stayed for about nine minutes, and the two of them did not know each other and did not speak.**

He said, to the clerk and not to her, that he now has eight of the nine on a sheet and that the ninth is a name and a street, and that he has to find out what order the nine go out in, and that a list of nine read in one sequence makes a different thing of itself than the same nine read in another.

He said in about nine seconds that the order they will go out in is the order the forms were printed in, that nobody in this city chose that, and that it was settled in about nine years by about four men in a print works in a third district who were setting a school place and did not think about the other eight.

**Nobody in that shop said that was a reason and nobody said it was not, and the clerk wrote the order of the nine on the back of a second docket and did not know why he was writing it and wrote it anyway.**

The woman of about thirty-five watched him do that and said one sentence, which was that if it is going to be read out in that order then the ninth one is the one about saying no, and that it is last, and that this city always puts the one about saying no last.

Nobody in that shop answered her.

---

**A man of twenty-two was at that counter for the whole of the afternoon and was not asked anything and said almost nothing about any of it, and the woman of about thirty-five spoke to him once.**

She asked him, in about four seconds, whether the width on the back of her hand was the width of the thing he had been looking at for about a month.

He said yes, and that he had worked out in a corridor about a month ago that a space the width of a thumb will take a signature laid down and will not take a name written out, and that he has not had a way to say that to anybody who prints anything.

She said, in about nine seconds, that he could have said it to her, and he said that she had not been in the room.

**That is the only exchange of any length between those two, and neither of them has repeated it.**

---

**Once that Friday's counter had cleared, there were about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A cupboard door in a workshop in the same building had two hinges whose screws had been set into the end grain of the stile instead of into the rail.**

He took the door off, filled the old holes, opened mortices in the rail, and refitted the hinges so that the screws went into the rail.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a hinge screwed into end grain is a hinge that works until the wood dries, and that about four doors in that building are hung on their stiles, and that one of them has a door that has dropped about half an inch and has been reported as a hinge that has worn for about four years.**

---

**A gas locker in the same yard had a hasp welded to the leaf that opens away from the frame, so that the door could not be secured.**

He cut the hasp off, welded a new one on the other leaf, dressed both faces, and ground the old weld back and painted it.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a hasp on the wrong leaf is a door that is not shut whatever anybody reports, and that about four lockers in that yard have the same hasp, and that one of them was counted as secure in a check about four years ago and has not been opened since.**

---

**A run of cable tray in the same building had a joint left unsupported for about nine feet at the point where a wall had come out.**

He cut the tray back, fitted a new joint and two brackets into the masonry, and re-hung the run on its original line.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that nine feet of tray with no support under it is nine feet that will sag when it is loaded, and that about four runs in that building cross a wall that has been taken out, and that one of them has been reported as a cable pulled tight twice and has been blamed on the cable.**

---

**A rising main in the same place had a gland packed with thread, which had stripped the thread on the main itself and let it weep into the cupboard below.**

He took the gland off, cleaned it, cut a fresh thread, refitted it with the packing it should have had, and left the cupboard dry.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that thread in a gland is a filler and not a seal, and that about four risers in that building are packed with thread, and that one of them has put water into a cupboard twice in about four years and has been reported as a leak from above.**

**That Friday came to eighty-eight pounds, exact, which is the whole of that Friday.**

**She was at that chair in the fourth of the four rooms by half past six, the door of that room shut behind her, and the eighth of eight lay on the shelf at three hundred and fifty-four days.**

**No person in this city has asked her one question about it, and nobody is going to.**
