# Chapter 860 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six And About Two Hours At That Counter, And A Man Of About Thirty-Three Who Went To A Laundry On A Road In A First District And Asked A Woman Of About Forty-One One Question And Was Refused In About Four Seconds, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Decided On That Tuesday To Ask One Man One Question On A Thursday In The Week After Next, And Neither Of Those Two Things Was Settled

**A man who drafts this city's forms went to a laundry on that Tuesday and asked a woman one question about what she wrote in her own book about nine years ago, and she refused in about four seconds and stopped trading with him for the rest of that morning. About two hours of that day were a counter with callers standing at it.**

---

Nine callers on that Tuesday's sheet, the ninth of the nine entered at about ten to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The four rooms off that service road are one thousand five hundred and seventy-six days, two hundred and twenty-five weeks and one day. The card standing on end in the two-place rail beside that door is one thousand five hundred and eighty days, two hundred and twenty-five weeks and five days. The twelfth of the nineteen lines on the board on two nails is one thousand four hundred and ninety-six days, two hundred and thirteen weeks and five days.**

---

**And this is what the sixteen days added up to, in a form nobody in this city can file: a sheet of nine things that somebody has to do, four of which cannot be done, and a woman who has left a standing body, and a box whose width is in pencil on the back of a docket, and nine uncollected answers about a working week on the back of a sheet.**

Nobody in this city has written that list down anywhere, and about four people in this city could write it and about nine of them have not, and the standing body has no way of being told it because there is no form for being told a thing.

**And the absence that is under all of it is this one, and it is the first thing in about nine days that a man of twenty-two has said out loud to anybody: there is no procedure in this city for asking a person whether they would accept being told what they were agreeing to stay inside of.**

He said it in about nine seconds to a counter with about nine callers at it, and about four of them heard it, and nobody answered him and nobody has answered him since.

---

**The man of about thirty-three went out at about eleven in the morning to a road in a first district where a woman of about forty-one keeps a laundry, and he asked her one question, and it took him about four seconds to get to it.**

He said that he has been told by about four people that about nine years ago she wrote four words into her own book while she was counting what came in on a Thursday, and that he is not going to ask her what the four words were, and that he is going to ask her whether she wants to be asked anything at all about it.

**She said no in about four seconds and the reason was about the asking and not about the four words.**

She said that the book is hers and that it is on the shelf behind her counter, and that she has told nobody what is in it and does not want to be told what it means, and that a person who is not asked anything is not a person who has agreed to anything, and that about nine years of writing something down in her own book because she had nowhere else to put it is not a thing to be asked about by a stranger with a list.

**Nobody in that shop said one word to her about any of it, and about four people in that shop have said since that they wanted to say something and did not.**

---

**And then she stopped trading with him, and that is what it cost, and it cost her and it cost him and neither of them has said so out loud.**

She took his money and gave him his change and would not look at him for the rest of that morning, and at about half past twelve she said, in about nine seconds, that he can leave her counter and come back on a Monday like anybody else, and that this is not about the question and is about a counter.

**He left at about ten past one and did not say anything on his way out and nobody in that shop said anything to him on his way out.**

He said afterwards that he had four of the nine on his sheet and that the fifth of the nine is to ask the three people who wrote it down by accident what they wrote, and that he has now asked one of the three and has been told to come back on a Monday, and that he is not going to be able to write the fifth one down as done.

**Nobody in this city has been told what that woman wrote and she is not going to be told what it means, and the asking has stopped, and it stopped because she said four words about the asking and not nine words about the four words.**

---

**And at about four in the afternoon he made one decision, and it took about nine seconds, and it is about a Thursday in the week after next.**

He is going to a first district, to a counter where a man of about thirty-three sits on Wednesdays, and he is going to ask that man one question, which is whether the width of the space at the foot of that sheet was ever chosen by anybody or whether it is only the size it is because it has always been the size it is.

He said that he cannot ask it sooner, and the reason is that the man of about thirty-three has four of the nine on his sheet and that about nine people in this city have told him in about nine days that a man in that position will say yes to a question asked on a bad day and no to the same question asked on a good one.

**Nobody in that shop said it was a good day and nobody said it was a bad one, and a woman of about twenty-six said in about nine seconds that it is a Tuesday and that the man of about thirty-three is not in that counter on a Tuesday, and that he would have to wait and that the waiting is free.**

He said that he knows the waiting is free and that the free part is the reason he has booked it two weeks out, and that about nine people in this city have given him something in about nine days that they should not have and that he would rather turn up having given nothing.

---

**A man of twenty-two is not the person who signs anything in this stretch of days and signed nothing on that Tuesday, and the fifth of the nine is not on any sheet in this city in anybody's hand.**

He is not the person any form was put up to on that Tuesday and was not the person the laundry woman was asked anything by, and he was in the shop and heard about it afterwards from a woman of about twenty-six who had been in the laundry and had not been asked anything herself.

He said that a person who says no in about four seconds to being asked anything at all has told you the whole of what there is to know about what she wants, in four seconds, and that about four people in this city have spent about nine years not managing that.

**Nobody at that counter took his side and nobody at that counter took the other one, and about four people in that shop have said since that they did not know what to do with it and have not done anything with it.**

---

**The balance of that Tuesday, after about two hours at the counter, was about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A meter box in a yard in the same district had its door hung the wrong way round, so that it opened towards the wall and could not be opened at all with the wall in the way.**

He took the door off, turned the hinges over, rehung it so that it opens away from the wall, and plugged the old screw holes.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a meter door that opens into a wall is a box that has been read once in about four years by taking the wall off, and that about four boxes in that yard are hung that way, and that one of them has a door that cannot be opened and has been reported as a locked box for about four years.**

---

**A sign post in the same place had been set in a hole that had been backfilled with the arisings dug up about nine feet away, so that it had about two inches of movement at the head.**

He dug the post out, took the compacted stuff out of the hole, backfilled with what belongs there, and re-set the post and packed it.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a post set in its own arisings is a post that moves in a wind, and that about four posts on that yard stand in made ground, and that one of them has been reported as a leaning post for about four years and has been taken out and put back twice.**

---

**A switch on a machine in the same building had been wired for a different machine and had never been tested, and the label on the case above it was for the machine that should have been on that circuit.**

He took the switch out, wired it to the machine it belongs to, and tested it about nine times under load.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a switch that has never been tested is a switch that stops nothing, and that about four machines in that building have a switch that was never tested, and that one of them has been reported as a machine that will not stop for about four years.**

---

**A hinge on a gate in the same yard had been hung with the pin head uppermost, so that the gate dropped about half an inch every time it was shut and lifted itself off the pin.**

He took the hinge apart, turned it, greased it, and refitted it with the pin standing up the way a pin stands.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a hinge with the pin head uppermost is a hinge that lifts itself off, and that about four gates in that yard have a hinge like that, and that one of them has been reported as a gate that will not stay where it is shut for about four years.**

**That Tuesday came to eighty-five pounds, exact, which is what those four came to between them.**

**At half past six the door of the fourth of those four rooms was shut with a woman of about thirty behind it, and the eighth of eight lay at the back of that room at three hundred and sixty-five days.**

**No person in this city has asked her one question about that page and nobody is going to. What is on that page is not given here.**
