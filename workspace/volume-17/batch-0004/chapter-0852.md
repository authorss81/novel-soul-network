# Chapter 852 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Counter In The Afternoon Where A Woman Of About Thirty-Eight Asked A Man Of About Thirty-Three What This City Would Have To Put On A Form In Order To Tell Somebody In Advance What They Were Agreeing To Stay Inside Of, And The Answer Was A List Of About Nine Things And Every One Of Them Was On A Form Already, And Nobody In That Shop Could Decide Whether That Was Good News

**The question was asked at about half past four on that Tuesday at a counter in a first district, and it was answered off the top of a man's head in about nine minutes, and the answer was a list of about nine things. About four callers had been in and out of that shop before it was asked. Nobody in that shop knew what the list was for.**

---

Nine names on that Tuesday's book and nine dates against them, the last of the dates at about ten to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The four rooms off that service road are one thousand five hundred and sixty-two days, two hundred and twenty-three weeks and one day, and the card standing on end in the two-place rail beside that door is one thousand five hundred and sixty-six days, two hundred and twenty-three weeks and five days.**

**The twelfth of the board on two nails is one thousand four hundred and eighty-two days, two hundred and eleven weeks and five days. The four rooms have run four days ahead of that card for about as long as anybody has been keeping either of them.**

---

The bench took him until two. A woman of about twenty-six came in at about ten with a carrier bag of work and wanted the same thing she had wanted on the two previous Mondays, and he did the same thing he had done on the two previous Mondays, and neither of them said a word about it.

He was at the counter from two until about six and about four of the callers were about a thing that was not the thing they brought in with them.

---

**The question came at about half past four, and it came from a woman of about thirty-eight who has asked it in five rooms in about nine days and had not been answered in any of the five.**

She said: what would this city have to put on a form, in order to tell somebody in advance what they were agreeing to stay inside of.

She said it to the man of about thirty-three, who was at the counter for about two hours that afternoon because his own work had brought him to this district and he had said he would stand at a counter and be asked one question.

**He did not go and get anything. He answered it where he was standing, and he took about nine minutes, and he went through it in the order the forms are printed.**

What it is called is on a works order. It has been on a works order since before the spring and it is the first line under the heading and nobody has ever read it as a description of anything.

What it is for is on a supply note, in a space headed with the word for what a person is getting, and about four supply notes a week go out of a counter in a second district with that space filled in by the person being supplied and not by anybody in the office.

How long it has been done is on a hire agreement, as a date at the bottom of the second page, and the date on that page is the date the agreement was signed and not the date anything began.

Who does it and how often is on a work contract, in a column headed with a day, and about four contracts in that building have that column filled in for a Tuesday and a Thursday and nothing else in the week.

What a person gives up is on a school place. What a person gets is on a loan agreement. What it costs is on a treatment consent and it is the only one of the nine that anybody in this city has ever put a full stop at the end of.

**What happens if a person stops is on a membership card, in a sentence that says a member may withdraw at any time, and about four people in this city have read that sentence and about none of them have been in a room where it was read out.**

---

**And the ninth one is on a tenancy schedule, and he was slower on that one, and the reason he was slower is that it is a name and a street and not a box.**

He said: who a person goes to if they want to say no is on a tenancy schedule. There is a landlord's name on it and there is a street on it and there is no box, and there is no box because a tenancy is not a thing a tenant is expected to complain about.

**Nobody at that counter said anything for about nine seconds, and then the woman of about thirty-eight said, in about nine seconds, that this city has been telling people in advance for about nine years and has been calling it nine other things.**

She said that a person filling in a school place is told what the school place is and a person signing a loan is told what the loan is and a person taking a supply is told what the supply is, and that nobody has ever put those nine sentences on one sheet, and that this city has not been failing to say it in advance.

She said that this city has been saying it in advance in about nine rooms a week in about nine different buildings and has never once put the nine in the same place.

---

**A man of about fifty-two who keeps a register at a counter in a second district came into that shop at about five for a torch and said no in about four seconds, and the reason was about the sending and not about the ink.**

He said that nine things on nine forms is nine things this city sends to its own, and that not one of the nine has ever gone out of this city to anybody who did not come into it, and that putting them on a sheet does not change who reads it.

He said that he has said this in three rooms in about nine days and that he is not going to say it in a fourth, and that he came for the torch and would like to stop talking about it.

**Nobody in that shop said he was wrong and nobody in that shop took his side, and about four people in that shop have said since that they heard what he was actually saying and about four have not.**

The man of about thirty-three said in about nine seconds that the answer to a question about a form is a sentence and not a destination, and the man of about fifty-two said that is the first sentence anybody has given him about a form that is not about the ink, and put the torch on the counter and paid for it and left.

---

**And nobody in that shop could decide whether the list was good news, and the man of about thirty-eight said so first.**

She said she came in ready to hear that this city has nothing, and she went away with nine sentences, and she did not know by the time she reached the door whether she was carrying a thing that could be used or a thing that could only be talked about.

The man of about thirty-three said in about nine seconds that the nine are on a sheet in no order at all, and that he wrote them in the order the forms are printed because that was the only order in front of him, and that whoever reads them out will read them in that order whether anybody chose it or not.

He said that this is the thing to be careful of, and that a list of nine read in one order is a different list from the same nine read in another, and that about four people in this city have been choosing this order by accident for about nine years.

**Nobody at that counter said the order was the wrong order and nobody said it was the one to use, and he tore the corner off the sheet and kept the eight lines he had written, and said he would bring the ninth line on the Monday.**

---

**And on that same Tuesday, in a second district, a man of about thirty-four took a wall map off a wall, put it in a drawer, and wrote on a run sheet that a run finishes at a town and not at a stretch of country.**

He did it in about nine minutes and he did not tell the man who keeps the map, because he does not know the man who keeps the map, and about four people in that building know it was done and the man who keeps the map is not one of them.

**The man who keeps the map has looked at that wall every working day for about a month and has not asked anybody why there is nothing on it.**

---

**A man of twenty-two was at that counter from about four until about six and was not asked anything and said nothing, and the woman of about thirty-eight asked him nothing, and he has said since that the only one of the nine he could check for himself was the sixth.**

He said that the sixth is what a person gets, and that it is the only one of the nine that a person can go and ask somebody else about, and that nobody in this city has ever asked anybody else what they get.

**Nobody at that counter asked him how he knew which one was the sixth and he did not say.**

---

**What was left of that Tuesday was about two hours of work in a fourth district.**

**A set of steps on a stair had a tread nosing loose at the top and a handrail bracket packed with mortar where the brickwork had been broken out badly.**

He took the nosing up, found the batten had been cut off square, fitted a new length, packed the bracket out of the mortar and drilled it into a course of brick, and refitted the nosing.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a nosing on a batten cut off square is a nosing that has nothing behind it, and that about four sets of steps in that building are like it, and that one of them has a bracket that has been reported as a wobbly rail for about four years and has been grouted twice.**

---

**A gate latch in the same yard had been packed with grease inside the case and worked only when the case was warm.**

He took the case apart, cleaned the whole of it out, packed the pivot with the grease that is for a pivot, and left the rest of it dry.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that grease inside a latch case is grease that gets onto the tongue and makes the tongue worse, and that about four gates in that yard are packed all over, and that one of them is reported as a gate that is stiff in the morning and has been adjusted twice without being opened.**

---

**A length of conduit in the same building had a draw-wire cut through it and both ends of the wire left standing out of the couplings.**

He pulled the wire out, cut the two ends square, dressed the mouths of the conduit, and drew a new wire through on the same route.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a cut draw-wire left standing is a wire that will cut a cable drawn after it, and that about four conduits in that building have been drawn by somebody who left the wire, and that one of them has had a run pulled through it twice in about four years and nobody has asked which drawing did it.**

---

**A hopper door in the same place had a catch filed flat, so that the door had come to hang on its hinge and the man closing it had been holding the corner up with his knee.**

He welded a new catch plate on, squared the door in the frame, and set the hinge pins so the door closes on the catch and not on the frame.

**Thirteen pounds. He told the man that a catch filed flat is a door that has no latch left in it, and that about four doors in that yard have been filed at the catch, and that one of them is described as a door that will not latch and has been taken off its catches twice by two different gangs.**

**That Tuesday came to eighty-nine pounds, exact, which is the four of them added.**

**At half past six the woman of about thirty sat down at her chair in the fourth of those four rooms and shut the door, and the eighth of eight stood behind her at three hundred and fifty-one days.**

**No person in this city has asked her a single question about that page, and the question is not going to be put by anybody here either.**
