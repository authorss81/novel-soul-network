# Chapter 853 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where The Room Was Open And No Sitting Was Held In It And No Number Was Said, And About Nine People Came Up Of Whom About Four Came About A Wording, And A Woman Of About Sixty-One Sat Near The End And Was Not Asked Anything Until She Was Not Asked That Either, And A Man Of About Thirty-Three Said In About Nine Words That Being Told In Advance Is Not Being Asked First

**The room was open on that Wednesday evening and about nine people came up, and about four of them came about the wording rather than about anything else. Nothing was counted in that room. A woman of about sixty-one sat near the end of a bench and was not asked a single question for the whole of it.**

---

Nine names and the same number of dates went onto that Wednesday's sheet, the last of those dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The eighteenth of the board on two nails is one thousand two hundred and eighty-one days, a hundred and eighty-three weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against the wall at the north end is one thousand one hundred and sixty-nine days, a hundred and sixty-seven weeks to the day. What is still owed on the page in front of him is one thousand two hundred and fifty-three days, a hundred and seventy-nine weeks to the day.**

**Behind the chair of the woman of about sixty a place has stood empty since a week in the spring. It was empty on that Wednesday and nobody in that room asked her about it and this page sets no figure against it.**

The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the whole of the room. **It did not move on that Wednesday and on no page of this stretch of days is the person who moves it named.**

---

**Nothing was counted in that room, and no count is announced anywhere in this movement, and the reason is a rule of four weeks and not a decision anybody took.**

A count is said at a sitting. A sitting is a Wednesday of every fourth week. The Wednesday of that week was not that Wednesday, and the next one is a week off, and about nine people who came up that stair were not told a number.

A woman of about thirty-four who is in that room said in about nine seconds that she had checked, and that the last Wednesday of that kind was three weeks before, and that the three weeks had not felt like anything to her at all, and that she had thought about that for three weeks.

**Nobody in that room said the four weeks were a good arrangement and nobody said they were a bad one, and about four people in that room had not known there was a four-week anything until about half past six.**

---

**And about four of the nine had come about the wording, and one of those four was a woman of about sixty-one who had come about nothing and had not said so.**

She has been on the standing body for about nine years. She keeps no minutes of it. She has a handbag on the floor between her feet and both hands in her lap, and from about half past six until about nine she did not speak, and about nine people in that room knew who she was and about four of them had not seen her in that room before.

She had not read the nine lines. Nobody handed them to her and she did not ask for them, and at about twenty to eight a man of about thirty-four put the sheet on the bench beside her and she moved her hand off it.

**Nobody asked her whether she had read it, and nobody asked her what she thought of it, and nobody has asked her since.**

---

**The nine lines were read out again on that Wednesday, in the same flat voice, by the same woman of about thirty-six, and the room heard them a second time and did not hear anything new in them except the second time.**

And at about ten past seven, which is the hour the same sentence has been said in that room twice in about a month, a man of about thirty-three said in about nine words: being told in advance is not being asked first.

**Nobody in that room agreed with him and nobody in that room disagreed with him, and about four people in that room have said since that they heard it and have not been able to say what they did with it.**

A man of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that if that is the shape of it then the nine lines do not touch it, because the nine lines tell a person what a room is and say nothing whatever about the room to the person, and that is not a fault in the nine lines and it is not a fault in the person either.

The man of about thirty-three said that was the first useful thing anybody had said about the nine lines in about two days, and that he had it on a sheet and was not going to put it on a form.

A woman of about thirty-one said in about nine seconds that a thing which cannot go on a form is not a wrong thing and is a missing one, and that this city has no way of telling those apart, and that the register of four that nobody counts is the same kind of thing with a smaller number on it.

**Nobody in that room said that out loud in those words and the sentence above is the closest any of them came to it.**

---

**And then at about a quarter to eight a woman of about sixty-one spoke for the first time, and nobody had asked her, and she said about nine words and then sat back.**

She said: nobody has ever asked me whether I would say yes.

She said it to the nine lines on the bench and not to anybody in the room, and then she put her hands back in her lap, and about nine people in that room heard it and about four of them wrote nothing down.

**Nobody in that room asked her a second question and nobody in that room has asked her one since, and a man of about thirty-four said afterwards that it is the only sentence anybody in this city has said to a standing body in about nine years that was about the standing body and not about the four hundred.**

Nobody thanked her for saying it. Nobody in that room said it made a difference, and a woman of about thirty-eight said in nine seconds that she did not know what would be done with it and was not going to be the one to decide.

---

**And the man of twenty-two was in that room and was not asked anything and did not say anything until about nine o'clock, and what he said then was about a bench.**

He said that the bench she was sitting on has four legs and that three of them were on tile when it was made and one of them is about half an inch off, and that in about nine years of that bench nobody has ever put their feet where the leg is wrong, and that he does not know why and has thought about it for about two days.

**Nobody in that room knew what to do with that and nobody asked him a second question.**

He is not the person who signs anything in this stretch of days, and he was not the one anybody handed that sheet to, and he has said since that a room that has never been asked a question has a bench in it that people sit on without noticing, and that this is not a finding about anybody.

---

**The balance of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and two hours in a fourth district.**

**A trolley wheel in the same yard had been running on a dry pin bearing against a steel rim and had cut a groove the width of a thumb into the rim.**

He came out the pin, turned the rim true, dressed the groove, and refitted the wheel with a bearing and a shackle in it.

**Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a dry pin on a steel rim is a groove that gets deeper and never gets shallower, and that about four wheels in that yard run on plain pins, and that one of them has a rim that has been re-faced twice and has been described as a wheel out of round for about four years.**

---

**A door closer on a stair in the same building had its arm set to the wrong side of the leaf, so that the door was being driven against its own stop and the closer had been adjusted twice to compensate.**

He took the arm off, reset it to the correct side, backed the adjustment off to where the door closes and stays shut, and let it go through about nine times.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a closer on the wrong side is a door that has to be told twice to shut, and that about four closers in that building are on the wrong side, and that one of them has been reported as a door that will not latch for about four years and has had its speed reduced three times.**

---

**A roof light over a workshop in the same place had been wired into a joint above the head and had nothing at all at the frame.**

He cut it out of the joint, ran a proper tail to an earth at the frame, and left the joint with nothing in it.

**Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a light fed from a joint is a light that has no return, and that about four lights in that building are fed off a joint, and that one of them has been reported as a light that hums in wet weather for about four years and has had a lamp changed twice for it.**

---

**A gate lock in the same yard had been fitted with its keep on the side the gate closes against, so that the gate had to be pulled to shut it.**

He took the lock off, turned the case round, refitted it so the keep is on the side the bolt comes into, and left the old screw holes plugged.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a keep on the closing side is a gate that shuts backwards, and that about four gates in that yard have it that way, and that one of them has been reported as a gate that is hard to shut for about four years and has been taken off its hinges twice by two different gangs.**

**That Wednesday came to a hundred and two pounds, exact, which is the four of them added together.**

**In the fourth of those four rooms a woman of about thirty has had her door shut since half past six, and the eighth of eight has lain behind her at three hundred and fifty-two days.**

**Nobody has put one question to her about it and nobody is going to. What is on that page is not given here.**
