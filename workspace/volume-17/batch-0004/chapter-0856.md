# Chapter 856 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where The Sixty-Second Sitting Was Held And A Count Was Said Once In About Nine Seconds In A Woman's Face, And Where A Sheet Of Nine Things Was Read Out In The Order The Forms Were Printed And Came Back Off The Table With The Space At The Bottom Of It Empty, And Where A Woman Of About Sixty-One Said In About Nine Words That She Was Leaving And Was Not Going To Put It In Writing Because Writing Would Want A Signature

**The sixty-second sitting was held on that Wednesday in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, and about nine people were in that room, and a sheet of nine things came in a plain envelope. A count is said at a sitting and this was a sitting, and it is said once, in a face, in about nine seconds, and nobody in that room had heard it said in nineteen years.**

---

Eleven names and eleven dates on that Wednesday's sheet, the last of those dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Line eighteen of the board on two nails is one thousand two hundred and eighty-eight days, a hundred and eighty-four weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against the wall at the north end is one thousand one hundred and seventy-six days, a hundred and sixty-eight weeks to the day. What is still owed on the page there is one thousand two hundred and sixty days, a hundred and eighty weeks to the day.**

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the whole of the room and it did not move on that Wednesday, and its mover is named on no page of this stretch of days.**

---

**There is a Tuesday between the Monday and this one, and nobody in this city is going to account for it, and the room had stood empty on it in the way rooms stand empty on most Tuesdays.**

The last time about nine people stood in that room for this purpose it was four weeks before. There has been one room since then and it was in a second district on the Monday, and about nine people were in that one too.

A woman of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that the four weeks are the only thing anybody in that room has ever been able to rely on, and that she had come on the Monday expecting a Tuesday and got a Wednesday, and that she had not minded and had noticed that she had not minded.

**Nobody in that room said anything about the four weeks and nobody asked her to say more about the Tuesday.**

---

**The sheet came in a plain envelope and the envelope was not opened until about ten past six, and the woman of about thirty-six read the nine lines he had brought her, and she read the ninth one last, and the ninth one is who a person goes to if they want to say no.**

**Nobody in that room stopped her and about four people in that room wrote nothing at all while she read it.**

A man of about thirty-four who owns four vehicles said in about nine seconds that the ninth one is the one they would all have put first if anybody had let them put it first, and the man of about thirty-three said in about four seconds that nobody had let them, and did not finish the sentence, and did not finish it later.

---

**And then, at about half past six, the woman of about sixty said the count once, in about nine seconds, into the face of the room, and that is the way it is said and it has been said that way for nineteen years.**

She said: sixty-seven, of which sixty-two correspond.

She said it in about nine seconds and she did not say it twice and she did not explain it and she did not say what any of the two figures was for.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and then a man of about thirty-four said nothing at all, which is what a man in that room has done every four weeks for about nineteen years, and the woman of about sixty said nothing back.**

**The book in the green binding stood at sixty-seven lines when that room opened and it stood at sixty-seven lines when the shutter came down at about ten, and the tin beside it was not opened that evening and was on seventy-three.**

---

**And at about twenty to seven a woman of about sixty-one stood up where she had been sitting since half past six, and she had not been asked anything, and she said about nine words.**

She said: I am leaving. I will not put it in ink.

She said it standing, and she said it to the nine of them and not to the woman of about sixty, and then she said that writing it would want a signature at the bottom of it and that she is not going to sign anything.

**Nobody in that room asked her to stay and nobody in that room asked her to sit down and nobody in that room said anything about her at all for about nine minutes after she had finished.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that there is no procedure for this, and that the absence of one is why a person has to stand up in a room to do it, and that she had not known that until she watched it happen.

Nobody in that room answered her and about four people in that room have said since that they would have liked to and did not.

---

**A man of about thirty-three asked her one question and it was not the question anybody in that room wanted to ask, and she answered it and left.**

He asked whether the nine lines are still going out, and she said that they are not hers to stop and that they will go out on Monday because nobody in this city has anywhere to stop them, and that this is why she is standing up.

**Nobody in that room said the ninth line should be taken off the end of the list and about four people in that room had wanted to say so for about nine days and did not say it.**

She picked up her handbag at about half past seven and left by the stair, and the ninth chair did not move while she was doing it.

---

**And a man of twenty-two was in that room and was not asked anything and signed nothing, and is not the person who signs anything in this stretch of days.**

The sheet of nine went back into the plain envelope at about ten to eight with the space at the bottom of it empty, and about nine people watched that happen and about four of them said afterwards that they had expected a name on it and that there had been a name on it every other time one of these sheets has gone round.

He said afterwards that the space at the bottom is about the width of a thumb and that a thumb is what a person puts on a line when they are not writing their name, and that this is the only measurement in that room that everybody already agreed on.

**Nobody in that room asked him where he had got that from and he did not say.**

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was the counter until about six and then about two hours in a fourth district.**

**A stair light in a passage had its switch on the landing instead of at the door, so that anybody going up had to feel for it in the dark.**

He took the switch off the landing, ran a longer cable into the door frame, and put it where a hand goes before a foot does.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a switch on a landing is a switch that cannot be found by somebody carrying something, and that about four passages in that building are like that, and that one of them has been reported as a dark stair for about four years and has had a bulb changed twice.**

---

**A bolt on a gate in the same yard had been fitted with a washer that had gone over the hole, so that the head of the bolt pulled through about half an inch every time the gate was shut.**

He packed the frame out, cut a new washer, refitted the bolt, and packed the frame again so the head sat flat.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a washer over a hole is a bolt that gets tighter every time somebody shuts the gate, and that about four gates in that yard are like that, and that one of them has been reported as a gate that will not stay shut for about four years.**

---

**A lamp holder in a workshop in the same building had been earthed to a bracket on a gas main, and the bracket had been painted over about nine times.**

He took the earth off the bracket, ran it to the conduit, and cleaned the paint back off the bracket where it had been.

**Nine pounds. He told the man that a bracket on a gas main is a bracket that cannot be an earth and that about four fittings in that building have their earths on pipework, and that one of them has been reported as a lamp that flickers for about four years and has been blamed on the lamp.**

---

**A kick plate on a step in the same place had been screwed into a hollow section, and about four of the screws had turned in their own holes.**

He filled the holes, drilled into the flange behind, and refitted the plate with a washer under every head.

**Eighteen pounds. He told the man that a screw into a hollow section is a screw that turns in its own hole, and that about four kick plates in that building are fixed that way, and that one of them has been reported as loose for about four years and has been packed with a wedge twice.**

**That Wednesday came to fifty-five pounds, exact, which is the whole of that Wednesday.**

**By half past six a woman of about thirty had her door shut on the fourth of those four rooms, and on the back shelf behind her the eighth of eight had been lying for three hundred and fifty-nine days.**

**Nobody in this city has put a question to her about that page and nobody ever is going to.**
