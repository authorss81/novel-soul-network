# Chapter 855 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where A Service Group Meets On Mondays And Which About Nine People Borrowed For An Hour, And Where A Man Of About Thirty-Three Read Out Loud Nine Things In The Order The Forms Were Printed, And Where By The Fifth Of Them The Room Had Stopped Hearing A List And Started Hearing An Order, And Where Nobody Could Change The Order Afterwards

**Nine things were read out in one room on that Monday in the order the forms were printed, and the reading took about nine minutes, and about nine people were on a bench in a room that a service group uses on Mondays and had borrowed without asking anybody. Nobody in that room knew what order they were about to be read in.**

---

Eleven names and eleven dates on that Monday's sheet, the last of those dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The four rooms off that service road are one thousand five hundred and sixty-eight days, two hundred and twenty-four weeks to the day. The sixteenth of the nineteen lines on the board on two nails is one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight days, a hundred and ninety-four weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand one hundred and thirty-four days, a hundred and sixty-two weeks to the day, and not one of the nine has been laid beside another.**

---

The bench took him until two and the counter took him until about six, and there were about nine callers and about four of them talked about the weather for a while before they said what they had come about.

**The room off that road belongs to a service group that keeps devices running, and the group is in on the Monday nights and about nine people borrowed the room for an hour and were asked by nobody whether they could.**

They were not asked by anybody because there is no procedure for asking, and a woman of about forty-three who has been in that group for about nine years said at the door, in about nine seconds, that anybody can use the room on a Monday if they clear it by nine, and that nobody has ever written that anywhere.

---

**The man of about thirty-three read the nine out at about half past six, standing at the front with the sheet in his hand, and he read them in the order the forms were printed and not in an order of his own.**

He said before he started that the order is not his and that he has worked out in about two days that the order is the whole of it, and that about four people in this city have been choosing it by accident since before the spring.

**He read the first four quickly and a woman of about thirty-one wrote them out. He read the fifth and the sixth and the seventh and by the eighth about four people in that room had stopped writing.**

A man of about forty-three said, in about nine seconds, that he had stopped writing because he could hear that the eight things in front of him were a case for the ninth, and that if the ninth is the last one it is going to be read as the last thing on a list of things this city already does.

He said that a list read once in a room is a thing about nine people agree with, and that the same nine read in another order is a different list and would be agreed with differently, and that about four people in this city have never worked out that a list has an order in it at all.

**Nobody in that room said he was wrong and nobody in that room took his side, and he wrote nothing down and has not been asked to.**

---

**And a man of about twenty-nine in that service group said no in about four seconds when he was asked whether his group would take the second item on, and the reason was about what his group is.**

He said that his group keeps devices running and does not choose, and that a group that keeps devices running cannot be the answer to what a person should be told they are agreeing to, and that putting his group's name against the second item would be a way of saying his group had chosen it.

He said in about nine seconds that he would rather be written down as not available than written down as willing, and that he has said that in about four rooms in about four years and has never once been asked to put it in writing either.

**Nobody in that room argued with him and nobody in that room disagreed, and about four people in that room have said since that he was the only person there who said the thing that was actually true about the room.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that a list of nine with eight of the items unowned is a list that will be read as belonging to somebody, and that the somebody will be whoever the form is addressed to.

---

**And he read the ninth one out last, at about ten past six, and then nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds.**

The ninth one is who a person goes to if they want to say no. He did not read the other eight out again and nobody in that room asked him to.

A woman of about thirty-one said, in about nine seconds, that the ninth one is the only one of the nine with a name in it, and that on a tenancy schedule the name is the landlord's, and that this city has never printed a name and a room anywhere that a person can go to and say no to.

**Nobody in that room said that was a fault in the nine and nobody said it was a fault in the city, and a man of about thirty-four who owns four vehicles said in about nine seconds that a name and a street is not nothing and is the only one of the nine a person could use at nine o'clock at night.**

The man of about thirty-eight said in about four seconds that a landlord is the last person in this city a person goes to at nine o'clock at night, and the man of about thirty-four said that was not a thing anybody had asked him about, and nobody asked him about it.

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three asked the room whether the order should be changed, and about four people said it should and about four said nothing, and nobody said it should not.**

He said in about nine seconds that changing it is not in his hands, because the sheet is going to a standing body on the Wednesday and the body will read it in the order it is written, and that the order it is written in is the order the forms were printed in, and that this is now the order and has been for about two days.

**He folded the sheet along a crease he had put in it himself on the Monday before, and gave the ninth line to a woman of about thirty-six to read out on the Wednesday, and kept the other eight, and neither of them asked the other for anything.**

---

**A man of twenty-two was in that room and was not asked anything and said nothing for the whole of the reading, and after it he asked one question and it was about the room.**

He asked, in about four seconds, why that room has nine chairs and a service group of about twenty-six people.

A woman of about forty-three said in about nine seconds that the group has had about nine of them for about four years and the other about seventeen are at a bench, and that nobody has ever taken the extra ones away, and that nobody in that group would know which of the nine to take.

He said that he had worked out during the reading that he could not tell which of the nine things on that sheet was the fourth one, and that he had got to the sixth before he understood that he was not going to be able to do it at all.

**Nobody in that room helped him and nobody in that room tried.**

---

**The balance of that Monday went on until about ten, and about two hours of it were in a fourth district.**

**A fan in a workshop in the same building had been belted with a belt a pitch too narrow, so that it had been running with two fingers' width of slack on a spindle that wanted about four.**

He took the belt off, found the pulley that had been swapped for a smaller one, put the correct pulley back, and belted the fan to about four fingers of slack.

**Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a narrow belt on a fan is a fan that has been belted twice to get the noise down, and that about four drives in that building are belted to a pitch, and that one of them has a pulley that has been changed twice and has been reported as a bearing for about four years.**

---

**A tap over a canteen sink had its washer fitted on the wrong side of the seat, so that it had to be wound down to shut.**

He took the tap apart, put the washer where it belongs, refitted it, and left the handle where a person expects it.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a washer under the seat instead of over it is a tap that closes downwards, and that about four taps in that building are like that, and that one of them has been described as a tap that will not shut off for about four years and has been reported as a broken washer twice.**

---

**A run of pipe lagging in the same place had been cut over a bracket and left the bracket bare, so that the bracket sweated onto the pipe every morning and the lagging soaked and came off.**

He cut the lagging back, fitted a piece of thin rubber under the bracket, re-lagged the run, and banded it properly.

**Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a bracket under lagging is a bracket that sweats, and that about four runs in that building are lagged over their brackets, and that one of them has been re-lagged three times in about four years and has been reported as a run that is wet in the morning.**

---

**An inspection lamp in a yard had been wired through an ordinary fuse in a junction that gets wet.**

He took the lamp out, ran it back to a fitting, and put a proper enclosure on the point.

**Thirteen pounds. He told the man that a lamp on a fuse in a wet box is a lamp that has been reported as unreliable for about four years, and that about four lamps in that yard are on that arrangement, and that one of them is now the third one to be moved.**

**That Monday came to seventy-five pounds, exact, which is the whole of that Monday.**

**She sat down in that chair at half past six, in the fourth of those four rooms, and shut the door, and the eighth of eight stood behind her at three hundred and fifty-seven days.**

**Not one person in this city has put a question to her about that page and nobody is going to.**
