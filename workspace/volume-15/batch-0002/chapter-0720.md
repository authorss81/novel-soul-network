# Chapter 720 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Stair In Saltmarket With A Light On The Second Landing, A Room At The Top Of It With About Four People At A Table And A Woman Of About Sixty At Her Place And A Green-Covered Book Shut On The Table And A Tin Beside It And Nobody In That Room Saying Anything At All, A Chair Hard Against A Wall With Its Back To The Room And A Clean Rectangle Of Floor Behind It, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Went Up And Came Down

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went up a stair in Saltmarket at about half past three for a light on the second landing, and there were about four people at a table at the top of it and none of them looked up, and the man of twenty-two was in that room for about eleven minutes and nobody said a number.**

---

The Wednesday carried eleven jobs with a date against each and eleven people over the counter, the last of them let in at half past five, and the shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and that had happened about nine times in a fortnight.

**The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is at nine hundred and thirty-eight days, one hundred and thirty-four weeks to the day, and the ask is at one thousand and twenty-two days, one hundred and forty-six weeks to the day, and those are two of the four figures in this page that come out as a whole number of weeks and the other two are on the bottom line.**

---

**The light on the second landing of that stair has been on the wall since the spring and it is the only light on that stair, and it came on at eight in the morning and went off at about nine at night, and about four people on that stair have mentioned it in about two years and one of them has mentioned it to anybody.**

He went up at about half past three. **There are eleven steps to the first landing and four more to the door, and the fourth one has a tread that has been replaced with a piece of plywood about nine years ago and has been loose at the front corner ever since and about four people know it and none of them has said anything.**

The light was on and it was working and it did not need anything.

He stood on the second landing with his bag at his feet for about nine minutes and then went up the four steps, because there is nowhere on that landing to stand that is not on somebody's way.

---

**The room at the top of that stair has a table, about eleven chairs, a stove, a window with a blind down, and a woman of about sixty in it who has been in that room for about nineteen years.**

**There were about four people at that table. Two of them had papers in front of them and one of them had a coat over the back of a chair. Nobody was standing up. Nobody was talking when he came in and nobody started talking because he came in.**

**The green-covered book was on the table in front of the woman of about sixty and it was shut, and it was on sixty-four lines, and the tin was on the same table about a foot from it with its lid on, and it was on seventy-three, and there was a receipt from a shop in it as a page marker, and the receipt is not hers.**

He looked at both of them for about four seconds. He did not touch either of them. Nobody in that room offered him either of them and nobody in that room mentioned either of them and about four people in that room knew what was in the book and about four of them knew what was in the tin and not one of them said a number while he was in there.

**A count is given in that room on a day when that room gives one, and about four people in that city have worked out roughly when those days are and about four of them are right and about four of them have never asked, and on this Wednesday that room gave nothing, and Marek stood in it for about eleven minutes and took away the fact that it had given nothing, and he did not write that down either.**

---

**The chair behind the woman of about sixty's chair was hard against that wall with its back to the room, and it did not move, and the place behind her own chair was empty and had been empty two hundred and ten days, which is thirty weeks to the day.**

About four people in that room had that figure before they came up the stair and about two of them knew it to the day and nobody said a word about it to anybody and nobody has asked her about it in thirty weeks and it is not on a form and it is not on the book and it is not in the tin and the only person in this city who writes day counts down as a job has it in his head and has printed it in his head and did not print it in that room.

**Nobody in that room said anything to the man of twenty-two. He asked one question, which was about the blind, and got an answer to it in about nine seconds from a man of about sixty-one, and the answer was that the cord had been cut about four years ago and been tied again with a bit of the cord, and would go again.**

"**Right,**" Marek said.

"**It'll go again,**" said the man of about sixty-one, and went back to the paper he had in front of him.

---

**He came down at about ten past four and he did not go back up, and about four people in that room did not look up when he went out, and one of them said nothing at all, and the woman of about sixty did not say anything to him and did not look up and he has not been told why and has not asked.**

**On the second landing, on the way down, he stepped over the loose corner of the plywood tread on the fourth step, and nobody has said anything about that step in about nine years, and the light on that landing was still on.**

---

**The rest of the Wednesday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and a man of about thirty-three came in with a gate brace he had pulled off a gate himself and would not go back on, and it took about nine minutes to get it to and about eleven to get it off, and he paid for the second one, and he did not get the first one back on and nobody in this city will.**

From about half past six until ten there were four people in those four rooms off that service road and one behind the three, and the woman of about thirty was in the fourth of them for the whole of it, and the chair the door is behind is hers, and the eighth of eight pages was in a ring binder on a back shelf and has been for a hundred and twenty-nine days, and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will.

---

**Three things on that Wednesday and none of them on a form.** A clean rectangle of floor behind a chair that has not been moved, and the dust line round three sides of it and not the fourth, and nobody in that city has cleaned under that chair in about nine years. **A blind cord on a window tied again with a bit of itself.** And a gate brace that a man of about thirty-three pulled off himself and would not go back on, which he has paid for twice and which nobody in this city is going to put back for him.

---

**He wanted somebody in that room to say the number.**

Not to say it to him. He wanted one of about four people at that table to look at a woman of about sixty and say, out loud, that a place behind her chair had been empty for thirty weeks, and he wanted it said because about four people in that room had the figure and had had it for about thirty weeks and a figure four people are carrying and nobody will say out loud is a figure that is being kept, and keeping it is a thing that a room does to a person, and the whole of what he wanted on that Wednesday afternoon was one of them to put it down on the table and let her see that it was there.

Nobody said it. The woman of about sixty said nothing at all in the eleven minutes he was there, and about four people said nothing at all, and he went down four steps and stepped over a loose corner of plywood and did not write it down.

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and he had turned down a call-out at about four o'clock in a third district because his own bench was free for the first time in about eleven days and he did not take the call and he did not use the bench, and that is about eleven pounds and about two hours and neither of them is in a load book.
