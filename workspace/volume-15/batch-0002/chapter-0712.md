# Chapter 712 — A Friday, A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A First-Floor Room In A Second District Where A Woman Of About Thirty-One Keeps A Counter Open Until Six And Is Asked To Stop And Stops In About Nine Seconds, The Reason She Gives Being About A Job And About A Man Of About Sixty Who Finishes At Five, And What The Room Does Afterwards, Which Nobody Says Has Changed

**A Friday was a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went to a second district at about half past two, to a first-floor room with a counter at the end of it, and about nine seconds of it were somebody saying no, and the room carried on and the way it carried on was different and nobody in the building remarked on the difference.**

---

Eleven jobs stood dated on the Friday and eleven people came over that counter, the last of them let in at half past five. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and that had happened about ten times in a fortnight.

**Seven hundred days is the separation, one hundred weeks to the day, and what sits in the post at the end of a corridor has been there eight hundred and sixty-eight days, one hundred and twenty-four weeks to the day, and neither of those two figures describes anything anybody in this city can point at.**

---

**The room is on the first floor above a shop in a second district, and it has a counter at the end of it and about nine chairs along one wall and a boiler underneath it, and the boiler is why Marek was in the cellar of that shop at half past two on a Friday.**

He had the front of the boiler off. There was a second thermostat wired to the first one, which somebody had done in about the spring, and both of them were live, and the one that was live first was the one that was wrong.

He was in about nine minutes and out in about eleven and the woman who kept the room came down the stairs twice while he was there, once for a torch and once because she had heard a noise.

**She is about thirty-one. She has kept that counter open until six for about six years, and about four hundred people have come through that door in that time, and the room has no name on the outside of it and no sign, and it takes no money and has never taken money.**

---

**The woman of about thirty-eight came in at about ten past three and asked the woman of about thirty-one to stop.**

She did not sit down. She stood at the end of the counter with her coat already on and she had about four minutes before she had to be somewhere else, and what she asked was in the plainest possible words: the district was asking every room that opened on that street to stop using the last approved sequence for the last hour of its day, because there were about nine of those rooms on that street and the district had started at the top of it on the Monday and this one was the third.

**And the woman of about thirty-one stopped.**

She said no in about nine seconds. The word was the second thing she said. The first thing she said was the word of the street.

"**Second,**" she said. "**Who wants it.**"

"**I do,**" said the woman of about thirty-eight. "**And I want it by the end of the week.**"

"**No,**" said the woman of about thirty-one, **and she said it flat and without any of the words that other people in this city have been putting in front of it, and she did not raise her voice, and Marek was under the floor at the other end of the room with a torch in his hand and about nine feet of boards between him and the two of them and he heard every word of it.**

---

**And then she gave the reason, and it took about nine seconds, and it was about a job and about a man.**

"**There's a man comes at six,**" she said. "**He finishes at five and he walks. He has come at six for about two years and if the counter's shut at six he stands in the stair, and the stair's cold, and the man who owns this floor has a key and he does not come up for a man in a stair.**"

"**Then bring him at half five,**" said the woman of about thirty-eight.

"**Then he comes at half five,**" said the woman of about thirty-one, "**and it is not the same thing and I am not going to pretend it is the same thing, and you can have the counter off the sequence at six and I will not fight you about it.**"

**And that was the whole of it. Nine seconds of the refusal, and about nine seconds of the reason, and no third thing, and the woman of about thirty-eight wrote something on a card about the size of a postcard and put it on the counter and said she would come back on the Monday and she did not come back on the Monday because the woman of about thirty-one had already done it.**

---

**Nobody thanked her. Nobody said she was right. Nobody in that room or in that building or on that street said anything at all about it, and the woman of about thirty-eight did not say it was a good answer and did not say it was a hard one and did not look like a person who has been thanked.**

**And the woman of about thirty-one went back to the counter and carried on, and the counter was shut at six on the Monday, and shut at six on the Tuesday, and shut at six for the rest of the fortnight, and about four people came at half five instead of at six and were let in, and the woman of about thirty-one opened the second door herself and stood at it, and the man of about sixty came at half five and did not comment on it, and has not commented on it, and does not know that anything was ever asked.**

**What changed is this and it changed without a word: two people were on that door from the Monday instead of one, and the second one was a woman of about twenty-two who was paid out of the room's own money and was told on the Monday morning and not before, and the room now keeps about eleven minutes more of the day than it used to, and the man of about sixty comes half an hour early and waits inside, and not one person in that building has said the word *changed* to anybody.**

---

**Marek came up out of the cellar at about twenty past three, on his knees with the front of the boiler still in his hand, and asked a question about the second thermostat, because it was a real question and there was a real answer to it, and the woman of about thirty-one told him, in about nine seconds, that whoever wired the second one to the first one had been trying to make a boiler come on twice instead of once, and that it worked, and that it cost her more in gas than it had ever saved.**

"**Who did it.**"

"**The man who did the boiler,**" she said. "**He is not a bad man. He is a man who was here in the spring and was told it was cold.**"

"**Right,**" Marek said, and went down the stairs.

**He did not say anything about the nine seconds. Nobody in that room knew he had been under the counter for them and nobody has asked him and he did not offer, and there is nowhere in this city to put a thing a man of twenty-two has overheard at the other end of a room.**

---

**The rest of the Friday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and a woman of about forty-five brought a pendant in a carrier and was told in four seconds that the pendant in the carrier was not on the circuit she wanted and bought a switch and has not been back, and a man of about thirty-three brought a lamp in a box and put it on the counter and paid for it and carried it out again and has not been back.**

There were four people in four rooms off that service road and one behind the three between about half past six and ten, and one of them was not one of the four. She is about thirty, the chair the door is behind is hers, and the eighth of eight pages has lain in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and nineteen days. It did not come out that Friday and nobody in that building said a word to her for three and a half hours, and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will.

---

**Three things on that Friday and none of them on a form anybody keeps.** A strip of tape on a door frame at about the height of a child's hand, going across the frame and about nine inches long, and no person in that room knows what it marks and about four of them have looked at it. **A second thermostat wired to a first one, both live, both working, and a bill that is larger than it was in the spring.** And a staircase with about four steps on it that have been painted about nine times and are still the same colour in the middle.

---

**He wanted the man of about sixty to have been made to come at half five.**

**Nobody asked him. The woman of about thirty-one had told the woman of about thirty-eight that the man comes at half five now and that it is not the same thing, and that was all she said about him, and Marek wanted the man to have been asked, in a face, whether half five was all right, and to have said no, and to have had to be argued with for about four minutes, and instead the man came at half five and was let in and nobody said a word to him about any of it, and Marek has not seen the man and does not know his name and has not asked and is not going to.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and the stair rail he had promised for the Saturday in a first district went to another shop, and the man who had asked for it did not tell him and did not have to.
