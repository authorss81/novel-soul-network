# Chapter 717 — A Friday, A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A Room In A First District Where About Nine People Leave Things While They Are At Work And Where A Man Of About Forty-Four Is Asked To Stop And Stops In About Nine Seconds For A Reason About A Lease And A Ninth Day And Nowhere To Put Nine Prams, A Man Of About Thirty-Three Who Asked, And Nobody In That Street Who Says The Word

**A Friday was a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went to a first district at half past two, to a room where about nine people leave things while they work, and the man who keeps it was asked to stop and stopped in about nine seconds, and neither the street nor the man of twenty-two heard a word of it.**

---

Eleven jobs with a date against each and eleven people over the counter was the whole of the Friday's traffic, and the last was let in at half past five. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and that had happened about ten times in a fortnight.

**The hardboard's seventeenth line is one thousand and ninety-nine days, one hundred and fifty-seven weeks to the day, and the column headed for the separation reads seven hundred and seven days, one hundred and one weeks to the day, and neither of the two of them is on a form and neither of them is anywhere anybody in this city could go and look.**

---

**The room is on the first floor of a building in a first district, it has a window, a bench the length of the wall, about eleven hooks and a bin, and about six hundred people in this city have left something in it.**

They leave prams. They leave bags and a child for an hour and a half while they do a shift. They leave a folded chair and go and get it at seven. **Nobody pays and nobody signs and the man who keeps it has never had a key to a cupboard of his own in it, and the door is open from six in the morning until about nine at night and has been for about six years.**

He is about forty-four. He works days. He was in the room on that Friday because the light over the bench had a fitting in it that had been in about nine years and had gone.

---

**And a man of about thirty-three came in at about half past three and asked him to stop keeping the door open past six.**

He said it in the plainest words there are, standing at the top of that stair, and he had a sheet of paper in his hand and he did not sit down.

"**The council's asked every room on this street to be shut by six,**" he said. "**And yours is the one that's open latest.**"

"**Right,**" said the man of about forty-four.

"**You can shut it at six.**"

"**I can shut it at six,**" the man of about forty-four said, **and he said it in about nine seconds and he did not argue about the six and he did not ask what the council wanted it shut for and he did not mention that about six hundred people had left something in that room in about six years.**

---

**And then he gave the reason, and it was about a job, and it was a different reason from any other reason anybody has given anybody in this stretch of days, and it took about nine seconds.**

"**I'm out on the ninth,**" he said. "**The lease isn't being done again and there's a man coming in on the tenth who's going to want it empty, and there's nowhere on this street to leave a pram. Not in a doorway, not in a passage. There's about four shops on this stretch and not one of them has got room, and the ones that have got room don't know I'm doing it and the ones that know are sick of me.**"

"**So you'll shut at six.**"

"**So I'll shut at six,**" he said. "**And the ninth is the ninth whatever the door does, and if I'd said no to you the ninth would still be the ninth and I'd have had eight more days of a thing that ends.**"

"**Right,**" said the man of about thirty-three, and put the sheet of paper on the bench, and did not say anything else, and went down the stair.

**And nobody in that street thanked him for stopping and nobody said he had done a difficult thing and nobody said anything at all, and the man of about thirty-three did not say the word and has not used it since, and the man of about forty-four has not used it either and does not know it is a word anybody would use.**

---

**What happened in that room in the following week is on this page and it is not on a form, and it happened without a single word being said about it.**

**A shuttered shop two doors down took it, from the Monday, before six, and the man who has that shop does not know the man of about forty-four and has not asked and would not have said no.** **The prams went in there from six until seven and out again at whatever time they were wanted, and about nine people who had left something in that room for about six years left it in that shop instead, and about four of them asked the man of about thirty-eight who keeps the shop what the arrangement was, and he said it was not an arrangement, it was a shop with a bit of room in it.**

**The man of about forty-four did not carry anything. Nobody asked him to and he did not offer, and he was in his own room until the ninth sweeping it, and he swept it three times over two days, and on the eighth a man of about thirty-eight next door carried that bench out into the yard and left it there, and the man of about forty-four watched him do it and said nothing.**

---

**Marek was in that room with a lamp in his hand for about twenty-two minutes and he heard all of it, and he did not say one word about any of it, and neither of the two men in that room knew he was there, because he was on a step with his head round a door and both of them were at the far end of a room the length of two buses.**

**He went in afterwards and did the fitting and the man of about forty-four paid him and said, "**That's the last thing I'll want in here,**" which was about the fitting, and Marek said it was a good fitting, and neither of them said one word about the other thing.**

He has not been back to that room. **He did not go on the ninth and he did not go on the tenth and he does not know what is in that room now and he has not asked anybody, and there is nothing on the rent book of a man in a first district that says what a man of about forty-four did in a room for about six years.**

---

**The rest of the Friday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and a man of about twenty-eight came in with a pram wheel and wanted it looked at and was told in four seconds that it could be and paid and has not been back.**

From about half past six until ten there were four people in the four rooms off that service road, one behind the three, and one other person in the fourth of them who is about thirty and whose chair is the one the door is behind. The eighth of eight pages has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and twenty-four days. Nobody in that building spoke to her for three and a half hours, nobody has apologised to her in any form, and nobody ever will.

---

**Three things on that Friday, none of them on a form.** Eleven hooks and about four of them with something on them and about seven of them bare, and the bare ones are the newer ones. **A bench the length of a wall that was on a Friday inside a room and on the following Tuesday in a yard, and the only man who carried it out of that room was a man of about thirty-eight next door, and he said the yard was where it wanted to be.** And a sheet of council paper left on a bench in a room that was about to be given up, which the man of about forty-four put in the bin, and there is no bin in this city that anybody has ever been asked to keep.

---

**He wanted the shuttered shop two doors down to have said no.**

**It did not say no, because nobody asked it, and nobody was going to ask it, and the whole of what he wanted was for the moving to be somebody else's decision and not a landlord's and not a woman's, and instead the man of about thirty-eight said it was not an arrangement and took the prams at six, and that has made the man of about forty-four the last man in the chain who nobody has asked anything, and Marek has thought about that for about four seconds on more than one day and has not resolved it.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and he had promised a woman of about forty-five a job at four in another street and did not get to it, because at about four that room had filled up with about nine people and their things and he was on a step with his head round a door, and he did not go back to that job, and she has had it done by a man from her own street for about nine pounds less than he was going to charge her.
