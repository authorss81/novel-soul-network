# Chapter 711 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Counter In A First District Where A Sheet Had Been Lying Since Tuesday And About Nine People Handled It Over An Hour And A Half, One Of Them Asking Whether It Was The Same Sheet And Getting An Answer In About Nine Seconds From A Woman Of About Fifty-Two Behind The Counter, And Nobody In That Room Asking Who Printed It

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went to a first district at about two, to a counter where a sheet had been lying since Tuesday, and about nine people handled it in an hour and a half, and one of them asked a question and got an answer from the woman behind the counter.**

---

The Thursday was carried by eleven jobs with a date against each and by eleven people over the counter, and the last of them was let in at half past five. and about four of the eleven had come about the day without knowing it. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and that had happened about nine times in a fortnight.

**Those four rooms and the one behind the three are at one thousand three hundred and nineteen days, a hundred and eighty-eight weeks and three days, and four days past them, standing on end in the rail by that door, is a card at one thousand three hundred and twenty-three days, a hundred and eighty-nine weeks to the day, and the rail takes two.**

---

**The laundry in that first district is on the ground floor of a building with a yard behind it, and the counter is eleven feet long, and there is a machine at the end of it that takes a coin and does one thing.**

He went there at about two with a switch in a bag for the extractor, which had been tripping the previous circuit for a fortnight and which the woman of about fifty-two had reported to nobody until Tuesday, when a man of about thirty-three came to look at it and told her that it was the machine and not the wiring, and she asked him how he knew and he did not answer that.

"**How long's it been doing it,**" he said.

"**Since the spring.**"

"**Then it's the machine,**" the man of about thirty-three said, "**and I'm not touching it, and you'll want somebody who'll do it twice.**"

**That is how Marek came to be standing at that counter on a Thursday afternoon at about two with a bag, waiting for a part that was in a drawer at the shop, because the part was in a drawer at the shop and not in his bag and he had not thought to bring it.**

He was not a customer. There was nothing on the counter to collect and no number to call. He stood at the end of it by the machine with his bag on the floor and waited, and after about nine minutes he moved to the other end of it, and after about another nine he moved back.

**And the sheet was lying on the counter about two feet from where he was standing, face up, and about nine people handled it over the next hour and a half.**

---

The first was a woman of about twenty-eight with a bundle under her arm who read it standing up and put it back and did not take it.

The second was a man of about forty-one who read it and turned it over and read the back, which is blank, and put it back and said to nobody, "**There's nothing on the back.**"

The third was the man of about thirty-three, who had been in at ten that morning, and who read it in about four seconds and said, "**I've read that,**" and put it back at an angle about two inches out of square, which is the sort of thing a man does when he is thinking about something else.

**The fourth and the fifth handled it at the same time and between them turned it over twice and folded it once and did not read it, and the woman of about fifty-two said, "**You can have one, you know. It's not a paperweight.**"**

The sixth was a boy of about fifteen who read the whole of it including the box at the bottom and then asked the woman of about fifty-two whether it was true.

"**I don't know,**" she said. "**I didn't write it.**"

**Nobody at any point in that hour and a half asked her whether it was true, except that one boy, and he asked her and not the sheet, and she said she did not know, and that was the end of it and it stayed the end of it.**

---

**And then a man of about thirty-six read it right through, twice, and put it down square, and asked his question to the counter in general.**

"**Is this the same sheet.**"

**He asked it the way a person asks a thing they have already decided the answer to, and there were about four other people in the shop and two of them looked up.**

The woman of about fifty-two said, in about nine seconds:

"**Same four steps. Different line under them, and there's a box at the bottom that wasn't on the last one. That's the whole of what's changed. It's been here since Tuesday morning and it came through the letter slot behind me in a bundle with a great many others in it, and I put them on the counter because a bundle in a letter slot gets taken away in the morning.**"

"**Right,**" said the man of about thirty-six.

"**That's the sheet,**" she said. "**That's not a thing I know.**"

**And that was the whole of it. She had not looked up. She had said two sentences to a man who did not buy anything and had gone back to the machine, and the man of about thirty-six had not asked her anything else, and about four people in that shop had heard both sentences and none of them had asked her a question, and none of them has asked her anything since.**

---

**Nobody in that shop that afternoon asked who printed it. Nobody asked where it came from, nobody asked who wrote it, nobody asked whether it was the only one, and nobody asked whether anybody knew, and the woman of about fifty-two was asked none of those things and did not volunteer any of them, and about four people in the room could have asked and did not, and the reason none of them asked is that the answer would not have changed what any of them was doing, which is a thing about about four people in a room and is not a thing about the sheet.**

The man of about thirty-three, who had told her it was the machine and had not touched it, did not say a word about it. The man of about thirty-six left with nothing. The boy of about fifteen did not take one, and the woman of about twenty-eight with the bundle did not come back for it.

**Marek heard both of the sentences. He was standing nine feet away with a bag at his feet that he had not needed to bring and had not been in a hurry to go back from, and nobody in that shop knew who he was or what he did or that he was listening.**

**He did not ask her anything either. He has not asked her anything since and is not going to, and there is no arrangement in this city for asking a woman behind a counter in a first district who sent a bundle of paper through her own letter slot, and there has not been one since about the spring.**

---

**The extractor came off at about half past three and Marek took the old switch out and put a new one in and it tripped again at about twenty to four, because the machine was the machine and the man of about thirty-three had been right about that and the part was not the part.**

He did it twice. The second time it held, and the woman of about fifty-two watched the second one and said, "**That's the one,**" and paid, and did not offer him anything and did not say anything else about the first one.

**He told her in about nine seconds that the machine wanted looking at before the winter and that nobody in this city would come and look at it and that she should ask the man who owns the building, and she said she had asked him in the spring, and he said right, and that was the end of the advice.**

---

**The rest of the Thursday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and about nine feet of conduit went out on a job in a first district and a man of about thirty-eight bought a length of cord and asked whether it was the right kind and was told it was the right kind, and has not been back.**

Four people were in those four rooms off that service road from about half past six until ten, and one room behind the three, and a fifth person in the fourth of them for the whole of it. She is about thirty and the chair the door is behind is hers. The eighth of eight pages has been in a ring binder on a back shelf of that room for a hundred and eighteen days, it did not come out that Thursday, and nobody has said a word to her or apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will.

---

**Three things on that Thursday and not one of them on a form.** A sheet lying face up on a counter for about two days, and a bundle that had come through a letter slot behind a counter and had been put out where people could take one instead of being taken away in the morning. **A gap in a rail that takes two and has one card in it.** And a machine that wanted a switch and wanted a part and wanted looking at before the winter, and the woman who owned the room had asked the man who owned the building in the spring and had been told to go and ask somebody, and she had said she would.

---

**He wanted, for about the whole of that hour at that counter, for the woman of about fifty-two to have been wrong.**

**She was not. The four steps were the same four steps, and the line under them was different, and the box at the bottom was not on the last one, and she had read all of it and could say what had changed in two sentences and would not say who had sent it. He wanted it to be a different sheet, and it was the same one, and the whole of what he wanted was that a counter in a first district could be a place where a piece of paper means nothing in particular and not be a place where two sentences are the answer to a question a man asks in a shop.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and he had carried a switch across a district to a machine he was not going to be able to fix on one visit, and it was in a drawer in Lattice Ward the whole of the time.

*A counter with a sheet lying on it, and an answer of two sentences.* **A sheet came into a first district in a bundle and was put on a counter where about nine people handled it in an hour and a half, and the only question anybody asked about it was whether it was the same sheet, and the answer was that the four steps were the same and the line under them and the box at the bottom were not, and that was given in about nine seconds by a woman who did not write it and does not know who did.
