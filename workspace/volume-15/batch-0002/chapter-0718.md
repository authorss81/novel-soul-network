# Chapter 718 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Door In A First District That Has Been Oiled On Its Latch And Not On Its Hinges And A Man Of About Forty-Four Who Walked Into A Room On That Street That He Had Never Been In And Asked Whether He Could Be Useful, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Did Not Write His Name On The Line In The Pad By The Till And Does Not Know Why He Did Not

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went to a first district at half past two, to a shop door that will not latch, and at about four a man of about forty-four walked into a room two doors along he had never been inside and asked whether he could be useful.**

---

The Monday brought eleven dated jobs and eleven people to that counter, and the last of the eleven was let in at half past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage.

**The four rooms, and one behind them that is not on anybody's list, are at one thousand three hundred and thirty days, one hundred and ninety weeks to the day, and the nine hand copies are at eight hundred and ninety-six days, one hundred and twenty-eight weeks to the day, and those are two of the three figures on this page that are a whole number of weeks, and the third is the sixteenth line.**

---

**The shop on that street has a door that will not latch, and it has not latched since before the spring, and the man who keeps it has been shutting it with a hand on it since before the spring, and about four people on that street have seen him do it.**

Marek went at about half past two. **The hinges are dry and the frame has been painted three times without the paint being taken off the hinge plates, and the latch is not the problem, and the person who oiled it oiled the latch and not the hinges, and the last person who oiled it did that about a fortnight ago and did it on purpose.**

He had the door on its hinges in about nine minutes and he was standing in the doorway with the door off its plate when the man of about forty-four came along the street.

---

**He was carrying nothing. He was not about forty-four in the way a man is about forty-four when he is going somewhere. He came along that pavement at about four o'clock in the afternoon, from the direction he had not come from, and he slowed at that doorway the way a man slows at a doorway, and he looked in, and there was a light on and a woman at a table, and he went in.**

The room has a table, about four chairs, a stove, a board on the wall with a list on it of things people had brought in and nobody could take away that week, and a woman of about thirty-eight in it who has been in that room for about two years.

She was about thirty-eight. She has no key to anybody else's room and nobody has asked her for one and about four people on that street have told her to send for somebody if she needs a thing done, and she has not sent for anybody, and that is the whole of what is known about it.

"**Is this the one you can come to,**" the man of about forty-four said.

"**It's a room,**" she said. "**Sit down if you want.**"

"**I don't want to sit,**" he said. "**I want to know if there's anything in it I can be useful at.**"

"**There's a thing on the board,**" she said. "**Two people can't get a table up three stairs and it's been on that board since Friday. I'm not going up with them and I can't do it myself.**"

---

**He did the table in about eleven minutes and he did it well and he did not ask for anything and he was gone in about twenty minutes, and nobody put his name anywhere, and nobody in that room knew his name, and he did not give it.**

**The woman of about thirty-eight did not thank him. He did not expect to be thanked and did not look like a man who had come to be thanked, and she said, "**Right,**" when he had finished, and that was the whole of what was said about it, and she has not mentioned it to anybody and did not mention it to Marek when Marek came back out of the shop doorway and found her in the doorway.**

"**You didn't send for him.**"

"**No,**" she said.

"**You could have.**"

"**I could have,**" she said, "**and I would have had a man in here for six hours who wanted to be useful and I have got a table on a board.**"

---

**And nobody sent him. That is the whole of it and it is about four seconds long, and about four people on that street know that a man of about forty-four walked into a room he had never been in and asked whether he could be useful in it, and about four of them have told nobody, and one of them was a man of twenty-two with a shop door in his arms, and he has not told anybody and is not going to.**

**There is no list of who comes through a door. There is no form. There is no number of anybody, and the room keeps a board of things that need two pairs of hands and the board is not a roll and the board does not have names on it and nobody has ever written a name on it, and nobody in this city has ever asked how many rooms there are where a man can walk in and be told to sit down.**

**Nobody knows how many doors the sheet is at. Two men asked him that week, in two different shops, in two different words, and he gave each of them an answer that was not a number, and neither of them asked the other one, and he did not tell either of them about the other one, and neither of them has asked since.**

---

**The rest of the Monday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and a man of about thirty-three came in and asked how many of a thing had gone out of a shop on a road off Saltmarket and was told that a number one man keeps is not the same object as a number people have taken, and that it is a membership and not a Tuesday, and he said right and bought a switch and has not been back.**

Those four rooms off that service road took four people between about half past six and ten, with a fifth room behind the other three, and one of them had somebody in the fourth who is about thirty and who sits in the chair the door is behind. Her page, the eighth of eight of them, has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and twenty-five days and did not come out, and nobody has said anything to her and nobody has apologised to her in any form, and nobody ever will.

---

**Three things on that Monday and none of them on a form.** A door in a first district that latches for the first time since before the spring, and a person who oiled its latch and not its hinges about a fortnight ago and did that on purpose. **A board on a wall with a list on it of things that need two pairs of hands, and no names on it, and no roll, and about nine of the things on it have been done and about nine of them have not.** And a pad by a till in Lattice Ward with a line ruled on it for names, and about eleven names on it for the last four weeks, and one Monday in it with no name on it at all where there should have been one.

---

**He wanted the man of about forty-four to have been sent.**

He did not want the man to have come. He wanted the whole of that man walking into a room to have been arranged — by somebody, in a face, in about nine seconds — so that it could be seen and stopped, because a thing that is arranged is a thing that can be called off, and a man of about forty-four who nobody sent for is a man nobody can ask a question about next week.

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and he did not write the man's name on the line in the pad by the till, and there is a line in that pad and it has been there since before the spring, and he has written eleven names on that line in four weeks and he did not write one that Monday, and he has not gone back and looked at the pad since, and he could not tell anybody why he did not, and he has tried about four times.
