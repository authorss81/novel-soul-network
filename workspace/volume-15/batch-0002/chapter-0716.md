# Chapter 716 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Cellar Under A Market In A First District Where A Compressor Would Not Take A Second Reading And Was Started By Hand Twice, A Technician Of About Twenty-Five Who Is Not Asked To Be Anything By Anybody And Who Does Not Offer To Be Anything, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Watched And Said Nothing About The Second One

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went under a market in a first district at about eleven in the morning, where a compressor took a reading and would not take a second one, and a man of about twenty-five started it by hand and did it again by hand, and nobody asked him to be anything.**

---

The Thursday was eleven dated jobs and eleven people over that counter, and about half past five was the last of them. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and that had happened about nine times in a fortnight.

**On the hardboard, the thirteenth line is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven days, one hundred and seventy-one weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and fifty-nine days, one hundred and thirty-seven weeks to the day, and those are two of the seven figures on this page that are a whole number of weeks and there are five more.**

---

**The market is over a cellar and the cellar is about four feet high and there are two compressors in it and one cold room, and the man of about thirty-eight who runs the market has a service book with a date and a time and a set of readings in it and has had that book since before the spring.**

Marek was there because the light over the door of the cold room had a switch on it that was not a switch, and what it was instead was a button from an old alarm, and the alarm was long gone.

He was on his back under the light for about nine minutes and the technician came down the ladder at about eleven and did not speak to him for about four minutes.

---

**He is about twenty-five. He has been doing this work for about four years and he came because the firm sent him and he has a van and a tool roll and a job sheet with nine lines on it and about four of the lines are for this building.**

**He was not asked to be anything by anybody in that cellar and he did not offer to be anything, and the whole of what he did in that cellar for about an hour and a half is work, and it is not on anybody's form, and nobody in that market has asked him a single question about any of it since.**

The compressor had run since five in the morning. It was drawing, and the suction gauge was steady where it should have been, and the man of about thirty-eight had a note in his book that said the plant was losing suction from about eleven and that it was back at about half past one, every day, for about three weeks.

"**It's not a fault,**" the technician said, when Marek asked what the fault was. "**It takes a reading and it doesn't take the second one. It needs about eleven minutes between them.**"

"**Then why hasn't it been running.**"

"**Because it needs about eleven minutes,**" the technician said, "**and the man who writes the book writes the times in it, and the man who writes the book is the man who stands there.**"

---

**And the technician of about twenty-five started that compressor by hand.**

He opened the valve, put his hand on the wheel, and brought it up in a way that took about four minutes, and he counted, and Marek could hear him counting from under a light with his back on a cellar floor, and the second reading came up when he said the number and not before.

He wrote nothing down. **He said, "**Right,**" and shut the valve, and then about eleven minutes later he did the whole thing again, and the second reading came up at the same point both times, and the second time he did not count out loud.**

**And in between those two things the technician asked Marek one question, which was whether the alarm button had been live, and Marek said it had not been live since before the spring, and the technician said right, and that was the whole of the conversation between the two of them, and it was about nine seconds long.**

---

**Nobody thanked him. Nobody said that was well done. The man of about thirty-eight wrote the times in his own book in his own hand and put the book in a drawer and said, "**It's the book,**" to nobody, and did not say what the book was for.**

**Marek was on his back under that light for about an hour and a half in total and he watched every second of the second start and he said nothing about it and he has not said anything about it since, and there are about four people in this city who would have wanted to hear about a compressor being started twice by hand in about eleven minutes and about four of them are not going to be told by him and the reason is that he was asked to do nothing in that cellar and he did nothing and he was on his back the whole time.**

---

**The rest of the Thursday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and about two of the eleven were about the week and neither of them knew it, and a woman of about forty-five asked whether a thing that took nine seconds every day could be somebody else's and was told it could and bought a switch and has not been back.**

**A woman of about twenty-four was in a second district that morning with a folder and went through about nine doors, and Marek was in a first district under a market and did not know that and was not told and has not asked.**

From about half past six until ten there were four people in the four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and a fifth person was in the fourth of those rooms for the whole of that time. She is about thirty. The chair the door is behind is hers, the eighth of eight pages has lain in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and twenty-three days, and not one person in that building said a word to her in three and a half hours, and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will.

---

**Three things on that Thursday and none of them on a form.** A calibration sticker on the side of a compressor with a date on it in a hand that is not the technician's, and the corner of it about a quarter of the way peeled, and nobody in that cellar has peeled it back or read what is under it. **A service book in a drawer with a time written in it twice for about three weeks and the two times about eleven minutes apart, in the same hand both times.** And a button from an alarm that was not an alarm any more, wired to a light, doing the job of a switch and doing it perfectly well.

---

**He wanted somebody whose job it was to ask the technician of about twenty-five what he had done in the eleven minutes.**

Nobody's job is to ask anybody anything. There is no arrangement in this city for a man under a market to be questioned about a compressor, and nobody in that cellar was going to do it and Marek knew that by about the second count, and he lay on his back under a light for another fifty minutes and did not ask either, and there is nothing in his job that would have let him ask without it being a different job.

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and he had a bracket in a drawer in Lattice Ward that he had printed twice, the first one about a sixteenth out and the second one right, and he put the first one in the bin when he got in and kept the second one, and about nine people in this city have a bracket in a drawer that they made twice and one of them is a man of twenty-two who is not proud of the one in the bin and has not printed it again.
