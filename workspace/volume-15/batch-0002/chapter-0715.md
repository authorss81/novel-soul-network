# Chapter 715 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Fourth District Room With A Door That Needed A Bell And A Woman Of About Twenty-Four Who Put A Card About Four Inches By Two On The Wall Of It In Her Own Name With Nine Words On It, A Man Of About Forty-Seven From A District Council Who Asked Her To Take Four Words Out And Was Refused In About Four Seconds With No Reason Given And None Asked For, And A Man Of Twenty-Two On A Stepladder Four Feet From It

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went to a fourth district at about half past two, to a room whose bell did not sound, and about nine words went on the wall of it in a young woman's own name, and about four seconds of it were a man from a district council being told no.**

---

The Wednesday was eleven jobs with a date against each and eleven people at the counter, and the last of them was let in at half past five. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and that had happened about twelve times in a fortnight.

**The hardboard's eighteenth line is one thousand and forty-three days, one hundred and forty-nine weeks to the day, and the ask is one thousand and fifteen days, one hundred and forty-five weeks to the day, and two figures that are each a whole number of weeks are on the page of a Wednesday on which about four seconds were spent refusing a thing and the nine words went up afterwards.**

---

**The room is on the ground floor of a building in a fourth district with a counter at one side and about nine chairs and a stove that is lit, and it is a place people come to find out what is happening on their own street, and it has no name on the outside of it.**

The bell did not sound because the striker had been off its spindle since the spring and the spindle had not been replaced and nobody had said so. He had a stepladder and about nine feet of bell wire and about eleven minutes.

**She came in at about ten past three with a card in her hand and a piece of tape and she did not ask him what he was doing and she did not offer to help and she did not stand under the ladder.**

She is about twenty-four. She has a key to a room of her own and not to his. **Her appointment's terms have not changed and she can look into the Commons and therefore into anybody who sits on it, and none of that is on the wall of that room and none of it was said in it that afternoon.**

She put the card up on the wall by the door at about the height of a man's shoulder and pressed the tape down with her thumb twice.

**It is about four inches by two. It is in her own name, which is on it, and there are nine words on it under her name, and the nine words are these:**

**Every share ends at the hand that holds it.**

---

**He was on the ladder four feet from it with a screwdriver in his hand when she put it up and he read it, and he read it twice, and the second time he read it because the first time he had been checking the wire.**

Nobody in that room said anything about the card. **About four people were in it and none of them looked at it and about four of them had seen it go up, and the man at the counter read it and went back to what he was doing, and nobody has asked anybody in this city what those nine words are for, and the only person who could answer that is the woman who wrote them and she has not been asked either.**

---

**And then a man of about forty-seven came in at about half past three with a sheet of paper in his hand and asked whether the card on the wall was hers, and she said it was, and he read it standing up and put the paper he had brought on the counter and did not sit down.**

"**We would like the phrasing brought down,**" he said. "**A council paper went round about it yesterday. Four words out of it and it says the same thing and it does not sound like a wall.**"

"**No,**" she said.

**And that was the refusal, and it took about four seconds, and she did not give a reason, and he did not ask for one.**

He waited. About four seconds went, the way about four seconds does.

"**Right,**" he said. "**I'll put it in as it stands.**"

"**Do,**" she said.

**And that was all of it. About four seconds, one word, and no reason, and no question asked for a reason, and neither of them said one word about the four words he had come about, and he did not come back on the Thursday and has not come back since, and he is not on a list and is owed nothing and was not thanked.**

---

**Marek was on the ladder four feet away with a screwdriver in his hand for the whole of those four seconds and he heard the man's voice and he heard the woman's voice and he heard the one word, and he did not know what had been asked and he did not find out and he was not going to, and the only thing he knows about those four seconds is that a man came in with a council paper and a woman said one word and the man left with the paper still in his hand.**

He finished the bell. The striker went back on the spindle and the bell rang twice, and about four people in that room looked up at it, and the woman of about twenty-four did not, and she paid him and did not say anything about the card and he did not say anything about the card.

"**That'll go,**" he said.

"**That'll go,**" she said.

---

**The rest of the Wednesday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and a man of about forty-four brought a chair in a bag and paid for a rewire and carried it out again in the bag, and a woman of about thirty-six asked whether a thing could be taken back to a shop and was told it could and bought a switch and has not been back.**

That evening put four people in the four rooms off that service road and one behind the three, and a woman of about thirty in the fourth of them from about half past six until ten where the chair behind the door is hers. The eighth of eight pages has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and twenty-two days and nobody has asked her for it and nobody has said sorry to her in any form, and nobody ever will.

---

**Three things on that Wednesday, none of them on a form.** A nail in a wall with a clean circle round it where a card had hung for a while and had been taken down, and the paper inside the circle is not yellowed while the paper round it is. **A card about four inches by two with nine words on it in a young woman's own name, on a wall in a fourth district, that nobody has to obey.** And a bell on a door in that room that has not rung since the spring and rang twice at about twenty to four on a Wednesday while four people were in the room and one of them did not look up.

---

**He wanted the man of about forty-seven to have come back on the Thursday and been refused a second time.**

**He did not. The man put it in as it stands and did not come back and is not on a list, and the card is still on the wall of that room in a fourth district with nine words on it in her own name, and the words on that card are the exact limit of every share in what this city has been doing for about three weeks, and the one person in this city who has read those nine words and understood them is a man of twenty-two who is not going to be told about them by anybody.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and he was in a fourth district until half past five and a job he had promised for four o'clock in a second district had been done by a man from that street, and the woman of about forty-one who had asked for it paid about four pounds more than he would have charged her and did not know that he was in a fourth district at the time.
