# Chapter 713 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Second Floor In A Third District Where A Room With About Nine Chairs In It Has Changed What It Does With The Last Hour Of Its Night Without Telling Anybody, A Man Of About Thirty-Four On A Crate Outside The Door Of It From About One O'Clock, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Noticed And Asked Nobody

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went to a third district at about half past eight at night for a switch on a landing, and the door of a room at the end of that landing was shut at one o'clock when it had been open until half past twelve, and about nine people had come up that stair in the four hours before it shut.**

---

The Monday carried eleven jobs with a date against each and eleven people over the counter, the last of them let in at half past five, and about two of the eleven were about the night. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook.

**Against the north wall, a man of about fifty-one: nine hundred and twenty-nine days, one hundred and thirty-two weeks and five days. Nine hand copies, of which there are nine: eight hundred and eighty-nine days, one hundred and twenty-seven weeks to the day; about eight are not finished, the ninth is torn, and no two have ever been compared.**

---

**The building is a launderette on the ground floor and two floors above it, and the second floor has a landing with four doors on it and a room at the end that people wait in between shifts.**

Marek went up at about half past eight because the light on the landing had a switch on it that worked from the wrong side, and the man who had reported it said he had put his hand in the wrong place twice, and that is a thing that happens to about four people in every hundred.

The room at the end of that landing is a waiting room. It has about nine chairs, a table with a cloth on it, an electric fire that is off because it is the spring, and a card in a frame by the door with the hours on it.

**It is run by a woman of about thirty-four who has been running it for about three years and is not the only person in it on any night, and about four people work there in shifts, and the shifts have never been written down anywhere except in her head.**

He was on the landing for about eleven minutes with the switch out and a new one in the box.

---

**And the door was shut.**

He noticed it because the landing is lit and the room is lit, and a shut door with light under it is a thing that is either a fault or a decision, and he has spent eleven years deciding which.

He knocked. Nobody answered, and he knocked again, and then a woman of about thirty-four opened it about four inches and looked at him and said, "**We're shut,**" and shut it again.

That was the whole conversation and it took about four seconds and he did not ask why, because there was nothing in the way she said it to ask.

**And he went back to his box and finished the switch and put the cover on and tested it from the wrong side, and it worked from the wrong side, and he wrote the time on the back of his own hand, and while he was doing that the door of the room at the end of the landing opened and shut about nine times in about half an hour and he did not look up for any of it.**

---

**What he had seen in the spring, when he had been on that landing for a different switch, was a door that was open until half past twelve every night of the week, and about four people in it, and a man of about thirty-four who came in at about ten and left at about half past twelve and who had carried a chair out of the room and put it back in the room twice.**

**What he saw on this Monday was a door shut at one o'clock, and two people inside it instead of one, and a man of about thirty-four on a crate in the corridor outside the door with a cushion on the crate, and about nine people going up that stair between about nine and one, and about four of them going down again before the door shut.**

**The card in the frame by that door had the hours on it and the hours on it were different from the hours that had been on it in the spring, and they had been rewritten in the same hand and the hand was not the woman of about thirty-four's, and nobody in that building had mentioned it to him and he did not ask and was not going to.**

---

**He did not ask the man on the crate. The man on the crate was about thirty-four and had a folded newspaper and a flask and was not asleep, and Marek went past him four times in about twenty minutes and the man raised the flask about twice and did not speak to him and Marek did not speak to him, and neither of them had any reason to, and that was the whole of the contact between them.**

**He did not ask the woman of about thirty-four either. He asked her one technical question when he came back up at about a quarter to nine, which was whether the fire in that room was on the same circuit as the landing, and she said it was, and said the switch was outside the room, and shut the door again, and he wrote that on the back of his other hand.**

**He did not go to the ground floor and ask the man at the launderette. He thought about doing it for about nine seconds on the way down and then did not, and the reason he did not is on the page and it is short: a man of twenty-two who has noticed a thing about a room is a man of twenty-two who has begun to be somebody who goes and asks, and there is no version of that in this city that ends with the room still being there.**

Nobody has asked him. He has not asked. **There is no arrangement anywhere in this city for asking a room in a third district why its hours changed, and about four people on that landing would have told him, and about four of them had no reason to.**

---

**At about nine o'clock a woman of about fifty-one came into the shop in Lattice Ward and said that the man next door could have done what he had done and had done it for nothing, and Marek said that the man next door did not do stair rails and she said that the man next door would have.**

He charged her the going rate. He was not asked to and he did not offer and she did not say anything about it and she has not been back, and the reason he charged her is that he did not want to have the conversation in which he said that a man next door would come for nothing, and he has thought about that for about four seconds since and has not resolved it.

**The charge was twenty-two pounds, exact, and a stair rail went up in a second district on the Tuesday and about nine feet of conduit went out of the door on it.**

---

**The rest of the Monday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and a man of about forty-one asked whether a thing that had been working for nine years could start and stop in the same afternoon, and was told that it could, and that nobody would be able to say why, and bought a switch and has not been back.**

From about half past six until ten there were four people in those four rooms off that service road and one behind the three, and the woman of about thirty was in the fourth of them from half past six until ten, and the chair the door is behind is hers, and the eighth of eight pages was in a ring binder on a back shelf and has been for a hundred and twenty-three days, and nobody has apologised to her in any form and there is nobody in this city entitled to take it out of the binder in her presence or out of it.

---

**Three things on that Monday and not one of them on a form.** A crate in a corridor with a cushion on it, and the cushion is newer than the crate. **A card in a frame by a door with hours on it in a hand that is not the hand of the woman who runs the room.** And a landing light that worked from the wrong side, which is a thing about four people in every hundred get wrong and which cost a man of about thirty-three a second trip before anybody worked out that it was not the wiring.

---

**He wanted the man on the crate to have told him without being asked.**

Nobody was going to. The man on the crate was not going to and Marek knew he was not going to by about the third time he went past, and he wanted it anyway, and he wanted the wanting to be a thing that happens to a man when a stranger decides he is worth telling, and in this city it does not, and the whole of what he wanted was a sentence from a man on a crate that nobody had asked him for.

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and he had charged a woman of about fifty-one the going rate for a thing a man next door would have done for nothing, and he does not know whether the man next door would have.
