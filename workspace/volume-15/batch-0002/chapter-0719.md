# Chapter 719 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Counter In Lattice Ward With A Woman Of About Thirty-Six Waiting For A Part And A Man Of About Forty-Four Who Signed A Sheet And Who Is Asked In About Nine Seconds What He Thought He Was Signing, An Answer About A Box, Nobody Asked Anything By Way Of Account, And A Pen With Its Cap Chewed Off

**A Tuesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one stayed at the counter, and a woman of about thirty-six asked a man of about forty-four what he thought he had been signing, and he told her about a box, and nobody asked either of them a second question and nobody asked them to account for anything.**

---

The Tuesday ran on eleven dated jobs and eleven callers, and the last of them was let in at half past five. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and that had happened about eleven times in a fortnight.

**The sixteenth line on that board of two nails is one thousand one hundred and twenty-one days, one hundred and sixty weeks and one day, and what sits in the post at the end of that corridor has been there eight hundred and seventy-nine days, one hundred and twenty-five weeks and four days, and there is not a single whole number of weeks anywhere in the whole of that load book for this day.**

---

**She came in at about eleven for a part and the part was not on the shelf and she was told it would be Thursday, and she sat down on the stool by the till, which about four people in this city do not do, and the man of about forty-four was at the counter buying a lamp.**

The lamp was in a carrier and he had the money out before Marek had finished saying the price.

They did not know each other. **She had been in the shop about nine times in about two years and he had been in it about four times, and neither of them knew the other's name, and neither of them said one.**

"**You signed one of them,**" she said.

He put the money down.

"**I did,**" he said.

"**What did you think you were signing.**"

**And he answered her in about nine seconds and it was about a box.**

"**The box,**" he said. "**There's a box at the bottom and I signed the box, and I will tell you honestly that I did not know there was anything under the box, because I did not look, and I signed it at a counter in about four seconds because I was late for something and the woman behind the counter said the box was the one that mattered.**"

---

**Nobody questioned him. Nobody asked him a second time. Nobody asked him why he had signed it, or who had asked him to, or whether he still thought it was a good idea, or what he had understood by it, or what his wife thought, and nobody in that shop treated what he had said as a defence and nobody treated it as a confession, and about four people in that shop could have said a great deal about it and none of them said anything.**

**He did not offer anything else. He did not say he had been shown anything, and he did not say he had not, and nobody asked, and the woman of about thirty-six did not ask him a second question and has not seen him since.**

"**Right,**" she said.

"**Right,**" he said.

**And that was the whole conversation between them, and it was about nine seconds of the question and about twenty of the answer, and it was the first and only time anybody in this city has asked a person who signed that sheet what he thought he was signing, and it was asked by a woman who had come in for a part.**

---

**Marek was at the bench about nine feet away with a bracket in his hand and he heard the question and the first nine seconds of the answer and lost the rest of it, because the man said it quietly and the extractor in the next bay was going.**

**He did not ask the man anything. He did not ask the woman anything either, and about four seconds after it was over he asked the man of about forty-four to wait, because the part for the woman of about thirty-six had come out of a drawer in the back and he wanted to put it in the carrier with the lamp so that she would not have to come back on the Thursday, and the man of about forty-four said right and waited about eleven minutes on the stool by the till.**

**And at the end of it, when the two of them had both gone, the man of about forty-four said, at the door, that he had told his wife he would be home at eight, and it was after eight, and that was all he said about it, and he said it the way a man says a thing he is not asking anybody to do anything about.**

He went. He has not been back and has not been asked to come back and nobody in this city has asked him one question about a box.

---

**The rest of the Tuesday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and a woman of about twenty-four came in at about half past four with a folder, said she would be about an hour, and went, and Marek was behind the counter and did not go out to her and did not speak to her and has not spoken to her.**

Four people were in those four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and a fifth one was behind the three, and a woman of about thirty was in the fourth of them until ten with the chair the door is behind hers. The eighth of eight pages has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and twenty-six days. Not one person in that building addressed her in three and a half hours, nobody has apologised to her in any form, and nobody ever will.

---

**Three things on that Tuesday and none of them on a form.** A pen on that counter with about four teeth taken out of the cap, and it is the pen the woman of about thirty-six writes with, and she has been chewing it since about the spring and about four other people in that shop have watched her do it. **A stool by the till that about four people sit on and about nine people are told they cannot.** And a sheet in a drawer that was folded in four at the top corner and was not folded that way by anybody in this shop.

---

**He wanted the woman of about thirty-six to have been angry at the man of about forty-four.**

He did not want her to be angry. He wanted her to have been angry, because an angry woman in a shop takes a thing outside, and a thing taken outside about four people hear, and about four people who have heard something about a box is a number of people in this city who could be asked a question. **She was not angry. She said right, and she paid for a lamp, and he paid for a lamp, and the two of them left the shop together talking about whether a lamp should be wired in or plugged in, and the man of about forty-four carried the carrier and the woman of about thirty-six carried the part, and neither of them said one word about a sheet.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and the eleven minutes he asked a man of about forty-four to wait on a stool by a till are the eleven minutes that man gave him and did not charge him for, and the man was at home after eight and had said he would be.
