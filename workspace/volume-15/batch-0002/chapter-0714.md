# Chapter 714 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Corridor On The Ground Floor Of A Block In A Second District Where Two People Are Waiting For A Lift That Is Not Coming And One Of Them Says Ten Words That Are About Two Men Neither Of Whom Is Named And Neither Of Whom Is There, A Man Of Twenty-Two At The Other End Of It With A Box Of Tools, And Neither Of The Two Of Them Knowing He Heard Them

**A Tuesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went to a second district at about two, to a ground-floor corridor with a box of tools at one end and twenty minutes of waiting at the other, and about ten words were said in that corridor that two strangers will both still be able to repeat a month from now.**

---

Eleven dated jobs and eleven callers went through the shop on the Tuesday, and the last of the eleven came in at half past five.. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and it has shaken every night since the first of them.

**The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand one hundred and fourteen days, one hundred and fifty-nine weeks and one day, and the hardboard's nineteenth is one thousand and twenty days, one hundred and forty-five weeks and five days, and there is not one figure on that board this month that is a whole number of weeks.**

---

**The job was a stair rail. Fourteen steps in a block in a second district, a rail that had come away from the wall in two places, and about four of the six flats on that landing had somebody in them who had heard it happen.**

He had the rail off and the wall out by about a quarter to three and he was on his knees with a box of tools at the foot of it, and the corridor is about twenty feet long and has a lift at one end that is out of service and a fire door at the other, and a handrail on the wall the whole length of it.

**The shine on that handrail is in the middle about four feet of it and dull at both ends, and about nine people in that block have put a hand on it every day for about eleven years and about four of them know why it is bright in one place only and none of the six households has ever said.**

Nobody came down for about nine minutes.

Then the fire door at the far end of the corridor opened and two people came in and stood by the lift and looked at the plate on the wall by the lift, and one of them pressed the button, and nothing happened, and the other of them said that had been happening since Friday.

---

**The man of about forty-one and the woman of about thirty-eight had been waiting for about four minutes when Marek came back down with the rail in his hand, and they did not know him, and he did not know them, and about nine people in this city have ever been in a corridor at the same time as one of the two of them and about four of those nine have heard one of them say something about work.**

The man of about forty-one said, "**It's not coming,**" and the woman of about thirty-eight said, "**I know it's not coming. I've got a bag in the car and I have got to be across the city by six.**"

"**Then take the stairs,**" the man of about forty-one said.

"**I've got a bag,**" she said.

"**Then I'll carry the bag,**" he said, and neither of them moved.

**And then the woman of about thirty-eight said the ten words, to the man of about forty-one, in a face, in about nine seconds, and Marek was at the other end of that corridor with his back to them and a rail in his hand and he heard all ten of them because the corridor is stone and about twenty feet long and neither of them lowered their voices.**

"**One is presumed to have agreed. The other is not.**"

---

**And then the man of about forty-one said, "**Which one,**" and the woman of about thirty-eight said, "**The one on the wards. He's about thirty-four. He pushes beds.**"**

"**And the other one.**"

"**The other one's about thirty-four as well. He signs the release at a water yard.**"

"**Right,**" said the man of about forty-one.

"**Right,**" said the woman of about thirty-eight.

**And that was the end of it. About nine seconds, and then nothing, and neither of them said one word more about either of those two men, and the two of them stood at that lift for about eleven minutes talking about a paperclip.**

---

**Neither of the men in those ten words was in that corridor. Neither of them was in that building. Neither of them was within about a day's bus travel of the other, and they had never been in a room together, and there is nobody in this city who has ever put them in a room together, and the two of them have not spoken.**

**The man who pushes beds works at a hospital a day and a night of bus travel away and Marek has never been there. The man who signs the release at a water yard in a second district has been in Marek's shop twice and in a room over a shop in a second district for about four hours, and Marek is the only person in this city who has watched him not know what a gauge was saying, and Marek has not said that to anybody either.**

**And so for about four days Marek had two men of about thirty-four in one corridor instead of one of them in a room over a shop, and on the Thursday he worked out that the sentence had been about the one at the water yard first, and the answer was the one he already had, and that was the whole of what the corridor gave him.**

---

Four people in the four rooms off that service road from about half past six, and one room behind the three, and a fifth person in the fourth of those rooms until ten. She is about thirty. The chair the door is behind is hers and the eighth of eight pages has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and twenty-one days, and not one person in that building put a word to her for three and a half hours, and no apology has been made to her in any form and none is coming.

---

**Three things on that Tuesday, and not one of them on anything.** A handrail in a block in a second district whose shine is in the middle about four feet of it and dull at both ends, which about nine people have been touching every day for about eleven years. **A lift plate on a wall with a button on it that has not done anything since Friday and about four people on that landing have pressed it anyway.** And a box of tools that stood at the foot of a stair for about forty minutes with a man standing over it, and the corridor was stone, and a thing said at one end of it in about nine seconds was heard entire at the other.

---

**He did not go back to that block. He did not go back on the Thursday and he did not go back at the end of that week, and the rail went up on the Wednesday morning, and about nine people in that block have a rail on their stair and a man of about forty-one and a woman of about thirty-eight who were in a corridor at the same time as a man of twenty-two and will not remember it, and Marek has not told anybody that he was there, and there is no column in this city for a man of twenty-two who has heard ten words in a corridor about two men he knows one of.**

**What he wanted, for about four days, was to have been wrong about which of the two men she had meant the first one. If she had meant the man on the wards, then the ten words would have been about a stranger and would have cost him nothing, and she meant the man at the water yard, and the ten words had a man in them he has been thinking about since the spring and has not told a single person on this earth about.**

He said none of it.
**He had told a man of about thirty-three at about two that he would be back at three, and he was not back at three, and he was not back at four either, and at about half past five the man of about thirty-three said that he had waited in the street for about two and a half hours and had not asked anybody why, and bought a switch, and has not been back.**
