# Chapter 750 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six And A Stair Up Saltmarket From Half Past Six, The Fifty-Fifth Sitting Of A Room In About Nineteen Years In Which A Number Was Said Once And A Book With A Green Cover Was Not Opened And No Line Went In And A Tin Beside It Did Not Move, And A Woman Of About Sixty Who Holds That Room

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one ended in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket with a book on it that did not open, a number said once, a tin beside it that did not move, and nobody in that room who remarked on any of it.**

---

Twelve callers and twelve dated jobs on that Wednesday, the last name of the twelve taken at about twenty to five, and the cage in the round-the-side had been going about nine times in the fortnight and went again when the shutter came down at about ten.

**Line fifteen on that board of two nails is one thousand one hundred and eighty-nine days, one hundred and sixty-nine weeks and six days, and the hold on nine crates and the floor they stand on is one thousand and seven days, one hundred and forty-three weeks and six days. Those are the two figures in the head of this page and the third exact figure of the day is in the conditions below, and there is nothing under any of them.**

The separation is at seven hundred and fifty-four days, one hundred and seven weeks and five days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old.

---

**The room is a first floor above a line in Saltmarket and it has been in about nineteen years and there are about nine chairs in it and there is a table and a woman of about sixty who holds the room and has held it for about nineteen years.**

**The book is a book with a green cover and it is on sixty-five lines. The tin beside it is on seventy-three with the lid on it, and it is not opened on a Wednesday and has not been for about eleven years, and it did not move tonight and nobody in that room touched it.**

The ninth chair is hard against the wall with its back to the room. It has not moved this fortnight and it has not moved in this stretch of days and the person who put it there is not named anywhere and is not going to be.

**There is a pencil on that table that has been sharpened on one side only, and it has been like that since before the spring, and about four people in that room have sharpened a pencil since then and none of them sharpened that one.**

**A woman of about thirty-four came in at about half past seven, said she was sorry she was late, and was not late, and has been coming to that room since before the spring. She has a page of her own in her coat and she has never put it on that table and nobody in that room has ever asked her to, and she is not the reason anything happens in that room and is not going to be.**

**She sits in the eighth chair. It is the one nearest the door and it is the only one in that room that anybody has ever chosen over the others, and about four people have offered her the ones nearer the wall and she has said no to all four of them in about four seconds each, and nobody has ever asked her why, and the reason is that from the eighth chair she can see the ninth chair's back and she likes knowing what is behind her.**

---

**Four came to the table on that Wednesday and the four of them were ordinary.**

A man of about fifty-eight who runs a laundry in a first district and has been in that room for about nine years said nine seconds about a boiler that will not come right on the last of a cycle and then said nothing else for the whole of the hour. A woman of about forty-four asked whether anybody had ever had the same thing happen with a meter and was told by the man of about fifty-eight that he had, once, and that it was the meter, and that it had gone back the next morning on its own.

A woman of about thirty-one put a hand on the table flat, for about four seconds, with nothing in it, and took it off again, and did not say what the hand was for, and has said since that it was not for anything.

**A man of about fifty-two asked the woman of about sixty a question about a form and she answered it in about nine seconds, and the answer was no, and the reason was that the form was not hers, and nobody in the room agreed with her and nobody in the room said so.**

---

**She gave the number at about a quarter to seven, once, to the room, in about nine seconds, and it is the only time anybody in this city will hear it this fortnight.**

"**Sixty of which fifty-five.**"

Marek was in the room and he was four feet from the table and nobody asked him anything and he has not been asked anything about it.

**There were about nine people in that room over about an hour and a half. Four of them came to the table. Five of them did not come to the table at all and one of the five was a man of about fifty-three who had not been in that room before and stood at the back and did not sit and left at about eight, and nobody has ever said anything about him and he was not on a list.**

---

**The book was on sixty-five lines at half past six and it was on sixty-five lines at eight and no line went in.**

Nobody asked her to open it. Nobody suggested it. **There is no rule in this city that says which of these it opens at, and the last time it opened a caller said a thing to her face, and the time before that, and there is nobody in that room who has ever been told which of the two is the reason and she is not going to say and Marek was in the room and did not ask her.**

**The book is shut and that is all that happened to it and nobody in this city has described it as a failure and nobody has described it as anything.**

---

**Nobody in that room remarked on the fortnight and nobody has since, and the man of twenty-two who was in it did not remark on it either and has not brought it up with anybody.**

He wrote the number down in a book of his own after he got home and he has looked at it once and he has not worked out what it is a count of, and it is not his business and he knows that it is not his business.

---

**About nine pounds of work he had already priced did not get done on that Wednesday because he shut the counter at half past six and went up a stair.**

He has not given it to anybody. It will be there on Friday and the woman will have found somebody and will not say so, and he is not going to ask her who, and that is a thing he decided at about seven and a half while about nine people were sitting in a room doing nothing on purpose.

---

**He wanted one of the two figures on that table to be the one that mattered, and he does not know which one, and he has not worked out how to ask which without it turning into a thing about the room.**

So he did not ask. He went down the stair at about eight and walked home and got in at about ten past ten.

**On the way he worked out that the two numbers have been on that table for about nineteen years, and that in that time the one on the book has gone up by one every so often and the one on the tin has not gone anywhere, and that nobody in this city has ever put the two together in a sentence, and that this was the first time in about nineteen years he had done it in his own head.**

He has not written it down and he is not going to.

**The stair has a window on the half-landing and the light from the street comes up it, and on that Wednesday the ninth chair at the top of it had a shadow across the seat of it that was not its own, and nobody in the building had been in that room between half past six and eight, and Marek did not go back up to look and has not gone back up since.**

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and a woman of about thirty in the fourth of them, in her chair, with the door of that room behind her, and the chair has not been moved in this fortnight and neither has she.**

Her page is the eighth of eight and it has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and sixty-three days. It did not come out on that Wednesday, nobody asked her one question about it, and nobody in this city has the standing to open that binder.

---

**The last of that Wednesday was a man of about fifty-seven who wanted to know why a light in his kitchen came on at about eleven at night and went off at about one, every night, for about a fortnight, and Marek went and stood in his kitchen for it.**

**He was there from about half ten and the light came on at about eleven and went off at about one, and he was in the room for both of those, and it is the timer on the extractor, and the man had had a new one fitted in the spring by somebody who did not ask him what it was for.**

"**It is doing what it was told to do,**" Marek said. "**Somebody set it to come on at eleven and go off at one, and the somebody was not me and I am not going to say who it was because I do not know and I have not asked.**"

"**I'd like it to come on when I cook,**" said the man of about fifty-seven.

"**Then you want a new one,**" Marek said, and charged about nine pounds to change a five-minute part, and the man paid and did not say thank you and did not need to.

---

He said none of it.
Twelve dated jobs went out and twelve came in, the shutter came down at about ten, and a book with a green cover in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket was on sixty-five lines at half past six and on sixty-five lines at eight, and a tin beside it was on seventy-three, and about nine people sat in a room for an hour and a half and four of them came to a table, and a hand went flat on that table for about four seconds with nothing in it, and nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.
