# Chapter 747 — The One Sunday, A Shop That Opens In The Morning And Shuts At About Two And Does Not Open Again Until Tuesday, Four Rooms Off A Service Road That Are Shut And Do Not Open On A Day Like This, A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Turned Up At About Seven And Waited About Twenty Minutes In The Cold, And A Woman Of About Thirty Behind A Door That Nobody Opened

**A Sunday in this city is a morning. This one had a bench until about two and a shutter down at about two, four rooms off that service road shut, a woman of about thirty behind a door nobody opened, and a man of about thirty-four who turned up at about seven for something that was not happening and waited about twenty minutes and went home.**

---

That day had two names on the book and two dates, and the last of the two at about eleven in the morning, and the shutter came down at about two and the cage went about four times, which is what a day like this does.

**The front of that one page, copied out nine times by hand, is at nine hundred and thirty-seven days, one hundred and thirty-three weeks and six days, and the hardboard's seventeenth line is at one thousand one hundred and forty-three days, one hundred and sixty-three weeks and two days. Not one figure on this day comes out on a whole number of weeks, and there is not one here to divide and nothing has been divided.**

The separation is at seven hundred and fifty-one days, one hundred and seven weeks and two days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and it was still there on the Sunday and nobody opened it, which is what Sunday is for.

---

**The four rooms off that service road do not open on a day like this and have never opened on a day like this, and the fifth one behind the other three is shut as well, and nobody has ever made an exception and nobody has asked to.**

He went past the door at about half past ten. There was a light on in the first of the four. There was nothing on any of the four about a day like this — not a card, not a sheet of paper, not a piece of chalk on the frame — and he looked and he is not going to write down what he did not find.

**He went back at about one and looked again, and the light was still on, and that is the whole of what a day like this gives anybody who is looking.**

**He stood on the other side of that road for about four minutes and there is a bus stop on it and it is a Sunday and the timetable in the shelter has a Sunday column printed on it and nothing written in it after about the eleventh, and he read it and could not tell whether that was because the buses stop or because nobody has filled the column in, and there is nobody to ask and he did not ask the man at the stop because there was no man at the stop.**

---

The eighth of eight of her pages has been shut in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and sixty days and it did not come out on that Sunday. **It did not come out on a Monday either, and it is not going to come out on a Tuesday, and nobody in this city is entitled to open that binder, and there is nobody in this city who has wanted to, which are two different things and both of them are true.**

---

**About nine people had arranged to be under a city in a first district at about half past seven on that Sunday, and one of them came.**

The man of about thirty-four keeps the keys to a building in a fourth district. He had told the woman of about fifty-two about half five on the Thursday morning in about four seconds and she had said she would be there or she would not and he did not know which and did not ask, which is what she had told him to do.

He came at about five past seven. **He stood on the top step in the cold for about twenty minutes and nobody came and the door behind him is not locked and has not been locked since before the spring and it did not open, because it is a Sunday and there is nobody in that building on a Sunday.**

He has Monday to Friday in that building and finishes at half four on a Friday. **He had told his wife on the Thursday that he would be out for about an hour on the Sunday and she had not asked what for, and he has not told her about the twenty minutes and is not going to, and she will not ask, and that is the arrangement and it is not a good one.**

**He has a key. The key to the door at the bottom of that stair is with the rest of the keys he keeps and it has been in his pocket since about half five that morning, and he did not use it, and not because anybody told him not to.**

He went home at about twenty-five past seven.

**About eight other people did not come, and the reason for every one of them is that they had something else on, and not one of them has said so to anybody and not one of them has said so to him, and on Monday about nine people found out that a thing they had all intended did not happen, and not one of them has asked anybody why.**

---

**The rest of that Sunday was a shop in Lattice Ward with the shutter up until about two, and two jobs, and about five hours of an afternoon.**

The first was a woman of about sixty-eight who wanted a fitting changed in a hall and who had decided to do it herself, and had bought the wrong fitting, and had a plasterer sitting in her front room at ten o'clock on a Sunday who was not going to wait.

**Marek took the fitting back to the shop, took the right one out of a drawer in about four seconds, went back and put it in, and it took about nine minutes, and she paid about nine pounds, and the plasterer went to another job and lost about half an hour and was right to.**

The second was a man of about thirty-three whose door had dropped in the frame since Friday and whom he found holding it open with a piece of card off a box that he had cut at the end of the week.

**He took the card out and the door shut on its own, and he told the man that the card was the only thing holding it and that the man had done the right thing on Friday night for about nine hours and that on Monday it will not be right any more. The man said he had not thought about Monday when he put it there. Marek said that is the whole of what there is to think about, and charged about four pounds, and did not say the rest.**

---

**One thing he noticed and there is no form for it: the four rooms have different numbers on them and one of the numbers is wrong.**

The first, second and fourth have brass numbers on the frame and the third has one that has been painted over and had another painted on top of it, and the top one is lower than the other three by about an inch, which is what happens when a person paints a number without taking the old one off properly and does it from a stepladder with the frame at a different height in that bay.

**Nobody in this city has been asked which is the right number for the third room and nobody is going to be, and about nine people who use that door every week have been going through it for years and none of them has ever said the number out loud.**

---

**He wanted one Sunday in this city to have something in it that he could walk to.**

There was nothing. He walked past four shut doors, and a shop with a shutter down, and a counter that had a woman of about thirty in it at about one, and he went home at about two and had about nine hours of the day in front of him and did about four of them and then did not do the other five, and there is no form on which any of that would have gone and there is nobody he could have shown it to.

**That is what a day off costs in this city, and it is not a number and the number is not what it is: it is that a day off is only free for a person who has somebody to be off in front of.**

**He has had about nine of them in nineteen years. Four of them were when he was about nine and about ten and there was a woman in the house. One of them was a fortnight in the spring that nobody has ever asked him about. The other four have been on his own and he has not been lonely in any of them and he has not been all right in any of them either, and those are two different sentences and both of them are true and he has never said either of them to anybody.**

---

He said none of it.
Two dated jobs went out and two came in, the shutter came down at about two, and about nine people found out on the Monday that they had all meant to be under a city at about half past seven on the Sunday, and one of them had actually been, and stood on the top step with a key in his pocket, and went home, and has not told anybody and is not going to.
