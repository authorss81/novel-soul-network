# Chapter 749 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six And A Stair Down Under A Building After The Shop Had Shut, A Cabinet At The Back Of A Room That Has Not Moved Since A Mapping Lapsed On A Sheet With Three Ruled Lines And A Signature And Eleven Days On It, About Nine Minutes With A Chisel By Hand, And A Place He Can No Longer Go And Look

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six and a stair down under a building after the shop had shut. This one was a folded sheet in a coat pocket, a cabinet at the back of a room, about nine minutes with a chisel worked by hand, and the last place in this city where he could have gone and looked.**

---

Twelve names on the book and twelve dates on that Tuesday, the last of the twelve at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten and the cage went about nine times this fortnight.

**Line sixteen on that board of two nails is one thousand one hundred and sixty-three days, one hundred and sixty-six weeks and one day, and the man of about fifty-one against the north wall is at nine hundred and seventy-nine days, one hundred and thirty-nine weeks and six days. There is no figure in this Tuesday's book that comes out on a whole number of weeks and there is nothing in here to divide.**

The separation is at seven hundred and fifty-three days, one hundred and seven weeks and four days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and he did not open it and he has never opened it.

---

**The sheet is folded in three and it has been folded in three for about a year and it has been in a file and it came out of that file on the Tuesday morning and it went into a coat pocket at about half past four.**

It has three ruled lines on it and a signature at the bottom of the third, and eleven days written at the top, which is how long the thing it authorised had been allowed to be temporary.

**The mapping lapsed. The plate did not.** A plate of a kind about the size of a hand goes in the back of a cabinet in the room at the bottom of that stair and it has not moved and nobody in this city has been authorised to touch it since before the spring, and the authorisation that was given then was for a sheet with three ruled lines on it and it ran out on the eleventh day of it and nobody has ever renewed it.

**On the back of it, in a different ink and in about nine words, somebody has written that the holder of a witnessed maintenance link may put a chisel to the plate in that cabinet and call it.**

He does not know whose hand that is. It is not the hand on the signature. **He has carried the sheet since about half past four on the Tuesday and he has read the back of it about nine times and it has not once occurred to him to ask who wrote it, because the writing is the part he does not need and the writing is the part that matters.**

---

**He was in that room from about a quarter past ten until about half past ten and nobody in this city knows that to the minute and nobody asked him to be there.**

The man of about sixty who keeps that building was at the top of the stair and did not come down, and had a cup in his hand again, and Marek did not ask him to and did not tell him and he would have come down if he had been asked.

**Nobody watched him do it. There is no second person in this city who knows the exact time of it and nobody was asked to be.**

---

**It is a plate let into the back of the cabinet with a rim of soft metal round it about a quarter of an inch wide, and the rim is the join, and the join is what goes first.**

He used a cold chisel and a mallet and he worked by hand and he did not use anything with a motor in it and he had a mallet in his own hand and nobody lent it to him.

**The first four minutes were the rim and the rest of them were the plate, and about four of the nine were his own hands shaking and not the work.** He got the rim in two places, and he did the plate in about five, and about forty seconds of those five was the chisel lying flat on the bench while he got his hands back, and it came out of the back of the cabinet in about nine minutes in three pieces, and none of them went anywhere, and he carried them up the stair in a coat because he could not think of anything else to do with them.

**The Crown Root Interface is in three pieces on a bench in a workshop off Lattice Ward and it is not going to be put back and there is nobody in this city who can tell you where a Crown Root Interface goes, because there has only ever been one and it has been in a cabinet its whole life.**

---

**There is a maker's mark stamped into the underside of the middle piece and it is upside down, so that a man has to turn the piece over in his hand to read it, and he read it and it is four letters and it is not a name.**

He read it twice. Nobody else ever will.

---

**The rest of that Tuesday, before the shop shut, was a bench in Lattice Ward and a woman of about fifty-one with a radio that only worked when it was held.**

Not held near anything. **Held.** When it sat on a shelf it made a noise like a machine two floors down and when it was in a hand it came in perfectly, and it had been like that since before the spring and she had learned to hold it, and she had learned to hold it so well that she no longer noticed she was doing it.

Marek offered to look at it and she said no, and then said yes, and then held it the whole time he had it apart, and the whole time she was holding it, it played.

"**That's not a fault,**" she said.

"**No,**" Marek said. "**It's a fault. It's in the case where the aerial's earthed through, and it's been earthed through since somebody dropped it, and the only reason it works is that you're holding it. That's not a design, that's you.**"

"**I know it's me,**" she said. "**I've had it eleven years and I know it's me. I wasn't asking you to fix it, I was asking whether you would.**"

"**I wouldn't,**" Marek said, and charged about four pounds for twenty minutes of looking, and she took it home in the same condition.

**He has thought about the answer to that about nine times since and it is the only job this year he is sure about, and there is no column for it and he is not going to make one.**

**He has been in about four shops in this city that have closed over the years and he keeps a list in his head of the ones he told the truth to, and he is not going to write that list down, and he knows exactly what the list is for, and it is not humility.**

---

**What he lost is not that he will forget.**

**He can still see his father's hands on a bench in a first district with a piece held wrong in them, and he can see it the way you see a room you were in as a child, and that is in him and is not going anywhere and is not affected by anything he did to a cabinet.**

What is gone is the going. **For about nineteen years there has been one thing in this city he could go and look at, and it was the wear his father had put into the face of that plate with his hands, and about a fortnight ago he put his own hand on it and could feel it, and now the piece with the wear in it is in three parts on a bench in a workshop off Lattice Ward and there is no way of getting back to it that does not mean putting it together, and nobody in this city is going to help him do that.**

That is the two of them and they are two, and the first one is not the second one, and only one of them was ever a thing anybody could lose.

---

**About nine pounds of work came out of his own shop on that Tuesday at about half three and it was not taken and the woman who wanted it was told he would be there on the Thursday.**

She went to a man on a road off Saltmarket and got it done cheaper. **Marek has not told her why and she did not ask and is not going to, and he decided that on the Tuesday and has not changed his mind, and it was not the right decision and he knows the shape of why.**

---

**He wanted his father's hand to have been in that room while he did it, and it was not, and there is no way of asking for a thing like that and he has not asked.**

**What he has instead is about nine seconds of a Tuesday that he will keep for the rest of his life, and he has not told anybody what is in them, and the man of about sixty at the top of that stair would have carried the pieces up for him and did not know they existed and was not asked.**

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six on that Tuesday a woman of about thirty in the fourth of them, with the door of that room shut behind her chair, and that chair exactly where it was when he carried it up a stair three weeks ago.**

Her page is the eighth of eight. It has been shut in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and sixty-two days and it did not come out on that Tuesday, and nobody has asked her anything about it, and nobody is going to.

---

He said none of it.
Twelve dated jobs went out and twelve came in, the shutter came down at about ten, and after the shop shut he went down a stair under a building and came up it at about half past ten, and nobody in this city knows what he carried up, and nobody will ever be told, and he will be able to remember it and he will not be able to go and look at it, and those are two things and only one of them is a loss.
