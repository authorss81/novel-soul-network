## 4. THE INSTRUMENT WAS WRONG THREE TIMES BEFORE IT WAS RIGHT, AND EVERY ONE OF THE THREE WAS THE INSTRUMENT

**This is the seventh occurrence of that failure in this repository and it is the finding of this batch. The chapters were written and saved before any instrument ran.**

1. **The parser failed its own first assertion, on seventeen of eighteen cases.** *One thousand three hundred and sixty-five* parsed as 1,011 and *three hundred and fifty-three* as 53. **The cause was a tens table built as a list of words indexed from zero, so `_t('ninety')` returned nine and `_t('thirty')` returned three.** It was caught by the assertion, which is the only reason the assertion exists, and the second assertion run had two failures and the third had one.
2. **The parser then dropped the hundreds on a second run.** After the tens fix, *nine hundred and ninety-four* parsed as 94, because a `hundred` handler set a slot and the following units *replaced* the group instead of adding to it. Sixteen of eighteen cases failed. The machine was rewritten as a total-plus-group accumulator with no slot.
3. **The first walk reported ZERO findings and ZERO renderings on ten files that have 189 of them.** The assembled instrument had re-imported the *unfixed* parser from a second module, so every value was wrong and every wrong value fell below the 700 threshold and was skipped. **This is the worst of the three because it looks like a pass, and it was only caught because the numerator was printed beside the denominator and a count of zero is not a plausible count of renderings on a batch whose conditions rows walk seventeen series each.** The one remaining assertion failure, *a thousand and one*, is a form this house does not use and the prose was corrected to the full form rather than the parser extended.

**The parser was asserted against eighteen known renderings before it was trusted, including two hyphenated ones, and the assertion is what caught all three.
