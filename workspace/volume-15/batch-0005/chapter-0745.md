# Chapter 745 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A Woman Of About Fifty-Two Standing In A Room Under A City Saying What A Room Is For In About Nine Seconds, A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Did Not Answer Her, And A Refusal He Had Not Planned To Give

**The bench was his until two and the counter until about six and then four rooms off that service road, and none of that is what a Thursday is. This one had a woman of about fifty-two in a room under a building saying a true thing about what a room is for, a man of twenty-two who did not answer her, and a refusal he had not planned.**

---

Eleven names went onto that Thursday's sheet and eleven dates went with them, the last of the eleven at about twenty to five, and the cage in the round-the-side went about nine times in the fortnight as it has been going.

**The hold on nine crates and the floor they stand on is at one thousand and one days, one hundred and forty-three weeks to the day, and the nineteen, the last line in use, is at one thousand and sixty-four days, one hundred and fifty-two weeks to the day. Those two come out exact and so do five others on this Thursday, and there is nothing written under any of them.**

The separation is at seven hundred and forty-eight days, one hundred and six weeks and six days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old.

---

**There are six people in that room and four of them work. The woman of about fifty-two does a door at about half five and is not a member of anything.**

She had come down the stair at about half past seven with the man of about thirty-four and had said nothing at all on the way down, which is what she said she would do, which was not the same thing.

Sera Quill was there. The man of about forty-three was there with a folded piece of paper in his coat and did not open it.

**There is a light fitting in that room with a bulb in it that is not the bulb the fitting takes, and it has been the wrong bulb since before the spring and about four people have decided separately not to be the one who replaces it.**

---

**She said it at about a quarter to eight, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular and to Marek.**

"**You have all got very good at bringing people down here,**" she said, "**and not one of you has said what the room is for. A room is not for people to come to. A room is for the one thing that has to be done in it, and anybody who comes to it who cannot do that one thing is in the wrong room, and it is not a room's job to go out and find them something else to be in.**"

That is about nine seconds and about forty-nine words and she did not stop in the middle of it and she did not say it again.

---

**Marek did not answer her and has not answered her and did not answer her then.**

He wanted to and had a sentence ready and it was the sentence that would have turned a true thing about a room into a thing about him, and he did not say it, and nobody in that room would have stopped him.

"**It is about a door,**" Marek said. "**Not about a room.**"

She said: "**Right.**" And went back to where she was standing, and did not look at him again that evening, and has not since, and he has not gone and found out whether that was agreement or the opposite of it.

**He could have said it. He had about four other things ready and every one of them would have been true and not one of them would have been about the man on the Thursday, which is what she actually asked.**

**The room went on.** The man of about forty-three opened his paper and looked at it. Sera Quill wrote about nine words on the back of her hand. The man of about thirty-four said the one about a light, and nobody thanked him.

**What she had said had been in her head since the Tuesday, when she saw a card go up over the mark it had made there before. She has not said it to anybody in this city and she is not going to, and the only other person who has any idea of it is a man of twenty-two who did not answer.**

---

**The rest of that Thursday was a shop in Lattice Ward and a kettle.**

A woman of about thirty-one brought in a kettle that switched itself off at about four minutes and would do it again only if it was turned over and plugged in at a different angle, and it took about half an hour to find out that the angle was the whole of it, because the switch on the base is a rocker and the rocker is what the moulding had been warped around.

**He could have run it for about nine minutes with the kettle in a sink of water and a weight on the base and told her it was fixed, and the kettle would have worked for about a fortnight and then it would not have, and he has done that twice in nineteen years and both times somebody found out.**

So he told her it was the moulding, and she said: "**Then it wants a new one,**" and he said yes, and she put it back in her bag and went, and paid about nine pounds for the half hour.

"**I'll think about it,**" she said at the door, and Marek said: "**Do. There is no rush on it and I am not going to chase you,**" and meant it, and it is the only sentence in that conversation he had prepared.

---

**He made a refusal at about twenty past eight that he had not planned to make and could not take back.**

A man of about thirty-eight who runs a market in a first district had come down with the others and had three lines written out on a sheet of his own, and he wanted them put on the sheet that is on the table in that room, so that people who came in could see them and know they had been thought of.

"**No,**" Marek said, in about four seconds.

"**Why not.**"

**And Marek did not have a reason, and the reason he actually had was that the sheet is not his, and he cannot say that to a man he asked to come down a stair, because that would be a reason about who owns a piece of paper and the man did not ask who owns it.**

"**Because I would be the one who put them there,**" he said, "**and then they would be mine.**"

The man of about thirty-eight said right, and folded his sheet, and put it in his inside pocket, and said that he was glad to know before he had left it, and went up the stair about nine minutes later and has not been asked anything since.

**Nobody in that room thought the refusal was about the three lines. It was about about eleven pounds a year of somebody else's paper and it is not in the load book and it will not be.**

---

**He wanted the true thing to have been about a door rather than about a room, because he could have done something about a door.**

He could not have done anything about it either. He has worked that out and it has not made him feel any better and he has not written it down.

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and a woman of about thirty in the fourth of them from about half past six, sitting where she sits, with the door of the room shut and behind her and nothing about that arrangement changed this week or any week.**

The page that belongs to her, and it is the eighth of eight, has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and fifty-seven days. It was still in it at the end of that Thursday. Nobody in this city has asked her one question about it and nobody ever is going to.

---

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in and the shutter came down at about ten, and a woman said a true thing in nine seconds in a room under a city and a man of twenty-two did not answer it and has not answered it, and a man of about thirty-eight put three lines back in his inside pocket and went up a stair nine minutes later, and a kettle went back into a bag in a carrier bag on a counter in Lattice Ward with nothing done to it, and the room went on.
