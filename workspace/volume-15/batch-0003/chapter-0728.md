# Chapter 728 — A Monday With No Figure For The Cage, A School Room In A Second District Where Eleven People Were Asked To Say Three Sentences Out Loud And Were Corrected When They Said Them Wrong, A Supply Point In A First District Where Food And Heat Are Given To People Waiting Between Shifts, A Woman Of About Twenty-Six Who Wants Her Name Taken Off A List, And A Form With Two Columns Where The Second One Is Empty

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one had eleven people in a school room being corrected over three sentences, a woman of about twenty-six asking to have her name taken off a list and being told exactly what she loses, and a form begun with two columns and one of them empty.**

---

Eleven callers against eleven dated jobs and the last name taken at half past five, and no figure for the shutter on a Monday and none since the spring.

**Line sixteen on that board of two nails is at one thousand one hundred and thirty-four days, which is one hundred and sixty-two weeks to the day, and the nine hand copies of the front of one page are at nine hundred and ten days, which is one hundred and thirty weeks to the day. Those are exact, and the four rooms off that service road at one thousand three hundred and forty-four days are the third, and there is nothing behind any of the three and nothing has ever been behind any of them.**

The separation stands at seven hundred and twenty-four days, one hundred and three weeks and three days. It is a number in a column and the column has no heading anybody has read this year.

---

**The school room is on the ground floor of a building in a second district and it has nine chairs in it and eleven of them were used, and a woman called Asha Reed stood at the front with a sheet of paper and did not teach anybody anything about this city.**

She teaches a form. Three sentences, and the paper has them printed on it at the top in large type so that everybody in the room can see them whether they can read or not.

> **I do not agree to this.**
> **I agree to this and not to that.**
> **I am stopping now and I know what that costs me.**

"**Right,**" she said. "**Say them. All three. Out loud. I am not going to do anything with them.**"

They said them. **Nine of the eleven got the first one and about four got the second and only one person in that room got the third, and she got it because she had heard it said out loud somewhere else and not in that room, and nobody asked her where.**

She spent forty minutes going round the room and saying what each of them had got wrong, and a man of about fifty-two had *I am stopping now* and not the rest of it, and was told to add the rest of it, and added it, and said it once and did not say it again. **Nobody thanked her and nobody asked her anything afterwards except a woman of about thirty-four who asked whether the sheet could be had in large print, which it could, and which was going to be done on the Friday, and which was not written down anywhere.**

---

**And a boy of about sixteen said, in front of everybody, that the third one was the only hard one because you are not supposed to know what it costs until afterwards.**

She said: "**Then it is not a choice. It is a door you are shown after it is shut. And saying that out loud, in a room, is allowed, and it is the only one of the three that anybody will answer to.**"

Nobody in that room answered it and about nine people in it had not known before that it could be said.

---

**The supply point is in a first district and it is three rooms behind a shop with a counter in the front one and a stove in the back one, and Dessa Kwan runs it and it has been running since before the spring.**

It gives a plate of food and a chair until four o'clock to anybody who comes in, and it keeps a list of names, and the list is not for anybody else and is not given to anybody and is not a count of anybody.

**It is not the only thing in this city that is warm and it is not the biggest thing. There is about eleven of these within a walk of a bus and two of the eleven are in this district and about four people in this city have said that the list should go and about nine have said that it should not and both of those numbers are guesses by people who have not been inside about nine of them.**

---

**The woman of about twenty-six came in at about half past two on the Monday and asked for her name to be taken off the list.**

She works nights. She has been on the list since the autumn. **If her name comes off, she does not lose a right to anything, because there is no right involved anywhere in this city, and what she loses is a plate and a chair until four and a room on a Thursday when the weather is the sort it has been.**

Dessa Kwan wrote nothing down and said, in about nine seconds:

"**I will take it off. That is your name and that is your business and I am not going to ask you for a reason. Here is what you lose: the plate, the chair until four, and the Thursday room. That is the whole of it. It is not a punishment and nobody has done anything to you and it costs what it costs.**"

The woman of about twenty-six said that she knew what it cost and had known since the autumn and that she wanted it off anyway.

"**Right,**" Dessa said, and took it off, in about four seconds, in front of her, and did not say anything else and has not said anything else about it since.

**She came in on the Wednesday for the plate. That is in this city and nobody has made an argument about it and nobody in that building has ever asked her why she came back and she has not said.**

---

**The form with two columns on it is on the same table in the same three rooms and it is new and it was started on that Monday by two people who were not asked to start it.**

The first column asks what a person loses by stopping. The second column asks whether anything at all can be done about any of it.

**Nine lines are filled in the first column. Nine lines are empty in the second column. There is no third column and there is no line under either of the two for anybody's name, and the form has a number at the top of it and the number is a number one of the two of them keeps and not a number anybody has taken.**

They got to about four o'clock and stopped, and about four of the nine entries in that first column are about money and about five are about a person being alone in a building, and none of the nine has anything written opposite it, and nine people in this city now know which nine things those are and not one of the nine is on this page.

---

**The rest of the Monday ran.** Eleven names and eleven dates, the last at half past five, and a man of about thirty-eight came in about four o'clock to have a table leg squared off and asked whether it was worth it and was told it was worth about two pounds and would not be worth two pounds in a year, and he paid and carried it out himself.

---

**Four people went into those four rooms off that service road at about half past six and one more into the room behind them, and the chair behind the door of the fourth one is the chair of the woman of about thirty who was in it all evening.**

The eighth of eight of her pages has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and thirty-five days and it did not come out and nobody has spoken to her and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody is going to.

---

**Three things on that Monday and none of them on a form.** A sheet of paper with three sentences printed at the top in type large enough to read from a chair. **A stove in the back room of a supply point that had been drawing badly since the spring and was drawing properly after about half an hour with a brush nobody had cleaned since the spring.** And a form with two columns and nine lines in one of them and none at all in the other.

---

**He wanted the second column filled in.**

He wanted it filled in on that Monday, in that room, by those two people, and he wanted it to have about nine things in it and he wanted them to be true and he wanted somebody else to have to find out whether they were. **Nobody asked him, there is no form he is on, and there is no arrangement in this city by which a man who fixes things gets told what nine people lose when they stop. He read the form at about five from the doorway and did not go in and has not been in that building since, and the only thing he did about it was to mend the stove in the back room before he left, which is about half an hour and thirty-one pence of parts and which is not the second column.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in on that Monday, the shutter came down at ten, and he got home at ten past ten, and he did not charge for the second fit of the flex in that kitchen because he was already in the building and because nobody was going to argue about it, and that is about four pounds and it is not in any book and he did not tell anybody he had decided that.
