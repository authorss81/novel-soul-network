# Chapter 723 — A Monday With No Cage In It, A First-Floor Room Above A Shop In A Second District With A Key Of Its Own And About Nine People Round A Table, Eleven Forms With A Column Nobody Has Ever Read Down, A Woman Of About Twenty-Four Who Refuses In About Nine Seconds To Let Four People Be Asked, And A List With No Line On It For A Signature

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one had about nine people round a table in a first-floor room going down a column on eleven forms, and a woman of about twenty-four said no to four questions in about nine seconds, and the four went back on the list unasked.**

---

Eleven dated jobs, eleven callers, and the last of them let in at half past five. There is no figure for the shutter on a Monday and there has not been one since the spring.

**Those four rooms off that service road have stood at one thousand three hundred and thirty-seven days, which is one hundred and ninety-one weeks to the day, and the nine hand copies of the front of a page have existed for nine hundred and three days, which is one hundred and twenty-nine weeks to the day. Those two are exact. The sixteenth on that board is exact too, and it is the third exact figure on this page, and it is the last one anybody has written a reason against.**

The separation runs at seven hundred and seventeen days, one hundred and two weeks and three days, and it is a number in a column that describes nothing at all and has described nothing since the spring.

---

**The room is on the first floor above a shop in a second district and it has a key of its own and nobody else's, and there are four chairs and a table and a second table against the wall with eleven more chairs on it that get used when it is a bad week.**

She is about twenty-four. Her appointment's terms have not changed and she can look into the Commons and therefore into anybody who sits on it, and neither of those facts is on the wall of that room and neither was said in it.

About nine people were round the table. Two of them were there all day. One of them has been doing that job for about four years and the other since the spring.

---

**The forms are the ones a local response runs on when the last approved sequence does not answer, and there are eleven days of them on that table in two piles, and every one of them has the same six boxes.**

The sixth box is the one that matters for what happened that Monday. It says that the person named in the second box has agreed to take part in what is being done in that building. Underneath the sixth box there is a seventh box, which is smaller, and the seventh box says who obtained that agreement.

**In nine forms out of the eleven days, the initials in the seventh box are the initials of the person who filled in the sixth box.**

The person who filled in the sixth box is, on all nine, the same woman, and she is a shift supervisor on a ward a long way from here, and the person in the second box is not, on any of the nine, the person who filled it in.

**Nobody put anything false on those nine forms. Every one of the nine people named in the second box has agreed. Four of them have been asked by somebody they know and three of them have been asked by the supervisor and two of them have not been asked by anybody at all and said yes to the supervisor about it afterwards.**

---

**A man of about thirty-eight at that table said the obvious thing, which was that somebody should go and ask the four.**

He said it twice, and the second time he said it to the woman of about twenty-four and not to the table.

"**We could put it to them,**" he said. "**We could go tomorrow and ask the four whether they said it.**"

"**No,**" she said.

"**It's four questions.**"

"**It's four people and about ten minutes,**" she said, "**and the second one of those four is going to say yes she agreed, because she did agree, and then she is going to go back to that ward and work the rest of the night knowing a piece of paper in a second district has her name in the wrong handwriting on it, and she has not done anything. She has not done anything at all. She agreed to a thing and she said so.**"

He said right and did not ask again and went back to the pile. **Nobody in that room said the word wrong and nobody in that room said the word fraud and nobody put anybody's name to anybody's face, and four people who were not in that room are named in nine forms on a table and none of them knows that this happened on a Monday.**

---

**The list has eight columns and nine lines filled in and there is no column on it for a name and no column on it for a signature and nobody has been asked to sign it.**

What goes in the list is a job: *obtains agreement for somebody else, on a shift of their own, in a building they do not work in.* That is all of it. **No one was asked whether they wanted to stop doing it. The team has no power to stop anybody and has not asked for any, and the man of about thirty-eight asked her on the Thursday before whether the team was going to go after anybody and she said no and he did not ask her anything else about it.**

**A thing about a shape is now written down on a piece of paper in nine words of a job and it is going to sit in that room until somebody with a different job reads it, and nobody in that room knows who that is.**

---

**The rest of the Monday ran.** Eleven names and eleven dates, the last at half past five, and a man of about forty came in with a lamp and asked whether the switch on the flex was meant to be that way, and was told that it was not and that it had been done that way for about eleven years and that a person of about thirty-eight at a hospital had put it there deliberately in a fortnight when about four hundred people had come in with burnt fingers.

He said right and bought a four-pound switch and fitted it himself at the counter in about six minutes and was thanked by nobody and Marek watched him do it and said nothing.

---

**Between about half past six and ten the four rooms off that service road were in use, there was one more room behind the other three, and a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them the whole of it with the chair the door is behind hers.**

Her page is the eighth of eight and it has been inside a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and thirty days, and it did not come out on that Monday, and there is nobody in this building with standing to take it out and nobody has apologised to her.

---

**Three things on that Monday and none of them on a form.** A seventh box printed smaller than the six above it, on a form that has been in use for eleven days and that nobody has asked to have reprinted. **An eighth column on a list with nothing in it, added on the Friday before by a man who did not know what it would eventually not hold.** And a lamp with a switch that has been the wrong way round since a fortnight in a year when about four hundred people came in with burnt fingers.

---

**He wanted the four of them to be asked.**

**He wanted it badly and he did not say it and he has not said it, and what he wanted was for four people who have agreed to things to be told that the agreement had been written down in the wrong hand, because as long as nobody is told then nothing in that room has ever happened. He knew as he wanted it that the woman of about twenty-four had just given the reason and had given it better than he would have given it, and that her reason was about what it would cost a person and not about whether the paper was right, and he wanted the asking anyway.**

He said none of it.
Eleven jobs went out and eleven came in that Monday, the shutter came down at ten, and he was home at ten past ten, and he had about eleven minutes of the morning in which he could have gone up one floor and knocked and did not, and he has never once been in that room and has no reason to be in it and there is no arrangement in this city by which anybody would have asked him.
