# Chapter 725 — A Wednesday And Two Sets Of Rules In One Day, A Repair Cooperative On A Street Of Small Workshops In A First District Where The Rota Is In Chalk And Four Vans Belong To Eleven People And Nobody Is Asked To Do A Second Thing, A Meeting At About Five In The Same District Where Four Streets Are Offered A Place Inside Something And Decline It In About Nine Seconds, And A Vice That Needed Greasing Before Any Of It

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one was spent in a first district, first in a workshop where the day list is in chalk and can be rubbed out by anybody, then in a room at about five where four streets were offered a place inside something and declined it in about nine seconds.**

---

Nobody at that counter saw eleven of anything. Eleven jobs were dated, eleven names went on the book, and the last of them came at half past five, and the cage shook when the shutter came down at ten for about the twelfth time in a fortnight.

**The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is at nine hundred and forty-five days, one hundred and thirty-five weeks to the day, and the ask is at one thousand and twenty-nine days, one hundred and forty-seven weeks to the day. Those two are exact, and the eighteenth line is the only other exact figure on this page at one thousand and fifty-seven days, one hundred and fifty-one weeks to the day, and there is no reason written under any of the three.**

The separation is at seven hundred and nineteen days, one hundred and two weeks and five days. It has been in a box in a form for about sixteen months and it is a number that has gone on being correct without anybody doing anything to keep it correct.

---

**The cooperative is on a street of small workshops in a first district and it has four vans and about eleven people and it started as three people mending each other's things and has not changed its rules since except to add a fourth van.**

The rules are painted on a board by the door and are also on the bench, and the day list is in chalk on the bench, and the chalk list is the one that counts. **Anyone may rub their own name off the chalk list. Nobody may rub anybody else's name off it. Nobody is asked to do a second thing in a day, and the reason for that rule is on the board and is about a woman who did eleven hours in a week in which two other people did nothing.**

Marek was in there from about half past ten until about one, with a vice on the bench that needed the spindle cleaned out and a new key fitted, which is about four pounds of parts and forty minutes, and nobody charged him for the forty minutes because he is not a member and nobody was going to ask him to be one.

---

**A man of about thirty-three put his hand on the chalk at about eleven and rubbed his own name off the end of the day, and a woman of about forty-two at the next bench looked at him and then looked away, and neither of them said anything, and he went out at about half past eleven to a job of his own on a Thursday.**

Nobody in that room asked him why. There is no form for it. The chalk took the name off and there is a second piece of chalk on the string for the case where the first has gone blunt and about four times since the spring somebody has come in and said the chalk was blunt and has been told where the second one is.

---

**The second half of that Wednesday was a meeting in a first district from about five to about twenty past six, in a room above a shop, and it was about whether four streets should be brought inside the standard response that has been running in this city since all of it began.**

There were about thirty people in it. **Leo Marr was on a platform at the front and she was there because she asked to be and she got nothing out of being there, and she has asked twice and been given nothing twice, and she is not angry about it and has not said so on any page of this book.**

A man of about forty-four from a district office put the case: the four streets were not on the list, their response was not on the list, and if they came inside then their people would be counted, and counting was the thing everybody wanted and nobody could say why they wanted it.

**Leo Marr stood up and said four sentences and one of them was the answer.**

"**Then we stop being four streets,**" she said, "**and we become four rooms, and the rooms have a person in them who decides, and that person is not us and we cannot see them and we cannot stop them. We have run this whole time on a rule anybody on that street can break and a pump that four people own. Take it away and we will be inside it in a morning and your count will be right.**"

The man of about forty-four said that he was not asking about anybody deciding anything.

"**You are asking us to be a room,**" she said. "**You may not have said it that way but that is the shape of the question.**"

He sat down. **Nobody in that room applauded and four people agreed with her out loud and about nine people disagreed with her out loud and about seventeen said nothing at all, and there was no vote, and the meeting ended at about twenty past six, and she has not been asked to be on anything since.**

---

**The rest of that Wednesday did not stop.** A woman of about thirty-eight brought a lamp into the shop at about four and wanted the flex cut back by about four inches and paid two pounds and asked whether the shop would take a second one in on the Saturday and Marek said he would and wrote it in the book, which is the only thing written in that book this month that anybody in this city has read twice.

---

**Four people went into those four rooms off that service road at about half past six and a fifth went into the one behind the other three, and the chair in the room whose door is behind it belongs to the woman of about thirty who was in it.**

Her eighth page of eight was still in a ring binder on a back shelf where it has been for a hundred and thirty-two days, and it did not come out, and nobody said a word to her in three and a half hours and nobody has ever said sorry to her in any form and there is nobody in this building who could.

---

**Three things on that Wednesday, and not one of them a matter of anything.** A piece of chalk on a string with about four knots in it where the knots mark how many times the second stick has been fetched. A form in a first district with a box on it for a number of households and a second box under it, smaller, that says *street*, and it was left empty by nine people. And a vice on a bench in a first district with the spindle cleaned out and a new key in it and forty minutes of somebody else's time.

---

**He wanted a van to break down outside the cooperative.**

He wanted it because the cooperative has never had to ask the four streets for anything, and he wanted to see what a van looks like at about two o'clock in the afternoon with a foot down on a street where nobody is connected to anything. He wanted to find out whether the two of them would speak to each other. **They do not speak to each other. There is no arrangement in this city by which anybody at that cooperative knows where the four streets are or what is on them, and nine people who have been in both places have said so in about nine different conversations and none of them has put the two together in a sentence.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, he went home at ten past ten, and the fan belt on his own van went at about five on the way back from a first district and he walked two miles with a bag in his hand and got the bus and it cost him about eleven pounds and two hours and neither of those is in the load book.

*Chalk on a bench, and a street that is not a room.* **Two things worked on that Wednesday and neither of them worked because anybody had an idea, and the two of them worked because the rules in them were built separately and by people who have never spoken to each other, and about nine people in this city have been in both places and not one of them has put them together in a sentence. The register of correct acts with no consequence stands at two and did not move on that day, and the reason is that keeping a rule you built yourself is not an act and refusing to be brought inside somebody else's is not an act either, and a name rubbed off a piece of chalk before eleven in the morning is a man going to work tomorrow rather than a deed.
