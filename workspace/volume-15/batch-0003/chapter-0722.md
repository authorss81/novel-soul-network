# Chapter 722 — A Friday, Eleven Names Taken By Half Past Five, A Pumping Station At The End Of A Terrace In A First District With Two Machines And Three People In One Room, A Man Who Asks Them To Choose The Hour And Is Refused In About Nine Words By A Woman Of About Thirty-One, And A Man Who Goes And Finds The Man Who Signs

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one had three people in a pumping station being asked to pick the hour themselves, and they said no in about nine words, and it worked anyway about two hours later with a signature on it.**

---

The book took eleven names before half past five and every one of the eleven had a date against it. The cage shook when the shutter came down at ten, which it has now done about twelve times in a fortnight.

**Eight hundred and eighty-two days is how long what is in the post at that corridor end has been there, which is one hundred and twenty-six weeks to the day, and the separation is at seven hundred and fourteen days, which is one hundred and two weeks to the day. Both of those are exact, and the only other figure on this page that is exact is the seventeen line at one thousand one hundred and six days, and none of the three has anything behind it.**

---

**The station is at the end of a terrace in a first district and it is one room under a roof with a door that shuts and a slate on the wall and two machines standing side by side on a slab of concrete.**

The running one is on. The other is off and has been off since it was last on, which was nine days ago. There is a changeover every day at four in the afternoon because that is when the demand comes off and the tank has to be filled from the other side for the night. Two people have to be in that room to do it, one at the switches and one at a machine, and the man who signs for it is a man of about fifty-two who comes from an office on the far side and signs on a slate and goes.

There were three people in that room. A woman of about thirty-one with a lamp in her hand, a man of about forty-four who does the rounds and had come in to change a belt, and a man of about fifty-two who does not sign but had come to look at a noise.

**A card had gone into that station nine days ago from a hospital and a transit office together, and the card says the same words are used now for both, and there is nothing on it about a sign and nothing on it about a slate and nothing about a changeover, and it is in a plastic wallet on the slate and nobody has taken it down.**

---

**The man who came at about two was called Oren Vey and he had a folder and he was not in a hurry about it.**

He asked whether the changeover could be done without the signature, because the man who signed was on a ward until Monday and the tank was going to be low by the end of the week. He said it as a question and he was not asking anybody to break anything and he had read the card.

The woman of about thirty-one put the lamp down.

"**No,**" she said. "**We keep them running. We don't pick the hour.**"

"**That's not what I asked,**" he said.

"**It is what I answered,**" she said, "**and there's a reason and it's not modesty. If I get that hour wrong on a Friday then nobody afterwards can tell whether I got it wrong or whether the tank was low, and there's no way in this world to tell those two things apart from the outside.**"

That was about nine seconds and it was all of it. **He did not argue with the middle of it. He asked one question, which was whether she would sign it herself if he made her the person who signed it, and she said that would be worse, and he said right, and put the folder on the bench.**

**The man of about forty-four said nothing at all during that, and he has been asked since whether he said anything and he says he did not, and the man of about fifty-two who had come about a noise says the same.**

---

**Oren Vey left at about ten past two and got a bus and then walked the rest and came back at about twenty to five with the man of about fifty-two who signs, and the two of them were in that room from about twenty past five until about ten past six, and the changeover was done at five minutes past six with a mark on a slate.**

It went perfectly. The second machine took the load in about four seconds, which is faster than it has ever done, and the first machine ran down and stopped, and the tank came up over the night and nobody in that terrace went without water at any point, and the second machine was louder than the first and the man of about fifty-two wrote that on the slate as well, because he writes everything that is true on that slate.

**Nobody in that room thanked anybody. Oren Vey stood in the doorway for about eleven minutes and did not come in and did not say anything, and he left before it was finished, and he has not been back to that station.**

---

**The rest of the Friday ran at the counter.** Eleven names and eleven dates, the last at half past five, and a woman of about forty-four came in with a handrail bracket that had sheared and wanted it made good and was told it would be made good and would not be as strong as the one that came off, and she said she knew that, and paid about six pounds, and asked whether the shop could hold it until her son came out of the hospital on the Monday, and Marek said yes and did not write it on the book.

**The card in the rail has been in the rail for one thousand three hundred and thirty-eight days and there is nothing in the rail beside it, and the four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand three hundred and thirty-four days with one room behind the other three.**

---

**From about half past six until ten there were four people in those four rooms and one more behind the three, and the fifth person was a woman of about thirty in the room whose door is behind her chair.**

The eighth of eight pages has been on a back shelf inside a ring binder for a hundred and twenty-nine days. It did not come out. Not one person in that building has put a question to her about anything in three and a half hours, and nobody has said sorry to her in any form, and nobody is going to.

---

**Three things on that Friday, and not one of them on a form.** A slate with two marks on it and one of them says the second machine is louder. **A lamp left on a bench in a pumping station with the cell going flat and a man who has taken it out of his own bag for the two of them and said nothing about it.** And a handrail bracket being held over from that Friday to a Monday because a woman asked and it was said yes to and not written down.

---

**He wanted the man of about forty-four to have said something.**

**The man of about forty-four did not say anything, and Marek would have given him the whole of the next four minutes to say one sentence about the hour and about who signs it, and the man would not have taken it, and he knew that four seconds after he wanted it. What the man did instead was carry the second lamp to the woman of about thirty-one and put it down within reach and go back to the belt he had come in for, and nobody thanked him for that either and nobody was going to.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and he had said yes to holding a handrail bracket until the Monday and had not written it anywhere, and if the shop had been shut when the son came out on the Monday morning it would have been nowhere at all.

*Nine seconds in a pumping station, and about eleven minutes in a doorway.* **A woman of about thirty-one said the word no to a man with a folder about the one thing her three people are trusted to do, and the reason she gave was not about modesty and was about the fact that a room can never afterwards tell a wrong hour from a low tank, and no room in this city has a way of telling those two things apart from the outside, and this is the first time anybody has put that in a face. The register of correct acts with no consequence stood at two and did not move on that Friday, and the reason is that keeping a second machine alive is not a correct act and neither is saying out loud what you will not be trusted with, and the two of them are the ordinary work of a Tuesday and are not on any list anybody keeps.
