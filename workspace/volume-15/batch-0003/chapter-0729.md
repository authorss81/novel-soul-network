# Chapter 729 — A Tuesday, A Block Of Flats In A First District With A Brick In It Holding A Door That Would Not Stay Shut, A Man Of About Sixty-Three Who Has Been Letting It Propped For About Eleven Days And Who Has To Have A Key Instead, A Clipboard With Two Boxes On It And No Third, And A Gate Latch Four Hundred Yards Down The Road That Was Done In About Four Minutes And Is Not On Anything

**A Tuesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one was four floors of a block in a first district with a brick in it, and a clipboard on a landing with two boxes on it, and four hundred yards down that road a gate latch that got about four minutes and nothing else at all.**

---

Eleven jobs, a date against each, and eleven names on the book with the last of the eleven at half past five. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and that is about fourteen times in a fortnight.

**Nine hundred and eleven days is how long the nine hand copies of the front of one page have existed, which comes out at one hundred and thirty weeks and one day, and that man of about fifty-one is at nine hundred and fifty-one days against the north wall of this shop, which comes out at one hundred and thirty-five weeks and six days. Those are the two figures at the top of this page and not one figure in this day's book comes out exact.**

The separation is at seven hundred and twenty-five days, one hundred and three weeks and four days. It went into a box in a form about sixteen months ago, and it is the only thing in this book that has not once been surprising.

---

**He had the call at about a quarter past nine and was on the landing of that block by about half past ten, and it is a bricklayer's door on the ground floor that has been on a hinge for about thirty years and has had no closer on it since before the spring.**

It would not stay shut. It had been propped with a brick for eleven days, which is what happens when a door has no closer and the street it opens on is a wide one and there is wind on it. The brick has worn a shallow groove in the threshold and there is a black mark on the edge of the door about eleven inches up where somebody has been catching it to shut it by hand, and the black mark is not paint.

**A man of about sixty-three came out of the caretaker's office with a clipboard while Marek was up a stepladder and watched him for four minutes without saying anything.**

He lives on the first floor and has been letting the door propped since the Friday and had not complained because it is warmer with the wind not coming in.

There was a closer in a van in about eleven minutes. It took about half an hour to fit and about four pounds of parts, and the door shuts now from anywhere on its swing, and the brick is on the sill by the radiator where the man of about sixty-three has put it and has not thrown away.

---

**The clipboard has two boxes printed across the top of a sheet and the sheet is a carbon copy with a pink sheet under it.**

One box says *Fault.* One box says *Time.*

He filled both in. He wrote the fault in about nine words, which was too many for the box, and the time as twenty to twelve in the morning, and then he handed the clipboard back and the man of about sixty-three said, "**Right,**" and said thank you, and Marek said it was nothing, which is what he says.

"**Who shall I put?**" the man said.

"**Put what.**"

"**There's no box for it,**" the man said, "**it's fault and time and there's never been a third.**"

"**Then leave it,**" Marek said.

**He wrote his name on the edge of the carbon sheet instead, in the margin, on the line above where it clips on, and the man of about sixty-three looked at it and said that it would get wet and that they were wet most weeks, and Marek said it would still be there for about a week, and the sheet went on the board and by the Thursday the name was gone and the two boxes were still on it.**

---

**The door had to be unlocked now, because it shuts.** That is the whole of the cost of that repair and it belongs to somebody who was not in the room and did not ask for the door.

**The man of about sixty-three has a key on a loop of wire and has to turn it from the outside and push, and about nine people in that block had walked through that propped door at some point on most days without touching anything, and about four of them were doing it with a full hand or a pram or a bag, and they now have to stop.**

Nobody in that block has said one word about it to him. A woman of about thirty-one said to him at about half past two that it was a shame about the door, and he said it was, and she said right and went in, and neither of them meant anything by either sentence and both of them knew it.

---

**Four hundred yards down that road there is a gate between two back yards and the latch on it has been off for about four months and anybody who wanted through it could have walked round and nobody wanted to.**

He saw it on the way back at about one. It took four minutes. There is no form for that gate, there is no caretaker for it, there is no landlord anybody knows of, and it belongs to nobody who has ever written anything down. **He was not asked to do it and nobody has found out that he did it and nobody paid him for it and nobody knows it was done except a man of about forty-four who came through it at about ten past four and found that it did what he had assumed it did.**

He did not put it on the book and it is not in the load book of this or any other day and there is no arrangement in this city that would put it there.

---

**The rest of the Tuesday ran.** Eleven names and eleven dates, the last at half past five, and a woman of about thirty-eight came in at about four with a heater off a bedroom wall and said it went on and off and was told that it would go on and off, and she said that is not an answer, and it was the same answer, and she paid about eleven pounds for a rewire that will not do anything for it, and knows that, and has not been back.

---

**The four rooms off that service road were in use from about half past six with one more room behind them, and the woman of about thirty sat in the fourth one with the chair the door is behind being hers until ten.**

Her page, the eighth of eight, has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and thirty-six days. It did not come out on that Tuesday, and nobody asked for it, and nobody apologised to her in any form, and nobody is going to.

---

**Three things on that Tuesday and not one of them the same kind of thing.** A brick on a sill by a radiator that has worn a groove in a threshold. **A name in the margin of a carbon sheet that had gone by the Thursday.** And a latch on a gate that cost four minutes and is not on any form in this city.

---

**He wanted to have been asked to put his name in a box that does not exist.**

**He wanted a man of about sixty-three to say, on a landing, that the form ought to have a third box on it, and to say it as a fault in a form and not as a favour to a man of twenty-two, and to write it down so that it existed, and nobody said it, and the sheet went on the board with two boxes and a name in the margin and by Thursday there was neither. He did not say anything. He has not said anything about it since and there is nobody here he could say it to.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and the call-out he turned down at about eleven in a third district was done by a man from that street for four pounds more than Marek would have charged and about ten minutes quicker than he would have done it, and the four pounds went into somebody else's pocket and not into this one and he has not rung that man.
