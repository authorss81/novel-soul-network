# Chapter 724 — A Tuesday, A Counter With A Table Bolted To The End Of It, A Woman Of About Thirty-Nine Who Has Been Refused Four Times Since The Spring And Who Is Given A Box At About Two Without Being Asked To Sit Down, A Man Who Says When It Was Written And Says He Is Going Back To His Office At Four, And About Four Lines Of Paper That Were In That Building The Whole Time

**A Tuesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one had a box put down on a bolted table at about two, and one person in that shop read one page of it and put it back, and nobody thanked the man who brought it or forgave him or told him anything about himself.**

---

A Tuesday of eleven jobs and eleven names, and the last name came in at half past five and not after it. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, which is about the twelfth time in a fortnight.

**One thousand three hundred and thirty-eight days is how long those four rooms and the one behind them have stood off that service road, which comes out at one hundred and ninety-one weeks and one day, and one thousand two hundred and fifty-eight days is the twelfth line up on that board of two nails, which comes out at one hundred and seventy-nine weeks and five days. Neither of those is exact on this page and neither of them is close to it, and there is not one figure in this day's book that comes out exact.**

The separation is at seven hundred and eighteen days, one hundred and two weeks and four days, and it is a subtraction and has been a subtraction since the spring, and there is nobody in this city who has ever had to look at it twice.

---

**The box is a card index box and it came in under his arm and he set it on the table at the end of the counter, which is four feet long, bolted through the floor, and put at about the height of the till, and which about four people in this city eat their lunch at.**

He is called Iven Sore and he has an office and the office has not been named on any page of this book before today. He set the box down, straightened up, and did not take his coat off.

"**Three thousand, six hundred and forty,**" he said. "**It is not four thousand. Somebody has told you four thousand and it is not four thousand.**"

"**What is in it.**"

"**Everything that office holds on the place called Marrow Falls,**" he said. "**Minutes, memoranda, the works correspondence, and the pre-season paperwork, which is the part nobody used to ask for because it is dull.**"

---

**She came in at about a quarter past two and stood at that table and did not sit down, and he did not offer her the stool and she did not ask for it.**

She is about thirty-nine. She has asked four people in four buildings for one document since the spring and has been refused four times, and all four refusals were correct, and one of them was a letter with a reason in it and the other three were people saying the request was not hers to make.

She opened the box on that table and went through it fast and in order and did not read anything twice.

At about eleven minutes in she laid one sheet down flat and turned it round so that it read the right way up for the man at the counter rather than for her, and she did not say that she had done it and it was not for him.

---

**It is a page with a stamp in the corner that has to be turned sideways to read, and it was written before the autumn, and it says what happens.**

It says that where a local group runs a provisional response in place of the last approved sequence, and there is no local release, then nobody in that group can bring it to a stop, and that this is known, and it says it in the plainest words anybody in this city has used about anything.

**About four lines of it are the part that matters. The rest is a routing sheet with eleven names on it, and one of the eleven is a woman who has never been in this shop and one is a man of about forty-four who has never been in this shop and about nine of the eleven are in offices.**

"**When was that written,**" she said.

"**Before the autumn,**" he said. "**Two people signed it at the top. One has retired. One is still in that building and has been for nine years.**"

"**Is that the whole of it.**"

"**That is the whole of it,**" he said. "**It was never a secret. It is a piece of paper in a drawer in a building with a name on the door, and four people have asked for it and been told that it is dull.**"

---

**She said one thing, about eleven seconds after she had read it, and it was not the thing anybody in that shop had been ready for.**

"**Then somebody has to stand up in a room and say that this was here,**" she said, "**and I do not know who that is going to be and I am not going to be told, and I wanted that piece of paper since the spring and I have got it and I would rather I had not.**"

Nobody in that shop answered her. He did not answer her and she did not ask him to. Marek put a kettle on at about half past two for no reason anybody in the room has since given, and that kettle is the only thing in this building that is asked to do anything all day.

---

**He said he was not resigning before anybody asked him, and nobody had asked him.**

"**I am going back to that office at four,**" he said. "**About nine hundred sheets in that box are other people's writing, and I am one of four people who know which drawer they are in. If I stop coming in then nobody in this city knows where the drawer is.**"

She said "**Right**" in a voice that was not angry and not grateful, picked the box up, and carried it out through the door it had come in by. He stayed at the counter for another hour and a half, did not explain himself again, and has not been back, and there is no arrangement in this city by which anybody would have written to him.

---

**The rest of the Tuesday ran.** Eleven names and eleven dates, the last at half past five, and a woman of about thirty-three came in for a length of cord and wanted to know whether it was being measured off the same bolt as last time, and it was not, because the bolt was replaced on Friday, and she said that was all right and paid and left it on the counter and came back for it after half past five.

---

**The four rooms off that service road were in use from about half past six, with one room behind the other three, and the fifth person in that building was in the fourth of them with the chair the door is behind being hers.**

The page that belongs with her is the eighth of eight. It has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and thirty-one days, it did not come out of there on that Tuesday, and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will.

---

**Three things on that Tuesday and none of them on a form.** A page with a stamp in its corner printed upside down for somebody standing at the other side of a table. A drawer handle worn pale on the left and dark on the right in a building about a long walk from here, where somebody has opened the same drawer about nine hundred times and nobody has oiled it. And a bolt of cord on a counter where the length has been measured off a different bolt since Friday and the two people who come in for cord have not been told.

---

**He wanted the man with the box to have taken the box back with him.**

He wanted that for about four seconds and then he wanted something worse, which was for the woman of about thirty-nine to have said something else, and she said what she said and what she said was better than what he wanted, and it was about a person standing up in a room, and he has never been in that room and does not know who is in it. **He read that one page standing up at the end of his own counter at about four and then put it back in the box face down, and it went out of the shop the way it came in, and nobody knew he had read it and he has not mentioned it to anybody and is not going to.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he got home at ten past ten, and there was a call-out in a fourth district at about half past four that he had driven past on the way in and had not put on the book and has not rung anybody about since.

*About four lines of paper, and nobody consoled.* **Iven Sore put three thousand, six hundred and forty sheets of his own office's records on a table in a repair shop and the sheets prove that what has been happening in this city since all of it began was written down before the autumn by two people, one of whom is still in that building, and a document that proves a thing is worse than a document that hides it, and nobody in that shop was comforted by any part of it. He was not thanked, was not forgiven, was not asked to resign and said so before anybody asked, and he went back at four because he knows a drawer. The register of correct acts with no consequence stands at two and did not move on that Tuesday, and the reason is that releasing a record is not an act of conscience and is not an act of anything else that this city has a column for, and being proved right about a thing that was already true is not a consequence. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.
