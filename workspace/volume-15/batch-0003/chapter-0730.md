# Chapter 730 — A Wednesday, A Counter Shut At Half Past Five Instead Of About Six, A Room Above A Line In Saltmarket With Nine Chairs In It And One Of Them Hard Against A Wall With Its Back To The Room, A Man Of About Fifty-Two Who Says A Thing To A Woman Of About Sixty In About Nine Seconds And Does Not Say It Again, Fifty-Nine Of Which Fifty-Four, And One Line

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one shut the counter at half past five and went up a stair, and a man said nine seconds' worth of a thing to a woman of about sixty, and one line went into a book, and a number was said out loud for the first time in a long while.**

---

Eleven names and eleven dates, and the last of the eleven at half past five, which was half an hour earlier than the shutter has been for about eleven days. The cage shook anyway, about fourteen times in a fortnight.

**Line eighteen on that board of two nails is at one thousand and sixty-four days, which is one hundred and fifty-two weeks to the day, and the ask is at one thousand and thirty-six days, which is one hundred and forty-eight weeks to the day. That man of about fifty-one at the north wall makes the third exact figure on this page at nine hundred and fifty-two days, one hundred and thirty-six weeks to the day, and there is no fourth, and none of the three has a reason written under it.**

The separation is at seven hundred and twenty-six days, one hundred and three weeks and five days, and it has never needed attending to.

---

**The room is above a line in Saltmarket and it has nine chairs in it and a table with a book on it that has a green cover, and there is a tin beside the book with a lid on it and a receipt in the tin as a page marker, and the receipt is not hers.**

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room and it has not moved. Nobody in this city has named the person who put it there and nobody is going to and it is not a question anybody in that room has ever been asked.**

The place behind the woman's chair is empty and has been empty since a week in the spring. **Nobody has asked her about it. About four people in this city can tell you how long it has been and none of them has said it to her and she has not brought it up and she is not going to.**

---

She is about sixty and she has held that room for about nineteen years and she has not been asked a question about anything in the whole of that time by anybody who came up those stairs.

**The book was on sixty-four lines when she opened the door at about half past six and it was on sixty-four lines when the first three people had been in and out of it, and the tin did not move and was not opened, because it is not opened on a Wednesday and has not been for about eleven years.**

---

**The man of about fifty-two came up at about a quarter past seven with the van parked on the line below and he did not sit down and he had not been in that room before.**

She let him stand. He said what he had come to say and it took nine seconds and there was nothing in front of it and nothing after it.

"**There were nine of them and every one of them was right,**" he said, "**and I was still four streets away at ten past four and the list had closed, and I am not here to have anything changed because it has not been changed.**"

She wrote for about four seconds.

Then she said the number, which she says at a sitting and on no other day and says once: "**Fifty-nine of which fifty-four.**"

And the man of about fifty-two said "**Right**" and went out and down the stair and got into his van and has not been up those stairs again, and nobody in that room said one word to him before he went or after, and she did not ask him a single question and did not ask him to stay, and the page has his description of a Friday on it and not his name.

---

**There were about nine people in that room over about an hour and a half and the whole of what happened was that four of them came to the table and said a thing and three of them did not, and one line went in and it is one line and there are sixty-five lines on that page now, and there were sixty-four at half past six.**

The woman of about thirty-one from the second week of that month came at about half past seven and said nine words about a box and did not say anything about anything else. A man of about fifty-three who had not been in that room before stood at the back for about eleven minutes and did not sit and did not say anything to anybody and left at about eight, and nobody has ever said anything about him since, and he was not on a list.

---

**Marek had been in that room from about twenty to seven and had said nothing at all.**

He had wanted to say the thing about the second one. He had it ready and it was eleven words long and it was on the tip of his tongue at about a quarter past seven while a man of about fifty-two was standing in front of a table saying nine seconds, and **he did not say it, and the reason he did not say it is that saying it there would have been a way of being in it, and being in it is the thing he has been refusing to be for about four years, and he got the same answer from a different staircase and he has still not told anybody what the answer was.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody and nobody in that room was taught, shown, assessed or helped by anybody in it, and nobody has been told that a line went into that book on that Wednesday except the four people who were in the room when it happened.**

---

**The four rooms off that service road were empty that evening for the first time in about a month and were not missed, and there was no fifth room behind them, and the woman of about thirty was not in any of them and nobody has asked her where she was.**

Her page, the eighth of eight, is still in a ring binder on a back shelf. It has been there a hundred and thirty-seven days, and it did not come out on that Wednesday, and nobody has apologised to her in any form, and nobody is going to, and she was not there and nobody noticed and nobody wrote it down.

---

**Three things on that Wednesday and none of them about the sitting.** A receipt in a tin that is not the property of the woman whose tin it is and has been there since the spring. **A chair that is hard against a wall with its back to a room, which has not moved and whose mover is not named.** And a shutter that came down at half past five instead of about six for the first time in eleven days, which made the cage shake once instead of at the end of the day.

---

**He wanted the book to have shut again by the time he got to the bottom of the stair.**

He wanted that more than he has wanted anything this month, and it shut, and he did not find out that it had shut until the man of about thirty-eight from a warehouse asked him a fortnight afterwards what had been written in it, and Marek said nothing to that either. **There is one line on that page now and there was one line on it before that he had never seen, and the two of them are not the same line and nobody in this city has put them together and neither has he.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at half past five instead of about six, and he went home at about eleven and not at ten past ten, and he has been up that stair before about nine times in nineteen years of it being there and has not been four times in a month before this.
