# Chapter 727 — A Friday, Nine Junctions In A First District Each Holding For The Right Length Of Time, A Man Of About Fifty-Two With A Watch In His Hand And A Van And A Clinic On The Far Side That Closes Its List At Four, A Woman Of About Forty-One Who Puts A Third Line On A Board Nobody In This City Has Counted, And Nobody In Any Of It Who Was Asked

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one was in a first district all afternoon, at nine junctions, each of which did the right thing for exactly as long as it is supposed to, and a man with a watch in his hand was still four streets away when the last of them finished.**

---

Twelve of nothing wrong, and eleven jobs dated and the last name at half past five. The shutter came down at ten, the cage shook, and that is about the thirteenth time in a fortnight.

**Line seventeen on that board of two nails stands at one thousand one hundred and thirteen days, which is one hundred and fifty-nine weeks to the day, and what is in the post at the end of that corridor has been there eight hundred and eighty-nine days, which is one hundred and twenty-seven weeks to the day. The separation makes the third exact figure on this page at seven hundred and twenty-one days, one hundred and three weeks to the day, and there is not a fourth and there is no reason under any of the three.**

---

**The junctions are in a first district and there are nine of them and they have been staffed for about eleven days by people doing the standard thing, which is to hold the crossing for the length written on a card and then to let it go even if there is somebody in it.**

Holding means that a marshal stands in the road with two boards and turns them, and the card says how long, and the length is the same at all nine and it is two minutes and forty seconds, and it is correct, and it has been correct for eleven days.

There are about four people on each shift and about nine people at the fourth junction alone because it is on a bus route, and none of them is paid for it and about half of them are doing it between other jobs and one of them has a van parked round the corner because she has to be at a school gate by half past three.

**Nobody at any of the nine junctions has broken the standard action. Nobody has held on. Nobody has let go early. There is no dispute and there is nobody to have one with and no instruction that asks for anything else.**

---

**The man of about fifty-two came into the fourth junction at about four o'clock with a watch in his hand and about six minutes of slack in it and a van.**

He had a woman of about thirty-one in the van with a bag. She had a clinic appointment at half past four on the far side, which runs on a Friday only, and which cannot be moved because the person who does it is not there on any other day.

He told the marshal. The marshal did not look up.

"**It is two minutes and forty,**" the marshal said. "**It is on the card. I cannot hold it for a van.**"

He said that he understood and he said it about four seconds after he said it and he did not say it twice.

**He sat in the van at that junction for two minutes and forty seconds while two boards turned over in the road in front of him at the correct length, and then he went through it, and then he was in a queue, and he arrived at about five past six, and the list had closed at four and had been closed for about two hours, and the woman of about thirty-one was told that on Friday.**

**Nobody in that junction was told about it afterwards. About four people saw the van and the watch and did not connect them. He has not said to anybody that he was held at a junction, and he has not said it to the woman in the van, and she has not asked, and she has been back to that clinic on the Monday and waited four hours and has still not been seen and has said nothing to anybody about Friday.**

---

**The woman of about forty-one came off shift at that fourth junction at about half past four and did not go home.**

The board is on a wall of a room in a first district that opens at six and closes at ten and has about nine chairs in it. It has been on that wall since before the spring. **On it there are two lines, both of them in a hand that is not hers, both of them about four months old, and nobody has counted them in the whole time that wall has carried a list, and nobody in this city has read the wall since a woman of about thirty-eight went past it and looked at it without stopping.**

**She wrote a third one at about twenty to five in the morning's hand, which is a hand that has been up since four, and she put it on the wall under the other two, and then she went home and cooked a dinner and did not tell anybody she had been there.**

It says: *Held the correct length at nine junctions on a Friday. Nothing came of any of it.*

---

**Nobody has counted that board. It is not on a list and it is not in an office and it is not in a book and it has no line under it for anybody to sign and it is not going anywhere.**

About four people walk past that wall every evening between six and ten. **One of them is a man of about thirty-three who goes to a room off a service road and has never stopped at it. One of them runs a counter in this city and has read that line and has told nobody and has not mentioned it to the man who works at that counter and is not going to.** Nobody has told the woman of about forty-one and she has not asked and there is no arrangement in this city for asking her whether she put anything on that wall.

**And what the third line cost is not the city. It cost the man of about fifty-two a wait of two minutes and forty seconds at a junction, a queue of about twenty people, a Friday afternoon and a Monday he cannot get, and his wife's name being in a book at a clinic that he has not been in the room of and has not asked about. Nine people held two boards for the right length of time and every one of the nine was correct, and not one of the nine will ever be told that the sum of the nine was a man who did not get to a place, and that is the whole of the first honest third instance of a correct act with no consequence that anybody in this city can point to.**

---

**The rest of the Friday ran and nothing in this city was broken.** Eleven names and eleven dates, the last at half past five, and a woman of about forty-four brought in a kettle that had stopped switching off and was told that the switch had been the wrong type for about eleven years and that a new one was a pound and about ten minutes, and she said she would think about it and has thought about it and has not been back and is still boiling by the sound of it.

---

**Four people went into the four rooms off that service road at about half past six and one more into the room behind them, and the woman of about thirty was in the fourth of them with the chair behind the door being hers.**

Her page, the eighth of eight, has been inside a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and thirty-four days and it did not come out on that Friday and it was not asked after by anybody and nobody has said sorry to her in any form and nobody is going to.

---

**Three things on that Friday, and every one of them is a matter of arithmetic.** Two boards and a card with two minutes and forty seconds written on it in about nine different hands over eleven days. **A line on a board that is the third line on it and the first one anybody has added since the spring.** And a clock on a dashboard in a van that reads a quarter past six and is four minutes fast and has been four minutes fast since the spring and which the man of about fifty-two did not look at once because he was looking at a queue.

---

**He wanted the man of about fifty-two to have said something at the junction.**

Not at the marshal. **He wanted him to have stopped the van and got out and stood in the road and been a man about a van with a clock on it in front of nine people, and the marshal would have had to do something about it and there would have been something on a piece of paper about the something, and instead he said that he understood and he sat in the van and he went through at ten past four and he has not said a word to anybody.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and he gave away a switch for a kettle that is worth a pound to a man of about thirty-three who asked for one and who did not know what a switch was for and did not say so and has fitted it wrong.
