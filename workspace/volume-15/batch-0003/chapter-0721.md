# Chapter 721 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Stairwell In A Second District With A Key On A Hook That Was Screwed Into The Wall Before The Spring, A Man Of About Forty-One Who Asked For A Door To Be Let Go At Half Past Three And Was Given Six O'Clock By A Woman Of About Fifty Doing The Job The Key Is For, And A Lift In That Block That Has Been Out Since Friday

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one went out to a second district at about half past three, to a stairwell where a key hangs on a hook screwed into the wall before the spring, and a man of about forty-one was given six o'clock by a woman doing the job the key is for.**

---

Eleven jobs with a date against each and eleven names on the book, the last name taken at half past five. The shutter came down at ten and the cage shook, and that had happened about eleven times in a fortnight.

**The card standing on end in that rail has been in it one thousand three hundred and thirty-seven days, one hundred and ninety-one weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns has been on the same floor of the same building for nine hundred and sixty-six days, one hundred and thirty-eight weeks to the day. Five other figures on this page are exact in the same way and there is nothing behind any of the six.**

The separation stands at seven hundred and thirteen days, one hundred and one weeks and six days, and it went into a one-line box in a form about sixteen months ago and there is nothing in the box and there has not been since the spring.

---

**He had gone out to the block at about half past three to fit a door closer on the second-floor landing, because a closer had been off its bracket since Friday and the door had been swinging into the corridor all weekend and the caretaker wanted it shut.**

The stair was cold and there was a smell of old coats. There is a steel case on the wall at the bottom of it with a keyhole in the top of it and a hook screwed into the brick beside the case at about the height of a man's chest, and the key has been on that hook since before the spring and the hook is at that height because a man who put it there in the spring when the case went in was working to the bottom of the light switch.

The key opens the case. Inside the case there is a handle and a length of cable going up the wall, and the cable goes to four doors and every one of those doors is on the same release. If somebody upstairs cannot get to the case, the release is run at the bottom, and the doors let go, and the case has to be reset by hand afterwards and the reset takes about eleven seconds.

---

**The man of about forty-one came up the stairs at about ten past four with a folded piece of paper in his hand and asked whether the top landing could be let go.**

He lives in the block. He has asked me before, about a different thing, and he pays when he is told a price, and he was not raising his voice and he was not late, and he had come up the stairs at a walk.

The lift in that block has been out since Friday and has not been looked at since Friday and nobody has been told anything about the lift.

"**There's a woman on the top landing,**" he said. "**She's eighty-two and she's got an appointment at the clinic on the far side at half past four and she's not walking down four flights in front of me, not today.**"

The woman of about fifty came out of number nine with her coat already on and a set of keys in her hand that was not the key on the hook, and she listened to all of it and did not interrupt him.

Then she said, in about nine seconds: "**I can't put that out on my own name. It's on a log and the log gets countersigned at six by the man from the office, and if I let that landing go at ten past four and somebody goes off it in the dark, there's a piece of paper in this building with my name at the bottom of it and not the building's.**"

The man of about forty-one did not say anything for about four seconds.

"**I could ring him,**" he said.

"**You could,**" she said.

"**He's on a ward until seven.**"

"**Then he'd come at seven,**" she said, "**and you'd have the landing at ten past seven and not at half past three, and I'd have written it down wrong.**"

---

**And that was the whole of it. She did not say the word no. He did not ask her to say it. Neither of them said anything about the clinic, and about four people in that block were in their flats with their doors shut and none of them came out, and the man of about forty-one did not say the word please and she would not have needed him to.**

He asked her one more thing, and it was four seconds long.

"**And if I go up and get her now? Can she come down on her own with somebody? Does that let go of anything?**"

"**No,**" she said. "**That lets go of nothing.**"

He said right and went up, and Marek went with him because the closer was on that landing, and the door was open and the woman of about eighty-two was on the edge of a chair with her coat on and a carrier on the floor and she asked what the man was doing and he told her and she said right and put her hand on the carrier and waited.

**Marek fitted the closer and it took about eleven minutes and neither of the other two said one word while he did it.**

---

**The release was run at six, and it worked.**

That is the whole of what happened on that stair. At six the man from the office came, countersigned the log, and the woman of about fifty pulled the handle and the four doors let go and stayed let go until the case was reset, and the woman of about eighty-two went down at about ten past seven in front of the man of about forty-one, and got down four flights at a walk.

**The appointment was at half past four. It had been half past four for about three hours and the clinic had closed its afternoon list at four and told the woman at the desk and the woman at the desk told the man of about forty-one and the man of about forty-one told the woman of about eighty-two in a corridor at about ten past seven, and he said he could not get anybody to come and open it earlier and she said right.**

**Nobody in that block knows that. The man of about forty-one has not told anybody in that block and the woman of about eighty-two has not asked and did not ask on the way down and has not asked since, and the woman of about fifty has not been told either, and she was not in the corridor and would not have known what was said in it.**

---

**The rest of the Thursday ran at the counter.** Eleven names and eleven dates, the last at half past five, and a man of about thirty-eight came in with a clock that had been slow by a minute a day for about four months and wanted to know whether it could be repaired, and was told that it could be repaired and that it will go slow again, and he said that he knew, and paid, and it was not put on the book because it was a fault he could live with.

A woman of about twenty-two came in at about four for a length of cord and paid for it and went out again with the door held for her by a man of about fifty-eight who was going the other way, and neither of them said anything and the door closed.

---

**Between about half past six and ten the four rooms off that service road were in use and there was a fifth room behind the other three, and there was a fifth person in the fourth of those rooms for all of it.**

She is about thirty and the chair the door is behind is hers. The eighth of eight of her pages has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and twenty-eight days and it did not come out of that shelf on that Thursday. Nobody in that building has spoken to her about anything since the spring and nobody has said one word to her about the page and nobody has apologised to her in any form or ever will.

---

**Three things on that Thursday and none of them on a form.** A key on a hook at the height of a man's chest with a mark in the plaster above it where the case used to hang lower. **A piece of paper on a log in a flat with four doors' worth of cable coming out of the bottom of it and one signature on it that came at six.** And a closer on a second-floor landing that is on a bracket and closes and does what it was bought to do.

---

**He wanted the man of about forty-one to be angry at the woman of about fifty.**

**He did not want her to be in any trouble and he wanted him to say something at her, and he wanted it said where two other people could hear it, because a man saying a woman has made his afternoon impossible is a sentence that somebody has to write down. The man said right and went up the stairs and he was not angry, and the woman of about fifty went back into number nine and shut the door, and neither of them said one word about the appointment, and the appointment was the only thing on either of their minds for three hours and neither of them knew that about the other.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and the four pounds he took for a closer and a bracket did not cover the eleven minutes of his own bench that he had given up to go and come back off that stair, and there is a job on the book for the Friday that he has moved twice and has not rung anybody about.
