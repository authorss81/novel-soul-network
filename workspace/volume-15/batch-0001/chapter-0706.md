# Chapter 706 — A Thursday, A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A Garage On A Wide Road With An Empty Bus In It, A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Said The Word And Watched The Other Three Steps Happen Anyway, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Who Kept A Warehouse Who Was Told One True Sentence He Was Not Supposed To Be Told

**A Thursday was a counter until about six and four rooms from about six. This one had a practice run at a garage on a wide road with nobody on the bus, and a woman of about twenty-nine who said the word at the moment the arm went down, and about four people who all did the right thing afterwards, and a brass plate with a smooth corner in it.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Thursday and eleven people came over the counter and the last of them was let in at half past five. At ten the shutter came down and shook the cage for the tenth time in a fortnight and there was no wind that day at all, which nobody remarked on and which was the first time in about eleven years that the cage had been shaken on a Thursday for nothing.

**Those four rooms and one behind the three stand at one thousand three hundred and five days, a hundred and eighty-six weeks and three days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand three hundred and nine, a hundred and eighty-seven weeks to the day, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is one thousand and one days, a hundred and forty-three weeks to the day, and the hardboard's fifteenth is one thousand one hundred and twenty, a hundred and sixty weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and thirty-eight, a hundred and thirty-four weeks to the day.

Six hundred and eighty-five days is the separation, ninety-seven weeks and six days, and nothing has been put back into it and nothing ever will be.

---

**He was at that garage at about eleven because the arm was playing up again, and about four people were in the building, and the bus in bay four was empty and had been empty since about half past six because it was going out at about eleven and it had a new strip in its nearside door and somebody had decided to run it empty first.**

The four steps are the four steps and they are about nine seconds long and Marek has seen them four times in two weeks.

**Put the arm down. Hold it about four seconds. Let it go. Take the hands off.**

The release is a person standing in a recess by the front axle with a plate in front of them, and the plate is brass and about the size of a paperback and it is worn smooth in one corner, and the person is the release, and the release does not decide whether the doors open. The release does the four steps and the doors do what the four steps do.

The woman of about twenty-nine was the release.

**She said it at the moment the arm went down.**

**Not loudly. She said it the way you say a thing to a machine, in a flat voice, once, in the middle of a nine-second routine, into a bay in a garage on a wide road, with four people in the building and nobody on the bus.**

He was standing eleven feet away with his hand on the frame of bay two and he heard it and the man of about thirty-eight who owns bus four heard it and the two men from the depot heard it.

The arm went down and stayed down for about four seconds and came back up and the doors opened and closed and the bus was empty and the doors opened and closed again on a second test and were open.

**Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.**

The depot man said, "**Right. That's the run. That's the run done.**"

And the woman of about twenty-nine put her hands in the pockets of her coat.

**She did that because they had gone still, and Marek was the only person in that building who could see that they had gone still, and he has not said that out loud to anybody and did not say it then.**

---

**What happened in that garage over the following half hour is the part of this Thursday that nobody has ever been told about, including by the four people who were in it.**

The woman of about twenty-nine asked whether the run had counted.

The depot man said it had not counted and asked her why.

She said: "**Because I said something.**"

The depot man said: "**You said something to me.**"

**And then the man of about thirty-eight who owns bus four said, "**It counted. The plate doesn't care what you said, it counts the four steps.**"**

And the depot man agreed with him, and the two men agreed with him, and the woman of about twenty-nine said "**Right**" and took her hands out of her pockets and put them on the brass plate and looked at the smooth corner for about eleven seconds and said, "**Right**", and that was the end of it.

**Nobody asked her what she had said. Nobody asked her why. Nobody wrote it down, and the plate does not have a log, and the depot has a sheet for a run and the sheet for that run is filled in in the ordinary way with the ordinary four ticks and the ordinary time and the ordinary initials of a man of about thirty-eight, and the initials are of the man of about thirty-eight and not of her, because that is how the sheet has been filled in at that garage for about eleven years.**

Marek was there for the whole of it and said none of it.

**He was not brave and she was not brave and nobody in that building was anything. A bus ran a practice run with nobody on it on a Thursday morning and four people did their jobs and one of them said a word.**

---

**The counter ran from about one. Eleven people and eleven jobs and the last of them let in at half past five, and at five the man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse came in for a strap he had left behind in the middle of the winter and put it on the counter and paid for it and Marek wrapped it and gave it to him and neither of them had said anything for about nine seconds.**

Then Marek said the sentence.

**He said it to a man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse at five on a Thursday afternoon in a shop in Lattice Ward, in about eleven seconds, and it is the only time in this stretch of days that he says anything to anybody that is about himself, and he has never taken it back and does not want to and has not repeated it.**

"**There is nobody else in this city who can say no to a room,**" he said, "**and there is no piece of paper anywhere with any other name on it, and I have not told anybody that in about four years because as long as it is only me then nobody can point at me and say you built this, and I have been telling myself that is modesty.**"

The man of about thirty-eight said, "**Right.**"

And paid, and went, and has not been back, and has never mentioned it, and **did not know what he had been told and told nobody that he had been told anything at all**, and it is possible that he understood it and it is possible that he did not, and there is no arrangement in this city for finding out which.

---

**From half past six until ten there were three people in those four rooms off that service road and there was one other person in the fourth of them for the whole of it and she is about thirty and nobody put a word to her, and the eighth of eight pages has now been in a ring binder on that back shelf for a hundred and thirteen days, and there is nobody entitled to take it out of the binder in her presence or out of it.**

---

**Three things on that Thursday and not one of them was on a form.** A brass plate in a recess by a front axle, worn smooth in one corner, and the smooth corner is where a thumb goes and it has been going there for about eleven years. **A hand in a pocket and then out of it, twice, about twenty minutes apart.** And a sheet in a folder in a depot office with four ticks and a time and the initials of a man of about thirty-eight on it, and the woman who did the four steps is not on it, and has never been on one, and has asked about it once and was told that the release is not recorded because the release does not decide anything.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday and for a good part of the Friday, for the sequence to stop.**

**It did not stop. It ran four steps, twice, with nobody on the bus, and it will run again tomorrow with nine people on it, and he wanted it to have been his fault, and on that Thursday it was nobody's, and he has not found anybody in the last two days who will take it and he has not built anything to take it with.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten for no wind, and he went home at ten past ten and did not go up a stair in Saltmarket and has not gone up one since the Wednesday four weeks ago.
