# Chapter 707 — A Friday, A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A Two-Man Print Shop On A Road Off Saltmarket Where A Man Of About Fifty-Eight Set The Type Himself For The First Time In About Nineteen Years, A Sheet With The Same Four Steps And A Different Paragraph Under Them And A Second Box At The Bottom With A Line Already In It, And A Man Of About Thirty-One From A Firm Who Is Not From This City And Carries It And Is Not Named And Says Nothing

**A Friday was a counter until about six and four rooms from about six. This one went to a two-man shop on a road off Saltmarket at half past three, and found a man of about fifty-eight setting type with a sheet in his head, and the sheet had the same four steps on it and a different paragraph under them and a second box at the bottom with a line already in it.**

---

The Friday carried eleven jobs with a date against each and eleven callers, the last of them at half past five, and about two of the eleven were about the day and neither of them knew it. The shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the eleventh time in a fortnight.

**Those four rooms and one behind the three stand at one thousand three hundred and six days, a hundred and eighty-six weeks and four days, and the hardboard's seventeenth line is one thousand and seventy-eight, a hundred and fifty-four weeks to the day, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand three hundred and ten days, a hundred and eighty-seven weeks and one day, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is one thousand and two days, a hundred and forty-three weeks and one day, and the post at the corridor end is eight hundred and fifty-four, a hundred and twenty-two weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and thirty-nine, a hundred and thirty-four weeks and one day.

**Six hundred and eighty-six days is the separation, ninety-eight weeks to the day, and it went into a one-line box in a form about sixteen months ago and there is nothing in it and nothing goes into it and it is a number of days and not a length of anything.**

---

**The shop on that road off Saltmarket is two rooms and a counter and a glass door with the lettering gone off it, and it has been there about nineteen years, and the man who owns it is about fifty-eight and he was the man who printed the leaflets in the spring and he has not been thanked by anybody in this city for that and has not asked.**

He had about four days of a sheet in his head, which is a thing only about four people in this city can do, and Marek had seen him do it about nine times in about four months.

"**You set this one,**" Marek said.

"**I set this one,**" the man of about fifty-eight said.

"**You didn't set the last one.**"

"**I didn't set the last one,**" said the man of about fifty-eight. "**Somebody sent a plate. I never saw a plate come in and go out and I never asked and I want to be straight with you about that, because it's the last time it's going to be true.**"

**Nobody had said thank you to him for the leaflets. He was right about the paper and the ink and the count and the money and he had said in the spring that he was going to stop and he had not said anything about it since and he was not going to be asked about it by anybody, including Marek, who had wanted to ask him about it every week since about the middle of the spring.**

---

**The four steps were the same four steps. They are the same four steps because they are the four steps, and about nine hundred sheets went out of this shop in the spring with them on it and nobody has ever printed a different four.**

Under them, where the paragraph was on the last one, there was a paragraph.

**Where a civic node does not answer, the last approved civic sequence may be used as a provisional response, and a provisional response continues until it is superseded.**

That was the whole of the paragraph and it was eight words longer than the one it had replaced and it was not tidier.

**And at the bottom of the sheet, under the box with the mark in it, there was a second box, and the second box had a line of type already in it, set and not to be filled in, and the line said this:**

**This response has no local release.**

**And under that, in the same box and smaller, one more line, and the one line is the reason the sheet was worth a second printing.**

**Only the Crown Key will end this response.**

**That was all of it. There were no instructions and no address and no name and no way of reaching whoever wrote it, and there was no page two, and there was nothing at the top of the sheet at all, which was the thing about it that Marek noticed first and could not afterwards explain to anybody.**

"**Whose type is it.**"

"**Mine,**" said the man of about fifty-eight.

"**Where did it come from.**"

"**It came from a plate that came in the box on Monday,**" the man said, "**and it came in the box and not through the door, which means it came from a firm in a post box, and I have been printing for about nineteen years and I have had a plate come through a door about nine thousand times and through a box about twice.**"

"**Twice.**"

"**Once in the spring,**" he said, "**and once on Monday.**"

---

**Marek asked him for the count, because the count was the thing he had come for and because the count was the thing he had been unable to stop thinking about for four days.**

The man of about fifty-eight put both hands flat on the bench and looked at him for about four seconds.

"**No,**" he said.

"**It's a sheet. It's on about nine letter slots on this road alone and I've walked past about nine of them on the way here and there were sheets in four of them and not in five and I don't know which is which.**"

"**I know which is which,**" the man said, "**and I'm not going to tell you, and that's not because it's a secret, it's because a number one man keeps is a different object from a number people have taken, and the second one is a membership and the first one is a Tuesday, and I have printed about nine thousand things in this room and about nine hundred of them were for somebody who wanted to know how many doors they were at.**"

**He had not printed a single number on the sheet. There was no number anywhere on it and Marek had been looking for one since about half past three.**

"**Then how do I know it worked.**"

"**You don't,**" said the man of about fifty-eight, and picked up a bundle of about nine hundred sheets and squared it on the bench, and counted it, and counted it again, and put the second count face down.

---

**He charged the going rate and Marek paid it and the man of about fifty-eight took the money and put it in a tin under the counter, and it was not a different tin from the tin the leaflets money went in and it was the same tin and Marek knew it was the same tin and said nothing about it.**

"**You set the type,**" Marek said at the door.

"**I set the type,**" said the man of about fifty-eight.

"**Then somebody can say you wrote it.**"

"**Somebody can say I set it,**" said the man of about fifty-eight, "**and setting it is not writing it and I know the difference very well because I have been doing one and not the other for about nineteen years, and I would rather be a man who set it than a man who says he only ever printed what somebody else set, and I would rather be that than be right about it.**"

**And Marek wanted him to have refused, and he had wanted it for about four days, and the man of about fifty-eight did not refuse, and there was a bundle of about nine hundred sheets in a room off a road off Saltmarket that had been collected from a post box at a firm by a man of about thirty-one who is not from this city.**

The man of about thirty-one was in the shop. He had come in at half past three with the bundle and had put it down by the door and had not knocked and had not asked for anything and had not said his name. He was about thirty-one and he has been in this story for six volumes and nobody has said his name and nobody asked him anything and he said, before he left, that he was not going to be asked, and he was not.

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Friday, and nobody thanked the man of about fifty-eight on any day, and Marek did not thank him and has not thanked him and was not going to.**

---

**The rest of the Friday ran. The counter took eleven names and eleven dated jobs and the last at half past five, and the money that paid for about nine hundred sheets came out of the day's takings and the day was eleven pounds short and the shop was not.**

At about half past six there were four people in the four rooms off that service road and one behind the three and one of the four was cold that night, and the woman of about thirty was in the fourth of them from about half past six until ten and the eighth of eight pages was in a ring binder on a back shelf and has been for a hundred and fourteen days, and nobody has put a word to her or apologised to her in any form.

---

**Three things on that Friday, none of them on any form anybody in this city keeps.** A bundle counted twice on a bench and the second count put face down. **A type case with about nine empties in it where there had not been about nine empties in it in the spring, and a man who did not go back and fill them.** And a plate that came into a shop in a box instead of through a door, which had happened twice in nineteen years.

---

**He wanted, for about the whole of the walk back, for the man of about fifty-eight to have refused.**

**He did not refuse. He set the type and he charged the going rate and he took the money into the tin the leaflets money went in, and he said a true thing about the difference between setting a thing and writing it that Marek will be thinking about in about six weeks, and on that Friday the only person in this city who was angry about it was a man of twenty-two who was angry on behalf of a man of about fifty-eight and had no standing whatever to be.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten with a shop day's money in a tin that was eleven pounds short and about nine hundred sheets in a room he does not pay for.
