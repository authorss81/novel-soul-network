# Chapter 709 — A Friday, A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A Room Above A Shop In A First District Where A Man Of About Forty-Five Has Been Saying The Same Six Words For About Eleven Years And A Man Of Twenty-Two Heard Them And Did Not Say That He Had Heard Them Before, A Card On A Wall Turned To Face The Wall, And Nine Minutes Of Work That He Did Not Charge For And Was Not Thanked For

**A Friday was a counter until about six and four rooms from about six. This one went to a room above a shop in a first district at half past two, where a man of about forty-five said six words that a man of twenty-two had heard in the summer, and the man of twenty-two said nothing, wrote nothing down, did the work in nine minutes and did not charge for it.**

---

The Friday ran eleven jobs, each with a date against it, and eleven people came over the counter and were let in one at a time until half past five, and after that the door was shut and the bell was not answered. The shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the thirteenth time in a fortnight.

**Those four rooms and one behind the three stand at one thousand three hundred and thirteen days, a hundred and eighty-seven weeks and four days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand three hundred and seventeen, a hundred and eighty-eight weeks and one day, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is one thousand and nine days, a hundred and forty-four weeks and one day, and the hardboard's seventeenth is one thousand and eighty-five, a hundred and fifty-five weeks to the day, and the post at the corridor end is eight hundred and sixty-one, a hundred and twenty-three weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and forty-six, a hundred and thirty-five weeks and one day.

**Six hundred and ninety-three days is the separation, ninety-nine weeks to the day, and it is a count of days that does not describe anything and it went into a one-line box in a form about sixteen months ago and there is nothing in it and there is nothing going into it.**

---

**The woman of about thirty-six who has a room above a shop in a first district and who has been in it for about nine years asked him for a switch in the middle of the summer, and it had gone intermittent again, and on that Friday at half past two he was on a stool with his arm in a box about four feet up a wall.**

The room is one room with a table, four chairs, a board, a fanlight painted over, and a man of about forty-five at the table.

**He has been in that room about eleven years. He does the returns for nobody else. He has never said a name to Marek and Marek has never said one to him and the two of them have been in that room about nine times since the summer and it has always been about a switch, a lamp, a bell, a door that sticks, and a stair rail.**

They did not speak for about nine minutes. Marek was on the stool and the man of about forty-five was at the table and neither of them had anything to say to the other about anything.

**And then the man of about forty-five said the six words.**

He said them to the room. There was nobody else in the room. He said them the way a man says a thing he has said a great many times, without emphasis, at the end of a silence that had lasted about four seconds.

**And Marek's hands stopped.**

He was on a stool with his arm in a box four feet up a wall with a terminal in his fingers and a wire coming off it, and his hands stopped for about two seconds, and then he went on with the wire, and he finished the switch, and he came down off the stool, and he did not say a single word about it.

**He had last heard those six words in the middle of the summer, in a first district, in a room with four chairs in it, from that man, and there are seven of them in his head and he has never written any of them down and there is no list and no column and no disclosure anywhere in this city.**

Nobody in that room on that Friday knew that anything had happened.

**The man of about forty-five did not look up. He put a pen down, and said, "**That's right,**" about the switch, and Marek said that it was right, and the man of about forty-five said, "**It'll go again,**" and Marek said that it would, and the man of about forty-five said, "**It always goes again,**" and Marek said, "**It always goes again,**" and that was the whole of the conversation.**

---

**He did not ask the man of about forty-five where the six words came from.**

He wanted to. He has wanted to since about four o'clock that afternoon and he has wanted to on and off for about eleven weeks, and the wanting is not on a form and is not in a sentence he said, and it went on for the whole of the rest of that Friday and for about four days afterwards and he did not do anything about it.

**The wanting is on the page and the reason is on the page and they are about eleven words long between them: he wanted the man of about forty-five not to have made them up, because a man who has made six words up is a man who can be asked where he got them from, and a man who has been saying six words for eleven years has never been asked.**

**Nobody asked him. He did not ask. There is no arrangement in this city for asking a man of about forty-five in a first district where his six words came from, and there has not been one since about the spring, and nobody is going to put one in, and that is not a failure of the arrangement, because there is no arrangement.**

---

**He did the work in about nine minutes and did not charge for it, and said at the door, in four seconds, that there was nothing on it, and the man of about forty-five said, "**Right,**" and did not pay and did not offer and did not say thank you.**

**Nobody thanked anybody in that room on that Friday. Nobody in this city has thanked that man of about forty-five in about eleven years and Marek has been in that room about nine times and has never asked him a question and has never wondered out loud about the six words and has never mentioned them to anybody, and it is possible that the man of about forty-five has no idea that there is anything in that room worth mentioning, which is the likeliest thing and the one nobody will ever check.**

---

**The rest of the Friday ran. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five, and about two of the eleven were about a switch and neither of them was the switch.**

At about half past four a woman of about forty-five came in and said she had heard there was a book in this city that had been written in for about nineteen years, and Marek said he had not heard that, and she said she had heard it from a man on a bus, and he said that was probably a different book, and she said right and bought a switch and has not been back.

At about five a man of about thirty-eight came in and asked whether a thing that took nine seconds every Friday for eleven years could be somebody else's, and was told that it could, and that he was the only person who could tell, and that the man of about thirty-eight said, "**Right,**" and bought about nine feet of cord he did not need and has not been back.

**From half past six until ten there were four people in those four rooms off that service road and there was one other person in the fourth of them for the whole of it and she is about thirty and the chair the door is behind is hers, and the eighth of eight pages is in a ring binder on a back shelf and has been for a hundred and sixteen days, and nobody in this city has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will.**

---

**Three things on that Friday, and there is no form in this city that any of the three of them is on.** A card on a wall in a first district, about the size of a postcard, with a name and a number on it, turned to face the wall, and the tape at the top of it has gone yellow and has not been re-stuck, and a man of about forty-five has not turned it round and nobody has asked him why not. **A pen on a table put down flat and picked up up again about four seconds later.** And a stool four feet below a box with a terminal in it, where a man's hands stopped for about two seconds on a Friday afternoon in a room that nobody in this city has ever said was important.

---

**He wanted the man of about forty-five to have got the six words from somewhere else.**

He did not ask. He went back down the stairs, and he did not knock on the door of the room on the way out even though the door was open, and he walked to the bus stop, and he stood at the bus stop for about four minutes in the rain for a bus that came in about three minutes, and **he has not been in that room since that Friday, and it is the second room of nine in this city he has been in and got nothing off, and he is not going to tell anybody that either, and he has not told anybody, and there is no column in this city for a man of twenty-two who recognises a thing in a room and keeps it.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten and did not go up a stair in Saltmarket.
