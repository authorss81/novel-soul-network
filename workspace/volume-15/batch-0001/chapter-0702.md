# Chapter 702 — A Wednesday, A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A Basement In A Second District Where A Pump Would Not Balance, A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Has Written Four Numbers On The Back Of His Own Hand Every Morning For Nine Years And Who This Morning Had None, And A Four-Second Answer That Was Not A Refusal Of The Man And Cost The Man Two Hours Of Standing In A Cold Room

**A Wednesday was a counter until about six and four rooms from about six. This one put a man of about thirty-four on a stool in a basement in a second district for most of a day, put a man of twenty-two on his own bench in Lattice Ward giving that stool up, and balanced about two hours of a pump by hand at half past two in the afternoon.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday. Eleven people came over the counter and the last of them was let in at half past five. The shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the sixth time in a fortnight.

**Those four rooms and one behind the three stand at one thousand two hundred and ninety-seven days, a hundred and eighty-five weeks and two days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand three hundred and one days, a hundred and eighty-five weeks and six days, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on the board of two nails is nine hundred and ninety-three, a hundred and forty-one weeks and six days.
The hardboard's eighteenth is one thousand and fifteen, a hundred and forty-five weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is nine hundred and three, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks to the day.

**The ask is nine hundred and eighty-seven days, a hundred and forty-one weeks to the day, and the post at the corridor end is eight hundred and forty-five, a hundred and twenty weeks and five days.**

The separation is six hundred and seventy-seven days, ninety-six weeks and five days, and the nine hand copies of the front of a page are eight hundred and sixty-three, a hundred and twenty-three weeks and two days, and about eight of the nine are still unfinished and the first row on which anybody has ever disagreed has still not been found, and not one of the nine has been laid against another.

---

**A woman of about thirty-four who runs a counter in a first district came in on foot at about a quarter to ten and stood at the end of the counter until he had finished a job.**

"**Basement of a block in a second district,**" she said. "**Pump will not balance. It has been off since Friday. Thirty-one flats on that side and about four of them are on the second floor and about eleven are on the fourth.**"

"**What is it doing.**"

"**Nothing,**" she said. "**That is what it is doing. It is doing nothing and the pressure is going up.**"

"**Who signs for the release.**"

"**He does. He has signed for it since he was twenty-five and he is thirty-four and he is the only one who has ever signed for it.**"

"**Then I want him here,**" Marek said, "**not the pump.**"

She looked at him for a second.

"**That is not what I came for.**"

"**I know what you came for,**" he said, "**and I have not got it and I am not going to go and look at the pump without the man who signs for it, because if I go without him then whatever I do to it he signs for, and he will not have been here.**"

---

**The man of about thirty-four came at about eleven and he came on foot and he was wet to about the knee, and the first thing Marek noticed was that the back of his right hand was clean.**

He had a plaster on the inside of his left wrist and it was not fresh.

They went down at about half past eleven. It is nine steps and a landing and it is colder than the shop by about as much as a street is, and there is one lamp on the second landing that has been the same bulb since before the spring.

The pump is an old one and it is on a concrete plinth and it has a lever and a gauge and a bypass the size of a forearm, and Marek put his hand on the pipe and felt it and it was doing what the woman in the first district had said, which is nothing.

"**How long have you signed for this one,**" he said.

"**Nine years,**" said the man of about thirty-four.

"**And it balances,**" he said.

"**It balances,**" said the man of about thirty-four.

"**Then something has changed on it and it isn't the pump.**"

"**It is the pump,**" said the man of about thirty-four. "**I have been on it nine years and it balances.**"

Marek got the gauge and a pencil and put a mark on the glass where the needle was and stood there for about nine seconds, and the needle went to the mark and stayed there, and the pump did nothing about it.

"**It's balanced,**" he said.

"**It's balanced,**" said the man of about thirty-four.

"**Then who's wrong.**"

And the man of about thirty-four said, in about four seconds, and Marek had the answer before the end of it:

"**I am. I'm wrong. I've been wrong for about four days and I've signed for it every morning and I've written the four numbers on my hand every morning for nine years and this morning there was nothing on my hand and I did it anyway.**"

---

**And then he asked the thing, and it was the thing Marek had come back to the shop on a Monday not expecting for another four days.**

"**You can tell me,**" the man of about thirty-four said. "**Can't you. There's a number somewhere and a room with a list in it and if I ask the right room then they'll tell me whether I'm allowed to switch it back.**"

Marek said no in four seconds.

"**Right,**" said the man of about thirty-four.

"**Not because you're asking me to do something I don't want to do,**" Marek said. "**Because there is no such room, and there is no such list, and if I stood in that basement and said there was, then in about eleven days there would be one, and it would have my name on the top of it, and every pump in this city that went wrong would be carried to it by a man who had been told to ask.**"

"**And you don't want that.**"

"**I want that not to happen,**" Marek said, "**and I am not going to be the reason it does, and I'm not going to be the person you asked and got a no from either, so here's the other thing: get out of the way and I'll do it.**"

The man of about thirty-four got out of the way and did not go up the nine steps. He stood on the landing for two hours.

**What was wrong with it was a union that had been replaced in the spring by about nine inches of something that looked right and was not, and the pump had been balancing against the wrong pipe for about four days and the gauge said balanced and the pressure on the fourth floor said otherwise, and it took about forty minutes and a spanner and one length of copper and about two hours of a man standing on a landing being told nothing.**

The union came off in one piece and was the wrong size by about a sixteenth and had been fitted by somebody who had been told it was the right one.

The man of about thirty-four looked at it for a while.

"**That's ours,**" he said. "**That's a Monday job. Somebody's fitted that on a Monday.**"

"**Don't go looking,**" Marek said.

"**I wasn't going to go looking.**"

"**You were,**" Marek said, "**and you were going to do it on your Friday and I am telling you now that if you do it on your Friday then you will be a man who went looking, and I would rather you were a man who wrote four numbers on his hand.**"

**Nobody thanked him. The man of about thirty-four said right and went up the nine steps and did not look back, and he has not been to the shop, and he is still doing the job, and Marek does not know whether he writes the four numbers on his hand and it is not on anybody's form.**

---

**He gave the man of about thirty-four his own bench in Lattice Ward from about one until about four and then lost the afternoon, and at half past four the woman of about thirty-four came to the shop and paid for the copper and for an hour and did not ask whether the pump was all right because she had been in the basement and had heard it.**

The rest of the day ran. A woman of about thirty-one brought in a chair frame with a leg off it and asked whether it could be made to stand up and was told yes in four seconds and did not charge her and she has not been back. A man of about thirty-eight bought about nine feet of conduit and said he had meant to come back for it and was told he did not have to and said he knew he did not have to, and neither of them said what it was for.

At about quarter to six a woman of about thirty-six asked whether a person could be told to do a thing and then find out afterwards that nobody had asked them, and Marek said yes, and that this happened about four times a week in this city and mostly to people who could not spell, and that the people it happened to most were the ones who were careful.

**From half past six there were four of them in the four rooms off that service road, and the fourth of those rooms was warm from about seven, and there was one other person in it from half past six until ten and she is about thirty and the chair the door is behind is hers. Nothing was put to her for three and a half hours on that Wednesday. The eighth of eight pages, in a ring binder, on the back shelf, did not come out, and there is still nobody in this city entitled to take it out of the binder in her presence or out of it, and nobody has apologised to her in any form.**

---

**Three things on that Wednesday, none of them on a form.** The back of a man's right hand, clean, on a morning when it had four numbers on it every morning for nine years. **A copper union on a landing in a basement in a second district, fitted on a Monday by somebody who was told it was the right one, and the man who found it said *that's ours* and then did not go looking, and Marek does not know whether he went looking.** And a lamp on a second landing that has had the same bulb in it since before the spring and is the only lamp in that building and has never once been reported.

---

**He wanted the man of about thirty-four to put his hand flat on the bench and shout.**

He did not. He stood on a landing for two hours and then he said *right* and went up nine steps, and the not-shouting is the reason Marek cannot work for the rest of the week, and the reason is on the page: a man who shouts has been let off something, and a man who says *right* has not, and this one said *right* with about thirty-one flats behind him and four on the second floor.

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten with the copper receipt in his pocket and the receipt for the bench in the other one.
