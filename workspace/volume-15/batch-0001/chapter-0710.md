# Chapter 710 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Room At The Top Of A Stair In Saltmarket With About Eleven People In It, Fifty-Eight Of Which Fifty-Three Given First And Once By A Woman Of About Sixty, A Book That Was On Sixty-Four Lines And Was On Sixty-Four Lines, A Man Of About Fifty-Eight Who Was Let In And Had Not Been Before And Was Not Asked Anything, And A Place Behind A Chair That Has Been Empty Twenty-Eight Weeks To The Day

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a room at the top of a stair in Saltmarket from about half past four. This one was the fifty-third sitting in that room, and a number was given first and once, and a book did not change, and a man of about fifty-eight was let in who had not been there before and was not asked a question by anybody.**

---

A Wednesday was carried by eleven dated jobs and eleven people at the counter, the last of them coming in at half past five and being let in. The shutter came down at ten, and the cage shook, and that had happened fourteen times in a fortnight.

**One thousand three hundred and eighteen days is where those four rooms off that service road have got to, a hundred and eighty-eight weeks and two days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand three hundred and twenty-two days, a hundred and eighty-eight weeks and six days, and the rail takes two.**

**Nine hundred and twenty-four days is the man of about fifty-one against that north wall, a hundred and thirty-two weeks to the day, and the ask is one thousand and eight, a hundred and forty-four weeks to the day, and two figures that are each an exact number of weeks are on the page of a Wednesday on which the only number anybody said out loud went up by one, and neither of those two figures is that number.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is one thousand and fourteen days, a hundred and forty-four weeks and six days, and the hardboard's eighteenth is one thousand and thirty-six, a hundred and forty-eight weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and fifty-one, a hundred and thirty-five weeks and six days.

Six hundred and ninety-eight days is the separation, ninety-nine weeks and five days, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**He had said he would not go up that stair again.**

He said it on a Wednesday four weeks ago on the bottom step, to himself, for about four minutes, having come down it. He has not been up it since. He went up it on this Wednesday at half past three and there was no reason on earth for him to be one of about eleven people in that room.

**Nobody had told anybody he was coming. About four of the people in that room did not know he was in the building until about twenty past four, and one of those four was a man of about fifty-eight who was let in at half past three by a woman of about twenty-nine who works somewhere in a second district and who was on her way to a shift and came in out of the rain, and he had not been in that room before.**

He was not asked anything. He stood at the back for about eleven minutes and then sat down, and nobody said a word to him, and at about a quarter past four a woman of about sixty gave a number and the man of about fifty-eight was in the room while she gave it, and he was not asked what he made of it, and nobody has asked him since, and he has not come back.

---

**At about a quarter past four she gave it, and she gave it first, which is what she does.**

"**Fifty-eight.**"

"**Of which,**" said the man of about sixty-one with the clipboard.

"**Fifty-three correspond.**" **She said it the way a person reads a temperature, and did not look up, and went on: "**It is up by one. It is always up by one. The five that do not correspond are the same five and I have never shown them to anybody and I am not going to start this afternoon.**"**

Nobody asked her about the five.
Nobody asked the man of about fifty-eight anything.
Nobody in that room said a word about the nine minutes, and about nine people in that city knew the nine minutes, and about four of them had been in that room since about half past four and none of the four said anything, and the reason there is no word about it in this file is that there was no word about it in that room.

---

**The book was on sixty-four lines when she opened it and it was on sixty-four lines when she shut it, about an hour later, and nobody in that room asked her to open it and nobody in that room suggested it.**

There is no rule anywhere in this city that says which sittings it opens at. **The last time it opened, a caller said a thing to her face, and this time nobody said anything to her face at all, and the book was not opened, and there is nobody in that room who has ever been told which of the two is the reason and the woman of about sixty is not going to say and the man of twenty-two was in the room and did not ask her.**

**The tin on the same table is on seventy-three with its lid on, and there is a receipt from a shop in it as a page marker, and the receipt is not hers, and the difference between the two of those figures has never once been printed as a number by anybody in this city and is not printed on this page.**

---

**Behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place, and it had been empty one hundred and ninety-six days, which is twenty-eight weeks to the day.**

**About four people in that room had known that figure before they came up the stair. About two of them knew it to the day. Nobody said anything to anybody about it, nobody asked her about it, and nobody has asked her about it in twenty-eight weeks, and the figure is not on a form, and it is not on the book, and it is not on the clipboard, and the only person in the world who writes day counts down as a job has it in his head and has printed it in his head and has not printed it on a load book.**

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room. It did not move on that Wednesday. It has not moved since the spring. Nobody in that room moved it, nobody in this city has ever said who put it there, and nobody has ever asked.**

---

**At about six o'clock the man of about fifty-eight who had been let in out of the rain stood up and left, and about four people looked at him and about seven did not, and he was out on the pavement in about nine seconds and he did not look up at the window.**

Nobody said anything to him. Nobody has ever said anything to anybody about that visit, and it is not on a form, and the man of about fifty-eight has not been asked and does not know that it was noticed.

---

**The rest of that Wednesday is not on this page because nothing else happened in it.**

The counter ran until about six. A woman of about forty-five heard about a book in this city written in for about nineteen years from a man on a bus, and Marek said he had not heard that, and she said that was probably a different book, and bought a switch and has not been back. A man of about thirty-eight bought about nine feet of cord he did not need. Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in.

**From half past six until ten there were four people in those four rooms off that service road and there was one other person in the fourth of them for the whole of it, and she is about thirty, and the chair the door is behind is hers, and for three and a half hours not one person in that building put a word to her, and the eighth of eight pages was in a ring binder on a back shelf and has been for a hundred and seventeen days, and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will, and there is nobody in this city entitled to take it out of the binder in her presence or out of it.**

---

**Three things on that Wednesday and not one of them was on a form.** The ninth chair, with its back to a room, and the fact that it did not move. A place behind the woman's chair with no coat on it and no bag under it and nothing at all in it, and about four people in that room who had a figure for it and did not use it. And a woman of about twenty-nine letting a stranger in out of the rain at half past three and then going to a shift and not saying a word about it and not telling anybody afterwards, and about four people noticing and none of them mentioning it to anybody including themselves.

---

**He wanted a coat on the back of the chair behind the woman of about sixty.**

**He has wanted that since the first time he went up that stair, which was in the spring, and he has not said so to anybody, and he has not said so to himself in writing, and there is nowhere in this city for a man of twenty-two to put a thing like that, and on that Wednesday at about half past five, standing on the bottom step with his hand on the rail, the wanting was the only thing he did that cost him anything at all, and he went out through the door and nobody came down after him and nobody went up to find him.**

He said none of it.
The shutter came down at ten and he went home at ten past ten and he was twenty-two years old and he had been on the bottom step of that stair for about four minutes and he had gone up it anyway.
