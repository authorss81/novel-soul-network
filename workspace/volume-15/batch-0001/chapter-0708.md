# Chapter 708 — A Monday, A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A Room Above A Hall In A Second District With About Nine Of Them In It And A Woman Of About Nineteen Who Asked Him To Say Something And Was Refused In Four Seconds, A Lamp And A Bench, A Page In A Book That Is Not The Exercise Book With One Word On It And Nothing Else, And Twenty Minutes Which Is The First Time In Eleven Years

**A Monday was a counter until about six and four rooms from about six. This one went to a room above a hall in a second district at half past three, was asked to say something to about nine people, said no in four seconds, fixed a lamp and a bench, came back twenty minutes late for the first time in eleven years, and saw one page on the way out.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Monday and eleven names went on the book, and the eleventh was taken at about ten past six instead of half past five and the reason is on the page. The shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the twelfth time in a fortnight.

**Four of those rooms and one behind the three stand at one thousand three hundred and nine days, a hundred and eighty-seven weeks to the day, and the hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and ninety-nine, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks to the day, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand three hundred and thirteen, a hundred and eighty-seven weeks and four days, and the rail takes two.**

**The nine hand copies of the front of a page are eight hundred and seventy-five days old, a hundred and twenty-five weeks to the day, and eight of the nine are unfinished and the first row on which anybody has ever disagreed has not been found, and not one of the nine has been laid against another, and the ninth of them has a corner torn on it and has had since before the spring.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is one thousand and five days, a hundred and forty-three weeks and four days, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is nine hundred and fifteen, a hundred and thirty weeks and five days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and forty-two, a hundred and thirty-four weeks and four days.

Six hundred and eighty-nine days is the separation, ninety-eight weeks and three days, and it is a count of days that describes nothing and it is not shorter.

**The woman of about nineteen came into the shop on the Friday before with a list of four things, which is a thing about her, and the list was a lamp, a bench, a switch that had gone intermittent, and a fourth thing she had written and then crossed out.**

"**Can you come and say something to them,**" she said. "**Not a lesson. Just — they're about nine of them and about half of them have never been asked anything by an adult who wasn't paid to ask them and you could just stand at the front.**"

"**No,**" Marek said.

"**Right.**"

"**I want to be exact about why,**" he said, "**because I have said no to about nine people in about four years and I have never once said why, and if I don't say why to you then the no is a habit and habits are what got nine of them into the situation they are in.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I would be the man in the room,**" he said. "**You have got nine of them in a room and a list of what may be shared and what may be kept, and the second list is the whole thing, and if I am at the front of that room then in about three years the second list is the list I wrote in my head at the front of the room, and nobody will be able to tell the difference between it and the one you wrote.**"

The woman of about nineteen looked at him for about four seconds and then said, "**That's the best reason anybody's given me for anything in about two years,**" and then said, "**It's also a load of it,**" and then wrote **switch** on the list and put the list in her pocket.

---

**The room above a hall in a second district has a corridor, two doors, about nine chairs that are not chairs, a bench that has been in it since the hall was a something else, and a lamp on a bracket that has a switch on it that goes on and off about four times in nine minutes.**

He did the lamp in about nine minutes and the switch in about eleven and the bench in about nine, and about nine people came in at about half past four and sat down and did not speak to him and did not ask him anything, and one of them is about nine years old and did not look at him once.

The woman of about nineteen did not speak to him again for the whole of the forty minutes. She read. Two of the nine talked about something else. Nobody asked him a question and he answered none and he taught nobody anything and demonstrated nothing and assessed nobody and helped nobody and the forty minutes were a lamp, a switch, and a bench.

**And on the way out, on a desk by the door, on top of a closed exercise book and not inside it, there was a page with one word on it in capitals and nothing else on the page, and the word was NO, and it was in a child's hand and it had been pressed over about four times with a biro, so that the paper had gone furry on the front of the N.**

Marek looked at it for about four seconds and went down the stairs.

**He did not pick it up. He did not ask the woman of about nineteen about it. He has not mentioned it to anybody, and it is still on that desk, and the girl of about nine who wrote it is not named and was not asked a question and has not been asked a question by anybody.**

---

**He came back at about ten past six and the eleventh name went on the book at about ten past six and the eleventh person was a man of about forty-one who had a lamp in a bag and who was not told anything about twenty minutes and was not charged for them and said "**No, that's all right**" when Marek said it was nothing, and meant it, and paid four pounds and went.**

**The shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the twelfth time in a fortnight and he went home at ten past ten and it is the first time in about eleven years that he has come back into that shop later than half past four.**

---

**From about half past six until about nine there were three people in those four rooms off that service road and then a fourth came in at about nine and stayed until about ten, and the woman of about thirty was in the room at the back from about half past six until about nine and nobody put a word to her for two and a half hours, and the eighth of eight pages was in a ring binder on a back shelf and has been for a hundred and fifteen days.**

---

**Three things on that Monday and not one of them was on a form.** A page on a desk with one word on it in capitals and nothing else on it, and the paper furry on the front of the letter because it had been pressed over about four times. **A chair at the back of a room above a hall in a second district with a piece of card taped to the leg of it about four inches by two and a name on the card and a date, which is a thing about somebody's grandad and not about anybody in the room.** And a bench that has been in the same room since the hall was something else and which nobody had ever sat on and which four of the nine sat on.

---

**He wanted, for about four seconds on a stair in a second district, for the child to have spelled it wrong.**

**It was spelt right. It was pressed over about four times and it was spelt right and it was on top of a closed book and not inside it, and the whole of the wanting was that a child of about nine who writes a word like that is nearly always copying something, and if she was copying then the page was a copy and a copy has an original and the original has a printer and Marek had been in a two-man shop on a road off Saltmarket about three days before and had watched a man of about fifty-eight set about nine words of type and had wanted him to refuse.**

She was not copying anything. He does not know that. He wanted her to be, and she was not, and he has not gone back.

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he walked home because the bus had gone, and it is about forty minutes from that shop and he has walked it about four thousand times and it is the only forty minutes of the day that belongs to nobody.
