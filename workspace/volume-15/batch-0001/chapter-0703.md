# Chapter 703 — A Thursday, A Counter Opened Late Because A Man Of Twenty-Two Was On A Roof Until About Ten, A Cistern In A First District That Fills From The Mains And Has To When The Mains Is Off, A Technician Of About Twenty-Two With A Length Of Wire And A Spanner, And Nobody In This City Who Says That Is The Answer

**A Thursday was a counter until about six and four rooms from about six. This one did not open until about ten because a man of twenty-two was on a roof until about ten, and a man of about twenty-two was on that roof with a length of wire and a spanner and forty minutes of a thing nobody has since described as anything.**

---

**The shop did not open until about ten on that Thursday, which it had not done in eleven years, and there were nine people outside it when it did.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and ninety-four days, a hundred and forty-two weeks to the day, and the hardboard's fifteenth is one thousand one hundred and thirteen, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks to the day.

**Four of those rooms and one behind the three stand at one thousand two hundred and ninety-eight days, a hundred and eighty-five weeks and three days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand three hundred and two days, a hundred and eighty-six weeks to the day, and the rail takes two.**

The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and thirty-one, a hundred and thirty-three weeks to the day, and the post at the corridor end is eight hundred and forty-six, a hundred and twenty weeks and six days.

Six hundred and seventy-eight days is the separation, ninety-six weeks and six days, and it is a count of days that does not describe anything and it was six hundred and seventy-eight this morning and it will be six hundred and seventy-nine tomorrow and nothing will be put back into it.

---

**The woman of about thirty-six had come in on the Tuesday and had said that the water in a building in a first district comes off a roof when the mains is off, and that about nine flats get it, and that the tank was empty on the Sunday and had been filling at about a quarter of what it should since the Monday.**

She had not asked him to come. He had asked her which firm fitted the cistern and she had said the firm that fitted it in the spring, and he had written it down, and he had not rung them because there is no such thing here.

He had found the technician outside a depot at half past eight by walking there, and the technician was about twenty-two and had a van and had a length of wire in his pocket and had not been told anybody was coming.

"**Right,**" the technician said, when he had heard the whole of it, which took about nine seconds.

"**You don't have to do it today.**"

"**I know I don't,**" said the technician. "**I've got a Thursday.**"

---

**The ladder went up the side in an alley about two feet wide and there are about nine flats in that building and four of them are on the second floor and about eleven on the fourth, and the tank is a fibre one about the size of a bath on its side and it sits on a stand of painted steel with a ball valve on a float arm the length of a cigarette and a stopcock underneath it.**

The float arm had been replaced in the spring. It was the right length and it was on the wrong axis and the ball was a slightly different weight, and the prepared thing that the building's release would normally do is put the ball down and take the arm up and hold it there for about four seconds, and the arm could not do that and would not do it and the tank sat at about a quarter.

**Marek went up the ladder at ten past nine and the technician went up before him and stood on the stand and looked at the valve for about nine seconds and then said, "**Right,**" again, and got on with it.**

It took about forty minutes. The technician took the arm off with a spanner and put a length of wire through the ball and bent it to a shape that was not the shape it came in and not the shape it should be, and put it back, and then stood there while the tank filled and watched it come up to the top and stay there for about eleven minutes with the mains off, which cannot happen.

**Neither of them said that this was the right thing to do. Neither of them said it was clever. The technician said, at half past ten, "**That'll go back,**" meaning the arm, meaning the bend, meaning in a year somebody would have to know he had done it, and Marek said, "**Leave it a bit proud,**" and the technician left it about a quarter of an inch proud, and that is the whole of the technical record of that Thursday and it is on nobody's form.**

They came down at about eleven.

---

**The nine people outside the shop were about nine people who had been outside a shop that does not open, and one of them was a woman of about thirty-six who had been the one who told him, and she said, "**I didn't know you were going up,**" and he said, "**I didn't either, an hour ago,**" and she said, "**Right,**" and got on with her day and has been back once since for a kettle element and paid and went.**

**At about eleven he did the thing he had come into the shop to do at nine, which was to open it, and the counter ran from about eleven to about six and eleven people came over it and the last of them was let in at half past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the seventh time in a fortnight.**

The rest of the Thursday was ordinary and is not written about here. A man of about fifty-eight asked whether a lamp that had been rewired by somebody else was safe and was told in four seconds that the man who did it was in this city and she could find him, and she said she did not want to find him and would he mind just saying it, and he said no, and she paid four pounds and took the lamp and has not been back.

At about half past two a man of about thirty-eight came in and asked whether a thing that had worked perfectly for nine years could stop working, and Marek said that it could, and that when it did it was almost always because something on the far end of it had been changed by somebody who was not the person who owned it, and the man of about thirty-eight said right and bought a switch and has not been back and did not say what he had been asking about.

**From about half past six until about ten there were three people in the four rooms off that service road and there was one other person in the fourth of them and she is about thirty, and nobody said anything to her, and the page of hers that is the eighth of eight stayed in a ring binder on a back shelf where it has stayed for about a hundred and nine days and where there is nobody in this city entitled to take it out.**

---

**Three things on that Thursday, and none of them was on a form.** A technician of about twenty-two who ties his left boot and leaves his right one alone, every morning, for as long as anybody in that alley has been watching, and who has never once been asked about it and who was asked about it at about eleven by a man standing at the foot of a ladder. **A stopcock under a fibre tank that has a quarter of an inch of paint on the handle in a different colour from the rest of the handle, and the paint is about four years old and is the only part of that building anybody has ever looked at twice.** And a length of wire in a technician's pocket that had a kink in it about nine inches from one end, from something he had done on a different job, and which he used on a Thursday because it was the wire he had.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday evening, for the technician to come back and ask him for a thing he is bad at.**

The technician did not come back. He asked Marek for a thing he was good at, at about eleven, by the foot of the ladder, and Marek did it in nine seconds, and the technician said right and went and has not said thank you and does not know his surname and did not ask for it, and the whole of what Marek got out of that Thursday was a man on a stand doing a thing that would have taken nine seconds in a room with about four people in it.

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he got home at about eleven and stood in the cold for about four minutes before he went in, because his hands were stiff and he wanted them to be stiff a bit longer.
