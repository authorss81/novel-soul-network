# Chapter 704 — A Friday, A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A Room Over A Shop In A Second District With About Four People In It And None Of Them Writing Anything Down, A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Can Read A Gauge And Does Not Know What Forty-One Means, And Eleven Minutes Each Way Twice

**A Friday was a counter until about six and four rooms from about six. This one had about four people in a room over a shop in a second district all afternoon trying, which is the kindest thing anybody in this city knows how to do. It did not work. Nobody wrote it down. At five the man they were trying for went back to work at something else and was slower at it.**

---

The Friday had eleven jobs dated against it and eleven callers, and the last of the eleven was let in at half past five, and the shutter came down at ten, hard, with the wind that had been up since about two, and the cage shook for the eighth time in a fortnight.

**Those four rooms and one behind the three stand at one thousand two hundred and ninety-nine days, a hundred and eighty-five weeks and four days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand three hundred and three, a hundred and eighty-six weeks and one day, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and ninety-five days, a hundred and forty-two weeks and one day, and the hardboard's seventeenth is one thousand and seventy-one, a hundred and fifty-three weeks to the day.

**Six hundred and seventy-nine days is the separation, ninety-seven weeks to the day, and it went into a one-line box in a form about sixteen months ago and it has been counted every day since and it has not got any shorter and nothing has gone back into it. The post at the corridor end is eight hundred and forty-seven days, a hundred and twenty-one weeks to the day, which is the length of a corridor from a door at one end to a door at the other.**

---

**The woman of about thirty-four came in at about eleven and said one thing at the counter and did not say it twice and did not ask him to do anything about it.**

"**He can read it,**" she said. "**He reads it fine. It's the number and he knows it's forty-one and he doesn't know what forty-one is.**"

"**Say that again slower.**"

"**I can't,**" she said, "**because it doesn't make any sense and I've said it four times this morning and three of those were to him.**"

---

**The room over a shop in a second district is one room with a table, four chairs, a board, and a window that does not open, and it is the room the water office has used for about nine years for the things it does not want to do in front of the street.**

There were about four people in it at about half past twelve.

The man of about thirty-four was at the table with a gauge in front of him and a piece of paper with three columns of numbers on it. The woman of about thirty-four was standing. A man of about sixty-one who has fitted things for about forty years was sitting with his hands on the table and had not touched anything. And Marek, who had walked eleven minutes to get there and had been let in without being asked a question, which he noticed and did not think about until the following week.

**What they did, over about four hours, was try, and trying is a thing four people in a room are extremely good at.**

The man of about sixty-one said the dial might be wrong, and got up and turned the dial with a spanner, and the number did not change, and said so.

The woman of about thirty-four said it might be the paper, and put her hand flat on the page with the three columns on it and said the paper had been the paper for nine years.

The man of about thirty-four said, patiently, four times over four hours, that he knew the dial and he knew the paper.

Somebody said have you eaten.

Somebody else said it's the cold, in that building, in that room, in that month, everybody in that building is slower in that room, and the man of about thirty-four agreed with that and then said, "**But I know what thirty-eight is,**" and nobody in the room had a way of answering that and about four of them tried and got it wrong.

**Nobody wrote anything down. There was a board on that wall with a month on it and about nine of the boxes on it had been filled in and the man of about thirty-four's was not one of them, and the box he had not filled in was filled in on the Monday after by a different man with a different hand and nothing in it, and nobody has ever compared the two hands.**

At about four o'clock the man of about sixty-one said, in the voice of a person putting down a spanner, "**Then do the other thing.**"

"**Which other thing.**"

"**The rounds. You can do the rounds. They're slower but you'll do them and I'll go with you the first one and then I'll go with you the first one a month.**"

And the man of about thirty-four said right, and stood up, and took his coat off the back of a chair, and Marek noticed the coat and the chair and the corridor and did not know that he was noticing them.

---

**At half past four Marek walked eleven minutes to the water office to ask for a thing about the man of about thirty-four and was not let in, because the woman of about thirty-four had gone home at about four and the man on the door was not entitled to let him in and there is no arrangement in this city for being let in by somebody who is not entitled to.**

He stood on the step for about four minutes and then walked eleven minutes back and did not knock at five, and there was a light on in the room over the shop and nobody came to the door, and he went and opened his own shop instead and it was ten minutes to six.

**The rest of the Friday ran and none of it was about the man of about thirty-four.** A woman of about forty-one came in and asked whether a thing that had been fine for a long time could go off, and was told that it could, and bought about nine feet of conduit and paid and has not been back. A man of about thirty-three waited from ten until about twenty past ten in the morning, which Marek did not know about until the Monday, and said nothing about it then or afterwards.

**From half past six until ten there were four people in the four rooms off that service road, and the fourth room was warm, and there was one other person in it the whole three and a half hours and she is about thirty and nobody said a word to her and the eighth of eight pages stayed in a ring binder on a back shelf.**

---

**Three things on that Friday, and not one of them was on a form.** A chair in a corridor in a second district with a coat over the back of it that is not on anybody's hook and has not been moved since before the spring and that a woman of about thirty-four has walked past about nine times a week for nine years. **A box on a board over a shop that a different hand filled in on the Monday after with nothing in it.** And a gauge that reads what it has always read and that a man of about thirty-four can read and cannot use.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Friday and for most of the weekend after it, for somebody else to be the person who told that man what had happened to his hands.**

He was the only person in that room who could have said it, and he did not say it, and he has not said it since, and nobody has asked him, and the reason it was him and not the man of about sixty-one is on the page and is about four words long: **he would have been the only one who knew that it was connected, and the only one who knew it was connected was him, and a man who says a thing like that out loud in a room of four people in a second district at half past two on a Friday is a man who has made it a fact, and it is not yet a fact, and four people have been trying to help a man all afternoon without any of it being that.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out with eleven names against them, and eleven people came over the counter, and the last of them was let in at half past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage hard because of the wind, and he went home at ten past ten and did not eat until about half past eleven.

*Four people trying, and a box filled in by a different hand.* **About four people in a room over a shop in a second district spent about four hours on a Friday trying to help a man who could read a gauge and did not know what forty-one was, and none of them wrote it down, and the box on the wall that was his was filled in on the Monday after by a different man with a different hand and nothing in it. The register of correct acts with no consequence stands at two and did not move on this day, and this is the day in the movement where the honest answer is closest to the counter: four people helping is not a correct act, it is four people who did not want to go home, and the man they helped is doing somebody else's job at a slower speed and the woman who sent him there knows it and has not written it down. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped in any sense the word is used in anywhere else in this city. Seven objects are named on this page and this page does not bring two of them together: a gauge with a dial that was turned with a spanner and did not move, a coat over the back of a chair in a corridor, a card standing on end in a rail of two, a board on two nails, a hand copy of the front of a page, a month on a wall with nine boxes on it, and a drawer under a bench with four sheets in it that was not opened on that Friday or on any day before it.**
