# Chapter 705 — A Monday, A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, A Bus On A Wide Road At A Stop With Its Doors Shut And About Nine People On It And About Four People On The Pavement Who Did Not Have Anywhere In Particular To Be, A Door Strip He Had Been Keeping For Four Months, And Eleven Minutes Which Is Two Longer Than The Nine And Which Nobody Noticed Was Longer

**A Monday was a counter until about six and four rooms from about six. This one had a bus standing at a stop on a wide road with its doors shut and about nine people on it, and about four people on the pavement who were not going to be told anything, and a man of twenty-two who gave away a part of a door mechanism he had been keeping for four months.**

---

On the Monday eleven jobs were dated and eleven people came to the counter, and the door was shut on the last of them at half past five. At ten the shutter came down, and it was the ninth time in a fortnight that the cage had shaken, and nobody in that street counted them but Marek did.

**One thousand three hundred and two days is where those four rooms off that service road have got to, a hundred and eighty-six weeks to the day, and the hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and ninety-two, a hundred and fifty-six weeks to the day, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand three hundred and six days, a hundred and eighty-six weeks and four days, and the rail takes two.**

**The nine hand copies of the front of a page are eight hundred and sixty-eight days old, a hundred and twenty-four weeks to the day, and about eight of the nine are still unfinished and the first row on which anybody has ever disagreed has still not been found, and not one of the nine has been laid against another.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and ninety-eight days, a hundred and forty-two weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and thirty-five, a hundred and thirty-three weeks and four days.

Six hundred and eighty-two days is the separation, ninety-seven weeks and three days, and it is a count of days that does not describe anything and there is nothing in it and nothing goes into it.

---

**He was on the bus at about a quarter to nine in the morning because it was the only one that went past the shop and he had a bag of conduit under his arm and about half an hour to be somewhere.**

The doors shut at the stop before the one where he wanted to get off.

**Not a fault. Nothing broke. The doors were shut and the bus was not moving and the driver got out and opened the cubby and did what drivers do, and after about a minute he came back in and said the thing that is going to be the whole of this Monday, which was: "**I'll have to do it by hand at the end. It'll open at the end. It just won't open here.**"**

**It did not open at the first stop after. It did not open at the second. At the third, the driver got out and got a bar and did it by hand, and about nine people got off a bus on a wide road on a Monday morning at about eleven minutes past nine, in about ninety seconds, one at a time, and one of them was a man of twenty-two with a bag of conduit under his arm who was the last one out because he would not push.**

The driver said the doors would be shut again in about a minute and would not open again until the end, and got back in, and went.

**And about four people were on the pavement.**

They had been on the pavement before the bus came. They had come out of two shops and one of those shops has its shutters half down and a board turned to face the wall and has had since before the spring, and they had been standing there about six minutes when the bus came, and they were still standing there about a minute after it went, and none of them said anything to anybody, and none of them asked anybody anything, and none of them looked at the man with the bag of conduit who had got off last and would not push.

**All four of them looked at the doors.**

---

**He got to the depot on a bus at about ten and asked for a door strip.**

The man at the counter was about twenty-six and did not want to give him one and gave him one.

"**They're for a fleet,**" the man said.

"**I know what they're for.**"

"**Then you'll know I can't do that.**"

"**You'll do it because I'm not asking for a strip out of stock,**" Marek said, "**and I have got one in a bag that I have been keeping for about four months because I have been waiting for a bus that needed it, and I would rather it went today than in four months.**"

The man at the counter looked at the bag.

"**Four months,**" he said.

"**Four months,**" Marek said.

"**Right,**" said the man, and gave him a new one as well, which he did not have to do and did not explain and Marek noticed and has not mentioned it to anybody.

**It was laid by two men from the depot and a man of twenty-two on a bus in a lay-by about eleven minutes further down the same road, in about twenty minutes, and the doors opened and closed four times while they did it, and the fourth time was on a test and not on a run, and the depot man wrote the time on the back of his hand.**

The strip he gave away had been in a bag in a cupboard in Lattice Ward since about the spring, and it was a strip for a nearside door of a type that had not been in service in this city for about four years, and it had come out of a job in the second week of the spring that had not needed it, and he had put it in the cupboard because throwing a strip away is a thing you do when you are tired and he had not been tired.

**He did not get a replacement until the following Tuesday and it took a call to a firm in a third district to find out that the type was still made, and that call is in the second week of this stretch of days and is not on this page.**

---

**At about eleven he went back to the stop on the wide road. The four people had gone. There was nothing there at all except a bit of paper about nine inches from the kerb and a shop with its shutters half down and a board turned to face the wall.**

He did not go into the shop.

---

**The rest of the Monday was the shop. Eleven jobs and eleven names, the last at half past five.** A woman of about thirty-six came in and asked whether a person could refuse a thing after they had already said yes to it, and was told that they could, and that it was easier if they had said yes in a room rather than on a form, and that she did not have a preference about which of those two things was better and neither did he.

At about three a man of about fifty-eight came in with a folded sheet of paper and asked whether anybody in this city printed things, and was told that there was a two-man shop on a road off Saltmarket that had been there about nineteen years, and the man of about fifty-eight wrote it down on the back of his own hand again and put his hand in his pocket, and Marek did not mention that this was the second time he had done it and the same man.

**From about half past six until ten there were four people in those four rooms off that service road and one behind the three and the one behind had a light off at about eight for no reason anybody has ever established, and there was one other person in the fourth of them from about half past six until ten and she is about thirty and the chair the door is behind is hers and the eighth of eight pages stayed in a ring binder on a back shelf and nobody has apologised to her in any form.**

---

**Three things on that Monday and none of them was on a form.** A shop on a wide road with its shutters half down and a board turned to face the wall, and the four people on that pavement who had come out of it and stood there about six minutes before a bus came, and Marek has not established whether they were waiting for that bus or for the shop. **A bag in a cupboard in Lattice Ward with one strip in it, out of a type that has not been in service here for about four years.** And a depot man's hand, with a time written on the back of it in the same four-number way a man of about thirty-four writes four numbers on his every morning, in a different city, for a different reason, and neither of the two men has ever seen the other's hand.

---

**He wanted, for about an hour on that Monday, for one of the four people on the pavement to look at him.**

**None of them did. All four of them looked at the doors of a bus that had gone, and then all four of them looked at the doors again about four minutes later when they thought it might be coming back, and Marek was about nine feet from the nearest of them for the whole of that and he was the one person on that pavement who could have opened them by hand in about twenty minutes if he had had a strip and a bar and a reason, and he had not got a reason, and he did not go and find out whether they had anywhere to be, and that is the cost of the whole Monday and it is not in a load book.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at ten, and he went home at ten past ten, and there was no strip in the bag any more and there had been one in it since the spring.
