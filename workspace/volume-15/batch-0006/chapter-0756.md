# Chapter 756 — One Sheet Of Paper With A Date And A Name On It, Replacing Something That Ran Until Somebody With A Key Decided It Was Finished, A Trestle Table In A Second District, About Nine People Arriving At Intervals To Be Asked Three Questions, And A Man Of About Sixty-One Who Said Nothing At All And Was Not Written Down As Agreeing

**One sheet of paper with a date and a name on it, replacing a rule that ran until somebody with a key decided it was finished. A trestle table in a second district, about nine people at intervals, three questions asked of each, and a man of about sixty-one who said nothing at all and was not written down as agreeing.**

---

Nine names went on that Tuesday's sheet and nine dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The cage in the round-the-side had gone about nine times in the fortnight.

**The nine hand copies of the front of one page stand at nine hundred and sixty days, one hundred and thirty-seven weeks and one day, and the thirteenth line on the board of two nails stands at one thousand two hundred and sixty-five days, one hundred and eighty weeks and five days. Neither of them is exact and neither of them has anything to do with the date on that paper.**

The separation is at seven hundred and seventy-four days, one hundred and ten weeks and four days, and it has not come down and nothing has gone back into it.

---

**The Crown Clause was written to last until somebody with a key decided it had done its work. What replaced it on that Tuesday is one page, a date on it, and a name.**

The name is a woman of about fifty-seven who is entitled to end it before the date and who lives about nine streets from that room, and who has to walk to that building to sign, and who has not been asked to do it once.

**Nobody gave a speech. About nine people came to a trestle table one at a time and were asked three questions by a woman of about thirty-eight and she wrote down what they said and read it back before they left.**

The three questions are on the sheet in large type and they have not changed since the spring.

**What will you let this city do for you while it is in force. What will you not let it do for you while it is in force. And who can stop the part you have agreed to.**

---

**The woman of about thirty-eight wrote this in about nine weeks and she has not been thanked for it and she did not put her name at the top of it.**

She has one of her own names at the bottom of the sheet under the third question and she says that is so that there is somebody to complain to and not so that anybody has to.

---

**A man of about sixty-one came to that table at about half past four and said nothing at all for about nine seconds and she wrote four words on the sheet and turned it round so he could read them.**

**Said nothing. Not agreement. Ask me again.**

"**That is what you get if you say nothing,**" she said. "**You do not get written down as a yes and you do not get left off. You get asked again.**"

He read it and nodded and went away and has not come back and nobody has chased him.

---

**Most of that Tuesday was the shop and about nine jobs, and one of the nine was not a job at all and he made it for nothing and gave it away.**

A man of about thirty-three brought a gate latch that has not been catching its keeper since before the spring. It was the keeper and the bar and the pivot, and the pivot had been set about a quarter of an inch too high by a man who has not been asked and would not admit it if he were.

He made the man a drawing.

It was a pencil drawing about eleven inches by nine of one gate latch seen from the side, with a line on it at the height the pivot wanted to be at, and nothing written on it except his initials in one corner, which is what he puts on everything he draws and is not a signature.

**He gave it away. The man put it in his inside pocket and did not ask for a second one and has not been back, and Marek has not been able to work out since whether he minded giving it away, and has decided that he did not mind at the time and is not certain now.**

A woman of about twenty-six brought a stool with a leg out of square and he took it apart and put it back together with a wedge in a place nobody had put one before, and charged about nine pounds for the half hour.

---

Nobody in that room treated nine seconds of a man saying nothing as a decision, and that is the whole of what changed on that Tuesday and it did not come from anybody and it cost nothing.**

---

**About nine people came to that trestle table on that Tuesday and about four of them said no to a part of it, and the page has four lines on it for a no and one line for a person who has not decided.**

A man of about thirty-eight said he would let this city put a light on in his stair and would not let it touch his mother's door, and wrote it in about nine words, and the woman of about thirty-eight read it back and he nodded.

A woman of about twenty-nine said she did not know yet and was going home to ask her brother, and the woman of about thirty-eight wrote *not yet* and did not write a reason.

**The page has room for about nine more names and there is a second page under it with the same three questions printed on it and nothing on it, and that second page is for the people who come after the people who came today, and nobody explained that and nobody had to.**

**What did not change is the wall in a first district with the records on it, and about four hundred signatures in a box that says what the last approved answer was, and every one of those is still public and still argued about.**

**He was offered the next version of that sheet to write and he refused it in about four seconds and the reason he gave is the only reason on that Tuesday that anybody said out loud.**

"**I will not be the one who signs the day it stops, because on that day it will be read as mine, and I am not entitled to it and neither is anybody in that room.**"

About four seconds.

---

**He wanted the person who can end that rule to be somebody who has to walk to a place to end it, and she does, and he does not know whether she has ever walked it, and there is no way of asking without asking her to come and do a thing for him.**

---

**What he gave up was a drawing he made of that room and its table and where the light comes in, which he made for nothing, gave to the woman of about thirty-eight, and cannot get back and did not ask to keep.**

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six a woman of about thirty in the fourth of them, at her chair, with the door of that room shut and behind her.**

Her page is the eighth of eight and has been shut in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and eighty-three days. It did not come out on that Tuesday, and nobody asked her anything about it, and there is nobody in this city who is entitled to open that binder in front of her.

---

**That trestle table came off the back of a van and went up two flights at about half past three and one of the legs is not the same as the other three and it wobbles about a quarter of an inch and nobody there has mentioned it since.**

**She put a piece of folded card under the leg before the first person sat down and did not say she was doing it and Marek saw her do it and did not say he had seen it.**

About four people have asked that woman of about thirty-eight where the card came from and she says it came off a shelf and that is the whole of the answer she gives.

He said none of it.
Nine dated jobs went out and nine came in, the shutter came down at about ten, about nine people went to a trestle table in a second district one at a time and were asked three questions, and one man of about sixty-one said nothing for about nine seconds and was not written down as agreeing, and a rule with a date and a name on it replaced a rule that had run on somebody with a key deciding it was finished.
