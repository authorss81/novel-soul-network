# Chapter 759 — A Converted Tram Depot On A Road In A Fourth District With Its Doors Off Their Tracks And A Scarred Workbench Down One Side Of It, About Eleven People Each Asked In A Doorway In Advance What They Will Put In And What They Keep Outside The Line And Who Can Stop The Exchange, And Nine Minutes Of Practice In Which The Man Who Had Spent Eleven Years Being The One Who Could Stop Something Was The One Who Was Stopped

**A converted tram depot on a road in a fourth district, a scarred workbench down one side of it, and about eleven people who had each been asked three things in a doorway in advance. Nine minutes of practice happened on that bench, and the man who had spent eleven years being the one who could stop something was the one who got stopped.**

---

Eleven names went on that Tuesday's sheet and eleven dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The cage in the round-the-side had been going about nine times in the fortnight and the van was not needed.

**The nineteen, the last line in use, reads one thousand and ninety-seven days, one hundred and fifty-six weeks and five days, and the card in that rail reads one thousand four hundred and five days, two hundred weeks and five days. Neither of those two comes out on a whole number of weeks and no figure on this page does.**

The separation is at seven hundred and eighty-one days, one hundred and eleven weeks and four days.

**The depot has its doors off their tracks and one bay with the rails still in the floor under a falseboard, and about nine coats on a rail by the office door with one hook bent about a quarter of an inch out from the line of the others.**

There is a scarred workbench about eleven feet long down one side with the grain standing up out of it in two places where about four hundred people have put clamps down hard.

**This is not the room under the building in a first district. That room is dark and stays dark and this one is four districts and about nine streets away and nobody has joined them and nobody is going to.**

---

**The woman of about forty-four has the keys to that depot and she is not a member of anything and she is not running any of it in the sense of running a session, and she is the only person in that building who has a set of keys.**

She asked eleven people three questions each, in person, over about nine days, in corridors and doorways and one car park.

**She has not been thanked. She has not been taught. She is not owed anything by anybody in this city and there is no list she is on.**

---

**The three questions are on a piece of card about the size of a hand, and they are: what you will put in. What you keep outside the line. And who can stop the exchange.**

About nine people answered in about nine words each and two of them answered in about four and one of them asked for the card to be taken away and it was taken away and nobody has since said a word about why.

---

**That Tuesday held a bench in it from about nine until about twelve and about nine jobs, and then a drive, and then about forty minutes standing in a depot before anybody came to it.**

He got to that road at about twenty to five. **The doors on it are off their tracks and the light in the main bay does not come on until somebody puts a hand on a switch by the office door, and he put it on and stood in about eleven thousand square feet of a building doing nothing at all for about forty minutes.**

There is a rail by that office door with about nine coats on it and one hook bent about a quarter of an inch out of the line of the others, and he looked at that hook for longer than a hook can be looked at and did not touch it.

**The bar of steel about four feet long was already lying on the scarred bench when he came in and he did not know who had put it there and did not ask, and about four people have since told him it was in that depot before that Tuesday, and he has taken their word for it because he cannot check and would rather not.**

---

He asked nobody to do anything they had not already said yes to in advance, and he checked one thing only, and the one thing was whether anybody had changed their mind since they said yes.**

Two people had changed their minds and both of them said so before anything started and neither of them was asked for anything else and one of them went and stood at the door and stayed for about eleven minutes and then came back in and was asked nothing further about it.

---

**It is the only teaching anybody has had in this stretch of days and it was about nine minutes long and he did not run it.**

He stood at the scarred workbench and did the thing he has been not allowed to do for eleven years, which was to be the middle of it, and it took him about four seconds to get into position and he did not do it gracefully and about four people have said since that they had not expected him to be the one who was held.

**Two people held the other end of a bar of steel about four feet long, and he asked a woman of about thirty-three who cooks to take it off them, and she took it off them in about four seconds and said the word they had agreed on, and he let go.**

About nine people put one thing in and about two of them kept a thing out and about four of them were stopped and about one of them stopped somebody else without being asked to.

**Nobody in that depot kept a count. Nobody wrote anything down. Nobody stood at the front. Nobody called that room to order at any point, and the only person in it who said anything that started anything was the woman of about forty-four with the keys.**

---

**About nine people had put one thing in on that Tuesday and about two of them kept a thing out, and the two who kept a thing out told nobody what it was until about twenty past six, about four hours later.**

A man of about thirty-eight said in about nine seconds that he was not going to say what he had brought in because the person beside him had kept something and he was not going to be the first one to break it, and the room went quiet for about four seconds and about four people looked at each other.

**Nobody in that depot asked either of them and about four of the eleven have said since that they were the reason nobody asked.**

The woman of about forty-four wrote nothing down, has never written anything down in that depot, and has said that if she had written it down she would have had to decide what to do with it and that is not a thing she is going to do at eleven o'clock at night.

**He wanted her to have asked him to do the second half. She did not ask him and he did the first half and the second half was done by about nine other people and he stood at a bench and watched it.**

---

**What it cost was about nine minutes of his own hands at a scarred workbench and about nine of those minutes are the only minutes anybody has asked him for in this whole of it, and his hands were not right for about two hours afterwards and that has happened four times in eleven years.**

---

**There are four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six on that Tuesday a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, in her chair, with the shut door of that room behind her.**

The eighth of eight is her page, and it has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and ninety days. It did not come out on that Tuesday, nobody has asked her one question about it, and nobody is going to ask her.

---

**His hands were not right for about two hours afterwards and that has happened four times in eleven years, and the other three were all in the winter and all of them were at a bench with other people in the room.**

**He told the woman of about forty-four about it in about nine seconds at the door of that depot and she asked him one question, which was whether he wanted to sit down, and he said no, and she said right and did not ask again and has not mentioned it since.**

About nine people in that depot watched a man of twenty-two get his hands taken off a piece of steel by a woman who cooks, and none of them has described it to anybody as anything.

He said none of it.
