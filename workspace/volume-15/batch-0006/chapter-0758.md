# Chapter 758 — A Friday That Began On A Bench And A Counter And Four Rooms Off That Service Road And Then Went Up A Stair To A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket, Where About Nine People Sat And Four Came To A Table And Where At About A Quarter To Eight A Woman Of About Fifty-Two Who Is Not A Member Of Anything Said One True Thing About A Chair In About Nine Seconds And Then Stood By The Door

**A Friday held the same three places it has held since before the spring, and then a stair going up to a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, and about nine people in a room over about an hour and a half, and four of them at a table, and a woman of about fifty-two who had come about her own business and had been in that room twice before.**

---

Thirteen names went on that Friday's sheet and thirteen dates went with them, the last taken at about twenty to five. The cage in the round-the-side had not been emptied since the middle of the week.

**Line twelve on the board of two nails reads one thousand three hundred and seventeen days, one hundred and eighty-eight weeks and one day, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall reads one thousand and three days, one hundred and forty-three weeks and two days. Those two do not come out exact on this Friday and neither of them is about the chair.**

The separation is at seven hundred and seventy-seven days, one hundred and eleven weeks to the day, in a one-line box about sixteen months old.

---

**That room is a first floor above a line in Saltmarket and it has been in about nineteen years and there are about nine chairs in it and a table and a woman of about sixty who holds the room and has held it for about nineteen years.**

The book on that table is a book with a green cover and it is on sixty-five lines. The tin beside it is on seventy-three with the lid on it and has not been opened for about eleven years.

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room. It has not moved in this stretch of days and it did not move on that Friday and Marek did not look at it twice and did not go near it and has not mentioned it to anybody.**

---

**A woman of about thirty-four came at about half past seven and said she was sorry she was late and was not late, and has been coming to that room since before the spring. She sits in the eighth chair because from the eighth chair she can see the back of the ninth chair and she likes knowing what is behind her.**

She has a page of her own in her coat and she has never put it on that table and nobody in that room has ever asked her to.

---

**The woman of about fifty-two came in at about half past six on her own business, and her business was a form in a second district that had been filled in for her.**

A tick in a box where her name should have been written, in a hand that is not hers, on a form about nine lines long, filled in about eleven weeks ago.

**She had been in that room twice before, both times about a bill, both times in the autumn before the spring, and she had sat in the sixth chair both times and had not been asked a question either time.**

She sat down in it at about ten to seven and said she wanted somebody to look at the form and not to do anything about it, and about four people heard her say that and none of them asked which eleven weeks or why she had come twice before and said nothing.

---

**She said it at about a quarter to eight, in about nine seconds, to the room and not to anybody in it.**

"**That chair has been against that wall since before the spring and nobody has ever sat in it, and this room has run for nineteen years without it, and it would run the same with you in it.**"

About nine seconds. About thirty-eight words.

**She was looking at the man of about fifty-eight who had come in with a laundry boiler that comes on at the wrong time, and not at Marek, and Marek was four feet from the table.**

---

**Nobody said anything.**

The woman of about sixty who holds that room did not say anything. **Nothing was written down. Nobody asked her a question, and nobody has told her since that there was anything in what she said except a chair, and if she is ever told she will say she was talking about a chair and she will be right.**

**Marek did not answer her and has not answered her.**

---

**She did not sit down afterwards. She stood by the door for about nine minutes with her coat on and then went out and down the stair, and nobody asked her to stay and nobody walked her to the stair.**

---

**He refused in about four seconds to answer a man of about forty-four who had asked him in about nine seconds whether it was true that this room would run the same with him in that chair.**

"**I do not know. I have never held this room and I am not going to say a thing about it in front of the people who hold it.**"

About four seconds. The man of about forty-four said right and did not ask again.

---

**The counter of that Friday had a man of about thirty-six on it at about half past three with the back of a chair frame in a bag and about four people behind him.**

The frame had been let go at one joint by somebody who did it on purpose in the winter because it was the cheapest way of getting it through a door, and it had been put back the same way, and Marek took it to pieces on the bench, glued the joint with a hide glue, and came back to it at about five.

The man asked whether it would ever come apart again and Marek said it would, if anybody took it apart again on purpose, and that was the answer he wanted and he said so.

**He charged about nine pounds and the man paid and did not say thank you and did not need to, and the chair went out of that door with the joint at ninety degrees and the man carrying it himself.**

A woman of about twenty-two came in at about ten to four with a bag of about nine hundred leaflets that she intended to deliver herself and wanted to know a sensible way round a street, and he drew her a loop on the back of an envelope in about four minutes and gave her the envelope, and she went, and has not been back.

---

And then the rest of that Friday happened, at about eight, on the same floor, in about eleven minutes, and it was not about any of the above.**

A man of about fifty-eight said in about nine seconds that a boiler on the first floor of a building in a first district comes on at about eleven at night and goes off at about one, every night, and that it was fitted in the spring by a man who did not ask him what it was for. **The woman of about sixty asked him one question, which was whether it was his, and he said it was not, and she said right.**

A woman of about thirty-one came to the table about half an hour in, set her palm down flat on the wood for about four seconds with nothing held in it, and lifted it again without a word about it. She has said since that it was for nothing.

---

**The man of about fifty-eight said nothing else for the whole of the hour, which is what he does.**

**There is a card of about four inches by two on a wall in a fourth district in a woman's own name with nine words on it and about four people walked past it that Friday on their way somewhere else and did not stop.**

The woman of about thirty-four in the eighth chair saw him looking at the door the woman of about fifty-two went out of and said, to nobody, that people do that, and then did not say anything else for the rest of the hour.

**He wanted the woman of about fifty-two to have sat down afterwards. She did not, and he has not worked out whether he minded, and he has not asked anybody.**

---

**What it cost was about nine minutes of standing four feet from a table with nothing to say, and his own evening.**

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six on that Friday a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, in her chair, with the door of that room shut behind her.**

Her page is the eighth of eight and it has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and eighty-six days. It did not come out and nobody has asked her a question about it and nobody is going to.

---

**The light in that room above the line in Saltmarket is on a pull and it takes about nine seconds to come up to what it actually does, and about four people have said so and none of them has said it twice in the same breath.**

**That night he went down that stair at about eight and he did not go up it again, and there are two other ways out of that building and he knows that and did not use either of them.**

He got home at about ten past ten and had about four pounds of the day's money in a bag and put it in the drawer in the morning because the till had been slow again and had been slow for about four years and nobody has ever written down why.

He said none of it.
Thirteen dated jobs went out and thirteen came in, the shutter came down at about ten, about nine people sat in a room above a line in Saltmarket for an hour and a half and four of them came to a table, a woman of about fifty-two said one true thing about a chair in about nine seconds and nobody answered her, the chair did not move, and a boiler came on at about eleven at night for about the ninth night in a row.
