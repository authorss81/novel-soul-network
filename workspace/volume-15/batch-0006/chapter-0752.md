# Chapter 752 — Nobody Was Standing In That Doorway For Any Reason But The One Reason: A Woman Of Fifty-Three Came Up A Stair Under A Building At About A Quarter Past Six Into A Doorway Where Four People Were Waiting, A Person Entitled To Say One Thing Said It In About Nine Seconds, And Nobody In That Doorway Forgave Anybody Or Condemned Anybody

**Nothing on a Monday in this city is the bench or the counter or the four rooms off that service road. This one was a stair going up under a building, four people standing in the doorway at the top of it, nine seconds said in a corridor by somebody entitled to say them, and a door shut from the outside at about half past six.**

---

Eleven names went on that Monday and eleven dates went with them, the last taken at about twenty to five, and the cage in the round-the-side had gone about nine times in the fortnight and went again on the way down.

**Line twelve on the board of two nails reads one thousand two hundred and ninety-nine days, one hundred and eighty-five weeks and four days, and the post at that corridor end reads nine hundred and twenty-seven days, one hundred and thirty-two weeks and three days. Both of those are true and neither of them has anything to do with a doorway.**

The separation stands at seven hundred and fifty-nine days, one hundred and eight weeks and three days, inside a one-line box that a man filled in about sixteen months ago in a room none of these people are in.

---

**The stair is the same stair. It goes down under a building in a first district and it comes up into a first floor, and the man of about sixty who keeps that building was at the top of it with a cup in his hand and did not come down and was not asked to and has not come down since.**

He has been asked once, in the spring, whether anybody knew the stair was there, and he said he knew, and that was the whole of it.

---

**She came up it at about a quarter past six on her own and nobody made her come up it and nobody sent her down.**

Iona Sorn is fifty-three. She had a coat over the back of one of the chairs in the room at the bottom of that stair on Monday, and she had a piece of paper in her hand with about nine words on it, and she came up the stair with it in her hand and did not put it anywhere.

**Four people were standing in the doorway at the top of it.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a room of lists on the first floor of a building in a first district, and who had come up that stair at about half past five and had gone down again and had come up a second time. A man of about thirty-four with the keys to that building, who had not gone down at all. A woman of about thirty-eight who lives on that floor and came out of her own front door and has never been in the room at the bottom of that stair in her life. A man of about fifty-six who works with the woman of about forty-four and had a folded sheet in his hand and did not open it.

---

**The woman of about forty-four said it in about nine seconds and it was nine words and she did not say it twice.**

"**You are held at the request of nine rooms. The hold has a date on it. I did not write it.**"

About nine seconds. About eighteen words and nine of them were the sentence.

**Nobody in that doorway said a word of forgiveness and nobody has said one since and there is no scene anywhere in this city in which anybody forgives her.**

---

**The morning of that Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and about nine things went through it that were not about any of the afternoon.**

A woman of about thirty-eight brought in a fan from a first floor that turns but does not move the air, and the spindle at the back of it had been turned about a quarter of an inch too far by somebody before the spring, and he took it back by about that amount and it moved air for about nine minutes and then stopped, and he told her that.

A man of about fifty-seven brought a kettle he did not want mended.

"**I want it to keep doing it,**" he said. "**It goes off at about four minutes and then it does not go off again until you turn it over and put it back at an angle. That is the only thing in my kitchen I can rely on and I do not want a new one and I do not want it fixed.**"

"**Then there is nothing to do,**" Marek said, and charged him nothing and wrote nothing down, and the man stayed about eleven minutes on a stool and watched him not do anything, and at the end said that he had come in expecting to be told it was not possible and that this was better.

---

Marek was on the stair at the time and was not in the doorway and was four feet down it, and he came up, and he was let into the room and not into the doorway.**

He has said since that the four feet were the whole of what he has been able to do with any of it.

**He was asked, by the man of about fifty-six, in about four seconds, to be the one who wrote down what had happened in that doorway, and he refused in about four seconds.**

"**No. I was four feet down a stair. Write down what you saw.**"

The man of about fifty-six said right and wrote nothing, and did not ask him a second time, and has not written anything down.

---

**She asked one question on the landing and it was not about the room under the building and it was not about the plate in the cabinet.**

"**Is there a list.**"

The woman of about forty-four said: "**There is a list. It has your name on it and a date and it is not a secret and anybody in that corridor can read it.**"

**About nine seconds, and neither of those two sentences was praised by anybody in that corridor, and nobody in that corridor said one word either way about what she had done.**

---

**What she said last was about nine seconds long and nobody has repeated it and nobody thanked her for it.**

"**Then it is a list and not a room,**" she said. "**I have been in rooms that were not lists. Put the date down in front of me.**"

The woman of about forty-four put the date down in front of her, and Iona Sorn read it, and did not sign anything, and was not asked to sign anything.

---

**She is fifty-three and she is in the first floor of a building in a first district in a room with a table in it, four chairs, a window with a bar across it, and a kettle that belongs to the building and has always belonged to the building.**

The door is shut from the corridor and there is a bell on the outside of it that anybody in that corridor can sound and about four people in that building know how to sound it and have agreed between themselves who sounds it and when.

**There is no cell in this city. There is a room, and a bar, and a list with a date on it, and a person who did not write the date, and a bell that four people can sound and nobody has sounded.**

She was not executed and she was not given a life and she was not let go. **She was told, in about nine seconds, by a man of about fifty-six reading from a folded sheet, what the terms are, and the terms include that she may be asked to work on repairs in this city under somebody else's hand and under a review she does not choose, and that refusing that costs her the only part of the terms she would want.**

She has not said whether she will. She was asked at about ten past seven and has not been asked since and has not been offered it again by anybody.

---

**The man of about thirty-four with the keys went down that stair at about ten past seven to look at the room at the bottom of it and came back up and did not say what was in it and did not say what was not in it.**

He has the keys to that building and about nine other buildings and he was asked, at about half past four, whether he would stand in a doorway, and he said yes in about four seconds and then said that Marek should have said the second part first, which is that he can go home at any point and nobody ever says that.

He stood in it from about ten past seven until about half past seven and went home, and he did not tell his wife and is not going to, and he has not been asked about any of it by anybody including Marek.

**About nine people in that building knew where that room was on that Wednesday and about four of them had been inside it once in about nine years and none of them has told anybody.**

**The woman of about thirty-eight who came out of her own front door said one thing in the corridor at about half past six and it was about a set of stairs and not about the woman.**

"**She has not been on that floor since Friday,**" she said.

**Nobody in that corridor answered her, and nobody answered her at the time and nobody has answered her since, and what she said is not a thing anybody is required to think anything about.**

---

**He wanted the woman of about forty-four to have been somebody who had been in that room at the bottom of the stair, and she was not, and she had read about it the way everybody else has read about it.**

That is the want. It is on no form and he has not said it to anybody and it is about four seconds long.

---

**What he gave up was a bus fare and about twenty-two minutes on that landing, and there is no charge anywhere in this book for a stair he did not go down.**

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six on that Monday a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them with the shut door of that room behind her chair.**

The page that is hers is the eighth of eight and has been shut in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and sixty-eight days, which is a whole number of weeks and has never been printed as one.

It did not come out on that Monday, nobody asked her a question about it, and nobody is going to.

---

**The bag that shop's money goes in is a canvas one with a strap and it has a mark on the bottom where somebody in the winter put it on a wet step and it came off in about half of it.** He has never replaced it and there is no reason on any sheet for it and the money goes in it about nine times a fortnight and it goes up a stair on it and it comes down a stair on it.

**On that Monday he noticed the mark on the bottom of the bag while he was waiting at the top of a stair for a woman of fifty-three to come up it, and he has not thought about it since except in the way he thinks about it, which is more than about most things.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in, the shutter came down at about ten, a woman of fifty-three came up a stair into a doorway where four people were standing, nine words were said there by a person entitled to say them, a door was shut from a corridor at about half past six, and nobody forgave anybody and nobody condemned anybody and a date was written down in front of a woman and she did not sign it.
