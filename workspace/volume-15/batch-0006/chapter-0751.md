# Chapter 751 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six And Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Six, And Four Pieces Of Work In Four Rooms That Stopped Doing What They Were Told, On Four Faces, And One Of The Four Is Twenty Minutes Away And He Is Standing In It

**The bench was his until two, the counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road after that. None of that is what a Thursday is. This one was four pieces of work that had stopped doing what they were told, on four faces, in about nine seconds each, and he was stood in one of them and heard about the other three from people who had been stood in them.**

---

Thirteen names went onto that Thursday's sheet and thirteen dates went with them, the last of the thirteen taken at about twenty to five, and the cage in the round-the-side had been going about nine times in the fortnight as it has been going since before the spring.

**The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is at nine hundred and eighty-one days, one hundred and forty weeks and one day, and the nine hand copies of the front of one page are at nine hundred and forty-one days, one hundred and thirty-four weeks and three days. Those are the two figures the Thursday is measured against and there is nothing whatever written under either of them.**

The separation is at seven hundred and fifty-five days, one hundred and seven weeks and six days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and the number in it has gone on being a subtraction all that time and does not know it.

---

**He was at that bench until two and there was nothing wrong with anything in it.** Two lamps, a bar heater, a bracket that came off a door frame in the spring and was not the bracket that came off it, and a woman of about thirty-four with a toaster that only works when the room is warm.

He told her the same thing he tells about four people a week, that the lever has a bend in it that about four hands have bent further, and she said she did not want it mended again, and he did not mend it, and she paid a pound.

"**It is not the toaster,**" she said at the door. "**It is that the plug is behind the boiler and you cannot plug it in without moving the boiler.**"

He had not thought of that and she had, and he wrote it on the back of a docket and did not put the docket in the file, and she went out without her coffee.

---

**The middle of that Thursday was nine jobs on a bench and two of them went back on the wall.**

A gate brace on a field gate in a fourth district had been done twice in the spring by two different men and had come off twice, and the woman who owned the gate brought it in a carrier bag on her shoulder. **Marek took it apart on the bench, put it back together twice, and found that the plate on the gate post had been drilled with a five-eighths hole by somebody in a hurry and filled with a plug that had started to move.** He made a new plate out of a piece of the shop's own flat stock, which took about forty minutes, and he told her the drill hole was still in the post and would still be there in ten years, and that the new plate would go over it.

She asked whether it would come off again and he said not from that.

**He did not charge her for the forty minutes and put about nine pounds on the docket as bench time and let the shop carry it, and there is no line anywhere for a bench hour that a person does not pay for.**

A lamp on a landing in a second district flickered only when the door at the bottom of that landing was shut, and it did it for about four minutes at a time, and it had been doing it since before the spring. **He took the switch out and put it back and could not find it, and told her so, and charged her about nine pounds for the taking-out and the putting-back, and she said that was fair.**

A boy of about fifteen waited on a stool until about half three for his mother's stair rail and asked twice whether it could be done twice on purpose, so that whoever came next could not undo it.

"**It can be done twice,**" Marek said, "**and the second time is the one that counts, and the second time takes longer than the first, and about four people have told me it takes longer and then watched me not do it.**"

He did the second time. It took about fifty minutes. **The boy watched all of it and said nothing at all and paid for it out of a fold of paper and did not give change and Marek did not have change and neither of them said anything about it.**

---

**He shut the counter at half past five and walked, and there is no form anywhere for the walking and there was no reason on any sheet for it.**

The yard is in a second district and it is about twenty minutes on foot and he has been in it about nine times in four years and it is not his and he does not hold anything in it.

There were three people in that yard when he came in and one of them was a woman of about thirty-nine with her sleeves turned up past the elbow and both hands wet to the wrist.

---

**She said it at about ten past six and she said it in about nine seconds and nobody in that yard consoled anybody after it.**

"**It came off the shared line at half past five and it is on its own now and it is on the third, and it will not come down to the second, and it will not sit on the third either. We are doing it by hand. That is forty minutes and it was forty minutes yesterday and it will be forty minutes tomorrow, and that is the pump.**"

About nine seconds. About forty-four words.

**There was no sentence in that yard about how big anything was and there has never been one.**

---

The thing on the wall above it had been taking one setting from one place for about eleven years. At about half past five it stopped taking it from that place, and what it took instead was the last setting anybody had put into it, and the last setting anybody had put into it was the third.

**A pump that will not balance and is done by hand.** Two men of about thirty and about fifty were on the lever with a bar and a wedge of steel between the coupling and the frame, and the woman of about thirty-nine had her hands on the delivery every time it came round, and it took about forty minutes, and at about twenty to seven it settled on the second and stayed there for about four minutes, and then it went back to the third and they did it again for about eleven minutes and it held.

Nobody said anything about that either.

**He was in that yard for about an hour and two hours of the forty minutes he had been told about were done before he got there, and he was not asked for anything, and he picked up one end of the bar twice and was let.**

---

**He did not see the other three and nobody asked him to and nobody has said since that he should have.**

A man of about fifty-eight came into that shop at about half past six, before Marek was in the yard, with a brace under his arm that had come off the back of a chair and would not go back on the first attempt and had come off the bench a second time.

He wanted to know whether the chair was finished.

"**It is not finished,**" Marek said. "**And I am not going to force it, because the last person who forced one of those put a twist in the seat rail and then it was the rail and not the chair.**"

He charged about nine pounds to look at it and did not do it, which is not a thing the shop normally does, and the man said right and took it away in his own van and has not come back and does not know about the other three.

**A man of about forty-one was on a bridge in a fourth district at about a quarter past six and found the barrier down and went back about four hundred yards and came down the other side on foot.** He told the man of about fifty-eight about it in a car park at about half past six and the man of about fifty-eight told Marek about it at about forty to seven. That is the whole of the route it took.

**A woman of about forty-seven had a bed on a trolley at the foot of nine steps with a turn on them at about half past five and could not get it up, and did not try again, and the person on it was on it when they stopped and was on it when the power came back and is not any worse and is not any better.** A man of about thirty-four who had come to ask about a lift for a different floor told Marek about that at about twenty to eight.

Four pieces of work in four rooms. **He has been in one of them and has not been in the other three and is not going to be, and there is nobody he could ask to be sent to any of them.**

---

**At about half past four on that Thursday a man of about forty-four brought in a kettle that has been tripping a circuit in a second district and had been doing it for a fortnight.**

The element had come loose from its terminal by about the thickness of a card and had been arcing behind the plate on every boil, and the plate had been over it and had been burned where nobody could see it until the plate came off.

Marek told him the kettle was fine and the plate was not, and showed him the burn, and the man said he had had that kettle for nine years and it had never once made a noise.

"**It has been making it about four times a day and you have been in another room,**" Marek said. "**That is what a fault sounds like when nobody is listening to it.**"

He did not fit the plate and charged about nine pounds to open it and tell him, and the man took the kettle away and has not come back and has not had it seen to anybody.

**The circuit on that second floor did not trip again after the man isolated the kettle, and nobody has worked out whether that was the whole of it.**

**He wanted to be asked which of the four he should have gone to, and he was not asked, and he has worked out that being asked would have been a way of handing him one of them.**

That is the want and there is no form for it and it is about four seconds long and it was on his face at about half past six in a yard in a second district with a bar in his hands.

---

**He gave up about eleven pounds of work he had already priced on that Thursday, and four pounds of his own for a bar and a wedge of steel that went on the invoice with the shop's name on it and not his.**

The wedge was his. The bar was the shop's and had been the shop's since before the spring and he put the wedge in beside it and did not take it out again and did not charge for it and it will be in that yard in the spring whether he is in it or not.

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and a woman of about thirty in the fourth of them from about half past six, with the shut door of that room behind the back of her chair.**

Her page, and it is the eighth of eight, has been shut in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and sixty-four days. It did not come out on that Thursday and nobody put one question to her about it and nobody is going to.

---

**There is a shelf over that bench with about nine things on it that are not for sale and that he has never moved.** A coil of wire from a cistern in a first district that came back as somebody else's problem. A length of conduit with a bend in it that was put aside in the winter because the man who brought it said he would come back and he did not. A face plate with the wrong screw slots in it. A tin of about nine screws that are all the same and none of which is the one anybody wants.

**He has moved that shelf twice in eleven years and both times because a delivery had to get past it, and about four people have asked him what is on the shelf and he has told three of them and not the fourth.** He does not know what he would say to the fourth and has not worked it out.

He said none of it.
Thirteen dated jobs went out and thirteen came in, the shutter came down at about ten, a pump in a second district came off a shared line at half past five and would not balance and was done by hand by three people in about forty minutes and again for about eleven, and three other rooms in this city had the same kind of evening and he was stood in one of them and heard about the other three from people who had been stood in them, and nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.
