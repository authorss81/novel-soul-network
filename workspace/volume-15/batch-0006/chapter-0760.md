# Chapter 760 — The Wednesday Of A Week In Which A Book With A Green Cover Opened And One Line Went In And A Number Was Said Once And Four Words Were Said Once By The Only Person Entitled To Say Them, And Then An Evening Meal, About Nine Metres Of Corridor, And Two Doors

**The last day of this stretch of days began the way every one of them began, and in the middle of it was a stair going up to a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, about nine chairs, a table, a woman of about sixty who has held that room for about nineteen years, and a book with a green cover that was on sixty-five lines at half past six.**

---

Twelve names went on that Wednesday's sheet and twelve dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The counter was shut at half past six and he went up a stair at about ten past seven with about nine pounds of the day's money in a bag because the till was slow again and it has been slow since before the spring.

**Rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind, stand at one thousand four hundred and two days, two hundred weeks and two days, and the nine hand copies of the front of one page stand at nine hundred and sixty-eight days, one hundred and thirty-eight weeks and two days. Neither of those two comes out on a whole number of weeks and the three on this Wednesday that do are in the conditions below.**

The separation is at seven hundred and eighty-two days, one hundred and eleven weeks and five days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old.

**That room is a first floor above a line in Saltmarket and it has been in about nineteen years and there are about nine chairs in it and a table and a woman of about sixty who holds that room and has held it for about nineteen years.**

The book is a book with a green cover and it was on sixty-five lines when she came up and it is on sixty-six lines now.

The tin beside it is on seventy-three with the lid on it and it did not move and nobody touched it.

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room and it did not move and nobody has ever looked at whether it moves.**

---

**About nine people were in that room over about an hour and a half. Four came to a table. Five did not.**

A woman of about thirty-four came at about half past seven and said she was sorry she was late and was not late, and has been coming to that room since before the spring, and sits in the eighth chair because from the eighth chair she can see the back of the ninth chair. She has a page of her own in her coat and she did not put it on that table and nobody asked her to.

A man of about fifty-eight ran the laundry boiler thing for about nine seconds and nothing came of it.

A woman of about thirty-one came to the table about half an hour in, put her palm down flat on the wood for the length of a breath with nothing held in it, and lifted it again without a word about it. She has said since that it was for nothing.

---

**The book opened because a man of about thirty-eight said one thing to the woman's face, and he had come four times and had never once said what he came about.**

A meter in a first district that has been reading about nine high for a fortnight, in a street of about eleven houses, and he has brought it up four times and has each time been asked a different question and has answered it.

"**It is nine high,**" he said. "**It has been nine high for a fortnight and I have four times sat in this room and not said so.**"

About nine seconds.

**She did not answer him about the meter. She opened the book, wrote one line in it, and read the line out to the room, and the line was about a meter on a street of about eleven houses that had been running nine high for a fortnight.**

**Nobody in that room had seen the book open in about four years and about four people looked at the page and one of them said nothing and has not said anything since.**

---

**One line went in. The book was on sixty-five lines at half past six and it is on sixty-six lines now and nobody in that room has said a word about the two figures and nobody in this city has ever made anything of them.**

**A woman of about forty-four asked him at about ten to eight, on her way out, whether he would put his name beside the line, and he said no in about four seconds.**

"**It is not about you,**" she said, and then at the door that by Friday it would be about him. He let both of those stand.

**She had written the line herself and she did not sign it either, and she went down the stair without being asked anything else.**

The line is the only thing that has gone into that book in about a month and it went in at about half past seven.

---

**The number was said once, at about a quarter to eight, to the room, in about nine seconds, and it is the only time anybody in this city will hear it.**

"**Sixty-one of which fifty-six.**"

**That is the fifty-sixth sitting and it is the fifth time in this city that a number has been said in that room about a book and a tin, and the fifth is not a pattern anybody has said out loud in that room and nobody in it is going to.**

**There are two counters in that room and they are separate and neither is the other. The stretch of days this room has been writing in is on sixty-five days. The sittings are on fifty-six. The two are not added anywhere in this book and there is no page on which they are.**

---

**Four words were said once, at about ten past eight, by the only person entitled to say them, in one line, and they decided nothing at all.**

The woman of about fifty-two who chairs that body and has never sat on anything in it had come up that stair for the first time in about four years, and she had a hand on the back of the ninth chair's neighbour and not on the chair.

"**The Commons Standing Committee.**"

About two seconds.

**She said in the same breath that nothing turns on it, that she is saying it because she is the person who says it, and that she said it once into a printed box on a second floor about four years ago and told that room then that she would not say it in a hall.**

Nobody in the room above the line in Saltmarket said the four words after her. Nobody said them before her. Nobody has said them since.

---

**What the four words did not do on that Wednesday, and this is the whole of it: they did not appoint anybody, they did not remove anybody, they did not start anything, they did not fund anything, and about nine people in that room would not have gone to the door if she had not said them.**

---

**About nine people were in that room over about an hour and a half and about four of them said the word *that is the first time* once, between them, and not one of them said it to anybody in this city afterwards.**

A woman of about forty-four said it about the book opening and about four people heard her and two of them agreed and neither of them has been back to that room since.

**The room is open, and the woman of about sixty who holds it is there on Wednesdays, and she has said one thing about it to a man of about forty-four who asked her directly and to nobody else in that room.**

**What she said was that about nine people have stopped coming to that room, and that they were not coming because of what she said and not because of what anybody else said, and that she has about nine other Wednesdays.**

**Nobody thanked anybody at the table or anywhere else in that room on that Wednesday and nobody has thanked anybody in this stretch of days.**

Marek was in that room and was four feet from the table and was not asked anything about the book and was not asked anything about the four words and has not been asked since.

---

**The whole of the daylight of that Wednesday was a shop and about nine jobs and a till that did not add up.**

That till has been slow since before the spring and on that Wednesday it came up about four pounds under and he counted the tray three times and the third count agreed with the first.

A woman of about thirty-eight brought a gate hinge on a strap and it came apart in her hands in the doorway before she got to the counter, and both pieces were in her apron and neither one of them was broken.

"**That is a hinge,**" Marek said. "**Bring me the other one in about nine days.**"

She said she would and she did, and the second one came in with about two coats of paint on it and it went back on and it is still on.

**He charged about nine pounds for the strap and did not charge for the ten minutes he spent telling her that a hinge does not come apart in a doorway, and that if it does, it has been told to.**

---

**He wanted one of the two doors to be left open. Neither of them was, and he shut his own, and he did not know until afterwards whether hers had shut first.**

---

**What it cost was one line in a book with a green cover, which he did not write and was not asked to write and has not been given, and about nine minutes of walking he has not charged anybody for.**

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, at her chair, with the shut door of that room behind her.**

Her page is the eighth of eight and it has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and ninety-one days. It did not come out on that Wednesday, nobody asked her one question about it, and nobody ever is going to.

---

**There is a bus that goes past the end of that street at about ten past ten and it has gone at about ten past ten for about four years and there were about nine people on it that night and none of them got up for anybody.**

**He stood at the door until it went and then went in and did not put the light on in the passage and did not need to.**

The tool roll is on its hook by that door with one screwdriver left in the fourth pocket, as it has been since before the spring, and about four people have asked about that pocket and he has told all four of them what it is for and none of them has believed him.
He said none of it, and then he went home and somebody cooked.

They ate rice and a stew that was two days old because that is what was in the pot, and bread, and there was no second thing and neither of them said there should have been. **She asked him about the book and he told her, in about nine seconds, that it opened, and that a meter went in, and she said right and asked about something else.**

They live near Civic Spine, about nine doors along from the tram stop, with a door each and a room each and a calendar each and a set of keys each, and about nine metres of corridor between the two doors.

**At about a quarter to eleven he went to his own door and she went to hers about four minutes after, and both doors closed, and about nine metres of corridor was left with nobody in it.**
