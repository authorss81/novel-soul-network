## 4. THE THREE FILES THE WRITER IS LEAST SURE OF, NAMED BEFORE STARTING, AND NOT CLAIMED CLEAN AFTERWARDS

1. **Chapter 758, where the question fifteen volumes old is answered.** The trap on one side is a peroration and on the other it is a person being turned into the answer. **Neither is available. It is about nine seconds said by a woman of about fifty-two who is not a member of anything, to a room, and the answer is a chair he does not sit in, and the sentence that carries it is a fact about a chair and not about a principle, and the chapter goes on afterwards and something else happens in it.**
2. **Chapter 751, where the Hush ends.** The temptation is to write the ending as a relief. **It is a set of about nine ordinary failures published on about four different faces in about nine seconds each, and nobody is consoled, and the damage is not small and is not described as small, and the two are different requirements and the prose has to hold both.**
3. **Chapter 760, the last page of the manuscript.** **The count is said once and one line goes in and the four words are spoken once by a person entitled, and an evening is eaten and two doors close, and the word `together` is not said, and the old hall stays dark, and none of that is a win and none of it is a failure, and the whole of the last volume has been the refusal of a phrase on a piece of paper and the last page is an ordinary Tuesday.**
