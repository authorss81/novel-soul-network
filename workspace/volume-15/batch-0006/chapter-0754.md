# Chapter 754 — A Key Put Down On A Table By The Man Who Had Kept It Alone For Six Years, A List Of Nine Service Groups That Nobody In The Room Was Entitled To Stop, A Woman Who Said Yes In About Nine Seconds To A Review She Has Not Read, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Asked To Leave A Room And A Corridor And Did Both

**A key put down on a table in an office on a first floor at about half past four, a list of nine service groups nobody in that room was entitled to stop, and a woman who said yes in about nine seconds to a review she has not read. A man of twenty-two carried one chair twice and was then asked to leave.**

**Oren Vey has kept exclusive control of Threadline for six years and about eleven of its service groups do not belong to him after about half past four on that Wednesday and about four of them do.**

He came at about ten past four. He had the key in his hand and put it on the table himself, which nobody had asked him to do and which he has said was the only part of it he decided.

**The list is of nine service groups and each one of the nine is overseen by somebody who does not run it.**

There are four people in the room who are entitled to know all nine names and there were five by about ten past four and a woman of about twenty-four sat on a chair against the wall the whole time and said nothing about any of it.

"**No.**"

Marek Senn, twenty-two.

**Between about ten past four and twenty to five he was in that office and what he did in it was ordinary work.**

There were about nine people in that room and eight of them were not about the key. A man of about fifty-two had a meter on a table in the corner that nobody had told him to bring and that turned out to be the meter of a flat he is not the owner of, and he had brought it because he wanted somebody to look at it, and he was told to take it away, and he took it away.

A woman of about thirty-three had a list of nine names on it and wanted to know whether the first name on that list could be the second name on it in about a fortnight, and was told in about nine seconds that it could not and why, and she wrote the why down.

**Marek carried a chair twice, twice for the same chair, because the fourth person in the row had to get past it and there was nowhere else for it to stand and nobody had thought about that before the room filled.**

"**You are in this as a person who works here. The next thing on that table is a list of people who do not work here. Go and stand in the corridor.**"

He went and stood in the corridor for about eleven minutes with his back to a fire door, and a woman of about thirty-eight who was going out past him said *thank you* to nobody in particular, and he has not worked out whether that was for him.

"**Yes.**"

"**You have not read it.**"

"**No. And I am saying yes to being reviewed and not to whatever is in it, and if the two of those come apart in about nine months that is what I will have bought and it is cheaper than the alternative.**"

About nine seconds. **About forty-one words. He has not been thanked for it by anybody and he did not ask to be, and the office he runs has not been absolved and has not been shut.**

**What is on the wall of that room is the public record and the public record is still disputed and about nine people are still arguing about it in rooms this man has never been in.**

The Continuity Office's records of what was known and when are on a wall in a first district in a building with a canopy over its door, and about four hundred people have signed a form whose box says what the last approved answer was, and that number is not on the wall and is not on any page of this book.

**Nobody in that room said the records had been kept well. Nobody in that room said they had been kept badly. The question is still open and it is on a wall and it is not going to be closed in this stretch of days.**

**The first thing that room did with the nine service groups was ask what happens to a person who works for a group that stops.**

A woman of about forty-one asked it in about nine seconds and there was no answer for about eleven, and then the man of about fifty-six said that in about four districts the group that stops leaves its people on that group's own books and pays them out of its own money for a period, and that in about four districts nobody has said that yet.

She said she wanted it on the paper and not in a room.

**It went on the paper at about twenty to five, in about four words, and nobody thanked her for asking and nobody thanked him for writing it, and about four people have since asked whether it applies to them and one of them is not in any of the four districts.**

**He wanted to have been in that room for the list, and he was not, and being out of the corridor for it was the correct thing and both of those are true.**

**What it cost was about nine pounds of his own for a locksmith he did not need, called at about six to change a cylinder in a unit off that service road because the last man who had a key to that unit was told at about half past four that he did not.**

He has still got the old cylinder in a drawer and he does not know what to do with it.
