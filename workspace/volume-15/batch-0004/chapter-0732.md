**The room is a first floor above a shop in a second district and it has been a room for about eleven years and it has been somebody's office for about six of those and it is being made into a room again on a Friday morning with a floor up.**

About eleven people are in it. Two of them are taking up the boards and about four are carrying them out to a yard at the back and a man of about thirty-one is planing a joist and is doing it well. **A woman of forty-five is standing in the doorway of the one room that is still shut, holding a cup, and she came in at about half past eight and has been in the building all day.**

She leads the maintenance council in this city. She can take a man out of a room in about four seconds and has done it once. On a day in the spring a man of twenty-two asked her to take him out of a piece of paper and she said no, and the reason she gave then was that there was nothing on him to take off, and he is not going to be asked to be careful about that.

**He came in at about eight minutes before four because he had walked four miles to get there and had meant to.**

He had a sheet of his own in his hand, not a form and not a letter, and he had not written on it and had not intended to, and he had carried it about four streets and then across two more because he did not know what else to do with his hands.

He said: "**I want you to stop the share.**"

She said: "**No.**"

**That is about nine seconds and there was nothing in front of it and nothing after it and about four people in that room heard it and about four of them carried a board out to the yard and did not turn round.**

---

Then she said why, and she said it the way she says everything, which is once and without stopping.

"**You want it stopped on a Friday and the people who can do it have never been told it in words and cannot be. There are eleven of them and I cannot put it on a sheet, because a sheet says what a thing is and not which way a wrist goes, and I cannot say it in a corridor, because a corridor is where you say a thing to about nine people at once and about four of them will be holding something.**"

"**About twenty minutes each,**" the man of about thirty-one said from the floor, not looking up.

"**About twenty minutes each,**" she said. "**That is three and a half hours of a Friday and I do not have three and a half hours of a Friday. If it stops on a Friday, then the rooms that were going to be shut on the Friday are still open on the Monday, and there are people in about nine of them who are waiting for a floor, and that is not a thing anybody can put back before the end of the week.**"

A man of about forty-four said, "**We could do the ones first and the rest after.**"

"**We could,**" she said. "**And I would then have about nine people who can do it and about two who cannot, and the one who has to pick which of those two does a floor is the person who is holding the keys, and that person is not a technician and has never been on a Friday in their life.**"

Nobody said anything to that and it was correct and the work went on.

**She was not loyal and she was not standing in front of anything. **What he got instead was not what he asked for and he did not ask again.**

She said, at about ten past four, in about nine seconds: "**You can be in the room on Monday at about nine and watch all of it and you will be about eleven people and a floor and nobody will explain anything to you. That is not being taught anything. I am not able to say you have learned it. You will not be able to say you have learned it. That is the whole of what I can give you and it is not a favour.**"

He said right.

**He was in that room on the Monday at about nine and about eleven people did a thing and he stood against a wall and nobody spoke to him for two hours and a half and he could see all of it and could not do any of it and has not said so to anybody.**

**He wanted her to have asked him a question before she said it.**

Not *are you sure* and not *think about it.* A question about the thing. About the eleven and the twenty minutes and the two who cannot do it, and he wanted the no to have come after about four of those, so that it would have been about a floor and a Friday and not about him standing in a doorway holding a piece of paper he had not written on.

**She did not ask him anything and there was nothing in it for her to ask and he knows that and it did not make it better.**

He said none of it.
Ten dated jobs went out and ten came in, the shutter came down at about ten, and he got home at about twenty past ten, and he had walked four miles to a building that two buses would have taken him to, and he had got there eight minutes early on purpose and had spent those eight minutes outside on a step counting about nine lorries, and there is no entry anywhere for the four miles and there is no entry anywhere for the eight minutes.
