# Chapter 740 — A Wednesday, A Room Above A Shop In A Third District Open From Six Until Nine With About Nine People Going In And Out And Nothing Decided In It, A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Asked For A Copy Of A Sheet And Was Refused In About Nine Seconds Over The Paper, And Forty Minutes In Which A Man Of About Thirty-Eight Was Out Finding Out Whether A Man On A Landing Had A Machine

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one was an ordinary Wednesday in a room in a third district that was open from six until nine, and about nine people came and went in it, and none of the three things on the table in it changed by anybody's hand before nine o'clock.**

---

Eleven names and eleven dates went on that Wednesday, and the last of the eleven at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten and the cage went about nine times this week, which is about what it has been doing for a month.

**That man of about fifty-one against the north wall of this shop is at nine hundred and sixty-six days, which is one hundred and thirty-eight weeks to the day. The outstanding ask is at one thousand and fifty days, which is one hundred and fifty weeks to the day. Those are two of the three that come out exact on that Wednesday and the third of them is in the conditions below, and there is nothing at all written under any of the three.**

The separation is at seven hundred and forty days, one hundred and five weeks and five days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and nothing has ever been put back into it and nothing ever will be.

---

**The room is a first floor above a shop in a third district and the key to it is still kept under the counter downstairs by a woman of about thirty-eight who has never been told what is on the table in the room and has not asked.**

It was open from about six until about nine. **About nine people came and went. Two of them were in it for about forty minutes. One of them was there for about two hours and said almost nothing.**

The sheet of lined paper was on the table in the middle. It had three headings on it and there were words under all three and there was no fourth line and there was no foot to it.

Nobody changed anything on it on that Wednesday. **Nobody took it away and nobody put it in a drawer and nobody photographed it, and nobody in that room, or in that building, has ever done any of those three things to it.**

---

**The one refusal of the evening happened at about half past six and took about nine seconds.**

A woman of about twenty-nine who had been in that room twice asked the woman of about thirty-three whether she could have a copy of the sheet. She wanted it for a woman in another district who had asked her for one.

"**No,**" the woman of about thirty-three said.

"**It is one sheet of paper. It is three lines.**"

"**It is one sheet of paper,**" she said, "**and if there are two of them then there are two of them, and one of them will get changed and the other one will not, and then somebody in that district is going to work off the wrong one and be right about it. I am not going to be the reason there are two.**"

"**Can I write it out?**"

"**You can write out what you saw,**" the woman of about thirty-three said, "**and that is not the same and you will know it is not the same in about a fortnight and it will not be my fault then.**"

**The woman of about twenty-nine did not apologise and nobody apologised to her and she did not come back that night and has not been to that room since, and nobody has gone after her, and there is nobody in this city entitled to.**

---

**And then everybody sat down and waited for about forty minutes, and the waiting was the evening.**

The woman of about forty-four was going to find out one thing: whether the man who holds the keys on a Tuesday and a Friday night in a third district has any way of being told that he is the one who holds them on a Tuesday and a Friday night.

A man of about thirty-eight went out at about half past six to find out. **He came back at about ten past seven and said that the man has no machine and has not had one since before the spring and that there is a woman of about thirty-four two streets away who takes messages for a pound a week and that nobody has ever asked her to take one for that building.**

Nobody said anything about that. **About four people in the room wrote the woman's name on the back of something that was not the sheet, and about four people in the room had not written it down, and the man of about thirty-eight did not write it down, and it was not written down anywhere on the front of anything.**

The woman of about forty-four sat with her hands in her lap for the whole of those forty minutes and did not ask how long it was going to be once.

**She is not consoled and nobody tried and the forty minutes are not a failure and they are not a delay that anybody is going to apologise for on the Thursday, and what the forty minutes bought is that a woman in a first floor room in a third district now knows that a man in a landing in a third district cannot be reached, and about nine people in that room knew that on Monday and one of them knew it on Friday and nobody in this city has done a single thing about it since.**

---

**The man of about fifty-one with the back room and the kettle is still not asked and still has not been told and is still not unhappy about it.**

Nobody opened the first line. Nobody has opened it since the Thursday. **It is on a sheet of lined paper on a table in a first floor above a shop in a third district and it says that a warm room is available between six and ten, and nobody in this city who is cold has been told that, and the man who offered it is not going to be chased and has said so once, out loud, to a stranger, and meant it.**

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one more behind them, and from about half past six a woman of about thirty in the fourth of the four with the chair behind that door, and that chair is hers, and she was in it at about ten.**

The eighth of eight of her pages has been shut in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and forty-seven days and it did not come out on that Wednesday and nobody has put a question to her and nobody is going to.

---

**He went past the bottom of the Saltmarket stair at about nine and the light was on in the room above it and he did not go up and has not been up it since the Wednesday before last.**

He thought about going up. **He has been up that stair about four times in about a month and about nine times in nineteen years and he has not been four times in a month in the whole of that nineteen years, and he has not worked out what that is and he is not going to be told.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated and eleven taken, the shutter down at about ten, and the last of the evening gone by about ten past ten, and about forty minutes of that Wednesday were a chair in a first floor in a third district, and it was his own bench time and nobody in that room knew that and there is no form on which it would have been written if they had.
