# Chapter 738 — A Monday, Nine Chairs Around A Table In A First-Floor Room Above A Shop In A Third District, Three Lines Finished On A Sheet Of Lined Paper, And The Third Of Them Disputed By A Woman Of About Forty-Four For A Reason About A Landing And A Shift And A Man Who Was Not In The Room And Was Not Asked And Was Not Going To Be

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one had nine chairs round a table in a first floor above a shop in a third district, about eleven people, three lines on a sheet of lined paper, and the third of the three taken apart in about nine seconds.**

---

Twelve names and twelve dates on that Monday, and the twelfth of them at about half past five. The shutter came down at about ten. The cage went about nine times this week and nobody has counted them and nobody is going to.

**Line sixteen on that board of two nails stands at one thousand one hundred and forty-eight days, which is one hundred and sixty-four weeks to the day. The four rooms off that service road and the one behind them are at one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight days, one hundred and ninety-four weeks to the day, and those are two of the three on that Monday and the third of them is in the conditions below with the other fourteen, where no two of the nine hand copies have ever been compared by anybody.**

Those three are the exact ones on that Monday and there is no fourth.

The separation is at seven hundred and thirty-eight days, one hundred and five weeks and three days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and it is walked every day in this book and has not been walked quickly once.

---

**The nine chairs went up a stair at about half past four on the evening before in two loads and about two of them came off a floor on the Friday and were still damp, and the man of twenty-two carried them up himself in his own van and nobody asked him to and nobody paid him for it and nobody has mentioned it since.**

**There were about eleven people in that room by about seven on the Monday. Eight of them were the people who were in it on the Thursday before and about three were not and had come on a word from somebody.**

The sheet of lined paper came out of a drawer. The three headings are the same three and the writing on them is four days old and it is in three different hands and one of the three hands is the hand of a man of about thirty-eight who does not come to these things every week.

---

**The first line was finished in about four minutes.** A warm room between six and ten in a first district, with a man of about fifty-one who would not have his name next to it. Two people who could drive somebody to a clinic on a Thursday and could not do it on a Wednesday because the clinic is shut on a Wednesday.

**The second line took twenty minutes and it is where the two thirds of that sheet get decided.** No list of names. A man of about forty-four said that the fault he has four times on the same machine should be written down with nobody's name on it and was told again, by a woman of about thirty-three, that it stays on the back of his hand, and that he had already said he would take it round himself, and that the fourth of those four cost about two hours of a stop.

**No name is written next to the first line. Nobody asked for it again and nobody is going to.**

---

**And then the woman of about forty-four took the third line apart.**

It reads: *whoever is holding the keys on the night.*

She said, in about nine seconds, standing where she had been standing since about ten past six:

"**That is a room and not a person, and it has been a room since the Thursday, and it is wrong in a way that will not show up until somebody needs it. In my two rooms the keys are on a hook and the hook does not know whose hands they were in last, and there are three of us on the nights and one of the three does Tuesdays and Fridays, and he has done those two nights for about nine years, and he is not in this room and he does not know that a room in a third district has got his job written down on a piece of paper. Put his name on it. Not a job. A name. And then, if he cannot do it one Tuesday, nobody in this city can tell him so, and there is no way of leaving a message in that building and he does not have a machine.**"

Nobody said anything for about a minute.

A man of about thirty-eight said, "**A name is a list. That is the whole of the second line.**"

"**A name is a person,**" she said, "**and a list of about four people who are not a member of anything is not a list, it is four people, and about nine of us in this room already know all four of them and two of them are not even in this building. The second line is about writing down who is in a room. That man is not in a room. He is on a landing at half past three in the morning with a set of keys and nobody has ever told him what he is standing in the middle of.**"

"**And who writes it,**" the man of about thirty-eight said.

"**I will,**" she said.

---

**And nobody in that room could stop her and nobody in that room could agree with her, and the third line on that sheet is still the same seven words it was on the Thursday.**

That is what happened between about ten past six and about half past six on that Monday. **There is no chair in that room that the man on that line could be put in. There is no line at the foot of the sheet. There is nothing that says who is entitled to change a thing that about eleven people wrote and no one of them is a member of anything, and the group does not own the sheet, and the sheet has no number on it and is not in a drawer and is on a table in a first floor above a shop in a third district.**

So it stands. **She is right and nobody has told her that she is right and she did not ask and she has not written his name anywhere and she will not write it in that room without somebody to send it to.**

---

**What it cost was a week, and the week belonged to a man who was not in the room.**

The group had agreed on the Thursday to open the first line on the following Wednesday, and on the Monday they decided they would not do it until the third line was settled, because the woman of about forty-four had said that she is not going to have nine people in a building doing a thing under a line that says a room when it is a person, and about four of the others in the room said that they would wait with her.

**The man of about fifty-one with the back room and the kettle has been asked for a fortnight and has not been asked again and was not told on Wednesday that nothing is going to happen, because nobody in that room had his number and nobody was going to go and get it.**

He is not unhappy about it. He said on the Tuesday before this one, to a man of about twenty-four who called about a kettle, that a back room with a kettle in it is a thing you offer and not a thing you chase, and that is exactly what he said and it is not modesty and nobody has called it anything.

---

**The rest of the Monday ran.** Twelve names and twelve dates, the last at about half past five, and a man of about thirty-three brought in a chest of drawers with a runner in it that has been binding for about four years, and it was unbound and eased and would stick again in about a year, and he was told both of those things and paid about eleven pounds and thanked nobody.

---

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind them from about half past six, and in the fourth of the four a woman of about thirty in the chair the door is behind, and she was not spoken to and has not been asked a question by anybody in this city this year.**

Her page, the eighth of eight, has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and forty-five days and it did not come out on that Monday and nothing was put to her.

---

**Three things on that Monday.** A washer under a table leg that has been there since before the spring. **A hook on a landing in a third district with a set of keys on it and no name on the hook.** And a third line of seven words that nine people have agreed is the wrong seven words and have agreed to leave there.

---

**He wanted to have been the one who told her that she was right.**

Not in that room, because he would not have been believed and because he has no standing in it and has never had. **He wanted there to have been a place where a person who has said a true thing about a room gets told that she has said a true thing about a room, and there is not one, and he has looked for one in about four years and it is not anywhere in this city, and the sheet has no line under it and the man of about fifty-one has not been asked and will not be.**

He said none of it.
Twelve dated jobs went out and twelve came in, the shutter came down at about ten, and he got home at about ten past ten, and nine chairs went up a stair on the evening before in two loads in his own van on his own time and two of them were damp and he carried them up anyway, and there is no charge for that anywhere in this book and there was no charge agreed.
