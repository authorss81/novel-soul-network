# Chapter 733 — A Monday, A Bag With A Strip Of Tape On It Carried From One Set Of Crews To Another In The Back Of A Van, Four People In A Second District Who Did The Last Two Steps In The Other Order, And Nobody Who Reported It To Anybody

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one had two men in a van with a bag between them and four people in a second district learning what was in it, and one of the two men came back in the afternoon and said nine words about it and then stopped talking about it.**

---

Twelve names and twelve dates, the last of the twelve taken at half past five. The shutter went down at about ten, and the cage has gone about nine times this week.

**The four rooms off that service road, and the one behind them, stand at one thousand three hundred and fifty-one days, which is one hundred and ninety-three weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of the front of one page are at nine hundred and seventeen days, one hundred and thirty-one weeks to the day, one corner torn and eight of them unfinished and nobody anywhere has ever laid one against another to see whether they differ.**

Those are two of the three that come out exact on that Monday and the third of them is in the conditions below, and there is no fourth and there is nothing under any of the three.

The separation is at seven hundred and thirty-one days, one hundred and four weeks and three days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and it is walked every day in this shop and has never been walked quickly.

---

**The van left a yard in a first district at about eight in the morning with two men in it and a bag between them on the floor.**

One of them is the man of about thirty-one who planed a joist on the Friday and is the same man and not a new one. The other is a man of about forty-four who came with him and has been in this trade about nineteen years. The bag is canvas and has a strip of tape on the outside of it and on the tape somebody has written a list of parts. **The list of parts is a list of parts. It is four lines long and none of the four lines has a verb in it and none of them is what was in the bag.**

There is no paper in this city that says what was in that bag and there is no form and there is no person in this city who could say what it was except the eleven people who were shown it, and nine of them were shown it on a Friday and two of them have not been shown it at all.

---

**It is about four minutes of a thing.** That is what the man of about forty-four said to the four of them in the second district, standing against a wall with his hands behind him, and he said it and then he did the four minutes and he did not explain any of it.

It is the order in which you take a fitting apart. There are four marks on the body of it and the fourth one is the one that everybody gets wrong, and the wrong one is not dangerous and it works on most of what it is fitted to. **The last two steps are the two that matter and the last two steps are the two that are easiest to get in the wrong order, and once you have got them in the wrong order the first two will not feel wrong at any point while you are doing them.**

A woman of about forty-three said, at about the second minute, "**That's the one.**"

"**That's the one,**" he said.

**He was right and she was right and about four people in that room had written nothing down and none of them asked a question and one of them asked, at the end, whether it was all right to do the fourth one first, and he said that it was not, and did not say what to do instead, and they wrote that on the third of four steps and did the other two in the order they had been given.**

---

**He came back in the van at about half past four and put the bag on the floor of the shop and untied it and did not untie the tape.**

Marek asked him how it had gone. He said, in about nine words, that they had got it, and that about four of them had got it, and that one of the four had asked which two were the last two, and that he had told her, and that was the whole of what he had said about it and he was not going to say any more of it.

"**Which two,**" Marek said.

"**The second and the third,**" the man of about forty-four said. "**And the fourth first.**"

He put the bag on the shelf and went out and it has not been up in this conversation again and there is no arrangement in this city by which he would be asked.

---

**A man of about thirty-eight, who has been in two of these rooms and is not a technician and does not want to be one, said at about half past five that the second district ought to be told.**

He said it well and he had thought about it and he was not being clever. **What he said was that four people had been given something by two men in a van and that if four people got it and about nine of that building did not, then the about nine would do it their own way, and that their own way would be the way it was done before, and that this is a thing that can be checked by asking the second district what order they did the last two steps in.**

The woman of about thirty-six who works in that building said no to it in about four seconds and gave one reason, which is the whole of it:

"**If we tell them it was a method, then the man who brought it has not given us a method and he has given us a favour, and a favour can be asked for twice. If we tell them it was a favour, then it was not a method, and it will be done their own way, and that is what would have happened anyway. There is no third one of those and the two of them are the same thing with the words off it.**"

Nobody said anything else about it. **The four people in that room and about nine people in the same building went on doing it in the order they had been given, which was three out of four right, and it works on most of what it is fitted to, and nobody in that building has written down which two are the ones that matter and neither has anybody in the yard in the first district.**

---

**The rest of the Monday ran and the rest of it was work.** The stove in the back room of a supply point in a first district was swept and swept again and drew properly. A woman put a sheet of three sentences in large type on a table in a shelter in a second district and about nine people said all three of them out loud, in that order, and one of the nine had to be made to add the rest of the third one and added it once.

**Neither of those is an event and neither of them is new and nobody in either room was thanked and neither of the two women running them has been asked to do anything for anybody this month.**

---

**A call-out came in at about half past two for a first district, an outside light and a fitting that had gone, and Marek was in a second district at the time and did not take it and the customer went and got the man who had done the door on the Tuesday and he did it and charged the same as Marek would have.**

Marek did not tell the customer he had been in the second district. **The customer does not know there was a choice and Marek did not tell her and has not mentioned it since and there is nobody he could mention it to.**

---

**There were people in four rooms off that service road from about half past six and in a fifth behind them, and in the fourth of the five a woman of about thirty sat in the chair the door stands behind, and nobody went near her between about six and about ten.**

A hundred and forty days is how long the eighth of eight of her pages has been in a ring binder on a back shelf. It stayed in on that Monday. It has stayed in on every day of this stretch and there is nobody in this city entitled to take it out.

---

**Three things on that Monday, and none of them was a thing anybody put on a form.** Four marks on the body of a fitting and the order they are taken off in. **A strip of tape on a bag with four lines on it, all of them parts.** And a list on a wall in a supply point in a first district that has nine names on it and is nobody else's.

---

**He wanted the second district to have sent somebody to ask.**

One person, on a bus, with a piece of paper and one question on it, which is the question of which two steps are the last two. He wanted that to have happened on the Tuesday morning and not on the Friday morning and he wanted to have heard about it from somebody who was not the man of about forty-four. **Nobody sent anybody and there is no form in this city that asks a question like that and no fee anybody is paid for asking it.**

He said none of it.
Twelve dated jobs went out and twelve came in, the shutter came down at about ten, and he got home at about ten past ten, and an outside light in a first district was done on the Monday afternoon by a man who charges what he charges, and the four miles he drove to a second district on his own time are not on any sheet in this shop and there is no column for them.
