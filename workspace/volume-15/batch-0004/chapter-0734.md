# Chapter 734 — A Tuesday, A Public Archive With A Desk In It And A Woman Who Was In A Room In The Spring And Who Is Asked For A Date And Who Gives Something Else Instead, A Man Of About Fifty With A Form That Has No Column For It, And A Refusal Of About Four Seconds

**A Tuesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one had a corridor outside a public archive, a chair in it that had been carried up two floors and was not wanted there, and a room at the end of the corridor in which a woman was asked for one thing and gave another.**

---

Eleven names, eleven dates, and the eleventh of them at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten; the cage went about nine times in the week.

**Against the north wall of this shop that man of about fifty-one is at nine hundred and fifty-eight days, which is one hundred and thirty-six weeks and six days. The four rooms off that service road and the one behind them are at one thousand three hundred and fifty-two days, which is one hundred and ninety-three weeks and one day, and neither of those two is a whole number of weeks and there is nothing in this book that comes out exact today.**

The separation is at seven hundred and thirty-two days, one hundred and four weeks and four days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and every day of this week it has been the same number of days and neither less nor more.

---

**The archive is on a first floor in a fourth district and it has a desk in it and about nine shelves and a lamp on the desk that is on at half past two every day whether anybody is in the building or not.**

The woman at the desk is about thirty-eight and she is not an archivist. **She is a witness. She was in a room in the spring, on an evening, with about eleven other people in it, and she has been asked about that evening by three people in four months and has answered two of the three.**

The man of about fifty is not from the archive either. He has a form and the form is a printed sheet with a heading across the top and four ruled lines under the heading and three of the four lines have a person's name on them and the fourth does not.

---

**He asked her for a date.**

He said it as a question and it was one, and it was about nine seconds long, and the date is a thing she would have been able to give him and everybody agrees she could have.

She said: "**It was a Thursday and it was the second Thursday of that month, and the paper says the Wednesday, and I am not going to be the one who writes the Wednesday on your form.**"

"**I am not asking you to write anything,**" he said. "**I am asking you which day it was.**"

"**It was the Thursday,**" she said. "**And there is no box on that for the Thursday.**"

---

Then she gave him the other thing, and nobody in that room asked her for it and it took about nine seconds.

"**There was no window in that room and there was one chair that had been moved away from the wall and put back before anybody else came in, and there were eleven of us and eleven names, and one of us went out and came back in and the name on the sheet is the name of the one who came back in, and it is not the name of the one who went out. I have never said that to anybody and I have thought about it every day since the spring and I cannot show you a thing about it and I am not going to try.**"

Nobody said anything for a while.

"**Who went out,**" the man of about fifty said.

"**No,**" she said.

---

**And he asked her for a copy, which was the fourth time anybody has asked her for one and it is the reason she has answered two of the three people who asked her.**

"**I would like you to sign a copy of that and I would put it in with the form and nobody would ever see it except the four of us.**"

"**No,**" she said, in about four seconds.

"**It is a copy of what you have just said.**"

"**It is a copy with my name at the top of it,**" she said, "**and a thing with my name at the top of it is a thing that gets asked for twice, and I have been asked three times in four months and I have answered twice and the third time I answered a different question. That is all I have. There is nothing else in this building I can give you and you have not asked me for it.**"

He did not ask her a fourth question. **He wrote on the form that she was present and he did not write down the day and he did not write down the chair and he did not write down the two names, and the fourth ruled line of that form is still empty, and it was empty before he came in.**

---

**In the corridor outside that door, from about half past one until about half past two, there was a man of twenty-two with a chair he had carried up two flights and put against the wall, and he was told to wait, and he waited.**

Nobody in that archive knows his name and he has not been into that room and has not been asked to be and would not have been allowed.

**A call-out came in at about ten past two for a first district, a tap that had been dripping since about ten in the morning, and he was in a corridor outside a door he had been told to wait outside of, and he did not take it, and a man from that street went and did it for the same money and got there at about half past three.**

The customer does not know that there was a choice. **He has not told her and there is nobody in this city he could tell and he did not write it on the sheet.**

---

**The rest of the Tuesday ran.** Eleven names and eleven dates, the last at about twenty to five, and a man of about thirty-three brought in a lamp that works and was told that it works, and said that he wanted to be told that it works by somebody who fixes lamps, and it was said to him, and he paid nothing and went.

---

**Four rooms off that service road opened at about half past six, there is another behind them, and the woman of about thirty was in the last of the four with the chair the door is behind, and that chair is hers on every other evening of the week as well.**

Her page is the eighth of eight and has been shut inside a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and forty-one days. Nothing was put to her on that Tuesday and nothing ever is going to be.

---

**Three things on that Tuesday, none of them in the room where the question was.** A lamp on a desk that is on at half past two whether anybody is in the building or not. **A chair carried up two flights and put against a wall in a corridor.** And a form with three names on it and a fourth ruled line that was empty before the man with the form arrived and was empty when he left.

---

**He wanted to have been the one who asked her for the date.**

Not because of the date. Because the man of about fifty had come in with a printed sheet and a heading and had got a Thursday and a chair and a room with no window out of a woman who did not want to be in a form, and Marek had been in a corridor for an hour with a chair against a wall and had been told to wait. **He wanted to have been in the room and to have asked it and to have been the one she said it to, and she said it to a stranger with a form and it made no difference to her and that is the part he is going to have.**

He said none of it.
Eleven jobs dated and eleven taken, the shutter at about ten, and the road home by about ten past ten, and a chair carried up two flights at about one and back down the same way, and the tap in that first district is off and the man who fitted it is the man who does them there and Marek has not gone back to that flat and is not going to.

---
