# Chapter 736 — A Thursday, Two Rooms In A Third District Whose Hot Water Came On At Eleven Instead Of At Ten Because A Crew Now Checks A Boundary Before It Undoes Anything, A Woman Of About Forty-Four Who Kept The Rota For Both Of Them And Who Said The Whole Of It In About Nine Seconds, And Nobody Who Consoled Her

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one was two rooms in a third district where nine people sleep in shifts and the water came on at eleven instead of at ten, and a woman of about forty-four who had been getting up for it for about nine years and did not want anything said about it.**

---

Nine names on the book and nine dates and the last of the nine at about half past four. The shutter came down at about ten, and the cage went about nine times this week and about forty times since the spring.

**Line thirteen on that board of two nails is at one thousand two hundred and twenty-five days, which is one hundred and seventy-five weeks to the day. The card in that rail, which takes two, is at one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight days, one hundred and ninety-four weeks to the day, and it stands four days behind the four rooms off that service road. Four days is the whole of the difference between those two and there is no other reason in this book for any figure on it.**

The separation is at seven hundred and thirty-four days, one hundred and four weeks and six days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and it is the same number it was on the Thursday before and it will be the same on the next one.

---

**The two rooms are on the top floor of a building in a third district and about nine people sleep in them across three shifts and they share one boiler and one cylinder and the cylinder is on the landing and the isolation for each room is on the landing as well, and both of those isolations were changed at some point since before the spring and neither of them has a label on it.**

The water came on at about eleven on the Wednesday night and at about eleven on the Thursday morning, and it has come on at about ten for about four years, and the reason is a check of about twenty minutes that is now done first, and the check is not a fault and is not a delay that anybody has decided to add.

**Marek went out there at about five on the Thursday to do the second half of a job that had taken half a morning on the Tuesday and had stopped half way.**

---

**A man of about thirty-three who keeps the keys to both rooms said, at about four minutes past five, before the door was off its hinges:**

"**Can you do this without the check.**"

"**No,**" Marek said, in about four seconds.

"**It is twenty minutes.**"

"**It is about twenty minutes,**" he said, "**and it is the only twenty minutes in this job that tells me which of two things went. The cylinder is on this landing and the two isolations are on this landing and neither of them has anybody's name on it, and if I undo the shared part first then I am guessing, and if I am wrong then neither of those two rooms has any water at all until somebody comes back, and I cannot tell anybody on Friday which of the two it was.**"

"**You could put a label on them.**"

"**I could,**" he said, "**and then a label is a thing that somebody has to be right about for the next nine years, and I would be right today, and there is no column anywhere for whether I was right.**"

**The man of about thirty-three said right and went to work and has not been in that landing since and nobody in that building has said the word label again.**

---

**The woman of about forty-four let him in and she had been up since about ten and she had not slept and she did not sit down while he was there.**

She keeps the rota for both of those rooms. It is nine names on a card by the boiler and it is not a list of anybody's business and it is not given to anybody and it has been hers for about nine years.

He asked her, at about ten past five, why it could not have been done in one visit.

She said it in about nine seconds, in one go, in that order, the way a person says a thing they have already said to themselves several times:

"**The check is twenty minutes and it is the only thing that stops me not knowing. Last month it came on at eleven twice and the man of about fifty-one on the top of the other room did not get up and did not have a wash until about seven, and I know that because I knocked on his door at about seven and he was awake and had not been, and I am not going to say to about nine people in this building that a thing I can do in one morning is better than a thing I can do in two.**"

**That is the whole of what she said about it and she said it to a man on a landing and not to a room and she did not ask anybody to agree with her and she did not ask anybody to do anything.**

---

**Nobody consoled her and she did not want to be consoled and there was no point at which anybody tried.**

A woman of about thirty-four came up the stair at about ten past six to ask whether the water would be on by eight and the woman of about forty-four said that it would be on at about eleven the next morning and might be on at about eleven the one after, and the woman of about thirty-four said *oh* and went down again, and **that is the entire conversation that the two of them had about it and it has not been had again since.**

A man of about thirty-one on the other side of that landing heard the whole of the nine seconds through a door and came out at about ten past six and said, "**For what it's worth, mine was off from about eleven last night as well,**" and the woman of about forty-four said, "**I know. I filled a kettle and did not pour it out three times this morning,**" and he said, "**Right,**" and went back in, and that was all of that too.

---

**The rest of the Thursday ran and the rest of it is not this.** A stove in a first district was swept and a plate of food came out of a back room at about half past two instead of at two, and about nine people who work nights came in for it and about four of them had been there at half past one waiting, and Dessa Kwan put the plates down and did not say anything about the half hour and did not apologise for it, and a woman of about twenty-six said, "**It's all right,**" and was told, in four seconds, that it was all right and that it was not going to be all right on Wednesday either, and she said that she knew.

Three sentences in large type were said out loud in a shelter in a second district at about four o'clock by about nine people. **Nobody corrected anybody and nobody was thanked and the woman who runs that room has not been asked to do anything for anybody in about four months and says so if you ask her, which nobody does.**

---

**From about half past six there were four rooms going off that service road and a fifth behind them, and in the fourth one a woman of about thirty sat in the chair behind that door, and that chair has been hers for about four years.**

The eighth of eight of her pages has been inside a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and forty-three days and it did not come out on that Thursday, and nobody has put a question to her, and nobody is going to apologise to her in any form.

---

**Three things on that Thursday, and one of them was a kettle.** Two isolations on a landing with no labels on them. **A plate that came out of a back room at about half past two instead of at two, and about four people who had been waiting since half past one.** And a woman of about fifty-one whose door was knocked on at about seven in the morning in the month before this one.

---

**He wanted her to have been able to say that it was a shame.**

That is all. **She could have. Any of them could have. About nine people in this city would have said it in about four seconds and moved on, and she had it ready and she had it in her mouth at about ten past six when the woman of about thirty-four came up the stair, and she gave the time instead, and he was standing about four feet away with a spanner in his hand and he heard the whole of it.**

He said none of it.
Nine dated jobs went out and nine came in, the shutter came down at about ten, and he got home at about half ten and he was twenty minutes over because of a stair, and about nine pounds of parts that were in his van on the Wednesday were taken out of it by another man and fitted in a fourth district and the labour went with them, and Marek did not see it done and has not mentioned it and is not going to.
