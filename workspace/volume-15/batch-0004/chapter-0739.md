# Chapter 739 — A Tuesday, A Man Of About Thirty-Eight Who Kept A Pocket Book And Who Struck A Line Out Of It That He Had Written Four Days Earlier And Who Handed Over The Only Copy Of Nine Years Of His Own Handwriting With His Name At The Bottom Of It, And Nobody Who Congratulated Him And Nobody With Standing To Stop Him

**A Tuesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one had a man of about thirty-eight with a rubber band on a book of his own, a line in it with a stroke through it, and about a hundred and forty hours of his own handwriting going out of a door in a bag.**

---

Ten names on the book and ten dates on that Tuesday, and the last one in at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten and the cage went about nine times this week, and that is what it does.

**The nine hand copies of the front of one page are at nine hundred and twenty-five days, which is one hundred and thirty-two weeks and one day, and the outstanding ask is at one thousand and forty-nine days, which is one hundred and forty-nine weeks and six days. Nothing in this day's book comes out a whole number of weeks and there is no figure on this page that can be got at by dividing it and having nothing left.**

The separation is at seven hundred and thirty-nine days, one hundred and five weeks and four days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and it is four days longer than it was the last time anybody in this shop had any reason to look at it.

---

**He is about thirty-eight and he keeps the keys to a set of rooms in a first district and he has kept them for about nine years, and on the Friday of last week he was at the back of a builder's merchant with a sheet of paper and a pen asking about one name, and afterwards he went back to that building and wrote something in a book of his own.**

The book is about the size of a hand and it has a rubber band on it and it is his and it does not belong to anybody and it has never been shown to anybody.

**What he wrote on the Friday was four lines. The fourth one said: *we do not pass anything on. There is no form for it and there is nobody to send it to and it is easier like that.***

He read it back on the Friday night. He has read it back about four times since.

---

**He struck it out on the Tuesday at about ten past four, and he wrote under it, in the same hand, that he would pass it on, and he wrote that down because he has never once in nine years done a thing in that building that he had not written down, and that has cost him about nine hours, all of it at an end of the week.**

Nobody in that building stopped him. **There is nobody who could have. He is not a member of anything, he is not on a list, he is not in a room with a chair in it, and the only thing anybody could have said to him was good, and there was nobody there to say it, and if there had been, it would not have made any difference, because there is no form on which a man can be stopped from giving away his own handwriting.**

---

**The thing is about nine years of one job, written out longhand, and there are four copies of it in that building and this was the fourth.**

It is not a technique that anybody has taught him. It is the order and the way of doing about nine things to a person on a floor, in a small space, when the person is not walking and cannot be got up on their own, and he wrote it out himself over about nine years because there was nothing written anywhere and because he did not want to be the only person who could do it.

**He made the four copies himself. The other three are in a drawer in that building. This one has his name at the bottom of the last page, and the name is not a signature exactly, it is how he has always signed the mending sheets, and it is the same in all four copies and it is on this one and it is going out.**

He put it in a carrier bag with a strap on it. He did not put anything else in the bag.

---

**The woman of about forty-four was in the corridor at about half past four and she is the one who asked him whether it could be taken back.**

"**No,**" he said, in about four seconds.

"**It is your own.**"

"**It is my own handwriting and it is in your hall and there is no form for a thing like that going back,**" he said, "**and I would not want it back, and I have known that since about ten past four this afternoon and that is the whole of what happened today.**"

She said right and went down and has not mentioned it since. **Nobody has congratulated him. A man of about forty-four who does the same work in a fourth district said, on the Monday after, in about four seconds, that he had heard, and that he was not going to do the same, and that he was not going to say why either, and Marek did not ask him why and has not asked him since.**

---

**It went to a man of about thirty-one who works nights in that first district and who has never done the nine things and who has about nine years of the other nine things instead, and it took him about three months to read.**

He has not done it. **He has told one person, in a corridor, in about nine seconds, that he has read it and that he is not going to be the one who does it this year, and that the man who wrote it is welcome any day in the week, and that this is not an act and should not be written down anywhere as one.**

**The three other copies are in a drawer in a first district and they are not in that drawer any more by about four pages, because he took the fourth out of it, and nobody has noticed, and there is no list of the four, and there was never any.**

---

**The rest of the Tuesday ran.** Ten names and ten dates, the last at about twenty to five, and a woman of about fifty-three brought in a toaster that has been off since about the spring and was told that it is the cable where it goes into the wall and not the toaster, and she said that she had not wanted to hear that, and it was said to her anyway, and it cost about nine pounds and about forty minutes and she has not been back.

---

**A fifth room behind four rooms off that service road, all five of them going from about half past six, and in the fourth of the four a woman of about thirty in the chair the door is behind, and nobody has asked her anything and nobody is going to.**

Her page, the eighth of eight, has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and forty-six days. It did not come out on that Tuesday and it will not come out on any day of this week.

---

**Three things on that Tuesday, and one of them was a rubber band.** A stroke through a line in a hand that is the same hand as the line under it. **A carrier bag with a strap on it and one thing in it going out of a door in a first district.** And a drawer in that building with about four pages missing out of it that nobody has counted and that nobody is going to.

---

**He wanted the man to have asked somebody whether he was allowed to.**

That is the whole of what he wanted and it is about four seconds long. **A man stood on a landing in a first district on the Friday of last week and wrote a line in a book of his own, and on the Tuesday he put a stroke through it, and there is nobody in this city with standing to tell a man that he may change what he wrote in a book of his own, and he knows that, and he did it anyway, and Marek wanted him to have gone and asked.**

He did not ask. **Nobody would have been able to answer him and nobody would have been entitled to and he went to a woman of about forty-four in a corridor and asked her whether it could be taken back, which is a different question, and she is the only one who could have said no about the copy and could not say no about the change.**

He said none of it.
Ten dated jobs went out and ten came in, the shutter came down at about ten, and he got home at about ten past ten, and a thing went out of a building in a bag that was not on the sheet and not on the book and not priced and not charged, and there is no column in this shop for a man of about thirty-eight giving away nine years of his own handwriting and nobody has asked him about it and he is not going to be.
