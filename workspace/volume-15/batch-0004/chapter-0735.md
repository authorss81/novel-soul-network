# Chapter 735 — A Wednesday, A Building In A Second District That Receives The Returns, A Woman Of About Thirty-Seven Who Fills In A Column That Has Never Been Empty Before And Who Is Refused Four Seconds When She Asks To Write A Word In It, And A Form With Eight Lines On It Where There Were Nine

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one had a sheet of paper on a counter in a second district with a ruled column that has never once been empty in the whole time that column has been on there, and it was empty on that Wednesday and nobody in the room knew whose it was.**

---

The book has eleven names on it and eleven dates, and the last one in was at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten and the cage has been going about nine times a week since before the spring.

**The outstanding ask is at one thousand and forty-three days, and that comes out at one hundred and forty-nine weeks to the day. Line eighteen on the board of two nails is at one thousand and seventy-one days, one hundred and fifty-three weeks to the day. Those are two of the three that come out exact on that Wednesday and nothing has ever been written under either of them.**

The separation is at seven hundred and thirty-three days, one hundred and four weeks and five days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and the column it sits in has a heading and the heading has not been read out loud this year.

---

**The building is in a second district and it is a landlord's office and a filing arrangement and there are four people in it who do the filing and one of them is a woman of about thirty-seven who has done it for about eleven years.**

They receive the returns. Whatever a room in this city sends back goes into that building and comes out of it as a line on a sheet, and there are about nine lines on a sheet and the sheet has four columns and the columns are printed at the top.

The first column is a name. The second is the date. The third is a place: a district and, on some of the sheets, a floor. **The fourth column has a printed heading over it and a rule under the heading, and the fourth column is where the answer goes.**

Nobody decided anything about that column. **It has worked the same way since before the spring and the way it works is that if there is nothing in it then no answer was given, and if there is something in it then an answer was given and nobody in that building has ever asked to see one and does not know what the answers are and is not permitted to look.**

---

**She got to the ninth line at about half past three on that Wednesday and she wrote the name and she wrote the date and she wrote the district and the floor, and then she stopped.**

The fourth column was empty and the rule under the heading was empty.

**She had never seen it empty. Not once in about eleven years. There are about four hundred sheets a month in that room and every one of them has had something in the fourth column, and there are people in that building who would tell you that a sheet comes back with an empty fourth column about once in a year and that neither of them has ever seen it.**

She sat with the pen on the paper for about a minute and a half and then she put it down.

---

**A man of about fifty-two who had come in with a bundle of his own said, in about four seconds:**

"**You could put a word in there. You could put *unable*, and that is a word, and that is what it is, and then it is on the sheet and it is done.**"

"**No,**" she said, in about four seconds.

"**It is a form. It is a column. It is not a man's name.**"

"**It is a man's answer,**" she said, "**and I cannot show you which one it is, so I would be writing it for him, and in about four months somebody in this room is going to read that sheet back to him and he is going to have to stand there and hear a word that I chose at about half past three on a Wednesday, and he did not get asked, and he is not here, and he does not know this sheet exists.**"

Nobody said anything for a while and the man of about fifty-two said the thing that was actually the question.

"**Then you could ask him.**"

"**No,**" she said.

"**He is one of nine.**"

"**He is one of about eleven in this city who could be standing at this counter,**" she said, "**and the sheet does not have his job on it and it does not have his floor on it and it does not have his shift, and if I go and ask him which of about eleven people he is, and he is one of the eleven, then I have told him that a thing about him is on a piece of paper, and he cannot un-know it, and he will spend the rest of that day wondering what word is in the column, and there is no column that I can fill in that is worth that to anybody.**"

**The man of about fifty-two said right and carried his bundle to the counter and did not ask again and has not asked since and did not say her name on the way out.**

---

**The sheet has eight lines on it where every sheet in that drawer has nine.**

That is the whole of what happened and it took about a minute and a half and a refusal of about four seconds. **Nobody in that building has told the person whose name is on the eighth line that the column was empty, and nobody is going to, and there is no arrangement in this city by which anybody could, and if that person is asked in about four years what they agreed to in a building in a second district, the true answer will be that nobody ever told them anything and they will not be believed.**

**Nobody was asked. Nobody was told. The column is a rule about something that is not known and not an answer, and the room where it was applied for a minute and a half was a room with a counter in it and four people in it and no decision of any kind taken by anybody in it, then or afterwards.**

---

**The rest of the Wednesday ran.** Eleven names and eleven dates, the last at about twenty to five, and a woman of about forty-five brought in a rail that has been catching and was told that it has been catching because the bracket behind it has moved about two millimetres in eleven years and that it is not going to move back, and she said that she had not come for that, and he said that he knew, and she paid about six pounds and the bracket is where it is.

---

**At about half past six four rooms off that service road began, and one more behind them, and the fourth of the four held a woman of about thirty and the chair behind that door, and both of those were still there at about ten.**

For a hundred and forty-two days the eighth of eight of her pages has been in a ring binder on a back shelf and it did not come out on that Wednesday, and nobody spoke to her, and nobody in this city has ever asked her a single question.

---

**Three things on that Wednesday.** A pen put down on a counter and picked up again four minutes later. **A column with a printed heading over it that was empty where it had never been empty.** And a bracket behind a rail that has moved about two millimetres in eleven years and is not going to move back.

---

**He wanted somebody in that room to have said the sentence out loud.**

That a person who cannot show which answer they gave is not the same as a person who said no, and that a person who said no is owed the whole of the credit for it, and that the difference between those two is the difference between a choice and a gap, and that about eleven people in this city are standing in a gap at this moment and one of them is on a sheet in a second district with their name on it.

**Nobody said it. He did not say it. The woman of about thirty-seven said two sentences about a man's answer and a man's not knowing, and she was right, and neither of them was the sentence, and it is not going to be said in that room because there is nobody there with standing to say it and he has none either.**

He said none of it.
The book took eleven and gave back eleven, the shutter came down at about ten, and he was through his own door at about ten past ten, and in the coat pocket of a man of twenty-two there is a note of about nine words on the number of lines on a sheet in a second district, and he wrote it on the Thursday and he has not posted it and it is going to be in that coat in the spring.
