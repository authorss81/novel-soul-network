# Chapter 685 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off A Service Road From About Half Past Six, A Man Of About Sixty-Eight With A Handcart Outside The Door At About Eleven Buying About Nine Foot Of Rope, Four Houses On A Stretch Where He Has Stood At A Gate For About Eleven Years And Did Not Stand At One Of Them This Morning, And Nobody In This City Being Sent To Any Of Them

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is about nine foot of rope, a man of about sixty-eight, and about four houses where nobody came.**

---

Nine jobs carried a date against each on the Monday. Nine people came over the counter, the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the twenty-second time in eleven years, and the last of them was let in at half past four.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they are one thousand two hundred and fifty-three days, a hundred and seventy-nine weeks to the day. The card on end in the rail beside that door is one thousand two hundred and fifty-seven days, a hundred and seventy-nine weeks and four days, four days beyond them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and forty-nine days, a hundred and thirty-five weeks and four days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and forty-three, a hundred and forty-nine weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and eighty-six, a hundred and twenty-six weeks and four days.

The separation is six hundred and thirty-three days, ninety weeks and three days, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, walked every day since, not shorter, nothing put back into it.

---

**The first two hours were nine jobs and a bench and the bench was the whole of it.**

A woman of about forty-four brought a gate in off its hinges to have it rehung and it was put back on the same hinge it came off, which cost eleven pounds and took about twenty minutes and is the single most pointless transaction of that Monday and one of about four hundred of them a year.

**A man of about thirty-five brought a length of a bar in a bag and said he did not know what it was for and wanted it cut to length, and Marek asked him what length and the man said he did not know, and Marek cut it to about a foot because it is a bar and that is what a bar is for, and the man said that would do.**

At about half past ten a woman of about twenty-nine asked whether a card of about nine words was still on the door of that shop, and it was not, because it came down in the wet in the spring, and she said right, and did not ask for another, and has not come back.

**At ten past eleven a woman of about twenty-nine asked whether a shop in this city would take a message for somebody, and was told in four seconds that it would not, and that nothing in this shop goes anywhere, and she said she had not expected anything else and she was asking to be able to say that she had.**

**At about twenty to eleven a man of about thirty-seven came in eleven minutes before he had to be somewhere and asked for a thing that takes about four minutes, and Marek said he would do it and the man said he would wait and then did not wait, and did not say he was not waiting, and Marek did the four minutes into a paper bag on the counter and the man was not in the shop when it was finished and came back at about twenty past eleven and paid for it and left and never said anything about it.**

---

**The cart was outside the door from eleven and it was there until half past twelve and nobody said one word to him about it.**

He is about sixty-eight. He has a handcart with a wooden axle and a wheel that has been off and on twice and a rope on the front of it that has been replaced about a year ago. He came in for nine foot of a rope and paid for it and did not ask for a receipt.

**He stands at gates. That is the whole of what he does and he has done it for about eleven years and he takes nothing and he leaves nothing and nobody has ever asked him why and he has never said.**

---

**He told the bench, not Marek, that he had not gone out that morning.**

"**Knee,**" he said. "**It is the knee and it is the rain and it is not one thing it is two.**"

Marek said nothing.

"**I have been round that stretch since I was fifty-six,**" he said, "**and I have never missed one and I am not going to stand up here and tell you which ones, and you have not asked me twice.**"

"**I have not asked you once.**"

"**No,**" he said. "**You have not, and there are about nine people in this city who would have.**"

---

**And he said the other half of it, standing at the end of that bench with the rope in his hand.**

"**About four.**"

"**Right.**"

"**I am not telling you which four and it is not because I do not trust you. It is because the second I have said it out loud to anybody, then that is a thing somebody could go and do something about, and there is nobody in this city who could go and do anything about it, and there is nobody who is going to be sent, and I have been on that street long enough to know what happens to a street that gets a visitor about four things.**"

Nobody sent anybody.

**That is the arrangement in this city and it is not an arrangement anybody drew up. A person who is sent to find somebody is a person who has been handed a route, and a street that has been handed a route about four houses becomes a street with a route about four houses on it, and about eleven years of a man standing at a gate saying nothing goes, and the thing that was protecting the four of them is the thing that a visit would take away.**

---

**A woman of about sixty-three who lives at one of those houses came out of hers at eight in the morning and looked down the length of the street and went back in.**

She is not on this page otherwise and she does not know that anybody in a shop knows anything about it and she has not told anybody and there is nobody for her to tell. **Nobody has been to any of about four doors on that stretch. Nobody has written to anybody about any of them. There is no form, no list, no note and no entry of any kind, and if a person had wanted to make one there would have been nowhere to put it that is not a drawer under a bench.**

---

**Marek did not ask which four. Not once, and he had about eleven minutes and a man of about sixty-eight standing at his own bench.**

The reason is that the man would have said them. **And the reason he would have said them is that nobody has ever asked him and he has been waiting about eleven years for somebody to ask him, and a man of twenty-two who asks a question he already knows the answer to is a man who is starting something, and there is no version of this in which the starting of it is his to do.**

**He could have gone and looked. That is the other half and it is not a small thing.** The stretch is one street and Marek does not know which street and did not ask and has not asked, and a man who did not ask could still have gone and stood at the end of it and looked down it, and there is nothing about doing that which anybody would have stopped, and it would have taken about an hour and forty minutes.

He did not do it, and the reason is four words long and he has never said them to anybody: **it would have made me the one.**

**A man who stands at the end of a street and looks down it after about four houses have not been looked at is a man with a list. There is no form and there is no drawer and there is nobody to hand a list to, and about four houses on that stretch have had nothing at all happen to them for eleven years, and the whole of what was protecting them was that no one had ever looked.**

**And about nine minutes after the man of about sixty-eight had gone, Marek picked up a pen, put it back in the block, picked it up again and put it back in the block, and wrote nothing down.** The only way there is for that man to be helped is for somebody to make a list, and a list is a route. **He has known that for about four months and there is no version of that man's Monday in which a list is the right thing, and the pen went back in the block twice and nobody saw him do it and he did not tell anybody about it and he is not going to.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Monday and not one of them was on a form.** **A chair in one of those front rooms facing a door with nothing at all in front of it.** A gate latch that has been oiled this month and is the only thing on that stretch that has been oiled this month. **A doorstep with a mark on it about the shape of a cart wheel that is nine years old and has not been made by anybody on that street for about nine years.** And a handcart outside a repair shop for an hour and a half with nobody in this city able to say what it was doing there.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Monday, to be a street where about four people have stopped coming back and to be the kind of street that notices.**

**There are about nine of those in this city that anybody has noticed at all and he has stood in none of them and there is no arrangement that would put him in one, and the four houses on that stretch have lost the only thing that had ever visited them, which was a man who took nothing, and nobody is going to be sent, and there is a reason for that which is not sentiment and is printed four hundred yards from here on a card in nine words by somebody else's hand.**

He did not say any of it. At ten past ten, with the shutter down and the cage shaking, he looked down the road in the direction the cart had gone and said **"Right,"** and shut it, and it was the twenty-second time and he has never once counted them out loud.

**At the top of a stair in Saltmarket: sixty-three lines in a green-covered book, seventy-three in the tin beside it, lid on. The place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room is empty eighteen weeks and five days.**

**One other person was in the building from half past six until ten. She is about thirty, she had the chair the door is behind, and in three and a half hours not one word was put to her in any form by anybody in that building.**

**The ring binder on the back shelf of that room holds the page with her name at the head of it as the eighth of eight, and it did not come out, and nobody has said sorry to her for any part of that evening and nobody ever will.**

*About four houses where nobody came, and the reason nobody is going.* **A man of about sixty-eight has stood at a gate on one stretch of road for about eleven years and has taken nothing and left nothing and has never been asked why, and this Monday he did not stand at four of them and told a man at a counter in a shop that it was his knee and the rain and that he was not going to say which four. The reason he gave for not saying is that the moment it is said out loud to anybody it becomes a thing somebody might go and do something about, and that there is nobody in this city who could, and that a street which has been handed a route about four houses is a street that has lost the only thing that was ever going to protect them. Nobody was sent to any of them. There is no form and no list and no note, and if there were one there is nowhere in this city to put it that is not a drawer under a bench. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.
