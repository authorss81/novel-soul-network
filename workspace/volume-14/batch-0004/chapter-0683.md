# Chapter 683 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off A Service Road From About Half Past Six, Ten Minutes Under A Bridge At About Ten Past Four With A Man Of About Sixty-Two Who Has A Table He Does Not Put Out, A Request At Two O'clock To Say What He Thinks Of It And A No In About Four Seconds, And Nothing Whatever Happening Under There All Afternoon

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is ten minutes under a bridge at ten past four, and a man of about sixty-two, and nothing at all happening.**

---

Ten jobs carried a date against each on the Thursday and ten people came over the counter, the last of them let in at half past four. **At ten the shutter came down and shook the cage for the twentieth time in eleven years.**

**The four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they are one thousand two hundred and forty-nine days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks and three days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and fifty-three days, a hundred and seventy-nine weeks to the day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and forty-five days, a hundred and thirty-five weeks to the day. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and thirty-nine, a hundred and forty-eight weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and eighty-two, a hundred and twenty-six weeks to the day.

The separation is six hundred and twenty-nine days, eighty-nine weeks and six days, ended in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, walked every day since, not shorter, nothing put back into it.

---

**At two o'clock a man of about forty-six asked Marek to look up at the bridge and tell him what he thought of it.**

He said no in four seconds.

"**You've not even looked at it.**"

"**No,**" he said. "**And if I looked at it I would be a man who has looked at a bridge from underneath it and told you what I thought, and you would go and tell about nine people what a man at a counter thought of a bridge, and one of those nine would have to go and look at it because a man at a counter thought of it.**"

"**It's been up since before you were born.**"

"**That is not a thing anybody has to hear from me.**"

**The man of about forty-six said that was fair and went and nobody in this city has since asked a man at that counter what he thinks of a bridge, and it is not going to be a thing anybody does.**

---

**The rest of the Thursday was a shop.**

A woman of about forty-seven brought in a length of formica that had come away from the wall behind a bath, and it came away because the seal round it had gone, and Marek spent about two hours on it and could not make a seal that lasts and said so, and charged her for the two hours and not for a seal, and she said that was the first time anybody had told her the truth about one of those.

A man of about twenty-six wanted a door closer fitted and would not say where the door was, and when he was asked again said it was not a door he had anything to do with, and Marek took the money for the visit and did not fit it and wrote nothing down.

**At about half past eleven a woman of about thirty came in with a printed sheet and asked what the line under the box was for, and was told in four seconds by a person entitled to say it that it says what the last approved answer was, and that it is a sentence about a piece of paper and not an instruction, and that if she has been reading it as an instruction then she is the second person in this city this month to do that and neither of them is going to be told about the other.**

**At about a quarter to two a man of about forty-four brought in a chair of somebody else's and asked whether Marek could tell from the underside of it how old it was. Marek said he could tell it was older than him and that that was all. The man said that is not much of an answer, and Marek said no it is not, and charged four pounds, and the man paid it.**

**At about a quarter to two a man of about twenty-seven asked whether a bridge is checked, and Marek said he did not know and would not guess, and the man said everyone says that, and Marek said everyone is right about that as well.**

**And at about half past three a woman of about fifty-eight came in ten minutes before she was due to leave, having forgotten why, and stood at the counter for about nine seconds and then said she was sorry and went, and he said she was not late.**

---

**At ten past four he went under the bridge because the bus he wanted was not due for another eleven minutes and the shelter on the other side has a seat in it and this side has not.**

There was already a man under there. He is about sixty-two and he has a folding table with him in a bag and he did not put the table out.

**Nobody said anything to anybody. Not about the bridge and not about anything else. Two men stood under a thing that carries what it carries and looked at a road and at a wall on the other side of it and neither of them said one word about the thing overhead.**

The bridge did not do anything. **It has a parapet on each side with the paint a different colour in about a foot of it than the paint either side of that foot, and the parapet has been repainted and the bridge has not been. The underside has a seam in it that runs the whole way across and the seam has a line of rust along about nine feet of it and nine feet of it has no rust at all, and there is no way from where two men were standing to tell which nine feet are which.**

**At about half past four a woman of about forty-five came along and stopped about nine feet away and looked up, and stayed about four seconds, and said nothing, and went.** She did not ask either of them. Neither of them said anything to her. **There are about nine people who walk under that bridge every day of the week and four hundred who drive over it and not one in this city has ever been shown a drawing of it, and there is not one in this city who could tell you which nine feet of that seam have rust on them.**

**He looked at all of that for about ten minutes and could not have told anybody whether it was ordinary or not, and the man of about sixty-two could not have either, and neither of them was going to say.**

---

**At about twenty past four a woman of about thirty-four came across and stood about four feet away and said, "**Sorry — is this the four to the market,**" and he said it was not, and she said sorry, and went back.**

That is the whole conversation. **No person that day said one word to him about that bridge, and no person that day said one word about it in his hearing, and he has asked nobody since, and the not asking is the only thing about that afternoon he would put on a piece of paper if anybody ever asked him for a piece of paper.**

---

**At about half past four the bus came and he did not get on it, and he has no reason he could give for that either.**

He had nine minutes of the afternoon left and he spent them under there with a man of about sixty-two and a folding table that stayed in a bag, and then he walked the four hundred yards back to a bench and finished two jobs.

**On the way back he did not go the way he usually goes. He went along the river for about four hundred yards and then up, and there is no reason on this page for that and it is a Thursday and he has done it about four times since the spring.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Thursday and not one of them was on a form.** **A foot of parapet that is a different colour from the rest of it and is not a different material.** Nine feet of a seam with rust along it and nine feet without, and nothing on the ground saying which is which. A folding table in a bag that a man had carried to a bridge and had not put out, and had not gone home with either, because he was still under there at ten past five. **And a bus that came at twenty past four with about nine people on it, none of whom got off there.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday, to be a man who looks at a thing and finds nothing and is still there at ten.**

He has not been that man since the spring and he was not that man on that Thursday either. **What he was instead was a man who looked at a bridge for ten minutes and could not say one word about it to anybody, including the man standing next to him, and then went back to a bench and did two jobs, and there is no arrangement anywhere in this city in which looking at a bridge for ten minutes is worth anything to anybody.**

**There is one thing he did with it and it is about nine seconds long and it is not on anybody's page.** He said to the man of about sixty-two, at about twenty past four, **"They've not put a stop on here in a while,"** and the man said **"No,"** and neither of them said anything else, and the sentence was about the paint on a parapet and both of them knew it was about the paint on a parapet.

**He wanted the man to say something else. He does not know what. There was about eleven minutes of that afternoon in which the man of about sixty-two could have said a thing about that bridge that Marek would have carried home with him, and about nine hundred men of about sixty in this city have something to say about something they stand next to every day, and he sat next to one of them for eleven minutes and got nothing, and the not getting anything was his own doing.**

---

**She is about thirty, and from half past six until ten she had the chair the door is behind in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road to herself.**

**Nobody spoke to her. Nobody has ever apologised to her in any form for that, and there are three and a half hours of it and there is a man of about fifty-one who came in at nine, went past her, and sat down against that north wall.**

**The eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room is hers and the binder did not come out, and a bridge on a wide road said nothing to anybody all afternoon and neither did this.**

**A green-covered book at the top of a stair in Saltmarket: sixty-three lines. The tin beside it: seventy-three, lid on. The place behind the woman's chair: empty eighteen weeks and one day, and unasked after.**

*Eight hundred and eighty-two and one thousand and twenty are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and twenty-six weeks to the day and a hundred and forty-six weeks and two days, and the first is the hold of nine crates in a yard in a town and the second is a run of rust nine feet long under a bridge, and nine feet is not a length of anything that has to be kept.*
