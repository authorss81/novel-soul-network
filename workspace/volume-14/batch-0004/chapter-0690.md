# Chapter 690 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off A Service Road From About Half Past Six, A Room Up A Stair In Saltmarket At About A Quarter Past Four With About Eleven People In It, Fifty-Six Of Which Fifty-One Given First And Once And On No Other Day Of This Fortnight, A Woman Of About Fifty-Nine Who Opens Doors And Who Said A Thing To A Face, A Line That Goes In And Is About A Box And Is About None Of The Four, And A Lid That Did Not Come Off

**The bench ran to two on that Wednesday and the counter to about six, and four rooms off that service road had the heat on in them from about half past six. This one is a number given first, a thing said to a face, a book that opens, and a line about a box.**

---

Thirteen jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and thirteen people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the twenty-seventh time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and sixty-two days, a hundred and eighty weeks and two days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and sixty-six days, a hundred and eighty weeks and six days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and fifty-eight days, a hundred and thirty-six weeks and six days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and fifty-two, a hundred and fifty weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and ninety-five, a hundred and twenty-seven weeks and six days.

The separation is six hundred and forty-two days, ninety-one weeks and five days. It was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, it has been counted every day since, it has not got any shorter, and nothing has gone back into it.

---

**At about a quarter past four a woman of about sixty opened a green-covered book on a table at the top of a stair in Saltmarket and gave the number before anything else, which is what she does.**

"**Fifty-six.**"

"**Of which?**" said the man of about sixty-one with the clipboard.

"**Fifty-one correspond.**" She said it the way a person reads a temperature. "**Up by one. It is always up by one. The five that do not correspond are the same five and I have never shown them to anybody and I am not going to start this afternoon.**"

Nobody asked her about the five.

**That is the only number given out loud in this city today. Six days before this one carried no sitting and announced nothing, and three days after it carry no sitting and will announce nothing, and not one of those nine days says what a sitting does when there was none.**

---

**About eleven people were in that room.**

A woman of about sixty who has held it nineteen years and is paid by nobody. A man of about sixty-one with one column of numbers on a clipboard that has never been totalled. **A woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs, who was there as a person, and who said nothing at all for about two hours.** A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse. A woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her. Six others.

**Behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place and it has been empty twenty weeks to the day and four people in that room had noticed and not one of them said so.**

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room. It did not move. Nobody in that room moved it and nobody in this city has ever said who put it there.**

---

**The caller came up that stair at half past four and she was not on a list and she had not come before.**

She is about fifty-nine and she opens the doors of a hall in a third district and she holds nothing else. **She said her name to a woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her and that woman did not look down, because it was not on the list, and said, "**Then you'll be the one with the sheets,**" and the woman of about fifty-nine said no and sat down.**

Then she waited until the fifth item was done, and stood up, and said to the woman of about sixty:

"**I open those doors twice a day and I have never once been asked whether a box at the bottom of a piece of paper was full before it came to me, and I could not tell you, and I am not going to go and find out.**"

Nobody in that room said anything for about eleven seconds.

"**Say the last part again for me,**" said the woman of about sixty.

"**I am not going to go and find out,**" the woman of about fifty-nine said. "**If I went and found out then I would be a person who knows, and there is nothing a person in my position could do with knowing and there are about eleven people who would find out that I had.**"

---

**And the woman of about sixty asked her one question, and it took about nine seconds.**

"**Do you want it written down.**"

"**No.**"

"**Will you say why not.**"

"**No,**" said the woman of about fifty-nine, "**and that is the last of it.**"

---

**And the woman of about sixty turned the book over and picked up the pen and wrote one line in it and did not read it out.**

The line is this: **The box at the bottom of the sheet was filled in before the sheet was printed.**

**She did not read it back. Nobody in that room asked to hear it read back and nobody in that room knows what any of the four things that failed in the last fortnight has to do with that sentence, and nobody asked, and the reader is not told either.**

It is the shortest line in that book and it is the sixty-fourth, and the book had been on sixty-three lines since a Wednesday in an earlier fortnight of this same stretch of days and about nineteen years before that. **It was on sixty-three lines at a quarter past four and it was on sixty-four at six o'clock and there is no rule anywhere in this city that says which sittings of the twenty years it opened at and which it did not.**

**The tin on the same table is on seventy-three and its lid did not come off, and there is a receipt from a shop in it as a page marker and the receipt is not hers, and the difference between the two of those figures has never been printed as a number by anybody.**

**And the woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her put her pen down at ten to six, and pushed the nine sheets into a neat pile without reading any of them, and carried them out under her arm, and the ninth of the nine was on the top and was not looked at, and nobody in this city knows what is on it.**

---

**The nine sheets were on that table by then and they had been on it since about four o'clock and nobody had picked any of them up.**

Eight of them carry a number in a hand. **There is no name on any of the eight and no building and no street. The ninth carries a single word, and nobody at that table said what the word is, and about four people in this city know that the ninth of the nine carries one word and not one of the four knows the word.**

The man of about thirty-eight with a warehouse said, at about twenty to five, "**Are those going to be added.**"

"**No,**" said the woman of about sixty. "**They were not added last time and they are not going to be added this time and I would like it understood that the reason is not manners. In this room the only figure that gets said out loud is the figure this room says out loud, and a sum of nine numbers from nine rooms is not a figure this room has and I am not going to manufacture one before six o'clock.**"

Nobody added them. Nobody has added them since.

---

**He was not in that room and he did not go up that stair.**

**About three quarters of that Wednesday was nine jobs, and one of the nine was a man of about forty-one who wanted to leave a job half done and pay for half of it, and Marek said a job is a job and it is not half anything until you have been in it twice, and the man said that is fair, and paid for all of it, and got all of it.**

**At about half past nine a woman of about thirty-one came in and asked whether a person who has been asked a question could refuse to say who asked it, and was told in four seconds that they could, and that nobody in this city would find out otherwise, and she said that was what she wanted to hear and did not say who, and Marek did not ask, and there is nothing on this page about it.**

**At about half past three a man of about thirty-eight came in and said he had nine sheets at home that he did not want, and Marek said he did not want them either, and the man said he will keep them, and Marek said that is the correct decision, and the man went.**

**At ten past twelve a man of about fifty-two came in with a door handle that had come off with the plate still on the wall and asked whether the wall wanted mending too, and was told in four seconds that the wall did not want anything, it just had a hole in it, and the man said right and put four pounds on the counter for the handle and nothing for the hole.**

He has not gone up it since a Wednesday in the last part of this stretch of days, which is nine weeks, and on that Wednesday he had gone at six in the evening when there was nothing at all to go for. **At about half past four, a woman of about fifty-nine came into that shop and asked him whether a door that binds on the latch side can be taken off its hinges and put back on, and he said yes in four seconds, and she said right and went out and up a road he knows, and he did not ask her where she was going and he never found out what she said.**

Thirteen dated jobs, thirteen callers, the last let in at ten past five, and **about ninety minutes of the afternoon were a woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs saying nothing, and a man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse asking about a sum, and four minutes of a man of about twenty-nine asking whether there was anything on this Wednesday that anybody wanted him to look at.**

**And at ten past six a man of about forty-four came into that shop, having come down a stair he did not have to come down, and asked a man of twenty-two whether anybody in this city ever writes down a thing they have said. He was told in four seconds that four people do and none of them is anybody in that shop, and he asked which four, and was told that a man who names four people at a counter is a man who has started something, and the man of about forty-four said that is fair and went home.**

**And he went back up that stair about nine minutes afterwards, and four people saw him do it, and nobody in this city has said a word about it since and nobody is going to, and nobody in that room this afternoon knew he was in the building until about half past six.**

**He was not on a list and nobody asked him to come and he was not thanked, and the ninth chair against that wall did not move for him either, because it has not moved for anybody since the spring and nobody in this city has ever said who put it there.**

**And a green-covered book in that room went from sixty-three lines to sixty-four between about a quarter past four and about six o'clock, and the woman who wrote the line did not read it out, and the man of about forty-four who came up that stair at ten past six was shown neither the line nor the count, and did not ask for either.**

---

**Four things arrived with that Wednesday and not one of them could be written down anywhere.** **A receipt in a tin as a page marker that had been on that table at the last sitting and had not been on it at the one before, which is a thing about a person tidying a table.** A pen put down on a book cover and picked up again without the cap ever being off. **Eleven people in a room at the top of a stair, and four of them breathing in at the same moment when a sentence was said, and going on breathing.** And a chair with its back to a wall that has been in the same place since a Wednesday in the spring and that nobody in this city has ever been named as the mover of.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday, to be in a room when a book opens and to be the reason, and to be told afterwards that he was not the reason.**

**He was not the reason and he was not told, and the line that went in is about a box and not about him, and the woman who wrote it did not read it out, and eleven people in that room are now the only people in this city who know that a book opened on this Wednesday and none of them has told anybody and about four of them will not.**

---

**A green-covered book at the top of a stair in Saltmarket stood at sixty-three lines at the start of that Wednesday and was on sixty-four lines at six o'clock. The tin on the same table is on seventy-three and its lid did not come off. Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room a place has been empty twenty weeks to the day.**

**From half past six until ten there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road. She is about thirty and the chair the door is behind is hers, and in three and a half hours not one person in that building put a word to her, and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will.**

**The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room. It did not come out.**
