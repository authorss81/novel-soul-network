# Chapter 681 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off A Service Road From About Half Past Six, A Woman Who Has Put A Plate In Front Of People Coming Off Nights For About Eleven Years, A Building That Ran Out Of One Size Of A Thing It Had On Friday, And A Man Who Was Asked To Drive There And Who Said No In About Nine Seconds

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is a building short of one size of a thing it had on Friday, and the woman who found out, and four people who have stopped coming.**

---

Nine jobs carried a date against each on the Monday and nine people came over the counter, the last of them let in at half past four. **The shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the eighteenth time in eleven years.**

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and forty-six days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks to the day. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and fifty days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks and four days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and forty-two days, a hundred and thirty-four weeks and four days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and thirty-six, a hundred and forty-eight weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and seventy-nine, a hundred and twenty-five weeks and four days.

The separation is six hundred and twenty-six days, eighty-nine weeks and three days. A one-line box in a form shut it about twelve weeks ago. It has been walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**The first three hours were ordinary and they are recorded here because ordinary is most of what this shop is.**

A man of about twenty-three brought a bedroom door in on his own shoulder and put it down in the wrong place, against the wall, where the damp is, and was told to turn it round, and turned it round, and it went out at half past eleven in one piece. **A woman of about fifty-five asked for a hinge that has not been made since about nine years ago and was told so in four seconds, and asked what the modern one looks like, and then did not buy it.**

At about twenty past ten a man of about thirty-three brought in a pump with its back off and asked whether it was the pump or the pipe. **It was neither and it was about four inches of hose that had gone stiff, and he said he had had that exact hose in his hand in the spring and had thrown it out, and there is nobody in this city he can show that to.**

**At about twenty-five to ten a man of about sixty-seven came in with a carrier bag and asked whether Marek had ever been able to mend a zip on a coat with a storm flap over it. He said no, and did not look at the coat, and the man said he had been told no about four times and had come anyway, and Marek said that was fair, and the man said it was, and went.**

**And at about half past ten a woman of about thirty-two put a paper bag on that counter with nine things in it that were all the same thing and asked whether they came in other sizes, and were told that three of the nine had been discontinued in the spring and about four of the nine had not, and she said right and bought two of the four, and there are nine identical items on a shelf behind that counter and they are not on a list.**

**At five a woman of about thirty-six came in about an hour before the shutter and asked whether it was worth mending a kettle that had stopped switching itself off, and Marek said it was about four minutes and about four pounds, and she said she would think about it, and she has not been back, and the kettle is in nine kitchens in this city that have not been thought about.**

**A sheet of paper came back over that counter on that Monday with a mark in a box at the bottom of it, and it was not the first and there were no figures, and Marek put it in the drawer under the bench without saying one word about it and the drawer is shut and there are four sheets in it and nobody has ever looked at the fourth.**

---

**Dessa Kwan came in at eleven with a cardboard box under one arm and she put it on the counter without taking her coat off.**

"**Are you open,**" she said, which she has said every time for about eleven years and which is not a question.

"**Open.**"

"**Have you got about nine foot of that,**" she said, nodding at the coil on the bench that is not a hose and is not a cable and is the fourth thing on that bench that gets asked for twice a month. "**The thick one. Not the thin.**"

He got it and cut nine foot off it and put it in a sheet of newspaper and folded the newspaper over the ends, and she did not offer money and he did not ask for any, and **that is the whole of the transaction and it took about four seconds and it is not on anything.**

"**There is a ward on that road where the buses turn,**" she said. "**It had eleven on Friday. It has none now. One size of a pad. They go through them by the box and there is one box of them a fortnight and there is not a second box of them a fortnight because the second box was never a second box, it was the same eleven.**"

"**Right.**"

"**Four people asked me on Saturday and four people asked me on Sunday and the four of them said the same thing, which is *there were loads on Friday*, and not one of the four of them was lying, and every one of the four of them was standing in a corridor saying it to another person who had also been told it already.**"

He waited. She was not finished and she had not come here to be hurried.

"**The figure is on a list. The list is in a drawer in a different building and the woman who has the drawer is in on a Thursday and there is no arrangement in this city for telling a building that it is nearly out of a thing before it is out of it, and there never has been, and I have looked.**"

---

**And then she said the other thing, and it was the other half of why she was in the shop on a Monday morning.**

"**There is a tray outside that ward and I have put a plate and a hot drink on it for people coming off nights, and I have done it about eleven years and it is three in the morning to seven and it is four days a week.**"

"**Right.**"

"**Four of them have not come for nine nights.**"

He did not say anything. **He had two questions and both of them were about four seconds long and neither of them was the one he asked, and the one he asked was this.**

"**Has anybody told the ward.**"

"**No,**" she said. "**And I am not going to tell them, because I am a person who puts a plate down and not a person who tells a ward what is in a drawer in a different building, and if I start doing that I will be doing it once and then they will expect it and then I will be late one night and they will notice that I was late and not why.**"

**That was the whole of it. She took the box, she said thank you for the hose which was about the hose and not about the rest of it, and she went, and she has not come back to this counter since.**

---

**A man of about thirty-seven came in at two with a job card and asked Marek to drive to that road and tell the stores woman what she had just told him.**

Marek said no in nine seconds.

"**You could take the van.**"

"**No.**"

"**You could take about an hour and a half and you'd not have to do anything else.**"

"**I do not know anybody in that building and neither do you,**" he said, "**and if I go, then that road has a man in it who was sent, and everything that happens on that road after today gets looked at through that. There is nobody in this city who is anybody's postman and there is not going to be one started on a Monday by me.**"

The man of about thirty-seven said that was a stupid reason and it was. **He was not on a list, he was not owed anything, he was not thanked, he was not a villain, and nobody in that shop ever found out what he went and did or did not do.**

**What happened after he left is on this page because it happened and because it is about nine words.**

At three Marek did a thing he has not done in four months. **He went out to the front of the shop, put his hands flat on both sides of the door, and stood there, and looked up the road in the direction that road goes, for about nine seconds, and then went back inside and shut it.** There is no reason on this page for that. **He has done it about four times since the spring, always on a day when somebody had been told the truth at that counter, and there is no arrangement in this city that requires a man to look up a road after he has said no.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Monday and not one of them was on a form.** **A tray on a corridor wall with a plate on it and a lid beside it and the lid not on the plate.** A list in a drawer in a different building, in a hand that is not the hand of the woman who has the drawer, because the woman who has the drawer has had it for about four years and the writing on it is older than that. **A coil on his own bench that has been cut from four times this month and never once charged for.** And a woman who said *there were loads on Friday* four times over two days without once, in nine seconds, saying the other thing that four people are saying.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Monday, to be the person a building asks what it is short of, before it is short.**

There is no such person. **A building in this city that has eleven of a thing on Friday and none on Monday is not the first and will not be the last and there is no post called anything like it and there has never been one, and the whole of the reason is that four hundred competent people in this city can each say out loud what a shortage is and not one of them can be sent.** He could have been one of the four. He is twenty-two, he is at a counter six days a week, he has a van, and on that Monday he was the only person in this city who knew two facts about one building that nobody else knew, and he went to a bench at two o'clock and worked until ten.

He did not say any of it. At ten, with the shutter down and the cage shaking, he said **"Right,"** and that was the second time he had said that word to an empty shop since the spring and it is not a habit yet.

---

**One other person was in that building from half past six until ten, in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road.**

She is about thirty. **The chair the door is behind is hers, and she was in it for three and a half hours, and not one person in that building put a word to her.** Nobody has apologised to her for it in any form and nobody is going to.

**What belongs to her is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room, and the binder did not come out.**

**Up a stair in Saltmarket a green-covered book stood at sixty-three lines at the start of this Monday and is on sixty-three lines now. The tin on the same table is on seventy-three with its lid on. Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room there is a place that has been empty seventeen weeks and five days and nobody has asked her about it.**
