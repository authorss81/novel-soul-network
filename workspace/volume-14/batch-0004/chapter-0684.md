# Chapter 684 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off A Service Road From About Half Past Six, A Yard With Standpipes In It In A Third District, A Man Of About Forty-Four Asking A Woman Of About Thirty-One For Something Ordinary About A Sunday, Nine Seconds, And Nobody In This City Afterwards Being Allowed To Ask Her What She Was Asked For

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is a yard with standpipes in it, an ordinary request about a Sunday, and nine seconds.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Friday and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the twenty-first time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and fifty days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks and four days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and fifty-four days, a hundred and seventy-nine weeks and one day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and forty-six days, a hundred and thirty-five weeks and one day. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and forty, a hundred and forty-eight weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and eighty-three, a hundred and twenty-six weeks and one day.

The separation is six hundred and thirty days, ninety weeks to the day. It was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and it has gone on being counted every day since, in one direction only, with nothing returned to it.

---

**The morning before two was nine jobs and none of them was about a yard with standpipes in it.**

A man of about sixty-one wanted a shelf bracket that has been made in three sizes for about forty years and he wanted the fourth, and Marek got him the third and charged him for the third and the man said he would have paid for the fourth if there was one, and there was not.

**A woman of about thirty-eight asked for a card of about nine words, which she said she had seen in four buildings in three weeks and had never once been asked where it came from, and he gave her one out of a jar without saying anything, and she said thank you and he said nothing back, and there is a jar of about nine of them on that counter and no order behind any of them and nobody in this city has printed which nine buildings**

**At about half past eleven a man of about forty-nine asked whether a part could be got for a machine that had not been made for about eleven years, and was told that it could not, and that when it cannot be got the price of the part becomes the price of the machine, and the man said that is the first time anybody has said that to him, and Marek said it is the first time he has said it to anybody, and both of them knew it was true.**

**At two minutes to two a man of about fifty-eight came in and stood at that counter for about four seconds with nothing, and then said he would come back, and did not, and Marek did not ask him what he had come for, because a man who comes to a counter with nothing and leaves in four seconds has come because of something in this building and not because of the road.**

At one o'clock a boy of about fourteen came in alone and asked for a bicycle chain and did not have money for it, and was told the price twice and was given about nine inches of it out of a length that was already cut. **He said thank you and went and has not been back and nobody in that shop has ever asked him his name and nobody ever will.**

---

**She came in at two for a padlock and a length of chain, and she is about thirty-one, and she has a yard with standpipes in it in a third district and it is not a water office and it has nothing in it but water and a hard standing and a shed.**

**"A four-inch,**" she said. "**And about six foot of the chain, the thin one, not the heavy.**"

He got both. She paid for them and did not ask for a receipt and he did not offer her one, **and that is the whole of that transaction and there is nothing else in this entry about her money.**

---

**At two the man of about forty-four who has a lorry was at the other end of that counter with a list on the counter in front of him and about nine sheets of paper under his arm, and he was not a caller.**

He had been in the shop since about half past one and he had been talking to nobody.

"**Are you the one with the standpipes,**" he said to her, and she said she was.

"**I've got a load of cans coming back empty on Sunday morning and I want them dropped in your yard rather than at the depot, because the depot takes them back to two days a week and Sunday's the only day I've got the driver.**"

"**We could do that,**" she said.

**Nine seconds went. He did not count them and neither did she, and nobody in that shop counted them, and they are nine.**

He said thank you and wrote the address on the back of a list and left it on the counter and went.

**And that is the whole of it. There was no second part and there was nothing else asked and nothing else offered and he did not ask her whether Sunday worked for her and she did not say anything about Sunday.**

---

**Marek did not ask her what she was asked for.**

He had about four seconds in which he could have asked it and the four seconds were there and he used them on wrapping the chain. **He has gone over it since and there is one reason and it is not a good one: he would have been a man at a counter who has just heard a yes and has asked about the yes, and a person who asks about a yes is a person who is making it into a thing, and in four days that would have been a thing she had to carry.**

He did not tell her she did not have to. He did not tell her what she had agreed to. **He did not tell her that a lorry driver with a list and about nine sheets under his arm is not a person who is owed anything by anybody, and he did not tell her that nobody in this city sends anybody anywhere, and he did not tell her that four people who had said no this month had been asked twice.**

**He did not tell her the other thing either, and this is the one that took the longest, and it is about four seconds long.** He did not tell her that the man of about forty-four had already been told no that morning by a man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse, over about eleven crates. **That is a fact about one man in one city on one Friday and it would have told her that her yard is one of about nine places somebody had been told no about this month, and she has a shed and a hard standing and about nine standpipes, and a person who knows that stops running that yard the way it was run.**

**None of that was hers to be told and all of it was his to have said and he said none of it, and she went at ten past two with a chain and a padlock in a bag and did not look back.**

**What she did in four seconds before the door, and only because he was the only person in the shop, was this.** She stopped and said, **"He didn't tell you he knew the driver."**

"**No.**"

"**Then he did not know the driver,**" she said. "**Or he did and he did not think it was worth saying to you, and those are the same thing to a man with a list.**"

**And that was all, and she went, and it is the only thing either of them said in that shop that was not about a padlock and it is not on a form and it is not going anywhere.**

---

**Nobody afterwards.**

Nobody at that counter on that Friday asked her what she had been asked for. Nobody at that yard on the Sunday asked her whether she had agreed to it, because nobody came to that yard on the Sunday and the drop did not happen and there is a form of words for that which is that the driver had a different load. **She has not been asked about it. She has not mentioned it. It is nine days now.**

**He is not a villain, he is not on a list, he is owed nothing and he was not thanked, and he has not been rescued from anything and there is nothing on this page about him after about two o'clock, and there is nothing on this page about her after about ten past two either.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Friday and not one of them was on a form.** **A plug in a yard that has been pulled out and put back in and is warm on the side that should be cold.** A can standing on its end with the cap off beside it and about a foot of water in the bottom of it. A tap with a washer put in new on a tap that is nine years old, so that somebody has fixed one tap and not the other. And a price list on the wall of that yard with one line on it written in pencil and every other line in ink, and the pencil line is not the cheapest.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Friday, to be a person who is told about a thing that went wrong, about nine days after it went wrong, by the person it went wrong to.**

Nobody in this city is going to do that. **A yard in a third district, a chain and a padlock in a bag, a Sunday that did not happen, and about nine days of nobody asking her anything at all, and the last of that silence belongs to her and not to this shop.**

He said none of it. At ten past ten with the shutter down, he said **"Right,"** which is the third time he has said that word to an empty shop since the spring, and he went home.

**And on the way home he went past the yard on the road where the buses turn, and it was shut, and there was a light on in a shed, and he did not stop, and nobody has asked him to and nobody is going to.**

---

**The chair the door is behind belonged to one other person from half past six until ten.** She is about thirty and she was in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road and she was in it for three and a half hours and nothing whatever was put to her.

**Nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody will.**

**On the back shelf of that room is a ring binder and the page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight in it, and the binder stayed shut.**

**At the top of a stair in Saltmarket a green-covered book stands at sixty-three lines and a tin at seventy-three with its lid on, and behind the chair of the woman who holds that room a place has been empty eighteen weeks and two days and nobody has asked her about it.**
