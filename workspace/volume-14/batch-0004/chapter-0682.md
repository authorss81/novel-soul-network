# Chapter 682 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off A Service Road From About Half Past Six, A Woman Of Twenty-Four At A Counter At About Eleven For About Six Minutes With A Card In Her Hand, A Man Of About Thirty-Eight Who Wanted A Different Figure Put On It, Two Whole Numbers Of Weeks That Are Each Other's, And A Coincidence Between Two Integers That Is Not A Finding And Does Not Go On

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is six minutes at a counter with a card in a hand, a figure somebody wanted changed, and two numbers that are each other's.**

---

Twelve jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and twelve people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the nineteenth time in eleven years.

**The four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they are one thousand two hundred and forty-eight days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks and two days. The card on end in the rail beside that door is one thousand two hundred and fifty-two days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks and six days, four days beyond them, and the rail holds two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and forty-four days, a hundred and thirty-four weeks and six days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and thirty-eight, a hundred and forty-eight weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and eighty-one, a hundred and twenty-five weeks and six days.

The separation is six hundred and twenty-eight days, eighty-nine weeks and five days, ended in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, walked every day since, not shorter, nothing put back into it.

---

**Two of the figures on that Wednesday are each other's and it is a Wednesday and both are in the same paragraph.**

The nine hand copies of the front of a page are **eight hundred and fourteen days** old this week, which is a hundred and sixteen weeks and two days, and eight hundred and fourteen is the day a post went up at the end of a corridor. The post at the end of that corridor is **seven hundred and ninety-six days** old, which is a hundred and thirteen weeks and five days, and seven hundred and ninety-six is the day nine people in four buildings began copying the front of a page and eight of them stopped.

**A coincidence between two integers is not a finding and does not go on.** Two numbers in one week that are each other's dates is what the calendar does and not what anybody in this city has done.

---

**The morning before eleven was ordinary and two thirds of it was not about anything.**

A woman of about thirty-eight brought a chair in with a leg coming away at the top joint, and it was put right with a plate and four screws in nine minutes, and she asked whether it would come apart again and was told it would, and said that was what she wanted, and paid and went.

A man of about twenty-two came in about a quarter past nine for a bar of a window handle that he had brought in as a photograph on a phone, which he turned so that Marek had to turn the phone round, and then said **"it's probably not going to be that one,**" and went, and Marek did not charge him and did not say anything and has not seen him since.

**At four a man of about thirty-four came in and asked whether a printed sheet could be got with a bigger box on it, and was told in four seconds that it could, and asked by whom, and was told he would have to ask the man who prints them and this shop does not print them and does not have a card for him.**

**At about half past nine a man of about forty-nine asked whether a form with a box ruled at the bottom of it could be got without the box. He was told in four seconds that it could not, that he was not wrong to want it and that nobody in this city prints that one, and he said that was fine, and he left with the form he had.**

A sheet came over that counter on that Wednesday from a woman of about thirty-four who did not want to give her name, and it had a figure written on it in a hand that was not hers and not anybody's in that shop, and Marek asked her one question about the handwriting and she said she did not know whose it was, and he did not ask her a second question and put it in the drawer under the bench. **The drawer is shut and there are four sheets in it and nobody has ever looked at the fourth.**

---

**Talia Venn came in at eleven with a card in her hand and stood at the counter for about six minutes.**

She is twenty-four. She was in that shop once in the spring at about half past four and this is the second time and neither time was about the other.

The card is about nine words long and it is in her own name and it is the exact limit of every share, and **she set it herself on a machine in a room in a second district, and she brought it to that counter because she wanted to know whether a thing about four words long could be read standing up in a doorway in the rain at a slant, which is a question about printing and not about the four words.**

He read it twice. **He did not ask what a share was. He did not ask who the limit was for. He did not ask whether she wanted him to read it a third time, and he did not offer to hold it while she took something out of her bag, and she had nothing in her bag.**

---

**The man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse had been in the shop since about ten and he had not gone out.**

"**That the number.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**That is a lower number than the one I have.**"

"**It is.**"

"**It is lower by eleven.**"

"**It is lower by eleven.**"

"**That is eleven of my crates on a day I have already counted twice,**" he said. "**And I have told about nine people that number and four of them have written it down.**"

"**Then four of them have a number that is eleven too high.**"

"**Right.**"

"**You can put the other one up,**" she said. "**There is no rule anywhere that says a card can only be printed once and you would have it on your wall in four days and it would be exactly as true.**"

He looked at it on the counter for about nine seconds. "**That would be two numbers,**" he said. "**I am a person with one number. Two numbers is a thing that has gone wrong.**"

"**It is. Your one number has gone wrong. Not the card.**"

---

**And then she said the sentence that was the whole of the six minutes, and she said it to the counter and not to him.**

"**I am not going to soften this and nobody should ask me to, and if somebody does ask me I am going to say no in four seconds and I am not going to give a reason the second time.**"

Nobody asked her again. **Nobody in that shop has ever asked her about a share and it is not going to happen. She was not thanked, she was not given a chair, a form, a list, a stamp or a number, she is not on a list, and she came into that shop on a Wednesday morning of her own accord and left it.**

**Neither of the two of them said one word about the other in the third person while the other was in the room, and neither of them has, and about nine people in this city have noticed that and none of them has said anything to either of them about it.**

---

**And he did not ask her which room she had been in on the Wednesday.**

There were nine rooms and she had been in one of them and he has been in a different building on nine Wednesdays and he knows which nine. **He could have said her name and said one word and been told, and it would have taken about four seconds, and he had six minutes and did not use them, and the reason is that a woman who has just refused to change a number for a man in front of that man does not owe the man at the counter anything at all, and he is the man at the counter.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Wednesday and not one of them was on a form.** **A card with nine words on it set with a ruler under it, and a place on the counter where the card had been lying flat that is a different colour from the rest of the counter.** A hand that writes about nine words and then writes them again underneath with the top line out of alignment by about a millimetre. **A man who looked at a number for about nine seconds and then did not ask for it to be changed, which is a different thing from agreeing with it.** And a door on that shop that has no closer on it and has never had a closer on it.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday, to be in a room where two people who have never met each other are both saying the same true thing, and not to be the one who has to answer either of them.**

That is not a room and there is no way to attend one. **About four hundred competent people in this city can each say what a limit is, and the two who said it out loud on that Wednesday did not say it to each other, and the one person in that shop who could have made them say it to each other did not, and would not have been thanked for it, and was right.**

**He had two other chances that day and did not take either of them, and neither of the two was about the card.**

At four a woman of about thirty-nine came in and said a man of about forty-six had told her that a number on a card in a third district was wrong, and asked Marek whether that was true. **He said he did not know the number and did not know the man, which is the plain truth, and it cost him about eleven pounds of work that a yes would have brought, and she said that was what she expected and went.**

At about half past five a man of about fifty-two came in with a folder of about nine sheets and wanted somebody to look at them, and Marek said he would, and then did not. **Looking at nine sheets is a thing you do once, and after that you are a person who has read nine sheets, and there are about nine things in this city that would stop being one-way the moment a person at a counter had read nine sheets.**

**At one o'clock a woman of about forty-four brought a stool in for a leg that had worked loose and said the leg had worked loose some time in the autumn. He tightened it, and it was about four minutes of work, and he said it would go loose again and that when it did she should not have it tightened again but should have the leg looked at, and she said she would not, and he said right, and did not ask why not, and she has not been back and nobody knows what became of that leg.**

---

**Sixty-three lines, and seventy-three in the tin.** That is where the green-covered book stands this Wednesday, a room at the top of a stair in Saltmarket, and it has stood there since before the morning started and will stand there after the shutter. **Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room a place has been empty eighteen weeks to the day.**

**Nothing whatever was put to her.** One other person was in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road from half past six until ten and she is about thirty and the chair the door is behind is hers, and in three and a half hours not one person in that building said a word to her, and nobody has said sorry to her in any form.

**Hers is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room. It did not come out.**
