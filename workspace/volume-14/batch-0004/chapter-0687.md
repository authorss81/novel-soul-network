# Chapter 687 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off A Service Road From About Half Past Six, A Man Of About Twenty-Six Reading Meters In A First District From Seven Until Eleven And Being Asked About A Meter And Nothing Else, A Woman Of About Forty-Three Moving Nine Machines In A Fourth District And Being Asked About A Machine And Nothing Else, And One Question At A Counter At About Five In The Afternoon

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is two people in two districts being asked about a meter and about a machine and about nothing else.**

---

**Ten dated jobs went out on the Thursday and ten people came over the counter, the last of them let in at half past four.** The shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the twenty-fourth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and fifty-six days, a hundred and seventy-nine weeks and three days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and sixty days, a hundred and eighty weeks to the day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and fifty-two days, a hundred and thirty-six weeks to the day. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and forty-six, a hundred and forty-nine weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and eighty-nine, a hundred and twenty-seven weeks to the day.

The separation is six hundred and thirty-six days, ninety weeks and six days, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, walked every day since, not shorter, nothing put back into it.

---

**He was in a first district from seven until eleven reading meters and every person he spoke to on that round spoke to him about a meter.**

One cover had the number stencilled on the frame and a different number written over it in marker pen and he read the stencilled one because that is the one the cover belongs to, and he said so in four seconds to a woman of about sixty-eight who had been arguing about it for about nine minutes with somebody who had left.

He was asked whether the thing was his fault. He said it was not and did not say anything else. **Nobody in that first district between seven and eleven asked him one word about anything that had not happened in the last hour, and nobody has asked him since and nobody is going to, and he has not told anybody anything and there is nobody to tell.**

**There were nine meters on that round and he got nine readings and one of them was a house that has been empty since about the summer and the reading on that one is the same as the last four times, and he wrote it down anyway, because the reading on a meter for an empty house is the reading on a meter and not a figure about a house.**

At about half past nine a man of about thirty-eight came out of a gate and said the same thing he says every week, which is that the bill has gone up again, and the man of about twenty-six said that it had gone up by a figure and named the figure, and the man of about thirty-eight said that was not the figure he had been given, and the man of about twenty-six said it was the figure on the dial and he could not speak for the dial. **That is the whole of what was said to him on that round by anybody who was not a meter.**

At eleven he went back and was given four more and did them, and he is paid by the hour and by the meter and he finished at four.

---

**She was in a fourth district from about half past nine until about half past four with nine machines, and every person who came into that room spoke to her about a machine.**

A man of about thirty-three brought a chair in for the fourth time this month. **She told him the same thing she tells him, which is that the chair goes against that wall and not in front of the door, and he said he knows, and she showed him where it goes and it took about nine seconds.**

A woman of about twenty-eight asked whether the third machine was taking longer than it was and said that it was not and did not say anything else. **Nobody in that room all day asked her one word about a Wednesday afternoon in a hall in a second district, and she did not bring it up, and there is nobody in this city who is going to, and she is not owed an answer by anybody including the woman who has one.**

**At about half past one she had nine machines running and nothing else to do and she sat down for four minutes, which she does once a fortnight, and the thing she did with the four minutes was fold a sheet. Not paper in the machines — a sheet of her own, out of her own bag, which she has carried since the spring and which she folded in half and in half again and put back.**

**Nobody came in in those four minutes. Nobody has ever asked her about the sheet and she has never mentioned it and it is not in a room in this city that anybody has been in.**

**At four a man of about twenty-seven asked whether the machines could be run overnight, and she said no, and he asked why, and she said because the room is not warm enough at night and because he would be the one standing there, and he said right and went.**

---

**The counter was open from nine and the day on it was not about either of those two people.**

A man of about fifty-eight brought in a gate latch that had dropped on him and would not lift, and it had not dropped on him, and he said so, and Marek said so, and the latch cost two pounds and eleven minutes.

**A woman of about thirty-one asked whether the shop would fit a washer on a tap that had dripped for about four years, and Marek said that was about eleven minutes of work and about four pounds, and she asked him to do it and he did it and it dripped afterwards, and he looked at it for about four seconds and said nothing and charged her for eleven minutes.**

**At four a woman of about forty-six came in and asked whether a person who says yes quickly can take it back afterwards, and was told in four seconds that nothing in this city keeps a record of what a person said quickly, and that it did not keep one of what she said slowly either, and she said that is not what I meant, and Marek said he knew it was not.**

At about half past eleven a man of about twenty-two came in with a printed sheet and asked whether there was a way to find out whether a room had been sent something, and was told in four seconds that there was not, and that he was not the person who would know and neither is anybody in this city, and he said that was what he had been told before and he said thank you and left and paid nothing.

**At half past one a woman of about thirty-five brought in a lamp that flickered and was told in four seconds that it is the switch, and that nine switches in this city have that, and she said she had four lamps with it and asked whether he would come and look at the other three, and he said this shop does not go out, and she said that is all right, I only asked, and paid eleven pounds and did not arrange anything else and has not been back.**

---

**Nobody sent anybody to either of them. Nobody wrote. Nobody asked.**

**There is no form in this city on which the question of what happened to either of them could be written down, and if there were one, the person who could fill it in is the person who cannot remember deciding, and the person who could not decide cannot be asked, and four people in this city would write it down in four seconds if there were a line for it, and there is not one and it is not going to be added.**

---

**At five a man of about forty-two came over that counter and asked Marek whether anybody had managed to find out which of two things happened to two people.**

"**This shop doesn't hold that,**" he said, in four seconds.

"**Right.**"

"**I'm not asking you to know. I'm asking you to write my name under it.**"

"**There's no line,**" he said. "**And if there was one you'd be the second name and it wouldn't be the first one that mattered.**"

The man of about forty-two said that was fair and went. **He is not on a list and he is not owed anything and nobody thanked him and nobody has heard of him since and he did not write his name under anything.**

**And then he said one more thing at that counter, standing in the doorway, and it is on this page because it happened and because it is four seconds.**

"**My mate says a fella at a water office got shown a form with his name on it.**"

"**Did he.**"

"**That's what he says.**"

"**Then that's what he says,**" Marek said. "**I don't know anything about it and there is nobody in this city who does.**"

**The man of about forty-two said that was worth the walk and went, and he has not been back, and his mate has not been back either, and there is no form in this city that either of those two men could put that on.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Thursday and not one of them was on a form.** **A meter cover with a number stencilled on it and a different number written over it in marker, in a street where every other cover has one number on it.** A chair carried into a room four times in a month and put in the same place four times. **Nine machines in a room in a fourth district with the door propped open on a broom handle, because nine machines running behind a shut door is nine degrees warmer by the door and four pounds of hot water a day.** And a counter with a question asked at it and refused in four seconds.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday, to be able to leave a thing alone and have the whole day turn out not to have been about it.**

**That is what happened and it cost him about eleven minutes at one o'clock in which he read the same four lines of a newspaper twice without taking anything in, and it is the first time in four months that a day has not been about the thing that day had to do with, and he did not enjoy it and he did not write anything down about it and nobody will ever know it happened except this page.**

He said none of it. The shutter came down at ten and shook the cage and he went home.

**He did not go to a first district and he did not go to a fourth district and he did not stand at the end of a street anywhere in this city on that Thursday, and there was nothing in the day that would have sent him, and he has checked, and checking is the part he did not do out loud.**

**From half past six until ten there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road, and she is about thirty, and nothing whatever was put to her.**

**Three and a half hours of it. Nobody apologised to her in any form and nobody has ever been going to.**

**The page that is hers is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and the binder did not come out, and there is nobody in this city who would open it in her presence or out of it.**

**And the green-covered book stands at sixty-three lines and the tin at seventy-three with its lid on, and behind the chair of the woman who holds that room a place has been empty nineteen weeks and one day.**

---

**At about twenty to seven a man of about thirty-four came into that shop and said he had heard there were two people in this city who could not say what they had done. Marek said that he had heard that too, and that he did not know whether it was two, and that if it was two then they were not his, and the man said that is the first time anybody has said they are not his, and Marek said it is the first time he has said it about anything.**
