# Chapter 671 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Three Offices In Three Districts That Have All Been Sent The Same Paperwork And Cannot See One Another, A Man Of About Twenty-Six Who Came To Ask Whether A Mark Can Be Lifted Out Of A Box And Was Told In Four Seconds That Nobody In This City Can Do It, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Would Not Say Who Else Has The Same One

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a form with a box ruled at the bottom of it that three offices in three districts have all been sent. This one is a man of about twenty-six who wants a mark lifted out of it, and a woman who will not say who else has the same one.**

---

Ten jobs carried a date against each on the Monday and ten people came over the counter, the last of them let in at half past four, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the ninth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and eighteen days, a hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and twenty-two days, a hundred and seventy-four weeks and four days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and fourteen days, a hundred and thirty weeks and four days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and eight, a hundred and forty-four weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and fifty-one, a hundred and twenty-one weeks and four days.

The separation stands at five hundred and ninety-eight days, eighty-five weeks and three days. It was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago. It has been walked every day since, it is not shorter, and nothing has gone back into it.

---

**The first one through the door at about nine was a man of about twenty-six in a jacket with a badge on it that he did not turn the other way up, and he had a sheet of paper in his hand and had been holding it the same way since before he came in.**

"**Is it you put a mark in this.**"

He put it on the counter face down, which nobody does.

"**Nobody has been in here except me and my lad and he was out,**" said Marek. "**I haven't been in this city since the last time I was, and I know that is a stupid thing to say to a man at a counter on a Monday, but I have never seen that sheet.**"

"**That is not what I asked.**"

"**No,**" Marek said. "**It is what you asked, and I have given you the true answer, and it is not the answer you were hoping for, and I am not going to give you a different one because you have crossed a road to ask for it. What I can do is look at the paper. Do you want me to look at the paper.**"

---

**He looked at the paper for about two minutes and did not touch it more than twice.**

There is a printed paragraph about four steps in the middle of it. At the bottom there is a box ruled the width of the paragraph. **Inside that box there is a mark, and the mark is a small line with a hook on it, and it is in ink, and it is not a cross, and there is a printed line under the box that says what the last approved answer was.**

"**That's not mine,**" said the man of about twenty-six.

"**Say the next bit.**"

"**I have never held that pen in my life. I have not been in that building since the Thursday. Somebody else has put that in there and I want it out, and I want it out today, and I want it out on paper.**"

Marek turned it round so it was the right way up and looked at the mark from about nine inches away.

"**I can tell you four things and only two of them are mine to tell.**" He put a finger a quarter of an inch above the box and did not touch it. "**One: I can see it is in ink and not in pencil, and I can see the ink is the same ink as the paragraph above it, which does not tell us who held the pen because they print them in one run and they come out of the same tin. Two: the mark does not cross the box, it sits in the top left, and the hook goes the wrong way for a cross, so whatever your office has been sending back is not marking with a cross. Those are mine.**"

"**And the other two.**"

"**The other two are not.**"

---

**He said the next bit in about four seconds and it was the part he had come for.**

"**I cannot tell you it is not yours,**" said Marek, **"and I am not going to say a sentence that would let you walk out of here believing I had, because I have not got one. What I can tell you is that nobody in this city can tell you that, including the man who gave it to you, and he will tell you the same thing I have just told you and he will do it in about four seconds and he will be right.**"

The man of about twenty-six stood at that counter for a while with his hands flat on it.

"**So it stands.**"

"**It stands on the paper,**" said Marek. "**On the paper it stands. That is all I am claiming and it is the whole of what is true, and I know it is not what you came for and I know what you are going to have to do with it on Monday morning, and I am not going to pretend I do not know that, because you are standing at my counter and I would rather be disliked and right than liked and two weeks later wrong.**"

---

**A woman of about twenty-nine came in at about half past two and had a list face up in front of her, and she asked for a pen and did not sit down, and she was the fourth Wednesday running she had been in this city.**

"**I'll borrow your tape measure.**"

She measured nothing with it. She held it against the bottom of a page and looked at the width and gave it back.

"**There is a second column on three kinds of form,**" she said, "**and on Monday I typed four of them, and on Friday I typed nine, and the four went to a room in a first district and the nine went to three rooms, and I have not asked the three rooms a single question and I am not going to.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because if I ask them then the word on the form is a thing a meeting decided,**" she said, "**and at the moment it is a thing somebody did on a Friday and I cannot say who and neither can anybody else, and I would rather have a thing I cannot explain than a thing that four people agreed about. I have been in offices for nineteen years and I know exactly what happens the second minute a thing gets agreed.**"

---

**And then she said the thing that is the whole of this day, and she said it standing up, with the tape measure already back in the drawer.**

"**What I want to tell you is what I have noticed, and I have noticed it and I have not written it down. All three of those rooms get their things emptied on the same round.**"

"**By the same man.**"

"**By the same man, on the same Friday, in one go,**" she said. "**And I do not know that he knows, and I have not asked him, and I am not going to, and if you are going to tell me that three rooms sharing a round is a fact about a round and not a fact about three rooms then I know that, and it is, and it is also the only thing that is the same about the three of them.**"

Nobody at that counter asked her which three. She said she would not say, and she was not asked twice, and she left at ten past three.

---

**There was no van and there was no fifth run. A man of about fifty-eight printed a fourth run in about three weeks and nobody has ordered one since, and a fifth will not happen until somebody orders it with money in it, and about nine hundred people in this city have one and nobody has paid him for a single sheet of the four he has given away.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Monday, and not one of them was on any form.** **A man who put a piece of paper face down on a counter, which nobody does.** A tape measure held against the width of nothing and handed back. A badge worn the wrong way up all day, which meant he had put it on in the dark, and he had not corrected it by two o'clock, so it was not vanity. And three rooms in three districts with one man between them and no one in any of the three knowing it.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Monday, to be the person a mark gets checked against.**

Not the person who put it there. **The person it could be brought to.** He has never in his life been the person anything gets checked against, and there is a counter in this city that eleven people a day bring things to be checked against, and on that Monday the one thing he could have offered a man of about twenty-six was a counter and a pair of eyes and the truth, and the truth did not fit in the question.

He wanted to be a wall to bring the paper to. **He thought about it at four o'clock with the bench behind him and nobody in, and he thought it for about nine seconds, and then he thought about the man of about forty-four who empties nineteen of them on a Friday and has never been asked anything, and he put the idea down where he had picked it up, which is the only place this city puts ideas down.**

---

**And at about ten past ten, with the shutter down, four seconds went on something. He did not ask which three rooms.**

There are three. He could have asked the woman of about twenty-nine in about four seconds, and she had not said no to the question, and she had told him more about the round than she had told anybody, and a person who tells you more than she has told anybody is not a person who has stopped.

He did not ask. **Because the three are a fact about a round, and if he had the names then the three would be a list, and a list is a thing that gets handed to somebody, and the man between them empties nineteen of them on a Friday and nobody has ever handed him anything.** That is the fifth thing he has given up in a fortnight that cost him about four seconds and nothing else, and he has not told anybody about any of the five.

---

**The green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket is on sixty-three lines, and the tin on the same table is on seventy-three with its lid on and a receipt in it used as a page marker that is not hers. Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room there is a place, and it has been empty thirteen weeks and five days, and nobody has asked her about it and she is not going to start. Down at four rooms off that service road, after half past six, one other person was in the chair the door is behind, and she was still in it at about ten. She is about thirty and nothing whatever is put to her. Hers is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a back shelf of that room, and the binder did not come out of it.**

---

*One thousand two hundred and eighteen and seven hundred and eighty-four are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day and a hundred and twelve weeks to the day, and the first is four rooms off a service road and one behind the three and the second is nine hand copies of the front of a page of which eight are unfinished. A mark in a box ruled the width of a paragraph is neither of those two figures and it is on nobody's board of two nails.*

*674. Monday of week 242, at ten, and the twenty-first day of this stretch of days and the first of the third ten. Ten dated jobs and ten names, the last let in at half past four. A man of about twenty-six in a badge worn the wrong way up put a piece of paper face down on the counter at about nine and asked whether the mark in the box ruled at the bottom of it was his, and was told in about four seconds that nobody in this city can tell him that it is not his, and that the man who gave it to him will say the same in four seconds and be right, and that it stands on the paper. A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm borrowed a tape measure she used to measure nothing, said that the second column is on three kinds of form, and then said that three rooms in three districts have all their returns emptied on one round on a Friday, and would not say which three and was not asked twice. Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Monday carried ten dated jobs and ten callers, the last admitted at half past four, and about three hours and a half of the day were a man of about twenty-six at a counter and about forty minutes of the afternoon were a woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her.** Callers: ten. Refusals: three, one a request to be told whether a mark can be lifted out of a box on somebody else's form, which is not a thing anybody in this city can do and cannot be supplied, and one a request for the names of three rooms, which was not granted and not argued for. Work: ten, two declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: fourteen pounds, exact. The heating in that flat has been above its winter setting for a ninth month, and four names on that tenancy are the whole of the difference. Those rooms: one thousand two hundred and eighteen days, a hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, warm. Rail card: one thousand two hundred and twenty-two days, a hundred and seventy-four weeks and four days, rail of two. The board on two nails: twentieth, none, and never begun; twelfth, one thousand one hundred and thirty-eight, a hundred and sixty-two weeks and four days; thirteenth, one thousand and eighty-nine, a hundred and fifty-five weeks and four days; fourteenth, one thousand and fifty-four, a hundred and fifty weeks and four days; fifteenth, one thousand and thirty-three, a hundred and forty-seven weeks and four days; sixteenth, one thousand and eight, a hundred and forty-four weeks to the day; seventeenth, nine hundred and ninety, a hundred and forty-one weeks and three days; eighteenth, nine hundred and thirty-six, a hundred and thirty-three weeks and five days. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall: eight hundred and twenty-four days, a hundred and seventeen weeks and five days. The ask: nine hundred and eight days, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks and five days. The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns: eight hundred and fifty-one days, a hundred and twenty-one weeks and four days. The post at the corridor end: seven hundred and sixty-six days, a hundred and nine weeks and three days. The nine hand copies of the front of a page are seven hundred and eighty-four days old, a hundred and twelve weeks to the day, eight of them unfinished, the first row nobody has disagreed about still not found, and not one of the nine has been laid against another on any day of this stretch. The separation: five hundred and ninety-eight days, eighty-five weeks and three days, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and subtracted every day since, no shorter, nothing put back into it.

*Three rooms and one round, and a mark that stands on the paper.* **Three rooms in three districts were sent the same form and their returns are emptied on one round on one Friday by one man who has had the round for about nine years and who has never been told that the three of them share it and who was not told it on this Monday. Each of the three has a box ruled at the bottom with a printed line under it saying what the last approved answer was, and a mark in it, in ink, in a hand nobody in this city can place. A man of about twenty-six who works at a water office brought one of the three over a counter in a badge worn the wrong way up and asked whether the mark was his, and was told, in about four seconds and as the plain truth, that nobody can tell him it is not his and that the man who gave it to him will say the same and be right, and that it stands on the paper, which is the whole of what anybody can say about it and is not what anybody wanted. A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm has a heading on a strip over the second column of three kinds of form and has asked two people in a corridor and not the people above, and she said out loud on this Monday that three rooms in three districts have all their returns emptied on one round on one Friday, and that this is a fact about a round and not a fact about three rooms, and that she would not say which three and was not asked twice. The register of correct acts with no consequence stands at two and did not move on this Monday: a woman noticing that three rooms share a round and not doing anything about it is a woman finding out a thing, and the two acts already on that register were not touched by an inch of this day, and a person who declines to say which three rooms is not a person who has done a correct thing and got nothing for it. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. There is a drawer under that bench and it is shut and four sheets are in it and the fourth of those four has never been looked at by anybody. Between half past six and about ten this evening one other person was sitting in one of the four rooms off that service road, in the chair the door is behind. The eighth of eight in a ring binder on a back shelf of that room is hers and the binder did not come out of it.**
