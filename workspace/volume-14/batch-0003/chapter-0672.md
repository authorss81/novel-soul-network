# Chapter 672 — A Sunday, A Shop That Opens At Ten And A Shutter That Comes Down At About Two, A Hall In A Second District That Is Open On This Day And On No Other Day Of The Week, Nine Machines Standing In It Until Two, A Woman Of About Forty-Three Who Washes For Four Buildings And Who Is In There Folding Sheets At About Half Past One, And About Four Seconds Of A Man Not Asking Her Why She Is There

**A Sunday was a shop that opened at ten and a shutter that came down at about two, and a hall in a second district that is open on this day and on no other day of the week. This one is nine machines finishing, a woman folding sheets at about half past one, and a question that was not asked.**

---

Six callers came over the counter on the Sunday, all of them between ten and one, and the shutter came down at two and did not go down the way it goes down on a working day, which is to say it did not bang, and there is no cage on a Sunday to shake.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and twenty-four days, a hundred and seventy-four weeks and six days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and twenty-eight days, a hundred and seventy-five weeks and three days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and twenty days, a hundred and thirty-one weeks and three days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and fourteen, a hundred and forty-four weeks and six days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and fifty-seven, a hundred and twenty-two weeks and three days.

The separation is six hundred and four days, eighty-six weeks and two days, and it does not describe anything at all, and it has been walked every day of this stretch including this one, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**The hall in a second district is let to this city on Sundays and on nothing else, and about nine washing machines stand in it from ten until two, and the woman who owns them puts them there herself.**

She is about forty-three. She washes for four buildings and she has done for eleven years and she is paid by two of the four and not by the other two. **She is not on a list and nobody in this city has asked her for anything this year and she has not asked for anything.**

On the Sunday morning about forty people came through that hall. A man with a duffel bag. Two women who knew each other and stood on opposite sides of the room. A man of about sixty with a shirt on a hanger and a newspaper folded under his arm, who did not put the shirt in a machine and stood by the window for about twenty minutes. **Nobody had to book anything and there was no charge for the machines at all and the reason there was no charge is that they cost her nothing when they stand in there and cost her about nine pounds a week to sit in a room in a fourth district for the other six days.**

---

**He went in at half past one because a machine of his own had been left standing open since about eleven and had been standing open for four hours with the drum full of water.**

He knew the machine. It belonged to a man of about seventy in a first district and it was eleven years old and the door catch had been repaired twice. He shut the door, ran a rinse, and found nothing wrong with it at all, and the water came out of it perfectly clear.

**A woman of about forty-three was at the far end of that hall with a trolley and about four sheets over her arm and she was folding them, one at a time, along the edge of a table, and the folding was not the folding of a person who does it in a hurry.**

The hall was empty. All nine machines had finished. The man with the shirt on a hanger had gone at one. **The two women who knew each other had gone at twenty past twelve and had not spoken to each other on the way out.**

"**You're the third today,**" she said, without looking up.

"**It's the fourth,**" said Marek. "**I counted you when I came in. You were by the window and you weren't washing.**"

"**I don't wash on a Sunday,**" she said. "**I put the machines here.**"

"**Right.**"

---

**And then there was a space of about four seconds, and he could have used all of it, and he did not.**

The question was there and it was not a hard one. It was the ordinary question a person asks another person in an empty hall on a Sunday afternoon, and it was this: why are you still here.

He had it in his mouth. **He had it in his mouth because the machines were finished and the trolley was half empty and there was nowhere for her to be until four, and because it is the sort of thing two people say to each other in a room with nothing in it, and because he has spent thirteen volumes not saying the sort of thing two people say to each other, and this is one of those and it is nearly nothing.**

**He did not say it.**

"**That one of yours, then,**" he said instead, and nodded at the machine with the door he had shut. "**Door catch's fine. It'll go another two years.**"

"**It's not mine,**" she said. "**They're all the same age and none of them are mine.**"

"**Then whoever it is, the catch is fine.**"

"**Everybody says that and nobody ever tells me whose it was,**" she said, **"and I have stopped asking, and that is not a complaint, it is just a thing I have stopped doing.**"

---

**The man of about sixty with the shirt on a hanger came back in at about five to one and put the shirt in the machine he had not used, which is the only reason she knew it was his, and stood by the window again until the cycle finished, and then took the shirt out and folded it himself, badly, and went.** She watched him do that and said nothing to him and Marek said nothing to him either, and afterwards she said, without any weight in it, that he does that most weeks and has for about two years, and that she has never once asked him his name, and that if she asked him his name then next Sunday she would have to say it back to him.

**She washed for four buildings and has done for eleven years and she is paid by two of the four.** She has a round and a list of the four on it and about nine people in each and she does them in an order that is not alphabetical and has never been alphabetical. The machines are hers in the sense that she pays for them out of what two buildings give her and has done for eleven years, and she is a fortnight behind on the one that does not pay, and she did not say that and it was not asked.

**He stayed about nine minutes and helped her push the trolley out to the step and then did not stay, and she did not ask him to.**

**Four things went into him on that Sunday, and not one of them was on any form at all. A door catch on a machine that was not faulty. A man of about sixty folding a shirt badly. Nine machines and about forty people in a room that is shut on the other six days. And a woman still folding at half past one with nothing left to fold.**

That is the whole of the Sunday. **Nothing in that shop is dated the way a Monday is dated. There was no eleventh caller at half past four because the shutter had been down since about two. There was no list face and no folder and no badge worn the wrong way up.**

**And there was no form on any table in that hall, and nobody brought one, and nobody mentioned one, and if you had walked in at half past one you would have found nine finished machines and a woman folding sheets and a man shutting a door.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Sunday, to be asked why he was there.**

Not asked what he was doing, and not asked what he would charge, and not asked whether he had come on a bus. **Asked why.** He came to a hall in a second district at half past one on a Sunday because he had thought about a customer standing by a window for twenty minutes with a shirt on a hanger, and he had come anyway, and there was no reason on any form, and he wanted somebody to want to know.

**Nobody wanted to know. The only person who said anything to him in that hall said it about a door catch, and she was right, and she did not ask him a single question, and he has been in this city a long time and knows exactly what that costs a person and he is not going to pretend it did not.**

---

**And at half past two, on a street with the shutter down behind him and four people going past, four seconds went. He did not ask her what the four sheets were for.**

They were folded along the edge and there was no stack and no machine and no form anywhere in the hall. **She was folding four sheets of a laundry and putting them on a trolley to take them back to four buildings, and if a person had asked her what the four sheets were for she would have said four buildings, and he did not ask, and there is a person in this city who can tell you how many buildings that is and he is not going to be the one to hear it from her.**

---

**A green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket stands at sixty-three lines. Beside it a tin is on seventy-three, its lid on, a receipt in it as a page marker that belongs to nobody who has ever claimed it. The place behind the chair the woman who holds that room sits in has stood empty about thirteen weeks and one day now. In the fourth of those four rooms off that service road, from half past six to about ten, one other person sat where the door is; she is about thirty; nobody in that building put a word to her. The eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room is hers and it did not come out.**

---

*Nine hundred and ninety-six and seven hundred and seventy-two are neither of them a whole number of weeks, a hundred and forty-two weeks and two days and a hundred and ten weeks and two days, and the first is a line on a board of two nails and the second is a post at the end of a corridor. A day on which no room in this city gives out a number is a day and not a gap.*

*675. Sunday of week 242, at two, and the twenty-second day of this stretch of days. Six callers between ten and one, and the shutter down at about two and nothing hanging off it. A hall in a second district that is let to this city on Sundays and on no other day of the week, with about nine machines standing in it and about forty people through the door, and none of the forty charged anything because the machines cost nothing where they stand and about nine pounds a week where they sit for the other six days. A man of twenty-two shut the door of an eleven-year-old machine that had been standing open since eleven, found nothing wrong with it, said in about nine seconds that the catch would go another two years, and did not ask her why she was in an empty hall on a Sunday afternoon, and there were about four seconds in which he could have. Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. Nothing in that shop was dated the way a working day is dated.*
