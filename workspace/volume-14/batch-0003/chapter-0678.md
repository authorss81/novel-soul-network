# Chapter 678 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Man Of About Twenty-Six Who Reads Meters Coming In At About Two With A Notice Of About Nine Names On It And Who Does Not Say The Thing He Has Come To Say Until About Half Past Three, A Woman Of About Thirty-Six Who Keeps A Newsagent On A Corner Who Says Out Loud In About Four Seconds That She Knows Which One Of The Three Was MEANT, And Nobody In This City Tells Him Anything

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a man of about twenty-six who reads meters standing at a counter with a notice in his hand for an hour and a half. This one is a man who has told four rooms what he is going to do and has not been told by anybody that it was done to him.**

---

Thirteen jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and thirteen people came over the counter, the last of them let in at five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the fifteenth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and forty-one days, a hundred and seventy-seven weeks and two days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and forty-five days, a hundred and seventy-seven weeks and six days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and thirty-seven days, a hundred and thirty-three weeks and six days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and thirty-one, a hundred and forty-seven weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and seventy-four, a hundred and twenty-four weeks and six days.

The separation is six hundred and twenty-one days, eighty-eight weeks and five days, and it does not describe anything at all, and it is walked every day of this stretch including this one, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**He came in at two with a notice in his hand and he did not give it over, and he stood at that counter until half past three, and he did not sit down in that time.**

He is about twenty-six and he reads meters for a water office in a first district and he has been in that job for about four years. **The notice is a printed sheet with a hole punched in the top corner and about nine names typed down the left side of it, and he has not put a mark in any box, and there is no box on it, and he is holding it the way a person holds a thing they have already decided is not theirs.**

"**Are you waiting for somebody.**"

"**I'm waiting for me,**" he said. "**That's not a joke and I'm not doing it as one. I've been standing here about an hour and a half and I have not been waiting for anybody. I want to be somewhere that isn't a corridor.**"

Nobody offered him a chair. Nobody at that counter thought of one.

---

**At half past three he put the notice on the counter, and it was face up, and he had not turned it the other way up, which was a change.**

"**I want to say a thing and I have said it in four rooms in a fortnight and I have not said it here, and I have walked past this door four times to say it here, and I'm going to say it and I want you to not do anything with it.**"

"**All right.**"

"**There are three of us who did a thing on a Wednesday a fortnight ago and about nine of us in a building who did the same thing, and two of the three of us did not decide to, and one of the three did, and I know which one, and I have not said so, and I am not going to.**"

---

**And he said the next part at about half past three and he said it in about nine seconds and it is the part that is on the record.**

"**And I am going to keep saying it in four rooms until somebody takes a piece of paper off me, and I have got a piece of paper.**"

He put his thumb on the notice.

"**That's got about nine names on it. Mine's the fourth. I have not signed it, I have not countersigned it, I have not sent it to anybody, and I am not going to send it to anybody, and I would like to know whether I am allowed to keep carrying it, and I have not got anybody in this city who can tell me, and I know that. I'm asking anyway.**"

"**Why are you asking me.**"

"**Because you have said no to about nine things in three weeks in four seconds each and you have been right about all nine of them,**" said the man of about twenty-six, **"and that is the only reason I am in this building, and I have walked past it four times.**"

---

**Marek looked at the notice for about nine seconds and did not touch it, and then he said the thing that this city has said four hundred times in three weeks and which is the only sentence available to him.**

"**Nobody can tell you that.**

"**There is nobody in this city entitled to tell you that you may keep carrying a piece of paper, including me, and the reason is not that somebody is stopping you. It's that there is no arrangement in this city about carrying paper, and a person who invents one on a Wednesday in about nine seconds at a counter is a person who will have made it by Friday.**"

"**So I'll carry it out of habit.**"

"**You'll carry it because you decided to on a Wednesday,**" Marek said, **"which is a completely different reason and it is the only one that is yours, and I am not going to take it off you and I am not going to tell you to put it down, and I would like it noted that I also did not ask where you got it or who else has signed it, and that I wanted to, twice, and did not.**"

---

**Nobody in this city ever put it to him that it was done to him, and he had asked.**

Not at that counter. **Not by the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a newsagent on a corner, who knows which of the three meant it and is not going to say. Not by a woman of about twenty-four who carried nine pieces of paper down a stair once. Not by the man of about forty-four who empties nineteen of them on a Friday and has never once been asked a question by anybody and would not know what to do with the sentence if it were said to him.**

**He did the thing in nine seconds for a reason he can defend in front of a room, and he has said so out loud in four rooms, and nobody has argued him out of it. He is on no list. He is owed nothing. He is not going to be told, and he is not going to be asked to be told, and he will carry a piece of paper with about nine names on it for as long as he carries it and there is nobody in this city who is going to take it.**

---

**At four the woman of about thirty-six came through that shop on the way to somewhere and saw the notice on the counter and stopped dead, and she did not touch it, and she said one sentence about it in about four seconds.**

"**That's the water office one.**"

"**You know it.**"

"**I know the room it came out of,**" she said, **"and I know which of the three of you meant it, and I'm not going to tell you which, and if I told you now you'd have to carry that as well and there is only room on that paper for one thing.**"

Then she went, and she did not say good afternoon, and the man of about twenty-six looked at the notice and then at the door and did not say anything either.

---

**He was still there at about five. He put the notice back in his inside pocket, which is not the same as putting it down, and he said one more thing on his way out.**

"**I'm not going to be one of the people who says in four years that nobody told me.**"

"**That is not a promise anybody can check,**" said Marek.

"**It's not a promise, it's a reason,**" he said, **"and I have got four weeks before the paper goes up in a building and somebody in a room asks me whether I agreed, and I am not going to be the one who says there was a man at a counter and he would not tell me. I am going to say I was the fourth of nine and I did it in nine seconds. That's all I've got and it's mine.**"

---

**He carried four things off that Wednesday, and not one of them was written down anywhere.** **A notice carried face up, which is a different thing from a sheet carried face down, and a man who has changed it between one street and another.** An hour and a half of a man standing in a shop to be somewhere that is not a corridor, and about four people in that shop who watched him do it and about nine in this city who will hear about it. A woman who knew which of three meant it and gave a reason for not saying which that had a number in it, about room. And a thumb held on a hole punched in the top corner of a piece of paper for about nine seconds by a person who then did not take it away.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday, to be the person who took the paper off him.**

Not the person who approved it. **The one who said: hand it over, I will carry it, it will not be yours any more and you will not have to hold it up in four rooms in a fortnight.** He has wanted to hold about nine things since the spring and he has held none of them and he has told four people in three weeks that he is not going to start.

**He wanted it for about an hour and ten minutes while a man of about twenty-six stood at his counter, and the reason he did not do it is printed in about four words on this page and they are that a person who holds nine numbers becomes the place the numbers come back to, and a courier of about twenty-two said that to him at that same counter on a Friday about a fortnight ago and was right.**

---

**Ten past ten on the Wednesday, shutter down, and four seconds went on something. He did not ask the woman of about thirty-six to come back in.**

She had been in the door for about nine seconds. She had said one true thing and one thing about a reason and both of them were hers and neither of them was free, and she was gone.

**He did not ask her. He did not ask the man of about twenty-six whether he would come back on the Wednesday. He did not ask for the name of the room the notice came out of, which is a room he could name in about four seconds, because a woman of about twenty-nine typed nine of them on a Friday and four of them on a Monday in a building with a canopy over its door, and the water office is a different building and he does not know that and will not be told.**

That is the tenth thing he has given up in about a fortnight that cost him about four seconds and nothing else, and it is the one he is most likely to be asked about.

---

**A green cover, a stair in Saltmarket, sixty-three lines, and a tin on seventy-three with a receipt in it as a page marker that does not belong to her. The place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room has been empty seventeen weeks to the day. After half past six, in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road, one other person was in the chair the door is behind until about ten. She is about thirty and not one person in that building has said a word to her on this day or on any other. The eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room is hers and the binder did not come out.**
