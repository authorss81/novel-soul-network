# Chapter 674 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Room Up A Stair In Saltmarket At About A Quarter Past Four With About Nine People In It, Fifty-Five Of Which Fifty Given First And Once And On No Other Day, A Caller Who Said A Thing To A Face And A Book That Did Not Open And Nobody In That Room Who Said A Word

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a room up a stair in Saltmarket at a quarter past four with nine people in it. This one is a number given first, a thing said to a face, and a book that does not open.**

---

Thirteen jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and thirteen people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the eleventh time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and thirty-four days, a hundred and seventy-six weeks and two days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and thirty-eight days, a hundred and seventy-six weeks and six days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and thirty days, a hundred and thirty-two weeks and six days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and twenty-four, a hundred and forty-six weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and sixty-seven, a hundred and twenty-three weeks and six days.

The separation is six hundred and fourteen days, eighty-seven weeks and five days. A one-line box in a form shut it about twelve weeks ago. It has been walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**At a quarter past four a woman of about sixty opened a book with a green cover on a table in a room at the top of a stair in Saltmarket and gave the number before anything else, which is what she does.**

"**Fifty-five.**"

"**Of which?**" said the man of about sixty-one with a clipboard.

"**Fifty correspond.**" She said it the way a person reads a temperature. "**Up by one. It is always up by one. The five that do not correspond are the same five and I have never shown them to anybody and I am not going to start this afternoon.**"

Nobody asked her about the five.

**That is the only number given out loud in this city today. Nine days of the last ten carried no sitting and announced nothing, and this entry does not say what a sitting does when there was none.**

---

**About nine people were in that room.**

A woman of about sixty who has held it nineteen years and is paid by nobody. A man of about sixty-one with one column of numbers on a clipboard that has never been totalled. **A woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs, who was there as a person, and who said nothing at all for an hour and three quarters.** A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse. A woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her. Four others.

**And behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place, and it has been empty for thirteen weeks and four days, and about four people in that room had noticed and not one of them said so.**

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room. It did not move. Nobody in that room moved it and nobody in this city has ever said who put it there.**

---

**The caller came up that stair at half past four, and she was not on a list, and she had not come before, and nobody had asked her to come.**

She was about thirty-eight. She said her name to a woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her and the woman of about twenty-nine did not look down, because it was not on the list, and said, **"Then you'll be the one with the sheets,**" and the woman of about thirty-eight said no and sat down.

Then she waited until the seventh item was finished, and stood up, and said to the woman of about sixty:

"**I put a mark in a box on a Wednesday and I cannot tell you whether I decided to.**"

The room did not go quiet. Rooms do not go quiet. **About three of the nine of them stopped writing and the other six carried on.**

"**Say that again for me,**" said the woman of about sixty.

"**There was a box at the bottom of it with something already in it,**" said the woman of about thirty-eight. "**I have a job. I have had it for nine years. I go in, and the answer that was in the box is the answer I have given every time I have gone in. So I put the same one in. That is all I did and it took me about nine seconds and I would do it again. And I have not been able to say since then whether I decided to.**"

"**You are asking me to write down that you did not decide.**"

"**I am telling you a thing,**" she said. "**I am not asking you to write anything at all, I have not come up here for a form, I have come up here because there is one room in this city where a person says a number out loud and I thought you were the person I would have to say it to.**"

---

**And the woman of about sixty put her hand flat on the book and did not open it.**

**About nine seconds went. Nobody in that room said a word.**

A man of about thirty-eight with a warehouse wrote something. A woman of about twenty-nine turned a page of a list and did not read it. The woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs put her hand flat on the table ten minutes before the end on a fortnight ago and put it flat on the table again now, and neither time was anybody watching her.

Then the woman of about sixty said: "**Thank you for telling the room.**"

**That is what she said, and the book did not open, and she did not pick up the pen, and the sixty-third line is still the sixty-third line and it is the shortest thing in that book and it is about a printed sheet.**

Nobody asked her why. Nobody asked her why not. **The man of about sixty-one with the clipboard looked at the book and then at the woman of about thirty-eight and then at the wall, and the order of those three things is on the record and no one has ever been able to do anything with it.**

---

**The tin on the same table is on seventy-three and its lid did not come off, and there is a receipt from a shop in it as a page marker and the receipt is not hers.**

The book was on sixty-three lines at a quarter past four and it was on sixty-three lines at half past five and it was on sixty-three lines when the woman of about thirty-eight went down that stair, and **it was on sixty-three lines at the forty-eighth sitting in the spring before a line ever went in, and it is on sixty-three lines now, and one line in a green cover on a table in this city is the whole of the difference between the two of those.**

**And nobody in that room knows whether it is a habit or whether it is a judgement, and she has not said, and she is not going to.**

---

**He was not in that room.**

**He has not gone up that stair since a Wednesday in the middle of the last volume, which is about seven weeks, and he did not go up it on this Wednesday, and there was nothing in that room he could have done and nobody would have spoken to him and nobody asked him for anything.**

He was at a counter in Lattice Ward from nine until six. **Thirteen dated jobs, thirteen callers, the last of them let in at ten past five, and four minutes of that afternoon were a man of about thirty-eight who wanted to know what a printed line under a box on a piece of paper is for, and was told in four seconds by a person entitled to say it that it says what the last approved answer was, and that it is a sentence about a piece of paper and not an instruction, and that if he has been reading it as an instruction then he is the second person in this city this month to do that and neither of you is going to be told about the other.**

---

**And at six the woman of about thirty-eight came down that stair and past that shop on the wide road, and she did not stop at the counter, and he was not looking up.**

**He could have asked her. She had come up a stair and said a thing out loud to a face and gone down again, and she was within four feet of a counter, and any person in this city would have said something, and he was the one person within four feet of her and he was the one person in this city who would not have.**

**He was not being careful. He was working. There was a pump on the bench with its back off and he was on his knees at ten past six with a screwdriver in his hand, and she went past, and he did not look up, and that is the true reason and it is a bad reason and it is the reason.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that room or on that stair or on that wide road. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.** The man of about thirty-eight with a warehouse was not thanked for asking whether the nine would be added up a fortnight ago, and the woman of about thirty-eight was not thanked for saying out loud that she cannot tell whether she decided.

---

**He saw four things on that Wednesday, and not one of them is the kind of thing that goes on a form.** **A hand laid flat on a closed book, which does not open a book.** A clipboard with one column of numbers on it that has never been totalled and a man who has written on it and not read it back. A page of a list turned over without being read. And a receipt in a tin that had been on that table at the last sitting and had not been on it at the one before, which is a thing about a person tidying a table and not a thing about anything else.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday, to be the person she had come up that stair to.**

**Not to be in the room. To be the counter she comes down to. To be the person she tells, because she is going to go down that stair and out into a wet street and she is going to say this thing out loud to one person and there is exactly one person in this city she can say it to and he is going to be on his knees with a screwdriver and he is going to let her go past, and he is going to be right to let her go past, and he is going to think about it for about nine years.**

**He is the person the numbers come back to. He has been told that by a courier on a Friday. He did not want it on that Wednesday and it is not a thing anybody can put down.**

---

**Ten past ten. Shutter down. Four seconds went on something. He did not go up the stair.**

He went out into the road at ten past ten and stood on the corner for about a minute and a half in the rain and then came back in. **About nine people in this city saw him do it and about four of them told somebody, and a woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs heard about it on the Thursday night, which she has now done twice, once about a fortnight ago and once about this.**

He has now been out in the rain on that corner four times. **There is no note of it anywhere. The fourth is a Saturday, or it is not.**

---

**The green-covered book at the top of a stair in Saltmarket was on sixty-three lines when that Wednesday began and it is on sixty-three lines now, and the tin on the same table is on seventy-three with a receipt in it as a page marker and the receipt is not hers. The place behind the woman's chair has been empty thirteen weeks and four days. In the fourth of those four rooms off that service road one other person sat in the chair the door is behind from half past six until about ten and not one person in that building said a word to her. She is about thirty. Hers is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a back shelf of that room, and it did not come out.**

---

*Nine hundred and fifty-two and eight hundred and forty are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and thirty-six weeks to the day and a hundred and twenty weeks to the day, and the first is a line on a board of two nails and the second is a man of about fifty-one against a north wall, and a hand laid flat on a closed book is neither of those two figures and it is not a length of time at all.*

The tin is on seventy-three and its lid did not come off.
