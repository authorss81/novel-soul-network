# Chapter 675 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Came In Off A Night On A Bus And Put A Thing On A Counter And Did Not Sit Down, A Woman Of About Thirty-One Four Days Of Bus Travel There And Back Who Had Said No Once Already And Was Asked A Second Time With The Box Already Filled In, And About Nine Seconds

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a man who came off a bus at about ten in the morning and put a thing on a counter and did not sit down. This one is his account of about nine seconds in a supply room in a town two days away, and the second no of a fortnight.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Thursday and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at half past four, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the twelfth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and thirty-five days, a hundred and seventy-six weeks and three days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and thirty-nine days, a hundred and seventy-seven weeks to the day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and thirty-one days, a hundred and thirty-three weeks to the day. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and twenty-five, a hundred and forty-six weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and sixty-eight, a hundred and twenty-four weeks to the day.

The separation is six hundred and fifteen days, eighty-seven weeks and six days, and it is not a length of time, and it is walked every day of this stretch including this one, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**The man of about thirty-four came in at about ten in the morning and put a bag on the counter and did not sit down, and there was a timetable folded in the pocket of his coat with the fold newer than the coat.**

He is the man who pushes beds for a firm that hires them by the week. He has pushed them for about nine years. He is a day and a night of bus travel from this city and he is paid at about four in the morning. **There is a man of about thirty-four in this city who works in a kitchen and who said, to a face, in the first week of this stretch of days, that the third printed step is wrong for a brace, because a brace holds a person's leg and a pump does not, and a person holding a brace is a person holding a door and not a door, and a brace is counted three and not four. He is the same age as this man. The two of them have never met, there is no reason in this city for either of them to be told about the other, and this man has not heard the kitchen man speak and has not been told what he said, and Marek has thought about it for about four seconds and has not written it down anywhere.**

He was given the bag at about six the previous morning by a man who was in a hurry.

"**That's from the other one,**" he said. "**The one you didn't ask about. The one in the fourth town.**"

---

**And he said the next part in about nine seconds and he had clearly been holding it since six in the morning on a bus.**

"**I did not do it.**"

"**All right.**"

"**No, listen to the shape of what I am telling you,**" he said. "**I am not telling you I did not fill in a box. I filled in a box. There was a piece of paper and there was a box at the bottom of it and there was a mark already in the box and I put a mark in the box as well, because I have been to that supply room about nine times since the spring and I know what the answer is and the answer in the box was the answer.**"

"**That is the same as a man of about twenty-six said on Wednesday.**"

"**It is the same as a man of about twenty-six said on Wednesday and it is not the same thing at all,**" said the man of about thirty-four, **"and I have been on a bus since six this morning trying to work out why, and I cannot, and that is why I am standing at your counter instead of in a queue somewhere.**"

---

**He laid it out the way a man who has been awake since three lays out something he has been turning over.**

"**One: I did not decide. Two: I cannot prove I did not decide. Three: the box now has a mark in it and the mark is the answer I gave anyway, so nothing has changed except that there is a mark on a piece of paper that says a thing I said in a room with nine people in it. Four:**" he stopped, and looked at the bag, and then at the counter, **"and I have been on a bus for a day and a night to say four sentences about a box, and I do not know who I am saying them to, and neither do you.**"

"**That is fair.**"

"**I know it is fair. I have known it was fair since about ten past four on the Wednesday eight days ago, when a woman in a supply room told me a thing and I wrote it down, and I got on the bus anyway.**"

---

**And then he said the sentence that is the whole of the reason he came, and he said it looking at the wall and not at the counter.**

"**I want somebody to tell me that I did not do it.**"

Marek put the bag where it was and did not open it.

"**I am not going to tell you that,**" he said, **"because I have got nothing behind the telling and you would go away with it and then somebody would find out in about four months that I gave you it, and you would have had a day and a night of bus travel and a man of twenty-two at a counter and you would have had nothing at all, which is worse than what you came here with. What I can tell you is what is true and it is not much. You filled in a box. A mark was in it. You put a mark in it. There is no person in this city, and there never has been, who can stand in front of a piece of paper and say whose hand that was.**"

The man of about thirty-four stood there for a while.

"**That is not what I came for.**"

"**No,**" said Marek. "**It is what there is.**"

---

**And that is all the two of them had, and he took the bag back at about twenty past ten and went to the corner and got a bus, and nobody in that shop offered him anything and nobody sent anybody with him and nobody in that shop has any way of telling anybody anything, and there has not been one in this city in longer than anybody there can remember.**

**He went back on the Saturday. It is a day and a night each way and he paid his own fare both times and he has never been reimbursed for anything by anybody, and the firm he pushes beds for does not know he has been and would not be told.**

---

**The woman of about thirty-one is in a town two days of bus travel from here, and she is in a supply room of about nine people, and on Wednesday afternoon, on the same afternoon as three other things in three other districts, a man of about thirty-four she had asked to go and was glad to see asked her a second time and put a piece of paper on a table in front of her with something already in the box at the bottom of it.**

**She had said no once already, in the spring, to two separate people, about nine pounds, and she had put a sheet in a box in her own supply room with about nine people waiting, and she was not going to be the fourth person to be asked for money and she is not going to be asked for money again.**

**He did not ask her for money on the Wednesday. He put the paper down and said he had to go back on the bus and that he was not going to sit there, and that she could leave it blank.**

"**I did not say leave it blank,**" she is on record as having said. "**I said you can leave it blank. That is a different sentence and you have had it wrong for about a fortnight and I have not corrected you because I have not been in this city to correct you in.**"

"**I know that. I wrote it down wrong.**"

"**You wrote it down wrong in a notebook and then went and asked a woman of about twenty-nine about it,**" she said. "**You did not write it down wrong to me.**"

---

**And here is her nine seconds, and she did not raise her voice, and there were about nine people in that room and all nine of them heard it.**

"**No. Again. And I am going to say the reason once and then I am going back to work, and the reason is not about the money and it is not about the bus.**"

"**I haven't mentioned the money.**"

"**Everybody mentions the money,**" she said. "**A mark in a box is not a decision. That is the reason. It is a line somebody drew and it is on a piece of paper and it is in ink, and if I did not draw it then I cannot prove that I did not, and I am a person who has to be able to prove things about my own supply room, and I cannot prove this one, and a thing I cannot prove is a thing that happened to me.**"

The man of about thirty-four wrote it down. He wrote it down on the Wednesday in a notebook, which is a separate document from the paper on the table and did not go into anything on a round. **Nobody has read that notebook. He has not offered it to anybody. It is a notebook and it is in a coat and it has been read twice by him.**

---

**And that is the second no of a fortnight, and there were nine people in a room in a town two days of bus travel and a man of about thirty-four with a notebook and about nine seconds, and a mark in a box that was in the box before anybody in that room came in.**

**Nothing in this city is going to go and stand in that room and say a word. Nobody has asked her to and nobody is going to, and she is not a person who is owed anything by anybody here, and the reason is on the page as two separate facts and is not explained anywhere: she is four days of bus travel there and back, and this city does not send anybody.**

---

**Four things about that Thursday stayed with him, and not one of them was anything a person could have written down.** A timetable folded into a coat pocket with the fold newer than the coat, which meant it had been opened and closed on a bus more than once. **A bag that was heavier going onto that counter than coming off it, and he put it back where it had been.** A man who said *I want somebody to tell me* and not *I want somebody to change it*, and there is a difference and it cost a day and a night of bus travel. And a wall in a supply room a day and a night of bus travel from here, with nine people in front of it, none of whom will ever be asked anything by anybody in this city.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday, to be in that room when she said it.**

**Not the person who agrees with her, and not the person she says a thing to. The third thing, which is to be one of the nine in that supply room at the moment she says out loud that a tick in a box is not a decision, and to be standing there with it, and to have to go back to a shift on a Monday with it.** He has known that since the Sunday, since a pen went into an apron, and knowing it and being in the room when she said it out loud are two completely different things, and one of them is available to him at a counter in about four seconds and the other one is a day and a night of bus travel away and costs about nine pounds a night in a room you sleep in for four hours.

**He could afford it. He has said to nobody that he could afford it and the figure is on no board of two nails.**

---

**The shutter was down at ten and by ten past ten four seconds had gone on something. He did not put the bus timetable back in the coat pocket straight away.**

It lay on the counter for about four seconds after the man had gone. **A day and a night there and a day and a night back, and the round trip is four days, and a man of about twenty-six and this man are two of the three who put a mark in a box in two districts of this city on one afternoon, and about four hundred people in this city are not standing in any of nine rooms and are not going to be sent to anybody, and there is a woman standing in a supply room right now saying a thing out loud to nine people who will not carry it here.**

He did not write the address down. **That is the seventh thing he has given up in a fortnight that cost him about four seconds and nothing else, and the timetable is still on the counter and the man has gone and it will be in a drawer by Friday.**

---

**Sixty-three lines in a green cover, and a tin on seventy-three with its lid on and a receipt in it as a page marker that is not hers, and the place behind the woman's chair empty sixteen weeks and one day. One of the four rooms off that service road, from half past six, had one other person in the chair the door is behind, and she was still in it at about ten. She is about thirty and nothing whatever was put to her. What belongs to her is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room, and it did not come out of the binder, and nobody has ever opened it in her presence or out of it.**
