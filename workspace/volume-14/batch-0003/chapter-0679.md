# Chapter 679 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Eleven Names Read Out In About Two Minutes In A Room Off That Service Road, A Woman Of Forty-Five Who Can Take A Man Out Of A Room And Who Took One Out Of A Plan Once In The Spring And Who Was Asked On A Thursday And Who Said No In About Nine Seconds And Who Gave One Reason And Who Did Not Soften, And The Reason Is That There Is Nothing On Him To Remove

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a room off that service road at about seven in the evening with eleven names read out of a piece of paper. This one is a man asking to be taken off a list, and the answer takes about nine seconds and it is no.**

---

The Thursday carried eleven dated jobs, and the same number of people came over the counter, the last of them let in at half past four; the shutter went down at ten, as it has gone down at ten about six hundred times, and it shook the cage for the sixteenth time in the whole of this.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and forty-two days, a hundred and seventy-seven weeks and three days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and forty-six days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks to the day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and thirty-eight days, a hundred and thirty-four weeks to the day. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and thirty-two, a hundred and forty-seven weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and seventy-five, a hundred and twenty-five weeks to the day.

The separation is six hundred and twenty-two days, eighty-eight weeks and six days, and it is walked every day of this stretch, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**There were four people in the first of those four rooms at about seven and it is not a meeting, and there is no chair at the head of it, and there is no minute, and nobody has ever written down that these four people meet.**

A man of about forty-one who repairs boilers and is deaf in one ear. A woman of about thirty-six who types for a firm. A man of about twenty-eight who works at a water office and is not the man of about twenty-six. A woman of about sixty-three who is a caretaker of three buildings and has the keys.

**And a woman of forty-five who takes a man out of a room in about four seconds, and who took a man out of a plan once in the spring on a piece of paper in a second floor, and who was not asked to then and was asked to now. She is on no list and has not been thanked for either and is not going to be.**

She is Sera Quill and Marek has known her name for about two years and has said it out loud in this city about four times and it is not on any of them.

---

**He asked her at nine o'clock and it was the shortest conversation of his life and it is the only one of the four in that room that took under a minute.**

They were in a corridor and not in the room, because the room was being used by a boiler. He had been standing in the corridor for about two minutes waiting to be asked to come in and had decided, while waiting, that he would ask, and then she came out of the room and he said it before she was past him.

"**Take me out of the room.**"

"**Out of this one.**"

"**This room. Whatever the eleven names in it are for, and whoever read them out, and me out of it.**"

She did not stop walking and she did not slow down, and she said the rest of it without turning round, in about nine seconds, and she has not softened on it since and is not going to.

"**No.**"

"**I've been on the thing for four months.**"

"**You have. And that is the whole of the reason I'm not going to, and I am going to say it once and I am not going to be asked it again, and if you ask me again I will say the same words.**"

---

**And here is the reason, and it is one reason, and it is nine words, and nobody in this city has been able to improve on it.**

"**There is nothing on you to remove.**"

"**That's not an answer.**"

"**It is the only one there is and I have had about nine years to find a better one,**" said Sera Quill. "**I take men out of rooms when there is a thing on them. A thing they've said, a thing they've done, a thing they've agreed to be part of, a name of theirs that somebody else needs. You have not said a thing. You have not done a thing. You are in this room because a woman of about fifty-nine asked nine rooms one question in nine buildings in one afternoon and went herself, and told none of them about the other eight, and you carried a tenth copy of it as far as the bottom of a stair and then said no, and that was in the spring, and every one of those nine rooms can say what you said to them and not one of them can say what it was for, and that is the whole of what is on you, and it is about four seconds and a stair.**"

"**Then what am I on it for.**"

"**You are on it because you were the first person in this city who said yes, and about nine people have been saying yes to you ever since without either of you noticing, and that is what being on it means, and it is not a fault and it is not a debt and it cannot be taken off you because there is nothing to take.**"

---

**And that is the whole of it, and it is about four minutes long including the silence, and no one else in that corridor heard any of it, and nobody in that room was told.**

He stood there.

"**That's a worse answer than yes would have been.**"

"**It is a much better answer, and you will work out why in about four months,**" she said, and went back into the room, and left the door open behind her, which is a thing she does not do and did once.

---

**Nobody has ever asked her to do that again and nobody is going to. She was not thanked. She was not asked whether she was sure. She was not told what he said next, because he said nothing next, and there was about nine seconds of a corridor with nothing in it at the end of it, and about six people in this city will hear about it and none of them from her.**

**This is the first time in this city that anybody has asked her instead of her having done it, and there is no arrangement anywhere that requires her to be asked, and the man who asked is twenty-two and has asked her for nothing in two years and is not on a list and is owed nothing and is not going to be given anything for having asked.**

---

**And here is the other thing about the four people in that room, which is that three of them have been doing it since the spring and the fourth joined in about six weeks ago, and none of them has ever asked the other three what the names are for, and all four of them have a piece of paper at home with about eleven names typed down the left of it, and not one of the four has ever said to another that they have got a piece of paper at home.**

The man of about forty-one with the boiler put it that way and he was not complaining. **"It is a Wednesday, it is two minutes, and then somebody puts the kettle on,"** he said, **"and if I asked the woman of about thirty-six what the eleven names were for she would have to answer me in the corridor, and she would not want to, and I would find out something about her that is not the point of a Wednesday. So we do not, and it works, and about four of us have been doing it since the spring.**"

The woman of about thirty-six said one thing about that on the Thursday and it was the only thing she said in the whole of the evening. **"There are about eleven names and about nine of them are people who have said yes to something small, and two of them are people who have said yes to something that was not small, and I know which two, and the man of about twenty-eight is one of them, and he is sitting in that room right now reading somebody else's handwriting out loud and he does not know that I know, and I have never told him and I am not going to."**

Nobody asked her which of the eleven. **She said in about four seconds that she was not going to be the person a man of twenty-two brings a piece of paper to about, and that if it is on a piece of paper then a person who is on the piece of paper has to be the one who says whether it is right, and that she has about four of those and none of them is her.**

---

**At half past nine he went down to the counter and put a piece of paper on it and it was the notice with about nine names on it that a man of about twenty-six had carried on Wednesday, and he had it because a man of about forty-one with a boiler asked him to bring it, and he did not read it.**

"**Is this yours.**"

"**No.**"

"**It's got a name on it that's the same as a name on that one,**" said Marek, **"and I know that because I have seen both, and I'm not going to read the other one out.**"

He put it down next to the man of about twenty-six's notice, which was still on the counter where it had been put on Wednesday and which had been picked up once and put down twice, and the two of them lay side by side with about four inches between them.

"**I'm not doing that on purpose,**" he said.

"**I know you weren't,**" said Marek. "**And I'm not going to move either of them.**"

---

**Four things about that Thursday he did not write down, and none of them is on any form.** A door left open behind a person who has never left one open. **The word *remove*, used by a person who can do it, in a corridor, about a man who is not in a room.** A second notice put down next to a first one with about four inches between them and neither of them moved. And eleven names read out of a piece of paper in about two minutes on a Wednesday evening by four people who are not a meeting and have no minute and have never been written down.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday, to be the kind of man who is asked to leave a room and goes.**

**Not the kind who is told. The kind who is asked, and who says yes in about four seconds, and who is then somewhere else and is not owed an explanation and has not got one.** He has wanted to be removed from about eleven things since the spring and has never once been asked about any of them, and this is the nearest he has come, and what came of it is a woman saying in nine words that there is nothing on him, and that is better and worse in the order he did not choose.

**He also wanted, for about four minutes in that corridor, to be a man who had said something in a room in the spring that four people could hold. He has never said it. There is nothing to hold. She was right and he has not been able to stop checking.**

---

**With the shutter down, four seconds went on something at ten past ten. He did not ask Sera Quill what the eleven names are for.**

He could have. She was in that corridor, and she had come out of a room where four people had read about eleven names out loud in two minutes, and she is the only person in this city who can remove a man from a room, and a person who can remove a man from a room is a person who knows what the room is for.

**He did not ask, and the reason is his own and not hers and he has not told it to anybody: she answered him in about nine seconds with the truest thing anybody has said to him in two years, and a person who has had that does not get to also have the other thing, and he has worked out in about four months that asking is not free, and that is the whole of what he has worked out, and it is not a plan.**
