# Chapter 676 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Card With Two Lines On It The Size Of A Playing Card That A Man Of About Fifty-Eight Printed Because He Had A Sheet Left And Nobody To Give It To, Which A Woman Of About Forty-Three Taped Inside A Door In A Second District Because People Kept Bringing Her Paperwork, And The Whole Of It Is Two Sentences And Neither Of Them Is A Number

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a card the size of a playing card with two lines of type on it. This one is where that card came from, and it is two sentences, and it names no figure at all.**

---

Twelve jobs carried a date against each on the Friday and twelve people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the thirteenth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and thirty-six days, a hundred and seventy-six weeks and four days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and forty days, a hundred and seventy-seven weeks and one day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and thirty-two days, a hundred and thirty-three weeks and one day. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and twenty-six, a hundred and forty-six weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and sixty-nine, a hundred and twenty-four weeks and one day.

The separation is six hundred and sixteen days, eighty-eight weeks to the day, and it was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, and it is walked every day of this stretch, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**He went to a two-man print shop on a road off Saltmarket at eleven in the morning because a man had asked him on Monday whether he would look at a card and had then not said anything about it for four days.**

The man is about fifty-eight. He owns the shop with a lad on Thursdays and he prints on Tuesdays and Fridays and he is sixty next year. **His name is Colin Nall and it is on the door in paint, and Marek read it on the way in and did not go in and say it out loud, and the man said it out loud himself in about four seconds when Marek asked what was on the fold of paper in the drawer on a Monday in the spring, and Marek has said it out loud in this city twice since and neither of those times was to him, and this morning he said it a third time, across the counter, in the middle of a sentence about a sheet of card, and the man did not look up.**

He was running a Heidelberg older than the partnership. There is one other in this city and a man of about thirty-three owns it and does not print. **He keeps a chase with nine ruled lines in it and nothing in them and has had it since the spring and has not thrown it out.**

"**You're the one that asked for a card,**" he said.

"**A man asked me on Monday if I would look at one. I did not know he wanted one printed.**"

"**Nobody asks me for a card,**" said Colin Nall. "**Nobody has ever asked me for a card. In nine years, no one has ever come in here and said print me a card. They want leaflets and they want a hundred of a thing and they want it Tuesday. A card is not a job, a card is a favour, and a favour is a job that somebody does not get paid for.**"

---

**And then he went and got it, and it was already cut, and he had not cut it for anybody.**

"**Had that in the drawer since eleven this morning,**" he said. "**Had a sheet left over from Thursday's run, card stock, quarter of an inch thick, and I don't keep card stock, so I don't know why I had any.**"

It was the size of a playing card and a half inch bigger. **One edge was cut on the guillotine and the other three were trimmed with a blade on a board, which is not how it is done and is how it was done, and he did not say why and was not asked.**

"**What goes on it.**"

"**Two lines.**"

"**Who wrote the two lines.**"

"**I did,**" said Colin Nall. "**About half past ten, before you'd got here, and I've been sat here for twenty minutes with a piece of card in my hand and a press and no order.**"

---

**Here is the whole of it, and it is two lines, and it is set in one of the two faces he has and it is the only thing in this city printed this fortnight that contains no figure of any kind.**

> Nobody has to be asked the same thing twice.
> Nobody has to have meant it.

"**That's it,**" said Colin Nall. "**That's the two lines. There is no third line and I did not put a name on it and I did not put a date on it and I did not put a shop on it, and if anybody comes in here in a year's time and asks me who that is for, I have not got a note of it, because a card is not a job.**"

"**Why did you print it.**"

"**I've had a sheet of card and it was quarter past ten on a Friday and I print on Fridays,**" he said. "**Don't make it a thing. It isn't a thing. I'd have thrown it in the bin by two.**"

**And Marek did not say the sentence, which was that a man who prints on Fridays and has a sheet of card and no order is a man who has just made a rule and put it somewhere, and that he does not know yet where and will not know for about nine months.**

---

**It was inside the door of a hall in a second district by two in the afternoon, taped at the top, and the woman who taped it is the woman of about forty-three and she is the one whose nine machines stand in that hall on Sundays and on no other day of the week.**

She did it because people had been bringing her paperwork since the spring.

"**They bring it here because I'm the only one in a building that's open,**" she said. "**I've had a woman bring me one on a Monday with a coat on and a man bring me two on a Thursday and neither of them knew what they wanted me to do with it and I couldn't tell them, so I said bring them back Friday, and about eleven came back Friday and I've read about four of them.**"

"**You read four.**"

"**I read four and I did not understand one of the four and I am not going to pretend to anybody that I did,**" she said, **"and I said that out loud to about eleven people in this room on Friday last and I said it to a man of twenty-two on a Monday at his counter and I said it to my own lad at six this morning, and every single one of those people went away and looked for somebody else.**"

---

**And that is the reason the card exists, and she is the reason, and she did not write it and cannot read it and did not know what it said until two o'clock. He asked her for one line, on the Friday morning, in about nine seconds, and she gave him one, and he wrote it down, and he did not tell her what he was going to do with it.**

"**He gave me that at the front door when I opened for the machines,**" she said, "**and I said I'd put it up because I've had eleven people bring me things they didn't understand, and he said fine, and he went away, and I read it standing in the doorway with the machines going off behind me and it took me about four seconds to read two lines.**"

"**You agree with it.**"

"**I don't agree with it, I agree that it's true,**" she said, **"and those are different, and I have watched about four hundred people in this city not know that they are different, and one of them is me most days.**"

---

**Here is what the card does not do, and it is on the page because it is on the page and not because anybody in this city has worked it out.**

**Nobody has to obey it. It is not in any arrangement. It was printed by a man who had a sheet of card left over, given away at a door by a woman who wanted eleven people to stop bringing her pieces of paper, and it is taped inside the door of a hall in a second district, and the hall is open on Sundays, and on the other six days of the week there is nobody there to read it and nobody in the building who has been told it exists.**

There is no list. There is no register of cards. **A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse does not know it is there. A woman of about sixty who holds a room up a stair in Saltmarket does not know it is there. A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm and has a heading on a strip does not know it is there, and she is the one person in this city who would be most able to do something about it, and she was not told and did not ask.**

**In about four months two people in this city are going to point at that card and say that somebody wrote the rule, and both of them will be right, and neither of them will be able to say who put it there, and the person who put it there is a woman of about forty-three who cannot read it and taped it up because she was tired.**

---

**At four o'clock a woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs came into that shop and asked Marek whether he had heard about a card.**

"**Heard about what.**"

"**There's a card in a door in a second district,**" she said, **"and a woman of about forty-three who washes for four buildings has taped it up, and about nine people have been in that hall today and none of them can read, and Colin Nall has had it in his hands and so have you.**"

Marek looked at her.

"**I have not. What card.**"

She looked at him for about four seconds and then she said, **"Then you haven't,**" and she did not say another word about it, and she has not raised it since, and she was right not to, and nobody in this city asked her where she had it from and she did not say.

**That is the whole of what anybody in this city knows about it as a fact, and it is a woman of about fifty-two saying that two people have had it in their hands when one of them has not, and she knew that inside about nine seconds, and she did not press it, and there is no arrangement anywhere in this city that would have told her to press it.**

---

**Four things about that Friday stay on this page, and not one of them is on any form in this city.** **A sheet of quarter-inch card stock in a drawer of a man who does not keep card stock.** A cut on three sides by a guillotine and a cut on the fourth by a blade on a board, which is what a person does when they are doing a favour and not a job. Eleven pieces of paperwork read and four understood and three days of somebody's week spent saying out loud that she did not understand them. And a woman of about fifty-two who is right about about nine things a week saying that two people have had something in their hands when one of them has not.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Friday, to be the person who wrote the two lines.**

Not the printer. **The one who worked out that a person does not have to have meant it, and said it in a sentence, and had it printed.** He has known it since the Wednesday, since a pen went into an apron in a room at the back of a hall, and he has said it to nobody, and he has been wrong about it once already, at a counter, on a Monday, when he told a man of about twenty-six that it stands on the paper and that is all anybody can say.

**It is not all anybody can say. A man of about fifty-eight on a road off Saltmarket just said it in four words and had it printed, and the four words are better than his were, and the printer did not know he was arguing with him and did not know the argument existed.**

---

**At ten past ten, the shutter down, four seconds went on something. He did not ask the printer to keep a copy of the plate.**

Colin Nall pulls a card when he prints a card. There is a plate in a drawer with a card's worth of type on it and the two lines on it and nothing else. **Asked, he would have kept it and given it to anybody who came in. He was not asked, because asking would have made it an order, and an order is a thing a man can refuse, and a favour is a thing he has already agreed to.**

**He did not ask. That is the eighth thing he has given up in about a fortnight that cost him about four seconds and nothing else, and the plate is in a drawer on a road off Saltmarket and nobody in this city knows it is there and it will still be there in about nine months.**

---

**Up a stair in Saltmarket there is a book with a green cover on sixty-three lines and, on the table beside it, a tin on seventy-three with the lid on and a receipt in it as a page marker that is not hers. The place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room has been empty sixteen weeks and two days. From half past six until about ten, in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road, the chair the door is behind had one other person in it. She is about thirty. The eighth of eight in a ring binder on a back shelf of that room is hers, and the binder did not come out.**
