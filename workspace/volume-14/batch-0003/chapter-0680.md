# Chapter 680 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Woman Of About Fifty-Two Who Chairs Nine Chairs Who Says In About Nine Seconds That Two People In This City Can No Longer Show Which Answer They Gave And That Nobody Is Going To Be Told Which, A Man Of About Twenty-Eight Who Reads Somebody Else's Handwriting Out Loud And Who Is One Of The Two On A List And Who Is Not One Of The Two, And The Ten Days Stop In The Middle Of Themselves Again

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs and who says a thing in about nine seconds and does not soften it afterwards. This one is the two people, and the not being told, and the ten days stopping in the middle of themselves.**

---

The Friday carried twelve dated jobs and twelve people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five. At ten the shutter came down and shook the cage for the seventeenth time in eleven years, and the whole of that day is in the two hours either side of four o'clock.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and forty-three days, a hundred and seventy-seven weeks and four days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and forty-seven days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks and one day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and thirty-nine days, a hundred and thirty-four weeks and one day. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and thirty-three, a hundred and forty-seven weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and seventy-six, a hundred and twenty-five weeks and one day.

The separation is six hundred and twenty-three days, eighty-nine weeks to the day, and it was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, and it has been walked every day of this stretch of days and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**She came in at half past four and did not take her coat off and she had been in that shop four times in a fortnight, which is twice as many as she had been in it in the previous nine years.**

"**Two things and then I'm going, and I have said both of these before and I am saying them again because I have been asked twice.**"

"**All right.**"

"**One. A mark in a box is not a decision, and the absence of one is not evidence that somebody did not decide, and both halves of that are now true in this city about two times over and nobody is going to be told which half belongs to which of the two people.**"

"**Two.**"

"**Nobody is going to be told,**" she said. "**Not you and not me and not the woman of about twenty-four and not the man of about thirty-four with the notebook. I have thought about it for a week and I have not found a person in this city who should be told and I am not going to invent one, and that is not a punishment, that is the arrangement, and it is the same arrangement as the one about the nine numbers, and you did not add those up either.**"

---

**And she said the other half of it, which is the half that is not about not knowing, and it is why she came in.**

"**I want the other thing said out loud too and I have not said it to anybody. There is a woman of about thirty-six on a corner who did decide, on a Wednesday, in four minutes, behind a shut door, on purpose, and she has said it to a wall and to nobody else, and she is the only one of the three who can say which of the two things happened to her.**"

"**And she hasn't.**"

"**And she hasn't and she is not going to, and I am not going to tell her to, and I have not told her that I know,**" said the woman of about fifty-two. "**That is the arrangement too. If I tell her, then in four weeks there is a person in this city who can say that one of the three meant it, and the other two of them stop being a question and become a number, and the two of them have spent a fortnight not being a number and I am not doing that to them.**"

---

**And then she said the sentence that is the whole of the ten days, and she said it in about nine seconds and she said it standing up with her coat on.**

"**More doors have a printed sheet on them than have anybody in them who has put a mark in the box, and nobody is going to be sent to any of the doors, and the ninth room will be asked again in four weeks or it will not and I do not know which and I am not going to decide it in advance, and it is a Wednesday, and there is no fifth run, and that is the whole of what this stretch of days did.**"

"**That's a list of things that didn't happen.**"

"**It is a list of arrangements,**" she said, **"and I have been in this city nineteen years and I have watched about nine arrangements get written down and I have watched nobody ever write down that they had been written down, and that is why none of them can be blamed for anything, and it is also why about four hundred people in this city are still standing at a door with a sheet on it and are not going in. I did not fix that. Nobody fixed that. I have been in this shop four times in a fortnight and I have not fixed that.**"

---

**Nobody thanked her. Nobody gave her a chair, a form, a sheet, a list, a number or a name, and she was not on a list, and she has not been thanked for any of it and did not come here to be.**

**He did not ask her about the two people. He did not ask which two. He did not ask her to say the names of the three, and he did not ask her what is written on the ninth of the nine sheets on that table in a second floor in Crown Terrace, and she did not say what is written on it, and there are four people in this city who know that the ninth of the nine carries one word and not one of the four knows the word, and neither of those two questions was asked either.**

---

**And a man of about twenty-eight who works at a water office and is not the man of about twenty-six came in at five and asked for a boiler part and paid for it and said four things that were about nothing.**

He is on the list of about eleven names. **He is one of the two who said yes to something that was not small, and the woman of about thirty-six in that room knows it, and he does not know that she knows, and he came in here on a Friday about a thermostat.**

"**Just the valve,**" he said. "**And can you not write the date on the ticket. My lad puts them all in date order and it's a mess.**"

"**All right.**"

"**You're not writing it.**"

"**I'm not writing it.**"

"**Right,**" he said, and went, and he is a person who has been in this shop about nine times in a year and has never once been told anything and has never once said anything that anybody had to do anything about.

**The four things that were about nothing were the valve, the date, a lad who puts tickets in date order, and whether the shop was open on the Saturday, and it is not open on the Saturday, and he did not argue, and the ticket went into a drawer with about nine hundred others in it.**

---

**There was no fifth run and there was no van and there was nothing under the awning next door at four, half past four, or at ten.**

**A man of about fifty-eight printed four runs of a sheet and has been paid for none of them, and there is no fifth run of anything and no order for a sixth, and about nine hundred people in this city have one of the four he has given away and nobody has paid him for a single sheet. Five days ago he printed two lines on a piece of card stock he had not ordered and gave it away at a door, and nobody has paid him for that either, and he pulled a plate afterwards and put the plate in a drawer.**

**A man of about forty-four emptied nineteen cages on the Friday round at eleven this morning and did not count them and has not counted them in nine years. Three of the sheets that came out of that round had a mark already in a box at the bottom of them. He put all three back where they were, in about nine seconds, and there is no rule anywhere for what else to do with a piece of paper that came back with a mark on it, and nobody told him he was right.**

---

**Four things about that Friday are on this page, and not one of them is on a form.** **A woman in a shop four times in a fortnight who has been in it four times in nine years, saying things she has said before because she has been asked twice.** A thermostat part bought on a Friday with the date deliberately not written on the ticket by a man who is one of the two on a list and does not know it. A coat still on at half past four. And the whole of the road between that counter and that door, walked four times in a fortnight by a man of twenty-two, and not once by anybody sent.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Friday, to be somebody's evidence.**

Not a counter. **Not a name on a list. A piece of evidence that a person held in one hand and could put in front of nine other people, and the evidence is that he was in this shop on four days in a fortnight and said true things on all four of them.** He wanted a woman of about thirty-one to be able to say in a supply room in a town a day and a night of bus travel from here, *there is a man in this city who has been in a shop four times in a fortnight and has never once asked anybody for anything*, and for that to be worth about nine seconds to nine people who are waiting.

**It would not be worth nine seconds. It is worth nothing. There is no arrangement in this city in which being decent to a stranger is evidence, and there has never been one, and nobody has ever written one down, and about nine people in this city could write one down in about four seconds this afternoon and not one of them will, and the reason they will not is on a face in about nine seconds and it is that there is no person here who should be told and she is not going to invent one.**

---

**At ten past ten, with the shutter down, four seconds went on something, and it was the last four seconds of the ten days. He did not tell the two of them which of the two things happened to them.**

He could not have, and that is the reason, and it is the only reason and it took him about four months to accept. **Nobody in this city knows. A woman of about thirty-six knows which one of the three meant it and will not say. A woman of about twenty-nine who typed the forms knows the order they went out in and has not been asked to look at any of them. A man of about twenty-four who came to a counter about a necklace has a card with four words and a thumbprint on it. And a man of about thirty-four in a town a day and a night of bus travel from here has a notebook with nine seconds of a woman's speech in it and has not been asked to read it out to anybody, including himself, twice.**

**There is nobody to tell and there is nothing to tell and the whole of the ten days is that a thing happened in three districts on one afternoon and cannot now be established by anybody, including him, and he is the person who is most able to establish it and cannot, and there is no arrangement anywhere that would let him.**

The place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room is empty and has been empty seventeen weeks and two days. The eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room is hers, and it did not come out, and nobody in that building has put a word to her about it, and nobody ever will.**
