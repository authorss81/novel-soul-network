# Chapter 673 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Three People In Three Districts Filling In Three Pieces Of Paperwork On One Afternoon, Two Of The Three Who Cannot Afterwards Show That They Decided To Give The Answer They Gave, A Man Of About Twenty-Six At A Water Office, A Woman Of About Thirty-Six Who Kept A Newsagent On A Corner And Who Decided, And A Woman Of About Forty-Three Who Did Not

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and one afternoon in three districts in which three people put something in a box that had something else in it. This one is the afternoon, and the two of the three who afterwards cannot tell anybody which of two things happened to them.**

---

Twelve jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and twelve people came over the counter, the last of them let in at five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the tenth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and twenty-seven days, a hundred and seventy-five weeks and two days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and thirty-one days, a hundred and seventy-five weeks and six days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and twenty-three days, a hundred and thirty-one weeks and six days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and seventeen, a hundred and forty-five weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and sixty, a hundred and twenty-two weeks and six days.

Six hundred and seven days is the separation, eighty-six weeks and five days of it, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, walked every day since, not shorter, with nothing put back into it.

---

**Here is the whole of the afternoon, in the order it happened, and it is three places and it took about two hours in all.**

**In a first district, at about half past two, a man of about twenty-six who reads meters for a water office sat at a counter in his own building with a pen and a piece of paper, and there was a mark in the box at the bottom of it that he did not put there, and he looked at it for about nine seconds and then he put a mark in it anyway.**

**In a second district, at ten past four, a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a newsagent on a corner and who asked in nine seconds on a Friday three weeks ago whether a cage on her corner is hers to empty went into the room at the back of that shop and filled one in, and she did it on purpose, and she has said so to nobody and will say so to one person.**

**In a second district, at about half past four, a woman of about forty-three who washes for four buildings was asked in the room at the back of a hall by a man who came in to ask about the machines, and she did not have a pen on her at the time, and she was given one, and she put a mark in the box at the bottom of it, and she has not decided since whether she decided to.**

---

**The man of about twenty-six did it first and he did it in nine seconds and he would give you the nine seconds as a reason.**

"**I have read it,**" he said, to a man of about thirty-one who was standing on the other side of that counter and who had no business there and did not go away. "**It says the box is for the answer you are giving. I am giving the same answer. That is not a different answer.**"

"**You don't know it is the same.**"

"**I know what the printed line under the box says,**" said the man of about twenty-six. "**It says the last approved answer. It is the same as ours. I have written ours on nine pieces of paper this month and I know what ours is.**"

"**Then you are agreeing.**"

"**I am putting the same word in the same box,**" he said, **"and if you want those two things to be the same thing then they are, and I have to warn you that about nine other people in this building are going to do exactly what I have just done, and I have not told one of them to, and I did not tell them not to.**"

**That is a man who knows exactly what he has done and has decided in about nine seconds to be the person who can live with it, and no part of this city is going to argue him out of it, and nothing that happened to him this afternoon was anybody's decision except his own.**

---

**The woman of about thirty-six did it in the room at the back of her own shop with the door shut and it took her about four minutes.**

She had the pen in her hand before she sat down. **The mark that was already in the box was in ink and it was not in a cross and she noticed that, and the box on her form has a printed line under it saying what the last approved answer was, and the last approved answer was the answer she had given on the counter in a fortnight, in ink, in a hand that was her own.**

"**I have done the right thing,**" she said, to the back of a room, **"and I want to be clear that I knew what I was doing while I was doing it, because in about three weeks somebody is going to tell me that nobody knows who did these, and I am going to be the one person in this city who can say that I did mine.**"

She said it out loud to nobody. **There is a wall in that room and a light fitting and a bin and no person, and she has not told anybody, and there is nobody she could tell.**

---

**And the woman of about forty-three was given a pen and did not ask for one, and that is the part that is on the record and the part that is not.**

The man who came in had come in to ask about the machines, and he had a sheet of paper in his hand and he was not staying and he said so in about four seconds. He put it on the table, and there was a mark already in the box, and there was a printed line under the box saying what the last approved answer was, and he said: **I have to be back at the other three by six, and I am not going to sit here, and if you want to leave it blank you can leave it blank.**

"**It's not a choice,**" said the woman of about forty-three. "**It's a box. You either put something in it or you don't.**"

"**That's what I've been saying to people for a fortnight.**"

"**Then you can say it standing up,**" she said. **"Leave it. It's not mine. I wash for four buildings, I don't fill anything in for anybody.**"

The man looked at her and then he went out through the hall and across the wet yard, and he did not see what she did, and he did not know that the woman in the room at the back had been the third of three that afternoon and that the other two were in two other districts.

**She picked the pen up about a minute later. She put a mark in the box. It is a small line with a hook on it and the hook goes the wrong way for a cross and it is the same mark as the one that was already there, and the two of them are not the same hand, and she looked at both of them side by side for about four seconds and then she did not put the pen down at all, she put it in her apron.**

---

**And that is the whole of it, and it is three districts and about two hours and nobody in any of the three rooms knows that the other two happened.**

There is no fourth thing. **Nobody compared the three. Nobody photographed anything. There is no list of the three, no code, no reference, no address book with three addresses in it, and the man who emptied all three that Friday did not see it happen because it happened on a Wednesday and he comes on a Friday.**

**A form with a box ruled at the bottom is a piece of paper. Three of them on one afternoon in three districts, each with a mark in it that is the same mark, in three hands, of which one is the hand of a person who meant to.**

---

**He was at a counter in a first district when the man at the back of that hall in a second district picked the pen up about a minute later, and he did not see it, and he has never been in that hall, and the two of them do not know that the other exists.**

Twelve dated jobs, twelve callers, the last let in at five. **A man of about twenty-nine came in at about four with a list face up in front of her and asked whether she wanted him to send out a second copy of a thing she had already sent, and he said no in about four seconds, and the reason he gave was not the reason.**

"**I'll say it once,**" he said, "**and then I'm not saying it again. If it went out once and it was wrong then it went out once. A second copy of a wrong thing is a wrong thing with a number on it.**"

"**You don't know it was wrong.**"

"**I know I can't prove it wasn't,**" he said, **"and I know that a thing nobody can disprove is exactly how a city ends up with four of the same mistake in four buildings, and I'd rather be the man who only sent one.**"

Nobody at that counter asked her why she had offered it.

---

**There were four things he took away from that Wednesday, and none of them had a box at the bottom of it.** **A pen that went into an apron pocket and stayed there.** Three rooms that had a person in each of them at about the same time on the same afternoon, and no way at all of knowing that, and no anybody who was in a position to know it. A man going down a stair at ten past five with a bag that was lighter going up than coming down. And a printed line under a box, about the width of the paragraph above it, that says what the last approved answer was, which is a sentence about a piece of paper and is on about nine hundred of them in this city and has been read by about four people.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday afternoon, to be one of the three people in three rooms.**

Not to be in the room. **To be the one who is asked, and who has a pen, and who has nine seconds to decide.** He has never had a box with anything in it in his life. He has been asked things in nine buildings in a fortnight and he has said no to most of them and he has never once been in a position where the whole of the answer was already written underneath.

**He wanted it for about four minutes at half past four and then he wanted the opposite thing, which is worse, and the opposite thing was to be the person who goes round afterwards and finds out which of the three of them meant it, and there is no way to do that, and he knows there is no way to do that because he has been looking for it since Monday and Monday only gave him three rooms and a round.**

---

**At ten past ten on that Wednesday the shutter came down and four seconds went on something. He did not tell the man of about twenty-nine to send the second copy.**

Not because she was wrong. **Because the three rooms that got one copy of a thing on a Wednesday and the three rooms that got one copy of a thing on a Thursday are the same three rooms, and the only difference between them is a day, and a person who goes round afterwards with a list is a person holding a list.**

That is the sixth thing he has given up in a fortnight that cost him about four seconds and nothing else, and he has not told anybody about any of the six.

---

**Sixty-three lines in the green-covered book, up the Saltmarket stair, and it does not open on a Wednesday unless somebody says something to somebody's face. The tin beside it is on seventy-three with its lid shut and a receipt in it doing duty as a page marker that is not the woman's. The place behind her chair is empty and has been empty about thirteen weeks and three days. In the fourth of those four rooms off the service road, after half past six, one other person took the chair the door is behind and was still in it at about ten. She is about thirty. Her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a back shelf there, and the binder did not come out.**

---

*Eight hundred and thirty-three and nine hundred and seventeen are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and nineteen weeks to the day and a hundred and thirty-one weeks to the day, and the first is a man of about fifty-one against a north wall and the second is a question that has been standing in this city since the spring, and a mark in a box ruled the width of a paragraph is neither of those two figures and it is a description of a thing and not a length of time.*

One of the three decided to give the answer she gave and has said so to a wall. One of the other two did it in nine seconds because the answer was the same as the answer he had given nine times this month, and warned the room that about nine others would do the same. The third was given a pen and did not ask for one and picked it up a minute after a man had gone down a stair and out of the building, and put the pen in her apron. The three marks are the same mark in three hands and no two of the three hands are the same and nobody has compared any two of them. A man of about twenty-four who was that day in a building four days of bus travel there and back did not see the third of the three and was not in that hall and is not one of the three. Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The three places are a water office in a first district, the room at the back of a shop on a corner, and the room at the back of a hall in a second district, and nobody in any of the three knows that the other two happened.*

*Three marks in three hands, and one of the three meant it.* **The afternoon happened between about half past two and about half past four in three districts, and there is no event in it at all and there is no moment in it that anybody could point at afterwards. A man of about twenty-six who reads meters put a mark in a box in nine seconds on the reasoning that the answer already in the box is the answer he has given nine times this month, and told a room that about nine others in his building would do the same and that he had told none of them to.
