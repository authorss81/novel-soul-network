# Chapter 677 — A Monday, A Town Two Days Of Bus Travel From Here And Not This One, A Shop Of About The Same Size As His Own With A Different Bench In It, A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Came Back Off Two Days Of Bus Travel On The Friday And Went Back To Work On The Monday And Was Shown A Thing By A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine At About Four O'Clock, And Who Cannot Show That He Did Not Agree To It And Is Not Going To Be Told That He Did Not

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six in this city, and a day and a night of bus travel away a man of about thirty-four went back to work and was shown a thing at about four. This one is the thing, and it is nine sheets, and the date on the fourth of them is in his hand and the mark in the box on it is not.**

---

Ten jobs carried a date against each in this city on the Monday and ten people came over the counter there, the last of them let in at half past four, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the fourteenth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and thirty-nine days, a hundred and seventy-seven weeks to the day. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and forty-three days, a hundred and seventy-seven weeks and four days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and thirty-five days, a hundred and thirty-three weeks and four days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and twenty-nine, a hundred and forty-seven weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and seventy-two, a hundred and twenty-four weeks and four days.

The separation is six hundred and nineteen days, eighty-eight weeks and three days, and it is not shorter than it was on the Sunday, and it is walked every day of this stretch including this one, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**The town he is in is a day and a night of bus travel from this one, the round trip is four days, and he has done it twice this fortnight and paid for both himself.**

He is about thirty-four. He has pushed beds for a firm that hires them by the week for about nine years. He is paid at about four in the morning. He is not on a list. **Nobody in this city has his name and about four people in this town have, and none of the four is anybody whose business it is.**

He was shown the thing at about four o'clock on the Monday afternoon by a woman of about twenty-nine who had come in on the Thursday with a bag of nine sheets and had gone back on the bus the same night.

**She had not been asked to come back. She had come back because the man of about thirty-four had written down nine seconds of what a woman said to him in a supply room, and she had wanted to see whether writing it down had changed anything, and it had not, and she had found that out by the middle of the afternoon.**

---

**Here is what she put on the bench in front of him, and there were nine of them, and they were the same nine.**

"**These are the nine that went out of this building in the spring.**"

"**I have not seen them before.**"

"**You filled one in,**" she said. "**You filled one in on the Wednesday and you told the man at your counter you did.**"

"**Then it will be one of the nine.**"

"**It is one of the nine,**" said the woman of about twenty-nine. "**I have got them in the order they went out, and the fourth one is yours, and I have known that since the Friday morning on a bus, and I did not tell you on the Friday and I am telling you now because you are here and I am here and there is nobody else.**"

---

**And then she put her finger on the fourth one and did not say anything for about four seconds.**

"**This is yours,**" she said. "**That is your hand, on the line above the box, where you wrote the date. I have compared it to nine other things you have written in this room and I am right, and you do not have to take my word for it, you can look at the two and see it.**"

He looked at it. **It is his hand. It is a date written in the ordinary way by a man of thirty-four who has written about nine hundred dates in his life and does not think about the shape of them.**

"**And that,**" said the woman of about twenty-nine.

**And that is a small line with a hook on it in the box at the bottom of the sheet, and the hook goes the wrong way for a cross, and it is not his.**

"**That is not mine.**"

"**No,**" she said. "**I know that it is not yours. I have had four people look at it and not one of them says it is yours. And I have to tell you the other half of it, and I have thought about how to do that for two days on a bus, and I have not found a way that does not sound like I am doing it to you, and I am going to do it anyway.**"

---

**And here is the sentence that has been in this city in one mouth for about two days and is going to be in none, and it is the whole of what happened to him.**

"**It is not yours and there is nothing on that paper that says it is not yours.**"

He put it down on the bench and looked at it for a while.

"**Who put it in.**"

"**I don't know. Nobody knows. I have asked two people in a corridor and I have not asked the people above, and the man who printed the paper set none of the type and will not name a person, and he has said in four words that he cannot name anybody in this city who could tell him who did.**"

"**So it's somebody's.**"

"**It is somebody's,**" she said. "**And I have to say the rest of it. You are going to go back to work on the Monday. In about four weeks somebody is going to come and ask you whether you agreed to what is on that sheet, and if you say you did not agree they are going to ask you how you know, and the answer is that you know because you were not there when it was put in, and that is not an answer, because you were not there when it was put in, and there is no way at all of showing that you were not.**"

---

**Nobody told him that what was done to him was done to him.**

Not the woman of about twenty-nine. Not the man of about thirty-four at a counter in a city a day and a night of bus travel away, on the Friday, who could have said it in about four seconds and would have been believed by nobody including himself. **Not the woman of about thirty-one in the supply room, who said a thing to nine people on the Wednesday and was not asked to say it again and will not be asked to say it again and is four days of bus travel there and back and is owed nothing by anybody in this city.**

He asked one question and it was the wrong one and he knew it before he had finished it.

"**Is there another one like mine.**"

"**There are nine.**"

"**Do the other eight have a name at the top.**"

"**Four of them do and five of them do not,**" she said. "**And I am not going to tell you which four and which five, and that is not me keeping something from you, that is me not wanting to be a person who knows which five.**"

---

**He was on the Monday shift. He pushed four beds to a house on a road out of that town and came back and did three more, and he was paid at about four in the morning, and the woman of about twenty-nine had gone back on the Thursday's bus pattern and was not in that town any more.**

**He has not written to anybody. He has not asked to see the other eight. He has told his employer nothing, because his employer is a firm that hires beds by the week and is not an arrangement and has no procedure and has never been asked one and would not know what to do.**

**The nine sheets are on a bench in a building in a town a day and a night of bus travel from here. The fourth of them is the one he is in, and his name is not on it at all, and the date at the top of it is in his hand, and there is nothing anywhere on that paper that says who put the mark in the box.**

---

**He was at a counter in this city at nine o'clock that Monday and he did not know any of this, and it was not told to him, and nobody in this city is going to tell him, and the woman of about twenty-nine did not write down that she had told him, and the notebook is in a coat and there is a second notebook in a different coat and neither of them is in this city.**

---

**Four things came out of that Monday, and none of them was a thing anybody could put a signature to.** **A date in nine hundred dates and the only one anybody has ever compared against anything.** A hook on a mark that goes the wrong way for a cross, and about nine of those hooks that somebody has described, and about nine of them described in four words. A woman who had worked out how to say the other half for two days on a bus and had not found a way that did not sound like she was doing it to somebody. And nine sheets on a bench in a town a day and a night of bus travel from here, four of which have a name at the top and five of which do not, and nobody will say which is which.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Monday, to be the person who says it was done to you.**

Not to fix it. **To say the seven words out loud, in that supply room or in that building or at that counter, to a man of about thirty-four who has been carrying it since six o'clock on a Friday morning and a night on a bus and who has not told anybody and will not.** He worked out the whole of the sentence four times between nine and one o'clock. It was not difficult. It was about seven words long and he has said harder things to harder people and he is paid for it.

**He did not say it, and the reason is on the page as three separate facts and is not explained anywhere: the man is a day and a night of bus travel away, the round trip is four days, and there is a woman of about twenty-four in this city who once carried nine sheets down a stair because she was asked to and who is owed nothing and who is not going to be asked twice.**

---

**The Monday ended with the shutter down at ten and four seconds gone at ten past ten. He did not write the address of that town down.**

He had it. **He had known it since the spring because he has pushed beds to four towns over nine years and the fourth one is a name he says in his sleep.** He did not write it and he did not put it anywhere and he did not tell the one other person in this city who would have carried something for him without being asked, and there is a man of about twenty-four in a co-operative of about eleven people in this city who owns their own hours and who is the last person anybody here would want to have a name.

That is the ninth thing he has given up in a fortnight that cost him about four seconds and nothing else, and it is the first of the nine that is not about a thing at a counter.

---

**Sixty-three lines in a green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket, which does not open on a Monday unless somebody says a thing to a face, and a tin on the same table on seventy-three with its lid on and a receipt in it used as a page marker that is not hers. There is a place behind that woman's chair and it has stood empty about fourteen weeks and four days. The fourth of the four rooms off that service road, after half past six, had one other person in the chair the door is behind until about ten. She is about thirty. Hers is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it did not come out.**
