# Chapter 667 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Eleven People Who Were Not In A Room And One Of Them Who Was Seen Going Home Without Coming In, A Woman Of About Fifty-Nine Who Says In About Nine Seconds That There Is No Such Thing As A Person Who Did Not Have A Hand, A Figure Of Non-Participation That Is Refused In Four Seconds By A Person Who Is Right, And Nobody Who Asks Her To Do It Again

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is eleven people and a hall and a sheet of paper with a line under a line, and nobody is going to be asked about any of it.**

---

Ten jobs carried a date against each on the Monday and ten people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Behind the three rooms off that service road there is a fourth and they are warm, and the figure for them is one thousand two hundred and eleven days, which is a hundred and seventy-three weeks to the day. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and fifteen days, a hundred and seventy-three weeks and four days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and seven days, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks and four days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and one, a hundred and forty-three weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and forty-four days, a hundred and twenty weeks and four days.

The separation is five hundred and ninety-one days, eighty-four weeks and three days, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, and walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**She had put the nine chairs back against the wall and the eleven against the other one, and she was doing it again when he came in, and she did not stop.**

"**How was it.**"

"**Nine in and nine out,**" she said, **"and I'll tell you the nine out, because that's the bit that isn't a number. Five put a hand up and said yes. Three put a hand up and said no. One did not put a hand up and said she'd rather not say out loud in a room and she'd rather not write it either, and she wanted it recorded that she'd been asked and she'd rather not, and I wrote that down on the sheet and put it in the folder, and that's the eighth.**"

"**That's eight.**"

"**That's eight,**" she said. "**The ninth is the one I'm going to have to tell you about and I'd rather you had it from me than from anybody else, so sit down if you're sitting down.**"

---

**He was not sitting down.**

"**The ninth put a hand up,**" she said, **"and she didn't say anything at all, and then after about four seconds she said *do I have to*, and I said no, and she said right, and she left, and she was seen going home up that road at about a quarter to nine without having come in.**"

"**So it was yes.**"

"**It was *do I have to*,**" she said. **"Which is not yes and it is not no, and I'm telling you it wasn't yes because I want it in this room as not-yes and not because I want it as something else. There were nine people in that hall and I have got nine things on a sheet of paper and one of the nine is a question.**"

---

**A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse came into that hall at about half past two on the Monday with the folder under his arm and had the whole thing in about four minutes, and he is right about the arithmetic and he is wrong about the sentence.**

"**Eleven not in the room,**" he said. "**Nine in. That's non-participation of about fifty-five per cent and I can put that on a form and take it to a district and it'll be the first real figure anybody's had in about four months.**"

"**No,**" she said.

"**It's arithmetic.**"

"**It's arithmetic and it's not a figure,**" she said, **"and I'll give you the number and take the rest of it away from you. Eleven. And here is why there is no per-cent on it. One of them was asleep. One was at work and finished at seven. One had a shift that ran long. One has a child with nobody else and it was a school night. And there are seven more, and I could not tell you one reason for any of the seven, because I do not know them, and I am not making seven reasons up so that you can divide by something.**"

"**Without a denominator you're just reporting a mood.**"

"**Without a denominator I'm reporting eleven people,**" she said, **"and the one who went home up that road without coming in is a person, and if you take her out and divide her by something then in about four weeks somebody is going to write *and a majority of people in that district did not support the arrangement*, and there is no arrangement to support, and I will not be the reason that sentence exists.**"

He stood there for a moment.

"**That's the most expensive sentence anybody's said in that hall,**" he said.

"**It's nine words,**" she said. "**I didn't plan it. It came out the way the box comes out on the sheet.**"

---

**And that is the whole of what he said in that hall and he did not improve on any of it and nobody improved on his.**

Marek had come at about two with a bag of cable ties he had said he would drop off, and he put the bag down by the hatch and did not stay, and there is a person in this city who would have spoken for about four minutes about what a showing of hands is for, and he did not, and he was in that hall for about twenty-two minutes and he said two things and neither of them was useful and one of them was a joke that four people laughed at and the woman of about fifty-nine did not.

---

**And a man of about thirty-one who types for a firm asked him at the counter on the Monday whether there is such a thing as not saying.**

"**Not saying what.**"

"**There's a room in a second district and on Wednesday a woman asked nine people a question and one of them said she'd rather not say it out loud and didn't want to write it either,**" he said, **"and she's me. That's my sentence. And I've been two days trying to work out whether that counts, because if it counts then it's on a piece of paper in a folder somewhere, and if it doesn't count then I stood in a room on Wednesday and did nothing and there's a difference and I can't tell which one I'm standing in.**"

"**I don't know.**"

"**You weren't—**

"**I know I wasn't,**" Marek said. **"I wasn't in that room and I wasn't in that hall and nobody told me and I'm not going to stand here in about four seconds and tell you what your own sentence was worth.**"

"**Right,**" said the man of about thirty-one.

"**What I'll tell you is what a woman of about fifty-nine told a man with a folder on Monday in about nine words, and you can have it or not,**" Marek said. "**She said *do I have to* is not yes and it is not no and she wanted it recorded that way. That's all I've got and it's hers and I'm passing it on and I'm not going to be the one who improves it.**"

The man of about thirty-one stood at that counter for about four seconds and then said **"Right,**" again, and bought a coil of cable he did not need, and went out, and Marek has no way of knowing whether that man said a third thing to himself in the street and has not asked.

---

**He noticed four things on that Monday and not one of them was on a form.** **A sheet of paper about the size of an A4 with the question on it in a woman's handwriting, and under the fourth line there is a word that has been rubbed out and written over, and the word on top is *raised* and the word underneath it can still be read as *show*.** Nine chairs against one wall and eleven against the other, and the two sets do not match. **A woman who walked to a hall on a Wednesday evening, was seen going home up that road without coming in, and is not known by anybody in that hall except by the back of her coat.** And a door in that hall with a fire exit sign on the wall beside it and the door is not the fire exit and has been opened and shut about four thousand times and has no closer on it.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Monday, to be one of the eleven people who were not in that room.**

Not to have been in it. **To have been one of the ones who did not come, or came late, or was at work, or could not be asked, and to be able to say so out loud to somebody without it meaning that he had a job on a Wednesday afternoon.** He was at a counter in Lattice Ward on Wednesday from nine until six. He could have said that. It would have taken about four seconds and it would have been completely true and it would have been the first thing he has put in a room in about five months that was about him and not about a form.

He did not say it, and the reason is that saying it would have been a request, and he has said no to about nine offers of being the middle in about four months and has not yet made one of his own.

---

**And at about half past five outside that hall, in about four seconds, he let one go. He did not ask her to do it again on the next Wednesday.**

There is a Wednesday in about a week and nine chairs and a woman who can open the doors and a sentence that has been in nine buildings since Friday, and the obvious thing is to ask her to put it in front of the hall again with the same nine chairs, and there is no reason on earth not to, and it is a nine-second sentence and he had it ready and he did not say it.

**A woman who holds a room every week is a woman who holds a room every week.** She would have said yes. She would have said yes in four seconds and it would have been correct and about four hundred people in this city would now have a number that came out of one hall and not nine, and one hall is a place and nine rooms is an arrangement, and he did not want to be the man who turned nine rooms into one hall with a better number in it.

He put the bag of cable ties by the hatch and left and she said nothing at all as he went, and it was not rudeness, and it was the fourth time in a fortnight that a person in this city had let him leave a room without saying anything and he has noticed every one of the four.

---

**The place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room has been empty for twelve weeks, which is a whole number of weeks, and nobody has said one word to her about it.** A green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket is on sixty-three lines and the tin on the table beside it is on seventy-three. **In the fourth of those four rooms, after half past six, there was one other person and she was about thirty and she was in the chair the door is behind until about ten**, and nothing whatever was put to her. **What belongs to her is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it did not come out.**

*Seven hundred and seventy-seven and one thousand two hundred and eleven are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and eleven weeks to the day and a hundred and seventy-three weeks to the day, and the first of them is nine hand copies of the front of a page of which eight are unfinished and the second is four rooms off a service road, and a sheet of paper about the size of an A4 is neither of those figures.*

He did not ask her to do it again on the next Wednesday and there is a Wednesday in about a week. Nobody thanked anybody.*

*Eleven people, one question, and a per-cent that was refused.* **A hall in a third district had nine people in it on Wednesday evening and eleven people not in it, and a woman of about fifty-nine who opens the doors of that hall gave both numbers out loud in about nine seconds and refused, in four seconds and nine words, to let the second of them be turned into a proportion. She named four of the eleven: asleep, at work, a shift that ran long, a child with nobody else on a school night. She said she could not give one reason for any of the other seven and would not invent seven reasons so that a man could divide by something, and she said that the one who was seen going home up that road without having come in had said *do I have to* and that she wanted it recorded in this entry as not-yes and not as anything else. A man of about thirty-eight with a warehouse had arrived at about fifty-five per cent of non-participation in about four minutes and was told that there is no arrangement to support and that she will not be the reason that a sentence about a majority of a district is available in about four weeks. He said it was the most expensive sentence anybody had said in that hall and she said it was nine words and came out the way the box comes out on the sheet. A man of twenty-two was in the room for about twenty-two minutes with a bag of cable ties and said two things and did not speak for the room and nobody improved on anything he said, including him.**

*What the Monday did, and what it left alone.* **For the first time in about four years in this city there is a figure for the number of people who were not in a room, and it is eleven, and it belongs to one woman in one hall and it is not a percentage and it has not been written on anything with an address on it.** The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two and this Monday does not move it: nine people asked a question in a room with no money behind it has nine answers in it and two of those answers are about a person who was not in the room, which is a consequence with people in it, and neither of the two acts standing on that register since about four years ago was touched by anything in this day. Nobody thanked anybody in that hall and nobody thanked the man of forty-four on Friday and nobody has thanked anybody in this city since about four years ago, which is a different sentence from the first one and both of them are true. Seven objects are named on this page and this one does not bring any two of them together: a sheet of paper about the size of an A4 with a word rubbed out under a word written over it, a card on end in a rail of two, a printed sheet with a box ruled at the bottom, a hand copy of the front of a page, a wire cage on a round-the-side, a bag of cable ties, and a plate of iron with four slots. That drawer under the bench in Lattice Ward is shut, four sheets in it, fourth of the four unread. A book up a stair in Saltmarket is on sixty-three lines and a tin beside it on seventy-three and the distance between those two numbers is not a number. A place behind that chair has been empty twelve weeks.
