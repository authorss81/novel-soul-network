# Chapter 665 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Figure Nobody Asked For And That Was Given Out Loud Anyway, Two Hundred And Six And One Hundred And One In About Nine Seconds, A Woman Of About Thirty-Five Who Came To A Counter And Not To A Room And Who Was Given No Chair And Was Not Thanked, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Does Not Write It Down

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is two numbers said out loud in about nine seconds, and one of them leaves this city in one mouth and is never written down.**

---

There were twelve dated jobs on that Thursday and twelve people over the counter, and the last of the twelve was let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Those four rooms off that service road are one thousand two hundred and seven days, a hundred and seventy-two weeks and three days, four of them and one behind the other three, all of them warm. The card on end in the rail by the door is one thousand two hundred and eleven days, a hundred and seventy-three weeks to the day, and that is four days past them and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and three days, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks to the day. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and ninety-seven, a hundred and forty-two weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and forty days, a hundred and twenty weeks to the day.

Five hundred and eighty-seven days is the separation, eighty-three weeks and six days of it, and it was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and has been walked every day since and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse came in at about half past ten with a folder under his arm and put it on the counter and did not open it.**

"**I've added them up.**"

"**Which.**"

"**The eight.**" He said it the way a man admits to something. "**Wednesday night, in a corridor, at about half seven. In my head, on the way down, and it took me the whole stair. I've not written it down and I'm not going to and I haven't said it out loud to anybody, and I've come down here at half past ten on a Thursday to tell a man who runs a repair shop a number I could not sit on.**"

"**You could have sat on it.**"

"**I could have sat on it until somebody with a folder asked me for it and then it would have been in a file,**" he said, **"and I know what this city does with numbers in files in about three weeks, because I watched it do it to a figure about nine days a week for about four months, and I invented the figure and told everybody I invented it and that did not stop it being in a file.**"

---

**Marek did not ask him for it.**

That is about four seconds on a Thursday morning and it is the first refusal of the day and the reason is that a man who asks for a number in a repair shop is a man who has started keeping numbers, and a man who keeps numbers in this city becomes a person with a route inside about four weeks.

"**Don't tell me,**" he said.

"**You might want it.**"

"**I might,**" he said, **"and that's exactly the problem and I'm not going to solve it by taking it off you in about four seconds on a Thursday morning when you've walked down here with a folder to be rid of it.**"

"**Right,**" said the man of about thirty-eight, and left the folder on the counter and went out without it, and it is a different folder from the one a man of about forty-nine carries and no sentence in this entry sets them beside one another.

---

**She came in at about ten past four and did not take her coat off, which meant four minutes, and she did not ask whether she could look at anything, and she was about thirty-five.**

"**You're the one who isn't on any of it.**"

"**I'm the one who isn't on any of it,**" she said. "**Leo Marr. I'm not going to give you a card and there's nothing to sign and there's nobody behind me at this counter except about nine people who do not know I'm here and would not want to be described.**"

"**Right.**"

"**And I'm not here about the four steps and I'm not here about the woman of about thirty-one four days of bus travel there and back, because she has said no to two people about money this week and I am the third and I've come to say I'm not going to be a fourth.**"

"**What are you here about.**"

---

**She gave the number out loud and she gave it in about four seconds and nobody had asked her for it.**

"**Two hundred and six,**" said Leo Marr.

He did not know what it was.

"**Wire cages. Outside post offices. Round-the-sides and one or two on corners. There are two hundred and six of them in this city and I have had that figure for about nine years and I have never given it to anybody, and I have said it out loud to you first because you are a man behind a counter and you will not repeat it, and because if I had said it in the room where the numbers are written down it would have been a number that room gave out and that room does not give out numbers like it.**"

"**Two hundred and six,**" he said.

"**Two hundred and six.**"

---

**And then he gave her the other one, in about four seconds, out loud, at a counter, because she had gone first and it seemed to him at the time like the only fair thing available.**

"**One hundred and one,**" he said, **"is the eight numbers added together and it was added up in a man's head on a stair on Wednesday night and it has not been written down anywhere in this city and I have just been given it and refused it and I am giving it to you now because you have been first.**"

"**You refused it.**"

"**I did not want it,**" he said. "**That's the whole of why.**"

She looked at him for a second longer than most people look at a counter.

"**Two hundred and six and one hundred and one,**" she said. "**That is the whole of the thing you have been doing for a fortnight and you did it in about nine seconds at a counter and neither of you has written anything down. Do you know how rare that is? I have spent nine years telling people that the only safe number is the one nobody has.**"

"**I know,**" he said. **"And two hundred and six is bigger than one hundred and one and that is the whole of the finding and it isn't a finding, it's two people saying two figures at a counter.**"

---

**And she said the reason, and the reason is wrong, and she said it out loud and she did not take it back.**

"**I'll tell you why two hundred and six is the number and not one hundred and something,**" she said. "**Because two hundred and six is the number of holes in this city. A cage is not a return path. A cage is a hole with a lid on it and about nine hundred pieces of paper have gone into it in about four weeks and nobody has read any of them and about four hundred and fifty people have been handed one by a man who did not know what he was handing out.**"

"**That's not right,**" Marek said.

"**No,**" she agreed. "**It is a cage with a flap and a rubber band on the hinge, and it is the oldest return path there is, and it goes round the back of the building, and it is a place. I know that. I've known it for nine years and I still said hole, because if I say place then somebody in about four weeks is going to ask me how many places, and if I say hole then nobody asks me anything.**"

"**You gave the number anyway.**"

"**I gave the number anyway,**" she said, **"and that's the part I can't take back, and I've been sitting in a room two days working out how to do this without giving the number, and there isn't one, and this morning I found out why: the minute somebody counts nine rooms, the counting has to be about more than nine rooms, and the only way to stop that is for the bigger figure to be in the mouth of somebody who can't be asked where it came from.**"

---

**Nobody offered her a chair and nobody thanked her and nobody said that she had been brave or useful or honest.**

She was not on a list. **She has not been given a chair at anything in this city, and she has not been given a form, and nobody in this building is going to ask her to sign anything, and nobody in this building thanked her, and about four people in this city know that Leo Marr stood at that counter at ten past four on a Thursday and about four of those four will say afterwards that they did not know that it had been the door number.** She was asked nothing. She answered two questions and volunteered a third and went.

"**Was the count right,**" said Marek, when she was at the door.

"**It's the count I've had for nine years,**" she said. "**It's right for the streets I walked. There's a man who empties them and he'll tell you a different one, and his will be right for his round, and neither of you is going to get the number of doors in this city out of either of you, and that's the correct outcome and I don't want to hear anybody say otherwise.**"

---

**He noticed four things on that Thursday and not one of them was on a form.** A folder left on a counter and not picked up. **A folded piece of paper in her coat that she took out at about half past four, unfolded once, showed him the back of, folded again and put away, and the back of it is blank and there is nothing written on either side of it and she carried it and did not read it.** A coat that has been mended at the cuff by somebody who used a different thread. And a person who came to a counter and not to a room, which is a thing about four people in this city have done this month and about nine hundred have not.

---

**And there were four seconds at about ten past six with the shutter down and he spent them on nothing at all. He did not write two hundred and six down.**

There is a pad under the counter with a spike through it and about two hundred sheets still in it. **He did not take a sheet and he did not put his thumb on the spike and he wrote nothing, and the figure is now in his head and in hers and it is on nothing in this city, and when somebody asks him about it in about four weeks, which a man of about forty-nine with a folder will do, he will be able to answer truthfully and completely and he will not want to.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday, to be the person who has the number and is asked to keep it.**

Nobody asked him. **He has spent a volume refusing to be the person a thing is sent to and about five weeks of it refusing to be the person who pays, and what he wanted on Thursday afternoon was to be the person a number stops at, which is the one version of being useful he has never once been offered, and it was not offered, and about four seconds of him on a Thursday evening were spent wishing for it, and he is not going to say so to anybody.**

---

**The green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket is on sixty-three lines this morning and was on sixty-three lines yesterday evening, and the tin on the table beside it is on seventy-three and its lid has not been off.** **Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room there is a place, and it has been empty for eleven weeks and five days, and nobody in this city has asked her about it.** In the fourth of those four rooms, after half past six, there was one other person and she was about thirty, and she was in the chair the door is behind, and she was still in it at about ten, and nothing whatever was put to her. **Her page is the eighth of eight and lies in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it did not come out.**

*One thousand two hundred and eleven and eight hundred and forty are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and seventy-three weeks to the day and a hundred and twenty weeks to the day, and the first of them is a card on end in a rail of two and the second is nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns that a man cannot hand over, and two hundred and six is neither of those figures and is a count of cages kept by one woman in her head.*

*Two figures at a counter, and a woman who is right about one of them.* **The number of wire cages outside post offices in this city is two hundred and six, and it was said out loud for the first time in about nine years on a Thursday afternoon at a counter, by a woman of about thirty-five who has never given a number to anybody and who came to the shop and not to the room, and who said in advance that she was not going to be the fourth person to offer money to a woman four days of bus travel there and back. The other figure is one hundred and one, which is eight numbers added together in the head of a man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse, on a stair, on Wednesday night, and it has never been written down and it was given out loud at that same counter because she had gone first. Nobody offered her a chair, nobody gave her a form, nobody thanked her, and nobody in this city is going to be told that she stood at that counter and what she said there.**

*What the Thursday did, and what it left alone.* **Two figures left this city in two mouths and neither of them is on paper, and a man of twenty-two has decided not to write either of them down, and the person who has given hers away is not on a list, is not owed anything, is not going to be asked about it, and would be within her rights to deny having said it.** The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two and is not moved by a woman giving away a figure that she had kept and did not want to keep, because a woman who gives away a count for nine years is not a person who has done a correct thing and got nothing for it: about four hundred and fifty people in this city already have the thing the count measures, and nothing she said can put it back in the cage. Nobody thanked anybody at that counter.
