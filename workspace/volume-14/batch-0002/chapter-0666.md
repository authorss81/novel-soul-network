# Chapter 666 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Nineteen Wire Cages On One Round And About Nine Of Them With Something In The Box At The Bottom Of It And No Rule For What To Do With Any Of Them, A Man Of About Forty-Four Who Empties Them And Who Is Right And Is Not Told So, And Two Whole Numbers Of Days That Are Each Other's Ages And Are Not A Finding

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is a sack, nineteen cages, nine pieces of paper going back in, and nobody in this city has ever asked that man a question.**

---

Fourteen jobs carried a date against each on the Friday and fourteen people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and came down hard, because the wind had got up over the afternoon.

**Those four rooms off that service road have one behind the other three and they are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and eight days, a hundred and seventy-two weeks and four days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and twelve days, a hundred and seventy-three weeks and one day, four days past them, and the rail takes two and has taken two for twelve weeks.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and four days, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks and one day. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and ninety-eight, a hundred and forty-two weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and forty-one, a hundred and twenty weeks and one day.

**The man of about fifty-one against that north wall has been against it for eight hundred and fourteen days, which is a hundred and sixteen weeks and two days. The post at the end of that corridor has been at the end of that corridor for seven hundred and fifty-six days, a hundred and eight weeks to the day. Those two figures are each other's on this Friday and a coincidence between two integers is not a finding and this entry does not go on about it.**

The separation is five hundred and eighty-eight days, which is eighty-four weeks to the day, and it went into a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and it has been walked every day since, it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a newsagent on a corner came in at about half past eleven and put a key on the counter.**

"**Is that cage outside mine yours to empty.**"

"**I don't empty them.**"

"**It's on my corner,**" she said, **"it's got my name on it in a way, it's the same as the one at the post office, and there's four sheets in it and they came out of my letter box on Thursday and Thursday and Thursday and I've put them back in every time because I wasn't going to stand on that corner opening other people's post.**"

"**Who put them in the letter box.**"

"**I don't know and I'm not going to find out,**" she said, **"and that's the whole of my question and I asked it in about four seconds because if the answer is that a man on a round empties them then I'll leave them, and if the answer is that somebody's meant to empty them on a day then I'd like to know the day, because I've got a key and I've got a corner and I'd rather it was Wednesday.**"

"**It isn't Wednesday.**"

"**It's a Friday,**" she said, **"and I knew that. I wanted to hear a person say it.**"

Nobody said it to her twice and nobody thanked her for asking and she picked the key up and went out, and the four sheets are in that cage on that corner and they were in it before she asked and they will be in it after the round.

---

**And the round came to that corner at about two o'clock and the man of about forty-four did not stop at it.**

That is on the page because the woman of about thirty-six has told four people and Marek is one of the nine people in this city she told and he was not going to be the ninth of them to say nothing. **He lifted the flap for about nine seconds, took out four sheets, put them back in, wrote nothing on any of them, and carried on down the street.** He has put back nine all afternoon and four of them were at that corner and about nine hundred people in this city have a printed sheet and not one of the four is any different from the rest.

---

**He came in at about ten past four with a sack over his shoulder and put it down on the floor rather than on the counter, which is what a man does with a thing he does not want to explain.**

"**Is that yours.**"

"**It's the job's,**" he said. "**I won't leave it down. I've got nine minutes of my round left and if I'm in here nine minutes I'm two streets short on a Friday.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I'll talk while I work,**" he said. **"It's the only round I've got and there's nineteen on it and I've had it about nine years.**"

---

**He was about forty-four. He emptied them into the sack as he went and he did not shake them and he did not look inside.**

"**They take paper,**" he said, **"about four hundred and fifty of them have come out of my nineteen in about four weeks, and that's a number I keep in my head and I've never written down and you're not getting it, because the second a figure like that exists on paper somebody's going to do sums with it in about three weeks and I don't want to be the man whose sums they did.**"

"**How many come out of one.**"

"**Anywhere between none and about forty,**" he said, **"and it depends what day it is and what the weather's been and whether there's a school run on that street, and I could not tell you and I would not want to guess.**"

---

**"What do you do with them,**" Marek said.

"**Put them back.**"

He said it without any weight at all, the way a man says the thing he has said on a Friday for nine years.

"**All of them.**"

"**The ones with nothing in the bottom go back,**" he said, **"and the ones with something in the bottom go back, and that's the whole job. There's no rule anywhere that says what to do with a bit of paper that has come back with a mark on it, because there isn't one, and I've looked, and I asked a woman in a first floor in a second district about four years ago and she said it goes in a drawer, and then I asked a man who was there before me and he said it goes in a drawer, and the drawer is in her office and it's not my drawer and I don't go in that office.**"

---

**He said the whole of it in about nine seconds and he was right about every part of it, and nobody told him he was right, and nobody thanked him.**

"**That's the correct thing,**" Marek said.

"**Is it.**"

"**There's no rule and there's nobody to hand it to and putting it back is what happens,**" he said. **"I'd have thought you'd worked that out.**"

"**I have worked out it's what happens,**" said the man of about forty-four, **"and working out what happens and knowing whether it's allowed are two different jobs, and I only get paid for one of them and it's the one where you lift the flap.**"

He laughed at that and it was not a bad laugh. He put the sack on his shoulder.

---

**And then he said the thing about the nine, and he said it standing up, and he said it because he had been carrying it since about eleven o'clock in the morning.**

"**Nine of them have something in the box.**"

"**Something in the box.**"

"**A mark. Most of them a mark.**" He shifted the sack. "**There's four sheets in one of them and all four have got one in the bottom and they've been in that cage since the spring, and that one is on Lock Street and it's not one of yours if that's what you're after, but I don't know whose it is and there's nobody to ask, because the woman in the first floor isn't the one who put them in and the man before me is gone.**"

"**Did you ask her.**"

"**I asked her about the drawer,**" he said, **"and she told me about the drawer. I didn't ask her about four sheets in a cage on Lock Street and I'm not going to, because the day I start asking a round about four sheets I've become a different man on that round and there's nobody to hand the round to.**"

---

**He did not ask which cage he has never emptied.**

That is the small thing and it took about four seconds on a Friday afternoon. There is one cage on that round that he has never put his hand into in nine years and it is about two streets past the works, on a corner with a wall on three sides of it, and Marek had it in his mouth and did not ask, because a man who asks what another man has never done is a man who has started a list, and a list about a round in this city is a route.

**The man of about forty-four told him anyway, at the door, in four seconds, because it was not a secret.**

"**You'll want to know which one,**" he said. "**It's the one on the wall by the works. Nine years and I've never opened it. There's nothing in it, there's never been anything in it, and I have never once thought about that until about a month ago and now I think about it about twice a day, and I've not told anybody that and I'm telling you it, so go and be somebody else's problem about it.**"

Then he went out into the wind and down the street with a sack over his shoulder and did not run.

---

**He noticed four things on that Friday and not one of them was on a form.** **Nineteen, and not a round number, and the nineteenth is the one by the works with a wall on three sides.** Four sheets in one cage since the spring, which is a cage and not a returned sheet and is not a leaflet and is not the four of nine that came back on a table three weeks ago and nobody in this entry has set them beside one another. A mark in a box that he described as *a mark* and would not describe further. **And a man who has done a job nine years and can tell you what happens and cannot tell you whether it is allowed, and who went and said the second one out loud to a stranger on a Friday afternoon because a stranger behind a counter told him that what happens is the correct thing, and then said *go and be somebody else's problem about it*, which is the sentence of a man who has just been told he is right and does not know where to put it.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Friday, to empty a cage.**

Not to count one. **To put his hand in one of them and take something out and have it be a thing he was doing.** Nobody has ever handed him a cage and nobody ever will, and there is no form on which a person may say that they emptied a wire cage on a round-the-side on a Friday afternoon, and about nine hundred pieces of paper have gone past this counter in four weeks and every one of them came in on somebody's arm and none of them is going to go back into a cage on his account.

He wanted it at about half past four and he did not have it and he did not ask.

---

**Nobody thanked anybody, and after half past six the fourth of those four rooms held one other person.** She was about thirty and she sat where the door is, and she was still sitting there at about ten, and nothing whatever was put to her in three and a half hours. Her page is the eighth of eight **in a ring binder on a back shelf of that room and it did not come out.** A green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket stands at sixty-three lines and a tin on the same table holds seventy-three with its lid on, and the two figures are not one figure. **Behind that woman's chair is a place which has been empty for eleven weeks and six days and which nobody has mentioned to her.**
