# Chapter 662 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Nine People In Nine Buildings Each Given One Sentence And None Of Them Told What The Other Eight Have Been Asked, A Tenth Room With About Nine Chairs In It And A Man Who Is Asked To Put The Question In It And Says No In About Four Seconds, And A Woman Of About Fifty-Two Who Says In About Nine Seconds That She Will Not Say What It Is FOR

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is one sentence going into nine buildings, and nobody in any of the nine is told about the other eight.**

---

Twelve jobs carried a date against each on the Friday and twelve people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and did not shake the cage because the wind was the other way.

**Those four rooms off that service road have one behind the other three and they are warm, and the figure for them is one thousand two hundred and one days — a hundred and seventy-one weeks and four days. The card on end in the rail by the door is one thousand two hundred and five, a hundred and seventy-two weeks and one day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and ninety-seven days, a hundred and twenty-eight weeks and one day. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and ninety-one, a hundred and forty-one weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and thirty-four, a hundred and nineteen weeks and one day.

The separation is five hundred and eighty-one days, eighty-three weeks to the day, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**A woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs gave nine people one sentence on the same Friday, and she did it in nine buildings, and she did it herself, and it took her most of the afternoon and about nine hundred yards of steps.**

The sentence was this. **Would you be handed a sheet of paper with the last answer already in the box at the bottom of it.**

She did not write it down anywhere that anybody saw. She said it out loud, in each of the nine places, and then she said the same sentence a second time to make sure the person had it whole, and then she said **that is the whole of it and I am not going to tell you anything else about it and you will not hear from me again until the Wednesday.**

"**What am I meant to do with it.**"

"**Ask it.**"

"**Ask who.**"

"**The people who come through your door.**"

"**What if they don't want to be asked.**"

"**Then you write down that you asked them and they didn't want to be asked,**" she said, **"and that is a correct thing to write down and it is not a failure and it is not a refusal and it is not a person who didn't care. It is a person who told you something in about four seconds and you have it.**"

---

**Eight of the nine said it back to her in slightly different words and one said it back exactly.**

The one who said it back exactly was a person who has been asked things in a room for about nine years and who did not say anything after saying it, and who took a piece of paper off the desk, wrote the sentence on it in about four seconds, folded it once, put it in a coat pocket, and said, **"That's the whole thing and I'm not ringing you about it,**" and she did not want to be rung and had not said so and had said something else instead.

**Marek was in none of the nine and was asked none of the nine questions and heard none of the nine being put.**

That is on the page and it is a Friday afternoon. He was at a counter in Lattice Ward from nine until six and eleven of his twelve callers came over it and the twelfth was a man who wanted a key cut and wanted it cut wrong on purpose and was talked out of it in four seconds.

---

**At about four o'clock the woman of about fifty-two came into the shop and put a handbag on the counter and did not sit down, and there was a ninth envelope in it, or there was not, and Marek did not know which for about nine seconds.**

"**You said nine.**"

"**I said nine.**"

"**You said eight just now. You said eight people in eight buildings and you said nine when you started.**"

"**I said nine,**" she said, **"and I have said eight since, and both of those are true, and the difference between them is not something I am going to explain at a counter to a man who is twenty-two.**"

"**Fine.**"

"**It's not fine and I'm not going to comfort either of us about it.**" She put a folded piece of paper on the counter and it was the sentence and nothing else on it. "**That goes to the tenth room. There's nine chairs in it and a table and a woman of about sixty who holds it on a Wednesday and there are about eleven people in it and none of them has been asked a thing in about four months except the count.**"

"**You want me to take it up.**"

"**I want you to take it up,**" she said, **"and I'm telling you what I want and not what I'm asking, and you can hear the difference or you can not.**"

---

**He said no in about four seconds and the four seconds are on the page.**

"**No,**" he said.

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**No,**" he said, **"and the rest of it is that I know what I am and so do you, and the rest of it is that about five weeks ago I put the first thing I ever proposed in this city into a room and had it taken off me in about nine seconds by a person entitled to take it off me, and it was not a punishment and I have not said so and I am not going to say so to you this afternoon.**"

"**That's not what I asked.**"

"**It's what I have,**" he said.

**Nobody said thank you and nobody said it was a shame and she picked the paper back up off the counter and put it in the handbag, and the whole of that took about four seconds, and neither of them was owed anything by the other and neither of them was thanked.**

---

**The tenth room did not get the question on that Friday and there is no sentence anywhere that says it got it later.**

The woman of about fifty-two put the paper back in the handbag and took it away with her and she has not been asked about it and has not mentioned it. **It is a sheet of paper with one sentence on it in a handbag, and it is not a leaflet, and it is not a return sheet, and it is not a card on end in a rail, and no sentence in this entry sets it beside any of them.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Friday and not one of them was on a form.** Eight people saying one sentence back eight different ways, and one saying it the same way twice. **A handbag with a folded paper in it, and the fold of that paper is a store fold and not a letter fold, because whoever folded it works in a shop that sells paper.** A cage on a round-the-side at the end of a street he was on that morning with nine or ten sheets standing up in it, none of them flat, all of them in the same lean. **And the word *last* in a sentence about a printed sheet, which is the only word on that page doing any work at all, because a last answer implies there was a first one and somebody kept it.**

---

**And then, on the Friday, at about ten past six, about four seconds went somewhere else. He did not ask who had carried the question to the ninth place.**

There are nine rooms. Six are in this city and three are in towns, and one of the three is four days of bus travel there and back, and a woman in that one has a season ticket on the Wednesday and has not had one on the Saturday since the spring and has said no to two people about nine pounds this week.

**A sentence reached her anyway. Somebody went on the Saturday bus and paid their own fare and told nobody, and Marek found out on the Friday afternoon from a woman of about fifty-two who was not going to say who, and he did not ask, and the reason he did not ask is that a man who asks who paid the fare is a man who has started to think about fares, and he gave that up at about ten past six and it cost him nothing and it was the first thing he has given up in about four weeks that did not cost him anything.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Friday, to hear the sentence being put in a room where he was not the only one being asked it.**

Nobody asked him anything. **Nine people in nine buildings were each asked one question on the same afternoon and he was not in any of the nine rooms and was not asked and did not hear it and did not offer, and the wanting was not the wanting of a man who wants to be important, it was the wanting of a man who has spent thirteen volumes being the person a room sends things to, and has been nobody's for about five weeks, and would have gone to a hall in a third district and sat at the back and been the fifteenth person nobody had to look at.**

He wanted it and he did not have it and he did not make it happen and nobody in this city will ever be told he wanted it.

---

**There was one other person in those four rooms after half past six and she was about thirty.** She sat where the door is and she was still sitting where the door is at about ten, and nothing was put to her and nothing ever is and there is nothing owed to her in this city by anybody in it and no apology of any kind has ever been offered to her. Her page is the eighth of eight, **in a ring binder at the back of a shelf in that room, and the binder did not come out.** A green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket is on sixty-two lines and a tin on the table beneath it is on seventy-three and neither of those two figures converts into the other. **There is a place behind that woman's chair and it has been empty eleven weeks and two days and about four people in this city know it and not one of them has asked.**

*Seven hundred and forty-nine and five hundred and eighty-one are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and seven weeks to the day and eighty-three weeks to the day, and the first of them is a post at the end of a corridor and the second is a separation that ended in a form about twelve weeks ago and describes nothing.*

*One sentence, nine buildings, and a name nobody will say.* **A question went into nine rooms on one afternoon and none of the nine rooms knows about the other eight, because the woman who put it there went herself and said the same words nine times and took nothing back and said it was the whole of it. What the question was is on this page and is one sentence about a printed sheet with a box at the bottom of it. She would not say what it was for and said so at a counter in about nine seconds, and she put a tenth copy of it on that counter and asked a man of twenty-two to take it to a room that has about nine chairs in it, and he said no in four seconds and gave a reason that was not the reason. She said she wanted and was not asking, and he heard the difference. The ninth room is four days of bus travel there and back and got the sentence anyway, from somebody who bought a bus fare out of their own pocket and has not said so and is not going to be asked, and the woman of about fifty-two confirmed that it had got there while also saying that she had said nine and had then said eight, and that the difference is not something she is going to explain at a counter. The tenth room was not asked and there is no sentence in this entry that says it was asked later. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.***

*What the Friday did, and what it left alone.* **Nine rooms have been asked the same question by nine different people with one sentence, and that sentence is now in nine heads and four walls and one coat pocket and one handbag, and it has been put to nobody who can put it to anybody else.** The register of correct acts with no consequence stays where it stood and this Friday does not move it, because a woman carrying one sentence into nine buildings and refusing to say what it is for has been given nothing and has had nothing done to her and has caused nine people to have to answer a question, which is a consequence and not an absence of one. Six objects get named on this page and no two of them are brought together: a printed sheet with a box ruled at the bottom, a folded piece of paper with one sentence on it, a card on end in a rail of two, a plate of iron with four slots, a wire cage on a round-the-side, and a hand copy of the front of a page. That drawer under the bench is shut with four sheets in it and the fourth has never been looked at, and it is not the drawer the four cardboard boxes were under. Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room there is a place, and it has been empty eleven weeks and two days and nobody in this city has asked her about it. After half past six a woman of about thirty sat where the door is until about ten and nothing was put to her and her page is the eighth of eight at the back of that room in a ring binder and did not come out.
