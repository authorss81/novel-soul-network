# Chapter 663 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Nine Hundred Yards Of Road And Then A Hall In A Third District With Eleven Chairs In It And Nine Of Them Wanted For A Wednesday, A Woman Of About Fifty-Nine Who Opens The Doors Of That Hall And Who Says In About Nine Seconds That A Room Of Nine People Is Not A Street, Nineteen Trips To A CupBOARD, And Nobody Who Says Thank You

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is eleven chairs in a hall in a third district, and a man who carried nine of them up a staircase and was not asked to say a word.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Monday and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Behind the three rooms off that service road there is a fourth and the four are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and four days, which is a hundred and seventy-two weeks to the day. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and eight days, a hundred and seventy-two weeks and four days, four days past them, and the rail has held two for twelve weeks.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred days, a hundred and twenty-eight weeks and four days. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and ninety-four, a hundred and forty-two weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and thirty-seven, a hundred and nineteen weeks and four days.

The separation is five hundred and eighty-four days, eighty-three weeks and three days, and about twelve weeks ago it was shut in a one-line box in a form, and every day since then it has been walked and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**The woman who rang the shop on Friday afternoon about a Wednesday was about fifty-nine and she opens the doors of a hall in a third district on a Wednesday and on a Saturday and on any other day if somebody who is not her pays for it.**

She came to the counter at about half past nine on the Monday morning and asked whether he would come at two and stack some chairs.

"**How many.**"

"**Eleven. I want nine out by half past two and the other two against that wall.**"

"**Right.**"

"**I'm not asking you to do anything else,**" she said. "**I want to be clear about that at a counter, because the woman who told me about you said you'd say yes to more than that and I don't want you saying yes to more than that.**"

"**I'll stack chairs.**"

"**You'll stack chairs,**" she said.

---

**He was in that hall from about two until about twenty past four and he carried nine chairs up a staircase in nineteen trips and put two against a wall, and nobody asked him anything and he said nothing.**

**Eleven chairs is not a large number of chairs.** He had been braced for it. He had been braced for it in the way a person is braced for a thing that has a name, and he went up and down that staircase nine times with one chair in his hands and a hundred and forty steps in front of him and there was nothing in the work of it at all.

The hall holds about forty when the tables go in. There are two tables stacked against the far wall under a dust sheet and there is a hatch in the kitchen side and there is a smell of something being kept warm in a room that is not the hall.

**One of the nine wanted chairs has a strip of tape on the seat of it, about four inches, and the tape has gone over the edge of the seat and is not on the underside, and it is the same beige as the tape on a box of staples and it is not the only chair in this hall that has ever had tape on it.**

---

**She said the thing about the room at about half past three and she said it in about nine seconds and she said it to the middle of the hall and not to him.**

"**You know this is not a street,**" she said.

He had it ready. He had it ready since the Friday. He was going to say it at about two o'clock and it was going to be the first thing anybody had said in that hall about any of it.

"**Yes,**" she said, before he got a word out. "**I know it's not. Nine people in a room is nine people in a room and it is not a road and it is not a street and it has not got a door number. I have been opening these doors for about eleven years and I have watched about four hundred people come through them and I have never once had anybody ask me how many people live on this road, because there is no road.**"

"**What do you get.**"

"**I'll tell you what I'll get and I'll tell you now so that nobody can say afterwards they didn't know.**" She stood with her hand on the back of one of the nine chairs. "**I'll get the people who come to a hall on a Wednesday evening because there's something on. Some of them will know what this is about and some of them won't and I'll not know which is which and neither will they. Somebody will bring a child. Somebody will come for a free thing if there's a free thing and there's not going to be a free thing.**"

---

**"How many people do you get.**"

"**Nine,**" she said. "**I've put nine out. I've had eleven in on other Wednesdays and I am not going to account to you for the two, and not because they're a secret, and not because I think you'd use them. Because there's nothing to account for.**"

"**What do you mean, nothing to account for.**"

"**I mean one of them was asleep,**" she said, **"and one of them was at work, and one of them had a shift that didn't finish, and one of them has a child with nobody else, and there are seven others and I could not tell you one reason for any of the seven if I sat here until the shutter came down on your shop, and I'd not be able to tell you because I don't know them, and I'm not going to make seven reasons up so that a man at a counter has something to write in.**"

"**Nobody is writing anything in.**"

"**I know nobody's writing anything in,**" she said. **"I'm telling you what I will say if anybody asks me, so that it's said once and it's on a person and not on a room.**"

---

**And he did not tell her the number he had.**

There were nine rooms. Six of them were in this city. He had been in one of them twice and had a strap of masking tape with a name on it behind a till that belonged to a man of about forty who came for a bracket. **He knew that in about four of the nine the people who had been given the sentence had said out loud, to somebody who was not the woman of about fifty-two, that a room of people is not a street.** He did not tell her that. He told her nothing at all about the other eight, and he did it on purpose, and it cost him a sentence he had ready, and the sentence was going to be useful to her and it would have made her the ninth person who knows about nine rooms, and a person who knows about nine rooms is a person with a route.

---

**He noticed four things in that hall and none of them was on a form.** A strip of tape on the seat of one chair. **Two tables under a dust sheet and the sheet is off by about nine inches at one corner and has been off by about nine inches at that same corner for long enough that the dust underneath it has a clean edge.** A fire exit sign at the far end and a door beside it that is not the fire exit and is a door to a corridor with a radiator in it. And **eleven chairs where nine were wanted, and the two against the wall are the two that are not the same as the other nine, having come from a different room and being a different colour, and nobody has ever asked her where a hall gets chairs from.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Monday, to be the first person to say that a room of nine people is not a street.**

She said it at about half past three. **He had it ready since Friday afternoon and it was better than hers, because his had a reason behind it and hers did not, and hers went out into the middle of a hall and his would have gone to a woman standing next to a chair, and that is the whole difference and he lost it and he did not say it and he has not mentioned it since.**

Nobody in that hall knew he had wanted to say it and nobody in that hall will ever know.

---

**And at about twenty past four on that Monday, in about four seconds, he gave up the sentence he had been carrying since Friday. He did not tell her the number of rooms, or what four of the nine had said.**

Not on a pad, not on a scrap, not on the back of her hand, not on the sheet of paper the sentence was on. **A woman with a tally in a hall is a woman with a list, and a list in a room is a route, and about five weeks ago a woman with a hand truck said a thing in nine seconds that has been the shape of everything since.**

She did not have a tally and she did not write anything and on the Wednesday she will tell nine people a number out loud and it will be the only one in the room and it will be gone by nine o'clock and there will be no piece of paper anywhere in this city with that number on it.

---

**He was not thanked when he left and he was not thanked at the counter on the Friday and nobody in that hall said the word once in about two and a quarter hours.**

**A woman of about thirty was in the chair by the door of the fourth of those four rooms from half past six until about ten and nothing whatever was put to her.** The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight and lies **in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it did not come out.** On a table up a stair in Saltmarket a book with a green cover was on sixty-two lines at the start of this Monday and is on sixty-two lines now, and beside it a tin with a lid holds seventy-three. **Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room there is a place, and it has been empty for eleven weeks and three days, and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**
---

*One thousand two hundred and four and seven hundred and seventy are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and seventy-two weeks to the day and a hundred and ten weeks to the day, and the first is four rooms off a service road and the second is nine hand copies of the front of a page of which eight are unfinished, and eleven chairs in a hall in a third district are neither of those figures.*

*Nine chairs and eleven, and a sentence he did not improve on.* **A hall in a third district was put into a state for a Wednesday by a man who was not asked to say anything and said nothing, and the woman who opens its doors told him in about nine seconds that a room of nine people is not a street and has no door number, and she said it to the middle of a hall about nine minutes before he was going to say it himself and better. She said the hall will hold nine on a Wednesday and has held eleven on other Wednesdays, and she named one who was asleep and one who was at work and one whose shift had not finished and one who has a child with nobody else, and then said there are seven more and she could not account for any of them and would not invent seven reasons for a man at a counter to write down. A man of twenty-two had a sentence ready and lost it and did not mention it. He carried nine chairs up a staircase in nineteen trips and put two against a wall and did not ask the woman to write a single figure down on anything, because a woman with a tally in a hall is a woman with a list. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped, and a woman was told a true thing about her own hall and did not thank him for it because he did not say it.***

*What the Monday did, and what it left alone.* **One room out of nine in this city now has nine chairs in it, and the woman who opens the doors has told one person in advance what she will get and will not account for, and there is no piece of paper anywhere with the number of people in that hall on it.** The register of correct acts with no consequence stands where it stood, and putting nine chairs in a hall with no money and no list is not a third instance of it: about nine people will be asked a question on a Wednesday in a room that is not a street, and that is a consequence with people in it, and the two acts already on that register were not touched by anything in this day. Seven objects get named on this page and this one does not bring any two of them together: a strip of tape on the seat of a chair, a card on end in a rail of two, a printed sheet with a box ruled at the bottom, a hand copy of the front of a page, a wire cage on a round-the-side, two tables under a dust sheet, and a plate of iron with four slots. The drawer under the bench in that shop is shut with four sheets in it and the fourth has never been looked at. A green-covered book is on sixty-two lines and a tin beside it on seventy-three, and the distance between those two numbers is not a number. Behind a chair up a stair in Saltmarket there is a place that has been empty eleven weeks and two days. After half past six one other person sat where the door is until about ten and nothing whatever was put to her, and the page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight in a ring binder at the back of that room and did not come out.
