# Chapter 668 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Second Column On Three Kinds Of Form In A Building With A Canopy Over Its Door That Has Had A Heading Printed On It Since Friday And That Nobody In That Building Authorised, One Word On A Strip Of Paper That Is The Width Of The Column And Not The Weight Of The Form, And A Printer Nine Hundred Yards Away Who Did Not Print It

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is a heading, one word, and a printer who says four words and means them.**

---

Nine jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and nine people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the seventh time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand two hundred and thirteen days, a hundred and seventy-three weeks and two days, four of them and one behind the three, warm. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and seventeen days, a hundred and seventy-three weeks and six days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and nine days, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks and six days. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and three, a hundred and forty-three weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and forty-six days, a hundred and twenty weeks and six days.

The separation is five hundred and ninety-three days, eighty-four weeks and five days. It was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago. It has been walked every day since, it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**She came in at about half past eleven with four forms in a plastic wallet and she put them on the counter and squared them up, which she does.**

"**These are back in the tray,**" she said. "**And they've got a heading on them.**"

"**On the second column.**"

"**On the second column,**" she said. **"And before Friday there was no heading on the second column. There was a second column about three weeks and it had no heading and I asked about it and I filled nine in and lifted nothing.**"

---

**The heading is one word.**

It is set in the same face as the rest of the form and it is about the size of the rest of the form and it is on a strip of paper that is the width of the column and has been laid over the column and not fixed down at the edges.

The word is **DECISION**.

"**That's not what it says anywhere else,**" she said. **"It's what it says on that column, and it says it in capitals, and it is the only capital word in the whole of that form.**"

"**When did you first see it.**"

"**Friday morning. Half seven, in the tray, under about eleven others.**"

"**And you typed them.**"

"**I typed nine of them on Friday and four on Monday,**" she said, **"and I typed them exactly as they came, including the word, because that's the job, and because nobody in that building has ever given me a heading for anything and I wasn't going to be the one who decided that word was wrong.**"

---

**Nobody in that building authorised it and there is no post in it that could have.**

She had asked two people on the Friday, in a corridor, in about four seconds each. A woman of about thirty-eight who works in that office and has been there about six years said she had never seen it. A man who has been the caretaker of that building since before it had a canopy said he puts the post in and takes the post out and has never in his life printed anything.

"**I didn't ask the people above,**" she said, **"and I'm not going to, because if I ask them then in about a week there'll be a meeting about it, and the meeting will be about whether we print it or not, and then it'll be printed, and then the word will be on the form because a meeting decided it, and at the moment it's on the form because somebody nobody's met put it there.**"

---

**He walked nine hundred yards up the road off Saltmarket at about half past two and asked a man of about fifty-eight four questions and got four answers.**

"**Did you print this.**"

"**No.**"

"**Did somebody give you a job for it.**"

"**No.**"

"**Have you had anything to do with a strip that size in about four weeks.**"

"**No,**" said the man of about fifty-eight, **"and I've got a chase stood in the corner of my shop with nine ruled lines in it that came back to me in the spring and has had nothing in it since, and that chase is for a thing about nine lines long and it is not this and I'd not put it to this.**"

"**Right.**"

"**Right,**" he said, **"and I'll tell you the four words I want said about that and then I'll go back to work. Nobody asked me for it. There is nobody in this city I can name who could tell me who did.**"

---

**And then he asked one question back, and it is the only question he has asked anybody in about three weeks, and Marek has not stopped turning it over.**

"**Then who did.**"

"**I've thought about that,**" said the man of about fifty-eight, **"and here's the true thing and it isn't comfortable and you're not going to like it. Somebody had to authorise that box. Not print it, not lay it out, not rule a line under it — authorise it, in a place where authorising a box is a thing a person does on purpose. And it wasn't the fella who set the type, because I don't set type any more and haven't since my partner left and everybody in this city knows that. So it's a person who did not come through my door, and who did not ask me for anything, and who did not give me a job.**"

"**That's a man in a grey van.**"

"**That's a man in a grey van,**" he said, **"and I'll tell you the thing about the van and then I'm going back to work. He has never asked me for anything. Not once, in three runs, and he has taken the money in a fold of paper and he has taken the chase back locked and he has never said one word to me that wasn't about the van. And a man who asks for nothing is the reason I can't tell you who.**"

---

**He was asked, on the way to the door, whether he would take the strip down and destroy it, and he said four words and then went back to work.**

"**Can't do that.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because it came off your building and it's on your forms,**" he said, **"and if I took it and burned it then in about four weeks there'd be forms out there with a box under them and no name on it and you'd have no way of knowing whether it was ever there, and I'd have made that decision for about nine hundred people and none of them asked me.**"

He went back to work. The chase with nine ruled lines in it is still stood in the corner of that shop and has had nothing put in it since the spring.

---

**He could have taken the strip. That is on the page and it took him about four seconds at a counter with the plastic wallet open in front of him.**

He has a strip of paper off a form in a fourth pocket in his tool roll, with glue residue on one edge of it, and it has been there about three weeks. **This one would have gone in beside it. It is the width of a pocket, it is clean, it has a word on it, and a man who has been keeping one piece of paper for three weeks and has never written down what is on the fourth thing in that pocket could have kept two and still not written anything down.**

He did not take it. He said, **"I'll have a look at that one and bring it back,**" and she said, **"There's no need,**" and he said, **"There's every need,**" and she said **"Then bring it back,**" and he brought it back on the Monday at about eleven o'clock and she put it in the tray with about eleven others and it went back under a canopy.

**That is the whole of the small thing and it is the first time in three weeks that he has turned down a piece of paper.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Wednesday and none of them was on a form, which is a thing to say on a day about a form.** The weight of the ink. **The strip is lighter than the form and has a machine edge on three sides and a hand-cut edge on the fourth, and a machine edge has been on everything else in that tray for about four years.** A capital word in the middle of a page of lower case. **And a woman who said *I'm not going to be the one who decided that word was wrong* and then said *that's the job*, and both of those are the same sentence and she knows it.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday, to be the person who printed the word.**

Not the printer on the road off Saltmarket, who did not print it and said so. **The person who decided what the word in the box at the bottom of a printed sheet is. About four hundred and fifty people in this city have a mark in a box and not one of them has been asked what the mark is, and the answer is now in capitals on a strip of paper about four inches long, and it has been there since Friday, and nobody put it there who can be reached.**

He wanted it the way he wanted the ninth address and the door with nothing written on it, which is to say completely, and out loud, and about four weeks too late.

---

**A Wednesday with no sitting in it, and no number given out in this city or in any other today.**

**A Wednesday is not a Wednesday anything is given out on unless it is every fourth one, and this was not one, and nothing in this entry describes what happens in that room on a Wednesday when there is no hour of it.** The green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket stood at sixty-three lines when the day started and is on sixty-three lines now. **The tin on that table is on seventy-three and its lid has not been off at any point today and there is a receipt from a shop in it used as a page marker and the receipt is not hers.** Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room there is a place, and it has been empty for twelve weeks and one day, and about four people in this city have noticed and none of them has said so.

**After half past six on that Wednesday there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms and she was about thirty and she was in the chair the door is behind and she was still in it at about ten.** Nothing whatever was put to her. **The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it did not come out.** There was no sitting on that Wednesday and nothing was given out in this city or in any other, and a green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket was on sixty-three lines when the day started and is on sixty-three lines now, and a tin on that table is on seventy-three with its lid on. **Behind the woman's chair there is a place and it has been empty for twelve weeks and one day and about four people in this city have noticed it and none of them has said so.**

*Eight hundred and nineteen and nine hundred and three are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and seventeen weeks to the day and a hundred and twenty-nine weeks to the day, and the first is a man against a north wall and the second is a question that has been standing in this city since the spring, and a strip of paper with one word on it is neither of those figures.*

*671. Wednesday of week 241, at ten, and the eighteenth day of this stretch of days. Nine dated jobs and nine names, the last let in at ten past five. Three kinds of form in a building with a canopy over the door have had a heading printed over the second column since Friday morning, and the heading is one word in capitals: DECISION. A man of twenty-two walked nine hundred yards up a road off Saltmarket and asked a man of about fifty-eight four questions and got four answers, including that nobody asked him for it and that he could not name anybody who could tell him who did. The strip is lighter than the form and has a machine edge on three sides and a hand-cut edge on the fourth. It was taken back to that building on the Monday and put in the tray. There was no sitting on this Wednesday and no number was given out in this city or in any other today. Nobody thanked anybody.*

*What the Wednesday did, and what it left alone.* **About four hundred and fifty people in this city have a mark in a box on a sheet of paper, and the name of that mark is now on a strip about four inches long, put there by nobody who can be reached, and about four hundred of those people have not been asked and are not going to be.** The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two and this Wednesday does not move it: a typist who types what is in a tray is not a person who has done a correct thing and got nothing for it, she has been handed a form she did not write and has had nothing asked of her and nothing done to her, and the two acts standing on that register since about four years ago were not touched by anything in this day.
