# Chapter 670 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Courier Of About Twenty-Two Who Is Asked To Carry Nine Numbers And Who Is Not Going To Carry Anything For Anyone Ever Again And Who Says So Before He Is Asked To, A Woman Of About Fifty-Two Who Says In About Nine Seconds That She Does Not Know Whether The Ninth Room Will Be Asked Again, And A Fortnight That Ends With A Number Of Doors In It And Nobody Going To Be Sent To Any Of Them

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is a man who was going to offer and did not, and a sentence about doors and hands, and a fortnight that stops in the middle of itself.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Friday and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the eighth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road, four of them and one behind the three, are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and fifteen days, a hundred and seventy-three weeks and four days. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and nineteen days, a hundred and seventy-four weeks and one day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and eleven days, a hundred and thirty weeks and one day. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and five, a hundred and forty-three weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and forty-eight days, a hundred and twenty-one weeks and one day.

The separation is five hundred and ninety-five days, eighty-five weeks to the day, and about twelve weeks ago it was shut in a one-line box in a form, and it has been walked every day since and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**Rafi Pell came in at about eleven with a moped chain in a carrier bag and put the bag on the counter and did not open it.**

"**That's not the chain.**"

"**It's a chain,**" said Rafi Pell, who is about twenty-two and is a courier and a mechanic and is in a worker co-operative of about eleven people who own their own hours. "**It's the chain from a moped that isn't mine and the moped is my friend's and the friend is me, and I'm not going to be told about the chain, I'm going to be given a cup of tea.**"

"**There's no tea.**"

"**There's never any tea,**" he said, **"and I've been coming to this counter since I was nineteen and I've never once had a cup of tea here and I've never once stopped mentioning it.**"

---

**He had come to say a thing and he said it before anybody got round to asking him, which is what he does.**

"**Before anybody says anything,**" said Rafi Pell, **"I'm not going to ask you to hold anything.**"

"**Right.**"

"**I've had it in my pocket since Tuesday and I'm giving it back,**" he said, **"and the reason I'm giving it back in words and not just leaving it is that I have watched a person in this city turn into the middle of something four separate times since I was nineteen and every single time somebody was decent about it first.**"

"**That's four more than I'd have given myself.**"

"**It's three,**" said Rafi Pell. "**And you were not there for the fourth, and I'll take that off you, and now you know what I know, which is that you would have said yes to a woman with a folder inside about four seconds and she would have had you for four weeks and you would not have got out of it with your hands clean.**"

---

**And the reason he gave it back, he said in about four seconds, standing at a counter with a bag of chain in it.**

"**Because a person who holds nine numbers is a person the numbers come back to,**" he said, **"and I have been the person things come back to in a co-operative of eleven people who own their own hours and I know what that does to you. You stop being somebody's repair man and you become the place. And then the second thing is worse, and the second thing is that a person who holds nine numbers has to be able to say what the ninth one was, and you can't, because a woman of about twenty-four told a room about a word on a piece of paper and not what the word was, and she is not going to tell you, and she was right not to.**"

He put the bag of chain on the counter.

"**That's it. I've said it. Don't be pleased with me, it's not a favour, I just don't want to be the reason.**"

"**I'm not pleased,**" Marek said.

"**No,**" said Rafi Pell. "**You didn't say yes either. That's the whole of the news and it's about four seconds and it's the best Friday I've had in about a month.**"

---

**The woman of about fifty-two came in at about half past four and did not sit down and did not take her coat off.**

"**Two things and then I'm going,**" she said. "**One: the ninth room will be asked again in about four weeks or it won't, and I don't know which, and I'm not going to decide it now and have everybody in nine buildings deciding it from me.**"

"**That sounds like a rule.**"

"**It is not a rule,**" she said. "**A rule is a thing you can point at. That is a Wednesday. Most things in this city are Wednesdays and there are about nine of them a fortnight and nobody has ever written one of them down and that is why none of them can be blamed for anything.**"

"**And the other thing.**"

"**More doors than hands,**" she said. "**Two thousand and something doors and about nine hundred and thirty hands and nobody in this city is going to be sent to find the ones that haven't got a hand in them, and I'm not going to be the person who asks for somebody to be sent, because a person who gets sent is a person with a route, and about four hundred people in this city already have one of those and it has not done them a single thing so far.**"

---

**Nobody at that counter asked her where either figure came from, and she did not offer, and it is on the page that she has never been asked for them and does not intend to be.**

**She was right about both numbers and she said them in about nine seconds, and she was wrong about one thing, which is that she said the doors outnumber the hands by more than double, and the two figures were said once on a Thursday afternoon at this counter and once on a Friday morning in a corridor and have not been added to anything since.** The bigger of the two has been in one woman's head for about nine years and the other has been in one man's since Saturday night and neither is on paper.

---

**There was no fifth run and there was no van.**

The shop next door has an awning and it is shut on a Friday and nothing was under it at about four o'clock, about half past four, or at ten. **A man of about fifty-eight printed a fourth run of a printed sheet in about three weeks and nobody has ordered a fifth, and a fifth will not happen until somebody orders one with money in it, and about nine hundred people in this city have one and nobody has paid him for a single sheet of the four he has given away.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Friday and none of them was on a form.** **Two thousand and something doors, said out loud in a repair shop by a woman who chairs about nine chairs and who has never given a figure in this city before.** A carrier bag with a moped chain in it that had not been handed over and was still on the counter at the shutter. **A woman's hand on that counter at about half past four, spread out flat, with nothing in it, and a plain band on the fourth finger and no mark on it.** And nine sheets of paper in a room in Crown Terrace that are not here and are not in anybody's bag and have not been added up.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Friday, to be a sheet that comes back.**

Not to be found. **To be handed back blank, which in this city is the correct outcome, and to be put on a table by a person who went out and came back and put it there.** He wanted to have been to nine buildings in a fortnight and to have come back through a letter slot with nothing on him and to be the ninth piece of paper on a table in a second floor in Crown Terrace, and nobody has offered and nobody is going to and he has never once in thirteen volumes been anything of the kind and he is not going to start on a Friday.

---

**And at about ten past six on the last Friday of the ten, with the shutter down, four seconds were spent on a question he did not ask. He did not ask for the ninth sheet.**

There are nine sheets on a table in a second floor in Crown Terrace. Eight have a number on them and one has a word on it, and none of them has a copy, and there is nowhere in this city that the ninth of them is written down. **He could have asked the woman of about twenty-four, in about four seconds on that counter, to bring him the ninth one to look at, and she would have said yes, because she carried nine of them down a stair once and has never been asked to do it twice and would not mind a third time for a person who did not ask her to carry anything.**

He did not ask. **The nine are hers and the ninth of them is hers and he did not ask to see it, and that is the fifth thing he has given up in about a fortnight that cost him about four seconds and nothing else, and he has not told anybody about any of the five.**

---

**The green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket stands at sixty-three lines and the tin on the table beside it at seventy-three with its lid on and a receipt in it used as a page marker that is not hers.** **Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room there is a place, and it has been empty for twelve weeks and three days, and nobody has asked her about it in twelve weeks and three days and she is not going to start.** **In the fourth of those four rooms, after half past six, one other person was in the chair the door is behind.** She was about thirty and she was still in it at about ten and nothing whatever was put to her. **What belongs to her is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room, and it did not come out.**
