# Chapter 664 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Room Up A Stair In Saltmarket At About A Quarter Past Four With About Eleven People In It And One Of Them Carrying A Bag, Fifty-Four Of Which Forty-Nine Given First In About Nine Seconds And On No Other Day Of This Fortnight, Nine Sheets Of Which Eight Carry A Number And One Carries A Word And Nobody Adds Them Up, A Line That Goes In And Is About A Piece Of Paper, And A Chair Against A Wall With Its Back To The Room

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a room up a stair in Saltmarket at about a quarter past four. This one is a number given first, nine sheets nobody sums, and a line going into a book about a piece of paper.**

---

Thirteen jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and thirteen people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the sixth time in eleven years.

**Four rooms off that service road and a fifth behind the three, all of them warm, and the figure for them is one thousand two hundred and six days, a hundred and seventy-two weeks and two days. A card on end in the rail by that door is four days beyond them: one thousand two hundred and ten days, a hundred and seventy-two weeks and six days, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and two days, a hundred and twenty-eight weeks and six days. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and ninety-six, a hundred and forty-two weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and thirty-nine, a hundred and nineteen weeks and six days.

The separation is five hundred and eighty-six days, eighty-three weeks and five days, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**At about a quarter past four a woman of about sixty opened a book with a green cover on a table in a room at the top of a stair in Saltmarket and gave the number before anything else, which is what she does.**

"**Fifty-four.**"

"**Of which?**" said the man of about sixty-one with a clipboard.

"**Forty-nine correspond.**" She said it the way a person reads a temperature. "**Up by one. It is always up by one. The five that do not correspond are the same five and I have never shown them to anybody and I am not going to start this afternoon.**"

Nobody asked her about the five.

**That is the only number given out loud in this city or in any other today. Nine days of the last ten carried no sitting and announced nothing, and this entry does not say what a sitting does when there was none.**

---

**About eleven people were in that room.**

A woman of about sixty who has held it nineteen years and is paid by nobody. A man of about sixty-one with one column of numbers on a clipboard that has never been totalled. **A woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs and who was there as a person and who said nothing at all in about an hour and a half, which is the fourth Wednesday running she has said nothing in this room.** A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse. A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm and has a list face up in front of her. Six others.

**And behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place, and it has been empty for eleven weeks and four days, and about five people in that room had noticed and not one of them said so.**

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room. It did not move. Nobody in that room moved it and nobody in this city has ever said who put it there.**

---

**A woman of about twenty-four came up that stair at about half past four with a bag in her hand, and she was not on a list, and she had not come before, and nobody had asked her to come.**

She said her name to a woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her and the woman of about twenty-nine did not look down, because it was not on the list, and said, **"Then you'll be the one with the sheets,**" and the woman of about twenty-four said yes and sat down.

Then she waited until the sixth item was finished, and stood up, and said to the woman of about sixty:

"**I counted the people and nobody counted the people.**"

The room did not go quiet. Rooms do not go quiet. **About four of the eleven of them stopped writing and the other seven carried on.**

"**Say that again for me,**" said the woman of about sixty.

"**Nine rooms asked nine people on Wednesday afternoon.**" The woman of about twenty-four had her bag on her knee with both hands on it. "**Eight of them wrote a number. One of them wrote a word. And in every one of the nine, the number that got written down is bigger than the number of people who were actually in the room when it was asked, and not by a bit, and in the one I was in it was out by eleven.**"

"**Out by eleven.**"

"**Out by eleven,**" she said, **"and I counted them myself because I am the person who counts, and I counted two people walk in behind me and go straight out again and a woman who was on the phone in the corridor and came in for the end of it. And nobody counted anything else except the number that got put on the piece of paper, and I don't think anybody lied. I think it is just what happens when you ask people to do a thing they have not done before.**"

Nobody in that room said anything for about eleven seconds.

---

**And the woman of about sixty turned the book over, and picked up the pen, and said not one word about it, and wrote one line in it.**

The line is this: **A printed sheet is going round this city with four steps on it and a box at the bottom, and the box has an answer in it before anybody fills it in.**

**She did not read it out. Nobody in that room asked to hear it read out. It is the shortest line in that book and it is the sixty-third, and the book had been on sixty-two lines for about nineteen years, and it had been on sixty-two lines at the start of that hour and it is on sixty-three at the end of it. The tin on the same table is on seventy-three and its lid did not come off and there is a receipt from a shop in it as a page marker and the receipt is not hers.**

---

**The nine sheets were on the table by then and they had been on it since about four o'clock and nobody had picked any of them up.**

Eight of them carry a number in a hand. **There is no name on any of the eight and no building and no street.** A woman of about twenty-nine with the list face up said that she had put them there herself, that they came up the stair in one envelope at about four o'clock, and that the envelope had nothing on it.

The ninth carries a single word.

**The word is *no*.** It is in the same kind of hand as the eight numbers, it is not bigger than they are, and there is no figure anywhere near it.

"**What does that mean.**" said the man of about thirty-eight with a warehouse.

"**I don't know,**" said the woman of about twenty-four, **"and I've had three days to decide what I think it means and I've come down to two. One of them is that somebody thought a number was a thing they had to be able to refuse, which is a very good reason and I am not going to argue anybody out of it. The other one is that somebody could not hold a pencil up in a room and wanted it recorded that they were there. And I can't tell you which, and neither can anybody in this room.**"

---

**"Are you going to add them up,**" said the man of about thirty-eight.

**"No,**" said the woman of about sixty, and she said it before he had finished asking, and then she said the rest of it in about nine seconds.

"**No, and I want to say why in this room while all of you can hear it, because I am the only person in this city who gives out a number and I am not going to have it said afterwards that I did not know. The only number this room gives out is the number this room gives out. Fifty-four, of which forty-nine correspond. That has been the arrangement for nineteen years and it is the whole of what this table is for.**" She put two fingers about an inch above the ninth sheet without touching it. "**Nine numbers on nine pieces of paper are not that number. If I add them up in this room then in about three weeks somebody in this city will say that this room counted something, and this room does not count that, and this room has never counted that, and I have not got a piece of paper to put it on and neither has anybody else, and that is not an oversight. That is the arrangement.**"

"**So they just sit there.**"

"**They just sit there,**" she said, **"and nobody writes them out a second time, and if they burn in about four years then they burn. And I'll tell you the true thing about all nine of them, which is that I have not read one and I am not going to.**"

She had read none of them. She was looking at the ninth with two fingers an inch above it and she did not turn it over.

---

**He was not in that room.**

**He has not gone up that stair since the Wednesday three weeks before last, which is about six weeks, and he did not go up it on this Wednesday, and there was nothing in that room he could have done and nobody would have spoken to him and nobody asked him for anything.**

He was at a counter in Lattice Ward from nine until six. **Thirteen dated jobs, thirteen callers, the last of them let in at ten past five, and about four minutes of that afternoon were a man of about thirty-four who wanted to know whether a sheet he had put in a wire cage would be emptied on a Wednesday, and was told by a person entitled to say it that it would be emptied whenever the man on the round came, which is a Tuesday sometimes and a Friday sometimes, and that the fourth of the four printed steps was correct about the emptying and wrong about the day.**

---

**At about six the woman of about twenty-four came down that stair and past that shop on the wide road with her bag, and she stopped at the counter, and he looked at her, and neither of them said one word about the ninth sheet.**

He knew there was one word on it. She had said one word in a room and had not said what it was, and he could have asked her in about four seconds, and it would have been a small fair ordinary question between two people who have both been in rooms this week.

**He did not ask. She carried nine pieces of paper down a stair once, one at a time, in a bag, nine hundred yards to a bus stop, and said at the top of that stair in about four seconds that if a room said no to her she would keep the paper and bring it back and put it on the table and would not go back in twice, and then said that it was not fair, it was just what she does. He was not going to be the person who asked her what the ninth one said so that he would know before anybody else.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that room or on that stair or at that counter. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.** The man of about thirty-eight with a warehouse was not thanked for asking whether the nine would be added up, and the woman of about twenty-four was not thanked for saying out loud that the numbers were bigger than the rooms.

---

**He noticed four things on that Wednesday and not one of them was on a form.** A pen that had been in that tin and came out of it and went back in a different hole in the block, **which nobody in that room saw, and which is the only reason there is a sixty-third line at all, and is not a discovery.** A receipt in that tin with a shop on it and a figure on it and the receipt is not hers. Nine sheets on a table, eight of them with a number on them. And a woman of about fifty-two who said nothing for an hour and a half and who put her hand flat on that table about ten minutes before the end and left it there.

---

**And at about ten past six, with the shutter down, four seconds went. He did not walk past the bottom of that stair.**

There is a shop on that corner that opens when it feels like it and it is not on the way to anywhere he goes. He went four hundred yards out of his way on the Tuesday and two hundred yards on this one. **About six people in this city saw him do it and about four of them told somebody, and a woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs heard about it on the Wednesday night, which she has never done about anything.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday, to be in the room when the book opened.**

He had wanted it since the Wednesday three weeks before last, and the reason is not that he thought he had earned it and not that he thought the line would be about him. **He wanted to be a person who was in a room when a thing was written down, once, for nineteen years, and he has not been in a room when anything was written down since he was about nineteen.**

**At about eight he was in the second of those four rooms and there was one other person in it, about thirty, sitting where the door is and still sitting where the door is at about ten.** Three and a half hours passed and not one person in that building put a word to her, and nobody ever will. **What belongs to her is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a back shelf of that room and it did not come out on that day or on any other day of this stretch.** Up that same stair a green-covered book went from sixty-two lines to sixty-three between a quarter past four and ten past six, and a tin on the table beside it is on seventy-three and its lid did not come off. **The place behind her chair was empty and has been empty for eleven weeks and four days and nobody in that room mentioned it.**
---

*Eight hundred and twelve and eight hundred and ninety-six are both whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and sixteen weeks to the day and a hundred and twenty-eight weeks to the day, and the first is a man against a north wall and the second is a question that has been standing in this city since the spring, and nine numbers on nine pieces of paper on one table are neither of those figures.*

*667. Wednesday of week 240, at ten, and the fourteenth day of this stretch of days, and the forty-ninth sitting. Thirteen dated jobs and thirteen names, the last let in at ten past five. Fifty-four of which forty-nine correspond, said first, in about nine seconds, once, and on no other day of this fortnight. About eleven people in a room up a stair in Saltmarket, a ninth chair against a wall with its back to the room, and a place behind the woman's chair empty eleven weeks and four days. A woman of about twenty-four came up the stair with a bag and was not on a list and said, to a face, that she had counted the people and nobody had counted the people, and that in every one of nine rooms the number written down was bigger than the number of people in the room, and out by eleven in hers. The book opened and one line went in and the line is about a printed sheet with four steps on it and a box at the bottom, and it was not read out and nobody asked to hear it read out. Nine sheets on that table, eight with a number and one with the single word *no*. Nobody added them up. The only figure given out in that room was fifty-four of which forty-nine. A man of twenty-two was at a counter in this city and did not go up that stair. Nobody thanked anybody.*

Refusals: four, one a request for a copy of a figure somebody had been given in that room, which is not a thing in this city and cannot be supplied, and one a man of about thirty-four who wanted to know which day a wire cage is emptied, which was answered in four seconds by a person entitled to answer it and was told the emptying is correct and the day is not fixed. *Fifty-four of which forty-nine, a sixty-third line, and nine sheets nobody sums.* **The forty-ninth sitting in nineteen years was held at the top of a stair in Saltmarket on a Wednesday afternoon with about eleven people in the room. The count was given first, before anything else, in about nine seconds and once and on no other day of this fortnight: fifty-four, of which forty-nine correspond, up by one as it always is, with five that do not correspond and are the same five and have never been shown to anybody. A woman of about twenty-four who does ironing three days a week on a wide road came up that stair with a bag and said one thing to the face of the woman who holds that room, and the thing she said was that she had counted the people and nobody had counted the people: nine rooms had asked a question on Wednesday afternoon, eight of them had written down a number, one had written down a word, and in all nine the number was larger than the number of people actually in the room, and in hers it was out by eleven. The book opened and one line went into it, and the line is about a printed sheet with four steps on it and a box at the bottom with an answer already in it, and it was not read out and nobody asked for it to be read. She said in the same breath that she had not read one of the nine and was not going to. A man of twenty-two was at a counter four hundred yards from the bottom of that stair and has not been up it in about six weeks. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.**

*What the Wednesday did, and what it left alone.* **For the first time in nineteen years a line went into a green-covered book about a piece of paper, and the piece of paper is the most ordinary object in this city, and the line is the shortest thing in that book, and the woman who wrote it has said nothing about it and will say nothing about it to anybody for as long as she lives.** The register of correct acts with no consequence stands at two and was not moved: a woman counting the people as they come through a door is a person finding out a thing and saying it out loud in a room, which has consequences on about nine people who wrote numbers, and the two acts already on that register were not touched by an inch of this day. The count of hands in nine rooms is not a count of this city and about four hundred people are not standing in any of the nine rooms, and neither of those facts was consoled this afternoon and neither was said out loud. Seven objects are named on this page and this one does not bring any two of them together: a green-covered book, a tin with a receipt in it used as a page marker, a printed sheet with a box ruled at the bottom, a hand copy of the front of a page, a card on end in a rail of two, a wire cage on a round-the-side, and a plate of iron with four slots. Under that bench is a drawer with four sheets in it and it is shut, and the fourth of the four has never been looked at by anybody. **He was not in that room, and at about eight he was in the second of those four rooms and there was one other person in it.** She was about thirty and she was sitting where the door is and she was still sitting where the door is at about ten. Nobody in that building put a word to her at any point in the three and a half hours and nobody ever will. **Her page is the eighth of eight, it is in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room, and the binder did not come out.**
