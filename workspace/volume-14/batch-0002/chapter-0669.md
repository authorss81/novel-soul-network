# Chapter 669 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Man Of About Forty-Nine With A Folder Who Wants A Number And Will Use It In About Four Weeks, A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Says In About Four Seconds That He Has Not Got It And Who Is Right, A Sheet In That Folder With A Box Ruled At The Bottom Of It And Nothing In It, And Two Figures That He Has And Does Not Say

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six. This one is a man who wants a number, and the only man in this city who has it has refused to write it down and is not going to say it.**

---

Twelve people came over that counter on the Thursday and every one of them had a date against a job, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten with the wind behind it.

**Those four rooms off that service road are one thousand two hundred and fourteen days, a hundred and seventy-three weeks and three days, four of them and one behind the three, all of them warm. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and eighteen days, which is a hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day, and that is four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and ten days, a hundred and thirty weeks to the day. The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and four, a hundred and forty-three weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and forty-seven days, a hundred and twenty-one weeks to the day.

The separation is five hundred and ninety-four days, eighty-four weeks and six days, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**He had the folder under his arm and he had it the way a man carries something he has been carrying a long time, with the bottom edge gone soft.**

"**Two minutes,**" he said, **"and I'm not opening it and it's not the one I had before and you'll want to ask me that so I'm telling you.**"

"**It's not the one you had before,**" Marek said.

"**No,**" he said, **"and it's the same folder, and those are two different sentences and I've had about four years of people confusing them.**"

---

**He asked for the number of doors in the first nine seconds.**

"**How many cages are on the round-the-sides in this city,**" he said. "**Not how many leaflets. How many cages. There's a difference and the difference is about four weeks and by the end of next month the difference is going to be the whole of my report.**"

"**Who is it for.**"

"**For a paper,**" he said, **"that goes to four people who cannot be asked in a room, and I'm not going to tell you their names and you wouldn't remember them. The paper goes up in about four weeks. I want the number before it goes up because a number I've found myself is worth about nine times a number I've been given.**"

"**Have you got one.**"

"**I've got two,**" he said, **"and both of them are mine, and one of them came off a list of premises and one of them came out of a man who empties them, and I'd rather have the second one than the first one and I've not been able to get hold of the second one.**"

---

**And Marek said no in about four seconds, and it was true, and it was the right answer, and it took him four seconds to work out why it was the right answer.**

"**I haven't got it,**" he said.

"**You haven't—**

"**I've not got it,**" he said. "**I heard a figure on a Thursday afternoon at this counter from a person I have not seen since and I did not write it down, and I did not write it down on purpose, and if you'd asked me about it on Friday I'd have given it to you in about four seconds and I'd have been wrong about that in about three weeks.**"

"**You heard it.**

"**I heard it and I did not keep it,**" he said, **"and that's the whole of my answer and I'm not going to soften it for you, because you are going to go and use a number in about four weeks and the number you use is going to be the number you found yourself, and you just told me that's worth nine times one you were given, and that is exactly why I'm not going to hand you one over this counter for nothing.**"

---

**The man of about forty-nine put the folder down on the counter and did not open it and stood there for about nine seconds.**

"**You're the fourth person who's said no to me this week,**" he said, **"and two of them said it for money reasons and one said it because he's right and one said it because it's his. And I can tell the difference and I'm not going to pretend I can't.**"

"**Which one am I.**"

"**That one's yours to know,**" he said.

He picked the folder up. **He did not open it, and about four minutes later at about half past four he came in again for a washer he had left on the bench, and he put it on the counter, and Marek put it in a bag, and the man of about forty-nine said, without opening the folder, that the fourth step on the printed sheet was correct about the emptying and wrong about the day, and that a man who empties a cage on a round does not empty it on a Wednesday, and that whoever wrote it knew that, and then he left with the washer and did not say what his two numbers were.**

---

**A sheet in that folder has a box ruled at the bottom of it and the box is empty.**

Marek did not see that and did not know that and nothing in this entry is a thing he did. **A man of about forty-nine carried a sheet out of that shop in a folder with the bottom edge gone soft, and on that sheet there is a box with a rule round it at the bottom and nothing in the box, and it is not a printed sheet with four steps on it and it does not come from this city, and nobody has asked him about it and he was not asked about it.**

---

**The sixth of the twelve jobs was a bell on the door of a shop on a wide road and it took about forty minutes because it was not the bell that was the job.**

The man who wanted it is deaf in his left ear and has been for about nine years and has never told anybody in that shop, and he said so at about four seconds, standing in the doorway, with his right hand flat on the frame.

"**It rings on both sides,**" he said. "**It's not loud enough on this side and I've stopped saying anything about it because there's nothing to say.**"

"**Where's the transformer.**"

"**That's the thing I'm asking you.**"

Marek went and found it above the door on the inside, on a bracket that has been painted over twice, and it is about nine inches from the door frame and the frame is brick and the bell is on the outside, and the whole arrangement has been put in by about four different tradesmen over about nine years, none of whom asked a man with his hand on a door frame which ear he could hear with.

He took the bell off the outside, moved the bracket to the inside corner of the door, put the bell back on the outside of that bracket so it is now about four inches from the frame, and reconnected it, and it rang on the man's side and it rang louder and it did not cost anything extra because there was nothing extra to charge for.

"**That's better,**" said the man.

"**That's the same bell,**" Marek said. "**It was in the wrong place for nine years and about four men never noticed and one of them was me and I've only just seen it.**"

The man paid eight pounds and went out and did not say thank you and was not thanked, and Marek did not tell him that he had been deaf for nine years and had told nobody, because that was not a thing anybody had asked him about.

---

**On that Thursday he noticed four things, and none of them was on a form.** The bottom edge of a folder gone soft on the side that sits against a body. **A washer left on a bench at about half past four and picked up at about half past four, by a man who had come in and out twice in a day for one washer and one conversation, and who had not needed either.** And a way of standing at a counter that he has seen in four people in this city and that he recognises from himself about three years ago. And **two figures he has, one of them in his head from a Thursday afternoon and one of them from a Friday morning in a corridor, and he has said neither out loud since, and there is no reason in this city that is better than that.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday, to be the person a number gets checked against.**

That is a small thing and it is the whole of what he wanted and nobody in this city could have given it to him. **A man of about forty-eight walked into a repair shop on a wide road and said, are these the right number, and a man of twenty-two said yes, and went back to work, and about four people in the room believed him.** He would have been the person who holds the figure and who says whether a thing somebody else counted is right, and he does not hold it, and the reason he does not hold it is his own decision and he made it in about four seconds on a Thursday afternoon of the previous week, and he has not regretted it and he would not change it and both of those are true at the same time.

---

**And about four seconds at ten past six, with the shutter down, went the other way. He did not say one hundred and one.**

The man of about forty-nine asked about cages and got nothing. **The other figure is the one that a man of about thirty-eight added up in his head on a stair on Wednesday night and has not written down and came down to a shop on Thursday morning specifically to be rid of it, and it went into this shop's ear and nowhere else, and the man of about forty-nine did not ask about it and Marek did not offer it, and the number of people who were asked a question in nine rooms in this city is now on nothing at all.**

That is the first time in about five months that he has had a number in his head and a person in front of him who wanted one and has refused to give it, and it was not a rule and it was not a performance and nobody in this city is going to hear about it.

---

**A green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket is on sixty-three lines and a tin beside it on seventy-three, and there is a place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room which has been empty for twelve weeks and two days, and nobody has said a word to her about it.** **One other person was in the fourth of those four rooms after half past six.** She was about thirty, she was in the chair the door is behind, and she was still in it at about ten, and nothing whatever was put to her in three and a half hours. **What belongs to her is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room, and it did not come out.**
