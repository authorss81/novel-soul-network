# Chapter 661 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Printed Sheet With Nothing At All In The Box At The Bottom Of It, A Man Of About Forty-One In A District Jacket Who Was Told In Four Seconds That Nobody Knows, Four Boxes Under An Awning And One Of Them Opened And Counted And Not Written Down

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is a printed sheet with nothing at all in the box at the bottom of it, four boxes under an awning, and a man of about fifty-eight who set none of the type.**

---

Ten jobs carried a date against each on the Thursday and ten people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the fifth time in eleven years.

**Warm, all four of them, with the fifth room behind the three on that service road, and the figure for those rooms is one thousand two hundred days — a hundred and seventy-one weeks and three days. A card stands on end in the rail by the door and it is four days past them and the rail takes two: one thousand two hundred and four days, which is a hundred and seventy-two weeks to the day.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and ninety-six days, a hundred and twenty-eight weeks to the day. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and ninety, a hundred and forty-one weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and thirty-three, a hundred and nineteen weeks to the day.

The separation is five hundred and eighty days, eighty-two weeks and six days. It went into a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and it has been walked every day since and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**He came in at about twenty past nine with a district jacket on over a shirt with a collar, and he put the sheet down flat, and he put one finger on the box at the bottom of it and left it there.**

"**That one's empty,**" he said.

"**I can see that.**

"**The others aren't.**"

He said it the way a man says a thing he has already said to four people this week. **The sheet on the counter was the same sheet. Four steps, a paragraph, a small drawing in the corner of a bracket and a pump and a bus door, and a box with a rule round it, and the box had nothing in it at all.** Not a tick, not a figure, not a strike-through, not a mark of any kind. The paper in the box was the same colour as the paper above it.

"**Where did you get it.**"

"**Out of the cage on the round-the-side.**" He took his hand off it and then put it back, which is the same thing a man did at that counter about a week and a half ago with a different sheet. "**There's one on that corner. There's about nine hundred of them come out of that cage in about four weeks and I have put my hand in it every one of those days.**"

"**And the others have something in the box.**"

"**Most of them have something in the box.**"

"**How many don't.**"

"**I didn't count.**" He said that quickly and then said it again more slowly, as though the second one might be better. "**I didn't count them and I'm not going to, because if I count them I'll have a number and then somebody'll ask me what it's a number of.**"

---

**Marek looked at the sheet for about nine seconds and then gave him the whole of it in four.**

"**I can't tell you what an empty one means and I'm not going to guess at a counter,**" he said, **"and I'll tell you why I'm not going to guess, because you're going to go and tell somebody I guessed. If I guess then you've got a man's opinion at a counter and it's in your coat by lunchtime. If I say I don't know, then you've got a man at a counter who didn't know, and that's worth more to you in about three weeks than anything I could have made up for you.**"

"**That's not an answer.**"

"**No,**" he said. **"It's the absence of one and I'm giving it to you free.**"

The man of about forty-one in the district jacket looked at the box at the bottom of the sheet for a while longer than he needed to.

"**The woman who does my building said it means the answer's been withdrawn.**"

"**She said that to you.**"

"**She said it in a corridor on Tuesday.**"

"**Then it's hers,**" Marek said, **"and I'm not going to sit here and take it off her in about nine seconds on a Thursday because I've had a different half of it. I don't know which half she's got.**"

---

**He did not ask which cage.**

That is on the page and it is about four seconds of a Thursday morning, and the reason is that a man who says *that corner* has told you a street, and a street is a thing you can put in a form, and a form is a thing a person can be found by, and about four weeks ago he watched a woman of about twenty-four carry nine pieces of paper down a stair and say out loud on a table in a second floor that she did not want to be a person who carried things.

He said instead: "**It's the same sheet.**"

"**Is it.**"

"**Same four steps. Same paragraph. Same box.**" He turned it a little so the light came across it. "**If it's not the same sheet then I've got nothing, and it is the same sheet, and the only difference on this one is that whoever put it there didn't.**"

"**That's worse.**"

"**That's much worse,**" he said, **"and it is the same sheet, and I have said two true things to you at a counter and neither one helps.**"

---

**The seventh of the ten jobs that day was a door closer on a flat door that has to be taken off its hinges to get at, and it took about nine minutes because the man who brought it had to go and find the screws, and while he was gone the man of about forty-one put the sheet back in his inside pocket and said one more thing at the counter that nobody had asked him for.**

"**There's a woman on that road who's put about four hundred of them in her front garden in a wheelie bin,**" he said. "**I've seen it from the van. It's not hidden. It's a wheelie bin with a lid and it's about four foot high.**"

"**Did you tell her.**"

"**No,**" he said. **"Because she'd want to know what to do with them and I've got nothing to tell her and she'd have to go and ask somebody and she's the last person in that street anybody should send to ask.**"

Then he went and found his screws.

---

**At about four o'clock a grey unmarked van came up the wide road and stopped opposite, and a man of about thirty-one who is not from this city got out and put four cardboard boxes under the awning of the shop next door, which is shut on a Thursday, and stacked them two and two, and got back in and went.**

Nobody asked him to. Nobody saw him do it except the man at the counter and a woman of about forty-five who was waiting for a bus and who has not been in any of these four weeks.

**The four boxes were under that awning for about an hour and a half and then Marek carried them in, one at a time, and put them under the bench at the back of the shop, and the drawer under that bench was shut and stayed shut and is a different thing from these four boxes and is not that drawer.**

---

**He opened one of them at about half past five.**

That is the whole of what happened between half past five and ten past six on that Thursday and it took nine seconds to count and about four minutes to decide to, and there was nobody in the shop. **Four hundred and forty sheets, printed two sides, in three reams and a part of a ream, and he counted them twice because the first time he got nine hundred and something and he wanted to be wrong about the nine hundred.**

Then he shut the box, and put the tape back the way it had been, and wrote nothing down, and there is no form in this city with a column for the number of sheets in somebody else's box.

**Four hundred and forty is a number one man keeps. It is not a number anybody has taken, it is not a count, and it is not going to be in any sentence in this city, and if a man quotes it in about three weeks he will be quoting a man who opened a box in a shop and counted and put the tape back.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Thursday and none of them was on a form.** A wire cage on a round-the-side that has a rounded top and a hinged flap with a rubber band round the hinge, and the band has been replaced about twice and both times with the same kind of band. **Four cardboard boxes of a height and a width that fits under a bench, and one of them had a scuff on the top face about the size of a coin, and the scuff is on the box and not on anything that was inside it.** A man who empties those cages and does not count what is in them, which was in the first half of this entry in a district jacket and was not counted. **And a sheet of paper in a wheelie bin in a front garden about four foot high with a lid on it, on a road he has driven down about nine times, which is the same sheet and which nobody in that street has been asked about.**

---

**And one small thing he gave up at about ten past six with the shutter down, in about four seconds. He did not take a sheet out of the box to keep.**

He has a strip of paper off a form in a fourth pocket in his tool roll with glue residue on one edge of it, and he has never written down what is on the fourth thing in that pocket, and a fourth box of printed paper would have gone in beside it in about two seconds. **A man with a paragraph in a drawer is a man with a copy** is a sentence he heard at a doorway nine hundred yards from here on Wednesday from a man of about fifty-eight, and he did not have a paragraph in a drawer on Thursday and he could have had four hundred and forty of them in one and he left them shut.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday, to be the person the fourth run was printed for.**

There is no such person and there has never been one. The sheet was ordered by somebody in a town four days of bus travel there and back, the money was handed over in a fold of paper on a Monday, and the printer was given a chase with nine ruled lines in it and nothing in it. **He wanted to be the reason about four hundred and forty of them exist, and the reason is that a man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse said a true thing in a room about four weeks ago and a woman of about fifty-two did not stop him and about four hundred people were handed one, and he was in the fourth room and was asked nothing and has been given nothing.**

He did not say any of it. He said, to the empty shop, at about ten past six: "**Right,**" and shut the shutter, and it was the fourth time in eleven years and it shook the cage.

---

**After half past six the fourth of those four rooms had one other person in it.** She was about thirty and she was in the chair the door is behind and she was still in it at about ten, and in three and a half hours not one person in that building put a word to her. **What belongs to her is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it did not come out, and nobody has ever opened that binder in her presence or out of it.** Up a stair in Saltmarket a green-covered book stood at sixty-two lines at the start of this day and is on sixty-two lines now, and the tin on the table beside it is on seventy-three with its lid on. **Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room there is a place that has been empty for eleven weeks and one day.**
---

*One thousand two hundred and four and five hundred and eighty stand on opposite sides of a whole number of weeks, and the first is a card on end in a rail of two and the second is a separation that ended in a form about twelve weeks ago, and four hundred and forty sheets in a box under a bench are neither of those figures and have no series at all.*

*664. Thursday of week 239, at ten, and the eleventh day of this stretch of days. Ten dated jobs and ten names, the last let in at ten past five. A printed sheet came over a counter at twenty past nine with nothing whatever in the box ruled at the bottom of it, and a man of about forty-one in a district jacket had taken it out of a wire cage on a round-the-side and had not counted how many came out of that cage in about four weeks and would not, because a man with a number is a man who is asked what the number is of. He was told in four seconds that nobody knows what an empty box means, and he was told why nobody was going to guess, and he was told that the woman who said it in a corridor on a Tuesday owns that half of it and is not going to be corrected at a counter on a Thursday by a man who has been given the other half. About nine hundred of the same sheet came out of one cage and a man put his hand in it every day for four weeks. A grey van put four cardboard boxes under an awning opposite at four o'clock and did not stop. One box of four was opened at half past five and counted twice and shut and taped and not written down. Nobody thanked anybody.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Thursday carried ten dated jobs and ten callers, the last admitted at ten past five, and about nine minutes of the middle of the day were the seventh job on the list, which was a door closer.** Callers: ten. Refusals: two, one a request to be told what an empty box at the bottom of a printed sheet means, refused in four seconds and not by anybody's rules, and one a request for a figure of how many sheets have come out of one cage on one street, which does not exist because the man who could have counted it declined to. Work: ten, two declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: twelve pounds forty, exact, and the seven-and-a-half of it is a door closer and a pack of screws and the man who brought it went back for the screws. The heating in that flat is above its winter setting for a ninth month, and four names on that one tenancy make up the difference. Those rooms: one thousand two hundred days, a hundred and seventy-one weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, warm. Rail card: one thousand two hundred and four days, a hundred and seventy-two weeks to the day, four days past the room, rail of two. The board on two nails: twentieth, none, and never begun; twelfth, one thousand one hundred and twenty, a hundred and sixty weeks to the day; thirteenth, one thousand and seventy-one, a hundred and fifty-three weeks to the day; fourteenth, one thousand and thirty-six, a hundred and forty-eight weeks to the day; fifteenth, one thousand and fifteen, a hundred and forty-five weeks to the day; sixteenth, nine hundred and ninety, a hundred and forty-one weeks and three days; seventeenth, nine hundred and seventy-two, a hundred and thirty-eight weeks and six days; eighteenth, nine hundred and eighteen, a hundred and thirty-one weeks and one day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall: eight hundred and six days, a hundred and fifteen weeks and one day. The ask: eight hundred and ninety days, a hundred and twenty-seven weeks and one day. The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns: eight hundred and thirty-three days, a hundred and nineteen weeks to the day. The post at the corridor end: seven hundred and forty-eight days, a hundred and six weeks and six days. The nine hand copies: seven hundred and sixty-six days, a hundred and nine weeks and three days, eight of the nine unfinished and the first row nobody has disagreed about still not found, and not one of the nine laid against another on any day of this fortnight. The separation: five hundred and eighty days, eighty-two weeks and six days, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and subtracted every day since, no shorter, nothing put back into it.*

*An empty box, and a tape put back.* **Four of the four steps on a printed sheet are correct and have been correct in four districts for about four years, and the fifth thing on the page is a box ruled at the bottom with a line above it saying what the last approved answer was for that street, and on about four hundred of the sheets a mark is already in that box in a hand that the person carrying it cannot account for. On about four hundred of the fourth run the mark is not there, and the paper inside the rule is the same colour as the paper above it, and nobody in this city can say whether an empty box is an answer being withdrawn or a run that was printed from a chase somebody else had filled in, and the printer of those sheets set none of the type and is not going to be asked to say who did. A man of about forty-one in a district jacket has put his hand in one wire cage on a round-the-side every day for about four weeks and has about nine hundred sheets coming out of it, and he has not counted how many have an empty box and has said out loud that he is not going to, on the grounds that a man with a number is a man who is asked what the number is of. He was told at a counter that nobody knows, and why nobody was going to guess, and that the woman who told him in a corridor on a Tuesday owns that half of the answer and would not be corrected on a Thursday by a man at a counter with the other half. A grey unmarked van left four cardboard boxes under the awning of a shop next door that is shut on a Thursday, two and two, and did not stop, and one of the four was opened at half past five and holds four hundred and forty sheets and was counted twice and taped shut again. That number is one man keeping it and it is on nothing. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped, and a man at a counter was told the absence of an answer and was not comforted and did not ask to be.**

*What the Thursday did, and what it left alone.* **A third of the printed sheet in this city now has nothing at the bottom of it and the nothing has a rule drawn round it, which is the first time in about four weeks that a thing has been published that somebody could be harmed by and nobody has asked anybody to stop. The register of correct acts with no consequence stands where it stood, and this Thursday is not a third instance of it: a man who takes a sheet out of a cage every day for four weeks and refuses to count it has declined to be used and has had nothing done to him and nothing done for him, and the two acts already on that register were not touched by an inch of this day.** Five things are named here and this page does not set any two of them beside one another: a printed sheet with an empty box under it, a wire cage on a round-the-side, four cardboard boxes under an awning, a card on end in a rail of two, and a hand copy of the front of a page. Under that bench there is a drawer, and it is shut, and it holds four sheets, and nobody has ever looked at the fourth, and four cardboard boxes are a different kind of thing entirely and no sentence in this entry sets them beside that drawer. A green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket is on sixty-two lines and the tin beside it is on seventy-three and the two figures are not convertible and the difference between them is not a number. A place behind a chair has been empty eleven weeks and one day. After half past six one other person sat in the chair the door is behind until about ten and nothing whatever was put to her, and what is hers is the eighth of eight in a ring binder at the back of a shelf in that room and did not come out.
