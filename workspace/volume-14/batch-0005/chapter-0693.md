# Chapter 693 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Room In A Second District Where Somebody Asked The Merger Question And Was Refused, And A Room A Day And A Night Of Bus Travel Away That Somebody Else Asked, And A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Asked A Thing First So That He Could Not Have Agreed To The Answer

**Two rooms, and one of them two thousand miles from the other. Bench until two, counter until about six, four rooms off that service road from half past six, and the whole of that is ordinary Thursday: the shape of this one is a question about that got no answer, and two sentences that are not the same shape as anything.**

---

Nine jobs carried a date against each on the Thursday and nine people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the thirtieth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand two hundred and seventy days, a hundred and eighty-one weeks and three days, four of them and one behind the three, warm. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and seventy-four days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks to the day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and sixty-six days, a hundred and thirty-eight weeks to the day.
The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and sixty, a hundred and fifty-one weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and three, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks to the day.

The separation is six hundred and fifty days, ninety-two weeks and six days, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, walked every day since, not shorter, nothing put back into it.

---

**He was in the fourth of the nine rooms at about ten and it is on the first floor above a firm that takes clothes in, and there are two people in it and the one who spoke was a man of about thirty-four.**

He said the sentence was going to come first, and the man of about thirty-four said no it was not, and said why.

"**Because I want to ask you something first and if I ask it second I'll have agreed to the answer.**"

"**Ask.**"

"**Have the other eight merged.**"

---

**Marek said no in about four seconds and he said it as a refusal and it was a refusal, and then he said the second thing and the second thing is the part of it that is on this page.**

"**No, and I am not going to soften that, and I am also not going to tell you why not, and both halves of that are true at once and you are entitled to be annoyed about it.**"

"**You are not going to tell me why.**"

"**If I tell you why then you have a reason and the reason is about them and not about you and in about a fortnight you will be in a room telling two people who did not ask, and they will ask me, and I will have to say no to three instead of one.**"

The man of about thirty-four looked at the door for a while.

"**That is a lawyer's answer.**"

"**That is an answer,**" Marek said.
"**I have got nine of them and I am going to give nine of the same one and it is not going to be a good one.**"

---

**And then the room gave its sentence, and it gave it as though the question had not been asked, and nobody in that room treated the question as having been answered.**

"**I will speak to the person it came from,**" the man of about thirty-four said, "**and I will tell them it arrived, and I will not tell them what I did with it.**"

"**That is your sentence.**"

"**That is my sentence,**" he said.
"**And it is about me and not about room, and I know that, and you have got eight more and one of them is a question and one of them is about Friday.**"

"**You have got three,**" Marek said, "**and you have not got the other six, and I am not going to tell you what I have got and I am not going to tell you what I have not got.**"

"**Then what is the point of the sentence.**"

"**The point of the sentence is that it is yours,**" Marek said, "**and if nine rooms end up with nine sentences then there is one thing in this city that nine rooms have said in the same week, and that is the whole of what anybody authorised and I did not authorise any of it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and then a woman of about twenty-six in the corner of that room said, "**That's the best answer he's had all week,**" and the man of about thirty-four said, "**It isn't an answer,**" and she said, "**No,**" and that was all three of them said.

---

**The fifth of the nine rooms is a day and a night of bus travel away and Marek did not go and nobody in this city sent anybody to find out whether it had been asked, and it had, and the person who asked it was a woman of about thirty-six who came down for the day on an earlier bus and went back on a later one.**

She is not in this city and she is not paid by this city and she was in that room for about eleven minutes.

"**One sentence,**" she said.
"**And I want to say first that I have not been asked to ask you this and I am asking you because a man of twenty-two handed me a card with a room on it in a car park and said go and ask, and that is all he said, and he did not say why, and I did not ask him why, and I came back on the bus and he was not on it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Anything that comes here goes back out of the same door it came in,**" she said, "**and the same person that brought it in takes it out.**"

"**And if the same person has stopped.**"

"**Then it waits,**" she said, "**and that has happened twice since the spring and both times it waited nine days and both times it went.**"

"**Who takes it out the second time.**"

"**The same one,**" she said.
"**There is one person in that room and when she is there she takes it out and when she is not there nothing goes out and nothing comes in, and nobody has ever asked her to be there on a Friday and I am not going to ask her because if I ask her then I am a person who has put her on a round.**"

---

**The bus back was at about half past four and she got it and went, and he did not go and stand near her and has not seen her since.**

**There is now no route in this city by which that room can be asked anything in a week, and there is a woman of about thirty-six who has been once and got the answer in eleven minutes, and a room a day and a night away that has said one sentence and will not say another, and no arrangement anywhere in this city for either of those facts reaching anybody who could do something with them, and nobody is going to build one.**

---

**Nobody in this city knows what that room's sentence is except a woman of about thirty-six who came back on a bus and a woman who is in that room, and neither of them has said a word about anybody, and Marek heard it and has not asked a fourth person anything about where any of the other four are.**

He has got five now, and there are four he has not asked, and one of those four is a day and a night away and the only reason it was asked at all this week is that he gave a card to a woman in a car park and said go and ask and gave no reason. **He asked her what the room said and she told him, and that is the first time in this whole stretch of days that he deliberately got less out of a piece of paper than was on it, because he did not ask where the card came from.**

---

**The counter ran the rest of the day and none of it was about any of that.**

At about half past one a man of about twenty-two brought in a leg to a chair and said he would rather not pay for the other one, and Marek said he would have to pay for all of it or take it away as it was, and the man took it away as it was and came back on the Friday, and paid for all of it, and said nothing about any of it.

At about half past three a woman of about forty-four asked whether a room could be asked a question by somebody who was not going to be told the answer, and was told in four seconds that it could, and that this was the whole arrangement and it had been the whole arrangement for nine weeks.

**At about ten past four a man of about thirty-six came in and asked whether it was possible to be in two rooms in two districts on the same morning, and Marek said that it was, and that he had done it, and that the day after that he had been in two rooms about a hundred miles apart and had not been in either of them.**

"**That's not the same thing.**"

"**It is the same thing with less of it,**" Marek said.
"**You get up early enough and the roads are what the roads are and you can do it on your feet. What you cannot do is do it and be useful in both. That's the part nobody has written down and I have had it written down about four times in my own head and never once on a page.**"

The man of about thirty-six said that was not what he meant and Marek said he knew it was not, and the man paid for nine feet of conduit and went.

**And that is the honest answer to that question and it is going to be the reason there are fewer than nine of these and not more, and the ones that will be missing are the ones that would have had to be asked by somebody who could have been in two rooms and been useful in both, and there is nobody in this city, including him, who is that person.**

At about five a man of about twenty-nine wanted to know whether two men who had never met could be called colleagues on a form, and was told no, and did not like it, and paid for about nine foot of chain anyway.

---

**He noticed four things on that Thursday and not one of them was on a form.** A door in a second-district room propped open with a folded invoice that had been folded at the same line twice before.
**A bus timetable in a town a day and a night away with one corner of it torn and the torn corner not recent.** Two rooms on the same Thursday about two thousand miles apart, and a woman who went to one of them because a man handed her a card in a car park and did not tell her why.
And a man of about thirty-four who asked a question and did not get an answer and then gave his sentence anyway, and did not appear to connect the two.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday, to be in two rooms at once, and the want was not about the sentences.**

He wanted it because a card in a car park is a thing he can hand out, and a thing he can hand out is a thing he can do twice, and twice would have meant eleven and not nine, and **he handed one card to one woman and went back to the shop, and the cost of that decision is nine words and eleven minutes on a bus somebody else paid for.**

He said none of it.
Nine dated jobs and nine names, the last let in at ten past five, the shutter at ten, and then the account of a bus that went at half past four while his went at five.

---

**From half past six until ten there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road and she is about thirty and she is in the chair the door is behind for three and a half hours and nothing whatever was put to her and nobody has ever said sorry to her for it in any form.**

The page that belongs to her is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it stayed where it was.
