# Chapter 700 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Half Past Six, A Stair In Saltmarket And A Room At The Top Of It With About Eleven People In It, Fifty-Seven Of Which Fifty-Two Given First And Once, A Book That Did Not Change, And A Place Behind A Chair That Has Been Empty Twenty-Four Weeks To The Day And About Which Nobody Asked

**A number given first, a room at the top of a stair with about eleven people in it, a book that stayed at sixty-four lines, and an empty place behind a chair that nobody asked about. The bench until two and the counter until about six happened first, and four rooms off that service road happened after, and neither of them is why this Wednesday is on the page.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at half past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the thirty-seventh time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand two hundred and ninety days, a hundred and eighty-four weeks and two days, four of them and one behind the three, warm. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and ninety-four days, a hundred and eighty-four weeks and six days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and eighty-six days, a hundred and forty weeks and six days.
The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and eighty, a hundred and fifty-four weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and twenty-three, a hundred and thirty-one weeks and six days.

The separation is six hundred and seventy days, ninety-five weeks and five days.
It went into a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, it has been subtracted every day since, it does not get shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**He went up that stair at about half past three on the Wednesday and there were eleven people in the room and there was no reason on earth for him to be one of them.**

He had not gone up it since the Wednesday four weeks ago, when he had gone at six in the evening with nothing at all to go for.
**He had not been asked to come. Nobody in that room had known he was coming and four of them did not know he was in the building until about twenty past four.**

---

**At about a quarter past four a woman of about sixty opened a green-covered book on a table at the top of a stair in Saltmarket and gave the number before anything else, which is what she does.**

"**Fifty-seven.**"

"**Of which?**" said the man of about sixty-one with the clipboard.

"**Fifty-two correspond.**" She said it the way a person reads a temperature.
"**Up by one. It is always up by one. The five that do not correspond are the same five and I have never shown them to anybody and I am not going to start this afternoon.**"

Nobody asked her about the five.

**That is the only number given out loud in that room, and it is the only number that room gave out loud in four weeks, and the nine days either side of this Wednesday carry no sitting and announce nothing and not one of them says what a sitting does when there was none.**

---

**The book was on sixty-four lines when she opened it and it was on sixty-four lines when she shut it, about an hour later, and nobody in that room asked her to open it and nobody in that room suggested it.**

Nobody knew why it opened on some Wednesdays and not on others and nobody in that room has ever been told and **there is no rule anywhere in this city that says which, and the woman of about sixty is not going to say, and the man of about twenty-two was in the room and did not ask her.**

**The tin on the same table is on seventy-three with its lid on and there is a receipt from a shop in it as a page marker and the receipt is not hers, and the difference between the two of those figures has never once been printed as a number by anybody.**

---

**Behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place.**

**It had been empty twenty-four weeks to the day. Four people in that room had known that figure before they came up the stair and about four of them knew it to the day and not one of them said so and nobody asked her about it and nobody has asked her about it in twenty-four weeks.**

**The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room. It did not move on that Wednesday. It has not moved since the spring and nobody in that room moved it and nobody in this city has ever said who put it there and nobody asked.**

---

**About eleven people were in that room and the whole of what any of them said is four sentences and one of them is a number.**

A woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs and who was there as a person and who said nothing at all for about two hours and then said this, in about nine seconds:

"**There's more doors with that sheet on them than there's anybody in a box on it, and the answering isn't coming from a middle, and nobody is going to be sent to ask.**"

Nobody said anything for about eleven seconds.

"**Is that all right,**" said the man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse.

"**No,**" she said.
"**And I'm not going to tell you it is. I'm telling you it's the arrangement and that I've said it out loud three times since the spring and that I'm tired of saying it and that nobody is going to be sent anyway, because a person who is sent to find somebody is a person who has been given a route.**"

"**Right,**" said the man of about thirty-eight.

"**Don't agree with me,**" she said.
"**If you agree with me then we're both doing it for the same reason and one of us is wrong.**"

And then she sat down and said nothing else for about an hour.

---

**And nobody in that room said one word about what the whole run of days since the spring was for.**

Not the woman of about sixty, who has held that room nineteen years.
Not the man of about sixty-one with one column of numbers on a clipboard that has never been totalled and was not totalled.
Not the woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her.
Not the man of about thirty-eight.
Not the woman of about fifty-two.
Not the man of about thirty-three who came up that stair at six o'clock for the fourth time in a fortnight and sat down at the back and did not raise his hand to anything.

**Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody said thank you to the woman of about fifty-nine who opens the doors of a hall in a third district or to the woman of about twenty-four or to the man of about fifty-eight on a road off Saltmarket or to the man of about forty-four who empties nineteen wire cages on a Friday round for about nine years, and none of those four was in that room, and the four of them were not thanked anywhere else either.**

Nobody said anything about the four things that failed in the last few weeks.
Nobody said anything about nine boxes.
Nobody said anything about a page under a rubber band with nine rows on it.
**Nobody said anything about anything, and that is not a decision anybody took; it is what a room of eleven people does at about quarter past five on a Wednesday when the number has been given and there is no second item.**

---

**At about five minutes past six the man of about twenty-two came down that stair and the room was still going and he did not go back up.**

**He stood on the bottom step for about four minutes before he went out through the door, and nobody came down after him and nobody went up to find him and the light on that landing has been the same since about autumn.**

There are about nine steps and the fourth one from the bottom has been loose for about four years and it is loose on that Wednesday and it was loose on the Wednesday four weeks ago and it will be loose at the next one.

**A man of about thirty-one who works for a firm was on the landing at about quarter to six and had been for about eleven minutes and had come up with a bundle he had put down outside the door and had not knocked on.**

He said, "**Are you going in.**"

"**I've been in.**"

"**Right,**" the man said, and picked the bundle up and went down.

**That was the whole exchange between two people on that landing and it was about four seconds and neither of them said the other's name and neither of them has been in that room in his life and one of them carries things for a firm and the other one fixes hinges, and neither of them has ever mentioned it to anybody.**

---

**And the last eleven minutes of that Wednesday are on this page because nothing else happened in them and because a room that has had one thing said in it for two hours is a different room at six o'clock than it was at four.**

**The man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse stood up at about ten to six and did not say what he had come to say.** He had come four times since the spring and had asked to be told a number each of the four times and had been told it each of the four times, and on this Wednesday he stood up and did not ask, and he has not asked since and he did not say why and nobody asked him why.

**The woman of about twenty-nine with a list face up in front of her put her hand flat on the list at about five to six and left it there for eleven seconds, and the list has four things on it and about two of them were done on this Wednesday and about two of them have not been done and are not going to be done by anybody in that room.**

She said, out loud, to nobody, "**That's the whole of it then,**" and the woman of about sixty said, "**That is the whole of it then,**" and neither of them said it to the other and both of them said it out loud and about four people heard both.

**Nobody said what the whole of it was. Nobody in that room has ever said what the whole of it was, and the woman of about sixty has been asked twice in nineteen years by people entitled to ask and has answered neither time.**

---

**At about a quarter past five a woman of about forty-one came into that shop and said she had heard that there was a room in this city where a number was given out loud on a Wednesday, and asked whether that was true.**

"**There is a number given out loud on a Wednesday,**" Marek said.

"**That's not what I asked.**"

"**It's what you asked and it's what you get,**" he said.
"**There is a room. There is a number. Somebody says it. And nobody else in this city says a number of anything out loud on any day of the week, which is why you have heard about it and which is why four people who have been in that room have never told anybody what is in it, including the ones who would have liked to.**"

"**Would you.**"

"**No,**" he said.

"**Right,**" she said.
"**I did not think you would. My brother was in a room that gave out numbers for about nine years and he never once told me and I have stopped asking him and he has never stopped telling me I ask.**"

She bought about nine feet of conduit and paid and went and was not thanked and **she has not been back, and there is no arrangement in this city for asking the woman of about sixty what is in her room, and there has not been one since about spring, and nobody is going to put one in.**

**At about a quarter to five a woman of about forty-five came into that shop and said she had heard there was a book in this city that had been written in for about nineteen years, and Marek said he had not heard that, and she said she had heard it from a man on a bus, and he said that was probably a different book, and she said right and bought nine feet of conduit and has not been back.**

He has never been in that room again and he is not going to be, and the stair is a place he has been in about nine times in about four months and has no standing in and nobody has offered him any.

---

**There were four things he saw on that Wednesday and not one of them was on a form.** A clipboard with one column of numbers on it and no total at the bottom, shut and not shut.
**A place behind a chair with no coat on it and no bag under it and nothing at all in it.** Eleven people breathing at slightly different rates for about an hour.
And a man of about thirty-three who has come up that stair four times in a fortnight, and who came up it at six o'clock and sat down at the back and did not raise his hand to anything.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday, for somebody to come into that shop and tell him that the whole run of days since the spring had been for something.**

**Nobody came. Nobody in this city has come, and nobody is going to, and the whole run of days since the spring is not going to be summed by anybody including him, and there is nobody he could ask who would not find the question stupid.**

He said none of it.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in and the shutter came down at ten and he went home at ten past ten and he was twenty-two years old and he had been standing where he was standing on the first day of it.

---

**From half past six until ten there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road and she is about thirty and the chair the door is behind is hers and for three and a half hours not one person in that building put a word to her and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will.**

**The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it did not come out on that Wednesday and it has not come out on any day since the spring, and there is nobody in this city who is entitled to open it in her presence or out of it, and no one has asked her permission because no one has asked her anything.**
