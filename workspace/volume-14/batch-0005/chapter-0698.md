# Chapter 698 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Half Past Six, One Page With Nine Rows On It Under A Rubber Band And No Signature At The Foot Of It, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Nine Who Asked Whether A Sheet Was Still Going Out And Was Told And Was Not Asked Why

**A page with nine rows on it went up on the front of a cabinet under a rubber band, and nobody signed it, and one of the nine rows is not like the other eight. That is the Friday. The bench until two, the counter until about six and the four rooms off that service road from half past six are all there too, running underneath, and none of them is why the Friday is on the page.**

---

Ten jobs carried a date against each on the Friday and ten people came over the counter, the last of them let in at five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the thirty-fifth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand two hundred and seventy-eight days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks and four days, four of them and one behind the three, warm. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and eighty-two days, a hundred and eighty-three weeks and one day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and seventy-four days, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks and one day.
The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and sixty-eight, a hundred and fifty-two weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and eleven, a hundred and thirty weeks and one day.

The separation is six hundred and fifty-eight days, ninety-four weeks to the day, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, walked every day since, not shorter, nothing put back into it.

---

**The page is on the front of a steel cabinet in a first floor in a second district with a rubber band over it and there is no signature at the foot of it and there is no name at the head of it.**

It is one side of a sheet with nine rows ruled down it in pencil and a line at the right-hand end of each row, and nothing else ruled on the sheet.

**Eight of the rows have three things in them: a date, a letter, and a small square with a tick in it. Six of the dates are the same Friday and two are the Friday before. The letters are the letters of the round. The ticks are the same tick.**

**The ninth row has a date and a letter and no tick in the square at the right-hand end of it. Instead there is a line of handwriting across the whole row in a different hand and it says that there has been nobody in that room since the summer and that whoever takes the round has been leaving the bundle on a chair and that the chair is still there.**

**Nobody in that building has read that row aloud. Nobody has taken the rubber band off. Nobody has put a second sheet underneath it. Nobody has written a reason for the row. About four people in that building have read it and four people have not and nobody has said anything to anybody.**

---

**And the page is not a thing anybody has said anything about, and here is the whole of what happened on it.**

Eight of the nine rooms sent back a sheet with a box at the bottom of it and nothing in the box.
The ninth did not send back a sheet at all and did not send back an answer and did not send back anything; it sent back a bundle that a person on a round left on a chair, because there was nobody there to sign for it.

**That is what beat it. Not an argument and not a plan and not a refusal and not a decision. A chair, in a room, with a bundle on it, in a building a day and a night of bus travel away, and nobody in that town in a position to say so.**

**And nobody knew that until somebody wrote it in the ninth row, and the person who wrote it did it in the margin of a page that was not theirs, and has not been asked about it and is not on any list and did not do it for any reason anybody has given.**

Nobody has said that it worked.
Nobody has said that it did not work.
**About four people in that building think one of those things and about four think the other and nobody has said either out loud to anybody including the person who wrote in the margin.**

---

**And nobody has said the word for what nine rows of pencil is, and nobody has drawn a line under the ninth row, and nobody has written a date at the top of the page.**

---

**Nobody has signed it and there is no copy of it anywhere, and four people in that building have seen the rubber band and about nine have seen the page, and it is on a cabinet and not in a drawer and it will be on that cabinet when the heating comes on.**

**A man of about thirty-one came in at about twelve, put a folder on the counter, and asked for about eleven minutes and got nine.**

Inside the folder were nine sheets and he wanted to know whether they were all the same shape.

"**They're the same shape,**" Marek said.
"**What I can't tell you is whether they were the same shape when they were printed.**"

"**That's not an answer.**"

"**It's the answer,**" Marek said.
"**I can look at nine pieces of paper and tell you they're the same size. I can't look at nine pieces of paper and tell you what somebody did to them on a Tuesday in a different building, and anybody who tells you they can has either been there or is about to lie to you.**"

The man of about thirty-one said that was fair and said the other half out loud, which was that he had been to the other building and had been shown them by a woman who types and had not opened them, and Marek said then you have been shown nine closed envelopes, and the man said yes, and Marek said that is what you have, and the man said that is what I have.

**He went away with the folder. Nobody followed him and nobody has asked the woman who types what she showed him and she was not asked at the time and she has not been asked since and she would not say if she were.**

---

**And at about half past eleven on that Friday a woman of about thirty-nine came into that shop and asked whether a sheet with a box on it was still going out.**

"**Not from here,**" Marek said.

"**I know it isn't from here. I'm asking whether it's going out.**"

"**Some went out on the Friday of the week before,**" he said.
"**That's a round and it goes to nine rooms and it isn't a thing this shop sends.**"

"**Right,**" she said.

She did not go, and he did not say anything else, and the shop was quiet for about eleven seconds.

"**Do you want to know why I'm asking.**"

"**No,**" Marek said.

---

**He said no in about four seconds and the no was correct and the reason is that a woman of about thirty-nine who has come in twice about a thing in four weeks is not going to be told anything useful by a man who asks her why, and there is nothing in this shop that is hers.**

**She did not tell him and she has not been back and he did not ask and is not going to and there are four people in this city who know why she asked and none of them is him. That is the whole of the cost of that Friday afternoon and it is four seconds long.**

---

**The counter ran the rest of it.**

At about nine a man of about fifty-two brought in a drawer that had come off its runners and did not want it fixed.

At about half past twelve a woman of about twenty-eight asked whether anybody had ever written down one of the things he said at a counter, and was told in four seconds that four people do and that none of them is anybody in that shop and that a man who names four people at a counter is a man who has started something.

At about three a man of about thirty-six wanted a thing done that would take about four hours and he wanted to know whether it would take about four hours, and was told it would take about four hours.

At about half past four a woman of about forty-four brought in a lamp and was told in nine words, in four seconds, that the switch is the switch.

**At about four a man of about twenty-six asked whether the shop would put a name to something he had done.**

"**No,**" Marek said.

"**I haven't got a name.**"

"**You haven't got a name and you want one,**" Marek said, "**and those are two different things and I can only answer the first one.**"

"**Then what's the second one.**"

"**The second one is that a thing with a name on it is a thing somebody can be asked about,**" he said, "**and you have not got anybody to ask you about it and that is why you want the name. Give it a name and somebody can come and ask, and then it is a thing that happened to somebody rather than a thing that happened.**"

The man of about twenty-six said that was not what he wanted and paid for about four feet of conduit and has not been back.

**Nobody has put a name to anything on his account and nobody in this city is going to and there is no register anywhere with a column for it, and a man who refuses to name a thing he has done is not a third instance of anything, and the count in that other room stands at two where it has stood before the winter.**

---

**He noticed four things on that Friday and not one of them was on a form.** A rubber band that has been stretched and gone white in one place and has been stretched round a square object and not a round one.
**A chair in a town a day and a night of bus travel away with a bundle on it and nobody in the room.** Nine rows ruled in pencil and a ninth one ruled across rather than down.
And a woman of about thirty-nine who asked a question, was answered correctly, was asked nothing back, and left.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Friday, for somebody to come into that shop and tell him that nine boxes had stayed empty and to be thanked for it.**

**Nobody came and nobody is going to and the nine boxes are not his and there is no part of them he had any hand in except refusing in four seconds to look at nine sheets of paper on the Thursday, and refusing was not a thing he did for the boxes, he did it because of a drawer in about four years.**

He said none of it.
Ten jobs out and ten callers in, the last at five, the cage at ten, and then the walk, which took about nine minutes longer than it has taken any other Friday.

**One other person was in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road that evening, from half past six until ten, and she is about thirty. Nothing whatever was put to her for three and a half hours and nobody has ever apologised to her in any form. What belongs to her is a page, and it is the eighth of eight, and the eight of them live on a back shelf in that room in a ring binder, and the binder did not come out and nobody in this city is entitled to take it down in her presence or out of it.**
