# Chapter 697 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Half Past Six, One Letter With A Sheet Folded Into It That Went Out To Nine Rooms And Came Back Nine Times With A Box At The Bottom Of It Left Empty, And A Man Of Thirty-Three Who Was Refused Four Seconds Of Looking At Nine Sheets Of Paper

**One letter went out folded round a sheet with a box at the bottom of it and nine came back over three days with the box on and nothing in it. That is the Thursday. Bench until two, counter until about six and four rooms off that service road ran underneath it, and a man of thirty-three was refused four seconds of looking at nine sheets.**

---

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand two hundred and seventy-seven days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks and three days, four of them and one behind the three, warm. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and eighty-one days, a hundred and eighty-three weeks to the day, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and seventy-three days, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks to the day.

The separation is six hundred and fifty-seven days, ninety-three weeks and six days. It was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago; it is walked every day, and it is not shorter, and nothing has gone back into it.

Nine dated jobs on that Thursday, and nine people over the counter for them, the last of them at half past four. At ten the shutter came down and shook the cage for the thirty-fourth time in eleven years.

The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and sixty-seven, a hundred and fifty-two weeks and three days, and nine hundred and ten days, a hundred and thirty weeks to the day, is what the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns stands at.

---

**There was one letter. It left a first floor in a second district on the Friday of the week before with a folded sheet inside it, and the sheet had four things on it all the same size and a box at the bottom, and a strip of paper about four inches long was laid over one of the columns and not fixed down.**

Nobody in this city has authorised that strip.
Nobody has asked about it.
A man of about fifty-eight who owns a two-man print shop on a road off Saltmarket printed the sheets and set none of the type and cannot name a person, and he was not asked about the strip and would not have known what it was if he had been.

**The letter went to nine rooms because that is the round that goes to nine rooms, and it went out as one letter and it was nine copies of one letter because that is how the firm does anything, and no person in that office counted anything.**

---

**They came back over three days. Nine of them, in no order that anybody established.**

**Every one of them had the folded sheet in it. On all nine sheets the box at the bottom was empty — no mark in it, no name, no address, nothing written on it at all, and not one of the nine sheets was signed, and the nine sheets were not marked in any way by anybody, and there is no reason on any of them.**

That is what survived.
Not an answer and not a refusal and not a sentence and not a list.
**Nine boxes that stayed empty because nothing in this city knows how to make a room put something in a box, and the reason it does not know is that a box that is filled in before the sheet is printed is a box and not a question, and the last two weeks have been about how long a person can keep not asking that.**

---

**A woman of about twenty-nine who types for that firm had nine returns on her desk on the Wednesday and about bundles going back out, and she did not open any of them before they went, and she was asked about it by one person and she answered in about nine seconds.**

"**They've come back.**"

"**With the box on.**"

"**With the box on and nothing in it,**" she said.
"**Nine of them. I put nine envelopes in a bundle on Friday because I am the one who puts envelopes in bundles, and I did not open any of them and I am not going to open any of them now they've gone back.**"

"**Do you want to know why.**"

"**No,**" she said.
"**And I want to say the second part as well, because you are going to come back with it on Monday: I am not going to write a reason on any of them either, and if I write a reason on one then somebody has to answer it and there is nobody to answer it and it will sit in a drawer in this building for about four years and then it will be found and it will be about that happened in a fortnight and will look like a decision.**"

Nobody in that office said the word for what had happened, and nobody said the other word, and **about four people in that building know that nine sheets came back with a box on them that was not filled in, and not one of the four has said it to anybody outside that building and not one of the four is going to.**

---

**What the nine rooms have been sent, and by whom, and how many times, is not on this page.**

**A woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs and who was not in that office and was not told anything about said, on the Thursday, at about eleven, to a man of about twenty-two, in nine seconds, that if the sheets were going out again then somebody ought to say so, and that nobody was going to say so.**

"**You could say so.**"

"**I could say so to nine chairs and there are about four people who would take it as me putting a stop on,**" she said.
"**And there is no version of me saying so where the nine rooms do not find out that somebody decided they should be told. So no. I am not going to say so and I am not going to stop them and I am not going to be asked about it again.**"

"**Are you going to be asked about it again.**"

"**Not by me,**" she said.
"**By somebody in about four weeks, yes, and by somebody who has been in one of those rooms, and then I am going to say no, and you are not going to be in the room when it happens and I have arranged that.**"

---

**She has not been in that office. He has not been in that office. The man of about fifty-eight who printed the sheets has not been asked anything about how many of them went out or how many came back, and has said, if anybody had put it to him, that there is no order behind the second run and that he would not know where it went if he did know the count.**

Nobody in this city has asked him and nobody is going to.

The nine rooms have never met.
Six of them are in this city and three are in towns.
None of them has been told what any of the others did.
**Nobody spoke to a ninth person, because there was no ninth person. Nobody in this city knows what nine of those rooms said since the spring, and the two people who know the most about the nine are the man who carried eight of them in his head and the woman of about twenty-nine who typed nine copies of one letter and neither of them has been told the number by anybody and neither of them has said it.**

---

**He was told about it on the Wednesday, by the man of about thirty-three, in about nine words, at about four, and he has not been told a single thing about since and was not asked.**

"**Nine of them came back with the box on.**"

"**I heard.**"

"**Who from.**"

"**I don't know,**" Marek said, "**and you have just told me in the words you have just used that you have not been told either, and I am not going to ask you a second question that you would have to go and find the answer to.**"

The man of about thirty-three looked at him for four seconds and then said, "**You don't ever want to know,**" and Marek said, "**I want to know about nine things a week and this week I have wanted to know about none of them,**" and the man said that was a strange thing to want and went and paid for nothing.

**That is the whole of what any person in this city was told about nine boxes that came back empty on the day after they came back empty, and it was nine words at a counter and it was not written down.**

At about ten past four on that Thursday a man of about thirty-three came into the shop with nine sheets of paper in a plastic wallet and put them on the counter and said they had come back with his round and he wanted a man of twenty-two to look at them and say whether they were in his handwriting.

"**No.**"

"**It's one question.**"

"**It's four seconds,**" Marek said, "**and the answer is no, and the reason is that if I look at nine sheets of paper and say yes or no then I am a person who has looked at nine returns, and there is no line anywhere in this city for a person who has looked at nine returns, and there is not going to be one, and in about four years somebody will find that out about from a drawer.**"

"**You haven't even looked.**"

"**And I am not going to,**" Marek said.

The man of about thirty-three said that was the second time this week somebody had refused him something small and went, and **he does not know what the nine sheets say and will not find out from Marek, and there are four people in this city who could tell him and none of them has been asked.**

---

**The rest of the counter.**

At about eleven a woman of about forty-six wanted to know whether returning something counts as having received it, and was told in four seconds that it does, and that this is a question nobody asks twice.

At about half past one a man of about twenty-four wanted a job done that there is no part of this shop that does, and was told there is not and went and came back at four with the same thing and was told the same thing and paid for a hinge.

At about five a woman of about thirty-eight asked whether she could have a receipt for returning something, and Marek said no and she said that is the whole of what I wanted and left.

**At about a quarter to four a man of about forty-six came in and asked whether anybody in this city was going to say anything about nine sheets of paper coming back, and Marek said in four seconds that he did not know what he was talking about, and the man said he knew that, and paid for four feet of chain.**

**That was twice on one afternoon that a stranger came into that shop and asked a question with a number on it, and both of them were answered in four seconds by a man who did not ask either of them a second question, and neither was thanked, and there is no line anywhere in this city for a person who has asked a question with a number in it and been told nothing.**

---

**He noticed three things on that Thursday and not one of them was on a form.** Nine sheets fanned out on a desk with the corners aligned to the edge of the desk and not one of them signed.
**A plastic wallet left on a counter by a man who picked it up again and did not leave it, which was the whole of his intention when he put it down.** And the fact that a strip of paper about four inches long lies over a column of a sheet and is not fixed down and that nobody has asked, and that a man of about fifty-eight who printed those sheets cannot name anybody.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Thursday, to be told that nothing had happened.**

**Nobody told him and nobody is going to and there were nine boxes on nine sheets in a first floor in a second district that stayed empty and he does not know that and was not told and refused in four seconds to be the person who found out.**

He said none of it.
Nine dated jobs, nine callers, the last at half past four, the shutter at ten, and a man of thirty-three who went without paying for anything because he had been told nine words and they had cost the shop nothing.

---

**Behind the chair of the woman who holds the room above a line in Saltmarket there is a place and it has been empty a long time and about four people in this city could tell you how long, and the tin on that table is on seventy-three with its lid on, and the book on the same table is on sixty-four lines and has not changed since the Wednesday before last and is not going to change again this side of a Saturday.**

Nobody has put an apology to her in any form, then or since. What she has is the eighth of eight pages in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room, and the binder did not come out.**
