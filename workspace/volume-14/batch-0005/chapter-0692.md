# Chapter 692 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Two More Rooms In Two Districts And A Man Of About Thirty-One Who Carries For A Firm And Who Says A Place And Not A Name, A Second Sentence That Is A Question, And Nobody In This City Who Asked Him Twice

**Two rooms and one man who has been in this city four years carrying things. The bench until two, the counter until about six, the four rooms off that service road from about six: all of that happened and none of it is the shape of this Wednesday. The shape is a place where somebody said something about where they came from, once, and will not say it again.**

---

Ten jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and ten people came over the counter, the last of them let in at half past four, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage for the twenty-ninth time in eleven years.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand two hundred and sixty-nine days, a hundred and eighty-one weeks and two days, four of them and one behind the three, warm. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and seventy-three days, a hundred and eighty-one weeks and six days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and sixty-five days, a hundred and thirty-seven weeks and six days.
The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and fifty-nine, a hundred and fifty-one weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and two, a hundred and twenty-eight weeks and six days.

The separation is six hundred and forty-nine days, ninety-two weeks and five days, shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, walked every day since, not shorter, nothing put back into it.

---

**He was in the second of the nine rooms from about nine until about ten and in the third of them from about eleven until about twenty past eleven, and he told neither of them anything about other, and he told neither of them anything about first.**

The second one is in a second district above a place that does takeaways until two, and there are two people in it, a man of about fifty-eight and a woman of about twenty-six, and they have both been there long enough that neither of them says a first name to a person who comes in.

"**One sentence,**" he said.
"**In your words. I am not going to write it down and I am not going to read it to you again.**"

"**Go on,**" the woman said.

He was ready with what he wanted.
She took about nine seconds over it.

"**If something comes to us and it is not ours,**" she said, "**how do we hand it back without deciding where it goes?**"

"**Is that the sentence.**"

"**That is the sentence,**" she said, "**and I know it is a question and I have thought about it being a question and I am leaving it as one, because we have never had a written answer to it and I would be inventing one if I turned it round.**"

He said he would come back with something and she said he would not and he said he would not.

---

**The third room is in a fourth district and it has one person in it and she is about sixty-eight and she has been in it about nine years.**

"**One sentence,**" he said.

"**I will give you one,**" she said, "**and I am telling you first that mine is a small one and I do not know what the others are and I am not going to pretend to.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**On a Friday we answer and on the other days we do not.**"

He wrote nothing down and had a piece of paper in his hand for about four seconds anyway.

"**Is that all of it.**"

"**That is all of it,**" she said.
"**We did not choose the Friday. A bus comes four times a day and it comes four times a day on a Friday and twice on the other days, and if I answer on a Tuesday then I am answering on a day when I cannot get anything anywhere, and a room that answers and cannot act is worse than a room that does not answer.**"

---

**And he has now got three, and there is nowhere in this city he can put them, and one of the three is a question.**

At about half past eleven he came out into the light on that road and stood with his back to a wall for nine seconds and did not say any of it out loud, and **there is no piece of paper anywhere in this city with any of the three on it, and there is no list, and there is no column, and the only person who has all of them is a man of twenty-two standing at a wall with a sheet of paper in his pocket.**

---

**The man of about thirty-one came in at about two with a case he did not put down.**

He works for a firm and has carried for it about four years, and there is a post box at that firm and there has been a kind of printed sheet coming out of it for longer than he has been there, and in the first week of that he carried four of them out of the box himself because the man who did it had gone home.

**He is about thirty-one and is not from this city and is not named in this city and has not been named anywhere, and about nine people have known him for four years and not one of them has ever asked him a question about any of it.**

"**That'll go,**" he said, and put the case on the counter.

"**It will,**" said Marek, "**and while you're standing there I'll ask you a thing and you're allowed to say no.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Nine rooms in this city have each given me one sentence in the last three days,**" Marek said.
"**Where did those come from.**"

**The man of about thirty-one looked at the case on the counter and then at the window and then took four seconds.**

"**They come from the same place mine came from.**"

"**Which is where.**"

"**The far end of the middle platform,**" he said.
"**Where the shelter is and where the vending is that nobody uses. There's about nine of us who go there and none of us know the others' names and about half of them have stopped going and the other half go and don't talk about it.**"

Marek did not say anything.

"**That's the place,**" the man said.
"**That's all you're getting.**"

"**That's all I'm asking for.**"

"**You are not going to ask me again,**" the man of about thirty-one said, and it was not a question, and Marek said that he was not, and it was true, and it was going to be true for the rest of his life and he knew it at the time.

---

**The man took his case and went and nothing else was said about it by anybody, and nine people in this city know which platform he meant and none of them has been asked and about four of them know which nine rooms and none of those four is in the same district as any of the others.**

**Nobody in this city has heard that man give the name of a person in connection with the nine sentences and nobody in this city knows what is at the end of that platform except the man of about thirty-one, and he is not going to say.**

---

**The counter ran the rest of the day.**

At about half past two a woman of about forty-one asked whether a thing a room does on a Friday is a rule or a habit, and was told in four seconds that it is a rule if it is written down and a habit if it is not, and that this city has a great many habits that people would call rules if anybody asked them to.

At about three a man of about fifty-three wanted a lock changed and did not want a lock changed.

At about a quarter to four a woman of about twenty-six came in and said she was not going to be thanked for the tea, and was thanked anyway.

**He went up to the second of the nine rooms at about nine in the morning and was let in by the woman of about twenty-six, and about four minutes of that visit were a thing nobody has written down and that has not come up again.**

He asked her whether the room had a book in it, for returns, of any kind.
She said no and then said that there was a book, and that it was a book of a firm, and that she had to sign a column in it on a Friday whether there was anything in the column or not, and that about eleven Fridays ago the man of about fifty-eight had started standing near her when she signed it.

"**He stands near me.**"

"**He stands near me,**" she said, "**and he has not said one word about it and he is not a supervisor and he does not sign that column and on a Friday he has to be in the office for about eleven minutes anyway.**"

"**Have you said anything to him.**"

"**No,**" she said.
"**And I am not going to, because if I say anything then he has to decide whether he is doing it, and right now he is a man standing near a column, and that is a thing that a person can stop doing.**"

---

**Nobody in this city knows about the man standing near the column and there are four other people who sign columns in four other rooms in this city and at least two of them have the same thing and none of the two has said so to anybody either.**

**He made that woman of about twenty-six a cup of tea at about eleven and did not charge for it and it took him nine minutes out of the only quiet half hour that Monday had, and he gave it away deliberately, because he had been in two rooms before nine o'clock and had not been alone once and had not noticed until the tea was in front of her.**

**At about eleven a man of about thirty-three came in with a bag of his own things and asked whether this shop was the one that had sent the sheet.**

"**I've never sent one,**" Marek said.

"**You might have sent one with a hole in the top right.**"

"**Then it's not mine,**" Marek said.
"**That's the whole of what I know about it and I've been in this city about four years and I have never put one in a letter slot, and neither has anybody who works for me, because there are two of us and one of me.**"

The man of about thirty-three put the bag down on the floor and stood there for nine seconds and then said, "**Somebody did,**" and Marek said, "**Somebody did,**" and that was the entire conversation and the man went out with the bag and has not been back.

**Nobody in this city is going to be told which letter slot the nine of them went into, and nobody asked, and the nine slots on that road are not a list and have never been made into one, and there is no arrangement anywhere for asking a slot.**

---

**He went to the third of those nine rooms on the Wednesday morning at about eleven and got there by walking there, and it took about forty minutes, and he did it again on the Thursday in the next week and on the Friday in the week after that, and he has walked that road about eleven times.**

**There is no bus that goes there that he can get back from in time for a counter at nine in the morning, and he looked at the times twice, and he did not buy a day ticket in the end, and the reason is in about nine words: a man who buys a day ticket to sit in a room is a man who has made it an appointment.**

Nobody in that room knows how often he has walked it and nobody in this city has asked him and he has not written it down anywhere.

---

**He noticed four things on that Wednesday and not one of them was on a form.** A case that a man put on a counter and did not put down.
**A woman of about twenty-six who washed her own cup.** Two rooms that were nine minutes' walk apart and had no idea of each other and would not have answered a letter if one had been sent, and there was no letter.
And a man of about thirty-one who said a place in nine seconds and has never said anything else about it and cannot be asked.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday, for one of the nine rooms to ask him a second question.**

**Nobody asked him a second question. He asked the same question nine times in three rooms and got three answers and the three of them do not agree about anything, and he has told nobody that they do not agree, and there is no version of this in which he can tell anybody, because telling them would be showing one room what another said and that is the one thing he promised on the first morning.**

He said none of it.
Ten jobs, ten callers, the last at half past four, the shutter at ten, and nine minutes of that walk in the dark doing sums that came out.

**The tin on the table at the top of that stair in Saltmarket is on seventy-three with its lid on and the book on the same table is on sixty-four lines and has been on sixty-four lines since a Wednesday about four weeks ago, and the twenty-eight days between that Wednesday and this one carried no sitting and announced nothing, and not one of them says what a sitting does when there was none.**
