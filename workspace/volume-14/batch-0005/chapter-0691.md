# Chapter 691 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Half Past Six, One Room Of Nine In A First District And A Woman Of About Forty In It Who Says One Sentence And Will Not Say It A Second Time, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Has To Carry It And Is Not Going To Write It Down

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off that service road from about six. This one is one room, one woman, one sentence, and a man of twenty-two who has to hold it and cannot put it down anywhere.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Monday. Eleven people came over the counter and the last of them was let in at five. At ten the shutter came down and shook the cage for the twenty-eighth time in eleven years.

**Four of those rooms and one behind the three are warm, and they stand at one thousand two hundred and sixty-seven days, a hundred and eighty-one weeks to the day. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand two hundred and seventy-one days, a hundred and eighty-one weeks and four days, four days past them, and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and sixty-three days, a hundred and thirty-seven weeks and four days.
The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and fifty-seven, a hundred and fifty-one weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred, a hundred and twenty-eight weeks and four days.

The separation is six hundred and forty-seven days, ninety-two weeks and three days.
It was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, it has been counted every day since, it has not got any shorter, and nothing has gone back into it.

---

**He got to the first of the nine rooms at eleven in the morning and it is eleven minutes' walk from a bus stop a man of about thirty-eight uses, and he went the whole way on foot because a return path is a place and he has never once in this city got to one of these rooms by being driven to it.**

The room is in a first district, above a shop that shuts at one, and it is one room with a door that has a fanlight and the fanlight has been painted over.
There is a table, four chairs, a board on the wall, and a woman of about forty at the table who has been in that room eleven years and does the returns for two other rooms as well and has never said so and is not going to.

**Nobody had told her he was coming. He had walked in and asked and she had said come in before he had got the second half of the sentence out, which is a thing that happens to people who are used to a knock.**

"**I want one sentence,**" he said.
"**From this room. In your words. And then I am going to walk out and I am not going to come back with it and read it to you.**"

She did not ask what for.
She put her hands flat on the table.

"**Go on then.**"

He said what he wanted, and it took him about four seconds and it was the wrong thing to have said, which he worked out about nine minutes later and not in the room.

The sentence is this, and it is in his head and on no piece of paper in this city:

**We will take anything that comes to us, and put it on the board, and if it is still on the board in a fortnight we will write on the front of it the day we first saw it.**

"**That is three things,**" she said.

"**Yes.**"

"**I could do it in one,**" she said.
"**I did three because it is what we actually do and I am not going to hand anybody a tidier version of it. If the other eight say it in one then we will be the room with the long one, and that is a thing I can live with, and you are not going to come back and make it one.**"

"**I am not going to come back at all.**"

"**Good,**" she said.
"**Because if you come back I will have had this conversation twice and the second time it will be a thing I have agreed to.**"

---

**And he asked her the only question he had come with, and it is the question the whole of this was for.**

"**Are there other rooms doing this.**"

"**There are other rooms.**"

"**That is not what I asked.**"

"**No,**" she said.
"**It is what I have. You will have noticed that I did not ask you how many and I did not ask you where they are, and that was not me being careful. That is me having eleven years of people walking in here asking me how many and where, and there is nothing in this room that is any of their business, and the sentence I have just given you is about a board, and a board is not a door number.**"

Somebody's bell went off on the floor below and neither of them looked at the door.

"**Are they merged,**" he said.

"**I do not know and I have not asked and I am not going to,**" she said.
"**And you are not going to tell me, because you have not asked them yet and if you tell me you will have broken something in four seconds and you will not have meant to.**"

He said nothing for about nine seconds.

"**That is fair.**"

"**It is fair,**" she said, "**and it is not a promise and it is not an agreement and there is nothing on this table and there is not going to be anything on this table.**"

---

**He did not write it down on the bus and he did not write it down in the shop and he has not written it down since, and there are now two sentences in this city that exist nowhere except in one woman's mouth and one man's head, and the second one of those two is the worse place for a sentence to be, and he chose it.**

**The way back took eleven minutes longer than the way there and he did not know that for about a street and a half.**

He came down the road past a firm that takes clothes in and past a shop that has been shut since the spring and past nine letter slots, and he counted the letter slots, and there are about nine of them and every one of them has got paper in it, and there has been paper in them since about spring, and none of it is a sheet with four things on it all the same size and a box at the bottom, and none of it is going to be.

**He stood at the second one for four seconds and then went and bought a paper for about elevenpence at the shop that has not shut and read it standing up, and there is no arrangement in this city for telling a letter slot what it is for, and there has not been one since the spring.**

At about a quarter past two he sat on the bench and got a piece of paper out of his pocket for nine seconds and put it back, and the reason is on the page: **a thing that is written down is a thing that can be read to somebody, and he had four seconds to do the reading in a corridor of a first floor in a second district and he was not going to spend them on that.**

---

**And then, because he had about an hour and nothing to do with it, he went back into that first-district room for about four minutes, for no reason at all, and did not ask for anything.**

The woman of about forty had her hand to her ear and was talking to somebody in the next room, and Marek stood in the doorway and did not say anything and did not go in.

"**You're back,**" she said, when she had finished, and she did not sound pleased and she did not sound disappointed.

"**I'm not.**"

"**Then you'll go,**" she said.

"**I'll go,**" he said, and he went, and **he had come back for four minutes and had not wanted one thing out of it except to have stood in the room where the sentence was said, and there is no column anywhere in this city for a man who has done that.**

Nobody saw him come back and nobody saw him leave, and about four people in that building know he was in that doorway at about half past three none of them has said so to anybody.

---

**The counter ran the rest of the day and none of it was about the room.**

At about half past twelve a woman of about thirty-four asked whether a thing could be asked twice in different words and get two different answers, and was told in four seconds that it could, and that the second answer would be about second set of words.

At two a man of about twenty-two wanted a bench frame planed and did not want it planed, and wanted to be told whether it was worth it, and was told it was not worth it, and he bought it planed anyway and paid for all of it and did not say anything. 

At half past three a woman of about fifty-eight came in with a lamp that had been rewired by somebody else and worked perfectly and she wanted him to say that it was safe.
**He said in four seconds that he did not know whether it was safe and that the man who did it was in this city and she could find him, and she said she did not want to find him and would he mind just saying it.** He said no, and she paid four pounds and took the lamp with her, and she has not been back, and nobody was thanked.

**At about five a man of about thirty-eight bought about nine foot of cord he did not need.** He said he had meant to come back for it.
Marek said he did not have to and the man said he knew he did not have to, and neither of them said what it was for.

**At about half past five a woman of about thirty-one brought in a chair frame with a leg off it and asked whether it could be made to stand up, and Marek said yes in four seconds and did not say what it would cost because she had not asked, and she stood in the shop for nine minutes watching him do it and said nothing at all the whole time, and when it was done she went and he did not charge her.**

**At about ten past five a man of about fifty-eight came in holding a folded sheet of paper that had been folded and unfolded about four times and asked whether he could have it copied, and was told in four seconds that the shop does not copy and has never copied and that there is a shop on a wide road that does and is not in this city.**

"**It isn't a copy,**" the man said.
"**It's a copy.**"

"**Then you know what it is,**" Marek said, "**and you know what it is for, and if you are asking me to put a second one of it into this city then I am going to say no and I am going to say it in four seconds.**"

The man of about fifty-eight said that was fair and put the sheet back in his pocket and left with it, and he has not been back and nobody followed him and nobody in this city has asked him what was on the sheet.

---

**From half past six until ten there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road and she is about thirty, and the chair the door is behind is hers, and for three and a half hours not one person in that building put a word to her, and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will. There is a page with her name at the head of it, the eighth of eight of them, in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room. It did not come out that Monday, and there is nobody in this city entitled to take it out of the binder in her presence or out of it.**

The binder is on a back shelf in a room four rooms off a service road and it is not on this page, and it has not been on this page on any of the days of this stretch.

---

**He noticed three things on that Monday and not one of them was on a form.** A door in that first-district room propped open with a folded bill that had been folded the same way twice before.
**A chair in that room that the woman of about forty keeps about two inches off the wall behind her and has never once been asked about.** And a man of about thirty-eight who came in for nine foot of cord and did not say what it was for, and did not have to.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Monday, to be the person who had all nine of them, written down, on a table, in a column.**

**He has got one and a half and no column, and the half is a sentence he cannot prove he has not altered since he heard it, and the reason he did not write it is that he has spent this whole stretch of days learning what happens to a piece of paper in this city and he is not going to start one.**

He said none of that.
Eleven dated jobs went out and eleven came in and the shutter came down at ten and he went home at ten past ten.
