# Chapter 695 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Half Past Six, Eleven Trays In A Canteen In A Second District And One Of Them Clean, And A Man Of About Forty-Seven Who Counted Them And Told Nobody Including Himself That He Had

**Eleven trays on a Monday, in a canteen in a second district, and ten of them used. Bench until two, counter until about six, four rooms off that service road from half past six: ordinary, and none of it the shape of this Monday. The shape is one chair moved nine inches and a floor cleaned more recently than the floor around it, noticed in four seconds by a man who will not say so.**

---

At ten on the Monday the shutter came down and shook the cage for the thirty-second time in eleven years. Nine dated jobs went out that Monday and nine people came over the counter for them, the last of them at half past four.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand two hundred and seventy-four days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks to the day, and four of them and one behind the three are warm. Four days past them, the card on end in the rail by that door is at one thousand two hundred and seventy-eight days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks and four days. The rail takes two.**

The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and seven days, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks and four days.
The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is nine hundred and seventy, a hundred and thirty-eight weeks and four days, and the hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and sixty-four, a hundred and fifty-two weeks to the day.

The separation stood at six hundred and fifty-four days, ninety-three weeks and three days, when the shop shut, and it is not a thing that changes in the afternoon. It was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago and it has been walked every day since and nothing has gone back into it and it is not shorter.

---

**He was in a canteen in a second district from about eleven until about twelve and the man who served it has done that about nine years and is about forty-seven, and the canteen is about the size of the room off that service road and there are two sittings and eleven people at the first one.**

The first sitting is at half past eleven and it lasts about eleven minutes.

He put out eleven trays, because there are eleven trays and there have been eleven trays every weekday for about nine years, and eleven people sit at one long table, and he has never once in nine years counted how many of the trays got used, and he counted this Monday.

**Ten of the trays were used. The eleventh went back on the rack with the marks still on it. And at the end of the long table a chair had been moved about nine inches out from under it, and the bit of floor under it had the pattern in it that the pattern has when something stands on it every day, and that bit had been cleaned more recently than the rest of that floor.**

That is the whole of what he noticed and it took him four seconds in the middle of laying out a tray and it did not stop him laying it out.

**He did not say anything to anybody about it. He has not said anything to anybody about it since, and he was not asked, and there is nobody in this city who knows that he noticed anything at all, and he did not write it down, and he was not asked to.**

---

**There was one more thing in that canteen on that Monday and neither of them connects to the other.**

At about five past twelve a man of about thirty-eight came in through the door, took his coat off, hung it on the back of the fourth chair, and sat down and ate his dinner in about nine minutes and said good afternoon to nobody in particular.

At about ten past twelve the same man put his coat back on and went out of the door and stood on the other side of it in his coat for about four minutes, and then the door opened and a woman of about fifty-two came out of an office and looked down the corridor and went back in, and the man came in and took his coat off and ate the rest of his dinner.

**Nobody in that canteen said a word about either half of that, and the man of about forty-seven did not say a word about it and about four people at that table were still talking about something that has nothing to do with it and did not stop when the man came in the second time.**

---

**Marek was not in that canteen and was not in that corridor and has never been to that works and does not know that any of it happened and nobody in this city has told him.**

He was at a bench until two and at a counter until about six and he asked nobody anything about habits, which is what he has done every day of this stretch of days and will go on doing.

**On the Friday he had walked past that works twice without going in, and he had not known until about half past twelve on the Monday that the man who stood in the corridor had a name, and he did not learn it then and did not ask and is not going to.**

There is no arrangement in this city for knowing how many people eat in a canteen in a second district and there has not been one, and there is not going to be one, and **the number of trays a man lays out is not a figure anybody has taken, and it is the same class of figure as the count of doors and the count of hands and the size of a print run, and there are four of those in this city and not one of them is on a page.**

---

**The counter ran nine jobs and not one of them was about a canteen.**

At about nine a woman of about fifty-five brought in a toaster that worked and wanted it made to work the way it used to, and it used to work worse, and he said so in four seconds and she said that was not the answer she had come for and paid four pounds anyway.

At about half past eleven a man of about twenty-eight asked whether a room could be sent something and then be sent nothing, which is what happened to one of them in a town a day and a night of bus travel away, and Marek said yes and that it had happened to about rooms in this city since the spring and he did not know which four, and the man said that was not much of an answer and Marek said no and charged him nine pounds.

At about half past one a woman of about thirty-six asked whether anybody was going to the two remaining rooms a day and a night of bus travel away, and said it out loud in a shop and did not ask him to go, and he said he was not going and she said right and bought four feet of conduit.

**At about three a man of about sixty-two brought in a chair frame with a split in it and Marek looked at it for about nine seconds and put it on the bench and gave it back.**

"**I'll bring it Thursday.**"

"**Bring it Thursday and I'll charge you.**"

"**You might as well charge me now.**"

"**No,**" Marek said.
"**If I do it now it's a repair and if you don't come back it's a free one and both of those are the same amount of work and one of them is a favour and I have decided not to do favours this week for reasons that are on a page in my head and not on anything else.**"

The man of about sixty-two said right and took it and went, and it is Thursday in three days, and he has not been in, and there is nothing about in this page except that he will not be.

**At about half past three a woman of about thirty-nine came in about ninety seconds after that man had gone and said she wanted to say something and had thought about it on the bus.**

"**Go on.**"

"**I counted the chairs in your shop and there are nine and about four of them have a coat on them and I have never once in about nine years come in here when there was not somebody in a coat,**" she said.
"**And I have worked out that that is because a person who is waiting in a coat is a person who is not going to be asked to sit down, and I am not asking you to change it. I am telling you that I noticed it and that it took me about nine years and that I am going to go home now.**"

"**Right,**" Marek said.

She said right and went and paid nothing and **has not been back, and nobody thanked her, and the four chairs with coats on them were there the next morning and the morning after that and there are four of them most days.**

---

**He asked her one question, at about two, and it was four seconds, and it is the only question he asked anybody that day that he had prepared.**

"**Do you want it written down.**"

"**No,**" she said.

"**Will you say why not.**"

"**No,**" she said, "**and that is the last of it.**"

He did not write it down.
Nobody in this city is going to be told that a woman of about thirty-nine noticed the coats, and she is not on a list and nobody has written to her and nobody is going to.

---

**He noticed four things on that Monday and not one of them was on a form, and all four of them were in his own shop or on the walk to it.** A coil of flex on a bench that had been cut and rejoined at the same place twice. A kettle that works and a man of about thirty-one who wanted the switch on it to be something else. A bench made up out of two others where the join shows and always shows. **And a woman of about thirty-six who asked out loud in his own shop whether anybody was going to two rooms a day and a night away, and did not ask him to go. He walked past that gate twice on the Friday without going in and would not have known the man who stood in the corridor if the man had come to his counter.**

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Monday, to be asked whether it was true.**

There is no sentence in this city he has heard that anybody has told him is true, and there is a set of seven that nobody has read, and there are two he has not sent anybody to, and **somewhere in this city on this Monday a man of about forty-seven counted eleven trays and one of them was clean and is not going to say a word about anybody including himself, and he does not know about seven and has never heard of them and nobody is going to connect the two.**

He said none of it.
Nine dated jobs and nine names, the last let in at half past four, the shutter at ten, and the walk home past a place where four people had been eating in coats and did not know he was not there.

---

**A green-covered book at the top of a stair in Saltmarket is on sixty-four lines and has been on sixty-four lines since the Wednesday before last. The tin beside it is on seventy-three with its lid on. Five days carry no sitting between that Wednesday and this one and every one of them announced nothing, and not one of them says what a sitting does when there was none, and one of those five is inside the run of five days immediately before the close and it carries nothing at all and is not on this page.**

---

**One other person was in that building from half past six until ten, in the fourth of those four rooms off that service road. She is about thirty. The chair the door is behind is hers and she was in it three and a half hours, and not one person put a word to her, and nobody has apologised to her in any form and nobody ever will. What belongs to her is a page. It is the eighth of eight of them. The eight of them live on a back shelf of that room in a ring binder, and the binder did not come out and nobody in this city is entitled to take it down in her presence or out of it.**
