# Chapter 696 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Nine Hooks On A Board In A First District And Eight Of Them Empty Of Use And One Of Them Not, A Woman Of About Forty-Four Who Began A Sentence And Finished Another One, And An Eighth Room In A Town And A Ninth Room That Wrote One Down

**Nine hooks on a board, eight of them used and one of them not, in a first district, with four other people who did not look up. Bench until two, counter until about six, the fourth room off that service road: none of that is the Wednesday. The Wednesday is a sentence begun at a quarter past eleven and finished in a different direction, and four inches by two of card in a pocket.**

---

At ten the shutter came down on the Wednesday and shook the cage for the thirty-third time in eleven years. Nine jobs carried a date against each and nine people came over the counter before that, the last of them let in at five.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand two hundred and seventy-six days, which is a hundred and eighty-two weeks and two days. Four of them and one behind the three are warm. In the rail by that door, standing on end and four days past the rooms, a card: one thousand two hundred and eighty days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks and six days, and the rail takes two.**

Nine hundred and seventy-two days, a hundred and thirty-eight weeks and six days, is the nineteenth line on that board of two nails.
The hardboard's sixteenth is one thousand and sixty-six, a hundred and fifty-two weeks and two days. The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is nine hundred and nine, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks and six days.

Six hundred and fifty-six days, ninety-three weeks and five days: the separation, which was shut in a one-line box in a form about twelve weeks ago, walked every day since, not shorter, nothing put back into it.

---

**He was in an office in a first district from about ten until about half past eleven with four other people in it and there is a board on the wall with nine hooks on it and a key on each of the nine.**

The keys go out with a round and come back with a round.
A woman of about forty-four has run that round for about four years and a man of about thirty-eight sits opposite her and has run it two. 

**Eight of the nine hooks had a key off them and put back on them in the last nine days. The ninth had had a key on it since a Monday eleven days ago, and the key had a card tied to it with a number on the card, and the number was not one of the nine and had never been one of the nine.**

"**That's not ours,**" the man of about thirty-eight said.

"**No,**" she said.

"**It's a spare.**"

"**It's not a spare,**" she said.
"**Spares are in the drawer and there are four in the drawer and I counted them on Monday.**"

"**So somebody's put it up.**"

"**Somebody's put it up,**" she said, "**and the person who used to take it has not, and he signs for it, and he signs for it at the door instead of in the book, and that has been every day for eleven days and I have not asked him why because I do not know what I would do with the answer.**"

---

**And at about a quarter past eleven she was going to say something about the man opposite her and she said something else.**

"**Do you want me to—**"

She stopped.
She did not stop for a dramatic length of time.
She stopped the length of a person swallowing.

"**—do you want me to put the kettle on,**" she said, "**because it has been about eleven minutes since I last looked at it.**"

"**Go on,**" the man of about thirty-eight said.

The other two people in that room went on doing what they were doing.
**Nobody looked up. Nobody in that room noticed that a sentence had started and been put down and a different one had been picked up, and the woman of about forty-four did not notice it herself and did not think about it again on the bus and has not mentioned it to anybody since, and it has not been written down anywhere.**

The reason is four words long and it is not a virtue: **she would have been talking about a person, and there is no column in that room for a person, and there are four people in it.** It was not restraint.
It was the shape of the room.

**That hook is still on that board. The key is still on it. Nobody has said a word about it and nobody is going to and nobody in this city knows it is there except four people who were in that room and one of them has not said anything and the other three do not know what they were looking at.**

---

**He was in that office for about eighty minutes and he is not on any page of what was said in it.**

He was there about the eighth hook and about a bracket, and about a door that had come off its top hinge in about winter, and a list of nine properties that had nine numbers against them.
He was not told about the ninth hook.
Nobody in that office knew he existed and nobody has told them and nobody is going to.

---

**Two things came back to him on that Wednesday and one of them he did not expect and one of them came on a piece of card.**

The eighth of the nine rooms is in a town a day and a night of bus travel away and he had said he was not going and he did not go.
**A man of about twenty-eight who works at a depot in this city had been asked, not by Marek and not by anybody in this shop, by a woman of about thirty-six who had been asked herself six days ago and had said yes, and that is how nine sentences happen and not by a man handing out cards in a car park.**

"**Go on,**" the man of about twenty-eight said.

"**Whatever we have already given somebody else, we keep giving them,**" he said.
"**And anything new comes to the counter and waits.**"

"**That's not a rule,**" the woman of about thirty-six said.

"**That's a sentence,**" Marek said, "**and I have eight.**"

---

**And the ninth one came back on a piece of card, and the ninth room is a day and a night of bus travel away, and on the Friday of the week before a woman of about forty-one went out there with a card in her coat and told the room she had come about a sheet of paper.**

The room did not want to say a sentence out loud.
It said, in about nine seconds, that a sentence out loud is a thing that gets repeated, and that the man who had heard seven of them could repeat them and had not been asked not to and would not be told not to, and that a room in a town with one person in it was not going to put a thing into a man who carries things.

**So it wrote it on a card. The card came back on the Friday with the round, in a hand that is not Marek's and not the woman of about forty-one's, and it has no name on it and it has no town on it and it is about four inches by two.**

He read it once in the shop at about half past four and put it in his inside pocket and **did not copy it, did not photograph it, did not read it twice, and has not taken it out since, and there is one of it in this city and it is in a pocket and not anywhere else.**

Nobody has been thanked.
He has not shown it to anybody.
There are now eight sentences in one man's head and one on a card in his pocket and **the ninth is the only one of the nine that exists in two places, and the reason it does is that one room was not prepared to say it out loud to a person who carries things, and that is not a defect in the room and it is not a defect in him.**

---

**The counter ran nine jobs and one of them was the wrong kind.**

At about eleven a woman of about forty-six asked whether a shop would go out and get a thing off a van.

"**No,**" he said.

"**I know that. I want it in writing.**"

"**There's no writing.**"

"**Then what have I got.**"

"**You've got me saying no in about four seconds,**" he said, "**and you can have that twice if you want it.**"

She said it once was enough and paid eleven pounds for a job that had been done in nine minutes and said the time was generous.

At about two a man of about fifty-two wanted a bench made up out of two others and did not want the join to show and it shows.

At about a quarter to five a woman of about thirty-one brought in a kettle that works and was told in four seconds, in nine words, that the switch on it is the switch.

**At about half past four a man of about twenty-four asked whether a thing that is on a person's record can be taken off it by the person it is on.** Marek said yes, in four seconds, because it can, and then said the other half, which he did not have to say and said anyway.

"**Say the other half.**"

"**He can take it off, and nobody can tell him not to, and nobody can tell him that he should, and in four days there will not be one line in this city that says he was ever allowed to do it,**" Marek said.
"**So it works. That's the trouble with it. It works and it leaves nobody standing there afterwards.**"

The man of about twenty-four said that was the first time anybody had put it that way and paid for four feet of flex and has not been back and nobody thanked him.

**At about a quarter to five a woman of about thirty-eight came in about ninety seconds after that with the same question, and Marek answered her in four seconds and gave her the other half as well, because he had just given it to somebody else and a thing said twice in ninety seconds is easier than a thing said once and remembered.**

"**You said that to somebody.**"

"**I did.**"

"**Why.**"

"**Because it is true and because you are not the first person to ask it this week,**" he said, "**and that is the whole of my reasons and both of them are bad.**"

**He noticed four things on that Wednesday and not one of them was on a form, and the first of the four is in a room he was in for eighty minutes and was told nothing interesting in.** A board on a wall with nine hooks on it and a key going off one of them and coming back to it inside four days, and a woman who did not say a word about the two hooks she did not look at.
A woman of about forty-four who began a sentence at a quarter past eleven and finished a different one and did not know she had.
A man of about twenty-eight who had been asked by a woman of about thirty-six who had been asked by Marek, which is how the eighth room happened and is the only route to any of the nine that he did not take himself.
And about four inches by two of card in an inside pocket.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of that Wednesday, to say the thing he has worked out.**

He has worked out that a room which says a sentence out loud to a person who carries things cannot take it back, and that the only room in nine that did not say its sentence out loud is the one that wrote it down, and that the writing down is the more durable of the two, and that he is the reason.
**He did not say it to anybody, and he is not going to, and the sentence he has worked out is about him and a thing he has been careful with for nine weeks and being careful with is how a person ends up holding the only copy.**

He said none of it.
Nine dated jobs, nine names, the last at five, the shutter at ten and the cage, and then nine minutes of walking with about four inches by two of card in a pocket and a thing worked out that nobody was going to hear.
