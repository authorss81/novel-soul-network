# Chapter 659 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Table In A Second Floor In Crown Terrace With Four Of Nine Pieces Of Paper On It And Three Weeks Old, A Woman Of About Twenty-Four Who Carried Them One At A Time And Who Said In Four Seconds That It Was Not Fair And Was Just What She Does, Four Hands On Four Different Pages In Four Different Rooms About Four Weeks Apart, And A Thing Nobody In This City Has An Arrangement For Which Is A Sheet Of Paper Coming Back With An Answer Written On It

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a table in a second floor in Crown Terrace from about three. This one is four pieces of paper, three weeks old, with four different hands on them, and nobody in this city has ever seen one before this fortnight.**

---

On the Monday there were twelve jobs with a date against each and twelve people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and from two until half past four one pair of hands was on that counter and then two were.

**The four rooms off that service road have one behind the other three, and they are warm, and the room is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven days old, or a hundred and seventy-one weeks to the day. A card on end in the rail is four days beyond that: one thousand two hundred and one days, a hundred and seventy-one weeks and four days. The rail takes two, and has taken two for eleven weeks.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and ninety-three days, a hundred and twenty-seven weeks and four days. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and eighty-seven, a hundred and forty-one weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and thirty, a hundred and eighteen weeks and four days.

The separation is five hundred and seventy-seven days, eighty-two weeks and three days, shut in a one-line box in a form about eleven weeks ago and walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**Four of the nine are on that table and they have been there for about three weeks and nobody has put them in a drawer and nobody has thrown them away and nobody has written anything on any of them except the people who wrote on them.**

She had carried them one at a time out of that room, on a Wednesday, down a stair, nine hundred yards to a bus stop, in a bag, and she had said in four seconds at the top of the stair that if a room says no to her she would keep the paper and bring it back and put it on the table and not go back in twice, and that this was not fair, it was just what she does.

"**Four,**" she said, before anybody asked her. "**You were going to ask me which four and I've been waiting three weeks for somebody to ask me which four so I could say the number and then say that the number's not the point.**"

"**What's the point.**"

"**The point's what's on them.**"

---

**There is nothing on three of them. That is the correct outcome and it is what the whole arrangement was built to make happen, and a person who hands a sheet to a room and the room says no and the sheet comes back blank has been answered.**

**On the fourth there are four words in a hand, and the four words are: *we have your form.***

He read it twice. **It is not a tick. It is not four digits. It is not a signature and it is not on a form with a column for it, and it is written on the back of a piece of paper that carries one address and nothing else, and the writing is in biro, and it is small, and it is at the bottom, and it is not signed.**

"**When.**"

"**Tuesday before last,**" she said. "**I know because it was the Tuesday and I do ironing on a Tuesday and I went back to that one on the Wednesday because I had four left and I had not finished, and the door was open and the light was on and there was nobody in and it was on the table at the front where you leave things.**"

---

"**There's a thing about that,**" said a man of about thirty-four who was in that room and had not been in it before, and who kept a warehouse and who has said one number out loud in his life that he made up.

"**There's about nine things about that,**" she said. "**Go on.**"

"**The four words say the room has the form. Not the answer. The form.**" He put a finger about an inch above the page without touching it. "**Somebody's taken a piece of paper that says *this is where to send it*, and they've written on the back of it *we have your form*, and that's not a refusal and it's not an answer, and it's the only sentence anybody's written on one of these in about four years that I know of, and I'd put money on there being nine of them in nine rooms in this city right now and four of them are on this table.**"

---

"**Why would anybody write that.**"

The man of about thirty-four looked at the four words for about nine seconds.

"**Because you turn up with a bit of paper and there's nobody there,**" he said, **"and you come back in a fortnight and the light's on and there's still nobody, and at some point you stop putting your hand on the door and you write something on the paper so that the next person who comes knows somebody came before. That's not a policy. That's what a person does.**"

"**That's exactly what a person does,**" said the woman of about twenty-four, **"and that's the worst thing anybody's said in this room in about four months, and I'd like everybody here to notice that it wasn't a villain who said it, it was a man with a warehouse who keeps a clipboard somewhere.**"

---

**Nobody thanked anybody, and a man who kept a warehouse was not thanked for saying a true thing, and a woman who does ironing three days a week on a wide road was not thanked for carrying nine pieces of paper and bringing four of them back, and the reason is on the page in four seconds: nobody in that room thanked anybody because the four words on the page were the reason and thanking somebody in a room where three blank pages and one written one are lying on a table would be a way of saying the arrangement worked, and it did not work, and everybody in that room knew it in about nine seconds.**

---

"**So what happens to it,**" he said. "**This one. The one with the four words.**"

"**It goes on the table,**" said the woman of about twenty-four. "**That's where it goes. It came back, it goes back on the table, and it stays on the table, and the next person who comes up that stair with a bag looks at it and knows somebody came before, and that's how it works, and it has no box and it has no column and nobody's keeping a list, and in about four months there'll be a stack of them and a woman of about fifty-two will say *that is not a way of filing* and she'll be right and there'll be nothing to do about it.**"

"**Except put a box on it.**"

"**Except put a box on it,**" she agreed, **"and I've thought about it for three weeks on a Wednesday and on a Saturday and I've come to the conclusion that the box is right and the tick is wrong, and I don't know how to say that to anybody without sounding like a person who wants a box.**"

---

**He wanted, in that room, on that Monday, to be the ninth piece of paper. Not the ninth address. The ninth address is a low building with a light on past a second row of shops and a door on the left with nothing written on it, and a woman of about thirty-one wrote it out from memory in about four seconds about four weeks ago, and she said a stranger would not find it and that this is on purpose.**

**He wanted it to be his.** He has spent thirteen volumes refusing to be a place a thing is sent to, and he has said no to about nine offers of being the middle in about five months, and the reason for all of it is a piece of paper with a name on it and a route through one person. **And what he wanted on that Monday was to be a door with nothing written on it, past a second row of shops, with a light on, that somebody could stand outside of on a Saturday and know that somebody had come before.**

He did not say it. He said, in four seconds: **"Four blank and one written. That's the first honest count anybody's taken about anything in about four months and I want it written down with her name on it as the person who took it, and I'm not writing it down because I don't write things down any more and everybody in here knows why.**"

"**Write it down,**" said the woman of about twenty-four.

"**No.**"

"**Then say it in a room on a Wednesday and a man with a clipboard will write it in a column he's never totalled.**"

"**He's not in this room.**"

"**He's in this building,**" she said, **"and he has a wallet of papers and he was up that stair on a Wednesday a fortnight ago, and I know because I was on the stair.**"

---

**Nobody went and got him. That is on the page and it is not a small thing and it took about four seconds of nobody moving, and the reason it took four seconds is that six people in a room all knew the same thing at the same time and all six of them knew the other five knew, and not one of them put a hand on a door.**

---

**A woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs was not in that room and has not been in it since the Wednesday before last, and she does not know about the four words, and nobody has told her, and about four people in this city know she does not know and not one of them is going to tell her this week.**

**Nobody in that room asked who had written the four words, and nobody in that room knew, and the woman of about twenty-four had been in the room where the sheet was picked up and had not looked at the writing because she was reading the address, which is on the other side, and that is the whole of how a person fails to see a thing that is four inches from their hand.**

**Four things on that Monday were not on a form, and none of the four is a thing anybody could ask him about.** Four hands in four rooms, and three of the four write like people who were taught to write at a board, and one writes like somebody who has been on a course. **A stack of about nine sheets face down under the window of that room, which has been face down since the spring and which the woman of about fifty-two has never turned over in front of anybody, and which nobody in that room turned over today either, and about four people in that room knew they were there.** And a man of about sixty-one in a corridor with a wallet of papers in a plastic bag who did not come up the stair and who was not asked to and who has been in that building for about four hours.

**And one small thing he gave up, in about four seconds, at about four o'clock, standing at the window in a second floor in Crown Terrace: he did not ask the woman of about twenty-four to go back out with the other five. There were five pieces of paper in a bag somewhere in this city and four of nine had come back and the arrangement was three weeks old and it was incomplete, and the obvious next move was for the woman who had offered to be the one who carried them to be asked to finish the job, and it is a nine-second sentence, and he did not say it, because the second she finishes she has carried all nine and she is a woman who has carried all nine and that is a job, and a job is money, and money is a route.**

---

**A green-covered book up a stair in Saltmarket is on sixty-two lines and was on sixty-two lines this morning, and the tin on the table beneath it has seventy-three in it and its lid has not been off.** Behind the chair of the woman who holds that room there is a place, and it has been unoccupied for eight weeks and five days. The ninth of those chairs stands hard against that wall with its back to the room, and it did not move, and its mover has never been named.

**One other person was in the fourth of those four rooms after half past six, thirty-odd, in the chair the door is behind, and she was still in it at about ten.** Nothing whatever was put to her and nothing ever is, and the page carrying her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight and it is in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room and it did not come out.


---

*One thousand one hundred and ninety-seven is a whole number of weeks and eight hundred and three is five days on from one, and the first of them is four rooms off a service road and the second is a man against a north wall, and four words in biro at the bottom of a piece of paper are neither of them.*
