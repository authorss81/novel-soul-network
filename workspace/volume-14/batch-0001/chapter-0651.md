# Chapter 651 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Woman Who Works In An Office In A First Floor In A Second District Who Has Had A Box Under Her Desk Since Friday Night, A Sheet Of Paper In The Hand Of A Man Who Does Not Know What To Do With It, Four Steps, And A Box At The Bottom Of It With Something In It

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is a piece of paper handed across a counter in a man's hand and a woman in a first floor in a second district who has had nine hundred of them since Friday night and told nobody.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Monday and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and shook the cage, which it has now done three times in eleven years.

**Four rooms off that service road, one of them behind the other three, are warm, and the room has been in use one thousand one hundred and eighty-three days, which is a hundred and sixty-nine weeks to the day. A card standing on end in the rail by the door is one thousand one hundred and eighty-seven days old, which is a hundred and sixty-nine weeks and four days, and it is four days past the room, and the rail has held two cards for eleven weeks and has wanted for nothing.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and seventy-nine days, a hundred and twenty-five weeks and four days. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and seventy-three, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and sixteen, a hundred and sixteen weeks and four days.

The separation is five hundred and sixty-three days, eighty weeks and three days, shut in a one-line box in a form about eleven weeks ago and walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**At about ten past nine in the morning a man of about forty put a sheet of paper on that counter and put his hand flat on it and did not take his hand off it.**

He was not a customer. He wanted a shelf bracket, and he had come in for the bracket, and the bracket was in a box behind the till with his name on a strip of masking tape, and he said the bracket could wait.

"**What's that, then,**" he said.

"Paper."

"**I've had paper.**"

He turned it round so it was the right way up for the man at the counter, and his hand came away from it, and then went back onto it, and he said: **"There's four things on it and I can do three of them and the fourth one's a box with a bit in it and it was like that when I got it."**

---

**The four steps were these, and they were correct.**

One: when a room cannot answer, the answer that came last is the answer. Two: the person who hands a sheet to a room keeps a copy, and the copy is the receipt. Three: the four steps are the same for a brace, a pump and a door, and the sequence is a hold, a count and a release, and the count goes in the order it went in the first time. Four: if a room is not there, the sheet goes into the box at the bottom and the box is emptied on a Wednesday.

**That is the whole of what is printed on the front of the sheet. There is a small drawing in the top corner, of a bracket, a pump and a bus door, and the three of them are the same shape, and the drawing is not wrong about that.**

Underneath the four steps there is a paragraph. And underneath the paragraph, at the bottom of the page, in a box with a rule round it, there is a line that says what the last approved answer was for this street, and there is a mark in that box, and the mark is a tick, and the tick is in a hand that whoever handed the sheet over could not have produced in front of him.

---

**A woman of about forty-four who works in an office in a first floor in a second district had opened that office on the Friday and found the box had come through the letter slot in the night.**

It was against the wall under the window where the bins go and it was the size of a shoebox and it had a strap round it. **She did not open it on the Friday. She opened it at about eleven on the Friday because nine hundred sheets of paper against a wall is not a thing a person leaves until Monday, and she counted them twice, and it is nine hundred and something, and the something is a number she cannot give anybody because the run was short two boxes and she has not asked for them.** She is paid by nobody to count anything. Her job is to take forms in, and she took the box, and she put it under her desk, and she told the woman she works for on the Friday afternoon that a box had come and she would deal with it on the Monday.

"**Is it ours?**" the woman she works for said.

"**I don't know. It doesn't say.**"

"**Then it's not ours.**"

"**It's got a box at the bottom of it,**" the woman of about forty-four said, "**and the box is already filled in.**"

That was the whole of the exchange and it was nine seconds long and it was in a corridor and nobody has written any of it down, and on the Monday morning at about half past eight she opened her desk drawer and looked at it and it was still there.

---

He read the sheet. He read it twice, and the second time he read it out loud under his breath without meaning to, and it took him about nine seconds, and in the nine seconds the man of about forty said, **"I can do three of them. I'd have said the first one was fine, actually, I'd have said all of it was fine, if you hadn't had the box on it."**

"**Say that again the other way round.**"

"**If you hadn't had the box on it I'd have signed it in the bin. I would have. I've got a bin.**"

"**What is it, this bin.**"

"**Outside the post office. Round the side. It's not a bin, it's a big wire thing, and it takes paper, and there's one on every street in this city that I've ever been on.**"

**Nobody in that shop has ever said out loud how many of those there are and the number was not said this morning and is not said anywhere in this entry, and the man who owns them is not a person in this book and has not been asked and will not be.**

---

**Then he asked the only question he has ever asked anybody, which was not asked of him and was not owed to him, and which took him four seconds to find the words for.**

"**Whose tick is that.**"

"**I don't know.**"

"**It's a tick in a box. Somebody ticked it.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**Then it's not nothing. If it's not nothing then the fourth thing isn't a bin, it's telling you what to put in it.**"

The man of about forty looked at the sheet for a while.

"**My name's on the copy,**" he said. "**Step two. That's the one I'd have done. I'd have kept the copy and I'd have gone home and I'd have thought that was me being careful.**" He put two fingers on the box at the bottom without touching the tick. "**And that thing's already got an answer in it, and I've had it in my coat since Friday.**"

---

**He wanted to say the man's name. He has a strip of masking tape with a name on it in a box behind the till and he could have said the name in about a second, and the reason he did not is that a name said at a counter on a Monday is a name the man carries home and puts in a coat, and he has spent thirteen volumes learning that a person in a shop is a thing a person can be found by.**

What he said instead was: **"Take it home and don't fill anything in. That's all. Don't go and find anybody, don't take it to a room, don't put it in the thing outside the post office, and if a woman comes to your door about it you say you've given it to nobody.**"

"**Right,**" said the man of about forty, and took it, and went out, and put it in his coat.

---

**At about ten past ten the same sheet came across that counter a second time and it was not folded the same way and it was in a different hand.**

This one came from a woman of about fifty-two who keeps a counter and who said, in four seconds, that she had had it since Thursday, and that she had put it in a drawer, and that the drawer is not locked and has never been locked and the reason she had put it in a drawer is that the fourth step says the box is emptied on a Wednesday and it is a Wednesday, and she wanted to see what a man in a shop would do with it.

"**You brought it to a repair shop.**"

"**There's nowhere else,**" she said. "**There's nowhere else you can take a piece of paper without it going somewhere. I've tried the other three.**"

---

**And at about four o'clock the same sheet came across that counter for the third time and nobody brought it, and it was pushed under the door of the shop with a shoe, and it was wet at one corner, and there was no way of knowing whose shoe it was or how long it had been lying there.**

He read it a third time. **It was the same four steps, and it was the same paragraph, and the box at the bottom was the same box with the same tick in it, and the tick in it was the same tick, and it was the same street on the line, and he had been in that street four times in nine years.**

That is the third of the three and there is nothing else to say about it. **He put all three on the bench, side by side, and looked at them for a long time, and they were not copies of each other, and the difference between them was the fold and the damp and about four words in the paragraph that had been re-typed, and the re-typing was worse.**

---

**Four things happened to him that Monday that are not written down anywhere, and here they are in the order he thought of them.**

A woman of about fifty-two who keeps a counter had a drawer that is not locked, and the reason she had put a sheet in it on a Thursday was that the sheet told her a Wednesday, and a Wednesday is a thing that happens to a person who has a trade. A man of about forty has a wire cage outside a post office on a round-the-side, and he has never once in nine years thought about the cage, and the sheet uses the word *bin* and the cage is not a bin and nobody told him the difference because it never mattered. **A tick that was already in a box had been in a man's coat for two days, and the man had spent the two days thinking he was being careful.** And the third sheet was wet at one corner, and somebody had walked to a repair shop on a wide road in the rain to leave a piece of paper under a door, and would have had to have a reason, and the reason could not have been that the door was open, because it was raining.

---

**He wanted, for the whole of Monday, one number. Not a count of them. A number that would let him say the thing that is actually true, which is not *these are wrong* and is *you cannot tell from the sheet whether the tick was there when you got it.* One number would have let him say it. There is no such number in this city and there has never been one and the man who would have it is a man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse and who was told something about a rota in about four months' time and who was not in that shop today and does not work in paper.**

**And one small thing he gave up on the Monday, and it was about four seconds, and it was this: he did not ask the man of about forty to come back on the Wednesday. He wanted to, because a Wednesday is when the box is emptied, and he wanted to stand in a street and watch a wire cage. He did not ask, because a man who says come back on the Wednesday has asked a man to be a person who is looking for something, and this man has a bracket with his name on it in a box behind a till and he came in for the bracket.**

The bracket went in a bag. Four pounds fifty, exact, and the man paid it and did not say thank you and he was not thanked.

---

**Nobody taught anybody anything at that counter on the Monday, and nobody was shown anything, and nobody was assessed, and nobody was helped, and a man was told the truth in four seconds about a box and was not comforted and did not ask to be.**

The three sheets are named on this page and this page does not set any two of them beside one another: a leaflet, a card on end in a rail of two, a hand copy of the front of a page, a form with a rule round the bottom of it, and a plate of iron with four slots.

That drawer under the bench is shut and there are four sheets in it and the fourth of those has never been looked at by anybody. The board on two nails has nineteen lines on it and the space under the nineteenth is about four inches of nothing that is four years old, and he put his thumb in it for about four seconds in the middle of the afternoon for the first time in about nine weeks and there is still a clean band about two inches across in the middle of it.

**One other person was in the fourth of those four rooms after half past six. She was about thirty and she was in the chair the door is behind, and she was still in it at about ten.** Nothing whatever was put to her and nothing ever is. The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight and it is in a ring binder on a back shelf in that room, and it did not come out.

**Up a stair in Saltmarket a green-covered book stood at sixty-two lines at the start of the day and at sixty-two lines at the end of it, and a tin on the table beside it holds seventy-three and its lid never came off, and there is a place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room that has been empty for eight weeks and five days.**


---

*One thousand one hundred and eighty-three and seven hundred and forty-nine are both whole numbers of weeks and the first of them is a hundred and sixty-nine weeks to the day and the second is a hundred and seven weeks to the day, and a piece of paper that is wet at one corner is not one of the sixteen figures and is not convertible into any of them.*

*654. Monday of week 237, at ten, and the first day of this stretch of days. Eleven dated jobs and eleven names, the last let in at ten past five. Three sheets across one counter between about ten past nine and about four: one in a man's hand, one out of a drawer, one under the door with a shoe. Four steps, all correct. A paragraph underneath, which is not the new part. A box at the bottom with a mark already in it, and a man who has had it in his coat since Friday believing he was being careful. A woman in a first floor in a second district with a box against a wall since Friday night, which is nine hundred and something, and the something is a number she cannot give anybody. A wire cage outside a post office that the sheet calls a bin. Four pounds fifty, exact. Nobody thanked anybody.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Monday carried eleven dated jobs and eleven callers, the last admitted at ten past five.** Callers: eleven. Refusals: two, one a request to be told which office to take a sheet to, refused in four seconds and correctly, and one a man who wanted the last sheet read back to him twice and was told he could read it. Work: eleven, two declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: fourteen pounds, exact, four pounds fifty of it for a bracket. The heating in that flat is above its winter setting for a ninth month, and four names on that one tenancy make up the difference. Those rooms: one thousand one hundred and eighty-three days, a hundred and sixty-nine weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, warm. Rail card: one thousand one hundred and eighty-seven days, a hundred and sixty-nine weeks and four days, rail of two. The board on two nails: twentieth, none, and never begun; twelfth, one thousand one hundred and three, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and four days; thirteenth, one thousand and fifty-four, a hundred and fifty weeks and four days; fourteenth, one thousand and nineteen, a hundred and forty-five weeks and four days; fifteenth, nine hundred and ninety-eight, a hundred and forty-two weeks and four days; sixteenth, nine hundred and seventy-three, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks to the day; seventeenth, nine hundred and fifty-five, a hundred and thirty-six weeks and three days; eighteenth, nine hundred and one, a hundred and twenty-eight weeks and five days. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall: seven hundred and eighty-nine days, a hundred and twelve weeks and five days. The ask: eight hundred and seventy-three days, a hundred and twenty-four weeks and five days. The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns: eight hundred and sixteen days, a hundred and sixteen weeks and four days. The post at the corridor end: seven hundred and thirty-one days, a hundred and four weeks and three days. The nine hand copies: seven hundred and forty-nine days, a hundred and seven weeks to the day, eight of the nine unfinished, the first row nobody has disagreed about still not found, and no two of the nine laid against another on any day of this fortnight. The separation: five hundred and sixty-three days, eighty weeks and three days, shut in a one-line box in a form about eleven weeks ago and subtracted every day since, no shorter, nothing put back into it.*

*Three sheets and a mark.* **A man of about forty put a paper on a counter at ten past nine and kept his hand on it, and said there were four things on it, that he could do three, and that the fourth was a box with a bit in it and it was like that when he got it. All four printed steps are correct, including the one about a brace and a pump and a door being the same shape, and the drawing in the corner is not wrong about that. Under the steps is a paragraph and under the paragraph is a box with a rule round it and a mark in it in a hand the man cannot account for. He said he would have signed it in the cage outside the post office and would have said all of it was fine if the mark had not been on the page. The same sheet came across the same counter a second time out of an unlocked drawer, from a woman of about fifty-two who wanted to see what a man in a shop would do with a Wednesday, having found there are three other places in this city you can take a piece of paper and every one of them sends it somewhere. The third came under the door with a shoe, wet at one corner, pushed by a person who would have had a reason and the reason could not have been that the door was open. All three were read twice and set side by side on a bench and none is a copy of the other, and the differences are a fold, some water, and about four words re-typed worse. The sheet is named once in this entry and is not described here as a plan, a pamphlet, a handbill or a leaflet, because a man who has read three of them in one day has not yet got a word for what they are.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **Three people in one day were handed a printed thing with a mark already in a box on it, and one of them had been carrying it in his coat for two days thinking he was careful, and a fourth person in a first floor in a second district has had about nine hundred of them under her desk since Friday night and has told nobody and has not been thanked and has not been asked whether she is all right.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter or in that office. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and three people declining to sign a printed sheet is not a third instance, because a person who will not sign a correct thing with a box under it has been made to do nothing and has had nothing happen to them, and the two that have stood on that register since about four years ago were not touched by a second of this day. Five objects get named on this page and no two of them are brought together: a leaflet, a card on end in a rail of two, a hand copy of the front of a page, a form with a box ruled at the bottom, and a plate of iron with four slots. Under that bench there is a drawer, and it is shut, and it holds four sheets, and nobody has ever looked at the fourth. The drawer in a first floor in a second district, which holds a box of a different kind altogether, is a different drawer and is not that one. The page carrying her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf, and it did not come out. Sixty-two lines in the green-covered book; seventy-three in the tin; and the distance between those two numbers is not a number and has never been printed as one.
