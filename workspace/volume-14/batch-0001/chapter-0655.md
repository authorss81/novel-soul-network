# Chapter 655 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Second Floor In Crown Terrace From About Three, One Question Asked Of The Only Person In This City Entitled To Be Asked It, A No In About Nine Seconds, Nine Rooms That Are Going To Have To Say Nine Different Things And Are Not Going To Know What The Other Eight Said, And Two Figures On This Page That Are The Same Number On Two Different Objects And Are Not A Discovery

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a second floor in Crown Terrace from about three. This one is one question, asked once, of a woman of about fifty-two, and nine seconds, and a no.**

---

Twelve jobs with a date against each on the Monday, twelve callers over the counter, the last of them in at ten past five; from two until half past four the counter was in one pair of hands and after that it was in two.

**Four rooms off a service road, one behind, warm throughout, and the room itself at one thousand one hundred and ninety days — a hundred and seventy weeks to the day. The card on end in the rail: one thousand one hundred and ninety-four days, a hundred and seventy weeks and four days. Four days separate the two, in that order, and the rail has room for two and has had it full for eleven weeks.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and eighty-six days, a hundred and twenty-six weeks and four days. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and eighty, a hundred and forty weeks to the day, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and twenty-three, a hundred and seventeen weeks and four days.

The separation is five hundred and seventy days, eighty-one weeks and three days, shut in a one-line box in a form about eleven weeks ago and walked every day since, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

---

**He went up at about ten past three with nothing in his hands, which is not a thing he has done in about four months, and six people were in that room and one of them was the woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs and who had said one word in it about four weeks ago and had not said anything since.**

He asked it straight. He did not sit down first and he did not put anything on the table, and he had the whole sentence ready and it was eleven words.

"**Will the nine rooms say one answer.**"

"**No,**" she said, and that was about nine seconds including the asking.

---

Nobody in that room said anything for about eleven seconds, and four of the six of them were against it before she said it and two of those four had said so out loud on arrival, and one man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse said, in nine seconds, that it was the only sensible thing anybody had said all week.

"**It's the opposite of sensible,**" she said. **"It's the only true thing anybody has said all week, and you want to know the difference and the difference is that sensible is a thing you can put in a column.**"

She put both hands flat on the table, which she had not done at any point in about nineteen years of holding that room, or at any point in about four months that anybody has been in it.

"**A sheet of paper with four correct things on it and a box at the bottom is not beaten by nine rooms saying the same thing in nine accents. It is beaten by nine rooms saying nine different things, and the only way to get nine different things out of nine rooms is to not agree about anything and to not be told what the other eight have said.**"

---

"**Then nobody will know what the nine said.**"

"**No.**"

"**And if one of them says something you think is wrong.**"

"**Then it will be in a room where nine people are and not in a column with my name over it,**" she said, **"and that is the whole of what a woman who chairs about nine chairs can give you this afternoon, and I have given you a no, and a no is not an order, and I want that understood before anybody goes up that stair with it, because if it is an order then I have put a woman in the middle of nine rooms and we are exactly where we were in the spring.**"

"**It's not an order.**"

"**Then it's worth having,**" she said, **"and you will not get a thank you for it, and there is nothing to be thanked for, because I have not done a thing. I have refused a thing.**"

---

**A man of about thirty-four who had driven up from a yard said the thing that four people in that room were thinking and that nobody else was going to say.**

"**Then somebody has to be able to tell whether they've said it,**" he said. "**Not you. Not a committee. Somebody. Because nine different sentences is fine until a person's in trouble and then it's nine people each waiting for the other eight, and that's not a federation, that's nine people alone with a piece of paper.**"

"**It's nine people alone with a piece of paper and that is the correct outcome,**" said the woman of about fifty-two, **"and I have held a room on a Wednesday for nineteen years and I have watched about four hundred people in this city find out that a person who will not be alone with a piece of paper is a person who will be alone with a person, and the second of those is worse, and neither of them is on a printed sheet with a box at the bottom of it, and I have got nothing to give you this afternoon except the no.**"

"**You could put a name in it,**" said the man of about thirty-four. "**One name. Somebody anybody can go to.**"

"**I could,**" she said, **"and in about four months you would be telling me the name is the reason nine rooms can't answer, and you would be right, and I would have done it in about four seconds.**"

**Nobody argued after that. The man of about thirty-four put his pen down and did not pick it up again for the rest of the afternoon, and he has not said a word in that room since, and he was not thanked for the question and was not thanked for not arguing.**

**A man of about thirty-three with nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over was not in that room and has not been in it since the spring, and there is a rectangle of clean concrete under the ninth of those crates the exact size of the bottom of it, and none of that was discussed and none of it is connected to any of the above and nobody in that room knew any of it.**

A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard and one column of numbers that has never been totalled wrote nothing in that column for about eleven seconds and then wrote a number in it, and **the number was not nine and was not the count of the six people in the room, and it has not been asked what it was and will not be.**

He wanted, standing in that room with nothing in his hands, to be the ninth chair with its back to the room. **That is not a want this manuscript has printed before in this shape and he has not got a name for it and it took him about four seconds to understand that what he was actually asking for was to be the one in the room that nobody looks at, and that about four people in this city have already arranged for that to happen to somebody else and have never mentioned it.**

---

**And then he had a second question in his mouth, and the second question was: *and when they are all different, who says so.* He did not ask it, and the reason he did not ask it is that the answer is her, and she has just told him, in eleven seconds and twice, that the answer is nobody, and a man who asks a question in order to be told a thing he already knows is a man buying himself a place in a conversation.**

Instead he said: **"Nine rooms, nine sentences, and nobody collects them."**

"**Nine rooms, nine sentences, and nobody collects them, and that is not going to be a plan, and it is going to look like nothing has happened,**" she said. **"In about six weeks somebody in this city is going to stand up in a room and say *we did it* about nine different sentences, and they will all be talking about the same thing and none of them will have read the other eight. That is the arrangement and not an accident. If you want a document you have come to the wrong room four months late.**"

---

**He left that room at about half past four and there were two people on a pavement outside a shop on a wide road at nine feet apart, and neither of them knew the other was one of the nine, and that is the second time in this manuscript that has happened and it happened at four o'clock on a Wednesday three weeks ago, and he was in the room and not on the pavement and did not go out.**

**There were seven hundred and fifty-six days on a bench in Lattice Ward where a hand copy of the front of a page was begun, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall has been at that wall for seven hundred and ninety-six days, and those are the same two numbers in the other order and the fact of it is a coincidence between two integers and nothing else, and no person on this page looked at the other one and no person on this page has ever looked at the other one.**

The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the room and did not move, and nobody in this city has ever said who put it there. **There are about nine chairs in that room and he counted them for the first time in about four months and there are nine, and the gap between the third and the fourth is wider than the gaps between any of the other pairs, and the person who put it there is not in the room and has never been named.**

---

**There were four things on that Monday with no form against them, and he has them in his head in an order he has never written down.** Nine chairs, and one of them at an angle to the wall that nobody has ever mentioned. The two public rules she signed a thing under about three weeks ago are about nineteen years old and she has never read either of them and said so once. **A pen on that table that has been the same pen for nineteen years and that has a flattened spot on one side about the length of a thumbnail, and a man of about sixty-one who writes a number in a column every time he is in that room has never once offered to lend her a different one.** And, under the window, a stack of about nine sheets of paper face down, which have been face down since the spring and which she has never turned over in front of anybody and which are not in this entry.

**And one small thing he gave up, in about four seconds, standing at the window in a second floor in Crown Terrace at about four o'clock: he had begun, in his own hand, on the back of a job docket, at about half past two, a fifth step. He got as far as four words of it and he put the docket in the fourth pocket with the other four things in there, and he has not written any of the other five steps, and he is not going to, and the reason is that a fifth step is a thing nine rooms can be given, and he is not a room, and about four people in this city have worked that out and none of them said it to him.**
