# Chapter 652 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Woman Of About Thirty-One In A Supply Room In A School In A Town A Day And A Night Of Bus Travel Away Who Has Been The Name At The Foot Of A Form Since The Thursday Of Week 234, A Sentence Of Four Words That Took Her Four Seconds To Say, A Box She Has Already Filled In And Cannot Take Back, And A Bus That Runs On A Wednesday And On A Saturday And On No Other Day

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is a supply room four days of bus travel away, four seconds at about ten past one, and a form that stopped eleven days ago.**

---

Twelve jobs carried a date against each on the Wednesday and twelve people came over the counter, the last let in at ten past five, and the counter was in one pair of hands from two until half past four and then in two.

**She was in that supply room at about ten past one and the supply room has nine boxes in it and eight of them are labelled in another hand and the ninth is not, and the ninth is a box the size of a shoebox and she had put a printed sheet into it on the Friday before last.**

It is four days there and back. **The bus runs on a Wednesday and on a Saturday and on no other day, and she came on the Wednesday and she is in this city until the Saturday and has been in this city since last Saturday and was not in it the Saturday before that.**

She said his name first and then did not say anything else for about nine seconds, and the nine seconds were not awkward and were not a pause for effect, and they were a woman deciding how much of it she was going to do.

"**Right,**" she said. "**Four things and I've only got time for one of them, so I'll do that one.**"

---

"**Your thing's finished.**"

She said it in four seconds and it was nine words and she did not soften it and she did not say which thing, and there was no reason she should have, because there has only ever been one thing.

"**Say the date on it again.**"

"**The Thursday of the week you call two hundred and thirty-four. Eleven days. That's what the two boxes say, a date and a number, and eleven is days, and you said the number out loud once and I heard you.**" She put two fingers on the edge of the shoebox and did not open it. "**It's been finished for seven days. I worked it out on the Monday because I count everything in this room, and I'm not paid to and I do it anyway, and that's the whole of why I know and it's not anybody telling me.**"

He did not say anything for about four seconds.

"**Who knows?**"

"**I know. You know. The woman who signs it knows because her name is at the foot of it and she never signed it and nobody asked her to.**" She said that in nine seconds and flatly and as though she were reading a shelf. "**And the man who asked for it, who I'm not talking about, does not know. That's the part I've been carrying since Monday.**"

---

"**Who else should know?**"

"**Nobody, and that's the sentence, and you wrote it.**" She turned the shoebox about a quarter turn on the table. "**Nobody is chased. No letter goes anywhere. It's on the sheet in your own words and I've read it and the words are right, and the words are the reason I know and the reason nobody else does.**"

"**Then it's done.**"

"**It's done,**" she said, **"and I want to say the other half, because you're going to hear the first half and stop, and I've watched you do that four times in about five weeks and I'm not doing it again today.**"

He waited.

"**A thing that stops by itself is a thing that was never properly going.**" **She said it in about nine seconds and she said it to a shoebox and not to him.** "**If I'd had to be told, I'd have had a day to do something about it, and I'd have wasted it, and then in three weeks I'd have said to you that it was a stupid thing to be running and you'd have said yes and we'd both have been wrong. Instead it stopped, and I found out by counting boxes, and now it's just stopped, and that's the first clean thing that's happened to that room since the spring.**"

Nobody in that room said anything for about eleven seconds. There was a bell somewhere outside and it was not for anybody.

---

"**Now the other thing, and it's the one I've got the box for.**"

She turned the shoebox round and put it in front of him and did not open the lid.

"**You've read one.**"

"**I've read three. Two of them came across a shop counter on a Monday.**"

"**Then you'll know the four steps are right.**" She said it without any weight at all. "**They're right. I've run this room for about four years and every one of those four things is something I've been doing badly, and doing it four times a day, on a bad day, with two of us, and nobody wrote it down. Someone wrote it down. That's all step one is. It's a good piece of writing and I'd not have sent it out myself because of the box.**"

"**And the box.**"

"The box is the only new thing in the world." **She said it in four seconds and then she said, in nine, what she had actually come to say, which was that she had put a printed sheet into the shoebox on the Friday before last at about four in the afternoon, and that the clinic is nine miles end to end and there are about forty people on the list on a Thursday, and that the four steps are correct, and that she did it anyway and she cannot undo it.**

"**There were nine of us waiting and I put it in and I shut the lid, and I'd do it again on Thursday, and I have not got a reason you can put on a form.**"

---

**He wanted to be the person she is waiting for on a Saturday when the bus has not come. It is a fourth shape of a want he has had three other ways in this manuscript and has never been in a position to have it in this one, and the reason is a sentence he wrote himself and had somebody else read.**

What he said was: **"You did the right thing and I can't tell you whether it was the right thing and I'm not going to be the one who tells you after the fact. If somebody comes to you with a sheet and there's a box on it, you hand it back to them and you put it on the table and you don't go in twice. That's the arrangement. It's not mine. It was there before me.**"

"**It was there before you,**" she said, **"and it's about four years old, and the four steps on that sheet are about four years old, and the box under the four steps is not four years old. You know that.**"

"**I know that.**"

"**Then somebody has added a fifth thing to a piece of paper that was already true,**" she said, **"and they haven't changed one word of the four, and that's the part that frightens me, and I have not told anybody that and I am telling you and you are not going to do anything with it today, are you.**"

"**No.**"

"**Good,**" she said, and put her hand flat on the lid of the shoebox, and said, **"that's the first time in about four months that somebody has said no to me in the first nine seconds.**"
