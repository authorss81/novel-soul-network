# Chapter 658 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Waiting Room With A Timetable On The Wall On Which Every Day Except Two Has Been Crossed Out In Pencil Over About Four Years, Eight Rooms That Can Say One Sentence Each For Nothing At All, A Ninth That Is Four Days Of Bus Travel There And Back And Cannot, A Figure Given In About Nine Seconds By A Man Of About Thirty-Eight Who Tells Everybody In The Room That He Has Made It Up, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Does Not Offer

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is nine rooms, eight of which can say one sentence each for nothing, and one of which cannot, and a number that is a man's and not a budget.**

---

On that Friday there were twelve dated jobs and twelve people at that counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten, and he was booked on a bus for the Saturday that leaves before he could have finished a conversation in that room.

**Behind the three rooms off that service road there is a fourth, and the four are warm, and the room stands at one thousand one hundred and ninety-four days, being a hundred and seventy weeks and four days. The rail by the door has a card on end in it — one thousand one hundred and ninety-eight days, a hundred and seventy-one weeks and one day, four days past the room — and the rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and ninety days, a hundred and twenty-seven weeks and one day. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and eighty-four, a hundred and forty weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and twenty-seven, a hundred and eighteen weeks and one day.

Five hundred and seventy-four days is eighty-two weeks to the day, and that is the separation, which ended in a one-line box in a form about eleven weeks ago and has been walked every day since without a day being taken off it, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

**Eight of the nine said their sentence within about four minutes of being asked and none of them asked why.**

The school on a wide road said: *we will not have a supply room standing open for anybody we have not agreed to have standing in it.* The water office said: *we will put our own name on our own answers.* The depot with a gate onto a yard said: *we will answer about our own vans and not about anybody else's.* The council house in a street with trees on one side said: *we will say no out loud and in the room and not in a form.* The kitchen in a back yard said: *we will not answer after eleven at night and that is all we will not do.* The room above the line in Saltmarket said nothing and wrote nine words and put it face down on the table and asked for it to be taken away, and it was taken away.

**And there are two more in towns, and one of those two said its sentence in about four minutes and one of those two is a day and a night of bus travel away.**

---

"**Say it again for me.**"

"**You want to know what it costs me.**"

"**I want to know what it costs you.**"

The woman of about thirty-one was in a supply room in a school on the far side of that town and it was about half past three there, or about half past two here, and he had eleven minutes before the Saturday bus and had not asked to be put on it.

"**It costs one bus,**" she said. "**There and back. Two of them, if you count going and coming, and the second one's a Saturday and the Saturday's the same as the Wednesday. I have a season ticket on the Wednesday and I haven't had one on the Saturday since the spring and I'm not buying one.**"

"**That's about nine pounds.**"

"**That's about nine pounds and you worked that out in about four seconds and I'd have got there in about nine,**" she said, **"and I want to say the rest of it, and the rest of it is: I am not asking you for nine pounds, and if a man in this city offers me nine pounds I will say no, and I have said no to two people about it already this week and one of them was a woman who was being kind at me.**"

---

**A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse was in that room and had not been asked for a figure and gave one in about nine seconds.**

"**Nine pounds a week,**" he said, **"to one address, four days there and back, every week, for as long as this goes on, is about four hundred and fifty a year. I have not got that out of that warehouse, and I have seen the book they keep and they have not got it either, and neither has anybody else in this city, and that's a number I made up in about nine seconds and I want everybody in this room to know that I made it up and to write it down as mine.**"

"**Why are you telling us that.**"

"**Because in about three weeks somebody is going to stand up and use it,**" he said, **"and if it's on this table first with my name on it, then when they use it, they'll be quoting a man who told them it was his own guess. That's all. That's the whole of it and I'm not asking anybody for anything.**"

---

Nobody in that room said a word for about eleven seconds.

A woman of about fifty-two who chairs about nine chairs was not in that room. A man of about sixty-one with a clipboard was not in that room. **Six people were in it and four of them were people he had met in this fortnight and two of them he had not and neither of the two has ever given their name to anybody in this city and both of them are on a list of nine addresses that does not exist.**

"**Right,**" he said. **"Then it isn't a budget, it's a man being honest in a room, and both of those are things that have happened in this city before.**"

---

**And then he did not offer, and not offering was the hardest four seconds of that Friday, and it was hard because about five weeks ago he had lost in about nine seconds the first thing he ever proposed in this city, which was to pay one woman for the time it takes to answer questions honestly, and he had never gone near it again, and there were nine pounds sitting on a table in a room four days of bus travel there and back and it would have taken him four seconds to say.**

**He did not say it, and the reason, which he gave to nobody and which took him about four seconds to work out standing up, is that a man who pays the fare is a man the sentence goes to, and about five weeks ago a woman with a hand truck said a thing to him that has been the shape of everything since, and a man who pays the fare is a man with a route.**

---

"**You didn't offer,**" the man of about thirty-eight said, at about half past two here.

"**No.**"

"**I wasn't watching for it. I want that on record, in whatever way you take records. I wasn't watching for it.**"

"**You were watching for it.**"

"**I was not,**" said the man of about thirty-eight, **"and I'll tell you why, because it matters and it took me four seconds: if I'd watched for it and you'd not done it, I'd have known something about you, and I don't need to know something about you, and I'd rather not.**"

---

**The woman of about thirty-one said one more thing before he got on the bus and it was nine words and it was not to him.**

"**Nine pounds, four hundred and fifty, a season ticket, a Wednesday and a Saturday, four days there and back. Put them on one piece of paper with nothing on it and no names, and if anybody wants to know what nine sentences cost they can do that sum themselves.**"

"**That is a return path.**"

"**It is a piece of paper with five figures on it and no addresses and no box and no tick,**" she said, **"and if you put one address on it you can have it back.**"

**At about six o'clock, in this city, in a waiting room at a bus station that is not this city, a woman of about sixty-two who sells tickets looked at a sheet of paper about the size of a postcard with five figures on it and no addresses on it, and turned it over, and put it in a drawer with a rubber band round about nine others of the same size, and there were eleven, and they had been there since the spring, and they were not hers, and she has never asked.**

**That is on another page of this fortnight and not on this one, and it is not a thing anybody in this city did, and the drawer is not the drawer under the bench and the eight sheets in it are eight sheets of a different kind entirely and are never put beside one another in a sentence.**
