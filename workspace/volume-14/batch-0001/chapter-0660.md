# Chapter 660 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Took A Strip Of Paper Off A Form On The Monday And Who Has Been Asked Eleven Times And Once By A Man In Overalls, A Man Of About Fifty-Eight Who Came Nine Hundred Yards To Ask Whether To Print A Fourth Run And Who Is Going To Print One Anyway For A Reason He Gives In About Nine Seconds And Which Is Correct, A Chase That Goes Back In A Van With The Money, And Nine Sentences In Nine Rooms And No Two Of Them The Same Shape And No Document Anywhere

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is a strip of paper taken off a form on the Monday, a man who came to ask a question and answered it himself, and nine sentences that do not know about each other.**

---

Nine jobs with a date against each on the Wednesday, nine people over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter down at ten, shaking the cage for the fourth time in eleven years.

**Warm, all four of them, with a fifth room behind the three on that service road, and the figure for the room is one thousand one hundred and ninety-nine days — a hundred and seventy-one weeks and two days. A card stands on end in the rail by the door, four days past the room and two to a rail: one thousand two hundred and three days, a hundred and seventy-one weeks and six days.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and ninety-five days, a hundred and twenty-seven weeks and six days. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and eighty-nine, a hundred and forty-one weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and thirty-two, a hundred and eighteen weeks and six days.

It is walked today because it is walked every day, and it has been since a form about eleven weeks ago took one line in one box, and it is five hundred and seventy-nine days, or eighty-two weeks and five days, and no day of it has been taken off, and nothing has been put back into it.

**She came in at about ten past four and did not take her coat off, which meant four minutes, and she had a strip of paper in her hand about the length of a ruler with glue residue on one edge of it.**

"**Off on the Monday.**"

"**How.**"

"**It came off in my hand. That's the thing, it came off in my hand, I didn't prise it, I picked the form up and it came away and I've got it here.**" **She was holding it up.** "**So there's a blank column on about nine forms and there's a form on the desk and a form on the pad and all three of them have got a blank column on them now, and I did that, and I'd do it again, and I've been asked eleven times since Monday what the second column was for.**"

"**By who.**"

"**Nine of them were the same question. Two of them were different.**"

---

"**The two.**"

"**One was a woman of about forty who wanted to know if the four steps were from the council. And one was a man of about fifty in overalls who said** ***is it the number of the box.***"

He did not say anything for about four seconds.

"**Did he say it like a joke.**"

"**He said it like a man reading a form,**" she said. "**He'd come in for a bolt and he read the form on the desk while he waited and he said it out loud, and then he looked up, and I said I didn't know, and he said *fair enough* and bought his bolt.**"

**Nobody in that shop has ever been asked what the second column is by a person reading a form on the counter, and about four people in this city have thought about the box for about three weeks and not one of them has thought about a man in overalls at a counter.**

---

**At about half past five, with the shutter half down and the light on in the window, Colin Nall came nine hundred yards up the road from Saltmarket and stood in the doorway for about nine seconds before he came in, which is about nine seconds longer than it takes him to cross a floor of his own shop.**

"**Am I talking to the counter or the man.**"

"**Both of them today.**"

"**Right.**" He put a printed sheet on the counter. **He did not have a bag and he did not have a box and he had come on foot.** "**I handed the money over on the Monday in a fold of paper and there was no order, so I came to ask whether there's going to be a fourth run.**"

"**I don't know.**"

"**Right.**"

"**That's the answer and it's the true one and it's about four seconds and you can go and print a fourth or not print a fourth and I can't tell you which is better.**"

---

**The man of about fifty-eight looked at the sheet for about eleven seconds and then he gave the answer himself, and he gave it in nine seconds, and it was the correct answer, and Marek did not thank him for it and did not ask him to do the other thing.**

"**I'll print one,**" said the man of about fifty-eight. "**Here's why and it's not a policy and it's not a favour to you. If I don't, a fella comes who'll set it fresh off your four steps and somebody typing out that paragraph again, and in about four years that paragraph's a piece of metal in a sack, and whatever anybody wanted it to do, it won't.**"

"**That's true.**"

"**It's not good,**" he said. "**It's true and it isn't good and I know both of those at the same time and I'm sixty next year and I print on Tuesdays and Fridays.**"

---

"**The chase.**"

"**Back in the van on Monday with the money. It's in the van now.**"

"**You could take it out.**"

"**I could,**" he said, **"and I've not, and if I had it in a drawer in a locked shop on a road off Saltmarket then I'm a man with a paragraph, and a man with a paragraph is a man with a copy, and a copy is a thing that gets reprinted by people who weren't there.**"

**Nobody said thank you and nobody said well done and the man of about fifty-eight said neither and left at about ten past six and went back on foot the nine hundred yards.**

**And he did not go up the stair in Saltmarket, and there was nobody waiting at the top of it and nothing on the table for him, and he has not been up it in about five weeks and did not go up it on this Wednesday, and there was nothing in that room he could have done and nobody would have spoken to him and he was not asked for anything.**

**Nine rooms have said one sentence each in about three weeks. Three of the nine have since written four words on the back of a returned sheet. Nine sheets of paper with one address on each went out and four came back and five have not and nobody has said where they are. A strip of paper has come off a form. A chase has gone back in a grey van with a fold of paper in it. A paragraph about what to do when a room cannot answer is on the back of a delivery note in a wallet in a drawer in a building, and it is at least twenty-nine years old and nobody can say how much more, and the only reason it is still on earth is that a man of about fifty-eight with a machine older than his partnership could not be bothered to throw a tea chest away in the spring.**

**No number was given out in this city or in any other today. A Wednesday in this city is not a Wednesday anything is given out on unless it is every fourth one, and this was not one, and nothing here describes what happens on a Wednesday when there is no hour of it.** The book with the green cover stood at sixty-two lines on its shelf before this day and is on sixty-two lines now, and the tin on that table is on seventy-three, and the lid did not come off, and the place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room has been empty for eight weeks and five days and about four people in this city have noticed and not one of them has said so.

**The sixth of the nine jobs that day was a lock.**

It was a euro cylinder in a back door of a house on a street with a lorry parked on it, and the woman who wanted it changed had brought the old one in a piece of newspaper and said the key had gone in the spring and had not been right since and that she had been meaning to do it about four months ago. It took eleven minutes. She asked, while he was in the door, whether the leaflet was any relation to it.

"**No.**"

"**It came through that letter box.**"

"**It did.**"

"**I didn't do anything,**" she said. **"I just want to say that, because there's a man on that road who's been telling people since about a week ago that everybody's had to do it. And I haven't.**"

"**He's not wrong that you can.**"

"**He's not wrong that you can and he's wrong that people have,**" she said, **"and those are two different things and in about three weeks they're going to be the same thing, and I thought I'd say it now while I could still remember saying it.**"

He did not write it down. She went out with the new cylinder in the newspaper and did not say thank you and was not thanked, and he was left on a step with a euro in his hand for about four seconds.

**The fourth of the nine jobs that day was a bracket, and it was the same bracket, and the man who brought the first sheet on the Monday was not the man who brought it and the man who brought it had been in the shop in the spring and knew the price, and nobody said anything about the sheet, and he did not have one, and Marek did not ask whether he had one.**

**Nobody was thanked; nine jobs went out of that counter and not one of the nine people who paid for them said a thing on the way out that was meant for him. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two and was not moved by a second of any of it.**

**He noticed four things on that Wednesday and none of them was on a form.** A woman of about fifty in overalls who reads a form on a counter while he waits for a bolt, which about four people in this city have never thought about, and who would not have said it if the form had not been lying there. The strip of paper, in a hand that has glue on it, which will not go back on and which is not thrown away, and which is now in a fourth pocket. Nine hundred yards of road off Saltmarket that a man of fifty-eight walked twice in one evening, once to ask a question he could have asked himself and once to go home, and both times on foot. And a door in Lattice Ward with a card on the outside of it that he has read about nine times a day for five days and has never once been stopped in the street by anybody about.

**And one small thing he gave up at about ten past six with the shutter down, in about four seconds, and it was this: he did not ask the man of about fifty-eight for the third run's sheet to keep. There was one on the counter and it was the only physical object in this city with a paragraph on it that anybody could hand to a person, and he did not ask for it and the man of about fifty-eight did not offer it, and neither of them mentioned it, and it went out of the door in a coat pocket with a man who is going to stop printing on Fridays.**

**And there was a sixth thing, which was that a man of about forty came in at about ten to six and asked whether the shop was taking work on a Saturday, and was told that it was, and asked whether the man who did the Saturdays was the same man, and was told that it was, and said right, and went, and Marek stood there for about nine seconds afterwards with a bracket in his hand and worked out that in about three weeks somebody in this city was going to come in and ask for him by name and a day, and that he would be the only man in the shop, and that this was the correct outcome of everything he had done in the last ten days and the reason the four rooms off that service road are warm.**

**And the one thing he wanted, for the whole of the ten days, was for one of the four steps to be wrong. Not wrong enough to stop them. Wrong enough that he could have said so and been right about it, in about nine seconds, to a man of about forty who had it in his coat. **In the fourth of those four rooms, after half past six, there was one other person, thirty-odd, and the chair by the door had her in it until about ten.** Nothing whatever was put to her and nothing ever is. The page with her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight, shut in a ring binder at the back of a shelf in that room, and it did not come out.
