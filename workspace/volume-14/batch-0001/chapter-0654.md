# Chapter 654 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Two-Man Print Shop On A Road Off Saltmarket With One Machine In It That Is Older Than The Partnership, A Man Of About Fifty-Eight Who Is Not Going To Say Who Set The Type And Is Going To Say Everything Else, A Pallet Of Dead Type In A Tea Chest Bought For About Nine Pounds, A Paragraph That Has Not Been Printed In Twenty-Nine Years And Was On The Type Anyway, And Four Words That Took Nine Seconds

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is about nine hundred yards off a counter, down a road off Saltmarket, through a door with a bell on it, and about eleven minutes with a man who is right about paper.**

---

Twelve jobs carried a date against each on the Friday and twelve people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and did not shake the cage, which it has now done three times in eleven years and did not do this time.

**There are four rooms off that service road with a fifth behind them, and they are warm, and the figure for the room is one thousand one hundred and eighty-seven days, which reads a hundred and sixty-nine weeks and four days. A card in the rail is one thousand one hundred and ninety-one days or a hundred and seventy weeks and one day, four days on from the room, and the rail holds two and has held two for about eleven weeks.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and eighty-three days, a hundred and twenty-six weeks and one day. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and seventy-seven, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks and four days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and twenty, a hundred and seventeen weeks and one day.

Eighty-one weeks to the day is five hundred and sixty-seven days, and that is the separation, and it is a day count and not a gap: one line went into a box in a form about eleven weeks ago, and it has been added to since, every day, without stopping and without anything being put back in.

---

**He did not go in to ask whether it was him. He went in because there are four print shops within a mile of that counter and three of them have closed and the fourth one has a bell on the door and a man in it who was in the trade when the man of about thirty-eight who keeps a warehouse was doing his accounts.**

The bell is one of those that is a coiled spring and a clapper and it has not been serviced and it goes off about two notes above the one it is supposed to go off at.

---

**He asked one question and it took him nine seconds to find the words for it, and it was not *did you print it* and it was not *who told you to*.**

"**How many times have you printed it?**"

"**Three.**" The man of about fifty-eight did not look up, because he had heard the bell and had already decided what it was. "**Three runs. First was about four hundred and forty, second was about four hundred and sixty, and the third is short.**"

"**Short how.**"

"**Two boxes.**" He put a hand on the back of the machine without touching it, which is a thing you do to a machine. "**I asked for them. He said he'd bring them Thursday and he didn't, and that's the whole of the shortfall and I'll not be able to tell you it's two boxes and neither will he, and that's the first true thing I've said about that paper and it's the one you'd want least.**"

---

"**Right. Who sets it.**"

"**I don't.**"

"**You don't know.**"

"**I don't set it, because it comes in a chase.**" **He said it in nine seconds and then he stopped, because he had just told a stranger something about how his shop works that he has not told anybody in about four years.** "**Locked up. Tight. You print it, you take the strapping off, you run it, and it goes back in the van with the money.**"

"**A van.**"

"**A grey one, no lettering, and a man about thirty-one drives it and he has been four times and he is not from this city and I have known that for three of the four.**"

"**And you don't know who sends it.**"

"**I know he's not the man who sends it,**" said the man of about fifty-eight, **"because on the third one he said a thing while I was banding it and he didn't know I was listening, and what he said was *it's not mine either*, and he wasn't talking to me, he was talking to the driver, who had come in off the yard for a collection docket and was standing at the end of my bench with his back to me, and the man in the grey said it twice.**"

---

**He looked at the machine for a while and it is a Heidelberg, and it is older than the partnership, and there is a man about thirty-three in this city who has a machine like it and does not print and that is the only other Heidelberg in this entry.**

"**Where's the type.**"

"**There's no type. It's a chase.**"

"**Where's the chase.**"

"**That's the thing I want to tell you about,**" the man of about fifty-eight said, and got up, and unlocked a drawer under the counter, and took out a wooden box about the size of a tea chest, and put it on the counter between them, and it was shut.

---

**He had bought the chest on a Tuesday in the spring for about nine pounds off a man who was clearing a shop that had shut on a road two streets the other way, and he had bought it because dead type is dead type and everybody knows it and nobody wants it, and he had taken the whole pallet without looking at it because nine pounds is nine pounds.**

He lifted the lid and there was type in it in about nine different states, most of it worn past reading, and near the top, under a cloth, there was a chase.

"**That came out of that in March,**" he said. "**Still locked. Still tight. Somebody locked a paragraph up about the time I was at school and threw the whole thing in a box and then the box went in a shop and then the shop shut.**"

"**Print it and see.**"

"**I've printed it three times,**" the man of about fifty-eight said, **"and the paragraph under the four steps comes off that, and it comes off it the same every time, and I never had to set a word of it.**"

---

He read it. **It is a paragraph of about nine lines about what to do when a room cannot answer, and it is not on the front of the sheet as a set of steps, and it is in the plainest hand any of them will read, and he has read it before, and he has read it in this city on the back of a page of paper more times than he has read it on the front of anything.**

The third sheet that came under a door on a Monday had four words in that paragraph re-typed worse than the other two. He has been carrying that difference around for two days.

"**This is on the back of a form,**" he said.

"**It's a foot of type. It doesn't know what it's on.**"

"**What was the front of it.**"

"**I don't know. I never lifted it out of the chase and I've had three weeks and I never lifted it out, and that's on me, and I'd have lifted it out the week before if you'd come on the Wednesday instead of the Friday.**"

---

"**How old is it.**"

"**I can't tell you. Type doesn't carry a date.**" The man of about fifty-eight put his hand flat on the lid of the chest. "**What I can tell you is the letters on the back of it are worn, and the letters on the front of it are not, and that's because the back is what gets looked at and the front is what gets left. There's a page of paper been printed off that thing maybe four thousand times. Maybe more. Nobody on this earth knows and the reason nobody knows is that the front got thrown away years before anybody thought to ask me about the back.**"

**He said that in about nine seconds and then he stopped and looked at the door and said, "You'll want to know there's two things in that chest that aren't dead."**

"**What's the other one.**"

"**A chase with nine ruled lines in it and no type in them at all,**" he said. "**Nothing in it. Nine lines and nothing in it, and I have had it since March and I have not had a use for it and I have not thrown it out, and I would not throw it out and I couldn't tell you why and I've stopped asking myself.**"

---

**He wanted to be wrong about the type. That is the whole of what he wanted on that Friday and it is a want he has never had in this shape in about four months of not being able to see the whole city, and there was no room on earth for it. He wanted the man in front of him to be the last man in a line and to be in the wrong, and the man in front of him was in the right about every single thing he had asked and had a chase with nine empty ruled lines in it that he would not throw out.**

"**Will you stop.**"

"**I've got the third run to hand over on Monday and then I'm done,**" the man of about fifty-eight said. "**There's no order. Nobody's given me an order, so there's nothing to invoice, so the money's been in a fold of paper in a drawer since the first one and I don't know what to call it and I haven't called it anything. I'm sixty next year and there's me and there's a lad on Thursdays and he can do forms on his own, and I'd go Tuesdays and Fridays.**"

"**Then what is on the fold of paper.**"

"**My name,**" said the man of about fifty-eight. "**Colin Nall, and it has been a fold of paper with a name on it since the first one and it has never once been an invoice, and a name on a piece of paper is a thing a person can be found by, and I would rather be a thing you can find by the road.**"

"**I am not going to write it down.**"

"**You were never going to,**" he said. "**That is not why I said it.**"

"**And the nine boxes.**"

"**Nine boxes, four and a bit prints, and the paragraph comes off the same type each time,**" he said, **"and if somebody comes in here in a year and asks me where it came from I'll tell them a man in a grey van and a chest I paid nine pounds for, and that's a bad answer and it's the only one I've got.**"

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop, and the man of about fifty-eight did not offer him a cup of anything and he did not ask, and the bell went off about two notes high when he went out and it did that on the way in as well.**

Four things on that Friday are on no form anywhere, and the fifth was his own thumb in four inches of dust. A letter on the type that is worn on one face and not the other, so the paragraph reads properly one way up and reads slightly wrong the other way up, and he read it both ways. A tea chest with a cloth under the lid and no dust on the cloth at all, which means the lid has been lifted more than once, by him, and he did not say so and was not asked. Nine lines in a chase with nothing in them, and the nine is not a figure anybody in this city has connected to anything. **And a machine older than a partnership, with a belt on it that has been taped once and taped well.**

**And one small thing he gave up, in about four seconds, at a counter on a road off Saltmarket at about ten past twelve: he did not ask whether the man of about fifty-eight had a copy of the first run. He had one. Every shop keeps one, and it was in the drawer with the fold of paper, and it was going to have the same four steps on it and the same box at the bottom, and there was a man of about thirty-four who had carried one in his coat since Friday and had been thinking he was being careful, and the man of about fifty-eight had a hundred of them under his counter and had not been thinking anything at all, and Marek did not ask, because the question would have been *for me*, and a question that is for me is a reason to be in a room.**

**At about four o'clock the same day, in a town a day and a night of bus travel away, a woman of about thirty-one in a supply room took a printed sheet out of a shoebox with a lid and read the paragraph underneath the four steps, and it took her about nine seconds, and then she sat down on the edge of a table with a box of staples in her hand and did not say anything to anybody for about four minutes.** She is not named in that room and nothing in this entry has her name in it.

**Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped at any point on that Friday. A room up a stair in Saltmarket has a book in it with a green cover standing at sixty-two lines and a tin on the same table with seventy-three in it, and the lid did not come off, and the place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room has been empty for eight weeks and five days.**


---

*Seven hundred and thirty-five and nine hundred and fifty-nine are both figures on this page and both are whole numbers of weeks, a hundred and five weeks to the day and a hundred and thirty-seven weeks to the day, and a paragraph worn on one face and not the other is not one of the sixteen figures and has no age on it.*

*657. Friday of week 237, at ten, and the fourth day. Twelve dated jobs and twelve names, the last let in at ten past five, a shutter down at ten that did not shake the cage. The other thing in that box is a chase with nine ruled lines in it and nothing in them. The printer is going to stop. Nobody thanked anybody.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Friday carried twelve dated jobs and twelve callers, the last admitted at ten past five, and about eleven minutes of the afternoon were in a print shop on a road off Saltmarket.** Callers: twelve. Refusals: three, one a request to be told how many of a printed thing existed, which cannot be answered by a man in a shop and was not attempted, and one a woman who asked whether a paragraph about what to do when a room cannot answer was old and was told that the man who would know is about four minutes' walk from where she was standing. The separation: five hundred and sixty-seven days, eighty-one weeks to the day, from one line in a box on a form about eleven weeks back, walked daily, not shorter, nothing put back in it.*

*Dead type and a locked chase.* **A man of about fifty-eight who owns a two-man print shop on a road off Saltmarket printed the three runs and set none of the type, because each run arrived in a locked and tight chase and went back in a grey unmarked van with the money, and the man who brings it is about thirty-one, is not from this city, and has been four times. The first run was about four hundred and forty sheets and the second about four hundred and sixty and the third is short by two boxes, and the shortfall is the first thing the printer said and he said it was the true thing and that it is the one a listener would want least. He paid about nine pounds in the spring for a wooden box of dead type off a man clearing a shop that had shut two streets the other way, and out of it came a chase with about nine lines still locked in it, in the plainest hand any of them will read, saying what to do when a room cannot answer; and that paragraph is the paragraph under the four printed steps, and nobody set it, and the way its age is established is not a date at all: the letters on the back of the type are worn and the letters on the front are not, because the back is what gets looked at, and the front of the page was thrown away years before anybody thought to ask about the back. The other thing in that box is a chase with nine ruled lines in it and nothing in them, kept since the spring, not thrown out, and not explained. The printer is going to stop. There has been no order, so there is nothing to invoice, and the money is in a fold of paper in a drawer and he has not called it anything, and he is sixty next year and there is him and a lad on Thursdays and Tuesdays and Fridays are free. He did not offer a cup of anything and was not asked and was not thanked, and the bell went off two notes high on the way in and on the way out, and Marek wanted to be wrong about the type and there was no room on earth in which to want it.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A paragraph about what to do when a room cannot answer turned out to be twenty-nine years old and to have been under four correct steps on about nine hundred sheets of paper for three weeks, and nobody in this city printed it, and nobody in this city has taken it off anything.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter or in that shop. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and a printer keeping a chase with nine empty ruled lines in it is not a third instance, because a man who has not thrown a thing out has done nothing to anybody and has had no consequence of any kind, and not one second of that Friday touched either of the two that have stood there since about four years ago. Six objects come on to this page and stay apart: a chase with about nine lines locked in it, a wooden box, a card on end in a rail of two, a plate of iron with four slots, a hand copy of the front of a page, and a chase with nine ruled lines in it and nothing in it.
