# Chapter 657 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Card Of Two Printed Lines Going Up On The Door Of That Shop With A Tack At About Ten Past Three, A Woman Of About Twenty-Four Who Put It There And Who Is Not Speaking To Him And Who Is Speaking About Him In The Second Line Of It, A Man Of About Thirty-Four In A Kitchen Standing In The Road Outside Reading It, And A Man Of About Thirty-Four Nine Hundred Miles Away Who Has Said Three Of His Four Sentences

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is a card, two lines, one tack, and a man reading it from the far side of a wet road who is not the man he is the same age as.**

---

Eleven jobs with a date against each on the Thursday, eleven callers over the counter, the last of them in at ten past five, the shutter down at ten, and the road outside it wet for the fourth day running.

**One room is behind the other three off that service road. All four are warm. The room is one thousand one hundred and ninety-three days, a hundred and seventy weeks and three days. The rail by the door holds two and has held two for eleven weeks; the card standing on end in it is four days further on than the room, at one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven days, which is a hundred and seventy-one weeks to the day.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and eighty-nine days, a hundred and twenty-seven weeks to the day. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and eighty-three, a hundred and forty weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and twenty-six, a hundred and eighteen weeks to the day.

The separation has been at eighty-one weeks and six days, or five hundred and seventy-three days, since a Thursday about eleven weeks back when a line went into a box in a form and nobody chased it and no letter went anywhere, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

**The card came at about ten past three and it went up on the outside of the door of that shop with a tack out of a jar on the counter, and the man who put it up was about nineteen and he has a box of staples and he was told where to put it by the woman who printed it.**

It is two lines and it is in her name and there is no logo on it and it is the size of a postcard and it is the fourth one of about nine that are going up on nine doors in this city this week.

**I can be asked whether a thing was agreed. I cannot agree it for anybody else, including the man who owns this shop.**

---

He read it standing up. He read it twice, which took about nine seconds, and then he looked at the door, and the door was shut and the card was on the outside of it, and he was on the inside of it, and the man of about nineteen was back at the counter with the staples and had not looked up and was not going to.

"**That's up,**" said the man of about nineteen.

"**I can see it's up.**"

"**She said the shop was the right one and she didn't say why and I didn't ask, and I've got seven more.**"

"**Seven more.**"

"**Seven more doors and then the office, and then I'm done.**"

---

**The man of about nineteen came back at about ten to five with the jar nearly empty and put it on the counter and said he had done six of the eight and that two of the eight were in offices and not shops, and that in one of them a woman of about forty read the card standing up, all the way through, both lines, and then asked him whether the man who owned the shop was there, and he said no, and she said right, and put it on the wall by the door at about shoulder height and not at eye height, and he did not know why and did not ask.**

"**Shoulder height,**" he said.

"**She'll want to look at it,**" said the man of about nineteen, **"and she'll be standing up.**"

"**That's a good answer and it's hers, not yours.**"

"**It's hers,**" said the man of about nineteen, **"and I wrote it down on the back of the list, which is where I write everything, and there's a thing on the back of that list now that says a card goes up at shoulder height where a person is standing up and not at eye height where a person is sitting down, and there's a thing on the back of that list that says the man who owns the shop is not in the building.**"

**He did not correct either of those two things. He has not been in that building since the Friday of the previous week and he has not asked to be, and about four people in this city have worked out that a man who is not in a building is a man who cannot be asked anything about it, and one of the four is a woman of about twenty-four and she has not said so.**

**Nobody came into that shop between about ten past three and about half past three and that is not a thing anybody arranged, and he has wondered since whether it was arranged, and has not asked the man of about nineteen whether it was, and the man of about nineteen is nineteen and was handed a list of nine addresses and did not know any of the people at them and has not said so.**

The card is a notice and not a form and there is no box on it and no tick and no four digits, and it says two things, and the second of the two things is him.

**He could have taken it down. It was his door and his wall and his tacks in his own jar, and a man who lets a card about himself go up on his own door has done something and this is the page on which he does it, and it took about four seconds, and what he said in the four seconds was not about the card.**

"**That second line's the whole job, then.**"

"**That,**" said the man of about nineteen, **"is exactly what she said, and she said it in the same words, and I've got it written on the back of the list.**"

He read the back of the list. **It is in her handwriting and it is the same two lines, and the handwriting is not the same as the printing, and she wrote them out for a man of about nineteen with a box of staples because a printed card cannot be read by a person standing in a doorway in the rain at a slant, and that is a different reason and it is a reason about nineteen-year-olds and not about him.**

**At about twenty past three the man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen came past the shop, stopped, went back about four paces, and read the card from the far side of the wet road. He read it twice as well. He is the same age as a man nine hundred miles away who pushes beds in a hospital and the two of them have never met and there is no reason in this city for either of them to be told about the other and Marek has thought about it for about four seconds and has not written it down anywhere.**

He came in and put the card down flat on the counter, face up, and put a printed sheet beside it, and said the fourth of his four sentences.

"**The third one's wrong for a brace.**"

---

"**Say more than that.**"

"**Right.**" He did not sit down. "**Step three says the four steps are the same for a brace, a pump and a door, and the count goes in the order it went in the first time, and the sequence is a hold, a count and a release. A brace isn't a pump.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**A pump you hold the thing and the thing stays where it is and you let go and it goes back. A brace, the ones on the old wing, holds a person's leg. You don't hold it. If you hold it you've got a leg in your hands and you're a person holding a door, and a person holding a door is not the same as a door.**" He put a finger on the third step without touching it. "**And the count. On a pump the count's four. On a brace the count's three, because the middle one's the one that holds, and if you do four on a brace you've counted the hold twice and you go early.**"

---

**The shop got very quiet for about eleven seconds and a woman of about thirty-four behind the till said, "**It's a sheet of paper,**" and then did not say anything else, and it was the correct thing to say and it was not the thing that was true.**

"**How long have you known that,**" he said.

"**Nine years,**" said the man of about thirty-four.

"**And you work in a kitchen.**"

"**I work in a kitchen and I did eleven years on the wards before that, and there's a woman on that wing who's forty-one and she'll tell you the same and she'd be annoyed that I said it first.**"

"**I'm not going to ask you to say it anywhere.**"

"**Good,**" he said. "**I wasn't going to say it anywhere. I said it to you because you have a counter and I'm in a mood about the four steps and you've got the paper.**"

---

"**It's not mine. It's been on about nine hundred doors in about three weeks and I read three of them on a Monday and I am not the man who put it on.**"

"**I know that,**" said the man of about thirty-four, "**and I'm not asking you to be. I'm asking you to not be quiet about it, which is different and which is a thing I can ask a person for in about nine seconds without it costing me anything, and if you do it I'll not know and if you don't I won't know either.**"

**And that was the whole of it, and he did not say the name of the man nine hundred miles away who would have agreed with him about the count of three, and he did not say it because that is a man he has met four times in about four months and a rule of this is that what a man says in a room in a town four days of bus travel there and back stays in that room until that man puts it somewhere.**

**At about half past four a woman of about twenty-four came into the shop and asked for a card and he gave her one out of the jar without saying anything, and she said thank you, and he said nothing back because saying something back would have made it a thing between two people, and she is the one whose name was said once in a room in about four weeks and she has never been asked to do anything by anybody who chairs anything and she does ironing three days a week on a wide road and has a season ticket.**

She put the card on the counter of that shop in Lattice Ward, face up, at about ten past five, and she did not wait, and she did not look at the inside.

---

**Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, and one person said thank you in about a quarter of a second on the way out the door and it was not directed at anybody, and the door went on being a door for the rest of that afternoon.**

**He noticed four things on that Thursday and none of them was on a form.** A tack in a jar on a counter that has about nine in it and there are nine doors, and somebody has counted the tacks. The card is printed on the same stock as about four other things he has seen this month and the reverse of it is blank and slightly furry, and the furry side is the side that goes on the wall. And a mark on the inside of the shop door about four feet up, at about the height of a person's eye, about the size of a thumbnail, in the paint, and he has looked at it four hundred times and has never known what it was, and it is where the card is now.

**And one small thing he gave up, at about half past four on a Thursday, and it was about four seconds, and it was this: he did not offer to put any of the other eight cards up himself. He has nine addresses in a fourth pocket in an order he has never written down, and there are eight doors, and he knows which eight, and about four people in this city have worked out that he knows which eight. He offered nothing and said nothing and let a man of about nineteen with a box of staples do eight doors on his own, and the reason is that a man of twenty-two who put nine cards up in a day would be a man whose door people look at first, and the second line of the card on his own door is about not being that.**
