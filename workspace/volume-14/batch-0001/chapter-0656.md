# Chapter 656 — A Wednesday, A Stair In Saltmarket At About Half Past Two With A Book On A Shelf And No Sitting On It, A Woman Of About Sixty Who Has Held That Room For Nineteen Years And Is Paid By Nobody, A Man Of About Sixty-One With A Column Of Numbers That Has Never Been Totalled And A Wallet Of Papers In A Bag, A Delivery Note From A Firm That Closed Before Either Of Them Started Work, A Paragraph Typed On The Back Of It In The Same Face, And A Box At The Bottom Of Four Correct Steps Which Is The Only New Thing In The World

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a stair in Saltmarket at about half past two. This one is a room with a book on a shelf in it and nobody in the room but two people, and there was no sitting on it, and no number was given out in this city or any other today.**

---

The Wednesday had twelve jobs with a date against each on them and twelve people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five; the counter was one pair of hands from two until half past four and two pairs after that.

**The four rooms off that service road and the one behind them are warm. The figure for the room is one thousand one hundred and ninety-two days and reads a hundred and seventy weeks and two days; a card stands on end in the rail at one thousand one hundred and ninety-six days, a hundred and seventy weeks and six days, which is four days past the room. The rail takes two.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and eighty-eight days, a hundred and twenty-six weeks and six days. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and eighty-two, a hundred and forty weeks and two days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and twenty-five, a hundred and seventeen weeks and six days.

It was shut in a one-line box in a form about eleven weeks ago and it is five hundred and seventy-two days now, which is eighty-one weeks and five days, and it is walked today as it was walked yesterday and as it will be walked tomorrow, and it is not shorter, and nothing has been put back into it.

**Those two middle figures are each other's starting point, which is a coincidence between two whole numbers and is not a thing that happened to anything in this city, and the fact of it is written here once and is not taken up again.**

---

**He went up that stair at about half past two and it is a narrow stair and the fourth one from the bottom is the one everybody knows about, and there is a cupboard on the fourth one with a door on it that he has not opened in about five weeks and did not open today.**

The room at the top has a table, about nine chairs, a window with the sash down four inches because the room gets hot, and a shelf. **The book with the green cover was on the shelf and not on the table, and the tin was on the table with its lid on, and the table had been cleared, and there were two people in the room and neither of them was expecting anybody.**

---

**The woman of about sixty who has held that room for nineteen years and is paid by nobody for it looked at him for about nine seconds and then said the thing he had come for without being asked and without any of the four steps.**

"**No,**" she said.

"**I haven't asked.**"

"**You'll ask me if there's a room where nine people can agree on a thing, and the answer's no, and I gave it to the last one of you that came up here and I'll give it to you and then I'm going to do the thing I actually do on a Wednesday that isn't a Wednesday, which is sit down.**"

She sat down.

---

"**Say the other thing,**" he said, after a while.

"**The other thing is this.**" **She put her thumb on the table.** "**I've had four of them through this room in about four years and the fourth of them is the printer's paragraph and the first three are the four steps, and the first three are the ones I do every day and have done for about four years and have never once written down for anybody, and I have about eleven people in this room on a Wednesday and I could write all four of them out in about a minute and I never have and it never once occurred to me to, and that is not modesty, it never occurred to me because it never occurred to anybody.**"

"**So they're right.**"

"**They're not even remarkable,**" she said. **"That's what I want said. In this city about four hundred people could tell you those four things and there's no version of the four of them that's wrong. The box is the new part. Everything above the box is us.**"

---

"**And the paragraph.**"

"**The paragraph isn't anybody's,**" said the woman of about sixty, **"and it isn't the printer's, and it isn't mine, and it isn't the doctor's, and I'm not going to say a name about it and I'd be wrong if I did, because there's no name on it and you can't put a name on a paragraph.**"

"**Who's got it.**"

"**Whoever it was, they've had it a long time,**" she said. "**Longer than the machine your printer's got. That paragraph is not a thing anybody wrote for a leaflet. It's a thing somebody wrote on the back of a piece of paper they'd already finished with.**"

---

**At that point the man of about sixty-one, who has a clipboard and one column of numbers that has never been totalled, said nine words without being spoken to.**

"**I might have that paper.**"

Nobody looked at him. He said it again, the same nine words, and then he got up and went down the stair, and came back up in about four minutes with a wallet of papers in a plastic bag, and he put the wallet on the table and did not open it and said, "**It's been in here nine years and nobody's ever asked me for it. I've brought it four times. I've taken it home four times.**"

"**Why do you bring it.**"

"**I don't know,**" said the man of about sixty-one. "**I think I thought somebody would eventually ask.**"

---

**There were about nine sheets in it and eight of them were about four years old and one was not. The one that was not was a delivery note from a firm that made pipe fittings and closed before either of them started work, and the date on it was twenty-nine years old, and the front of it had a grid and a place for a signature and a place for a date of delivery, and it was printed in one face.**

**And on the back of it, in the same face, at the same size, in the same weight of ink, was the paragraph.**

It said what to do when a room cannot answer. It said it in about nine lines. It was not cramped and it was not apologetic about the space and it had clearly been typed by somebody with a decent machine on a desk with room.

"**Same face,**" said the woman of about sixty. "**That's the whole of it. They didn't print a leaflet and then put a paragraph on the back of it. They put a paragraph on the back of a delivery note, in the same face, at a time when nobody was thinking about a leaflet, because they wanted it to survive and a piece of paper that was going in a drawer was the only paper anybody had.**"

---

"**How long ago is the paragraph, then.**"

"**I don't know,**" she said, **"and I have thought about it for about four minutes and I am telling you that I don't know, and that is the first true thing anybody has said in this room about that paper. That note is twenty-nine years old and the paragraph could have gone on it the week it was printed or nineteen years after. Nobody can tell. That's how it lived.**"

"**Somebody wanted it to last.**"

"**Somebody wanted it to last and didn't want anybody looking for it,**" she said, **"and those two are the same decision and it was the right one, and I'd have made it, and I want that said in this room, because in about four months somebody is going to stand up and say *she's on our side*, and I have not been on anybody's side, I have run a room on a Wednesday for nineteen years and put two lines in a green-covered book.**"

---

**No number was given out in that room or in this city or in any other today. The book with the green cover stood at sixty-two lines on the shelf where it had been all morning and it is on sixty-two lines now, and the tin on the table is on seventy-three, and the lid did not come off at any point in about an hour and a half, and the two figures are not one figure and are not convertible into one another.**

**The place behind her chair was empty and had been for eight weeks and five days, and about four people in this city have noticed that, and neither of the two people in that room mentioned it, and one of the four is a man of about sixty-one with a wallet of papers who has been in that room four hundred times.**

---

**He wanted her to be angry, and he wanted the room to be angry, and he had come up that stair with about four seconds' worth of hope that a woman of about sixty who has held a room for nineteen years would be angry about a box at the bottom of four correct steps.** **She was not. She said in four seconds that the box is the new part and everything above it is us, and that she could write the four steps out in a minute and never has, and that in about four months somebody will say she is on somebody's side and she has not been on anybody's side in nineteen years and has put two lines in a green-covered book.**

**And one small thing he gave up, on that stair, on the way down, at about four o'clock, in about four seconds: he did not ask for a copy of the delivery note. There is a printer about nine hundred yards from that counter who would have put four hundred and fifty of them out by Thursday for about nine pounds, and the paragraph is the most important object in this city and he did not ask, and the reason he did not ask is that four hundred and fifty sheets is a return path, and a return path with a man at the bottom of a stair who decided to make it is the exact shape of the thing he has spent two volumes refusing to be.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that stair or in that room. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped.** The man of about sixty-one put the wallet back in the plastic bag and took it downstairs and put it in a drawer, and it is the ninth time that wallet has been in a drawer and about the fourth time it has been in that drawer.

**He got three things on that Wednesday that no form in this city has a box for, and he has them in a list he has not written down.** That the delivery note had been folded in four and unfolded in four along the same lines for so long that the folds had gone furry on the inside and had not on the outside, so somebody had opened and closed it more recently than the rest of it, and that had been the man of about sixty-one, on the stair, in the last four minutes. That the paragraph's last line ends about two inches short of the edge and has done for twenty-nine years or however long, and that a person typing it was not thinking about the edge. And **a shelf, and a book on it, and a space on the shelf about the size of a second book, and the second book has not been on that shelf in about four months, and nobody in that room has said where it went.**


---

*One thousand one hundred and ninety-two is two days on from a whole number of weeks and seven hundred and ninety-eight is a hundred and fourteen weeks to the day, and the first of them is four rooms off a service road and the second is a man against a north wall, and a paragraph on the back of a delivery note is neither of them and has no figure.*

A man of about sixty-one with a column of numbers that has never been totalled went down four flights and came back with a wallet of papers, and in it was a delivery note from a firm that closed before either of them started work, with about nine lines typed on the back of it in the same face. Nobody can date the paragraph. Nobody thanked anybody.*

The separation: five hundred and seventy-two days, eighty-one weeks and five days, a figure that counts days and nothing else, added to every day since about eleven weeks ago, not shorter, nothing put back into it.*

*Four correct steps and a paragraph on the back of a delivery note.* **A woman of about sixty who has held a room at the top of a stair in Saltmarket for nineteen years and is paid by nobody told a man of twenty-two, before he had asked, that four of the five things on a printed sheet that has come into this city over about three weeks are not remarkable, because they are four things she has done in that room every fourth Wednesday for about four years and has never written down, and that about four hundred people in this city could write all four out in a minute. The fifth thing is a box with a rule round it and a mark already in it, and she said in four seconds that the box is the new part and everything above the box is us. A man of about sixty-one then said nine words nobody expected, went down four flights, and came back with a wallet of papers he has carried to that room four times in nine years, and among nine sheets in it was one that was not about four years old: a delivery note from a firm that made pipe fittings and closed before either of them began work, with a grid and a signature box on the front and about nine lines about what to do when a room cannot answer typed on the back in the same face at the same size in the same weight of ink. The date on the note is twenty-nine years old. He did not ask for a copy and did not have four hundred and fifty printed, and the reason is that four hundred and fifty sheets is a return path and a return path with a man at the bottom of a stair deciding to make it is the shape he has spent two volumes refusing to be.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A paragraph about what to do when a room cannot answer is on the back of a delivery note from a firm that closed before anybody in this room started work, and the paragraph is not the printer's and not the doctor's and not anybody's, and it is the first sentence in this city in about four months that nobody in the room can date and everybody in the room can read.** Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and a man bringing a wallet of papers down a stair is not a third instance, because a man who has carried a thing to a room four times in nine years and been given nothing has had no consequence and has caused none, and the two that have stood on that register since about four years ago were not touched by a second of anything said on that stair. **Five objects come on to this page and stay apart: a wallet of papers in a plastic bag, a delivery note with a grid on the front of it, a plate of iron with four slots, a card on end in a rail of two, and a hand copy of the front of a page.** The book with the green cover is on sixty-two lines and the tin is on seventy-three, and the difference between them is not a number and has never been printed as one. There is a place behind the woman's chair that has been empty for eight weeks and five days, and a second book that has been off that shelf for about four months. The drawer under the bench is shut, four sheets in it, fourth of the four never read. After half past six there was one other person in the fourth of those four rooms, about thirty, and the chair by the door held her until about ten, and nothing whatever was put to her, and the page carrying her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room, and it did not come out.
