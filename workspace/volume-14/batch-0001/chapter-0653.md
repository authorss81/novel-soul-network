# Chapter 653 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Types For A Firm, A Reception Form With Two Columns Where It Used To Have One, A Strip Of Paper Laid Over The Old One And Not Taken Off, Nine Of Them Filled In By Hand In About Four Weeks, And Nobody In That Building Including Herself Who Knows What The Second Column Is For

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. This one is a reception counter in a building with a canopy over it, and a strip of paper about the width of a ruler, and nine forms.**

---

Eleven jobs carried a date against each on the Thursday and eleven people came over the counter, the last of them let in at ten past five, and the shutter came down at ten and the rain came with it.

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind them, and all of them warm, and the oldest of the four has been a room for one thousand one hundred and eighty-six days, being a hundred and sixty-nine weeks and three days. On end in the rail by the door, four days past it, a card: one thousand one hundred and ninety days, a hundred and seventy weeks to the day. Two go in that rail. It has taken two for eleven weeks.**

The nineteenth line on that board of two nails is eight hundred and eighty-two days, a hundred and twenty-six weeks to the day. The hardboard's sixteenth is nine hundred and seventy-six, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks and three days, and the hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns is eight hundred and nineteen, a hundred and seventeen weeks to the day.

Five hundred and sixty-six days, or eighty weeks and six days, is where the separation is, and it describes nothing at all, and it got there when somebody ruled a box in a form about eleven weeks ago and wrote one line in it, and it has been counted on every day since including this one, and no day has taken any of it off, and nothing has gone back into it.

---

**She came in at about half past four with nine sheets of the same reception form folded into a shape about the size of a paperback, and she had been on the counter of that building for about three years and had never brought anything into a repair shop in her life.**

"**Are these yours?**" she said.

"**What are they?**"

She put them on the counter and undid the fold and spread about four of them out, and they were cream, and they were the same form that had been on the desk by that door for three years, and they had two columns on them where the desk form has one.

"**That's mine,**" he said. "**Where'd you get these?**"

"**Off the pad by the door. There's about four hundred of them in the tray and they're the same as the ones on the desk and the ones on the desk are different.**"

---

**That is the whole of it, and it took her about nine seconds to say, and she had noticed it on the Friday and had said nothing to anybody for about four weeks because nobody had asked her and because she is twenty-nine and works in a building where a person who asks about a form is a person who has not got a lot to do.**

The old form has one column and the column is headed with the word VISITOR and under it there is room for a name and a firm and which floor.

The new one has two. **The second column has no heading at all. It is about the same width as the first, it is ruled, and there is a strip of paper the width of a ruler laid over the top of where the heading used to be, and the strip has been glued down, and the glue has gone yellow, and the strip has not been taken off.**

"**Who's put that on?**"

"**I don't know. It was there on the Friday.**"

"**Four weeks?**"

"**Three.**"

"**And you've filled nine of them in.**"

"**I've filled nine of them in and I fill them in about three times a day and the strip's been on the desk since the Friday and I've never lifted it,**" she said, **"and I want you to tell me what the second one is for and if you can't tell me then I want to go back to work.**"

---

He looked at the second column for about eleven seconds.

It was not a name column. It was not a date. **It was about the same width as the first and it had four short horizontal lines across it, spaced like a number, and a person writing a number in it would get about four digits and no more.** There was no code on the sheet that he had seen. There was no number on the front of the printed thing that anybody had handed to anybody, so far as he knew, and he has not seen the whole of what has been printed in this city in a fortnight and will not pretend to.

"**I can't tell you,**" he said, **"and I'm not going to guess at a counter, because if I guess and I'm wrong, you write it on nine forms and nine forms is a thing somebody can look at.**"

"**That's the first straight thing anybody's said to me about it,**" she said.

"**It's not straight. It's the other thing. There's a difference and I know the difference and so do you.**"

---

"**Right,**" she said, and squared the nine of them up on the counter, and did not put them in her bag.

"**What are you going to do with them.**"

"**I've got a drawer.**"

"**You've got a drawer and it's not locked.**"

"**Nobody locks a drawer.**"

"**I know nobody locks a drawer,**" he said. "**I want to say it anyway, because there's a woman on a wide road who's had a thing in an unlocked drawer since Thursday and she's not locked it either, and neither of you has done anything wrong, and both of you have got a piece of paper in a drawer in a building, and the paper's the paper. That's all. The drawer doesn't do anything.**"

She thought about that for about four seconds and then she said the thing he had been waiting nine minutes to hear and had not expected to hear in a shop.

"**We all have one.**"

"**How many have you.**"

"**I've asked two. One's got it in a tray and one's got a copy of it on the wall,**" she said. "**Nobody's got it in a drawer. That's what I'd have said a month ago.**"

---

**He wanted to be the room the form goes to. That is a want he has never once had in this shape and it is not the same want as being asked and it is not the same want as being the person a name is said to, and he noticed it arrive and he noticed that it arrived as a relief and that is the part he did not expect.**

**There is a room above a line in Saltmarket and it is about nine minutes on foot from that counter and it holds about eleven people every fourth Wednesday, and one of the things that happens in that room is that a person says a number out loud and it goes nowhere and comes back once every four weeks. If the second column on nine forms said a number and the numbers went there, then he would be a place a piece of paper could arrive at, and a person in a building with a canopy over the door would be able to type four digits into a strip of glued-down paper and know that a person about two miles away had read them.**

**He did not say it. He has said no four times in about four weeks and this was going to be a fifth, and the reason he did not say it is not humility and he knows exactly what it is: a man who says come and type four digits into my column is a man who has asked a company with a canopy over its door to route a column through him, and about nine weeks ago that is the sentence he was taught by a woman with a hand truck, and it is the whole of what this city has learned from him in about four months.**

---

"**If you want it gone,**" he said, **"tear the strip off. It'll come off. It's paper on paper.**"

"**And then the form's got a blank column on it.**"

"**Then the form's got a blank column on it and nobody can write a number they don't have.**"

She looked at him for a second and then she said: "**You're right and I don't want to do it on a Thursday and I'd like to do it on a Monday.**"

"**Do it Monday.**"

"**Monday,**" she said, and put the nine of them back in the fold, and then took one out again, and left that one on the counter. "**That one's got my own handwriting on it. I'm not taking that one back in.**"

---

**At about two o'clock a man of about fifty-one in overalls came in and asked, in about nine seconds, whether a thing in a second column was a number, and was told that it was four spaces for a number and nobody knew what number, and he said that is what I thought and went out, and he did not buy anything, and he is the same man who asked about the box on the Friday, and neither of them has given his name to anybody in this city.**

**At about ten past six, with the shutter already half down, a man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen came past the shop, went four paces, came back and put a printed sheet on the counter, and said four sentences about it in about nine seconds, and three of them were about how he gets to work in the mornings and the fourth of them is not on this page and is on one later in this fortnight.**

He did not look at it while the man was speaking. He looked at it after, with the shutter down and the cage rattling, and it was the same four steps and the same paragraph and the same box at the bottom with a rule round it, **and in the box there was a mark, and the mark was not a tick, and the mark was four short lines filled in with four digits, and he had seen the column that those four digits were for about an hour and a half earlier.**

Nobody said goodnight. Nobody said thank you. The nine sheets and the one sheet are named on this page and this page does not set any two of them beside one another: a reception form with two columns, a strip of paper glued down over a heading, a card on end in a rail of two, a hand copy of the front of a page, and a plate of iron with four slots.

**A book with a green cover is on a shelf in a room at the top of a stair in Saltmarket and it has sixty-two lines in it, and a tin on the table under that shelf has seventy-three in it and its lid has not been off.** The place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room has been empty for eight weeks and five days, and the ninth chair is against the wall with its back to it and did not move and nobody in this city has ever said who put it there.

**After half past six one other person was in the fourth of those four rooms, thirty-odd, in the chair the door is behind, and she was still in it at about ten.** Nothing whatever was put to her and nothing ever is. The page carrying her name at the head of it is the eighth of eight, in a ring binder on the back shelf of that room, and it did not come out.


---

*One thousand one hundred and ninety is a whole number of weeks and seven hundred and ninety-two is one day on from one, and the first of those is the age of four rooms off a service road and the second is the age of a man against a north wall, and neither of them is the age of a form with two columns on it.*

*656. Thursday of week 237, at ten, and the third day, in the rain. Eleven dated jobs and eleven names, the last let in at ten past five. A woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm brought nine cream reception forms out of a building with a canopy over the door, and the form on the desk there has one column and the form on the pad by the door has two, and the second has no heading, and a strip of paper the width of a ruler is glued down over the place where the heading was. She has filled nine in and has lifted nothing for three weeks. The second column is the width of a name, ruled across four times, and would take four digits. He said in four seconds that he could not tell her what it was for and would not guess at a counter. She left one of the nine on the counter because it has her own handwriting on it. At about ten past six a man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen brought a sheet in and the box at the bottom of it had four digits in it. Nobody thanked anybody.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Thursday carried eleven dated jobs and eleven callers, the last admitted at ten past five, and about an hour and a half of it were at that counter.** Callers: eleven. Refusals: two, one a request to be told whether a strip of paper glued over a heading could be taken off by a person who did not work in the building, refused in four seconds and correctly, and one a man who wanted the four steps read out to him and was told they were on a page and that he could read. Work: eleven, two declines, nothing escalated, nothing handed back. Charge: eleven pounds, exact. The heating in that flat is above its winter setting for a ninth month, and four names on that one tenancy make up the difference. Those rooms: one thousand one hundred and eighty-six days, a hundred and sixty-nine weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, warm. Rail card: one thousand one hundred and ninety days, a hundred and seventy weeks to the day, rail of two. The board on two nails: twentieth, none, and never begun; twelfth, one thousand one hundred and six, a hundred and fifty-eight weeks to the day; thirteenth, one thousand and fifty-seven, a hundred and fifty-one weeks to the day; fourteenth, one thousand and twenty-two, a hundred and forty-six weeks to the day; fifteenth, one thousand and one, a hundred and forty-three weeks to the day; sixteenth, nine hundred and seventy-six, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks and three days; seventeenth, nine hundred and fifty-eight, a hundred and thirty-six weeks and six days; eighteenth, nine hundred and four, a hundred and twenty-nine weeks and one day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall: seven hundred and ninety-two days, a hundred and thirteen weeks and one day. The ask: eight hundred and seventy-six days, a hundred and twenty-five weeks and one day. The hold on nine crates in a yard in the second of the four towns: eight hundred and nineteen days, a hundred and seventeen weeks to the day. The post at the corridor end: seven hundred and thirty-four days, a hundred and four weeks and six days. The nine hand copies: seven hundred and fifty-two days, a hundred and seven weeks and three days; eight unfinished; nobody has yet disagreed in the fourth line; not one of the nine set against another on any day of this fortnight. The separation: five hundred and sixty-six days, eighty weeks and six days, a count of days and not of anything, added to every day since about eleven weeks ago, no shorter, nothing put back.*

*Two columns and a strip of paper.* **A reception form in a building with a canopy over the door has had a second column on it for three weeks, ruled four times, about the width of a name, with no heading, and a strip of paper the width of a ruler glued down over the place where the heading used to be, the glue yellow, the strip not lifted. A woman of about twenty-nine who types for that building has filled nine of them in and has lifted nothing and has told nobody, and she came to a repair counter on a wide road at half past four in the rain with nine in her bag and asked one question and was given one, and the answer was that nobody knew. He said he would not guess at a counter, because a guess written on nine forms is a thing somebody can look at, and she said that was the first straight thing anybody had said to her about it, and he told her it was not straight and that she knew the difference. He told her the strip would come off and that tearing it off would leave a blank column nobody could fill with a number they had not got, and she said she would do it on a Monday and not on a Thursday, and left one of the nine on the counter because it has her own handwriting on it. She said three people in three buildings have that sheet and that a month ago it would have been one. He wanted the second column to send its four digits to a room two miles away that takes a number out loud every fourth Wednesday, and did not ask, and the reason is a sentence said to a hand truck about nine weeks ago, and the wanting was a relief and he noticed the relief and did not act on it. At about ten past six a man of about thirty-four who works in a kitchen came back and put a printed sheet on that counter, and the box at the bottom of it had four digits in it, and those are the four lines of the second column, and no page of this entry says the two are the same and they are on a page together in one sentence and are not joined by anything.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A form in a building with a canopy over its door has had a second column on it for three weeks, and about nine forms in that building have four digits in it that nobody can account for, and one of those nine is on a counter in Lattice Ward and will stay there.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter. Nobody was taught, shown, assessed or helped. The register of correct acts with no consequence is closed at two, and a woman refusing to guess at a column is not a third instance, because a person who declines to write a number they have not been given has refused an instruction and not complied with one, and a second of this Thursday did not reach either of the two that have stood on that register for about four years. Nobody has ever read the fourth of four sheets lying in a shut drawer under a bench, and no two of the five things named just here are set against each other: a form with two columns, a plate of iron with four slots, a strip of paper glued down over a heading, a card on end in a rail of two, and a hand copy of the front of a page. Somebody was still sitting in one of those four rooms at about ten, about thirty, in the chair the door is behind, and no one in the building said one word to her between half past six and the light going off, and her page is the eighth of eight and it was shut in a ring binder on a back shelf and it did not come out. A tin in that room up a stair in Saltmarket has seventy-three in it and a green-covered book has sixty-two lines in it, and about four people in this city have noticed that the place behind the chair of the woman who holds that room has been empty for eight weeks and five days, and not one of them has said so.
