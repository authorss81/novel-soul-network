# Chapter 101 — One Line In A Specification

The rota board was in the corridor outside the parts cage and it had been there for the four months Marek Senn had been in the outfit, and on the Monday morning of the first week of the new term it had his name on it twice, on a Monday, which was not new, and on a Friday, which was.

He looked at it for about nine seconds at ten to eight with a cup of tea he had not made in a machine and understood that both names were grade two and grade three of the same man, and that the board did not distinguish between them, and that a board is a thing a person can be found by afterwards.

He had four days a week on the Monday and the rota started on the Friday.

---

The letter went out at nine and he heard about it at twenty past nine because a woman of about thirty-one with a training diary was standing at the machine by the canteen door with a folder under her arm and had been standing there for about four minutes, waiting for somebody, and he was somebody.

"It went," said Nia Bramhall.

"I did not think it would not."

"**It is not going to me. It is queued for the system and a woman in Crown Terrace with a template pressed the send, and I read the whole thing twice on the Thursday and I have got a note on it and the note says do not add, and I have not added.**" She did not open the folder. "**Third paragraph. We would like to recognise that the open session held earlier this week was helpful in bringing this matter to our attention.**"

"I am sorry."

"Do not be sorry, it is not yours and it is not hers and it is going to be the only thing in this whole case that anybody is actually pleased about." She turned the folder over once and did not open it. "**Four hundred and six people are going to go home tonight and tell their households that they were at the meeting where the company found the fault. In about four years somebody is going to put that in a room to get somebody elected.**"

"Is there anything in the reply-to box."

"**There is a reply-to box. It came on the template. It has been on the template for about two years and it is filled in with a general enquiries address and about nine hundred people are going to reply to it because it is the only thing on the letter that looks like it wants an answer.**"

"And about how many of them are going to say the session is what the third paragraph says it was."

"**About a hundred and forty of them came for the lunch.**" Nia Bramhall looked at the machine instead of at him. "**I have got nine hundred bookings in this diary and I know what the ones who came for the lunch bought, because I sell them the tokens.**"

---

At about eleven he was on the ramp of bay four with a torque wrench and a fault on a packer that had nothing to do with anything that had happened in the last fortnight, which was the first fault he had been given in a month that was not a consequence of a document.

A man came across the yard towards him with a laminated badge on a lanyard and a folded A4 in his hand, and he had the walk of somebody who has a start time and arrived four minutes early on purpose.

"Marek Senn?"

"That is me."

"**Corin Vasska. District practice assurance.**" He held the A4 out and then did not give it away, which is a thing a man does when he has read a document a lot. "**You will not know what that is, and the reason you will not know what that is is that it has got about eleven thousand contributors and it does not have a name on the door, because it is a levy and not a company, and a levy is a good deal harder to point at than a company.**"

"Then tell me."

"**It is a small fund. It is paid out of a levy on about eleven thousand registered working premises, it is administered by a committee of about nine, and what it does is run about forty free evening talks a year on working practice, in a room, with folding chairs, and about forty people come to each one, which is about one and a half per cent of the people who pay for it, which is normal for anything free in the evening.**" He turned the A4 round. "**And I have got a series this term. Eleven sessions, and the third one is called *Who to trust in a practice*, and I am doing them, and I would like you on the panel for the third one.**"

Marek Senn put the wrench down on the trolley.

"What is the panel for."

"**Ninety minutes. Fifteen of mine, and then questions. It is not a lecture and it is not a training course and it is not a certification and nobody gets a card and there is nothing to book anybody onto afterwards.**" Vasska folded the A4 in half and put it in his pocket. "**I am going to be honest with you, because you have a job and I have watched four hundred people walk past that door. I am doing this series because the fund is being reviewed in the spring and a fund that has done nothing that anybody remembers is a fund that does not get renewed.**"

"About nine hours of your own life," said Marek Senn.

"**About four hours. Two of them are in the room.**"

"Then why me."

"**Because you were in a hall on the Tuesday of the last term with a microphone in your hand and about four hundred people watched you not do the thing, and the number of people who rang this office about it was about ninety, and eighty of them were angry that a technician was made to look stupid in front of a room, and about nine of them asked whether the technique had been issued by somebody and if so who.**" He said it evenly. "**And then in the middle of the term there was a recall, and the company that did it has said in its own paper that other documentation may exist which these facts do not reach, and about nine thousand people have read that sentence, and I have had a small grant of attention and I would like to put it somewhere useful.**"

The packer made the noise it made when it was running and Marek Senn did not look at it.

"Then what is the specification."

Vasska took the A4 back out of his pocket, which told Marek Senn that the document was not a leaflet.

"**It is a page and a half. It is on the fund's letterhead, it is signed by the chair of the committee, and it is the only document in this that anybody will ever be shown.**" He held it up so that a man four metres away would not be able to read it. "**Section one, the aims. Section two, the delivery. Section three, the budget. Section four is coverage and section four is one line and I am going to read it to you because it is the only reason you are standing here.**"

He read it.

"*The series will include, in the view of the committee, a session addressing the current public concern regarding unofficial and unrecorded technique sharing, the practice of holding techniques in private and circulating them without reference to any issuing body, and the organised promotion of disconnection as a substitute for safe practice.*"

The yard went on being a yard.

"**That is one line of about two hundred and forty words,**" said Corin Vasska. "**I have read it about nine times, because it is the line that makes this series defensible and it is also the line that would get me put in front of a committee if I left it out.**"

"Who wrote it."

"**I did not, and I am not going to tell you who did, and you are not going to be able to make me, and I would like that on the record in the same way your booking clerk would like things on the record.**" He put the page away. "**What I will tell you is that it is a good line. It is not a lie. There is unofficial technique sharing. There are techniques held privately. There are people in this city who will tell you with total accuracy that the answer to both of those is a card and a course, and about nine of those people are on this committee, and they are not crooks, and four of them have sent a child to a clinic that used one of these things and got lucky.**"

"Then the line is true."

"**The line is true. The line is also, word for word, the thing a man of about fifty-two said to a market in Lattice Ward about four weeks ago, and I did not write this line off that man, and I would not have written this line if he had not said it, and I am not going to pretend that is a different thing from what it is.**" He shifted his weight. "**And that is the whole reason I want you on the panel, and I will now tell you the part that makes me the villain of your week, which is that there are about eleven people in this city who have said that sentence out loud in public, and exactly one of them has been asked to say it on a stage with a microphone, and it is going to be a man called Peter Amado, and he has said yes, and he is being paid a fee out of a line in a budget, and he has a wife's heating.**"

Marek Senn picked the wrench up off the trolley.

"No," he said.

"Because of the fourth condition?"

"Because a panel produces a record," he said. "**A panel produces minutes, and a minute has a date on it, and a date is a day a person can be found by, and I have spent a term arguing that the whole of the trouble is a document. If I stand up in a room in Crown Terrace for ninety minutes and say the true sentence, then in about four years there is a piece of A4 with a heading on it and my name under the heading, and about nine people who were in the room will remember that I said it, and every one of those nine will be able to find me.**"

"That is a correct answer and it is also the answer a man gives when he is frightened."

"Yes."

"**Both.**" Vasska did not look away. "**I have got four sessions before the third one and about a hundred and forty people have booked the first two, and the third one has got ninety in it, and one of the ninety is a woman of about twenty-nine who came to a session in a trading estate in the last term because her employer paid for it, and she is a good engineer and she is frightened of getting something wrong at work, and she is the person I built the session for. And I am telling you that because you have a job and I would rather you knew what the money is actually for than have you work it out from a leaflet.**"

"Then do it without me."

"**I will do it without you. I am going to ask four other people and two of them will say no, and one of the two will say no because of a job and one will say no because she does not want to be in a room where eleven thousand contributors can be told who to trust.**" He put the A4 back in his pocket for the last time. "**And I am going to put a job title on the fourth session instead, because the fund's guidance says a programme with a name on it is a programme that can be removed, and a programme with a job on it is a programme that happens, and I read that in somebody else's document in about week twenty-eight and I have never worked out whether I stole it or whether it is just the kind of thing anybody in this trade knows.**"

He walked back across the yard and Marek Senn stood with a torque wrench in his hand for about a minute and a half.

---

In the canteen at about twenty past one, a man of about thirty-four on the floor in front of the washers said the sentence he had said in a hard standing in week twenty-five, and put it slightly differently, and it was slightly better:

"**The word is for the pallet that is not ready.**"

"Have you said that to anybody else."

"**I said it to a man in a market once and I have not seen him since. That is twice. I do not go round.**" The man scrubbed his hands. "**You are on the Friday rota, then.**"

"Who told you that."

"**It is on the board.**"

"Right," said Marek Senn.

He thought about the board for the rest of the afternoon. Eleven folding chairs and about forty people, and eleven people a week on a rota with him on it, and a specification with a line in it that was word for word the thing a market had heard, and a job title on a fourth session that had not been written yet, and not one of those five numbers had ever been in the same sentence as any other one of them.

He counted the exits of bay four twice, which was four, and the washers, and the rack.

---

*104. Monday of week 31, about ten past two in a yard, and this entry is about a man of forty-four with a microphone and a page and a half, and about a woman of thirty-four in front of the washers saying a nine-word sentence to me for the second time in her life and neither of us has said it to anybody else, and about a board in a corridor outside a parts cage with my name on it twice.*

*And the page.* He read the whole thing while a man held it up at arm's length so that nobody four metres away could read it, and then he read one line of it again in his head about nine times over the rest of the day, and I am going to write the line out once here and not once more in this book. **The line is true.** That is the part I cannot get round and I have not got round it in three days. There is unofficial technique sharing. There are techniques held privately and circulated without reference to anything, and there are about four hundred people in this city who have done 4-19 modules one and two and there are about nine men who have never been issued anything and have never once been asked, and the whole of the edifice is standing on them. **A man in a district jacket said a true thing in a market and eleven people went two seconds quieter, and the true thing has been written into a specification and signed by the chair of a committee, and the money is honest, and the money is a levy that about eleven thousand people pay, and it is the cheapest possible way to buy a sentence.** I have spent a term refusing to say the sentence in a room and a man in Crown Terrace has bought it off a fund that nobody can refuse, and he said so to my face in about eleven seconds and did not pretend otherwise, and he is right about the money and he is right about the ninety and he is right about the woman of twenty-nine and I have not got one word against any of it.

*And the refusal, and the reason for it.* **A panel produces a record.** A minute has a date on it and a date is a day a person can be found by. I said that in about nine words and I meant it and it is true, and I did not put the other half in, and the other half is that I have worked out in week twenty-eight that I have stopped wanting a room and started wanting an audience to be given a sentence, and a man who wants an audience will find a way of being on a panel, and I found one in about four seconds, and I want it recorded in this book that the reason I gave was a mechanism and the reason underneath the mechanism was that I did not want to be in a room saying it again after the last one, and those are not the same thing and I know they are not the same thing. **He is not a villain and he is not lying and I have to write that down about nine times in this volume or I will turn him into one in my own head and then everything I say about him afterwards will be a thing I wanted to be able to say.**

*And the fourth session, and the sentence he said he stole.* **A programme with a name on it is a programme that can be removed. A programme with a job on it is a programme that happens.** Wyn Dunleavy said that to me in a parts cage about four weeks ago and I have written it in this book once and I am not going to write it twice, and a man of forty-four in a yard has now said it to me back with a different accent and a worse reason, and the worse reason is that he read it in somebody else's document. **If that sentence is true, and it is true, then about eleven thousand people are paying for the right to be told a job title is the fourth beat, and there is no name on it to remove, and that is the sentence this volume is going to be about, and I have been in this city for a term and a half and I have never once seen a hinge that worked by being a job instead of a person, and this one is a job, and the person who built it is a levy.** I am not going to be able to fix that and I know that. Entry 104. Enid Sarkis's five lines are in the drawer under this bench and I have not opened the drawer, and the fourth step from the bottom of her stairs is still loud, and it is still me, and I have not been up those stairs in a fortnight, and that is a line in this book about a person and not about me, and it is the fourth time I have used her for something I have not done.
