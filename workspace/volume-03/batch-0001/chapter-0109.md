# Chapter 109 — Not Mine

The shutter was up and the lamp was on and the light had gone about an hour, and Marek Senn had a meter bag open on the bench and about nine pounds' worth of nothing in it, when the bell on the door went and a woman of about thirty-five came in and looked at the door, at the shutter, at the drawer under the bench, at the list in the plastic sleeve on the wall and at the shelf above the bench, in that order, in about nine seconds.

"**I am not here to buy anything,**" she said, "**and I would rather say that in the first eleven seconds than have you offer me something.**"

"Everybody who comes in here gets offered something."

"**Then I will say it again: I am not here to buy anything.**" She did not sit down. "**My name is Leo Marr. I have never met you and I have read about four things you have said in a term, all of them second hand, one of them badly, and I am going to ask you for a piece of paper and you are going to say no, and I want the no before I sit down.**"

"What paper."

"**A photocopy. Four sheets, stapled at the top left. A rota with about two hundred names and a box beside every one. Eleven medical notes. One paragraph on the last page. It came out of a letterbox in this street on the Thursday of last week at about four in the afternoon and I know it came out of a letterbox in this street because I know who put it there and I have not told you who and I am not going to.**"

Marek Senn put the meter bag lid down.

"I did not write it," he said.

"**I know you did not write it. I want it.**"

"No."

"**Right,**" said Leo Marr. "**That is faster than I expected and I am going to leave it there and not ask twice, and I want to be very clear that I am not going to tell you I will not ask a third time.**"

"Ask whatever you want. I am not giving you it."

---

"I have been called a lot of things in nine years," she said. "**Cell, group, movement, and about four years ago somebody in a district office wrote a name into a field on a form and after that it was a name.**"

"Not by you."

"**Not by me. Ever. There is no house. There is about nine people in three districts who used to run relay plates and about four hundred who come to meetings, and every one of us has a different opinion about what we are, and the only thing we have ever agreed on is that a person must be able to disconnect without being found out for it.**" She put a hand flat on the bench. "**And somebody has now put our name on a document that says the omission belongs to a company, and that document is cleaner and better laid out than anything that has come out of us in nine years, and it is in about four thousand pockets, and a leaflet has a name on it that is ours.**"

"I have not seen your name on it."

"**Neither have I. That is what I have come to tell you, and I have told nobody, and I am telling you because you are a man who has said that a name in a room is a bad thing, which is the only reason I am standing in a repair shop at six in the evening instead of somewhere with a door and a list.**"

---

"And there is a fifth line," said Leo Marr.

Marek Senn did not say anything.

"**On the back of the last sheet. Four printed steps, all of them right, and a fifth in ballpoint.** I have seen it. I have been shown it. Not by you."

"Who showed you."

"**A woman of about thirty-nine who has read six copies of a bundle and has a page of her own that she has not given to anybody and is never going to, and who telephoned me on the Monday morning, which she has never done, and who asked me one question: is that one of ours.**" Leo Marr said it evenly. "**And I said no. And then I asked her for four days to find out who it is, and she said yes, and on the Wednesday she rang back and said she was not going to help me, and she was right not to, and I have been angry about it for about two days and I have stopped.**"

"Why is it not one of yours."

"**Because we say leave.**"

She said it as though it was a fact about plumbing.

"**That is the whole of the position and it is the reason about half of us will not walk into a hospital with a person, and it is the reason a great many people in this city think we are a threat, and it is the only sentence I have said to the police and to a clinic and to a district office in nine years.**" She straightened up. "**We say leave. If you cannot get out, leave. If the plate is in your building and the plate is not yours, leave. If somebody is holding a word and you do not have the word, leave, and do not wait for the word, because waiting is how it starts to be yours.**"

"And the fifth line says do it anyway."

"**The fifth line says do it anyway, and the load is yours, and I have got it in my head for about four days and I have got three things about it and I am going to say all three, and none of them is reassurance.**"

---

"**One. It is written by somebody who was in a gap with a load on it and nobody came, and the load came down, and they thought: I do not need that. That is a human being doing arithmetic about their own life and I am not going to stand in front of a man in a lanyard and tell him it is wrong when I do not know how it came out.**"

"Two."

"**Two. It is the most dangerous thing anybody has put in this case, and it is more dangerous than the four words on a laminated sheet, and I have thought about that for nine days and I have got to the end of it and I do not like where I have got.**"

"Why."

"**Because a hinge is broken. That is the whole of what a hinge is, and I have spent nine years telling people that a hinge is a thing with a hole in it, and you can point at the hole, and a frightened room will accept a hole.**" She put her finger on the bench. "**And that is a card with four correct steps and a fifth line that says you are a person who acts, and it is not broken, and it is not going to frighten anybody, and in about four months it is going to be on a table in a ward in Bower Street and a man of fifty-one is going to read it and think, that is right, that is what I would have said, and he is going to be right about himself, and he is going to be wrong about a thing he cannot see.**"

"And three."

"**Three. It is much better than the company's card and I hate that and I have not been able to get round it and I have tried for four days.**" She almost laughed. "**A company card says wait eleven seconds for a supervisor and a woman's card made at a kitchen table says if the word does not come put it down and say so, and both of those are correct, and this one says do not wait, and on the plain numbers this one is safer, because a person who acts on their own judgement and knows it is their own judgement is doing a fourth beat, and the whole of your case, all of it, the term and a half, is that the fourth beat is a person deciding on their own.**"

The room was quiet for about four seconds. Outside, a shutter went down somewhere down the hill.

"Marek Senn," said Leo Marr.

"Yes."

"**You are the hinge now.**"

He did not say anything.

"**Grade three, two days, the release rate, from the Friday of last week, about eleven times on a Friday, eleven parts, in a company's system, on a plastic sleeve with a form in it and an empty box and a strip of tape somebody else put there.**" She had not raised her voice once. "**You said the argument out loud in an office and then you said yes in one syllable and you have a course exception with an empty reason box in it and a manager of fifty-two who has undertaken in writing to leave it empty. And in about four years a foreman somewhere is going to tell a boy of twenty-one that the correct procedure is to wait for the technician, and the boy will be right, and you will be the reason, and there is no lanyard in this city big enough for me to be angry about it.**"

"That is not wrong."

"**No, and that is the difficulty, and I have come here to say it to a person rather than to a leaflet, and I have now said it, and I am not going to do anything about it.**"

"Why not."

"**Because I cut the wire.**" She said it as a fact about her own trade. "**That is what I do and what my nine people do, and I have done it about eleven times in nine years and I have never once hit anybody, and it is the reason about half of us will not walk into a hospital with a person, and it is the reason a great many people in this city think we are a threat.**"

"You have not cut it."

"**Not yet. And I want to be careful here, because I have come to say the sentence and then stop, and I am going to stop, and here is the reason, and it is the only interesting thing I have said in this conversation.**"

---

"**If I cut it, the two days go, and then the district has eleven men in about nine hundred bookings a week and nobody at the end of the telephone, and in about five weeks a man falls off something on a Friday at half past four and the finding is that the district attended nine hundred and one jobs and the nine hundred and second was not one of them, because a woman with a knife had been in a depot office.**"

"That is a good reason."

"**It is a reason and it is not the reason. The reason is that in about nine days I am going to find out where a card is being photocopied, and if the wire comes down first then the woman who is doing the photocopying is going to hear about it from a district notice and she is going to be frightened and she is going to stop and she will be right to, and I will have spent nine years and about eleven good nights of my life to make a frightened woman in a quiet quarter cautious.**" She looked at the drawer under the bench. "**And I would rather keep two days of a man's time and have a name than have a principle and no name. That is the whole of the disagreement I have with nine out of ten people who agree with me and it is why they think I am compromised, and I have stopped explaining it.**"

"Then the wire stays."

"**For now. And do not thank me, because if you thank me I have done a week's work in a shop for nothing.**"

---

The bell went again and a woman of twenty-four came in with a case and a folder and put them on the bench, and she said, "**You are the man who was in the hall,**" and Marek Senn said, "Yes," and Talia Venn said, "**I am not, I am the woman who telephoned you, and I am in here for about nine minutes and then I have got a thing at four.**"

She put the case down and looked at Leo Marr, and Leo Marr looked at her, and neither of them said hello, and Marek Senn stood behind the bench and had the specific feeling of being in a room that had been built for him by two other people.

"**Leo,**" said Talia Venn.

"**Talia.**"

"**You have not come in here in three years.**"

"**No.**"

"**And you have come on a Thursday with a piece of paper and he is twenty-two and he has a lanyard, and I want to say the thing that is in the room before either of you says anything else, and then I am going to go and do my thing at four.**"

"Go on," said Leo Marr.

"**Ianthe Roke's ward. The man of fifty-one. A care-link set up on the Friday because he was brought in on the Thursday and could not answer anybody and there was nobody in the room with him, and the party on the other end of the narrow share is his firm, because he signed something in about nine minutes in a previous life and did not read it.**" Talia Venn put her hands in her pockets, which she did instead of folding them. "**A firm is a party. That is the whole of what I came to say. It is not a disagreement, it is a fact and it is in his file.**"

"**Name a person,**" said Leo Marr. "**One name and one number and he can end it.**"

"That is what I am going to write."

"**Then say who chooses the name. Say who is not asked. Say how that reads to a man alone in a bed in a ward in Bower Street with a frame on his leg, in about four years, when somebody has to explain to him what is connected to him.**"

"I have got about eleven of those in a document and I am going to bring it to a meeting and it is going to be argued about by nine people who are frightened, including me, and the argument is going to be about whether a name is better than a firm, and the answer is yes, and it is not enough, and I know that it is not enough."

"**It is not enough because you cannot get a list,**" said Leo Marr, "**and if you cannot get a list then whoever fills the list fills it from whoever is nearest, and the nearest to a man who installs lifting equipment for a living is the man who pays him, and that is the firm, and you have gone round a corner and got back to where you started with better handwriting.**"

"That is not fair."

"**It is extremely fair and it is the only argument I have got and I have been saying it for nine years and it has never once been won, and I would like you to notice that you have just agreed with it in eleven words and then carried on.**"

"I have noticed," said Talia Venn.

And then Marek Senn, who had been standing behind a bench for about four minutes, said the only sentence available, which was the true one, and which neither of them wanted.

"**There is no list because counting them is a register, and a register finds the people in it. That is the same argument from the other end, and it is the argument a woman of forty-five made to me in a corridor on Monday, and I would say you are both wrong except that I have been trying to explain it for a term and a half and I have not got anywhere, so one of you is closer than the other and I do not know which.**"

The two women looked at him.

"**How many is closer,**" said Talia Venn.

"I do not know."

"**That is the second time this week,**" said Talia Venn, "**and I am going to let it go because I have got a thing at four, and because I know what it costs, and because I am not going to be a woman about it until about four years.**"

She picked up the case and the folder and went, and the bell went about a second later, and neither of the two women said anything on the way out, and the two of them had not said one word to each other that was not about a man of fifty-one in a bed, and about half of what had been said in the room in about eleven minutes had been about something else.

---

Leo Marr went to the shelf above the bench before she went to the door.

"That," she said, "on the shelf. In front of the box of washers."

"What about it."

"**It is a sequence card. Grey. Four printed steps, and a fifth line in a hand, and there are three names along the bottom and one of them has been taken off the whole row with something sharp, and there is a name written in a fourth space with a line drawn under it.**"

"Yes."

"**Who took himself off.**"

"A man of about sixty with a knife, in a fish market, at seven in the morning, about a week after it was written, and he wrote his own removal in the fourth column, and the reason is a clearance in about four years and a daughter of thirty-one."

Leo Marr looked at it for about four seconds.

"**Then the whole of it is a room of eleven with a card in a tin and a name in a drawer and a man on a Friday rota, and none of it is in a system, and one of you has been taken off the row with a knife.**"

"Two of us, and neither of them is me."

"**Two of you, and I have never been one of the three,**" said Marek Senn.

"**That is more than most,**" said Leo Marr.

She put the second card on the shelf the way she had found it, which was the left-hand end, in front of a box of washers, where you would not see it unless you were looking for it.

"**I am going to say one more thing and then I am going, and it is the only thing I have come for that I have not said.**"

"Go on."

"**You are going to be the reason a room stops,**" said Leo Marr. "**Not in a market. In about four months, in a building, with a form, and it is going to be somebody else's form, and about nine hundred people are going to be told that the danger is a group of people, and the group is going to be a name on a leaflet, and there is going to be a man of about forty-four with a microphone and a man of about fifty-one with a fee, and the whole thing is going to be true in about nine words and none of the nine words is going to be about you.**"

"Yes."

"**And when it happens you are going to be the most findable person in the city, and you are twenty-two, and you have got a lanyard and a drawer and about eleven people a week, and I have been in nine rooms this year where somebody like you was standing with a clipboard and a card, and every one of those nine rooms got quieter in about four seconds, and not one person in any of them knew they had done it.**" She put her hand on the door. "**I have come to say it to your face while there are about nine witnesses who would not take my word for it, and I have got a woman of twenty-nine in a taxi rank who heard me say about half of it, and that is the only advantage I have and I am not giving it up.**"

The door went.

The lamp was on over the bench and the shutter was up and outside it had started to rain, and Marek Senn stood in a repair shop in Lattice Ward and worked out, in about eleven seconds, that a woman of thirty-five had been in his shop for about thirty minutes and had told him four things that were true, and had not asked him for anything, and had offered him a knife she had not used, and had gone.
