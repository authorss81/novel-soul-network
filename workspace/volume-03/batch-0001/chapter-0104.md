# Chapter 104 — The Other Side

It came through the letterbox of a repair shop in Lattice Ward on the Thursday afternoon of the first week of the new term, at about four in the afternoon, while a woman of about thirty-one was on the step arguing with him about a lamp.

"There is nothing wrong with it," she said.

"It is flickering."

"It is flickering because it is a lamp in a stairwell and it is about eleven years old and it has a starter in it that costs about nine pounds." She had a child on her hip who was asleep and had been asleep through the whole argument. "**You have told me twice that you would order it and I have come back twice and it is still flickering and the child has had about an hour and a half of sleep in daylight so I would like to be able to say that I came.**"

"I have not ordered it."

"**I know you have not ordered it. I have come here three times and you have not ordered it and you have not told me not to order it either, and that is a different thing and I would like it acknowledged.**"

He looked at the letterbox.

There was a piece of paper in it, folded once, and it had not come through a door because the door was shut and the shutter was up, and it had come through a slot about eleven inches high that a person has to crouch for.

"Somebody has put a letter through that," he said.

"Then open it, and I will wait on the step, and I will put her down if she wakes up."

---

It was four sheets, stapled at the top left, photocopied, and the first sheet was a title in a plain typeface.

**A SECOND ACCOUNT. ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE.**

There was no letter, no name, no address, no date, and no stamp, and the staples were the kind you buy in a packet of a thousand.

The first two pages were a rota. Six weeks, Monday to Saturday, and about two hundred names, and beside every name a box, and about a third of the boxes had a word typed into them, and the word was **NONE**.

Marek Senn stood in the middle of his own shop and read a rota he had seen on a market trestle table under a bag of onions and on a canteen door off the Meridian and in a car park and in six other places since the Wednesday of week twenty-one, and this one was clean, and this one had the boxes aligned, and this one was a photocopy of something that had been a photocopy of something.

He did not have to be told what it was. It was a copy of a copy, made by somebody who had a copy and a machine and about four hours, and it had been made in order to be handed out, and the person who had made it had improved the layout.

The third page was eleven medical notes. He did not read them. He turned the sheet over and put it face down on the bench, which is a thing he has never done in his life and which he did without deciding to.

The fourth page was a single paragraph, typed, and it was the only page on the whole thing that was not a copy, because it was typed in the same machine and it had been typed from something rather than copied, and Marek Senn read it standing up.

"*Regarding the practice named in the delivery documentation supplied to Meridian consolidation point, lot onward, and to a second site on the same contract, the fourth step of the sequence as issued to trainees is not present in the practice as taught by the instructing organisation's own field staff, and its absence was identified by a field supervisor in the district service organisation, whose role is to confirm scope and completion. The wording distributed to trainees on the card issued to them is the wording of the instructing organisation. The practice from which the sequence was derived holds no record of the omission. The omission therefore originates within the practice of distribution and not within the practice as recorded.*"

He read it three times.

Then he sat down on the bench, which he does not do in the middle of a day, and put his hands on his knees, and worked out what he was looking at.

Half of it was true, and he knew which half.

It was true that the card a trainee is issued carries the wording of the distributing company. It was true that the fourth step is not in the practice as taught by field staff. It was true that the practice from which the sequence was derived holds no record of the omission, and that is not a disputed fact in this case, it is the only fact everybody agrees on, and there is a recall notice in about nine hundred inboxes saying the company did not find it, and a letter in four hundred and six postboxes saying it did.

And it was true, as far as anybody in this city can currently establish, that **the omission did not originate inside a company's practice of distribution.** It came off a van. It came from a building. The field for the originating practice is blank on three documents in three buildings and on a company's own stock docket, and a lot reference is not an address, and a lot onward is not a building anybody in that company has ever been inside.

**So the sentence on that page — the omission originates within the practice of distribution — is the sentence that a man of about fifty-two said in a market in week twenty-four, written down, typed, and put through a letterbox, and it is the sentence the levy series has been built to say out loud on a stage, and it is wrong, and it is wrong in the specific way that everything else in this case is wrong: in the direction of a person who is easier to find.**

And it was the answer he had spent a term refusing to give, and the reason he had refused was not that it was unkind. It was that he could not say it without putting a name in a room.

Somebody else had said it for him, in a room he was not in, to a man of about fifty-one with a wife's heating, and it had arrived through his letterbox in a shroud.

---

The woman on the step was still there.

"Sorry," he said. "I have got to shut the door."

"That is fine. I am going to leave her card and I am going to telephone on Friday and if you have not ordered it by Friday I am going to order it myself and send you the bill, and I do not want you to say anything about that, because I have decided it."

"That's fair."

"**It is not fair, it is Wednesday's problem and I am telling you about it on Thursday so that you know.**"

She went off down the hill with the child still asleep, and Marek Senn shut the door and stood with his back against it.

Then he turned the fourth sheet over.

There was a fifth line on it.

The front was typed. The back was not. On the back of the last sheet, in ballpoint, in a hand that was not the machine's and not the four sheets' and was bigger than a hand ought to be on that paper, there were four printed steps and a fifth line, and the four steps were a balance step for carrying a load across a gap, and they were correct. All four of them were correct. He has taught himself not to be able to do that and he could not stop himself from checking.

And under them, in the ballpoint:

*Five. If the word does not come, do it anyway. The load is yours.*

He looked at it for about four minutes.

Somebody had written that. Not a company — the type was the type of a form, but the four steps had been photocopied onto a photocopied sheet, and the fifth line had been written by hand, on the back, in a hand that had got impatient with the ballpoint, and the last full stop had gone through the paper.

And he could reconstruct the whole of it without meeting anybody. A person. A gap. A load. A Saturday, a doorway, a man in a lanyard saying a word, and a moment when the word did not come, and something happening that was fine, and the person thinking *I did not need that*, and then a person writing it down, and then a person being asked to prove it and being given a photocopier.

**And the sentence is true. That is the difficulty. It is true for that person, on that day, and it is the most dangerous sentence anybody has put in this case, because it is the fourth beat inverted, and the fourth beat inverted is not a hinge and is not a practice and is not a thing anybody can point at, it is just a person telling the truth about themselves and then being believed by a stranger.**

He put both sheets on the bench, side by side, the typed paragraph and the handwriting.

The typed paragraph says the omission is the company's.

The handwriting says you do not need anybody.

**And both of them are the reason that a man of forty-four can sell a lecture about who to trust, and neither of them was written by the same person, and neither of them was written by anybody who wanted anything.**

---

He did not ring anybody.

He sat on the bench for about half an hour with the two sheets in front of him and worked out, in order, what he was not going to do, and he wrote the list on the back of a docket, and then he did the last thing on the list, which was to put the list in the drawer, and then he found that he had to take the list out again, because the list was a list of people.

Number one: he was not going to give it to Talia Venn, because a care-link advocate with a certificate to finish who holds a sheet that says the omission is the company's would use it, correctly, in about four years, in a room, and it would be the fourth time a document had got a person into a room.

Number two: he was not going to give it to Ruslan Petric, who had put a question through a box on Weaver Street with no target on it and got four sentences back, and who had said out loud in a room above a shop in the last week of the term that he had just said so for Marek Senn and had not known that was what he was doing.

Number three: he was not going to give it to the woman of thirty-nine who had brought a visitor book into that room, because she would know in about nine seconds who had typed it, and a woman who knows who has typed something in about nine seconds becomes a person somebody pays.

And the fourth item was the one he stopped on.

**He was not going to use it to keep two hundred names out of circulation, because the two hundred names are already in circulation in six places and cannot be un-done, and this copy is cleaner than the others and more likely to travel than the others, and the reason is the layout.**

The layout was the crime. Whoever had made this had sat down and taken a rota with handwriting in the boxes and a medical note and a page of names and had *tidied it*, so that the boxes lined up, so that the NONE entries read down the page like a column of type, so that a person glancing at it in a kitchen would see a document and not a mess. **And tidying is what turns a leak into a statement.** A leak is a piece of paper somebody lost. A statement is a piece of paper somebody made.

He took a pen out and did the only thing on the list he was going to do.

He did not copy the four sheets. He did not photograph them. He did not write the typefaces, or the stapling, or the wording of the paragraph, or a single one of the two hundred names, and he was aware while he was deciding this that this was the sixth time in a term he had declined to write down a true thing, and that the sixth time is the one that starts to be a habit rather than a position, and that a man of fifty-eight with a pencil has been able to say *if I write it down I have to say what I did instead* for twenty-seven years and Marek Senn is twenty-two and has been doing it for fourteen weeks.

Then he put the four sheets in an envelope with nothing written on it, and put the envelope in the drawer under the bench, and shut the drawer.

The drawer had four things in it. A letter with a reference number in the corner whose own last line said it should not be produced in any proceeding without an office being asked first. A page four of a company's induction booklet with no page before the page on it. A booking confirmation for a room he has been sat in. And a plastic wallet with a pencil line on the back of a company's form, in four sentences, with no name and no date, about a man's shoulder, given to him by a man of thirty-nine who said keep it or bin it, and who has not rung to say he has binned it.

**Five things. None of them can be produced to anybody. Four of them are other people's sentences and the fifth is about two hundred people and a woman of thirty-one who has read it six times and has a page of her own that she has given to nobody and never will.**

---

He opened the shop at about half past four and the telephone on the bench went at about ten past five, and it was the depot.

"**Forty-one,**" said Nia Bramhall. "**It is forty-one and it is Thursday and it is about ten to six, and they go out at midnight.**"

"Replies to the letter."

"**Replies to the letter. Nine of them have got a reply-to box on them and I have got the inbox and it is the same inbox I book nine hundred sessions out of.**" She let a beat go past. "**Thirty-eight of them are saying some version of, I was on that rota, is that me.**"

The yard outside went on happening.

"Read me one."

"**No.**"

"Nia—"

"**No, because I know what the answer is going to be for about nine of them, and I know it because I have the rota and I have the site and I have got a woman's induction date, and I am not going to read them to you on a Thursday at ten past five so that you can find out which nine.**" Paper. "**And I am not saying no because I think you would misuse it. I am saying no because about four of them are going to be a person of about thirty-four who has never had an injury and is now going to spend the rest of the year wondering, and I am not the person to tell her, and there is not a person in this city to tell her, and I have known that since the middle of last term.**"

"Then who do they think will tell them."

"**They think the company will. That is the entire reason they have written, and the entire reason I have not read them out is that the honest answer is no and the company answer is yes, and about nine people are going to get the company answer, because there is only one address on the letter.**"

"Have you told them that."

"**I have not told them anything. I told a woman in Crown Terrace with a template that there were replies and she said thank you and closed the mail merge.**" Nia Bramhall's voice did not change. "**And then she said, in about eleven words, that it would be good to send them a short covering note, that it would save people ringing, and I said, and then we would be telling them, and she said, no, we would be acknowledging them, and then she went to somebody else.**"

The line was quiet for about four seconds.

"Write that down," said Marek Senn.

"**I have written it down. In a diary, with a date on it, in a handwriting nobody will be able to read but mine, and that is exactly as much as it is worth and I know it.**"

*What came through the letterbox.* Four sheets, stapled, no name, no date, no stamp, a second account of the same thing that has been on six tables since the Wednesday of week 21. **And the crime is the layout.** Somebody tidied it. Somebody sat down and made the boxes line up and the NONE entries read down the page like a column of type so that a stranger glancing at it would see a document and not a mess. A leak is a piece of paper somebody lost. **A statement is a piece of paper somebody made, and the making of it is the whole crime, and the tidying is how you can tell.** That is the whole of what I have added in a fortnight and I have not said it out loud to anybody.

*And the paragraph, and which half of it is true.* I am not going to write it out again here. **The half that is true is the half everybody in this case agrees on, and that is why there is a recall notice in about nine hundred inboxes that says the company did not find it and a letter in four hundred and six postboxes that says it did.** The half that is not true is a sentence, and the sentence has been sold to a man of forty-four in a yard, and a man of about fifty-two said it in a market in week 24, and somebody typed it and put it through my letterbox. **The sentence is the one I have refused to say for a term because I could only say it by putting a name in a room, and it turns out that refusing does not make it go away, it only makes me the only person in the city who is holding it back.** I have got no conclusion about that and I am not going to manufacture one tonight.

*And the fifth line.* **Four printed steps, all four of them correct, and a fifth in ballpoint on the back of the last sheet in a hand that got impatient with the pen. I am not writing the fifth line out again and I am not going to explain why, and the reason is the whole entry.** It is true. That is the difficulty and I have turned it over about nine ways. It is true for whoever wrote it, on whatever gap they were on, on whatever day, and it is the most dangerous sentence anybody has put in this case because it is the fourth beat turned the other way up, and the fourth beat turned the other way up is not a hinge and is not a practice and is not a thing anybody can point at. **It is a person telling the truth about themselves and then being believed by a stranger, and that is worse than the four words on a laminated post, because the four words on the post have been there twenty years and nobody has photographed them, and this is in biro and it is on a photocopier.** I have got to find out where a photocopier is. I have not got a way of finding out where a photocopier is that does not involve asking a person to describe a page, and a person who describes a page is a person who has read a page.

*And forty-one replies on a Friday night.* Nine of them will get the company answer because there is one address on the letter and it is a general enquiries address and it has a template and a woman in Crown Terrace who closed a mail merge. **The answer that is owed is a person telephoning another person, and it is not going to be a letter, and there is no post in this city whose job it is to make the phone call, and a woman of thirty-one with a diary has been told twice this term that she has no standing and she has been told it by me.** Five things in the drawer under this bench. None of them can be produced to anybody. I am not going to make the sixth by writing any of this down twice, and I know that is the fifth time in fourteen weeks, and a man of fifty-eight has been able to say the sentence about the tenth time for twenty-seven years and I have been able to say it for fourteen weeks and the difference between those two numbers is the whole of what I have left.
