# Chapter 106 — Cut Off

The notice on the door of Civic Spine 7 was a single A4 in a plastic sleeve at chest height with a clip at the top, and it had been there since about eleven o'clock on the Monday morning of week thirty-two, and by ten past nine there were four men standing in front of it not saying anything to each other.

Marek Senn read it twice from nine feet back, which is how you read a thing that is about somebody else.

*Service arrangements, Meridian corridor. From the Monday of week 33 the district will not be represented at customer sites where attendance falls to the attending contractor. Queries to the district service office.*

There was a date at the bottom in a different typeface and it was the Monday of week thirty-three, which was seven days away.

He had come in on the Monday because he had a relay plate to look at for a man on a Friday rota and Spine 7 was the only place in this city that had one on a shelf, and he had come in on the Monday about nine hundred times, and it was the first time in a term and a half that four men had been standing in front of the door when he arrived.

Nobody said good morning.

"It is the attending contractor," said a man of about twenty-six who had been in the doorway for about eleven minutes. "**That is the whole of it. If somebody is booked to come, somebody comes. If the booking does not say who comes, then nobody comes, because nobody knows which of the eleven of us is the one that was meant.**"

"Since when is the booking in that state."

"**Since about the spring, in everything except the parts. Parts have got a name on them. Attendance has got a job on it.**"

---

Sera Quill came out of the back four minutes later with a tool braid in one hand and a rag in the other and read the notice from about eleven inches away without touching it, and then went back in, and then came out again and said:

"Inside."

The bench against the long wall had his job sheet on it from the week before and a coffee cup on it that was not hers and was about a week old. The parts cage was open. The locked relay cabinet had a printed label on it with no name and no date on the label and there is no such thing in this city that keeps that cabinet's key in a book, and there are two keys and one of them is in a tin in a drawer in a room in Nine Bridges that belongs to a man of about sixty who gives grey cards away.

"Read it out," said Sera Quill.

"You have read it."

"**I have read it. I want to hear whether a third-year reads it the same way, because in about four years somebody is going to print it in a handbook and it is going to be the wording on that sheet, and if you read it differently from me then one of us is going to have to be right about it in a room.**"

He read it out.

She listened with her arms folded.

"**That is one of four sentences,**" she said, "**and there are about eleven words in it that do the work, and the work is *the attending contractor*, and I will give you the whole of it in about nine words and then you can go and do your day.**"

"Go on."

"**Nobody decided to do this.**"

"That's not nine words."

"That is four words and I have not got the other five and I have not got them this morning, and I have been standing in this room since seven trying." She put the braid down on the bench. "**Here is what happened, and I know what happened because I was in the room in the spring. A service level got rewritten. It is a schedule, it is about nine pages, and nobody voted on it because a schedule is not a thing anybody votes on. And in the spring somebody in a district office realised that our attendance on a customer site is a subcontracted service, and our subcontract is with the customer, and the customer's own people are not ours, and a man whose card is in date is not the same thing as a man from the district. And so the district stopped being the thing that turns up when nobody has said who turns up, and now it is whoever is on the booking.**"

"Who wrote the clause."

"**A procurement officer, in the middle of last term, on a form, and she did it in a fortnight and she is not a monster and she did it because the alternative on her desk was eleven men being on eleven sites for a company that had not bought the cover.**"

"Which means the crew on a Friday—"

"**Means that if a rigging job goes wrong at half past four on a Friday afternoon, and there is a card in the cab and no booking with a name on it, then there is nobody there. Not us. Not them. Nobody.**" She said it without any weight at all. "**And in about three weeks somebody is going to fall off something, and somebody is going to say to a newspaper that the district pulled out of customer sites, and that will be true, and it will be a lie, and I will be able to prove it in about nine words and I will not be able to prove it in nine words to anybody who was not in that room in the spring.**"

---

He sat down on the bench, which he does not do at Spine 7, and looked at the job sheet that was not this week's.

"You distrust the company," he said.

"I distrust the clause. I do not distrust Threadline and I have said so to a man of forty-four in a yard a week ago and I would say so again."

"And you distrust the Quiet House."

"**I distrust the Quiet Quarter and the leaflet and I distrust anybody who is going to be able to say that they are not connected to anybody, because everybody is connected to somebody and the people who say it loudest have usually been hurt by somebody specific and have turned the specific into a general.**" She turned round. "**That is half a reason. You are going to ask me for the other half and I am going to tell you that I am not going to give you it, and you are going to do the thing where you wait.**"

"I am not going to wait."

"You were going to wait. I have watched you wait nine times in a term and a half and it is your best trick and it works on four people in this city and I am one of them and I have got four minutes before I stop being one."

"Fair."

"**It is not fair. It is a boundary and I am allowed to have one and I have just used one and I did not apologise for it, and you did not ask me to, which is the reason I am telling you.**"

---

She went and got two mugs and did not ask him whether he wanted one, and poured the water, and sat down on the parts cage, which is not a thing anybody at Spine 7 does at nine in the morning.

"**Now. The thing you actually came in here for, which is a plate for a man who is on a Friday rota, and you did not come in here for the notice, and you have not looked at the notice once since you read it, and that is the first thing you have done in a term that I have noticed and I have not written it down anywhere.**"

"Go on."

"**You have not got a second exit.**"

"I have not."

"**Right. And the answer to a hinge is not a louder voice.**"

"I know that."

"**You do not know that, you have written it in a book, which is not the same as having it, and the difference is that a book is something you can quote in a room and a thing you have is something you can do on a Friday. It is not a louder voice. It is a second exit.**"

"A second exit out of what."

"**Out of the room.**" She put a finger on the bench. "**A man says do not release on your own judgement, and a technician is standing there, and the technician is the only person in the room who can say go. That is not a bad practice. That is a good practice with a single door in it, and a single door is not a practice, it is a bottleneck, and a bottleneck is a hinge, and you have spent a term and a half proving that.**"

"Then the manual."

"**The manual is 3-08. It is the revision before the card and before the company and it has got a boundary in it printed at the bottom of the page in a box, and I have read it nine times, and it is the safest document in this city and it is completely useless, and I will tell you why.**"

"Because a printed boundary is a claim and not an action."

"Because **a printed boundary is the same in every room.**" She said it fast. "**3-08 says the same thing on page forty-one in a bay in Nine Bridges and on page forty-one in a ward in Bower Street and on page forty-one in a house in Lattice Ward, and the risk on page forty-one in each of those is a different risk, and a document that cannot tell the difference between them is a document that gets followed in the one where it is wrong.**"

Marek Senn looked at the wall of handwritten notes above the bench, about four hundred of them, in nine hands, none of them printed, none of them signed.

"Then what is it."

"**It is a second person in the room who is allowed to say go.**" Sera Quill put the mug down. "**Not louder. Not certified. Not in a system. Allowed. A second person who can say *we do not wait for him* and put the load down and go home, and nobody has to answer to anybody about it, and there does not have to be a form because there is not going to be a report and there is not going to be an investigation and there is not going to be a document with a date on it in about four years.**"

"And how many of those are there."

"**In this city? At this minute? I have no idea and neither do you and that is the entire problem and I have been trying to explain it to four people for a term and a half and I have got nowhere, and I have got nowhere because the only way to count them is to know all of them.**"

---

He was quiet for about eleven seconds, and then he said the thing he had been carrying since Friday, which was that he was on the rota.

"I am on the Friday release callout. Grade three, about eleven times on a Friday, from last week. Eleven parts, about eleven seconds, and I am the only person in the room who can say it."

Sera Quill did not move.

"**Are you.**"

"Yes."

"**And are those eleven parts correct.**"

"They are correct."

"**And is there anybody else in those eleven rooms who can put the load down.**"

"No."

"**Then you are not the fourth thing in a room, Marek, you are the door.**"

She got up off the parts cage and took the two mugs to the sink and ran the water over them for longer than the mugs needed.

"**I have been in this room about nine years,**" she said, with her back to him, "**and I have taught about nine apprentices and about four of them are still doing it, and every single one of them thinks the job is knowing the eleven parts. And it is not. The job is being the second exit for the person who says the eleven parts, and there is not one job on any piece of paper in this building for that, and in about four years one of those four is going to go out on a Friday on his own and be good at it and be alone in it.**"

"Then somebody should have put it on a form."

"**No, and I want to be very careful here because you are about to say it again and I have heard about nine people say it this year. A form is a document about a person, and a document about a person finds the person, and the second exit is not a document about a person. The second exit is a person who happens to be there.**"

---

He got the plate, and he checked it, and it was fine, and he wrote it up on the back of the notice in his own hand with a date and his initials at the bottom, and he was aware while he was doing it that the initials at the bottom of a line on a piece of plastic in a clip on a door are not the initials at the bottom of the fifth line on the list in a plastic sleeve on a wall in Lattice Ward, and that the two pieces of paper have the same four letters and mean opposite things, and that there is no such thing in this city that keeps a register of which is which.

"There is one thing I want to say and then I am going to go and do my day," he said.

"Go on."

"**There are seven people in this city who have written down a protection of their own and have not met each other, and there are about nine more of them that I could not name. A woman of sixty-eight with five lines and no date. A woman of twenty-seven with nine hundred words in a drawer. Nine people in a co-operative with a ruled empty box. A records clerk who does not know three suppliers exist. A man of twenty-three with a sheet through a postbox. A first-year who read two hundred and forty words out loud. A foreman of thirty-four who said it in nine words to a stranger. And about four more that are not those seven.**"

"You have not met them either."

"No, and I am not going to, and I have not called it anything, and I have written down in a book that a word for that would get them a reference number, and a reference number tells the people who wrote in it that they wrote in it."

"**Good,**" said Sera Quill. "**Now go and be a door.**"

At the door she said one more thing, not turning round.

"**If anybody asks you on that Friday whether you have a partner, you say yes, and you say the name, and if you cannot produce a name then you are not a door, you are a hinge with a card on, and I will not have built a thing in this room for eleven years that is the same shape as the thing on a post off the Meridian with no name and no date on it.**"

The four men had gone off the step and the notice was still in its clip.

*109. Monday of week 32, half past ten in a workshop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is about a woman of forty-five with a tool braid and four men who stood in front of a door at ten past nine and did not say good morning to each other, and a man of about twenty-six who read a four-sentence notice correctly in a doorway in about eleven seconds.*

*The notice, and the four words that do the work.* **The attending contractor.** Nobody decided it. A service level got rewritten in the spring, a schedule of about nine pages, and a procurement officer in a district office did it in a fortnight because the alternative on her desk was eleven men being on eleven sites for a company that had not bought the cover. **Nobody is a villain in this and I want that written down in her favour, because a man of forty-four taught me in a yard last week that a line in a document can be true and still be the worst sentence anybody has read, and the reverse is also true, and a true sentence in a schedule is worse than a lie in a schedule, because a lie gets argued about on a Tuesday and a schedule gets implemented on a Monday.** In about three weeks somebody is going to fall off something at half past four on a Friday and a newspaper is going to say the district pulled out, and that will be true and it will be a lie, and she can prove it in about nine words to four people in this city and I am one of them and that is the entire audience.

*And the refusal, and the boundary.* **She gave me half a reason and kept the other half and told me she was going to tell me she was not giving it, in advance, and then she used it on me and did not apologise, and I did not ask her to.** That is the fourth time this term somebody has drawn a line with me and not apologised for it and I have now been on the receiving end of more boundaries in a term and a half than I have drawn, and I do not know whether that is growth or whether I have simply run out of people to be careful with. **I am not going to write down what the other half is. I have a feeling about it and it is about a card and a typeface and about a year in which somebody was very careful, and she has never said a name out loud in this building in eleven years and neither have I, and a man of forty-four taught me last week that a name is a person in a room, and so I have written nothing and I have written here that I have written nothing, which is a compromise I am not proud of and is the only one available to me tonight.**

*And the thing she said to me in a room with about four hundred handwritten notes on the wall, which is the sentence this whole volume is going to be about.* **The answer to a hinge is not a louder voice, it is a second exit.** And then the second half of it, which I had not got, and which took her four seconds: **a second exit is a person in the room who is allowed to say go and put the load down and go home, and nobody has to answer anybody and there does not have to be a form, because there is not going to be a report and there is not going to be a document with a date on it in about four years.** And the count. **There is no way to count them and that is the whole problem and I have been failing to explain it to four people for a term and a half, and the reason I have been failing is that the only way to count a second exit is to know all of them, and knowing all of them is a register, and a register finds the people in it.**

*And the manual, which is still 3-08 and which she read in about nine seconds and told me why it is worthless.* **A printed boundary is the same in every room.** Page forty-one in a bay and page forty-one in a ward and page forty-one in a house, and three different risks, and a document that cannot tell them apart is a document that gets followed in the one where it is wrong. That is the best argument against every piece of paper in this case that I have heard from anybody who has to sign her own name, and it is better than mine, and I have had a term and a half to find a better one and I have not.

*And what I am now.* **A door.** That is the word she used and she used it about eleven seconds after I told her I was the only person in about eleven rooms a week who can say the eleven parts, and I have not got an argument against it. I have got seven people in this city who have written down a protection and have not met each other and are not going to, and about nine more of them that I could not name, and a woman of forty-five telling me that the four of them I have spoken to are not in the eleven rooms, and she is right, and I have not been in one of those eleven rooms with anybody in it who can stop me except Marguerite Okpara, who can put a load down and go home and who will not be the one who says the number. **If anybody asks me on a Friday whether I have a partner I have to say a name, and I do not have one that is not a person who can end a share I do not own, and that is the gap I have been walking about in for a term and a half and I have got it written down now and that is the first thing this term has cost me that I can put in a file.**
