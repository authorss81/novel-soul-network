# Chapter 110 — The Second Line

There were four jobs on the Friday and three of them were in nine and the fourth was in the Lower Wards and finished at ten past four, and at four minutes past five he was in a corridor outside two rooms and a corridor off a depot, holding a job sheet, waiting for a man of fifty-two to come out of his own office with a form.

He had not got the appointment. He had not booked it. On the Friday of week thirty he had been in this corridor and had been told it would be a Friday, and neither of them had written it down, and it had happened on the day, which is the correct outcome and is not the same as a diary.

Wyn Dunleavy came out of his office at ten past five with a form on a clipboard and a pen in his shirt pocket and said, "**In. Door open. I have got about eleven minutes and then I have got a thing.**"

He sat down at the desk and did not sit in the chair, and put the form on the desk, and there were four fields on the top part of it and one of them was greyed out.

"**Read it out,**" he said.

"**Course exception. Holder's name. Course reference. Renewed or withdrawn. Reason.**"

"**And the fifth is the one I have been waiting six weeks for.**" He uncapped the pen. "**Say the reason.**"

"I have not got a reason I am willing to give."

"**No. And I am not asking twice and I did not ask once, and I want that on the record in the same way you wanted it on the record in the office six weeks ago, which is that you have said it out loud before you said the word, and I am not going to have anybody in about four years read this form and think that I got it out of a man who was tired on a Friday.**"

He wrote in the box: *No reason given by holder.*

"That is not what the box says," said Marek Senn.

"**The box says *reason*. The box does not say *reason if not held*, and I have looked, and there is no other box, and the reason there is no other box is that nine hundred of these are signed a year, eight hundred and forty of them have got something in it, and eleven of them have got something in it that a person wrote for themselves rather than their line manager, and the eleven are the ones I think about.**" He turned the form round. "**That is a better answer than the one on it, and it is worse, and both of those are correct. Sign at the bottom.**"

Marek Senn read the line and signed it.

"**Done,**" said Wyn Dunleavy, "**and that is a Friday in week thirty-two and the rota runs to the last Friday of the term, and I will do it again in about thirteen weeks in this chair, and if the reason is different in ten weeks I will write whatever you give me and if it is the same I will write the same thing, and I would like you to have both of those on the record today so that you cannot be surprised in the spring by a man who is fair.**"

"Thank you."

"**Do not thank me. I did the thing that was in front of me and the thing in front of me was a form with a box in it.**"

---

"There is a second thing," said Wyn Dunleavy, "**and I have thought about whether to put it in this corridor or in a room, and I am putting it in this corridor because I have got four minutes and because a corridor is a thing nobody keeps a note of.**"

"Go on."

"**The programme that your man in the yard turned down.**" He did not have a folder and did not open one. "**I have read it. It is a page and a half. It has been amended. The amendment is dated the Thursday of last week.**"

"What did it add."

"**One line. It goes in the section on coverage, and it is a second line, and it reads: *and a session addressing the standing confusion in the trade between a supervised confirmation and a technician's own judgement, and the current position of the district's release callout arrangements.***"

Marek Senn did not say anything.

"**And it has got no name on it. There is no name on the second line. There is a job on it. There is a job title on it, and it is a job title that is in four hundred pay packets in this district, and one of those four hundred is yours.**" He put the pen down. "**I read that line on the Monday morning at eleven and I read it twice and I knew exactly what it was within four seconds, and I have read the time slot, and I know what two days a week is, and I have known what two days a week is since week twenty-two.**"

"And you have done nothing."

"**I have done nothing about it,**" said Wyn Dunleavy, "**and I would like to say it checked and I would like to be believed and I would like it written down in the same line, and that is not me being dramatic, that is the whole of what I am able to offer you and I have offered it twice this term and once in this corridor.**"

"You checked and you cannot do anything about it."

"**I checked and I cannot do anything about it. Here is the whole of it, and it is about nine words, and I have thought about it since the Monday.**" He turned the clipboard over. "**The fund's committee meets in six weeks. There are nine people on it. The chair signs the specification and the chair is an office and not a person, and the office is a woman of about fifty-eight who has never been in a bay in her life and who signs about eleven of these a year. There is nobody in that structure who can be shown a line and asked: whose job is this, and did they agree to it, and who ends it. And if I telephone that office on Monday and ask, I am a district service manager of fifty-two who has read a document and has a preference, and in about four years that is a man with a preference in a file.**"

"So it gets said out loud."

"**It gets said out loud, and it gets said on a stage, and the man saying it will be somebody who is paid, and he will not be a liar, and he will say the second line is about a confusion in the trade and he will be right, and four hundred people will hear the word *technician* and go home and be frightened of the wrong person.**"

"It is my job title."

"**It is a job title and it is in four hundred pay packets, and by about the middle of next month there is going to be a leaflet in about four thousand pockets with a microphone and a fee on it, and there is no version of this in which somebody who is not a technician stands up in that room and says, that is not about him. It will be about him. The whole of the second line is *him*, and he does not have to agree to it and it does not need his name, and that is the entire point of it.**"

He looked at the corridor.

"**And the thing I have not been able to get round, and I have had a week, is that I said the other half of this to you in this building a month ago in a parts cage with a clipboard, and I said it as a mechanism, and I said it because it was true and it was useful, and I did not think about it for one second in terms of a leaflet. And it is now a line in a document that I have read. And you can have that for nothing, because I am not going to pretend I did not say it, and I am not going to pretend I would take it back, because it is still true, and if I took it back it would only be true in a cage.**"

---

They stood in the corridor for a minute and a half.

"Whose name is on the amendment," said Marek Senn.

"**Nobody's. It is a section, not a bullet. The chair's office added two lines to a coverage section a week ago because a question came in from somewhere and the answer that would have satisfied the questioner would have been a refusal, and a refusal is not an answer to a coverage section, and the chair's office has nine of these to get through.**"

"A question came in from somewhere."

"**Somebody asked the fund a question in the middle of last term. It is not on the file with a name on it. It is in a mailbox, and a clerk has turned it into two lines, and that is the entire distance between a woman of thirty-four who read a leaflet and a job title in four hundred pay packets.**"

"Was it the hall."

"**I do not know and I am not going to look, and I am telling you that I am not going to look, and you can decide what to do with that.**" He put the clipboard under his arm. "**That is the whole of the second thing. A line, a date, and a man of fifty-eight who signs nine of these a year and has never met a technician. And there is nothing in this I can put in a system, and the reason there is nothing in this I can put in a system is that the second line is not a fact about anybody. It is a question with a job on it, and a question with a job on it cannot be asked of a person, and that is the whole of the trouble and I have been doing this for thirty years and I have watched that trick come in four times and it has never once been anybody's fault.**"

---

He got to the shop at twenty to six and Rafi Pell was on the bench with a strip of card in his hand and a look on his face that Marek Senn had seen nine times and had never got used to.

"What."

"**There is a leaflet in the corridor at the depot. Four of them, same as the ones in the pocket by the parts cage, and I have read the third one on it and it has got a session called something about a technician, and I have read it twice and I have to ask you a question and I have thought about it on the way here for an hour and a half.**"

"Ask."

"**Are they going to say your name.**"

"**No,**" said Marek Senn. "It is worse than that and it is better than that. It is a job title, and it is in four hundred pay packets, and my name is not going to be on it and I could not stop it if it was."

"Right." Rafi Pell turned the strip of card over in his hand. "**Then the second question.**"

"Go on."

"**Is that the fourth beat.**"

Marek Senn stood in a repair shop in Lattice Ward with the shutter up and the light gone and the lamp on, and worked out that a courier of twenty-one had asked him the only question that mattered, and that it had taken him four seconds, and that he had about nine words.

"Yes," he said. "**And it has got my face on it, in about four weeks, without my name on it, and there is nothing in this city I can do about that except be worse at the job or be somewhere else, and I am not going to be worse at the job, and I have not worked out about the other thing.**"

"Then write it on the shelf," said Rafi Pell.

"No."

"**You said put it on the shelf last time and I said that is not an answer.**"

"That is still not an answer," said Marek Senn.

The lamp was on over the bench. On the shelf above it, at the left-hand end, in front of a box of washers, a grey transit sequence card with a boy's handwriting in the fifth line, two names against a review date in the spring, and a third name taken off the whole row in a fish market at seven in the morning with a knife, and the taker's name in the fourth column, because that is where the person who changes a card puts their name.

In the fourth pocket of a tool roll on the bench there was an original. Grey, thin, four beats, and a fifth line that had been blank for about four years and was still blank, and nobody was going to fill it in.

On the wall there was a list in a plastic sleeve with a fifth line on it and his initials at the bottom of the fifth line, and the initials meant *not done*, and there was no longer any document in this city that required that list to be on the wall, and he had not taken it down.

About eleven people a week were going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and he had a job that was four days a wage and two days a question he could not refuse, and there were about eleven thousand premises paying a levy into a fund that had bought a sentence with one line in it and was about to buy a second line, and he was the most findable person in the city and his name was not going to be on anything.

**And those two numbers had never been in the same sentence. Not once, in a term and a half, in a book in a drawer, in a market, in a hall, in a corridor, in an office, in a yard, on a form, in a company, or in a voice. About eleven people a week who are going to wait for him. About eleven thousand people who pay for the sentence that says who to wait for.**

He was the only person in this city who could put those two numbers in the same sentence, and he could not do it, because putting them together was a document, and a document was a thing that had a date on it and a name on it, and the name would not be his.

It would be four hundred people's.
