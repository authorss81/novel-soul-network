# Chapter 102 — Six Weeks Late

The lamp was on over the bench and the shutter was up and it was ten past ten on the Tuesday night of the first week of the new term, and Marek Senn rang the number and it rang twice and was answered by a woman who said his name in a way that meant she had been holding the telephone for four minutes and had decided not to make that part of the conversation.

"I am late," he said.

"You are six weeks late."

"By the Friday of week thirty at about ten at night, when I had just come out of an office and said yes to two days a week on a rota, to a manager who asked me to say the argument out loud first and then wrote one line on a form."

"Talia."

"That is the whole of it. I have not got a better reason than that and I have had six weeks to build one, and every one of the six weeks I could hear the sentence you gave me, and every one of them the sentence was *ring me on a Tuesday*, and the Tuesday was always two days after the thing, and I kept not dialling on a Saturday or a Sunday, which you did not ask for, so I want it on the record that I did it the way you said and not the way that suited me."

There was a chair moved on the other end.

"**Go on then,**" she said.

---

He gave her the review in about nine words because there was nothing in it to give.

He was not thanked, the file is closed, the room was not able to record a decision because nobody could establish what it was convened to decide, and a corrections clerk in another room is going to be able to confirm the closure on the record. Then the part he had not expected: the report went back to a district office and it went to a Continuity Office caseworker who has never met Marek Senn, and it went to a person in Crown Terrace whose name he never learned, and it was not because anyone had been cruel to him. It was because a review is filed, and filing is what happens to a thing at the end of a procedure, and a procedure does not ask a file where it is going.

"That is the correct outcome," she said.

"Yes."

"**And you are disappointed.**"

"I am not disappointed. I am—" He stopped, and started again. "I had three words. That is the thing I have not told you and I am going to tell you now, on a Tuesday, because it is six weeks late and there is no version of this in which I get to pick a day."

"Say the three words."

"They were a person's name, and a date, and the word *was*. And I did not say them."

"You did not say them because I told you not to say the second half."

"Because the second half was true when you gave it to me and it was not true at half past ten that morning, and I am not going to say a true sentence I do not believe, and that is the only reason and it took me about a second and a half and it is the first time in a term that the truth has caught me instead of a lie."

"And nobody noticed."

"Nobody noticed. And I sat in a chair for about eleven minutes afterwards being astonished about that, and the reason it works is that people are not looking for a sentence, they are looking for a person to leave with."

---

She was quiet for long enough that he looked at the second-hand phone.

"What did you actually ring me about," said Talia Venn.

"That is not fair."

"It is completely fair and you have done it to me four times this year and I have done it twice." A cup went down on a surface. "**Ring me on a Tuesday and tell me afterwards. That is not a request for a debrief, that is a request for you to have said a thing out loud to a person who is not in a room. It is not a request for a list.**"

"I know what it was."

"Then say it."

"I am on the Friday release callout rota," said Marek Senn, "from the Friday of last week. Grade three, the release rate, two days, and I am the fourth thing in about eleven rooms a week, and about four of the eleven are not on the rota at all because a company does not hold a course on a Friday. That is what I rang about. I wanted to tell a person who is not a manager."

He heard her put her hands flat on whatever was in front of her, which is a thing she did when she was deciding whether to be angry or to be useful, and he waited.

"Say the number."

"About eleven a week. Wyn Dunleavy said about eleven and he has not been wrong about a figure yet. About nine of them are on the rota already and about four are not. One of the four is a woman of about twenty-nine who is frightened of getting something wrong at work, and she came to that open session in the trading estate because her employer paid for it, and she is the person a man of forty-four built an evening talk around this week, and I have met her and she is good at her job."

"**And the rota means you are the person who says the word.**"

"Yes."

"**Marek.**"

"I know."

"**You have a card exception, and the condition on the rota is a card in date or an exception recorded, and you are going to be standing in about eleven rooms a week confirming a release to people who will be taught, by the practice itself, that a release waits for you.**" Her voice did not rise. "**How long has that rota been agreed.**"

"Since the Friday of week thirty at ten past five in the afternoon. In a chair. He asked me to say the argument first and I said it and it was a good argument and I said yes anyway in one syllable, and I said I would like it written down that I said the argument before I said yes, and he wrote that down."

"**Was it a good argument.**"

"It was the best argument I have got. It is the argument that a certificate is a portable dependency and that in about four years a boy of twenty-one with a count of his own is going to be told that the correct procedure is to wait for the technician. And it is correct. And I am not able to hold it against a wage."

There was nothing in the line for four seconds.

"**Do you want me to say the thing you have not said,**" said Talia Venn.

"Which thing."

"**You have not asked me anything.**"

"I have not asked you anything because there is nothing to ask."

"**Ask me anyway.**"

He looked at the wall in Lattice Ward. The list in a plastic sleeve was on it, and the initials at the bottom of the fifth line meant *not done*, and there was no longer any document in this city that required it to be there, and he had looked at it eleven times since the Friday and had not taken it down.

"I have done it nine times since the Friday of week thirty-one," he said. "**Nine times, and about nine hundred of those have been done by people who have never been asked, and none of the nine is written down anywhere, and I have not written mine down either, and I did not know until four seconds ago what that was.**"

"Then what happens," he said, "on a shift when the word does not come."

He heard her stop.

"You did not have a question," she said.

"I have got a question."

"**Say it again.**"

"What happens on a shift when the word does not come. If I do not say it. Not at half past three on a Tuesday in a hall. In a bay, on a Friday, in about eleven rooms a week, to a person who has been taught since they were nineteen that the release waits for the word. What happens then, and what do they do, and how do they learn it."

She took long enough that he looked at the number on the display.

"**Nothing,**" she said.

"That is not an answer."

"**It is the only answer, and I have known it for two years, and you have known it for about four months, and neither of us has said it to the other one, and that is the first time in two years I have caught us both doing the same thing.**" Her voice went flat in the way that meant she was being accurate on purpose. "**Nothing happens. There is no fault. There is no incident, there is no form, there is nothing anybody can report, and the load is not on your floor, and the person standing there holds the thing they are holding for about eleven seconds longer and then they do the safe thing on their own, which is put it down, which is correct, and they do it correctly nine times out of ten without knowing there was a question.**"

"That is the hinge," said Marek Senn.

"**That is the entire hinge, and it is four words long, and it is *nothing happens*, and I have been writing consent material about it since before you were in that hall.**" A chair. "**And then the second thing happens, about four months later, and the second thing is that a man who held a load for eleven seconds too long and put it down safely, and did it again the next week, and did it nineteen times, starts being the man who is careful. And a foreman who cannot write down the tenth time in twenty-seven years because he has never known what he did instead starts being a man who is lucky. And about four years after that, somebody in an office is going to write down that unsafe practice in this district is performed by technicians who are not in date, and it is going to be true, and it is going to be true because people who are careful do not get counted as good, they get counted as slow, and slow is what you put in a report.**"

The lamp buzzed.

"I am not arguing with you," she said. "**I want to be very clear that I am not arguing with you, because you have just asked me a question you could have answered and you asked me because you have got a man in a lanyard on a rota and you have started to be frightened of what a floor is.**"

"Then why did you not say so last term."

"Because last term you were the fourth thing in a room on purpose and you knew what it was, and I was not going to be the woman who explains a man's own job to him while he is in the middle of it." She said it without heat. "**And because of the thing about the Saturdays.**"

Marek Senn did not say anything for a second and a half.

"**I am not going to be a woman about it,**" she said. "**That is the Friday. That was the Friday of week twenty-four and I have been on the other side of it for about six weeks and there is nothing in that for you to get.**"

"I know."

"**Then stop sitting in a dark shop at ten at night working out that I have noticed you have not told me something. You have not told me three things. I have watched you not tell me three things over a telephone in nine minutes and I have counted them, and I am not going to ask, and one day you are going to tell me one of them and it is going to be the wrong one, and I am telling you now so that it is on the record that I saw the number.**"

"Three."

"**Do not count them out loud to me, that is how you do it.**"

---

She was going to ring off and then she did not.

"**One more thing, and it is small, and it is because you asked me a question instead of looking it up.** My team has a woman in it of about thirty-one who is in charge of an exchange room in a building in Bower Street, and she has a care-link diary with about eleven dates in it, and one of the dates was the middle of last term. **I asked her about it in a corridor and she said she had not got anybody whose job it is, and I said that is not a thing you can say, and she said I know, and she is right and I did not have an answer.**"

"Is that the review date of a care-link?"

"That is the review date of a care-link and a review of a care-link is a phone call. **Not a letter, not a form, not a review meeting. A person telephoning another person to say come back and say no.** And the design of the entire thing is that one person knows the other one is coming, and the whole safety of it is the phone call, and there is no post in this city whose job it is to make the phone call, and it is the only document I have ever read that gets better the longer nobody looks at it."

"Somebody has to ring them."

"**Somebody has to ring them, and the somebody is a person who is busy in a ward on a Tuesday, and it is her fault, and it will still be her fault in four years, and there is no form of the fact anywhere.**"

"Does she know about the four months."

"**She knows about the four months. She has written the date on the top of a page in her own hand and she has not moved it and she has not told anybody and she has not asked anybody, and that is the correct thing to do with a date you are frightened of, and I have watched three of us do it now.**"

Marek Senn looked at the lamp.

"The rota is a Friday," he said.

"I know what day the rota is."

"You said the fourth condition does not bite on a company's yard."

"**It never has. The fourth condition is on the Institute's equipment, the Institute's premises and the Institute's insurance, and a bay four in a yard on the far side of the city is none of those three, and I have known that for about four months and I have been waiting for you to work it out and you have not, and I have been saying nothing so that you would.**"

"You have been saying nothing so that I would."

"**I have been saying nothing so that you would,**" said Talia Venn, "**and that is the worst thing I have ever done to you, and I am going to do it again if I have to, and you may put that in the same book as everything else.**"

Click.

He sat on the bench for about eleven minutes afterwards with the dead telephone in his hand and did not turn the lamp off, and he did not open the drawer, and the list on the wall was still there with his initials at the bottom of the fifth line meaning *not done*, and the fifth line in the original in the fourth pocket of the tool roll was still blank, and outside the shutter there was a man in a district jacket walking up Lattice Ward at ten past ten at night with a notebook, and Marek Senn counted the exits of a room he was alone in, and the number was one, and he has known that since he was nineteen.

*105. Tuesday of week 31, ten past ten at night, and this entry is about a woman of thirty-one with a care-link diary who is not in this story and is not going to be thanked for it, and about a woman of twenty-four four miles away who made me say a number out loud so that I would stop pretending I had not been counting.*

*I said the three words were not said.* I have not written down what the three words are, on purpose, in this book, for the first time, and the reason is not integrity. **The reason is that if I write them down here then there are two copies, and one of them is in a shop in a drawer under a bench, and a copy in a shop is a copy a person can be found by, and I have had that argument out loud with a man of forty-four in a yard and I cannot now go and do the other thing.** So: not said, in a room, at half past ten, on the last Friday of week 30, and correct, and I have not written it down. That is the fourth time this term I have declined to put a true sentence in a document and the third time this term I have declined to put it in a document with anybody's name on it. **Somebody will read this entry in about four years and think I am building something. I am not building anything. I am refusing to hand over a person, and the person is a member of staff of twenty-two years who said out loud in a room of four hundred and six that the fourth beat is the safest piece of engineering he has ever been taught, and he is right, and I could have corrected him, and correcting him would have ended his career, and so I have said nothing for a term and I will go on saying nothing and that silence is now the most expensive thing I own and it is not going to get any cheaper.**

*And the eleven.* **Grade three, two days, the release rate, a card in date or an exception recorded and renewed.** Nine of the eleven on the rota and four not, and one of the four is a woman of about twenty-nine who is frightened of getting something wrong at work and who came to a hall because her employer paid for her, and the man building a talk about her is right that she is the person the talk is for. **I have put eleven people a week next to a woman of twenty-nine and I have written it in this book, and the two halves of that sentence are in different buildings about eleven miles apart, and I do not know her name and she does not know mine and that is the correct state of it and it is also the exact shape of the thing I have been arguing against for a term, and I have got no answer.**

*And what she said about the fourth beat.* Four words. **Nothing happens.** I have had this in my own head since the Wednesday of week twenty-four and I have never said it out loud to anybody who writes things down for a living, and it took a woman four miles away about eleven seconds, and I am not going to say I do not remember that being useful, because I do remember it, and remembering it is the whole of the difficulty I have with her and it is the reason I could not ask her this in person. **And then the second half, which I had not seen, and which is worse, and which is about four months and a foreman and a report.** A man who does the safe thing for eleven seconds too long, nineteen times, becomes the careful one. The careful one does not get counted as good, because there is nowhere in this city to put good, so he gets counted as slow, and slow is what goes in a report, and four years later a district office writes that unsafe practice in this district is performed by technicians who are not in date, and it is true. **That is the mechanism of the next volume and I did not find it and I am not going to pretend I found it, and I have written it down in this book, and a book in a shop is the only place in this city where a thing like that can exist with nobody's name at the top of it, and that is either the most useful thing I own or the most dangerous, and I genuinely do not know which.**

*And the thing about the fourth condition.* **It never bit. It bit in week twenty-five on a hard standing and it does not bite in a bay four in a yard, and she has known that for four months and said nothing so that I would work it out, and the reason she said nothing is that she wanted me to be frightened in the right place by my own job, and I do not know whether that is care or a technique, and I am not going to ask her, because if it is a technique then I have to know, and if it is care then I have to keep it, and I cannot do both, and a man in a district jacket in a market last term would say I am building a case.**
