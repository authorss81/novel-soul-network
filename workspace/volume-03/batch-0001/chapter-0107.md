# Chapter 107 — The Review Date

The exchange room was on the first floor of a building in Bower Street in a room that had been a store cupboard until four years ago and still had the shelving brackets in it, and it had good bones, and the plate on the wall was the kind that takes about eleven seconds to come up and a person standing in the room to hold, and there was one chair in it and a table and a rack of about four hundred consent leaflets in a plastic box with a rubber band on it.

The woman who had it was thirty-one and her name was Ianthe Roke and she had a diary open on the table and nine sheets of paper with columns on them, and she said, before he had got the door shut, "**If you are here about the leaflets, they are new and I have not read them yet and you can have a bundle.**"

"I am not here about the leaflets."

"**Then say what you are here about in the first thirty seconds, because I have eleven things on the ward list and one of them is a man who has been here eleven days and has been the fourth one in this room every day of it, and nobody has asked him why he is allowed to.**"

"I am here about the room."

"Say more than the word *room.*"

"**I am here because somebody told me in a corridor last week that there was not anybody whose job it is, and I am not going to tell you who, and I have now had that answer twice from two people and I did not believe it until I sat in this room.**"

---

She put the pen down.

"**That is fair, and I said it in about eleven words, and I have been thinking about it since, and I would like to say the next eleven before you say anything.**"

"Go on."

"**There is no post in this city whose job it is to make the phone call that a care-link exists to make. I have looked. I looked when the post was not there either, and I have asked two people above me, and one of them said it is not a thing we do and the other one said it is a thing we do, and both of them were being honest about what is written down.**" She turned the diary round so that he could see it, and then took her hand off it, which was a decision rather than an accident. "**A care-link is a narrow share made when a person cannot answer. It has a visible reason, a limited duration and a later review. That is the whole design. There are no three others.**"

"And the review is a phone call."

"**The review is a phone call.**"

"How many open at once."

"**Eleven.**"

"And how many reviews are in the last four months."

"**Four.**" She said it levelly. "**And I want to be exact, because you are a technician and you are going to do arithmetic. Four of the eleven have gone past their review date. One of them went past it in the middle of last term. One went past it in the week of the twenty-fourth. Two went past it before the summer and I inherited them, and one of those two is four months past, and it is not his, and I found it on the back of a form in four seconds and I have not rung anybody and I am not going to ring anybody today.**"

"Then four of eleven."

"**Four of eleven, and it is not a rate, and if you tell anybody it is a rate I will not have given you the number, because there is no sample. There is a room with eleven things in it and a girl of twenty-nine who started in the middle of last term.**"

---

"I am not here about that," Marek Senn said. "I am here about the eleventh."

Ianthe Roke did not ask which one the eleventh was. She said, "**The man who lifts people out of beds.**"

"How do you know that is him."

"**Because he is fifty-one and he has been here eleven days and he cannot stop telling anybody who asks him what he does, and he does not know that it is the first thing anybody asks a person about in here.**"

Roy Amiss was in a bed by a window on the second floor with a frame on the left leg and a catheter bag on a hook and a chart on the end of the bed that Marek Senn did not read, because that is not a thing a man in a lanyard reads in a ward.

He was pleased to see somebody from outside. That was the first thing and it was a completely ordinary thing and it took Marek Senn four seconds to understand that a man of fifty-one who has been in a bed for eleven days is pleased to be spoken to by anybody whose job is not to bring him a pillow.

"You are the fourth one," said Roy Amiss.

"I am sorry?"

"**I have been the fourth one in about six rooms a week for six years and I have had a card for six of them and I have been on nine courses and I have never once been asked where I learned the step.**" He said it with some satisfaction. "**And I have been in this bed for eleven days and I have been the fourth one in this room every day, and nobody has asked me anything about it.**"

"What do you mean, the fourth one."

"**The one they wait for.**" He shifted about ninety degrees and did not appear to enjoy it. "**In the ward. On the plate. There is a share thing on that wall and if the room is doing something and the staff are not sure, they put the room into a hold and it sits there, and it sits there until somebody with a card comes and says the word.**"

"Who has the card in here."

"**Me.**" Roy Amiss said it without any irony at all. "**They found out on the second day that I was the only person on this ward with one and they have been using me since about the Wednesday, and I have done it about forty times, and I have got quite good at it, and I would like to be able to tell you that I do not like doing it and I cannot tell you that, because it is the single most useful thing anybody in this building has asked me to do since I came in.**"

---

"The share on this wall," said Ianthe Roke, from the doorway, "was not put there for him."

"I know."

"**You do not know, and I am going to tell you anyway, and then you are going to do the thing you have come here to do.**"

She put the chair down the other way and sat on it.

"**On the Thursday of last week a man of about forty-four came round the wards with a clipboard. District practice assurance. Eleven rooms in this city are in their sample this term and this is one of them, and he is allowed to ask what we do in this room, and he asked me what I do, and I said I run about eleven narrow shares and I keep a diary and I make about nine phone calls in a term.**"

"And he asked for the list."

"**He asked whether the shares are recorded. And I said yes, they are recorded, and there is a record, and it is not for him, and he said that is fine and would not press it, and he was not offended, and he thanked me, and he left.**" She stopped. "**And on the Friday I looked in the tray, because that is what you do, and there was a form in it. It is a one-page form. It is headed something like room, number of narrow shares in the last four months, and there is a line that says patient identifiers, if available.**"

Marek Senn did not say anything.

"**I have not filled it in,**" said Ianthe Roke.

"Good."

"**I have not filled it in and I have not torn it up, because it is in a tray with about nine other things in it and four of the nine are mine and one of the four is about a man who cannot lift his own leg, and if I tear up a form that came round the wards then in about four weeks there will be another form round the wards and it will have fewer words on it, and eventually somebody will write an audit that works.**"

"So the room is on a list and the list has a line on it for names."

"**The room is on a list. The list has eleven rooms. I have got a form with a box on it that says patient identifiers, if available, and if available is a very interesting two words, because it means the person who wrote it knew that sometimes they are not, and did not write that down.**"

"Roy Amiss is in this room on a share."

"**Roy Amiss is in this room on a share and he is the only one of the eleven who is named on the sheet he filled in on the second day, which is the admission sheet, and the admission sheet is not a care-link. The care-link is a different thing and the care-link is a phone call that nobody has made.**"

---

"He wants to see it," said Ianthe Roke. "He is standing outside the door and I have known he was going to say that within a minute of him standing there, so I am going to say the answer first, and then you can decide what you want to do with your face."

"Go on."

"**No.**"

"He is entitled to see his own—"

"**He is entitled to a great deal and I have got about nine things on the ward list, and I will tell you exactly what he is entitled to. He is entitled to be told that a share is in place, what it is for, how long it goes on for and who can stop it. He is entitled to a review, which is a phone call. He is not entitled to the record, and neither are you, and the reason is not confidentiality, it is that the record is about a person, and a record about a person that has been read by a third party is a thing that can be referred to in about four years as a thing that was read by a third party, and he will be in it for the rest of his life and I will be in it for the rest of my employment.**"

Marek Senn did not say no and did not say yes.

"That is right," he said.

"**Thank you.**"

"**And I want to be straight with you, because I have spent a term telling people things and then not telling them the other half, and that is the reason I have not got the fourth beat back.** I cannot tell you why I asked."

"**I did not think you were going to.**" She picked the diary up. "**You asked because a thing went wrong somewhere and you have a hole in your head about a fourth beat and you have decided that a care-link is where the next one is going to be. And I will tell you the thing nobody tells you, which is that it is not, and it is not because care-links are good. It is not because a care-link is a copy of a bad thing. It is because a care-link is the only piece of paper in this city that is built out of a date and a phone call, and both of those are made by a person, and neither of them is stored anywhere, and so it rots from both ends at once and the rotting is invisible, and in about six weeks the only trace that a man of fifty-one was ever connected to anything in this room is that somebody remembered to ring him.**"

"What happens if you ring him and he says no."

"**Then I take it off, and it comes off in four seconds, and the room goes back to what it was, and I write one line in the diary with the date on it and nobody ever reads it.**"

"And if you do not ring."

"**Then nothing happens,**" said Ianthe Roke, "**which is what always happens, and it is the reason this is on the form and not on the front of the ward round.**"

---

He stood in the corridor outside for four minutes afterwards and then went back in, because he had a question and it was the only one he had come for.

"Mr Amiss. When you came in. How did they decide it was you?"

"**I was on the plate.**" Roy Amiss had got a cup of tea and was holding it with both hands. "**They brought me in on the Thursday and I was not answering anybody and there was nobody in the room with me because there is nobody in the room with me, which is a thing I have been saying for about nine years, and then on the Friday a woman came with a form and asked me to sign something about emergency connection and I said what to and she said it is so that if you cannot answer somebody can do it for you, and I said who, and she said the company.**"

"Which company."

"**Mine.**" He said it in the tone of a man who has been surprised twice this year by the same fact. "**It is in my contract. I have been an engineer at the same firm for nine years and I have never read it and I signed it in nine minutes, and it turns out it says that in an emergency the firm may connect to me, and I have thought about that every day since and I have got two things.**"

"What are they."

"**One. I did not know it was in there, so nobody in that room could have said I agreed to it. Two. I would have agreed to it if anybody had asked me.**" He put the cup down on the trolley. "**And that is the part I have not been able to get past for eleven days, and I have got a frame on my leg and I have been doing a great deal of thinking.**"

"Then what is the part you cannot get past."

"**That I would have said yes, and there was no way for anybody to find out, and the woman who signed me in did not lie to me, and she had a form, and the form was correct.**" Roy Amiss looked out of the window. "**I asked for who do I ring in a room over a market about four weeks ago. You were there. I had a card in my hand and I had been carrying it about for six years and I put it in a tin because I did not know what else to do with a question like that at ten past one in the afternoon.**"

"I remember the question."

"**I have not got the answer and I have stopped expecting it and I have got something better, which is that it is not one person. It is a form and a room and a firm and a girl with a diary and about nine days.**" He picked the cup up again. "**And I am going to be straight with you, young man. If that had been you with the plate I would have been the fourth one for you, and I would not have minded, and you would not have asked me a single thing.**"

---

Marek Senn said goodbye and went down two flights and out into Bower Street and stood on the pavement for a minute and a half, and he did not go back up.

He had five things in a drawer under a bench. Four of them were other people's sentences. The fifth was about two hundred people and a woman of thirty-nine who had read the original six times and had a page of her own that she had given to nobody.

He did not add a sixth, and he wrote down on the back of a docket, in his own hand, with a date on it, the only two lines he was going to allow himself to write about that afternoon.

*Ianthe Roke, 31, was right.*

And then, because he had begun a list and had nine minutes before his bus, he added underneath it, in the same hand, in the same ink:

*She asked me why and I did not say. Fifth. Do not make it six.*

---

*110. Tuesday of week 32, half past one on a pavement in Bower Street, and this entry is about a man of fifty-one in a bed with a frame on his leg who found out that the answer to a question he asked in a market about a fortnight ago is that there is no single person, and about a woman of thirty-one with a diary who said no to me before I asked, and got both halves right, and about a form with a box on it that says patient identifiers, if available.*

*The room.* A cupboard with shelving brackets in it and good bones, a plate, a chair, a table, and about four hundred consent leaflets in a plastic box with a rubber band on it. **Eleven open shares and four past their review date, and I am not going to call that a rate and I said so out loud to a woman I had known for nine minutes, because there is a room with a girl of twenty-nine in it who started in the middle of last term and there is no sample and a number without a sample is a complaint.** One of the four dates was the middle of last term, one was the week of the twenty-fourth, and two were inherited from before the summer, and **one of those two is four months past, and I have put that number in this book and I have not put the name on it, and the name is the thing I am not going to do today.** **And the answer to a care-link, said by a woman of thirty-one in about nine words: it is the only piece of paper in this city built out of a date and a phone call, and neither of those is stored anywhere, and so it rots from both ends at once and the rotting is invisible, and in about six weeks the only trace that a man of fifty-one was ever connected to anything in that room is that somebody remembered to ring him.** That is the third time this term somebody has handed me the whole of a thing in nine words and I have been the fourth thing in the room each time and I have not been in the room in any of the other two.

*And the man.* **Fifty-one, nine years with one firm, installed and maintained lifting equipment, and the fourth one in about six rooms a week for six years, and a card for six of them and nine courses and never once asked.** He told me in a bed, with some satisfaction, that he has been the fourth one in this ward every day for eleven days and is the only person on the ward with a card, and that he cannot tell me he does not like doing it, because it is the most useful thing anybody in the building has asked him to do since he came in. **And the second half. He signed a form on the Friday that says his firm may connect to him in an emergency, in nine minutes, without reading it, and he did not know it was in his contract, so nobody in that room could have said he agreed to it — and he would have agreed to it if anybody had asked him, and that is the part he has been stuck on for eleven days, and it took him about nine seconds to say it and it is the truest sentence anybody has said to me this term.** He asked his question again, in front of me, and then answered it himself: **it is not one person, it is a form and a room and a firm and a girl with a diary and about nine days, and if it had been you with the plate I would have been the fourth one for you and you would not have asked me a single thing.** He is not a hinge and he is not a victim and he is the best critic of the case I have met and he is lying in a bed and I am not going to be able to do one single thing about any part of it.

*And the form.* A one-page district practice assurance form in a tray with nine other things in it, headed room, number of narrow shares in the last four months, and a line that says **patient identifiers, if available.** **I am not going to write down what I think that is going to be for, and I did not ask, and I want it recorded that I did not ask, because a man of forty-four in a yard told me last week that a line in a document can be true and still be the worst sentence anybody in the city has read, and this one is in a box on a form, and it is already the worst sentence anybody in the city has read.** The line *if available* means the person who wrote it knew that sometimes they are not, and did not put that down, and I have now met three people in two days who have all done that on purpose and not one of them is lying.

*And the refusal, and what it cost.* **She said no to me before I asked and gave the mechanism in about nine words and she was right on both halves: he is entitled to know that a share is in place and to a review, and he is not entitled to the record, and neither am I, and a record about a person that has been read by a third party is a thing that can be referred to in about four years as a thing that was read by a third party.** And then she gave me the second half, unasked: **she asked me why and I did not say, and she said she did not think I was going to, and that she worked out in about nine seconds that I was here because I have a hole in my head about a fourth beat and I have decided a care-link is where the next one will be, and that it is not, and that it is not because care-links are good.** Five times now. I have said nothing five times to a person who was owed something, and the cost of the fifth is in the last two lines of this entry and I have written it down and I am not going to put a number on it.
