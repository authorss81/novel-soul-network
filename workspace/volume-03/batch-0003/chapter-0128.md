# Chapter 128 — Who Is It Waiting For

The Exchange meets on the first Wednesday of the month at about eleven at night in a back room with the shutters up, and there were nine people in it, and there is a tin on the table, and the tin is not locked, and a four-column book is on the table with forty-three lines in it, and the fortieth line is three years old.

Bea Nunn is sixty-one and she has not run this room for nine years because anybody asked her to.

"Two minutes," she said. "**I am not doing the two minutes this month and I have told the room why and the room did not like it.**"

---

The reason is that there is a new thing in this city and the room has been talking about it for a fortnight, and Bea Nunn does not run a room in which the only business is the thing everybody came in with.

"There is a leaflet," she said, "**and there is a notice, and there is a card on about nine walls, and there is a woman of thirty-eight in a clinic who took a share off a woman of sixty-eight in a bay on the Monday, and every one of you in this room has read about at least two of those things. I am sixty-one and I have run this room for nine years and I have never once had to say that, and the reason I am saying it is that a room that only hears the thing people came in with becomes the thing people came in with.**"

Somebody at the back said that was not what the room was for.

"No," said Bea Nunn. "**It is exactly what the room is for and that is why I am not doing the two minutes, because a first-year of nineteen has been saying the same sentence in this room seven times for about nine weeks and she is going to say it in four minutes and I would like the room to have heard something else in the room first.**"

---

Asha Reed is nineteen and she has been coming to this room since she was fifteen and she has been on a first-year common room's rota since about week nine, and she said the sentence for the seventh time and it was the same seven words it has been every time.

"**The case is Senn's and the technique is nobody's yet.**"

That is the whole of it. That is what she buys with five pounds out of a tin, and the rule of the room is that it is said in the first person and unchanged, and she has never once changed it and she has never once been asked to.

Then she said the second half, which is not part of it and which she has said twice before and which is about four words long.

"**It is the seventh time and it is now the only sentence in this city that is wrong, and I am going to say it anyway, and I would like somebody to tell me in about four years what I should have said instead.**"

---

The woman of thirty-four with a hip was in the room. She came on her own time and she brought four lines on a piece of paper that is not a form, and she has not been in this room since the spring, and four people in it knew her by a bay number.

"I am not here about the card," she said, when her four minutes came, "**and I have got about four things and none of them is a practice and I am going to say them anyway because the rule says four minutes and I have not had four minutes since the spring.**"

"**Go on.**"

"One. I am all right. Everybody keeps asking me if I am all right and I am, and I would like it written down that I am, because in about four weeks somebody is going to write something that says I was a case study." She looked at the ceiling. "**Two. There are about nine hundred of those leaflets in this district and I am not on any of them, and I have been trying for about a fortnight to work out why not, and I have got a reason, and the reason is that nobody could find me.**"

Nobody said anything.

"So I am safe," she said. "**That is the whole of what I came to say. I am safe because there is no name on me anywhere, and the reason there is no name on me anywhere is that in the spring a woman of thirty-four filled in a box on a form on my behalf and nobody asked her to, and that is the single most useful thing anybody has ever done for me and she was disciplined for it, or nearly, and I have never been able to work out how to say thank you for it to a person who is still working.**"

---

The item that took the book from forty-three lines to forty-four came in the fourth four minutes, and the woman who brought it had a form with nine fields on it in a plastic wallet and her own name in the first box.

"**Adaeze Nwosu, thirty-eight, eleven years in a clinic, four narrow shares in eleven years and asked about none of them, and I took one off on Monday at seven minutes past ten because a card said to and I told the woman holding the diary while it was still on.**"

Bea Nunn said: "**The rule is the caller's words, in the first person, and a signature at the end. I am not going to write it down for you and call it yours.**"

"I have got both," said Adaeze Nwosu. "**I have written it. I have read it back. It is on the back of the form, and the form is in the wallet, and I have brought the form.**"

"**Then read it.**"

She read it. It took forty seconds and it is worth doing properly because the sentence with the hole in it is the whole of this chapter and it belongs to a woman of thirty-eight.

"**I have been the one holding it since Wednesday. There is no word in it and there is never going to be a word in it, and the line on the card said if the word does not come, withdraw and report, and the word did not come, and I withdrew it, and it came off in four seconds, and a member of staff in this building found her on her feet eleven minutes later and she was not hurt and she was looking for a door.**"

And then:

"**And I did not tell her daughter and I did not ring her daughter and I did not have a reason and I did not need a reason, and the person who should have rung the daughter is a woman of thirty-one with a diary in a building in Bower Street and I rang her at half past ten and she said it was hers and she would do it, and she did not do it until four o'clock, and the daughter was told at about eleven at night by a man at her work, and there is no field on nine forms for that.**"

---

"**There is a name missing out of that,**" said Bea Nunn.

"**Yes.**"

"**Say whose.**"

"**No,**" said Adaeze Nwosu.

---

It went in at eleven minutes to midnight, and it took four minutes to write, and Bea Nunn wrote it with a hole in the middle of it where a woman of sixty-eight in bay four of a clinic in Crown Terrace should have been, and the hole is still there and it is on the page and it is going to be there in four years.

"I am putting it in with the hole," she said, while she was writing it. "The rule is that the caller's words go in, in the first person, and the caller's words have a hole in them, and it is not mine to fill it and it is not mine to refuse it over. I have refused to enter a line twice in nine years and both times I was wrong and one of those times it was about my own book." She put the pen down. "**And it is my name in the fourth column and I would like everybody to notice that, because in about four years somebody is going to read line forty-four and want to know what the room thought about a bay in Crown Terrace, and it is going to be me, and I did not think about it and I would have said no if anybody had let me.**"

---

And then, at ten to midnight, with one four-minute block left and nobody in it, Bea Nunn put her pen down and asked a question in a room, and four people in this city have ever heard it asked.

She said it to Asha Reed and she said it out loud and she did not frame it.

"**Who is it waiting for.**"

The room did not go quiet. Asha Reed looked at her for four seconds.

"Nobody," said Asha Reed. "**That is the answer and it has taken seven meetings and a year and four people saying things in corridors to get to, and it is nobody, and that is why the ones with a name in them are the dangerous ones and the ones with nobody in them are the ones that get a leaflet.**"

"**Then how does anybody tell the two apart.**"

"They don't," said Asha Reed. "**You cannot. A fifth line is a shape and a shape does not say who cut it, and I said that in a market office in the Saltmarket a fortnight ago to a market clerk of thirty-one and she agreed with me inside four seconds and I have not been able to stop thinking about the fact that a woman who sells fish got there before I did.**"

"And that is the whole of the finding."

"**No. That is the half of it. The other half is that the question you have just asked is the only one that works, and it only works at one place, and there is no form in this city that gets taken to a load.**"

---

Marek Senn was at the back wall for about four hours and did not speak once.

He was not asked for an item and he did not have one, and Bea Nunn told him that at half past eleven, in about nine words, in front of the room, and he said that was correct, and she said she had said it in front of the room on purpose.

He left at ten to midnight. Asha Reed walked out to the corridor with him, which she had done once in a term, and she said:

"**You did not say anything for four hours.**"

"**I had nothing to say that was better than what nine people said.**"

"That is not why." She had her coat over her arm. "**You had a thing in your pocket on Monday. I could see it from the back of the room on the second item because you have stopped putting your hand flat on your leg, and you do that when you have got something in the pocket you are not going to be asked about, and you did not put your hand flat once in four hours, and that means you have got a piece of card and you have had it since Friday.**"

He did not answer that for four seconds.

"**Yes,**" he said.

"Good," said Asha Reed. "Because I want to say one thing to you as a first-year and then I am going home. Seven times I have said the case is yours and the technique is nobody's, and that sentence is the only reason anybody in this city can tell the difference between you and a leaflet, and on Monday a woman of thirty-eight used a piece of paper to take a share off a woman of sixty-eight in a bay, and she was right, and it was a card, and a card is not a name." She put her coat on. "**You are going to have to say the sentence out loud to nine people in six days who write things down, and if you say it in a room it is a fact about a man with a lanyard, and if you do not say it then the sentence is still yours and the technique is nobody's, and in about four years that will be the only sentence anybody in this city can make themselves say. I do not know which of those two is better. Nobody does. That is what a first-year is for.**"
