# Chapter 124 — Six Places

There are about six thousand copies a month of a one-page district circular in this city, and it goes out with the monthly return to about nine depots, and a copy goes on a board in an office in Crown Terrace, and in the middle of last term a clerk of twenty-six posted one to a trade paper because the paper asked her to, and the paper printed it, and neither of them had to be asked twice.

The one that went out on the Thursday of week thirty-five was called **UNAUTHORISED PRACTICE MATERIAL — SITE ADVICE** and it was one page and it had a reference on it and no date on it, because the system that prints it has a field for a reference and a field for a date and the date field is not on page one.

It said four things, and all four of them were true, and the third one is the reason eleven people in this city have been named in a document that does not name them.

*Material in circulation which is not issued by a company or a training provider has been identified at a customer site in Nine Bridges. The material carries four steps and an additional handwritten instruction. The instruction is not part of the practice as taught by field staff. Members of the public who hold unissued material should not rely on it for any decision in the course of work and should refer the question to their supervisor. The matter has been referred to district practice assurance and is scheduled.*

And at the bottom, in a box, in a size of type about two millimetres smaller than the rest of it, because that is how those systems print a field that was added later:

*Group: [the name]*

---

Nobody chose the name in the box.

It was chosen on the Wednesday evening by a night shift supervisor of forty-six with nine people on nights, because a report on a company's system has nine fields and the two at the bottom are drop-downs with no free text, and *other* sits in a free field underneath, and a free field is not a source.

And it was printed on the Thursday by a clerk of twenty-six who typed it into a template that had the group box in it already, in a size of type about two millimetres smaller, because the template had come that way and templates do not arrive by themselves.

And the site is the only thing on the page that is not a general advice, and it is there because a supervisor filling in nine fields at ten past five on a Wednesday afternoon puts the field that is the shortest and cannot be argued with.

---

The bundle has been in six places since the Wednesday morning of week twenty-one.

It is four sheets, stapled at the top left with packet staples, no letter, no name, no address, no date, no stamp. A training register of about two hundred names with a column somebody has put a pen through and written CARD down thirty-one times. A six-week rota with four technicians and, beside the newest name, in the same capitals, **NOT CARDED. A CARD IS A CONDITION OF A CALLOUT. ASK HIM WHY HE IS NOT CARDED.** Eleven pages, nine of them occupational-health notes with *any condition, injury or medication affecting your ability to work at height* typed in as NONE, a name and a site. One of the remaining two is Sondra Mbeki's line and says nothing about a condition at all. The last of the eleven is an induction record for a boy of nineteen. And on the back of the last sheet, four printed steps, all four of them correct, and a fifth line in ballpoint in a hand that got impatient with the pen and pushed the last full stop through the paper:

*Five. If the word does not come, do it anyway. The load is yours.*

Six places is what happened to it and it happened fast: a back room, a car park, an inbox, a photocopy at a trade counter, a company, and about nine hundred postboxes when a company recalled a bundle on four facts. **Seven is what it is now, and the seventh place is a trade paper, and the seventh place is the only one of the seven that cannot be un-done by anybody.**

---

Because the circular names the site.

Not the eleven. Not a name. A customer site in Nine Bridges, which is where the card was found, which is the whole reason the circular exists, and which is on about two hundred lines of a rota in a bundle that has been in six places for fourteen weeks.

**Eleven people worked that site in the six weeks of the rota. Nine of the eleven have a health note with NONE in it and one of them has a line that says she has no card and one of them is a boy of nineteen who did not exist when the rota was printed. The circular does not know that. The circular has never seen the rota. The circular and the rota are two pieces of paper about a place, and eleven people can be put together out of them in nine seconds by anybody who has both, and there are about four thousand people in this city who could be sent a copy of each.**

Nobody sent anybody a copy of each. That is the finding and it took a week and it is the whole of this chapter.

---

Ellis Marsh is thirty-one and he works nights at that site and he has a hand, and in the middle of last term a woman of thirty-eight who wanted a sentence to write down read fifteen boxes of a company form out loud down a telephone to a man of thirty-one in a van, and Ellis Marsh put NONE in the box about his hand because there was a box and because the question was *any condition, injury or medication* and the question was not a good one.

**They put the word NONE in a box about a hand. And nobody has ever asked me about my hand.**

He did not want the sentence written down and he signed the note on the Wednesday and rang the company back because a woman named Rae Tolley was not going to, and he needed the hours.

He was in a canteen at half past eight on the Thursday morning of week thirty-five, and the circular was on the board, and a man of forty-one he does not work with said, in front of four people, in the tone of a man making conversation:

"**Did you bring that in.**"

"**No.**" Ellis Marsh said it and then had to say the rest of it, and the rest of it took him eleven seconds, and the eleven seconds are because of the stutter and have been since he was nine.

"**I— I have never— I have not seen one. I have not been— I have not been in that yard with a card. I have been on nights for six years and I did not— I did not know there was a customer site on that page.**"

He had not been asked to be defensive and he was not being accused and he did nine seconds of a man's whole afternoon in a canteen because a piece of paper had a place on it and he was one of eleven people who worked in that place.

---

The person who found him in the canteen was Marek Senn, who had come in for a meter reading at nine and who stood about eleven feet away with a job sheet and did not say anything, which was the correct thing and cost him four seconds.

Then Ellis Marsh said the thing that has been waiting for him since the middle of last term, which is that there is a form somewhere with the word NONE in a box about his hand, and that he rang the company and they asked him to sign it, and that he did, and that the reason he rang back was that somebody else had told him not to ring back.

"**Somebody told you not to ring back.**"

"**A woman. Rae. She is not a manager. She said if nobody rings you then it is not a complaint, it is just— it is a box.**" He turned his coffee cup. "And I rang. And I needed the hours."

"**I know. I was there.**"

"You were on the telephone." Ellis Marsh looked at him for a second. "**Then you are the one who knows about the box.**"

"**I know about the box and I did not write your sentence and I was refused when I offered, in under a second, by somebody who was right.**"

"**Why.**"

"**Because a document is only ever a receipt, and a receipt is what you show a company that has stopped answering its telephone, and the second your hand is in writing with my hand on it, there is a document about your hand with my handwriting in it, and in about four years I am the fourth thing in a room and that room will be about your hand.**"

Ellis Marsh said nothing for four seconds.

"That is a good answer," he said, "**and I am not going to be able to use it at eleven o'clock tonight in a canteen when one of the four men who was in this room about that page finds out I have a hand.**"

---

Sondra Mbeki rang the shop at eleven in the morning and asked two questions and was not angry about either of them, because she has not been angry since the Thursday of week nineteen and there is no reason on earth for her to start now.

She is thirty-nine and she has six years on a route and a hand she cannot make a fist with, and there is a note on a file somewhere saying she has not got a card, and that note is now in a document about two hundred people.

"**Is the site on the page the one on the yard,**" she said.

"**Yes.**"

"**And there were eleven of us on that rota.**"

"**There were eleven pages with a name and a site on them and the names are not on the circular, and eleven people worked that yard in those six weeks, and I am not going to sit here and tell you those are the same eleven, because I have not checked, and nobody has, and the two documents are in two buildings and no document joins them.**"

"Then somebody will join them," said Sondra Mbeki. "**In about four days. Not a person. Somebody will just put the two pages next to each other because that is what people do, and then it will be joined, and neither of them will be wrong.**"

---

And then she said the sentence that is the turn of this whole fortnight, and she said it in the tone of a woman who has been thinking about it since eight in the morning.

"**Do you know what made us news.**"

"No."

"You did." She let four seconds go by. "**Six weeks ago, twelve weeks ago, whatever it is, four of us were on a page and nobody outside about four rooms knew and a woman of thirty-eight had a conversation with a man of thirty-one in a van and a woman of thirty-one had a diary and nobody was on the front of anything. And on Thursday morning a piece of paper came out with a place on it, and a card came out of that place, and a card with no name on it is the only kind of bad news anybody in this city can use, and now we are in a trade paper, and it is not because anybody did anything. It is because there was a card.**"

"**You are saying the card did it.**"

"I am saying the card is the reason there is a story to put us in," said Sondra Mbeki. "**And the card is not ours, and the card is not the company's, and the card is four correct steps and a fifth line that tells a person to stop, and I have read it and it is a good card, and it is the best thing anybody has handed anybody this term, and I would like somebody in this city to be able to hold those two sentences at once and I have not been able to do it since half past eight this morning.**"

---

At half past two in the afternoon the telephone rang and it was Ianthe Roke, and she had rung him twice in a term and both times it had been about a room, and this time it was about a room too.

"**Do not come to my building this week.**"

"**All right.**"

"**I am going to tell you why and then I am going to ring you back on Monday as though this had not happened, and I want both of those to be true, and the second one is not a promise that everything is normal, it is a promise that a Tuesday is a Tuesday.**"

"**Go on.**"

"A district notice went out this morning with a customer site on it, and my room is a room with eleven open narrow shares in it, and I do not have a post, and there is a girl of twenty-nine with me who has a form with a box that says *room contact* in it, and the box says a room and a number and no name." She was not hurrying. "**If a technician with a lanyard comes into that building in the week a district notice names a site, then in about four years there is a document that puts a man with a lanyard in a room with eleven open narrow shares, and nobody will be able to find the document, because I do not have a post and there is no file, and the only record of that room is a diary in a drawer that I can be asked for.**"

"**I was not going to come.**"

"**I know you were not going to come. That is not why I rang, and you know that is not why I rang, and if we both pretend it was that then we are doing the thing where two people agree to have had a conversation they did not have.**"

"Then why did you ring."

"Because I am about to be a thing in a story and I wanted one person in this city to hear me say the eleven are fine before it happens." A pause. "**The eleven are fine. The plate is fine. The girl is fine. The woman with the hip is fine and she is at home. Do not come to my building this week and do not ring me between now and Monday.**"

---

He sat on a bench outside a launderette for four minutes and worked out how to write the correction, and there are four correct versions of it, and every one of them is a document that names a person.

Version one: *The material was produced by a commercial print shop in the Quiet Quarter and distributed through a hire place that does not record a name.* True, and it puts a shop and a street in a trade paper, and a shop is a person.

Version two: *The material originates from a customer of that shop, who paid in small sums through a mailbox.* True, and it puts a payer in a document, and he has been told by a woman of thirty-five in a café that the payer is somebody who could not pay forty pounds at once.

Version three: *The fifth line was written by hand on the reverse of a photocopy before printing, by a person who is not the printer.* True, and it is the only version of the four that says the thing that matters, **and it can only be written by somebody who has seen the file.**

Version four is *We are unable to comment.*

He wrote version three out in full on the back of a docket, in a hand he could read in the morning, and then he did not send it, and the reason is not in the sentence and is in the next entry.
