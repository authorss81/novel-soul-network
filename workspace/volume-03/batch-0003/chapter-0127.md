# Chapter 127 — One Fact, Unasked

Oren Vey sent a car at half past seven in the morning on the Tuesday of week thirty-six and the driver said the meeting was in the car park of a repair shop in Lattice Ward and would take eleven minutes, and Marek Senn said no, and the driver said that was the whole message.

So it was in the repair shop, with the shutter up, on a folding chair that is not good for anything, at ten past eight, with the lamp on over the bench.

He is forty-one and he is the operations director of a company that assembles, sells and employs, and he has been in this case for a term and a half and has never been the villain of it and is not the villain of it now and will not be the villain of it in about four years, and everybody in this city has been waiting to see whether he would say something that made him one, including him.

"**Four hundred and thirty-two,**" he said. "Is that the number."

"Yes."

"**Then I have got nine minutes and a taxi fare and I am going to spend them in the order I think will survive, and the first thing is that I am right about the card and wrong about the other thing, and I would like both of those in the same breath so that you cannot have the one without the other.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I am right about this. Four hundred and thirty-two cards, four correct steps, a fifth line with no name on it and no version and no company, in about nine places in one district in six weeks, and no two of them can be traced to the same decision, and they cannot be withdrawn, because a company recalls things it has issued and there is no mechanism in this city that recalls something nobody has admitted to issuing.**" He put his hands on his knees. "That is a supply problem and it is the only kind of problem I am actually good at and I have been in this case for a term and a half waiting for somebody to hand me one."

---

"**And the other thing.**"

"**And I am wrong about the other thing, and I have been wrong about it since the middle of last term, and I have been wrong about it in public twice, and I am going to be wrong about it again in a fortnight unless somebody stops me.**"

"**What is the other thing.**"

"That they are the same." He said it quickly, the way people say the thing they have rehearsed. "**The card on a fire door and the nine words on a control post are the same shape and come out of the same family and I have been saying so for a term and a half and I still believe most of it, and I am telling you that I am wrong about the part of it that matters, and I do not know what the part is.**"

"**Tell me the part anyway.**"

"**I cannot. I have got a job and I have got a taxi fare and I have got nine minutes, and if I say the part wrong on a Tuesday morning in a repair shop then in about four years somebody quotes me and I have said something on a Tuesday.**" He sat back. "But I have got a question, and it is a real one and it is the only one I have got, and I have been asked it twice this term by two people who were both talking to themselves."

"**Go on.**"

"**What is it waiting for.**"

---

Marek Senn put both hands flat on the bench, which is a thing he does and which is not a technique and which in about four years somebody might be able to read.

"No," he said. "**You do not get that one.**"

"It is a fair question."

"**It is the only question there is and I have not got it in a form yet and if I hand it to you on a Tuesday in this shop then in about four weeks you will be standing in a room saying it, and you will get it slightly wrong, and a man of thirty-one will be holding a printing press because of it, or a woman of fifty-eight, and I would rather it stayed in this room for about six days.**"

"**Six days.**"

"**Six days. There is a committee meeting in the first week of next month and I have been told about it by a man of fifty-two who will not tell me what is on it, and I am going to be in a room with nine people in it in six days and I have got nothing for them.**"

Oren Vey looked at the bench for a while.

"Then I have got something for the six days," he said, "**and I was not going to give it to you, and I am giving it to you, and I would like that recorded as the second strange thing that has happened to me in about four months.**"

---

"Page two," said Oren Vey. "**Of the six-sided thing that went out with the leaflets. About four thousand copies.**"

"Yes."

"**There is a photograph on it. A card in a plastic sleeve on a lanyard, about the size of a postage stamp, and the four steps are legible, and it is captioned *the sequence as issued*.**"

"Yes."

"**That is our photograph. We put it there. It has been in every one of those handouts since the spring, because a case study with no picture of the thing reads as a rumour, and somebody in marketing said so in four minutes.**"

He let that sit for four seconds.

"I have not seen that page in about nine months," he said, "**and it has been in four thousand pockets since the spring, and a woman of fifty-eight in the Quiet Quarter enlarged it and checked four steps against each other and got all four of them right, and I could not have told you on Friday morning and I could not have stopped it on Friday morning, and that is not a defence, that is an account of how this works, and I have been an operations director for nineteen years and I have never once been shown our own marketing.**"

---

"**Why are you telling me.**"

"Three reasons and none of them is generosity." He held up a finger. "**One. My own assurance is going to be cited in a leaflet. It is going to be held up as the thing that fixed this, and about four years from now there is going to be a training pack that is the best my firm has ever written about asking, and not one word of it is going to be about a card in a stairwell, and I have known that since the Monday of week thirty-four and I have done nothing about it and neither has anybody else.**"

"**Two.**"

"Two. We have been telling the trade since the spring that the sequence is issued on a card and on nothing else. That is true and it is in four hundred and six postboxes. And this morning a man in this room has established that it has been in about four thousand pockets as a photograph, on a page, at a size where you can read it with a loupe. So we cannot write the second letter." He put the finger down. "**And the second letter is the one that matters. The first letter cost us about nine thousand two hundred pounds and the second one is the one somebody brings a class action on, and I have got a meeting in the spring with a distributor who has been asking me a question about card numbers since the middle of the summer and I have not got an answer for him and I was not going to get one, and now I have got the opposite answer and I cannot un-get it.**"

"**That is a fact against your company.**"

"**That is an instruction against my company, and it is going to cost me about four years, and I have known about the four years since the Monday of week thirty-four and I have done nothing, and there is a difference between those two things and it is the difference between a man who knows and a man who has decided, and I have got about six weeks of the second one left in me.**"

"**And the third reason.**"

"**Third is that if I keep saying they are the same, then in about four years a company is going to be the author of a fifth line with a person's handwriting on it, because a company that has told a regulator the two are one thing has to stand behind it.**" He shrugged. "I have watched a company get convicted of a thing it did not do twice and both times it was because somebody in a corridor said the two things were related in order to be useful."

---

Marek Senn did not thank him and Oren Vey did not wait to be thanked, and the two of them sat in a repair shop with a lamp on for four minutes and neither of them said anything.

"The page," said Marek Senn. "**I would like a copy of page two.**"

"**No.**"

"**You have just told me it is our page.**"

"**I have just told you it is a controlled document of a company of about four thousand people, and the controlled copy of it is in a folder in a building on the far side of this city from your bench, and if I put a copy of it in your pocket then in about four years there is a company document in a technician's pocket, and a company document in a technician's pocket is a document about the technician's pocket and not about the page.**"

"That has happened before."

"That has happened to me four times and twice it was me who did it." He did not soften it. "**Here is what I can do and I have decided to do it before you ask, which is the first time in about four months. In six days, in a room with nine people in it, on the record, with my name and my job on it, I will describe page two, say where it appears, say how many copies, and say that a printed sheet carrying a legible copy of our sequence has been in circulation since the spring. And I will say that I found out about it from a technician in a repair shop.**"

"You do not have to do that."

"**I do not have to do that and I have known it for four seconds and I am going to, and I would like to say the reason, because you have been writing things down all term and you will write this down and I would rather it had a reason in it. The reason is that if I say it in a room, it is a fact about a document. If you say it in a room, it is a fact about a man with a lanyard who has been in nine hundred rooms this term.**" He stood up. "I have watched you lose about nine things this month by being the person who says true sentences in rooms. I am not going to be the eleventh."

---

At the shutter he stopped and said one more thing, and he said it without turning round.

"**You are going to ask me who pays a man in a district jacket to say it again. I am not going to answer it and I am telling you now, before you ask, that I know about it, and that I have not asked him myself, and that a man who is not asking a question has a much better position than a man who has asked one.**"

"Nobody has said a word to me about a district jacket since the Thursday of week thirty-three."

"Then nobody has told you what I am telling you," said Oren Vey, "**and I have just told you, and it is the third thing I have said in a repair shop this morning that I have not got a reason for, and I am going out to a car and I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to wait to see whether you use any of it.**"

He got into the car and it went, and the driver had waited with the engine running for twenty minutes.

Bryn Maw telephoned the shop at eleven in the morning, at four minutes past, from a hut on the Meridian corridor with a kettle in it and a rack of leaflets for a charity giving away bread, and he is fifty-eight and he has been at that gate for thirty-one years.

"I am not ringing about the card," he said. "**I am ringing because a man came to the gate on the Saturday and read the nine words out to nine of my men again, the same way as in the summer, and I did not take it down, and I have still not taken it down, and he has been asked whether he would come and say it in a room and he has not said no.**"

"**Asked by whom.**"

"**He did not say and I did not ask and I have been thinking about that for three days and I have worked out that I did not ask because I have been in this industry for thirty-one years and you do not ask a man that in a hut.**" A pause. "He is a good bloke. He came in the summer when nobody wanted to be here and he read nine words out loud to nine men and he was right and it was the only true thing anybody said on that site for a term."

"What do you want me to do."

"Nothing," said Bryn Maw. "I have got a tray in that prefab office that goes in about ten weeks and a box number on page nine that I have not written down and have not been asked to write down, and I have got nine men and a Saturday that somebody is going to want me to do." He put the phone down, and then he said, into it, one more thing: "**And I have got a fifth line of my own in a site diary in pencil and it is the same as the one on the card, and I wrote it in the summer, and I have never met the man who wrote the one on the card, and I am fifty-eight, and I do not think I am going to.**"
