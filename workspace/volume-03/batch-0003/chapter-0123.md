# Chapter 123 — Not Mine

A block of flats on a street with a launderette in it in the Quiet Quarter has four hundred and nine of the sort of card that nobody in the building's management is going to see, and on the Wednesday afternoon of week thirty-five there were two of them wedged behind the fire door on the first landing, and one of them had been there since about the middle of last week.

A man of thirty-three who works in the building opposite and who has not been named in this book and is not going to be came up the stairs at four o'clock with nine cards in his coat, and the ninth one he put behind the fire door, and it was the first time anybody had put one there on that landing, and he did it because it is the landing people actually use.

A woman of thirty-nine came down at ten past four with a washing bag over her shoulder and stopped, and read it, which took her eleven seconds, and Marek Senn, who had been in the stairwell for twenty minutes and had not come up, came up.

---

"**Do not do that,**" she said.

"**I was not going to.**"

"**You were stood in a stairwell for twenty minutes and you came up the moment somebody read one.**"

"**That is fair.**"

"**It is not fair, it is a description, and I have read a card before and I did not have a man come up a flight of stairs about it.**"

The card was behind the fire door and it had four printed steps on the front and a fifth line on the back in a hand that got smaller towards the end, and it said: *If the word does not come, withdraw and report.*

She read it twice, the second time with her thumb holding the corner, which is what people do to a thing they are going to be quoted on.

"Where did you get it," she said. "**And do not say a man, I have met four men this week and none of them was you.**"

---

Marek Senn said the true thing, because it was the fourth time in a fortnight and he had stopped being able to see the shape of doing it.

"**I got it from a woman of thirty-five in a café on Tuesday who put nine of them on a table and told me she had seen four on a bench nine days earlier and cannot get any two of them to the same person. The four steps are the company's four steps. The fifth line is in a hand that is not the company's and not a printer's. I have not got a name, I have not got a place, and I have got a woman in a market office holding eleven of them in a box that is a fortnight old, and I can prove that one of those eleven is a copy of a copy, and that is where I have got to in nine days.**"

She looked at him for a moment and then she said:

"**Not mine.**"

"**I know.**"

"You do not know." She put the washing bag down on the step. "**That is the fourth one. A man of twenty-six asked me on Monday, and he was very polite and he had a printout, and he wanted to know if it was one of ours. And now you.**"

"**One of whose.**"

"Of anybody." She picked the washing bag up again. "**And I have got a thing to say to you and I have not said it to anybody and I am going to say it to you because you are the fourth one and because you have not asked me a question yet, which the other three did inside eleven seconds.**"

---

"It is not mine," she said, "**and that is the first sentence in about four years that anybody who is frightened would accept from a person, and it is no use at all, and I would like both of those on the record at the same time.**"

"**Go on.**"

"Six years, in and out of three sites. I am on a rota that a company prints and I am on a line in a bundle that somebody posted through a letterbox in a room of nine people, and there is a box beside my name in it with the word NONE typed into it, and I have read six copies and I have never given that page to anybody and I am never going to." She was not angry and she was not performing and she did not stop to find the words. "**I have been asked twice this term by people who were being kind to me whether I knew who put it out, and both of them wanted me to say a name so that they could write it down and take it somewhere, and both of them were disappointed when I said I did not know, and neither of them has done it since, and that is the correct outcome and I want it noted that it is correct.**"

"And the card."

"**The card is the fourth thing that has been put in front of me in a fortnight that says I do not need anybody, and the fourth thing is the only one of the four that has been printed.**"

She said that last part carefully, as though she had heard herself about to say it and had checked it twice.

"**The leaflet on the levy note. The letterbox bundle. A man in a district fleece with a printout. And a card on a fire door.** And I have said no to all four and I am going to keep saying no, and the reason I am saying it to you in a stairwell instead of going up to a woman of thirty-one in a room is that the woman of thirty-one in a room is doing a job and I am not going to make her do a second one on a Tuesday."

---

Marek Senn looked at the card behind the fire door for a while and then he said:

"**There is no name in it.**"

"**No.**"

"**There is nothing on it that says who wrote the fifth line, and nothing that says who printed it, and there is no key-holder in it anywhere, and if the word does not come then the instruction is to withdraw and report, and there is nobody in that sentence for anybody to wait for.**"

"Yes," she said. "**That is what I have been saying to four people all week and one of them was you and you have only just got there at four o'clock on a Wednesday in a stairwell.**"

"**I know.**"

"**Then you know that I have been telling the truth for eleven days and it has not once made anybody put the card back.**"

---

And that was the whole of the conversation, and it lasted nine minutes, and the ninth refusal of a fortnight happened at the bottom of a flight of stairs six minutes in, when he said, without deciding to:

"**Can I see the page of your own.**"

"**No.**"

"**You have not got to answer that.**"

"I know I have not got to answer it, which is why I have answered it." She shifted the washing bag. "**You are the second person this term to ask me that and the first one was a woman of thirty-five and she asked it in about nine words and she did not want it either. And I am telling you both of you the same thing and it is one sentence: the page of my own is the only thing I have got that is mine, and if two people have asked for it then three will, and a thing that is asked for three times is a thing that has been in a file.**"

"**I am not going to put it anywhere.**"

"**I did not say you were going to. I said the other two will.**"

---

The man of thirty-three came up the stairs at half past four with an empty coat and found them there, and he was not pleased, and he was also not the villain of anything, which is worth saying at the top of this section because he has a face for it.

"I have put nine out," he said. "**Four at the stall, four at the letting agent, and one behind that fire door, and I am not paid by anybody and I do not know who printed them, and I have had one in my kitchen since about the middle of last week and my wife asked me on Sunday why it was in the drawer with the takeaway menus.**"

"**Have you taken it out of the drawer.**"

"No." He said it without any hesitation at all. "**Because the first person I gave one to was a woman who does four nights and has a baby in a cold flat, and she read it and looked at it for a minute, and then she said: this is the first thing anybody has given me this year that tells me I can stop.**"

---

Marek Senn stood on a landing and heard the sentence he has been carrying since the Wednesday morning of week thirty-four, which was a woman's of forty-four saying *that is what I did on Friday and it is what put me in your box*, and he heard it again from a man of thirty-three in a stairwell about two hundred yards from where she said it.

"Do you know that it is a fifth line," he said. "**Do you know that the fourth line is a balance step and the fifth is not on the company's card at all.**"

"No," said the man of thirty-three. "**And I am telling you that I know it is a card with an instruction on it and not one of ours, and I have been putting them out for nine days, and nine people have asked me where they come from, and I have said I do not know, and I am not going to start lying about it in front of two people I have never met.**"

"You are handing out an instruction that tells a person to stop," said Marek Senn. "**You are doing that in a building where nine people work nights.**"

"I know what I am doing." The man of thirty-three shifted his weight. "**And I want you to say the other half, because you have had your face on since you came up those stairs and you have not said the other half.**"

---

And Marek Senn said the other half, and he said it well, and it cost nine seconds, and it is the fourth time in a fortnight he has said the best sentence of his life into a room and the first three of them were about himself.

"**The line does not say stop. It says withdraw and report, and withdrawing from a narrow share takes four seconds, and in a wet bay nine miles from here that is the safest sentence anybody has handed those people all term, and in a room with somebody in it who cannot answer you, it takes a person's link off them, and there is no version of the same five words that is safe in both, and the difference between the two is not in the words, it is whether there is a person thirty feet away.**"

Nobody said anything for four seconds.

"Right," said the man of thirty-three. "**That is the other half and I have not got an answer to it and I am not going to pretend to. I am going to go and do my Friday and I am going to think about the thirty feet.**"

---

Yasmin Okoye is forty-six and she is a night shift supervisor at a service address in Nine Bridges and she has nine people on nights, and at ten past five on the Wednesday afternoon she filed an incident report on a company's system, and the report is the whole of the next nine days and the whole of the reason the case goes quiet.

It is nine fields. What happened, where, when, what was found, who found it, what was done about it, and two at the bottom that say *source* and *group*, and the two at the bottom are drop-downs, and there is no free text in either of them.

"It is not a list," she will say later. "**I want that on the record. It is not a list, it is a pair of boxes, and there is a difference, and the difference is that a list is written by a person and a pair of boxes is chosen by a person and there is one more step in choosing.**"

The card came off a machine in a bay on a Friday and off a machine in a canteen on the Saturday and off a phone in a corridor on the Monday, and by the Wednesday it had been in about six places and had been in a stairwell in the Quiet Quarter with nine people, and it is not the company's and it is not a training provider's, and there are eleven recognised entries on the drop-down and one of them is a name that nine people in this city have heard and about four hundred people have read this month on a leaflet that came round with the quarterly levy note.

She typed it in at ten past five because the form will not submit without it, and the other ten options are a company, a training provider, an employee of either, or a supplier of either, and none of those is true, and *other* is in the free field underneath and is not a source.

"So I picked the one that fitted," Yasmin Okoye said, when she was asked, which was on the Thursday, and she said it in one sentence and would not say it again. "**And the one that fitted is on a leaflet, and the leaflet came through my door with the levy note, and about eleven thousand premises pay that levy, and there is now one system in this city with my nine nights and a levy and a name in it, and I did all three of those things in eleven minutes and I was doing the correct thing in each of them.**"

*126. Wednesday of week 35, eleven at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is about a woman of thirty-nine with a washing bag on a landing who has said not mine four times, and about a woman of forty-six with a pair of boxes on a screen who picked the one that fitted.*
