# Chapter 126 — Eleven Minutes

A clinic in Crown Terrace has about nine bays on a general floor and one of them has been occupied since about the Saturday of week thirty-three by a woman of sixty-eight called Aurelia Carrow, who is on a drip and who cannot answer reliably and who has a daughter two hours away who does not know yet.

A narrow share was opened on her on the Monday of week thirty-three at ten past two, over a table speaker, by a woman of thirty-one with a diary, on the authority of a firm and a form, with a date on it and an end on it and a review in it.

Nobody has asked the person holding it whether she is willing to hold it, and there is no form on which to ask her, and the reason there is no form is that a care assistant is not a party, and the answer to who is a named party in any room in this city is nobody, and it has been nobody for a term and a half.

---

Adaeze Nwosu is thirty-eight and she has been a healthcare assistant at that clinic for eleven years and she has held four narrow shares in eleven years and she has been asked about none of them.

She was not on the rota for the weekend. She came in on the Monday morning of week thirty-six at seven, on days, and the share had been live since about the Wednesday morning, and nine people had been in the bay on the Saturday and the Sunday and had not known there was a thread out of that bay at all, which is correct and is the design.

She had the card in her pocket. She had had it since the Friday, when a colleague of forty-five who does nights put one on the break room table in a stack of eleven, and said there were nine of them and they came from nowhere, and said the one on the top had got a line on the back that was worth reading.

---

"I want to say what I am going to do and I want to say it before I do it," said Adaeze Nwosu. "**Because I have been sitting on this since half past seven and I have decided that the sitting is worse than the doing, and that if I do it and I have said it out loud first, then nobody can say afterwards that I did it because nobody was there.**"

The woman of thirty-one had a telephone in her hand and a diary open on a table.

"**Go on,**" said Ianthe Roke.

"**The card says: if the word does not come, withdraw and report. And there is no word. There has never been a word in this, there is no card and no lanyard and no load and no balance step, and nobody in this building is going to say a word to me in bay four at any point in the next three weeks, because there is nothing to say one about. So the line applies. That is all. That is the whole of my reasoning and it took four minutes and I would like it written down as four minutes and not as a decision.**"

"I am writing it down," said Ianthe Roke, "**and I am going to tell you what I am going to do first, which is that I am going to telephone the person who opened this. I have to. He gave me the authority on a speaker on the Monday of the thirty-third at ten past two and it is his share and it is his mother's and he has not been told there is a fifth line in a pocket in this building.**"

"**You are going to ring him.**"

"**I am going to ring him, and I am going to be four minutes, and then I am going to ring the firm, and then you are going to take it off, and then we are both going to write a line down, and there is no form for any of that and there is a form for one of it.**"

---

She made the two calls in eleven minutes and the second one was to a man of thirty-four in a car park on a service road in Nine Bridges who said *fine, do it, tell her I said fine*, and he was not angry, and Adaeze Nwosu wrote the word down because she thought he might want to have said it later.

"**Say the words,**" said Ianthe Roke.

"**I am taking the share off Mrs Carrow. It is seven minutes past ten on the Monday. I am the one holding it. It is coming off now and I am telling you while it is still on, which is the second condition of it.**"

It came off in four seconds. Ianthe Roke wrote one line in the diary with the date on it, in the room, in front of her, and read the line back, which she does not always do.

Then she said, to nobody, in the way of a person who has been in this building for four years:

"**And nobody will read that.**"

---

The form is the clinic's own. It is nine fields and it is on a clipboard on the wall in bay four and it has a box that says *withdrawn by* and a box that says *time* and a box that says *reason* and a box lower down that says *contributing factors* with about four lines under it.

Adaeze Nwosu filled in every one of them in four minutes, and she put her own name in the first box, and in the reason box she wrote: *applied fifth line from an unissued card held by a member of staff.*

"I want that in the reason box," she said, "**and not in the contributing factors, because it is the reason and the card is the contributing factor, and I am not going to hand anybody a form that has got them the wrong way round.**"

"That is not one of the options," said Ianthe Roke.

"**It is one of the boxes.**"

---

And then a bay went off.

It was at ten past ten, and it was a porter called **Dev** who has been in that building for nine years and who is not on any of the forms and has never been on any of them, and he found a woman of sixty-eight on her feet at the end of the bed with a drip stand three feet away from where it had been, and she was not hurt, and she was not distressed exactly, she was *looking for a door*, which is the phrase the staff use afterwards and which does not appear on any of the nine fields.

She was back in the bed in two minutes and asleep in about eleven.

The daughter was called at about eleven and arrived at about six.

---

She is forty-one and she drove two hours and she is not the woman of thirty-four with a hip and she is not Tess Anley and she has never been in this building before.

"They said she was fine," she said, at twenty past six, in a corridor. "**They said she was fine and they were right, and I want to say that first, and I have been sitting in a car park for about an hour working out how to say the next part and I have not got it and I am going to say it badly.**"

"**Say it however you like.**"

"She has been in twice this year, once in the spring and once in the summer. And both times somebody rang me and told me she had come in and both times I was here in ninety minutes and both times I was told which bay and I was told by a person's name." She had her hand on the wall. "**Nobody rang me today. I found out at eleven o'clock tonight from a man at my work, because my mother's neighbour saw the light on in the front room and rang her and she tried my mobile and I have not switched it on since the summer.**"

Nobody said anything for four seconds.

"So she was on her own," said the daughter of forty-one. "**Not on her own. There was somebody in the bay. But she was on her own in the way that means it, and I have been on my own with that since half past six this evening and I have not been able to make it into a sentence that anybody can do anything about, and I do not think anybody is going to be able to.**"

---

Ianthe Roke stood in the corridor and let that finish and then said the whole of it in about nine words, and she said it to Adaeze Nwosu and not to the daughter.

"**The share is what rang you. That is the only thing in this building that rings anybody and it is a date and a telephone number in my book and a person, and I am the person, and I did not ring you because a member of staff withdrew the share at seven minutes past ten this morning using a card out of a drawer and she told me while it was still on, which is correct, and she filled in a form and put her own name in the first box, which is correct, and the card told her to, and the card is right.**"

"Then whose fault is it."

"That is not a question this building has got a box for," said Ianthe Roke. "**And I am not going to answer it in a corridor at twenty past six on a Monday to a woman who has driven two hours, and I have been in this building four years and I have watched nine people be asked that question and answer it and get it wrong, and the getting it wrong is what happened.**"

---

The district came back at ten past nine on the Tuesday with a withdrawal instruction, and it is one page, and it is correct, and it is the best document anybody in this case has produced in six weeks.

*Withdrawal of a narrow share. A narrow share may be ended at the request of the person it concerns, or at the request of the person holding it. A person holding a narrow share who no longer wishes to hold it does not require a reason and does not require a form. Where a share is ended other than at the request of the person it concerns, the attending service is to record the date and the time, and the reason if one is offered, and is not to require one.*

And at the bottom, in a small type, because that is how they print things that were added later:

*Notified: [ ]*

There is no box for the person who removed it. There is no box for who asked. There are four fields on the whole page and one of them is a tick.

"That is a good document," said Adaeze Nwosu.

"It is a good document and it will not work," said Ianthe Roke, "**and I can tell you exactly how it will not work, and it will not work because there is no box for me either. I am the person in the chain. I open shares, I keep a diary, I ring the review, and if a member of staff ends one and fills in a form, then in about four years somebody reads a form that says a share was ended and the form does not say by whom, and the only person in this building who could answer that question is me, and I am a person, and a person can be asked, and I will be asked, and I will remember, and I will be right.**" She put the page down on the table. "And about four years from now there will be a page in a folder that says a share came off a bay and no person on it, and whoever reads it will conclude that nobody was there, and there will be a woman of thirty-eight who was there."

---

Marek Senn rang at ten past two on the Monday afternoon, which is the first working day after a Thursday, and she had told him not to ring between the Thursday and the Monday and it was the Monday.

"I know you were told not to ring," she said. "**I told a man of forty-five on the Wednesday about the plate and I told a man of thirty-one on the Friday about the plate and I told you, and I have been right about all three of you and I would like it recorded that I have been right about all three and that none of the three of you has ever once been on time.**"

She told him the fifth line in eleven words. Then she said:

"**I am telling you that because you will go and find a worse one, and there is not a worse one, and the one we have is the best one anybody has handed anybody this term, and I want you to have heard it from me before you get it from a leaflet.**"

"**Why.**"

"**Because I have got a diary with a line in it from ten past ten this morning and four people in this city are going to want to know what that line says, and you are the fifth of them, and I am not going to say no to you about it twice.**"

---

That was the eleventh minute.

There were eleven minutes between seven minutes past ten, when a share came off a woman of sixty-eight in bay four in a clinic in Crown Terrace, and a quarter past ten, when a woman of sixty-eight was asleep again with a door in the room she could not find. **Nobody was hurt. The clinic was not negligent. The card was right. The form was correct. The district's instruction came back in twenty-five hours and is better than anything anybody else has produced this term, and it does not have a box for the person, and the woman who has that person is thirty-one and has a diary nobody reads.**

And in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, a man of twenty-two who has said the word nine, ten and eleven times a minute for about eleven months looked at a docket with a count on it and understood that **there are now two fifth lines in this city and they do not agree and there is no place in this city where both of them can be found.** One of them was written by the wife of a man of fifty-eight in the Saltmarket because he cannot spell, and it says *if nobody comes, put it down and say so.* The other one was written by an unknown hand on the back of a photocopy and printed four hundred and thirty-two times out of a photograph of a postage stamp, and it says *if the word does not come, withdraw and report.* One of them is true in a wet bay with a man thirty feet away. The other one is true in a corridor with nobody thirty feet away, and it is true in exactly the same words, and there is no form, no minute, no district, no committee and no leaflet in this city that could hold the two of them and say which is which.
