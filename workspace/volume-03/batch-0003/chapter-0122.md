# Chapter 122 — The Hire Place

The Quiet Quarter has about four places where you can rent a box by the month and give it a number instead of a name, and the reason there are four is that nobody has ever argued about it in nine years, because the design is right and a right design does not need arguing about.

A woman of thirty-five was sitting at a table by the window of a café on a street with a launderette in it at nine in the morning on the Tuesday of week thirty-five, and she said her name in the first eleven seconds.

"**Leo Marr. I am not here to buy anything and I have not come to find out who you are, because I already know who you are and it has taken me about nine days not to be here and I have used the nine days and I have found nothing and I am telling you that because you would have found it on your own in about a fortnight and I would rather be the one who says it first.**"

---

He put the bag down. The same bag. A spare meter, a pen-torch, three-core.

"The bag is honest," she said. "**The bag is the most honest object in this café. Do sit down.**"

He sat down.

"I have read about your Monday," said Leo Marr. "**Not from you. From a woman at a district office who telephoned me at about eight this morning to ask whether the group she is writing a notice about is the one I organise, and who could not say the group because the notice has a field that says *group* and the field wants a name in it, and there is no name, and she wanted to know whether the absence of a name was mine.**"

"**What did you say.**"

"I said I would come and look at the notice." She turned her cup a quarter turn. "**And I am going to, and I have not, and I have nine minutes before I have to be somewhere, and I am going to spend them here because if I do not spend them here I will spend them in a taxi looking at a notice that has a field in it.**"

"**You are not going to tell me who filled the field in.**"

"**No.**"

"**That was fast.**"

"I have been not telling people things for nine years and I do it in a second and I have never once apologised for it afterwards and I am not going to start with you." She said it without any weight on it at all, which was the thing that made it difficult. "**What I am going to say to you is the other half and you are not going to like it, and then I am going to go, and I have not asked you for anything and I am not going to.**"

---

"There is a hire place on the next street," said Leo Marr. "**It is not a front for me and I am not going to tell you it is not, because I do not know and not knowing is the correct position for somebody who has been organising for nine years. It takes a number and a month and money. It does not take a name. I have watched four of them be set up in nine years in three districts and not one of them has ever asked anybody for a name, and I have argued with two of their owners about it and lost both times, and I was wrong both times, and I want that on the record in a café at nine in the morning because it is the only thing I have got.**"

"**So the mailbox is the safest thing anybody has given those people.**"

"**The mailbox is the safest thing anybody has given those people all term, and you know that, and I have watched you not say it in a room four times in a fortnight.**"

The café had about nine tables and a rack of leaflets by the door for a charity that gives away bread, and a man of thirty-three who worked in the building opposite had taken four of the leaflets and put them face down on the table nearest the window, which is what people do.

"**There is something on that table,**" said Leo Marr. "Go and look at it."

He looked at it.

It was a piece of card with four printed steps on it, and a fifth line in a hand that was not a printer's and not a company's, and the fifth line said: *If the word does not come, withdraw and report.*

He looked at it for a long time.

"**When did you last see one of those,**" he said.

"**Nine days ago, on a bench, in about four places, and I have seen the same card in about nine places since and I have not got a single one of them to the same person and there is no version of this in which the four of them came from four decisions.**"

---

"Right," said Marek Senn. "**Then the person who paid for four hundred of them.**"

"**No.**"

He had not finished the sentence.

"Before you finish it," said Leo Marr, "**because I have been sitting here for nine minutes waiting for you to finish it, and I would have said no whether you finished it or not, and I want it recorded that I said no to an unfinished question.**"

"**I had not got to the payment.**"

"**You had got to the person, and the payment is under the person, and you would have got to the payment in four seconds and I know that because I do the same thing and I have watched you do it twice.**"

---

He put his hand flat on the table, which is a thing he does and which is not a technique and which in about four years somebody might be able to read.

"**Give me the customer.**"

"**No.**"

"**Give me the hire place.**"

"**No, and the reason for that one is different and I am going to give it to you because you will otherwise spend a week working out that I was being clever.**"

She took a breath and started again.

"The person who paid pays in small sums, and pays them often, and does not pay forty pounds at once, and there are exactly two reasons a person does that and one of them is hiding and the other one is that forty pounds is a real amount of money to them this month." She looked at him. "**If you take that customer off a hire place and put a name on the four hundred cards, then in about four years there is a document in this city with a person's name on it, and the person's name belongs to somebody who could not pay forty pounds at once. And you will have spent four months proving that a shape does not tell you who cut it, and then you will have handed a district office a person, and there is no version of that in which the person and the four hundred cards are not the same thing to whoever reads the file.**"

"**I know.**"

"**Then know it out loud, in this café, in about nine words, because a thing that is only known in a book is a thing that gets lost on the day somebody needs it.**"

Marek Senn looked at the card on the table.

"**A printed thing can be unprinted. A name cannot be un-said, and I have spent a term and a half treating those as the same problem and they are not, and the one I cannot un-say is the one I have been chasing since the ninth week, and I have never once been able to use the one I can un-print.**"

Leo Marr put her cup down.

"That is nine words," she said, "**and I did not say it and I have not said it in nine years, and I would like it noted that a man with a lanyard said it better than I have said it in about nine years and that I am not bitter about it, I am just going to sit here for a second.**"

She sat there for a second.

---

The man of thirty-three who worked in the building opposite came over with his cup.

"**Are you two talking about the cards.**"

"**No,**" said Leo Marr.

"Right," he said, and put the leaflet face down again, and did not go, and stood there, and after four seconds said: "**I have had four of them off a man at a stall in the market and I have had four off a woman at a letting agent's and I have had two off a lad who does the bins on a Wednesday, and I have given out about nine, and I have not given one to anybody I know and I have not been paid for one and I do not know where they come from and I have stopped asking, and my wife asked me on Sunday why there is a card about walking away from a job in our kitchen drawer.**"

"**What did you say to her.**"

"I said it is on the wall," he said, "**and she said it is on the wall, and that is where we have got to.**"

Then he went back to his table, and neither of them said anything about him, and Leo Marr said:

"**Write down what he said. All of it. Including the kitchen drawer. That is nine people in this district tonight and none of them know where the card came from and all of them have one.**"

"**I am not going to put that in a book with your name in it.**"

"**I know you are not. Put it in a book with his first name in it and no last name, or no name, and then in about four years it is a true thing about a man in a café rather than a thing you learned off me.**"

---

He rang her at ten past ten from a bench outside a launderette, and the Tuesday is for saying a sentence to a person who already knows it, and he had said that in a corridor in the week of the thirty-third and had not improved on it since.

She took four rings.

"You have found the middle of something," said Talia Venn. "**I can hear that you have and I am not going to ask you what it is, because you will tell me, and I have got a clinic in nine minutes.**"

"I found it and it is a copy of a copy and the box is downstream of it, and a woman of thirty-one has put that in her own words and she is right, and she is going to be the one carrying it."

"**Then say the other half,**" said Talia Venn.

"What other half."

"Say the half where she is not the only one who now knows where the middle of it is." Her voice did not change and neither did anything else about it. "**A woman of thirty-one put a copy of a copy in a box and said out loud that she is in charge of the middle of it. You wrote it down. And there are now two of them and the second one is you, and in about four years somebody is going to ask her where she got it and she is going to have to say a man's name, and it will be the only name in that conversation, and she will have given it for a reason that was good at the time.**"

He stood at the bench for nine seconds.

"That is eleven words," he said.

"**It is more than eleven. Go to work.**"

---

*125. Tuesday of week 35, half past ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is about a woman of thirty-five in a café by a launderette who said no to an unfinished question, and about a man of thirty-three with a card in his kitchen drawer and a leaflet face down on a table.*

*The nine days, run out, and nothing found.* She was in the café at nine and she has been in that café every weekday morning for about nine days, and she told me that before I had sat down, and she told me the time it took her to stop not coming. She cut the wire is a thing she could do and has not done, and she has not done it twice now, and the second not doing is not indecision, it is a judgement nine people copying cards, and she said the judgement out loud: **if the wire comes down first then the woman doing the copying hears about it from a district notice and stops, and stopping is the right thing for her to do, and I do not want her to stop because of me.** She is not the author of anything in this book and she is not a villain and she is not defeated and she does not join anything, and she has still not used the word *house* about anything, and the word only exists because it came out of a form into a district office about four years ago and after that it was a name.

*And the two refusals, and the order they came in.* **I asked for the customer. She said no to an unfinished question and made me wait for it, and then she said no to the person and no to the hire place, and the two are not the same refusal and she gave me the reason for the second one and not for the first one, and the second one is the only reason in this batch I have written down in full.** A person who cannot pay forty pounds at once is a person with a name that must not go in a file, and the person is not hiding, or may not be, and there is no version of putting that name in a district document in which the name and four hundred cards stop being the same thing to whoever opens the file. She was right and I have got nothing, and it is the ninth refusal in a fortnight and the ninth correct one, and she did not apologise for it and neither did I.

*The card on the table, and the nine words.* Four printed steps and a fifth line in a hand that is not a printer's and not a company's, and the fifth line says *if the word does not come, withdraw and report*. Nine days ago she had seen four of them on a bench in about four places and since then she has seen about nine in nine places and cannot get any two of them to the same person. **And the hire place is the safest thing anybody has given those people all term, and I have known that since the Monday of week thirty-four, and I have watched myself not say it in a room four times in a fortnight, and a woman of thirty-five who has organised for nine years without ever once using the word house put it on a table in a café and made me say it out loud, and then sat there for a second because I said it in nine words and she has not managed it in nine years.** I am not going to improve that. It stands.

*The man of thirty-three, and the cost, which is a kitchen drawer.* **Four off a stallholder, four off a letting agent, two off a boy who does the bins on a Wednesday, nine given out, none to anybody he knows, not paid by anybody, and he has stopped asking, and his wife asked him on Sunday why there is a card about walking away from a job in their kitchen drawer and he said it is on the wall and she said it is on the wall.** That is the whole of the counterfeit's travel in one man in ninety seconds and he is not in this case and is not a villain and is not counted, and she told me to write it down with his first name or with no name at all, so that in about four years it is a true thing about a man in a café and not a thing anybody learned off her. I have written it with no name on it, which is her way, and which is a form of her way that I did not have eleven weeks ago.

*And the Tuesday, eleven words, and it is the whole of this entry.* **She does not ask me questions on a Tuesday and I have told her the Tuesday is not for telling her things, it is for saying a sentence to a person who already knows it, and I said it and she gave me the other half and it is the half about a person and not about a mechanism: a woman of thirty-one put a copy of a copy in a box and said out loud that she is in charge of the middle of it, I wrote it down, and there are now two of them and the second one is me, and in about four years she is going to have to say a man's name to a district office, and it will be the only name in that conversation, and she will have given it for a reason that was good at the time.** Entry 125. The book is at forty-three lines and is opened on the Wednesday of the week of the thirty-six. A card with a fifth line on it is in about nine places in one district and nobody can get two of them to the same person, and I have found the middle of something and the middle is a woman of thirty-one with a rubber band, and a woman of thirty-five has refused me twice correctly in nine minutes, and a man of thirty-three has a card in a kitchen drawer and does not know where it came from.
