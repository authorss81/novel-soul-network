# Chapter 148 — The Same Shape

It was the sixth of the eleven sessions and it was on the Friday, and the room was in Crown Terrace, and there were forty folding chairs in rows and nine of them empty and a microphone on a stand at the front with a chair beside it and a trestle table by the door with about four hundred leaflets in a stack.

Admission free. Fee paid. The panel was five and all five of it was on the front of the leaflet, listed by job.

---

He had been on the Friday rota since eight in the morning and had done eleven jobs, nine of them in date, and the word had come at nine and at ten and at eleven a minute for most of it, and at half past six he was in a chair at the back of a room in Crown Terrace with a docket in his hand and a lanyard on that he had not asked for and could not take off.

Both of the two people printed on the front of the leaflet as a job were him. A system had chosen them out of two job sheets in the week of the thirty-fourth and nobody had chosen them, and a man of fifty-two had stopped offering to check it, and the condition on his share stops him doing the thing and does not stop a member of the public asking him about the thing in a room with a microphone.

---

Corin Vasska read the specification the way he reads everything, in the same voice, and then he read the third line, which is not a section, and about nine people did not hear the difference.

Then he put down the sheet and said: "**I am going to do something this evening that is not in my job, and I want the officer of this fund to hear me say that I know it is not in my job, and I want it minuted if anybody is minuting.**"

Nobody was minuting.

"**Page two of the six-sided handout that this fund distributed with a district note has a photograph on it, about the size of a postage stamp, of a card in a plastic sleeve on a lanyard, with four steps legible on it. About four thousand of those handouts went out. A woman with a print shop four feet wide in the Quiet Quarter has four hundred and thirty-two cards in about nine places in one district whose four correct steps came off that photograph, and she has said so out loud, and she is in this room.**"

---

She was standing by the trestle table with her hands in her pockets and she had come in nineteen minutes after the doors and had not sat down.

"**I have come because a levy officer asked me to and because I am not going to be asked twice,**" said Sunniva Bellhouse, "and I have not brought anything except two pieces of paper and I would like them put on the table with the other two and not with mine."

Somebody at the front put the leaflets down and made a space.

There were four things on the trestle table by a quarter past seven: a company card, revision 4-19, in a plastic sleeve, taken out of a pack that a man of forty-four had carried from a depot; a card about the same size with four steps and a fifth line, taken out of an envelope under a till four feet of shop away; a printout of page two of a six-sided handout; and a blow-up of the same page off a machine that used to do a weekly for a school that has shut.

Nobody said anything for eleven seconds.

---

"**Pick them up,**" said Sunniva Bellhouse. "All four of you. There is a man at the door with a list and there is a woman in the fourth row with a notebook and there is a man at the front who has read four hundred leaflets. Pick the two cards up."

They did.

"Now read the first four lines of each one and then read the fifth."

A man at the front read them out loud, both cards, the same voice for each, and it took nine seconds.

"**Same four steps,**" he said. "Anchor, three beats, hold. Same order, same intervals printed. The five lines are in the same five places."

"And the fifth."

"**The fifth line on the company's says release on the word, and if no word, withdraw and report.**"

"**And on yours.**"

"**If the word does not come, withdraw and report.**"

Nobody said anything for four seconds and about nine people in that room looked at the same two cards.

"**Read the next part out loud,**" said Sunniva Bellhouse, "because it is the part that is in the leaflets and I have read about four of them."

"**If no word, withdraw and report,**" said the man at the front. "That is what the company's says."

"**And it is what mine says,**" she said. "Two cards, the same five words, and not one of them the same sentence. And I have never been able to make mine better than that one, and I have printed four hundred and thirty-two of them, and a card that cannot be made better and is better is the whole of what I have got."

---

"**So where is the difference,**" said somebody at the back.

Nobody answered for four seconds, and then Corin Vasska said it, and he said it the way he reads things out, and he put two fingers on the company's card and then two fingers on the counterfeit's and did not pick either of them up.

"**The difference is who it is waiting for,**" he said. "The company's card is step four of a bundle that is issued against a register, and there is a list of named holders in a building in another part of this city that nobody in this city has read, and the person its fourth beat waits for is a key-holder, and a key-holder is a person in a room. The counterfeit's has no name on it and no company and no version and no register and no person in it anywhere, because there is no room in it."

He put his hand flat on the table.

"**And I have been the officer of a fund for four years and I have put that sentence in a minute, and there is a leaflet going out to about nine hundred rooms that cites the company's assurance as proof, and the assurance is where she got the four steps for free, and both of those are true on the same afternoon, and I cannot stop the leaflet and I have checked twice, and I did not know it until a fortnight ago.**"

---

A man in the fourth row stood up.

Peter Amado is about fifty-one and has said a true sentence into a microphone six times and has been paid a hundred and forty pounds and travel and has a wife and a heating bill and about five Fridays left of a series.

"I am going to say something and I am going to say it because I am the only person in this room who gets paid to say things, and I want the microphone off for it."

Somebody turned the microphone off.

"**I said a true thing in a market in the week of the twenty-four and it was that a technique is being handed round in this city and nobody is putting their name to it, and eleven people went two seconds quieter, and I have been paid for it six times and I have never once been asked whether I wanted to say it.**"

He put his hand on the back of the chair in front of him.

"**I am going to be asked to say a different sentence, and the different sentence is that a practice in this city belongs to nobody, and I am going to say it, and there is a fee, and I am going to take the fee, because the fee is the heating.**"

Somebody said: "You do not have to."

"**I do not have to and that is the first time anybody in this room has offered me that in six Fridays, and I am going to take the fee anyway and I would like it written down why.**" He looked at the floor. "Because after tonight nobody in this city will ever be able to tell whether I believe it. That is the price and it is mine and I have worked it out in a car park and I have not got anybody to work it out with."

---

Then he turned round.

"And I am going to say the other thing because I am the man in the district jacket and a man of forty-four has said twice in public that a rule about who may be asked is a rule about who is findable, and I have been in four thousand leaflets under a job title and I never chose it, and I said a true thing in a market and I have never been asked where I got it and I am not going to be asked now."

"Nobody is asking you."

"**Then I have got nothing to say and that is the best Friday I have had,**" said Peter Amado, "and I am going to keep coming."

---

Then the district officer of thirty-four stood up in the fourth row from the other side with a folder she had not opened, and a woman of fifty-eight came out from behind the trestle table and took the folder off her and put it on the table, and the folder had a sheet of paper in it that a boy of nineteen had walked to a building in Crown Terrace on his own legs on the Tuesday afternoon and had not put through a door with a window in it.

"**I am not going to say a name and I am not going to say a street,**" she said, "and I have got nine words and I am going to say them to a man with a lanyard on, and he is not going to be able to answer them, and I have thought about that for a fortnight."

"Go on."

"**Nobody is going to say whose practice this is.**"

She did not say anything else for four seconds, and then she said the rest of it, which was not in the nine words and which she has worked out in nineteen years and not in a fortnight, and it went round a room of forty people at the volume of a person who is not apologising.

"**About four hundred people are going to be handed a page with their own date on it and no person at the top of it,**" said Sunniva Bellhouse. "They wrote in, or they are on it, and in about four years they will be told that a practice in this city belongs to nobody. That is a sentence almost nobody in this city can make themselves say about their own work, and I have printed four hundred and thirty-two cards and I cannot make myself say it either, and the man who made that sentence possible is standing at the back of this room and he is not going to be able to argue with it."

"No," said Marek Senn.

"No," said Sunniva Bellhouse. "**I did not say it to be unkind to you. I said it because it is the price of the thing you did on Monday and you did it on purpose, and because a printer is the only person in this room who has had to decide what four hundred pieces of paper are for.**"

Nobody in that room voted on anything.

---

At about a quarter to eleven Corin Vasska said one more fact and did not editorialise about it.

"**A company has not sent anybody to this evening and has sent nothing at all, and this was the sixth of the eleven, and in about four years there will be a note in a file saying that the assembler and the seller declined to attend, and that note will be true.**"

And then nine people asked the man at the back of the room a question about the card, in a room with a microphone that was switched off, and he answered it in nine words each time, and the condition on his share does not stop a member of the public asking him about the thing, and there is no mechanism in this city that can tell the difference between a man doing a thing and a man being asked about a thing, and it is not resolved, and it is the third time it has been said, and the first time it has been said nine times in one room with a man in a district jacket in the fourth row writing it down.
