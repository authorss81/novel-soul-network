# Chapter 149 — A Box In Pencil

The counter is on the ground floor of a building in Bower Street and it has been there for longer than anybody in this case has been working, and on the Wednesday morning of week forty-five there were about two hundred of the district's withdrawal pages on it in four stacks with a rubber band round each one, and nobody had collected them since the spring.

Four fields. One of them a tick.

No document in this case has been better written in a term and a half, it does not work, and the reason it does not work is a box that is not on it.

---

Ianthe Roke is thirty-one and she is in charge of an exchange room on the first floor of that building, which means she has a key and a diary and a list, and that the room is not where the building says it is.

The plate outside it says 2-14. The room is 2-07. About four hundred consent leaflets in the rack are all printed 2-07, and a district man of thirty-four with a screen can see 2-14 on every document he has and cannot see 2-07 anywhere on any of them, and a girl of twenty-nine is the only person in this city who has taken a photograph of a wall, and a photograph of a wall is not a record of a wall.

Somebody came in the week of week thirty-seven with a fault ticket that carried no name on it, read it against the wall, wrote 2-14 on the wall again in the building's own typeface and closed the ticket.

That was the correct action and it was eight weeks ago and the room is still on a shelf.

---

What she had to work with was a telephone call.

It came on the Thursday of week forty-fourth from a district nurse of twenty-four who rings that office on Thursdays about her own reasons and has rung it for four years, and it lasted nine seconds, and it said the name of a district and nothing else, and it did not say a name and it did not say a street and it did not say why.

Ianthe Roke wrote *district* and the name of the district in a diary and she has not shown the page to anybody, and she could not have done anything with nine seconds of it if it had said a name.

What she could do was go down to the ground floor and take the rubber band off one of the four stacks.

---

Petra Lindqvist is fifty-three and has worked at that counter for nineteen years and has never once been asked to make a decision.

"**You have got a date on it and you have not got a box,**" she said, "**and there is no box for it, and I have been told twice this term that there is no box for it. The first one was a woman in the spring who did not give her name and was right, and the second one is a line printed at the foot of a district instruction that says no local additions, and I have read that line every day for nineteen years and I have never once not obeyed it.**"

"Then I am not asking you to break an instruction."

"**Everybody asks me that. It is what the counter is for.**"

"**I am asking you to draw a line on a piece of paper in pencil, and to write three words over it in your own hand, and to put nothing in it today,**" said Ianthe Roke. "**And if it comes back then it comes back to me and not to you, because my name is at the bottom of it and nobody's is at the top.**"

Petra Lindqvist looked at the bottom of the form for a while.

"**It comes back to whoever's name is on it.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**Then hold it flat.**"

She ruled it in under the fourth field, about the size of a stamp, and wrote three words over the top of it in pencil, and the words were *who ended it*, and then she put the pencil down and squared the page and pushed it four inches along the counter, away from herself.

There is no reference number on it. Nothing was typed. Nothing was copied. Nothing was entered in anything, and it is a form and not a system, and there is no box for the person who removed a share on any other page in this city.

---

"**You have put my name on it,**" said Ianthe Roke.

"**I have put your name on the bottom of it and nobody's on the top of it, and you told me to put it there and I have not been asked whether I wanted to.**"

"Nobody is going to thank you."

"**I have not done it for that,**" said Petra Lindqvist, "**and if somebody does they should be thanking the woman who wrote the four fields, and she does not work here and I have never met her, and I have had that line on a district instruction for nineteen years and nobody has ever told me who wrote it.**"

Then the woman of thirty-one said what the cost of it is, and she said it to the counter.

"**A date and a time and a telephone number and a name at the bottom of it, and not one of the four of those is a post, is a person who cannot be ill, and who cannot be on a Friday, and cannot be on a Thursday, and I have a job on a Thursday. There is no post above me whose job it is to make that call and there has not been one since before I came here, and what I have got is a diary and a name at the bottom of a piece of paper, and if I stop doing it the form does not stop, and the form will sit on that counter with a date on it going past like a bus.**"

"**Then it is the best thing available,**" said Petra Lindqvist, "**and everything that has gone out of this counter in nineteen years was better printed than that one, and not one of them was any use to the person who took it away, and I have watched that happen about four hundred times and I could not tell you one name.**"

---

The date on the page was a Wednesday and the time was a quarter past two, and at a quarter past two on the Wednesday of this week Ianthe Roke telephoned the first one on the diary from the counter with a pencil in her other hand, because a date is not a telephone call.

It took four minutes. The share came off at the request of the person it concerns, which is the only case the four fields were written for.

The person on the other end said thank you.

"**Do not thank me, because I have got a box on a form with nothing in it and that is the whole of what I have done this morning,**" said Ianthe Roke, "**and if I am not here when it comes, ring the number on the sheet and leave it and do not wait, because there is nobody to wait for and there is not going to be.**"

She wrote the time in the diary and not on the form. The box was made on a Wednesday morning and it was empty on the Wednesday afternoon, and that is what it was made for.

---

The other one is in a drawer in the room on the first floor, and it is a page, and it is one of these.

The four of them filled in in ink, one of them a tick, and a date and a time at the top of the page, in a hand that is not hers, from the Tuesday of week thirty-eight. It took four seconds. A care-link came off a woman of sixty-eight and a bay went off eleven minutes later and nobody was hurt, and the four fields are correct, and there is no box on that page because there was no box to rule, and there is no person anywhere on it, and there is not going to be.

The person who took that share off was the person holding it. She was thirty-eight and nobody has ever asked her whether she was willing to, and nobody is going to, and a nurse of thirty-four in another building has said out loud that the difference between that woman and a front is that a front cannot say no in about nine words about a share, and she has said it in a room and not in a minute, and she is not on the page and her name is not going to be on it.

So the box is in pencil, on one page, on a counter on the ground floor, with a date on it, and it was empty the day it was made.

Nobody thanked her. The clerk did not mention her again, and put the rubber band back on the other three stacks, and has not mentioned her since.

---

*152. Wednesday of week 45, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a counter on the ground floor of a building in Bower Street with about two hundred forms on it that nobody had collected, and a woman of thirty-one with a diary, and a clerk of fifty-three who has been told twice.*

*What was on the counter, and what had happened to the wall.* **About two hundred of the district's withdrawal pages in four stacks, four fields, one of them a tick, uncalled for since the spring. The plate on the first floor says 2-14 and the room is 2-07 and about four hundred consent leaflets in the rack are all printed 2-07, and somebody came in the week of the thirty-seventh with a fault ticket that carried no name on it and wrote 2-14 on the wall again in the building's own typeface and closed the ticket, and that was the correct action eight weeks ago, and the room is still on a shelf and about forty-one people in this district are still on a link that nobody can end.** The only route out of that corridor runs through a counter on the ground floor and a woman with a diary, and there was nothing else available and nobody has invented anything since.

*What she had to work with, and it was nine seconds.* **A district nurse of twenty-four who rings that office on Thursdays about her own reasons and has rung it for four years, and who said the name of a district and nothing else, and did not say a name or a street or a reason, and it went in a diary that nobody has been shown.** It is the one clean call a week she had and she spent it and she has not told anybody she spent it, and nobody is going to thank her for it either, and those are one decision and not two. I did not know she had done it when she did it and I do not know now.

*The resolution, and the whole of this volume, in about four hundred words.* **A form on a counter, a date, a time, a telephone number, a name at the bottom of it and nobody's at the top, and a person whose job it is to telephone, and the job is a diary and a woman's habit, and there is still no post and nobody invented one.** The clerk said there was no box for it and had been told twice there was no box for it, once by a woman in the spring who did not give her name and once by a printed line at the foot of a district instruction. What was asked of her was not that she break it: **draw a line in pencil, write three words over it in your own hand, put nothing in it, and take the return.** Who ended it. **It is a form and not a system, no reference number, nothing typed, nothing copied, nothing entered anywhere, and it is the only box in this city for the person who removed a share, and it was empty the day it was made.**

*What each of them paid, and neither of them is thanked.* **Her cost is a person: a date, a time and a telephone number together make somebody who cannot be ill, and she has a job on a day she cannot do it on, and the paper keeps its date whether she does anything about it or not, and she told me the shape of that in nine seconds and she said it to a counter.** The clerk's cost is nineteen years of being the person who obeys the line at the foot of somebody else's instruction, and she has never once been told who wrote it, and she has said out loud that the credit for what is on that counter is not hers, and she is a printer's habit in a building that is not a print shop. **She was asked for a line and three words and nothing in it, and she gave the instruction back in her own handwriting, which is the first time in nineteen years she has written on a district page at all, and she is not going to be thanked for it and would be embarrassed.**

*And the two things it did not do.* **She made one telephone call on the afternoon it was made, from the counter, with a pencil in her other hand, and it took four minutes, and nothing came off that anybody had to be talked out of, and the person at the other end of it said thank you and was told not to.** **And there is one of these in a drawer on the first floor with four fields filled in and the tick and a date and a time in a hand that is not hers, from the Tuesday of the thirty-eighth, when a care-link came off a woman of sixty-eight in four seconds and a bay went off eleven minutes later and nobody was hurt, and there is no box on that page because there was no box to rule, and there is no person anywhere on it and there is not going to be, and the person who took that share off was the person holding it and was thirty-eight and nobody has ever asked her whether she was willing to, and nobody is going to ask her.** A nurse of thirty-four has said in a room that a front cannot say no in nine words about a share, and she is not on the page and her name is not going to be on it. **Entry 152.** The book is at forty-four lines and it is not a register. The master is in an inside pocket on a chair four miles from that counter, and the drawer under the bench holds five things, four of them other people's sentences, and nobody rang a man of fifty-one with a leg before or after any of it, and a boy on a till in the Quiet Quarter is going to run a machine on Saturday for the first time in his life.
