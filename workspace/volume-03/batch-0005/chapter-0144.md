# Chapter 144 — Not A Group

Corin Vasska has wanted to be asked a question for four years and in the last three weeks he has been asked four, and on the Wednesday afternoon of week forty-third he asked one himself, and it took him nine minutes to get to it and about four seconds to be refused.

The room was the one above the print works in Crown Terrace with nine folding chairs stacked against the wall and a projector that has not worked in five sessions and that nobody has tried to make work.

There were three of them in it: the officer of the levy, a man of twenty-two with a job title, and a woman of thirty-five who had said her name in the first four seconds because that is what she does.

He had not asked her to come and she had not agreed to come, and the way that had happened was that he had sent her four pages of something and she had telephoned him back and said, *if this is about a room then I will come to the room, and if it is about a group then you do not need me in it.*

---

"**Before I ask, you are going to want to know why now,**" said Corin Vasska, "because I have wanted to ask you for four years and I have not asked you once, and a woman of thirty-five is entitled to know that the reason is a date and not a change of heart."

"Go on."

"**The sixth session is on the Friday of next week. There is a leaflet going out with the note on it to about nine hundred rooms, and about four thousand went out with the last one, and the leaflet cites an assurance. It is a page and a half of a company's own assurance, and it is being put in front of the rooms as evidence that the practice is sound.**"

"And it is not sound."

"I have not got a reading of it that says it is not sound. I have got a reading of it that says it was written about a different thing, and I read it on the Friday and I have not slept properly since, and that is a sentence you are entitled to discount." He put the four pages on the table face down. "I cannot stop the leaflet. I have checked twice. It is a district internal distribution and it goes out with a district circular, and the only thing I can do about anything in this area is remove a series from a calendar in about eleven weeks."

"**So you are asking me because a thing you are paid for is going out on Friday.**"

"**I am asking you because in about four years somebody is going to hold up that leaflet and say the officer of the levy stood in a room and knew,**" said Corin Vasska. "And I did not stand in a room. I sat in one."

---

"**I want to say what I am about to ask for before I ask for it,**" said Corin Vasska, "so that if you say no then you have said no to a thing with a shape and not to a man being friendly in a corridor."

"Say it."

"**A limited emergency notice. One number, one person's name, one sentence, and it does not survive the year.**" He had it on the back of a hand. "If a room in this district goes on a share and cannot end it, and the person who was in the room is not answerable to anybody in the levy, then somebody outside my money gets one telephone call. That is all. It is not a group and it is not a meeting and it is not a line on a form, because there is no form and I have looked."

He put the hand down on the table.

"**And I know what I have just described. I described it to nine people on the Wednesday of the thirty-seventh and they minuted that they had been told two things and had done nothing, and I have read that minute more times than I have read the specification.**"

---

Leo Marr did not answer for about four seconds, which from her is an answer.

"**No group. One limited notice. Do not ask twice.**"

Then she said: "**And now I am going to tell you what that costs and I am going to say it before you have written it down anywhere, because you have got a specification and a specification is where a sentence goes to have a person attached to it.**"

"**There is no form.**"

"**There is a column. A notice with no name on it is a thing that goes to a district. A notice with a name on it is a thing that goes to a person, and a person can be found.**" She had her hands flat on the table and she was not looking at either of them. "I have been unfindable for nine years. It is the only thing I have that is mine."

Nobody said anything.

"**So that is the nine words and I have said them once and I am not going to say them again in this term. The other half is not in the nine and I am giving it to you because you have never once in four years gave me nothing that was not a true sentence.** Put my name on it, in my own hand, and not a category, and not the name of a thing I belong to. **I have been a category in about nine pieces of paper in this city in four years and every one of them cost somebody else.**"

---

"**You understand what a name on that notice does,**" said Corin Vasska.

"**I have understood it since the Thursday of the thirty-sixth and I said it into a telephone to a stranger, and you are the second person in this city to have offered me a place and you are the first one to have asked.**" She looked at him then. "A name on it means that if the district has a question in four years there is a person to ask, and it means that if somebody in this city decides to be unpleasant about a card that came out of a printer's counter, there is one address instead of a category, and a category is what gets taken apart."

"**And it means anybody can ask you whether you said a thing,**" said Marek Senn.

"**Yes. That is the price.** I have spent nine years making sure that nobody in this city could ask me anything, and I have just agreed to be askable once a year, on a Wednesday, about a telephone call, and I have done it in a room with a projector in it because the man who asked me asked me in a room and not in a stairwell."

---

Corin Vasska wrote the nine words on the back of his own hand under the ones that were already there, and then he put a pen on the table and pushed it four inches towards her, and did not say anything, which took him a visible effort.

She wrote her own name under them.

It took about two seconds and she did not spell it out loud and she did not date it and she did not put anything else on the paper, and when she had finished she turned it round and pushed it back.

"**There is one more thing and it is not a condition, it is a fact about me, and you are going to want to know it in about four years so I am telling you now. If the call comes, I will hold what I am holding. I will not hold anybody else's. There is no version of this where a woman of thirty-five is the thing that holds a ward together because she is the only one who answered the telephone.**"

"**That is not what a limited notice means.**"

"**It is what it will come to mean in about four years if nobody writes down what it does not mean, and there is a woman of thirty-one in a building in Bower Street who has been writing that down in a diary for four years and is not thanked.**"

---

She checked the room on the way out, the way she does, and she was at the door in under a minute.

"**One more thing and then I am gone, and I am saying it because the two of you have both spent a term telling people that a name is the cheapest way to close a room, and I want it recorded that I have put a name on a piece of paper in the same fortnight on purpose.**"

"Go on."

"**A name is the cheapest way to close a room when the room is not yours. When the room is yours and you have chosen the name and you can say no to being asked, then the name is the room.**" She had her hand on the door. "You are both going to be asked about that sentence by people who want it to be simpler than it is. Do not let anybody make it a rule."

She did not say the word. She has never used it.

---

The paper went into a folder in a building in Crown Terrace and it is not a minute and it is not on a specification and it has no reference number on it, and in about four years the only two people who will know what the nine words were are a man of forty-four and a man of twenty-two, and one of them will have to look it up in a drawer.

There is no such thing in this city as a person whose job it is to look at a stranger's wrist.

A book in a back room is at forty-four lines. A plate in Bower Street says 2-14 over a room that is 2-07. A number and a four-letter post town are in a left-hand page in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and there are two people in this city who could put a hand on it.

And about eleven people a week went on being taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, on a Friday, at grade three, at the release rate, and the crisis that would have made him necessary lasted nine minutes on a Tuesday in the autumn and is over, and nobody noticed, and the woman who noticed said nine words in a room and then went down a stairwell.

---

*147. Wednesday of week 43, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is about a woman of thirty-five who checked the door on the way out, and about a man of forty-four who has wanted to be asked a question for four years and asked one and was refused in four seconds.*

*What he asked for, and the shape of it.* **A limited emergency notice. One number, one person's name, one sentence, and it does not survive the year. If a room in this district goes on a share and cannot end it, and the person who was in the room is not answerable to anybody in the levy, then somebody outside my money gets one telephone call. That is all.** He said the shape of it out loud before he asked, so that a refusal would be a refusal of a thing with a shape and not of a man being friendly in a corridor, and he said in the same breath that he described it to nine people on the Wednesday of the thirty-seventh and that they minuted that they had been told two things and had done nothing, and that he has read that minute more times than he has read the specification.

*The nine words, said once, and not once since.* They are the only nine words anybody has agreed to in this case and I have not written them out in here, and the reason is that a book is a thing a person can be given, and if they are in this one then there are three of us and not two, and she said two. She did not answer for about four seconds, which from her is an answer, and then she said the cost out loud before he had written it down anywhere, because he has got a specification and a specification is where a sentence goes to have a person attached to it.

*And the price, which is a name she put there herself.* **A notice with no name on it is a thing that goes to a district. A notice with a name on it is a thing that goes to a person, and a person can be found.** I have been unfindable for nine years and it is the only thing I have that is mine. She told him to put her name on it, in her own hand, and not a category, and not the name of a thing she belongs to, and that she has been a category in about nine pieces of paper in this city in four years and every one of them cost somebody else. She wrote it in about two seconds and did not spell it out loud and did not date it and put nothing else on the paper. **A name on it means that in about four years there is a person to ask instead of a category, and a category is what gets taken apart, and it also means anybody can ask her whether she said a thing, which is the price and she has paid it deliberately, on a Wednesday, in a room, because the man who asked her asked her in a room and not in a stairwell.**

*What she refused, and the thing she would not let it grow into.* **If the call comes she will hold what she is holding and she will not hold anybody else's, and there is no version of this where a woman of thirty-five is the thing that holds a ward together because she is the only one who answered the telephone.** She said that is not what a limited notice means, and she said it is what it will come to mean in about four years if nobody writes down what it does not mean, and she named the person who has been writing it down for four years in a diary in Bower Street and is not thanked. She is not in a group, she is not on a specification, she is not a minute, the paper has no reference number on it, and **in about four years the only two people who will know what the nine words were are the two of us and one of us will have to look it up in a drawer.** She has not cut the wire and she is not going to and she does not join anything and she is not thanked.

*And the sentence she gave away at the door, which is the sentence both of us will be asked to simplify.* A name is the cheapest way to close a room when the room is not yours. When the room is yours and you have chosen the name and you can say no to being asked, then the name is the room. Do not let anybody make it a rule. She said it on the way out and she checked the door on the way out, and she has still never used the word, and four seconds is how long she gave him. **Entry 147.** The book is at forty-four lines and does not move before the Wednesday of the week of the forty-fourth. About eleven people a week went on not being rung today, and a woman of thirty-five put her own name on a piece of paper in a folder in Crown Terrace on purpose, and there is no reference number on it, and neither of those two things is going to be noticed by anybody.
