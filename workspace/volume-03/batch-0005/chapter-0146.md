# Chapter 146 — Four Feet Of Shop

Sunniva Bellhouse has run four feet of a street in the Quiet Quarter for nineteen years and she has a sign on it with her name in it in a typeface she paid for out of her own money in her first year, and she has never once had a district in her shop that she did not put the kettle on for.

On the Tuesday afternoon of week forty-fourth a district practice assurance officer of thirty-four was in it with a folder, and an officer of a district practice assurance levy of forty-four was in it without one, and a boy of nineteen was on the till with his coat still on.

---

He had been let out at half past five for four years and he was on the till at ten past two on the Tuesday afternoon because she had told him to stay and had not given him a reason, and when the levy officer came in she said the reason out loud before anybody asked her for it.

"**He stays. There are three people in this shop and two of them are going to say something today and I am not having one of them said to a wall.**"

The boy did not look up from the till. He is nineteen and has been on it four months and has never run the machine.

"You have never run the machine."

"No."

"You are going to."

---

Rosalind Toomes, thirty-four, put the folder on the counter and it was thin, and she said her name first, which is what she does, and then she said the thing that had been sitting on her for a fortnight.

"**It is in a district file. It has been in one since the Monday of the thirty-seventh week. It has a street on it and a shop front on it and the name on the sign, because the name is on the sign, and there is nothing else in it. It has got no payer on it and it has got no boy on it.**"

"Say the last part again."

"**There is no boy on it. Nobody asked for one.**" Rosalind Toomes looked at the boy on the till for a second longer than she needed to. "That is not a protection. That is a form that wanted a contact and did not get one, and if a person had been in a worse mood on a Monday morning I would have got a boy's name out of you in about nine words and I have thought about that more than once."

"You have known since the Monday."

"**Since the Monday evening, at about nine, and I have said nothing to anybody, and I am not going to be praised for it and I would like it written down that I have had a fortnight of it.**"

---

The levy officer of forty-four had come because he had to and said so in the first four seconds, which was more than he had expected of himself.

"**I have been in this shop twice in nineteen years,**" he said, "once about a bin and once about a sign that a woman took down and put back up, and I have got nothing to do with you and I have not come as the levy. I have come because there is a leaflet going out tomorrow to about nine hundred rooms that puts a company's own assurance in front of them as evidence, and the assurance has a page two with a photograph on it, and I have read page two."

He put his hand in his inside pocket and took out a folded sheet and put it on the counter, and it was page two of a six-sided thing, and about a quarter of the page was a photograph about the size of a postage stamp of a card in a plastic sleeve on a lanyard with four steps legible on it.

"And I have got this," said Sunniva Bellhouse, "and I have had it since the Friday of the week of the thirty-first, and it is a blow-up of that page on a machine that used to do the weekly for a school that has shut, and I have had it in a drawer under the counter since that Friday."

She put it down next to the folded sheet.

---

"Do not say anything yet," she said. "Look at it."

Four people in a shop four feet wide looked at two pieces of paper on a counter for about eleven seconds.

"**That is my page,**" she said, "and that is a blow-up of it, and I have put them together and I want three people in this shop to see them at the same time." She put one finger on the photograph and one finger on the enlargement. "Four steps. I have counted them about nine times. They are correct. Every one of them. I have not got a company's stock and I have never handled a company's card and I checked each step against the other three and against the page and against the pack it came out of, and a wrong step looks like a wrong step, and there are four correct ones and they are the four correct ones that everybody in this city has been arguing about for a term and a half."

"And the fifth line."

"**The fifth line is mine and I wrote it in nine minutes on a Friday afternoon and it is not in the picture, because a photograph of a card in a sleeve on a lanyard cannot have a fifth line in it, and it cannot have anybody in it, and a key-holder is a person in a room.**" She straightened up. "I have printed four hundred and thirty-two of them and they went out of a mailbox four streets away in eleven instalments of eleven pounds, and there is a hundred and forty-one pounds in an envelope under the till and there is no name on the envelope and there is no address on the envelope, and I have been sitting on it since the spring and I cannot send it back."

Nobody said anything.

"**That is the whole of the counterfeit and it took me nine minutes and it is the only thing I have ever printed that I have been proud of, and I have not been proud of it once since about the middle of the summer.**"

---

"Say the rest of it," said Sunniva Bellhouse, to Rosalind Toomes, "because you have come in here with a folder and I want to know what a folder is for before I put my enlargement in it."

"The folder is for the street."

"**Then the enlargement goes in it and the boy walks it.**"

"To where."

"**To whatever building it goes to, on foot, in his hand, and he is nineteen and he can walk,**" said Sunniva Bellhouse, "and I am not posting anything to anybody and I am not going to have this come back through a door with a window in it."

The boy on the till said, without stopping, "Can I take it now."

"**No,**" she said. "You can take it now and I have not decided to, and both of those are true and you will find out which one it turns out to have been."

---

Then the levy officer of forty-four did the thing he had come not to do, and he did it out loud, in a shop, to two women and a boy, and he wrote it on the back of his own hand underneath the nine words from a Wednesday afternoon.

"Write this down," he said. "Page two of a six-sided handout, distributed with a district note by this fund, carries a photograph of a card in a sleeve. That photograph is the only source of the four correct steps on four hundred and thirty-two cards printed by hand in this city in six weeks. The company's assurance is not evidence that the practice is sound. The company's assurance is where somebody got the steps for free."

He put the hand flat on the counter.

"**I have said the true sentence and it is a sentence about a page, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I want it noted that a leaflet goes out tomorrow which cites the assurance as proof, and I cannot stop that leaflet, and I have checked twice.**"

"Then what are you doing here."

"I am here because in about four years somebody is going to hold up that assurance and ask whether the officer of the levy read page two," said Corin Vasska. "And I read page two."

---

The boy asked the question at about twenty to four and nobody had asked him anything for four months.

"**Is the other one the same,**" he said. "The real card. The one in the picture. Is it the same as mine."

Nobody answered for four seconds.

"**Yes. It is the same card,**" said Sunniva Bellhouse. "And I have been waiting two weeks for somebody in this city to ask me that, and you are the one who asked, and I am going to tell you the true thing and then I am going to tell you the whole of it, because you are nineteen and you work in here."

She put both hands on the counter.

"**It is the same four steps and the same shape and it is a card in a plastic sleeve and it goes on a lanyard and it is about the size of a postage stamp in the picture. Mine has a fifth line on the back and it says if the word does not come, withdraw and report. Theirs has a fifth line on the back and it says release on the word, and if no word, withdraw and report. Two cards, the same five words, and not one of them the same sentence. And the difference between the two of them is not on either of them, and I have printed four hundred and thirty-two of mine and I cannot tell you where it is, and I have been asked that twice this term by a man who writes things down and I have given him the same answer twice.**"

"So mine is the better one."

"**Mine is the better one,**" she said, "and I know that because a man of thirty-four told me so in a lift on Friday and I have not been able to argue with him about it, and about nine people in one district have one, and there is no mechanism in this city that can take a card back off anybody, and I have known that since the Friday of the thirty-first. And I cannot tell you in one sentence why it is the better one, and I have been trying for a term and a half, and I am not going to improve it by saying it a third time in one afternoon."

---

"And now the part that is mine," she said, "and I am going to say it before anybody asks me, and then nobody is going to ask me."

She took the envelope out from under the till and put it on the counter and did not open it.

"**I am not printing any more of them. There are four hundred and thirty-two out and I cannot get them back and I am not going to pretend that stopping is the same as undoing, and it is not, and everybody in this room knows it is not.**"

"That is the cost," said Rosalind Toomes.

"That is one pound of the cost," said Sunniva Bellhouse. "Here is the rest of it and it is mine and I have worked it out in nineteen years and not in a fortnight. I have been printing for nineteen years for about four hundred people on this street and a school that has shut and two churches, and I have never once had to decide what a piece of paper is for. Somebody hands me a thing and I print the thing. And in the spring I decided what a card was for, and the deciding is not the same as the printing, and the printing is the good part and the deciding is the part that goes in a file."

She squared the envelope.

"**And there is a person who paid me eleven pounds eleven times out of a mailbox and I have not got a name and I have got the money, and if they come back into this shop I am going to have to look at a person I cannot name and decide in four seconds whether to print, and I am fifty-eight and I have not got a better way of deciding than a stranger with a clipboard has.**"

Nobody said anything for a while.

"**I would like somebody to write that down who is not me,**" she said.

---

She telephoned a repair shop in Lattice Ward at about ten past five and said her name and four things and hung up.

The first thing was that she had said a street to a district and had had it in a file for a fortnight and had said nothing.

The second was that a levy officer had read page two.

The third was that there were four hundred and thirty-two of them and no mechanism.

The fourth was a boy's name, said once, and then she said: "**He is not in it and that is luck and not protection, and the only thing I have got to put in a room is a Saturday, and I have told him he is going to run the machine.**"

Then she put the phone down and let the boy out at half past five, four minutes late, which she has never done.

*149. Tuesday of week 44, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is about a printer of fifty-eight with four feet of shop and a hundred and forty-one pounds in an envelope with no name on it, and about a boy of nineteen who asked the only question anybody in this city has asked about the two of them.*

*What she had known and had not said.* **A street in a district file since the Monday of the thirty-seventh week, with the name on the sign, and no payer and no boy, because a form wanted a contact and did not get one.** She has had a fortnight of that and has asked not to be praised for it, and the reason she asked for that is the reason she is telling you at all: nobody in this case has ever thanked the person who said nothing in time.

*The two pieces of paper, and the fact that she put them together herself.* **Page two of a six-sided handout that went out with a district note on a levy officer's money, and a blow-up of it off a machine that used to do a weekly for a school that has shut.** She asked for the officer's copy and she has had hers since the Friday of the thirty-first, and she did not say so for four days. Four steps, counted about nine times, all four of them correct, and she asked for it to be written down that she asked, before anybody asked her where the steps came from, because a printer who cannot say where her steps came from has a different kind of problem from the one she has. **And the fifth line is not in the photograph, because a photograph of a card in a sleeve on a lanyard cannot have a fifth line in it, and it cannot have a person in it, and a key-holder is a person in a room.**

*What the man of forty-four did that he came not to do.* **He put a sentence about a page on the back of his own hand, in a shop, in front of two women and a boy, and the sentence is that the assurance his own fund distributed is where somebody got four correct steps for free.** He said before he said it that it was not in his job and that he wanted it minuted, and nobody was minuting, and he has read that minute more times than he has read the specification. **I did not ask him for it, it was in the room, and I was in a corridor, and I have written down in this book four times this term that a thing gets said in a room and not by me, and this is the first time the thing said has been bigger than me.**

*And the boy, who asked the question nobody had asked.* **Is the real card the same as mine.** She waited two weeks for somebody in this city to ask her that, and the answer she gave him is that it is the same card, and that she cannot say where the difference between the two of them is. **That is the only fact in this entry that is not about a document, and it is the one a room in Crown Terrace is going to have to say out loud in front of about forty people, and she will be standing by a trestle table in that room and she will not be able to argue with any of them.** About nine places in one district have one of hers and no mechanism in this city can take a card back off anybody, and she has known that since the Friday of the thirty-first.

*The cost, said before anybody asked her for it.* **Stopping is not undoing, and everybody in that shop knew it and nobody had said it before her.** Nineteen years of printing for about four hundred people on that street, a school that has shut and two churches, and a decision taken in the spring about what a piece of paper is for, where the deciding is the part that goes in a file and the printing is the part that is the good work. And there is a person who paid eleven pounds eleven times out of a mailbox, and she has the money and not the name, and if they come back into that shop she will have to look at a person she cannot name and decide in four seconds whether to print. **She asked for somebody to write that down who was not her, and the somebody was in a corridor on a Tuesday at grade two, and she does not know that, and I am not going to put it in here either.**

*How it moved.* **The enlargement went into a folder on a counter, on foot, in a boy's hand, to whatever building it goes to, and she said both things at once — he can take it now and I have not decided to — and she did not soften either of them.** The boy asked his question at twenty to four and nobody answered him for four seconds. She telephoned the shop at ten past five and said her name and four things and hung up, and the fourth thing was a boy's name said once, and then she said: he is not in it and that is luck and not protection, and the only thing she has got to put in a room is a Saturday, and she has told him he is going to run the machine. She let him out at half past five, four minutes late, which she has never done. **Entry 149.** The book is at forty-four lines. I was not in that shop, and I did not telephone her, and I have not thanked her, and I am not going to be the person who says this case was about a printer.
