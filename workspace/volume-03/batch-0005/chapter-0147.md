# Chapter 147 — A Ledger Is Not A Book

The Exchange meets at eleven at night in a back room with the shutters up, and there were nine people in it, and there is a tin on the table and the tin is not locked, and there is a four-column book on the table with forty-four lines in it.

The book is not a register and it is not going to become one. The fortieth line is three years old. The rule of the room is that what goes in is the caller's own words, in the first person, unchanged, and a signature at the end, and four minutes an item, and no vote for anybody.

There is a fifth chair against the wall. It has been empty since the spring and nobody has moved it and four people in the room have noticed that it is still there.

---

Bea Nunn is sixty-one and she has not run this room for nine years because anybody asked her to.

She did not do the two minutes. She put a sheet of typing paper on the table instead of the book, and it was blank, and it was not a page out of the book.

"**I am going to say why that is a different piece of paper from that one,**" she said, "**and I have got four minutes, and I am not going to use them on the reason first, I am going to use them on the reason last, and I want that written down as a decision and not as a style.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**There are three of you in this city who have said in nine words what your practice will do and what it will not do, and one of you said hers to me in a corridor and two of you said yours to him in your own building, and not one of the three of you has asked for anything, and I have been sitting in this room since the Wednesday of week forty with a piece of paper in a drawer and I am going to put it on this table.**"

---

The three of them came in at half past eleven and not together.

Sinead Corrigan is thirty-four and has a plastic wallet with eleven rooms on a list of her own that is not a district list, and a file with her name at the top of it, and she is not disciplined. Sera Quill is forty-five and has a braid of tools in her pocket and a letter with one sentence in it that has been a letter for eleven years about nine things. Rafi Pell is twenty-one and has a grey card with two names on it and neither of them is anybody in this room.

Nobody introduced them and nobody voted on anything and nobody in that room was a node in anything, then or afterwards, and there is no record anywhere that says they were.

---

"**The three of you are going to write this yourselves, in your own hand, in the first person, one each, about your own practice and not about anybody else's,**" said Bea Nunn. "**One page, three hands. I am going to rule it. I am not going to type it and I am not going to take it and I am not going to put it in the book, and I am going to say again that the book is the book.**"

"And the fifth line."

"**There is no fifth line,**" said Bea Nunn, "**and I have looked for four days for a fifth line and I am telling you that I did not find one and that the reason I did not find one is that the three of you have each written sentences and a sentence is not a fifth line, and if a sentence were a fifth line then every leaflet anybody in this city is carrying would be a hinge and not one of them is.**"

---

Sinead Corrigan wrote first, and she wrote in a small upright hand with the letters close together the way a person writes when they are writing something they intend to be read by a stranger.

It said, in the first person, that any person might bring an ending to her and not a card and not a form, and that she would do it or not, and that if she would not she would say why in about nine words and the reason would be about the share and not about the person, and that nothing goes on anybody else's form including the nine-field one, and that she would not put anybody's name at the top of it including her own.

Then she put the pen down and said: "**If anybody asks me in about four years who started this I am going to say that a woman of thirty-five said no to a hire place in a café in the Quiet Quarter, and that I read about it in about four thousand leaflets. I am not going to say when. The when is the part that dates it, and a dated thing is a thing somebody can check, and I have had four of those this term.**"

---

Sera Quill wrote in block capitals, one letter under another, the way she writes a warning on the back of a form, and she wrote it on the same sheet and below the nurse, and it said, in the first person, that a mechanic would take an ending out with his hands in four seconds, and that he would write the name of the person who asked in his own hand on the back of his own job sheet and his own name underneath, and that the job sheet went in the parts cage and stayed there and was not on a system and had never been on a system.

Then she said: "**I have spent eleven years writing things that are not records and a man has just written one on a piece of paper in a back room, and the difference is that this one says my name under his, and if anybody comes through that door in about four years with a folder I want it known that it is on a piece of paper and not on a system, and that is a worse place to look and a better place to be.**"

---

Rafi Pell wrote third, and he wrote on the back of an invoice with a biro, leaning forward, in a courier's hand that goes downhill to the right.

It said, in the first person, that any ending might be brought to a yard off a service road and taken to a door in person and waited for, and that if nobody answered he would leave it in a hand on the step and telephone once from the cab, and that if it was not answered he would leave a second one the next working day and would not leave a third, and that any person might refuse to receive it, and that there was no card and no form and no signature.

He did not sign it either.

"**That is on the back of an invoice,**" he said, "**because that is the only paper I have ever had in my hand that was already mine, and I have not signed it and I have not put my name at the top of it, and if anybody in this city in about four years wants to know who wrote the third one, they will have to come and ask me, and I will say.**"

"And nobody took a vote about you."

"**Nobody took a vote about anything tonight,**" said Rafi Pell. "**There is a sheet of paper and three hands on it and if you counted the hands you would find a number and the number is not a decision.**"

---

Bea Nunn said the reason last, and she said it in eleven seconds, and it is a rule and it has been there since before she ran the room.

"**That is a ledger. That sheet is a ledger. The reason it can exist is that a practice can write about itself in the first person and nothing else can.**"

She put a finger on the four-column book.

"**And this is a book of what a caller said. A caller is a person. The rule is that the caller's words go in, in the first person, unchanged, with a signature at the end, and there is a signature at the end because a person signs their own words and a practice cannot.**"

She looked at the two pieces of paper on the table.

"**So: three practices, one page, three hands, no signatures, no votes and no names at the top. And forty-one people, a plate on a wall, a girl with a pad and a man of fifty-one who went into a building for a leg and came out of it on a Thursday afternoon, and not one of them is standing in this room, and I have still got a first person and I am still not going to write about a wall.**"

Nobody said anything for a while.

"**I have refused to enter a line twice in nine years and I was wrong both times, and I have come close three times this term, and this is the second time this month that I have come close, and the difference between the two is that the second one had three people in it who could each have gone home.**"

She squared the three hands and left the book closed.

---

Marek Senn was in the corridor outside from half past seven with a docket in his hand, and a piece of card two inches by three was in the inside pocket of a jacket over a chair in Lattice Ward, four miles from where he was standing.

He did not come in. There is a rule about that room and it is not about him and it has never been about him: the caller brings it, and he has not had a caller in nine weeks.

At ten to midnight the door opened and the three of them came out into the corridor one at a time, and each of them said one sentence to him on the way past, and none of the three said his name.

The nurse said: **Any person. Not a card.**

The repairer said: **Four seconds, and I write it on the back of my own sheet.**

The courier said: **I will not leave a third.**

He asked none of them a question. He has been asked that about by a man of twenty-one in a yard off a service road and he has not got a better sentence about it than the answer he gave then, which was that he was not going to be the reason it had a name on it.

They went down the stairs. The corridor was eleven metres long and there is a window at the end of it and the shutters on the room's window are still up.

---

Nobody has adopted the sheet. Nobody is bound by it. Nobody in this city is on a distribution for it, there is no copy of it, it is not filed anywhere, and it is in a drawer in a back room and it is not on a system.

There are three visible routes to end a narrow share and none of them belongs to a man with a lanyard, and none of them belongs to a company, and the hidden default has not been restored, and a book in another building is at forty-four lines with a hole in line forty-four.

---

*150. Wednesday of week 44, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is about a woman of sixty-one with a pen who said a rule out loud at ten to midnight for eleven seconds, and about three hands on one sheet of typing paper and a fourth column of nobody's.*

*What was on the table, and what was not.* **Nine people, a tin that is not locked, a four-column book at forty-four lines with the fortieth line three years old, a fifth chair against the wall that nobody has moved since the spring, and one sheet of typing paper that was not a page out of the book.** She did not do the two minutes. She said at the top of her four minutes that the reason was coming last and that she wanted that written down as a decision and not as a style, and the reason turned out to be that a sheet of typing paper and a page out of that book weigh the same and are not the same object, and nobody in that room could say why until she said it.

*What a ledger is, and what having one costs.* **Three practices, one page, three hands, no signatures and no names at the top, and a rule that a practice can write about itself in the first person and nothing else can.** The cost is in the part nobody argued about, which is that a practice that has written down what it will do has to go on doing it, and one of the three has said out loud that she would rather be a poster that admits it, and the other two have said nothing this term about what happens to them on a Thursday. **The book still has a hole in line forty-four, there is still a first person in that room, and she is still not going to write about a wall.**

*And what that room did not do.* **Nobody introduced them, nobody voted on anything, nobody in that room was a node in anything then or afterwards, and there is no record anywhere that says they were.** If you counted the hands on that sheet you would find a number, and the number is not a decision, and a man of twenty-one said that in the corridor afterwards and was right. She looked for a fifth line for four days and did not find one, and the reason she gave is that a sentence is not a fifth line, and that is the only thing in this case that three people who have never met each other can all be right about at the same time.

*What I did, which was nothing.* **I was in the corridor from half past seven with a docket, and a piece of card two inches by three was in the inside pocket of a jacket over a chair in Lattice Ward four miles from where I was standing, and I did not go in, and the reason is a rule and the rule is not about me: the caller brings it and I have not had a caller in nine weeks.** At ten to midnight the three of them came out one at a time and each of them said one sentence and not one of the three said my name. Any person, not a card. Four seconds and I write it on the back of my own sheet. I will not leave a third. **I asked none of them a question, and a man of twenty-one asked me in a yard off a service road in the week of the forty-second whether I was going to be the reason it got a name on it, and I have not got a better sentence about that than the answer I gave him then.** **Entry 150.** The book is at forty-four lines and it does not move before the next Wednesday. Nobody has adopted the sheet, there is no copy of it, it is in a drawer in a back room, and it is not on a system.
