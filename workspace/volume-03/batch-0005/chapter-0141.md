# Chapter 141 — The Cheapest Way To Close A Room

A document that goes out of a building and comes back, or goes out and does not, has a front sheet, and on the front sheet there is a box, and the box on this one is headed **name of the person this concerns**, and the box is empty, and it is empty because a man of forty-four said no to it on the Friday of the week of the forty-first and has spent two days since then being asked whether he meant it.

Five sets of the five documents were already on the trestle table in a room above a print works in Crown Terrace on the Monday afternoon of week forty-two, one set for each of five things that were about to be put in front of about eleven thousand registered working premises with a levy note.

---

Corin Vasska is forty-four and he has been the officer of that levy for four years, and he did not lie about any number in it, and he has wanted to be asked a question for four sessions, and he has now been asked one and the question was no.

"There is a chair in this building," he said, "whose office signs about eleven of these a year, and she has read the front sheet, and she has asked me on the Friday whether the box is going to be filled, and I said it was not my box, and she said it is on my document."

"It is on your document."

"It is on my document because I drafted the covering paragraph," said Corin Vasska, "and the covering paragraph is the only reason there is a document. That is the whole of my administrative guilt this quarter and I would like it minuted."

---

Nobody minuted it.

The chair asked him what to put in it, and he said the true thing, and the true thing is the reason he said no.

"**A name is the cheapest way to close a room and it costs the room nothing.**"

He put two fingers on the front sheet where the box was.

"**About forty people have written in since the spring asking who is dealing with this, and one of them has a frame and a stick and a leaflet on his own table, and one of them is a woman who is going to be telephoned about a case study in about six rooms a week, and both of them have been told in four thousand leaflets that a district is dealing with it. A file with a name on it can be closed by asking the person whether they are still working on it. A file with no name on it can only be closed by somebody deciding it is finished, and there is nobody in this building who is going to be asked to decide that, and I have worked out in about nine days that this document is going to be about four hundred people's names and not about a technique.**"

Somebody at the table said: "**It has got to say somebody.**"

"**It has got to say somebody, and it is not going to be me, and it is not going to be anybody in this room, because there is not one of us whose name on that sheet would be true.**" He squared the stack. "The moment it has got a name on it, the name is the case, and in about four years somebody in a district office is going to open a folder with my name at the top of it and read the covering paragraph I wrote and not one of the five documents in it."

---

The chair said: "Then it goes to the next name on the list."

And the next name on the list was not a name.

It was a job title. District practitioner, release callout, two days a week, grade three, and the job title is in about four hundred pay packets in one district, and one of those four hundred pay packets is in a building on the far side of the city from the print works, and nobody chose the man who holds it, and the reason nobody chose him is that no human being looked.

Corin Vasska did not put the job title in the box. He left the box empty and he telephoned the depot and asked for the man by name, and the depot said he was on a job, and Vasska said tell him it is not a job, and he came.

---

Marek Senn was in that room for eleven minutes and he is not in the fund and he has never been on anything the fund buys.

He said the thing he was going to say before anybody had asked him for it, and he said it out loud, and there were nine people at the table and not one of them agreed with him.

"**There is not going to be a name on the front of that,**" he said, "and I am the person you have come for, so I will say it now and you will only have to ask me once."

"**Sit down, and we will go through it in order,**" said the chair.

"**I will stand, because I would like it to be visible that I am not on a panel and that I have not got four minutes.**" He put both hands on the edge of the trestle table. "**And I am not going to give you a reason that helps, because I have got one and it does not help. The reason is that about eleven people a week are going to be handed a card that says the release waits for a man in a lanyard, and the only thing that has ever made that stop being true is one man not being findable, and I have been not findable for a term and a half, and it is the only thing I have got.**"

"**That is about your own situation and not about this document,**" said a man of thirty-eight.

"**It is about the document, because a name on the front of that document is the end of not being findable, and I am the one who would be findable, and there are about nine hundred rooms with my job title on a piece of paper in them already, and I have spent a term being careful with that and a fortnight being careless with it, and I am not going to do it again in a font.**"

Nobody said anything for four seconds.

Then Corin Vasska said, out loud, in a room of nine people, in the same voice he reads things out in:

"**You have said that sentence in nine rooms in three weeks and I have never once heard you say it in a room where it cost you something. This is the first one, and I want you to notice that the reason it has cost you something is that you have a wage.**"

---

And then the chair did the thing that nobody in that room could stop, which was to say that a document cannot go out of a building with two empty boxes on it, and a woman of twenty-six who typed things said that she was not going to invent either of them, and the chair said that somebody would fill them in by hand, and Corin Vasska took a pen off the trestle table and wrote *no name* in the first box and *no name* in the second one in his own hand and put the pen down.

"There," he said. "**That is mine and not yours, and I have done it in front of nine people, and I am going to be the one who gets the letter about it in four months when somebody asks why a levy return went out with two boxes that say no name, and I will write to them and I will not be able to tell them a reason that is worth the paper.**"

"**You could say the reason,**" said Marek Senn. "You could say a name on the front of a document is the cheapest way to close a room."

"**I have been the officer of this for four years and I have just found that out on a Monday afternoon in a room above a print works,**" said Corin Vasska, "**and I have got about eleven hundred letters to write before the end of the quarter and not one of them is about a card, and I am not going to stand here and be praised for writing two words in a box. I am going to be the man who put no name in a box. Put that in a minute if anybody wants it.**"

Nobody wanted it.

---

He went down the stairs at half past four, and a man of thirty-eight was in the corridor outside with two sheets of paper under his arm. He had the expression of a man doing the ordinary Monday-afternoon work of a man who has been handed a thing and told where to take it.

"Are you the name."

"**No,**" said Marek Senn.

The man looked at the sheets.

"Then who is going to answer the forty."
