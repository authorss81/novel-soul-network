# Chapter 143 — A Number And Not An Address

The gate at Meridian consolidation point does not ask who you are, because the booking has already done it, and the booking is a printed page in a folder in the cabin of a van with three columns on it and the company in the middle one.

It was half past seven on the Monday morning of week forty-third and the air on that yard had the smell every yard off that corridor has, which is oil and wet concrete and a heater that has been on since before anybody came.

---

The prefab office had a desk, a chair, a heater, a kettle and a filing tray with about four inches of paper in it, and the tray was still in the way.

Bryn Maw is fifty-eight. He has a carpenter's pencil behind his ear and a site diary on the desk held flat by a beer mat, and he has been on that gate for thirty-one years, and he does not have a job title and is not going to be given one this term.

He got the tray out of the way with his foot because it was in the way, which is how it has been done for four years.

"**You have been in here before,**" he said, "and you have not written anything down, and I have not asked you about it, and I have got about nine other things to do before eleven."

"**I know.**"

"**Then whatever it is, say it in one sentence and I will tell you whether I have got time.**"

---

Marek Senn said the thing out loud, in the office, with the heater on, before anybody had asked him for it, which is what he does.

"**I am going to write the number down, and I am going to say it to you first, and the reason I am saying it to you is that if I write it down and say nothing then there is one person in this city holding it and one person holding a thing is a person, and a person can be found.**"

He put the printout on the desk. Nine pages, squared, page nine on top.

"**And you are the second one, and I have thought about that for a fortnight and I have decided I would rather it was a foreman of fifty-eight who answers a telephone on a wall than a man of twenty-two with a lanyard in nine hundred rooms. That is the whole of the arithmetic. There is nothing in it about courage and I would like it written down in those words.**"

Bryn Maw did not touch the paper.

"**You have had a fortnight,**" he said. "The tray goes in the skip on Friday."

"**I know that as well.**"

"**Then you have not got a fortnight, you have got today, and you have had it since the week you came in with a lamp, and I want to say that I have watched a man leave a tray in the way for four months so that he would have a reason to come back, and I have not said a word about it, and it is not my business and it is also not nothing.**"

---

Page nine had a blank field on it in a typeface a system uses, and about an inch and a half under the field the string *Meridian consolidation point, lot onward* typed in a stores clerk's hand, and about two inches below that, in biro, in a different hand, a box number a man wrote on a printout about two years ago because a man on a wagon had asked where the gear came from.

Under the box number there was a post town in a print so small it was nearly a smudge. It was not Nacre. It had four letters in it.

He took his own book out of the side of his bag. The left-hand page, the day, and the number, and the town, in ink, in his own hand, and then he read the number out loud once, and the town out loud once, and Bryn Maw listened with his hands still on the desk.

"**There,**" he said. "That is yours now and not mine and I have got it out loud in a hut, which is the only addressable place on this site."

"**Yes.**"

"**I am not going to write it in the diary.**"

"**No.**"

"**I will tell you why, because you will assume I have refused you and I have not. A site diary goes in a container when this site shuts for the winter and this is a stranger's address and it is not my yard and I have got nine men whose names are on the front of it, and there is not one line in that book that is not a person.**" He put the pencil behind his other ear. "You have written it in a book that is not a system and that has got no reference on it and that nobody in this city has any reason to ask anybody for. That is the best place anybody has put it in all year and I am not going to be the one who improves it."

---

The cost of it took about four minutes to arrive and it arrived out loud, which is how the things on that site arrive.

"**You have made me a second person holding it,**" said Bryn Maw, "and there is a telephone on that wall and I answer it, and it has been on that wall since before the mobile came and it is the only number in this office that a voice comes out of. I have not got anybody to hand it to. I have not got a wife who would take a thing like that and I have not got a son and I am fifty-eight and I have been in this office with the door propped since about the spring."

"**Then it is a cost and I have put it on you.**"

"**I am not saying it to make you feel it. I am saying it because I have been on this gate thirty-one years and I have watched about four companies put a thing in a file that they should have put in a man's head, and every single one of them came out of an office and none of them came out of a hut.**" He looked at the door. "You have put it in a hut. That is why I am telling you the telephone exists."

---

The lamp was the packer lamp by the second gate and its ballast had been changed, and the fault ticket was closed with a part number on it, and he had told nobody which of the two reasons was the reason.

He told Bryn Maw on the Monday morning, with the heater going, in about four sentences.

"**I booked that lamp in week twenty-two because I wanted an excuse to read page nine. The ballast was going anyway. Both of those are true and I have never said it out loud to anybody, and I am saying it to you because you are the only person I have met this year who would not decide it was an excuse.**"

Bryn Maw did not look up from the diary.

"**It was both,**" he said. "I have watched men book lamps for nine years. A reason does not have to be a good one to be a real one, and a man who tells you only one of them is selling you the one he likes."

---

Then he opened the site diary.

He had never shown it to anybody. He had ten lines in pencil in it, and every one of the ten was the same entry, and he had been writing them since the summer, and his hand got worse as it went because he is fifty-eight and does not have anybody to write it out for.

He turned the book round on the desk and put it in the middle with the printout, and he said, before Marek Senn could say anything:

"**There is no name in this and there is no date and there is no company, and it is the fifth line and not the first four, and it is the same five words as the one you have not taken out of your pocket and it is not the same sentence, and I have known that since about the middle of the summer and I have not said it to a soul because a man who says he has got a fifth line in a hut on this corridor is a man with a fifth line in a hut on this corridor.**"

The line was in pencil and it said *put it down* and the rest of it.

There is a piece of card about two inches by three in an inside pocket on a chair in a repair shop in Lattice Ward with a fifth line on it in a hand that got smaller towards the end.

He did not take it out.

"**I am not going to,**" he said. "You have had one of these in a pocket for four months and you have not taken it out in about nine rooms, and I have read about nine rooms this year where a man with a lanyard has taken something out. I am not going to be the eleventh thing that happens to a sentence in your pocket."

"**It is not the same sentence,**" said Bryn Maw.

"**I know it is not. That is the whole of it and I have known since the summer and so have you.**" He squared the diary. "I have never met him. I am not going to. And I am showing you the book because that tray goes in a skip on Friday and this is the only other piece of paper on this site that has a sentence on it, and I am fifty-eight, and I would like there to be two people in this city who know there are two of us."

They stood on either side of a desk in a prefab office and neither of them said anything else about it, and then Bryn Maw said *tea* and put the kettle on, and neither of them mentioned the card in the pocket once.

---

On the Friday the office was cleared and the tray went into a skip at about eleven and the printout went with it, nine pages, page nine on top, and a man in a fluorescent vest who had a clipboard and could not say what he had come for was given the skip and went away from it.

The number was in a book in Lattice Ward in his own hand with the day against it, and the post town had four letters in it and it was not Nacre, and there are two people in this city who could put a hand on it.

There is no mechanism in this city that could withdraw four hundred and thirty-two cards. There is a plate in Bower Street that says 2-14 on a wall where the room is 2-07 and a district's schedule that agrees with the plate. There is a book in a back room at forty-four lines. There is a man of sixty-one with nine words in a pocket that nobody can be given.

And every one of those is held by a mechanism, and not one of the mechanisms is a person: a schedule that agrees with a plate, a book with four columns in it, a rubber stamp in a visitor column, a system that chose two names out of two job sheets, and a form that would not submit without a source. A tray went into a skip at about eleven this morning with a box number on page nine in it and a man in a fluorescent vest could not say what he had come for, and about eleven people a week are going to go on not being rung.
