# Chapter 145 — The Bottom Of The Page

A Friday is a Friday. Eleven jobs and nine of them in date, the eleven parts said eleven times, and the word at nine, ten and eleven a minute for most of the morning. The count is on the back of a docket in a jacket pocket over a chair in Lattice Ward, and a piece of card about two inches by three is in the same pocket, and he took neither of them out all day.

At half past one in the afternoon he was on a goods lift in Nine Bridges with a man of thirty-four who works nights, and the man had a card in a plastic sleeve on a lanyard and had had it since the spring.

---

"**It says the word comes from somebody,**" said the man. "Not a word. The word."

"It does."

"**So I wait for you.**"

"**You do not wait for me. I am four days a week in a depot on the far side of the city and two days on a Friday and I am not in this building on a Tuesday, and if you have waited for me this term then you have given me a Tuesday for nothing.**"

The man took the card out and looked at the back of it, which was the fourth thing anybody in this city has done in his hearing.

"**It is on the back, then. If no word, withdraw and report.**"

"**Who told you that.**"

"**The fella who gave me the other one.**" He held up a second card. It had four correct steps and a fifth line in a different printing and no name on it and no company and no version, and he had it in a wallet with four more in it. "He said anybody handing you one of those is a front."

Marek Senn looked at the two cards side by side for four seconds and then went back to the lift and finished the job, and he did not say one word about it on the way down, and the man of thirty-four said at the doors that he would see him next Friday and would not wait.

---

The mobile clinic is a converted shop unit in Lattice Ward with a folding screen, nine chairs, a table, a kettle, and a district leaflet on a pinboard she has never taken down.

It is four miles and fifty minutes on foot from the bench he works at and he walked it, and he had a docket in his hand and no reason to be there that he could have put in a return.

She had nine people in and a man of seventy-eight on a drip and a girl of nineteen doing observations on the end bed, and she was in the bay with him and had her back to the door.

He put the docket on the table and waited.

She finished what she was saying to the man of seventy-eight, which took two minutes, and then she came out and looked at the docket and then at him.

"**You have been on a Friday since seven,**" she said. "Say the thing you came to say, and do not put a job in front of it, because you have put a job in front of two things this year and both of them were a man asking you about a card."

---

There was a stack of about four hundred leaflets on the counter and, on top of them, a printed sheet that had come back from the district on the Monday of week thirty-six and was never collected.

It was one page and it was correct.

*A narrow share may be ended at the request of the person it concerns, or at the request of the person holding it. A person holding a narrow share who no longer wishes to hold it does not require a reason and does not require a form. Where a share is ended other than at the request of the person it concerns, the attending service is to record the date and the time, and the reason if one is offered, and is not to require one.*

Four fields. One of them a tick. And no box anywhere on the page for the person who took it off.

She had one of these in front of her with two dates written on it in a hand that was not hers, and she turned it round and put it down in front of him without a word.

---

"**You have read that,**" she said.

"**I read it the week it came back. It is the best document anybody in this case has written in a term and a half and it does not work, and I have not said that to anybody who can do anything about it.**"

"**Say it to me and I will tell you what I can do about it, which is nothing, because it is a district's page and I have two hundred and six of them in a building in Bower Street and sixty-one of those are mine.**" She sat down on the other side of the table. "I have not added a page in a term and a half. I have said that out loud once, to you, in a corridor, and I said it as a thing I was proud of."

"**You have added one.**"

---

She did not pretend that she did not know what he meant.

"**It is in the drawer under the trolley and it is one page and it is mine and it has got my name at the bottom of it and nobody's name at the top of it,**" she said. "It is not the district's. It has got a date on it, because a care-link that does not come to a review is a document and not a practice, and it has got a telephone number on it and a time, and it has got the four fields and one of them is a tick, and there is no box anywhere on it for the person who took the share off, and I have left that out on purpose, and I have known what I was doing since the spring."

"And no name at the top."

"**And no name at the top,**" said Talia Venn. "I have had a term and a half of being findable and this is me writing it down as a decision rather than as a preference, and I would like it noted that the reason is not modesty. The reason is that a page with a name at the top of it goes to a district, and a page with a name at the bottom of it comes back to a person, and in about four years one of those is a policy and the other one is a woman who is on her own rota."

---

He said nothing for nine minutes, which she let him have, and then he said the thing from the Monday.

"**On Monday I stood in a room above a print works and a man of forty-four asked me to put a name on the front of a document and I said no, out loud, and nine people did not agree with me and one of them paid for it. I said the sentence they wrote down afterwards and it is the sentence your page is built on. A name is the cheapest way to close a room.**"

"**Yes. That is why it is on the bottom.**"

"**You have got a better sentence than I have.**"

"**I have got an older one and it costs me about nine chairs a week.**" She turned the page over and there was nothing on the back of it. "**Here is what I am not going to do, and I am saying it now so that you cannot spend a fortnight working out how to ask me. I am not taking that page to Bower Street. I am not taking it to a district. I am not taking it to the woman with the diary and I am not taking it to the fund, and I am not going to give you a copy of it, and if you ask me for a copy I will say no in about nine words and you will not be able to argue with it.**"

---

"**I was going to ask you for a copy.**"

"**I know you were. I have known since half past four, and I have had the answer since the spring and I have not said it because you were on a Friday and you would have heard it as a door closing.**" She put the page in the drawer and shut the drawer. "I will ring the woman of thirty-one on the Thursday because I ring her on Thursdays, and she is a person I have rung for four years for her own reasons and not yours, and when I am in that corridor I am going to say the name of a district and no more, because that is what a corridor is for, and whatever she does with it will be hers, and it will have cost me the one call a week I have got that nobody has anything to do with, and she will not know I spent it on this, and that is the price of the drawer staying shut."

"**She has got a diary and no post and it is four months past the review date.**"

"She has."

"**And you have got two hundred and six pages and no rota and a kettle.**"

"**I have,**" said Talia Venn, "and I have said that sentence in a corridor in the spring and I am saying it again in a converted shop unit in Lattice Ward, and I have noticed that a sentence you have said twice is a habit and not an argument, and I have decided to let it be one."

---

Neither of them said one word about the other two things, and one of those two things was in a drawer under a trolley four miles from that table and the other one was a telephone call on a Thursday that neither of them is going to be thanked for, and about a minute later she said the sentence about the kettle and the two hundred and six pages for the second time in a fortnight and heard herself say it, and he let her get to the end of it before he said anything.

They talked about the four fields and the tick for eleven minutes, and she told him that a woman of twenty-nine with a pad had rung the room in a clinic in Crown Terrace and had said a number out loud and had not been asked to, and he told her that about eleven people a week are being taught to wait for a man who is not there, and that he is the man who is not there, on purpose, and that this afternoon a man of thirty-four held up two cards and told him the other one was better.

She said: "**Then the other one is doing your work and you are doing the fund's work and neither of you is paid for it.**"

"No."

"**You are paid for yours.**"

"**I am paid for mine,**" he said, "and I would like it recorded that a woman of twenty-four told me in a corridor in the week of the thirty-eighth to ask the nurse first and the repairer second and not in a corridor, and I did it in that order, and the only reason there is a third one at all is that she told me the order, and I have not told her that, and she has not asked me for it. And I would like it recorded that a woman of twenty-nine told me in a canteen on the Friday of last week not to call a number a rate, and I have not called it one since, and I have told nobody the number, and the number is on the back of a card in a handbag in this city and about eleven people a week are going on not being rung."

---

He left at four and he did not take the page and he did not ask again, and there is a drawer in a converted shop unit in Lattice Ward with a piece of paper in it that would close a corridor in Bower Street in four minutes, and she has told him where it is, and there are two of us, and he has told nobody else and is not going to, and she has told nobody either and gave him that as the reason the drawer can stay shut.

The master's decision is still not made. The master is in an inside pocket on a chair in a repair shop in this city and the drawer under that bench holds five things, four of them other people's sentences.

He walked the four miles. It took an hour and ten minutes and he did not think about a fifth line for any of it, which is the first Friday in a term and a half on which that has happened.

*148. Friday of week 43, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is about a woman of twenty-four who has two hundred and six pages and has not added one in a term and a half, and about a drawer in a converted shop unit with a page in it, and about a man of thirty-four with two cards who told me the other one is better.*

*What a Friday is, and a lift in Nine Bridges.* **Eleven jobs and nine in date, the word at nine and at ten and at eleven a minute, and the count on the back of a docket next to the card.** A man of thirty-four on nights had two of them in the same wallet, one on a lanyard since the spring and one out of a printer's counter, and he had been told by a man he has never met that the one on the lanyard is a front. **I looked at the two of them for four seconds and said nothing, and the reason I said nothing is not dignity.** The counterfeit is doing my work better than I am, he has got both cards in that wallet, and about nine places in one district have got one of his.

*And a page, on a counter, that has been printed correctly and does not work.* **Four fields, one of them a tick, and no box anywhere on it for the person who took the share off.** It came back from a district on the Monday of week thirty-six and was never collected, and it is the best document anybody in this case has written in a term and a half and it does not work, and the reason it does not work is the same reason her own page does not work, and she wrote hers in the same spring with the same gap left in it on purpose.

*Her page, and where her name is on it.* **One page, in a drawer under a trolley, hers, with her name at the bottom of it and nobody's name at the top of it, and a date and a telephone number and a time, and the four fields, and no box.** She has not added a page in a term and a half and she said that once in a corridor as a thing she was proud of, and she has added one. The reason is not modesty: a page with a name at the top goes to a district, and a page with a name at the bottom comes back to a person, and in about four years one of those is a policy and the other one is a woman who is on her own rota. **And she has left out the one field that would have made it work, and she left it out on purpose, and she told me that inside four seconds, and I have not got a better sentence about it than the one she gave a nurse in the autumn.**

*What I did not do, and what it has cost.* **I did not take the page and I did not ask again.** There is a drawer in a converted shop unit in Lattice Ward with a piece of paper in it that would close a corridor in Bower Street in four minutes, she has told me where it is, and there are two of us. What it has cost is not nothing, and I had it written down as nothing until tonight: it has cost her the one call a week she has got, and it has cost me a fact I cannot use and cannot put down, and neither of those is a good reason. I wrote that sentence down in a corridor in the week of the thirty-eighth, about two things, and I am writing it down again tonight about two different things, and both times it is true and both times I would rather it were not in here at all. We talked about the four fields and the tick for eleven minutes, and about a woman of twenty-four who gave me the order of three doors in a corridor and has never been told what the order was for, and about a woman of twenty-nine with a pad who rang a room in a clinic in Crown Terrace and said a number out loud having not been asked to. She said the other card is doing my work and I am doing the fund's work and neither of us is paid for it, and I said I am paid for mine. I walked the four miles. It took an hour and ten minutes and I did not think about a fifth line for any of it. **Entry 148.** The book is at forty-four lines.
