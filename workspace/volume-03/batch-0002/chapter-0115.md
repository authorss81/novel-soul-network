# Chapter 115 — Fee Paid

The Friday was the same as every Friday, which is the sentence Marek Senn had been trying to stop writing since the Friday of week thirty-one.

Eleven jobs. About nine of them with a card in date. **Four of the eleven were men and women whose card had expired or had never been issued, and there was no course on a Friday, and they were not on the rota and they were not on the board and they were in about four rooms anyway.** The eleven parts in eleven seconds, eleven times. The word at nine, ten and eleven a minute between about ten past nine and about two o'clock, counted on the back of a docket because counting is the one thing in his own head the word has not managed to put a different end on. One job in Nine Bridges at about eleven in the morning where he talked over a woman for four seconds because he could not wait, and apologised, and she said nothing at all, which was the correct thing for her to do and the worst thing that has happened to him this term.

At half past five he sat in the cab and wrote the docket out in a hand he could read in the morning.

*Fri wk 33. Eleven. Nine in date. Four not, no Friday course, not on the board, in the rooms anyway. Counted 9/10/11. Talked over a person at eleven and said sorry and got nothing back.*

Then he went to a room in Crown Terrace with eleven folding chairs at the front and forty more stacked against the back wall, and a microphone on a stand, and a table by the door with about four hundred leaflets on it.

---

About seventy of the ninety came, and there was a man of fifty-eight in a fleece by the door counting them in, because a levy session in a district venue in Crown Terrace is not a lecture and it is not a class and it is not a meeting, and the man in the fleece had been sent by the committee and did the door and went home.

Corin Vasska sat in the fourth chair and had a folder and a lanyard of his own and a glass of water he had not drunk.

"**Right,**" he said. "Three things before anybody stands up. One: this is the third of eleven and it is called *Who to trust in a practice*, and the money for it comes out of a levy on about eleven thousand registered working premises, and forty of you are in this room, which is about one and a half per cent of the people who pay for it, which is normal for anything free in the evening."

Nobody laughed. It is not a funny number to the people who pay it.

"**Two: I am the officer for the levy and I wrote the specification and section four of the specification is the line that got this series built, and I am going to read that line out loud in this room, because I have read it about nine hundred times and I have never read it to the people it is about.**"

He read it out. It took forty seconds and it was the sentence a man of fifty-two had said in a market in Lattice Ward, word for word, and Vasska said so before he started, in two sentences, flat, without any performance at all, and the room went two seconds quieter, and that was the second time in this case that eleven or seventy people had gone two seconds quieter.

"**Three. There is a fee for the next speaker and I am going to say the number of it out loud, and Corin, and I am going to say that the number is not mine, and I am going to say why the number is not mine.**"

He sat down.

---

Peter Amado was fifty-one and he had a grey jacket and a notebook and glasses he took off and did not clean and put back on, and he had the look of a man who has said a thing in a market and been asked to say it in a room and has not decided whether the room is worse.

He took four minutes to get to it, and three of those minutes were him looking at the floor in front of the first row, and in the third minute he said: "**I am going to read it rather than say it, and if anybody objects to reading then I will say it.**"

Nobody objected.

"*There is a technique being handed round in this city and nobody is putting their name to it.*"

He shut the notebook.

"**That is the whole of it. That is what I said in a market in the week of the twenty-fourth, in a district I am not going to name, to about nine people, and eleven of them went two seconds quieter, and I have been getting letters about it since and about four of those letters are from people I have never met.**"

"And is it true," said somebody in the fourth row.

"**It is true,**" said Peter Amado. "I want to be careful about how I say that, because in a room like this a man who says *it is true* sounds like a man who has come to sell you a thing. It is true in the way that a thing is true when you cannot get to the bottom of it. I have been in this trade for thirty-one years and I have never seen a practice handed round with a fourth step off it and no name on it, and I have heard about it from four people, and three of them are people I trust and one of them is a stranger who telephoned me."

"Who is it," said somebody.

"**No. And this is the part I get to be honest about: in a room of seventy people, which is the wrong room to say that in. If I give you a name you will write it down, and in about four years that name is going to be in a document that somebody did not write.**"

"Then what is the point of you being here," said a voice at the back.

---

"**That is fair,**" said Peter Amado, "and I have got an answer and the answer is going to make half of you unhappy, and I am going to ask for the microphone to be switched off for it, and Corin can decide whether to switch it off."

Corin Vasska looked at him for a second and then reached over and turned the volume down on the amplifier until the room went flat.

"**Say it.**"

"**The line in the specification that got this series built is mine.**"

The room was completely flat and eleven seconds of it.

"**Not the words,**" said Peter Amado. "The sense. All of it. Unofficial sharing, techniques held privately, disconnection being sold as safety. I said that in a market in the week of the twenty-four in nine sentences and about four hundred people have been told since then that a man of fifty-two said it and he was in a district jacket and he was right. I was the man. I was not fifty-two, I was fifty, and I was not in a district jacket, I had a coat on, and I was not right about all of it, and I have not been able to say any of that in a room until now."

He put his hands flat on his knees.

"**And here is the part that is the actual part. I said three things in that market. One: a technique is being handed round with nobody's name on it. Two: nobody can trace where it came from, and I said that as a complaint and not as a finding. Three: and this is the one that has traveled, and I have read it on nine pieces of paper since, and it is not what I said — if a technique is being handed round with nobody's name on it and nobody can trace it, then every company that touches it looks like the author of it.**"

"Nobody has said that here," said Corin Vasska.

"**Nobody has said it here,**" said Peter Amado. "It is on a typed page that went through a letterbox in Lattice Ward in the week of the thirty-first, and it is on four of these leaflets in a different wording, and I have not put a name to it and I have not rung the people who have, and I have had nine weeks of it."

He looked up for the first time.

"**So there is a sentence on that table by the door that I did not say, and there is a sentence in that specification that I did say, and the two of them are the same fear with the truth taken out of it, and the one with the truth taken out of it is the one that is going round, and that is not a conspiracy, it is a thing that happens to sentences on their way through about four hundred and forty institutions.**"

"Why are you telling us this in a room you are being paid for," said a woman in the second row.

"**Because the specification is honest,**" said Peter Amado. "That is the difficulty and it is the whole difficulty and I want it in a minute. About about eleven thousand premises pay a levy into a fund and the fund is run by nine people and the money is counted and the money is real and the officer who has written that line into section four has never once been in a bay in his life and has told me so himself, twice."

"That is a compliment," said Corin Vasska.

"**It is a fact and you have taken it well and I am going to leave it there.**"

---

Corin Vasska turned the amplifier back up and Peter Amado sat down for ninety seconds and then got up again, which nobody had asked him to do.

"**There is one more thing and it is the one I have thought about for about a week, and if I do it now I will regret it in nine seconds.**"

"**Do it now,**" said Corin Vasska, "because if you do it in nine seconds you will not do it at all."

"**I went to a gate.**"

Marek Senn was standing at the back with a leaflet in his hand and stopped breathing, which is not a thing he chose to do.

"**On the Meridian corridor, on a company site, in the middle of last term, on a Saturday, in a hut with a kettle in it. There is a man of sixty-one in that hut who has been there four years and who has never once been asked about the card on the control post six metres inside the shutter, and I asked him. I wrote the nine words out on a bit of card off his own pad and I photographed it and I told him he did not need a copy of it, because it was on the company record and it had been there before I was born.**"

"**And you were right to say that,**" said somebody.

"**Was I,**" said Peter Amado. He put his hand flat on the lectern. "I do not know whether I was right, and I have not gone back, and that is the part I would like a room of seventy people to take out of here. There is a man of sixty-one in a hut on that corridor who had a thing written down in his own handwriting because a stranger asked him to write it down, and I then took the reason for him to keep it, and there is now a bit of card in his pocket with nine words and a Saturday on it, and whether he has still got it is a thing I have had nine weeks to find out and have not."

"**He has got it,**" said Marek Senn.

Every head in the room turned round, including two people who had been in the room for an hour.

He did not take a step forward and he did not raise his voice and he said the only thing he could say in a room where seventy people had just gone quiet on a sentence.

"**I have seen it. I have not spoken to him since the week of the thirty-first. I am not going to say anything else about it, and the reason is that I have said nothing to four people in a term and a half and I have not got a better reason than that.**"

Peter Amado looked at him for four seconds, and then at the man in the fleece by the door, and then back at the room.

"**Then we are two,**" he said.

---

The question from the floor came at twenty to nine and it came from a woman of twenty-nine who had come because her employer paid for the course, and who was the person the session had been built for, and who did not know that until Corin Vasska said it in his introduction and made everybody laugh for four seconds.

"**Can I ask a practical one.**"

"**Please.**"

"**How do I tell the difference,**" said Marguerite Okpara, "between a card somebody handed me in a car park and a card somebody handed me in a corridor with a lanyard on it?"

Nobody answered for eleven seconds.

"**I cannot,**" said Peter Amado. "I have been asked that four times since the middle of last term and the answer I have given four times is that I cannot, and I have given it in a slightly worse way each time."

"Can anybody."

"**No.**" said Corin Vasska, and he said it as an officer of a levy and not as a man with an opinion, and he had a folder open and he put his finger in it. "No, and that is the fourth session, and the fourth session is on the Thursday of the week of the thirty-six, and it is the one where the committee has agreed to put something on the panel that is not a name, and I am not going to say tonight what it is, because it is not written yet and because nine people in this city would like to know and one of them is in this room."

Nobody asked again.

And a woman of fifty-two, in the fourth row, who had said the sentence in a market in the week of the twenty-four and who had been asked twice since to say it on a stage and had said no twice, said the only thing she said all evening, which was: "**Then somebody is going to be told a job title and it is not going to be me,**" and Peter Amado turned round and looked at her and said, "No," and she nodded, and that was the whole of the conversation the two of them ever had.

---

The fee was a hundred and forty pounds and travel, and he said so twice without being asked, and the room did not think that was a lot, and he told them what it was.

"**It is eleven weeks of a gas bill,**" he said. "I have got a wife and a boiler and a notebook and this is the third time I have been asked to stand in a room and say one sentence, and I said yes to the first two because I said the first one for nothing and I have been asked about it since, and I said yes to this one because a levy is a real thing and a fee paid out of a levy is honest money, and I want that said in a room where seventy people are sitting."

"Somebody ought to say it," said somebody.

"**A great many people ought to say it and four of them have,**" said Peter Amado. "And I am the one who is standing here and I have got about a hundred and forty pounds' worth of the answer and the answer is a boiler, and I would like somebody to write that sentence down somewhere, and I have noticed that I would like it written down somewhere, and I have noticed that wanting a thing written down is a habit, and I have not got time tonight to look at the habit."

---

In the corridor afterwards there were nine people and a man in a fleece and a stack of chairs and a microphone on a stand that nobody had switched off.

"**Would you say it,**" said Peter Amado, "if they paid you."

"No."

"**No. Right.**" He was looking at the stack of chairs and not at Marek Senn. "I want to say the next part properly, because I said it badly in there. That is not a moral difference between us. That is a fee. If a district had offered me four hundred and forty pounds I would have said the same four words and thought about it at home on the way, and I would have thought about it harder than a hundred and forty because four hundred and forty is rent, and there is no version of me that is a person who says no to rent."

"That is the most honest thing anybody has said to me in a term."

"**It is the cheapest thing anybody has said to you in a term, and you have had a week of my sentences, so you have lost the ability to tell them apart, and that is not my fault and it is the job.**" He put his notebook in his pocket. "You are on the fourth one."

"I know."

"**Are you.**"

"**I have read a line in a specification with my job on it and no name, dated the Thursday of week thirty-one, and a manager of fifty-two has read it and told me it checked and cannot do anything about it, and I have done nothing about it for ten days, and there is a committee in about four weeks and I have no way to reach it.**"

"**Then that is what you are on,**" said Peter Amado. "And I have said yes to that one as well, and I do not know what the sentence is going to be, and I would like to say to you that I have started writing it out in the notebook and I have got four lines and I have crossed out three of them, and the three I crossed out were worse than what is in the specification, and I have been paid nothing extra for those three."

The man in the fleece was stacking the eleventh chair.

"**Go on, then,**" said Peter Amado. "Go and be a door. I have read about it."

---

He walked to the bus with a leaflet in his hand that he did not have at seven o'clock, and the leaflet had a stylised open hand on it and a thumb and a forefinger about a millimetre apart, and eleven session titles, and *fee paid*, and a fund's address, and about nine hundred of the district's own rooms were going to get one of these on a distribution that Corin Vasska had described as routine in a corridor, and the one he was holding had been through four thousand pockets and a market in Lattice Ward and a fish market and a yard and a woman's handbag in Nine Bridges before it got to him.

He read the specification's line on it in the streetlight and put it back in his pocket, and did not say it out loud, because he had said it out loud once in front of about four hundred and six people in the last week of the term that had ended, and had been called nervous, and had been told *love*, and had not been believed, and had said he would not say it twice.

It was nine minutes' walk to the bus and he said it once, in his own head, without a word on the end of it, and counted the eleven folding chairs in the room behind him instead.
