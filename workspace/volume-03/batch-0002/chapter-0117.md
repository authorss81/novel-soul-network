# Chapter 117 — A Heading About Safety

The handout came round with the leaflets on the Friday, in the middle of the bundle, and it was about four hundred copies of six sides with a strip of the fund's letterhead down the side of page one, and it had a heading on page four that eleven people in a building in Bower Street saw over the course of Monday, because eleven people is what a ward is on a Tuesday.

*Shared rooms, safe practice: a case from Bower Street.*

It was not a bad heading. That is the thing about it and it is going to be the thing about it for years.

**The case was Roy Amiss.** Fifty-one. Eleven days in a bed on the Friday of the week of the thirty-second, in a ward on the second floor of a building in Bower Street, with a frame on the left leg and a card for six of the rooms in the building and nine courses and no fourth beat of his own anywhere. It said that on the Friday a narrow care-link had been set up so that a person who could not answer could still be connected to a lifting practice he had used for six years. It said that the counterparty on that share was the patient's employer, under a compliance clause of nine years' standing, and it said the words *the patient's employer was a party to the arrangement* in a sentence that was approving, in a list of things that had gone right, with a full stop at the end of it.

It also said, in a small line under a small heading: *Identifiers for this case were supplied by the attending service.*

Nobody wrote that line to be cruel. That is the whole of it. Somebody wrote that line to make a form look complete.

---

Roy Amiss was in bay four with his frame against the bed and a stick he did not need yet, and he had the handout, and he had had it since about eleven o'clock the previous morning, when a man of thirty-one from his own firm had brought one in and had stood at the end of the bed for nine seconds and then gone to find a nurse.

"I did not know," said Roy Amiss. "I want that said first, because everybody in here is going to assume I knew. I did not know. He came in with it and he said *have you seen this* and I said give it me, and he went out, and I read page four."

"Have you telephoned your firm."

"**I telephoned my firm at about two o'clock and I asked them whether they knew that they were in it, and a woman I have never spoken to read me a clause of a contract out loud, and it is in there, and she read it out twice, and she was extremely polite, and then she said the sort of thing a company says when it is going to have to look at itself, which is that they would be in touch.**" He put the handout face down on the blanket. "Nine years. I signed it in nine minutes. I have said that sentence to about four people in eleven days and I am not going to say it again today."

---

Ianthe Roke came in at ten past ten with the diary under her arm and stopped in the doorway, which she had never done.

"**Mr Amiss. I am going to say one sentence and then I am going to ask you a question and you are going to be cross with me, and you should be.**"

"Say it."

"**I did not fill in the form.**"

"Then who did."

"**A district nurse of thirty-four called Sinead Corrigan, and she filled it in on the Friday of last week at eleven in the morning while I was on the ward round, and she filled in the identifiers because a patient was in the room and the identifier was available, and she put her own name in the box at the bottom because the box at the bottom is the box that says who did it.**"

Somebody in the bay said *oh*.

"**Say it out loud,**" said Ianthe Roke. "Somebody in this bay has just said *oh*, and I have heard that noise about nine times in about four years, and it means somebody has understood something, and I would like to know who, because I would like to know whether it is me or whether it is her."

Nobody said anything, which told her it was her.

"**Right,**" she said. "Then here is the second sentence, which is the one that is mine. He is entitled to a review, and a review is a telephone call, and there is no post in this city whose job it is to make that call, and so the person who makes it is me, and I am going to make it in four minutes, and I am going to make it in this bay, and I would like the bay to be a bay and not a meeting."

She looked at him.

"**Do you want the share off.**"

Roy Amiss thought about it for four seconds, which is longer than anybody expected and shorter than anybody wanted.

"**Yes,**" he said. "Yes, take it off, and I want to say the thing I have been saying for eleven days to anybody who stands here, which is that I would have agreed to it if anybody had asked me, and I have not been able to make anybody hear it, and I would like it written down, and somebody told me on the Friday that it would not be written down anywhere."

"It will be written down."

"**Where.**"

"**In the diary,**" said Ianthe Roke. "With the date on it. That is all it has ever been: a diary with a date in it. And I will tell you the truth about a diary, which is that nobody reads it, including me, and that is not a complaint, that is the design, and the design is a date and a telephone number and neither of those is stored anywhere, which is why I am telephoning you instead of looking it up."

She telephoned him. It took forty seconds and it came off in four, and she wrote one line in the diary with the date on it, and nobody read it.

---

Marek Senn was in the corridor at eleven o'clock and she came out and said, before he had got a sentence out:

"**No, and I am telling you no about four things and you have not asked me one of them, and I would like to do them in an order that saves us both nine minutes.**"

"Go on."

"**One. You are not entitled to a record and I told you that on the Tuesday of week thirty-two and it has not changed. Two. There is no post above me whose job it is to make that call, and there was no post above me on the Tuesday of week thirty-two either, and you have now been told that twice by two people and I would like it to stop being news.**"

"That is not what I came for."

"**Then it is three. You are not a party. There is a man in bay four with a share that was with his employer, and the employer is a party, and the room is a party, and the woman who filled in a form is a party by nine seconds of handwriting, and you are none of those, and being the man in this case is not a standing, and I know that is expensive to hear and I am telling you anyway.**"

"I was not asking for a standing."

"**You were not,**" said Ianthe Roke. "That is the fourth thing and it is the one I have thought about for a minute and a half, and here it is: you were not told first. It was known in this building at eleven o'clock this morning and I found out at ten past ten and I did not telephone you, and I want to say out loud that that was not an accident, and I want to say why, and the reason is that you would have come, and if you had come then I would have had to let you stand in that bay, and about nine people would have seen a man in a lanyard come into a room where a share had just come off a bed, and that is a thing that can be referred to in about four years as a man in a lanyard being in a room."

"That is correct."

"**Thank you. It is correct and it cost me ninety seconds of not being decent and I would like it noted that I noticed.**"

---

Sinead Corrigan came at half past two with a folder and did not sit down.

She was thirty-four and she had been a district nurse for nine years and she ran the ward leaflet circuit for three buildings, and she had been to one of the evening talks in the middle of last term, and on the Wednesday of week thirty-two she had stood in a corridor over the Saltmarket and asked a woman of sixty-one with a key on a string whether she could sit in a room and listen, and had been told no, and had written her name down instead, and had come as a person who came.

"**I filled in the form,**" she said. "I am not going to start anywhere else."

"Tell me about the form."

"**It came round on the Thursday of week thirty-two in a tray with nine other things in it and I was on the circuit and I asked Ianthe whether they were going to fill it in and she said no, and she said it the way people say no when they mean it, and I left it.**" She opened the folder and did not take anything out of it. "Then on the Friday at eleven I came back because the office wanted the returns before the session and there was a woman in bay four who could not answer and the identifier was on the board, and the form said *patient identifiers, if available*, and they were available, and I am the person holding the pen."

"And the box at the bottom."

"**My name. It says *completed by*. It is a form and a form has got a box for the person who filled it in, and I have filled in about four hundred forms in nine years and I have never once thought about the box.**"

"Who asked you?"

"**Nobody. That is the part I cannot get past. Nobody asked me. A clerk in a district office sent it round and a woman of thirty-one in this building said no to it and I said yes to it on my own, on my own initiative, on a Friday, because I was the one holding the pen and the identifier was on the board and the office wanted it back that day.**"

"**You have been told twice you have no standing,**" said Marek Senn.

Sinead Corrigan looked at him.

"**Who told you that.**"

"**Nobody. And that is the ninth time in a fortnight that I have been the wrong person in a room, and I have decided to stop apologising for it, and I am not going to tell you who told you or not.**"

"**It is a fair trade.**" She put her hands flat on the folder. "I have been told it twice. Once in the middle of last term by a woman who signs my timesheet, and once in the week of the thirty-second by a man in an office who wanted to know whether a district nurse could countersign a form about a patient in a room she did not run, and I said could he define *countersign*, and he could not, and then he said the second thing, which is that a district nurse is not a named party in a room, and that is true, and I have never been a named party in any room."

"**And you filled the form in.**"

"**I filled the form in, and I am going to say the next part and it is the part I have not said to anybody, and I would like it said to a person and not written in a book, although I know that is not on offer.**" She was not looking at either of them. "For nine years I have not been a named party in any room and I have held the pen in four hundred forms about nine hundred people, and every one of those forms was about somebody who could not answer, and the whole of my job, on a bad day, is being the person who is nearest when a person cannot say no and somebody else needs a name."

Nobody said anything for eleven seconds.

"**So the answer to who telephones a care-link review in this city,**" she said, "is a woman of thirty-four on a leaflet circuit with a folder and no standing, and there is a woman of thirty-one in that room with a diary and no post, and the two of us are the entire mechanism, and neither of us is a post, and if one of us is ill this month then eleven shares in a building in Bower Street are not reviewed, and that is not a scandal, that is just Tuesday."

The cost to her was not a telling-off, and by the Wednesday there were three parts of it and none of them was anybody being angry.

**The first part was a note.** The district's practice assurance office wrote on the Tuesday afternoon and asked, in a courteous paragraph, for a short written account from the attending service of what was understood by the line *patient identifiers, if available* at the time of return, and there was a box for it and the box was not optional and it went into a file with her name at the top of it. She wrote it on the Wednesday evening at her kitchen table in about four hundred words. It is the correct document. It says she understood the line to mean that identifiers should be supplied where lawfully available and that she supplied one, and that she would not supply one again without a person with standing asking her to, and that she believes the form should say so. **In about four years somebody can read that note and refer to a district nurse who supplied a patient identifier to a third party in a room she did not run. That is a thing that can be referred to. It is also the only document in this case that says the form should be changed.**

**The second part was a sample.** The fund's officer of forty-four sent a note to three buildings on the Wednesday morning — polite, correct, four lines long — that the office would be re-sampling this term, and that returns would be requested again with identifiers in a sealed return and not on the page. Which means what it means. **Every one of about eleven rooms in this city that is in the sample has to fill in a form again, with a person holding a pen, and one of those rooms is in a building in Bower Street, and the woman of thirty-one who has the diary in that room has eleven open narrow shares with four of them past their review date and one of those four months past, and the girl of twenty-nine under her has now got about nine forms to hand to nine people who do not have a post either.** And the second part of the second part: the office marks its returns by district, and a district with a return in question is a district that loses a place in the spring, and the district that loses a place is the one whose rooms stop being asked what they do, and the rooms that stop being asked are the rooms where nobody notices a plate.

**The third part was the employer.** Roy Amiss's firm read page four, and a firm that is a party to a share that has just come off a bed in public, under a heading about safety, with an approving full stop, has to do something on the Tuesday, and what it did on the Tuesday was send one line to the ward.

Roy Amiss read the one line out loud in the bay at four o'clock on the Tuesday afternoon and then gave it to Ianthe Roke.

"*We are reviewing our emergency connection arrangements with all staff and will contact you individually.*"

"**Individually,**" said Ianthe Roke. "In about six rooms a week, for six years, you are the fourth one, and they are going to contact you individually, and that means that in six rooms a week somebody is going to have to be the fourth one, and there is no course on a Friday, and the man you would have to train is a man of thirty-one who will be given a card in four seconds by a company."

"Then it is me that has to train him."

"**Yes,**" said Ianthe Roke. "And I am sorry, and I did not know that was going to happen this morning, and I have been sitting in this room for four days doing this properly and the one line came from nine miles away and it is eleven words long."

The handout was not withdrawn.

Corin Vasska's office telephoned the printer on the Tuesday and the printer said about four thousand copies had gone out with the leaflets and ninety were in a room in Crown Terrace and the rest were in about four thousand pockets, and Vasska's office asked whether the case study could be removed from the remaining stock and was told that it could, at a cost, in about eleven days, and did not pay it, and did not say why to anybody, and the reason was written in his own note and is not in this case: **the case study is the only part of that handout that a person who does not come to an evening session will ever read, and he had spent four hours with a woman of thirty-one and a woman of thirty-four and a man of fifty-one and he could not make himself be the man who says take the one true thing out.**

What changed, on the Wednesday, was a line at the bottom of page four, in the reprint for the fourth session on the Thursday of the week of the thirty-six, and the line is nine words long and it says that the case is reproduced with the consent of the attending service and that readers should direct questions about arrangements of this kind to the service named above.

The service named above was a district clinic and a number, and both of those were correct, and Roy Amiss's employer was not named above at all, which is the omission that will matter in about four years, and Ianthe Roke read the line, and said, "**That is the sentence that is going to keep a person out of a file, and it is a sentence about who to ask, and it has taken four days and about nine hundred leaflets and a man of fifty-one in a bed, and I want it in the book that I have watched every version of a document in this case arrive in this city and the only one that was made *for* a person instead of *about* a person came out of an office on a Wednesday because it cost money not to reprint.**"

At four o'clock on the Tuesday Marek Senn was in a corridor with a folder he had not asked for and a tray that had gone out on the Friday with a form in it, and the tray came back on the Wednesday, empty, because a tray comes back.

The general enquiries box in the company's system had four hundred and one replies in it, because the letter to about four hundred and six people had gone out on the Monday of week thirty-one with a third paragraph on it, and nine of the first forty-one had got the company answer, and four of them were a person of thirty-four who had never had an injury and who was going to spend the rest of the year wondering, and nobody in that building had read any of the other three hundred and sixty.

He stood in the corridor and thought, in the order in which it happened rather than the order that makes sense, that a room that is on a list of eleven has eleven names in it this week that were not there last week, and that a form in a tray is the cheapest document in this city, and that the two words that matter on that form are *if available*, and that they mean the person who wrote it knew that sometimes they are not, and did not write that down, and that on the Friday of week thirty-two a woman of thirty-four stood in a room in Bower Street and did not write that down either, and that she had never claimed to be able to say yes for anybody, and that the whole of what she did was hold a pen while a person who could not answer was in the room.

Fifth. Do not make it six.*
