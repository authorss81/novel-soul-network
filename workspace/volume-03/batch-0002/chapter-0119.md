# Chapter 119 — He Said It in Four Seconds

The discharge was set for eleven o'clock and Roy Amiss was in the day room at ten with his frame against the chair and his coat on, and the handout was on the table in front of him with page four turned down, and Tess Anley came in with about nine forms in a plastic wallet and put them on the table and then took the handout off the table and put it back in the same place turned the other way up.

"**I have been putting that face down and face up for two days,**" she said, "and I have decided it is stupid, so it is going to stay whichever way up it happens to be."

"How many forms."

"**Nine. One per room in this building, and one for the two rooms in the building next door, and they are the new ones with the sealed return and a line at the top that says *do not write identifiers on this page*, which I have read out loud four times this morning to about four people who have already read it.**" She sat down. "And every one of those nine has got a box on it that says *room contact*, and the box is new, and the box is because of page four."

"There is no named person in this room."

"**There are three,**" said Tess Anley. "There is a woman of thirty-one with a diary and there is a girl of twenty-nine who started in the middle of last term, and there is a porter called Dev who has been here for nine years and who is not on any of them."

---

She got the woman of thirty-four with a hip on the second ring, at about a quarter past ten, on a Thursday, without warning, and she put the call on the table speaker because she had not worked out how to hold a piece of paper and a telephone and a plastic wallet at the same time.

"**Mrs Iversen. My name is Tess Anley and I am in the exchange room in this building and I have got a list and you are on it.**"

"I have been on a list?"

"**Your share has been open since before the summer and it went past its review date four months ago and nobody rang you, and I am ringing you now because I have been told to re-ask every room in the sample and your room is one of them and because I am the person who does the asking, and there is nobody else, and I am twenty-nine and I started in the middle of last term, and I am sorry about the four months.**"

There was a pause of four seconds.

"**What is it connected to.**"

"**A narrow share with a reason and an end date and a review, and yours has a review date on it and the review date went past in about the summer, and nobody rang you, and that is not right and I am not going to tell you it was a system because it is not a system, it is a date in a book and a telephone number in a book, and the two of them are made by a person.**"

"**Then take it off.**"

"**It comes off in four seconds. I have to say, on the phone, and it goes in a book with the date on it, and that is the whole of it.**"

"**Do it now.**"

It came off. Tess Anley wrote one line in a diary with the date on it and said thank you and the woman said there was no need and put the phone down.

Then the line went quiet for four seconds and Tess Anley said, to nobody:

"**She did not ask a single question about what it was or who could see it or how long it had been there, and I am going to write that down as well, and I am going to write it down exactly as she said it, because in about four years somebody is going to write a minute about this case and they are going to say she did not object.**"

---

"She did not object," said Ianthe Roke from the doorway. "And you are about to ask her the second question, and the second question is the important one, and you have got it in front of you, and you are going to ask it badly, so do not ask it at all."

"What is the second question."

"**You are going to ask her whether she objects, in the future tense, which is a different sentence and a much better one, and which she has just handed you for nothing by not having asked the first one.**"

"She has gone."

"**She has gone and she will be quite right not to be there when you ask her, and you should not ring her back, and I want to be very clear that I am telling you not to ring her back.**" She came into the room. "Ring a person about a share once. The first time is a review, which the design promised her, and the second time is you explaining yourself."

Roy Amiss said, from the chair:

"**That is the second time somebody in this week has said the right thing to me about a phone call and both of them were women who work in this building.**"

---

He had the whole of it in nine seconds and he told Tess Anley to write it down, which she did, and then he said the sentence that had been standing in that bay for eleven days and which he had said to a stranger in a corridor and to a market foreman and to a woman with a folder.

"**I would have agreed to it if anybody had asked me, and there was no way for anybody to find out, and the woman who signed me in did not lie to me and she had a form and the form was correct, and I have got a frame on my leg and I have been doing a great deal of thinking about it, and there is one thing I have never said to anybody in this building and I am going to say it now because there is a page four.**"

The room stopped.

"**Nobody has ever asked me once in six years whether I could do this on my own.**" He said it to the middle of the table. "Six years. About six rooms a week. A card, nine courses, and not one person in a room I have been in has ever said: could you do this without him. And I have thought about that for about four months and I have got a whole speech about it, and on Monday a man of thirty-one brought a page into this bay and asked me whether I knew my firm was in a pamphlet, and I did not know, and that was the first time anybody has asked me a question about a practice in six years and it was the wrong question, and the wrong question has my name on it, and the right one is in a pamphlet four hundred times."

Nobody wrote that down, and he had asked for it to be written down, and Tess Anley told him afterwards that she was not going to write it down because it was not about a share.

"**It is about a share,**" said Roy Amiss.

"**It is about you,**" said Tess Anley, "and this book is about shares, and I have got nine forms to fill in and about nine people to ring and I am not able to do both properly today and you are the one I am going to do properly today."

---

He went down to a car at eleven o'clock with a frame, a stick he did not need for the car park and did need for the corridor, and a leaflet in his coat pocket that he had put there himself, which nobody had given him, which is the detail Ianthe Roke noticed and wrote nothing about.

At the door he stopped and said one more thing to Tess Anley, who was standing in the corridor with nine forms in a plastic wallet.

"**You have to fill those in.**"

"**I have to fill those in.**"

"**Then do not put your name in the box.**"

She looked at the box on the top form, which said *room contact*, and which was new, and which was going to be in nine documents by about Wednesday.

"**I am the room contact,**" said Tess Anley. "There is no other name to put and I have been here since the middle of last term and I am the youngest person in this room and I have noticed, this week, that when a thing wants a name it takes the youngest one, and nobody has to be unkind to anybody for that to happen."

---

That is the beat, and it happened at about eleven o'clock on the Thursday of week thirty-four in a corridor in a building in Bower Street, and only four people in this city know that it happened, and three of them are in the ward.

**On the Tuesday, an office in Crown Terrace had discovered that eleven rooms in a sample had no named contact, and had corrected its own form to require one, and the correction was right, and the correction was sensible, and the correction was the answer to a question nobody had asked, which was how you find a name in a room where there is no post and no officer and no manager.** The answer is the person who is nearest and youngest and holding a pen, and there is no mechanism anywhere in this city that would put anything else, and there does not have to be one, because a form is enough.

Ianthe Roke said the whole of it in about nine words to Tess Anley in the corridor, and Tess Anley wrote it on the back of a form, in pencil, in a minute and a half, and then did not tear the form out, and then did tear it out and did tear it up, and then said out loud that she had done both of those things in eleven seconds, which is a sentence nine more than anybody in that corridor needed.

"**If my name is on nine documents about eleven people,**" she said, "then in about four years somebody can ask for the document and find me in it, and find eleven people through me, and the eleven people will be people who could not answer, and that is the whole of the case and it is nine forms and a Saturday."

"**Yes,**" said Ianthe Roke.

"**Then I would rather it said *room* and a number.**"

"**Then it says *room* and a number, and there is no name on nine documents, and the answer to who telephones a care-link review is still a woman with a diary, and there is no post, and there was no post on the Tuesday of week thirty-two and there is not one now.**"

"And if a review is missed again."

"**Then it is missed,**" said Ianthe Roke. "And I will ring it, because I am the one, and I have been the one for four years, and I would rather be the one who rings a man about a share than be the one whose form has got a girl's name in the box on it."

---

The man of thirty-one came at half past two.

He was an engineer with the same firm and he had a card in a plastic sleeve that had been issued to him in four seconds in a corridor in Lattice Ward in the middle of last term by a woman who had not read his name, and he was called Callum Reece and he did not know why Roy Amiss was in a corridor with a frame and a stick, and Roy Amiss told him, in ninety seconds, standing up, because he is a foreman and because he is fifty-one and because it was the only thing available.

"**In six rooms a week, and in about four of them you will be the one they wait for, and I am going to be in three of them until about the spring, and I am going to show you what I do and I am going to tell you the four things I was never told, and you are going to write them down, and then I am going to tell you the fifth thing, and the fifth thing is that I do not know how to do it on my own, and I have got eleven weeks of a frame to find out.**"

"**Why are you telling me.**"

"**Because there is a page four,**" said Roy Amiss. "And in about four years there is going to be a clause in a contract that says a company may connect to an employee in an emergency only where the employee has been asked, and there is going to be a training pack about it, and it is going to be the best training pack that firm has ever written, and it is going to exist because a woman of thirty-four filled in a box on a form and because a foreman of forty-five stopped work in a wet bay, and not one word of it is going to be about you."

Callum Reece said, "**All right,**" and got out a pen, and that is the whole of what happened between them.

---

At four o'clock a man of forty-four from the district's assurance office rang the ward about the sealed returns, and asked whether the room had a named contact, and Tess Anley said that the room was a store cupboard with shelving brackets in it and that there was no contact and that the room did not have a post and had never had a post, and the man said that he was sure she understood that the office needed a named point of contact for the sample, and Tess Anley said that she understood it perfectly and that she was not going to be it, and that she had worked that out at eleven o'clock that morning from a form, and that the person who had worked it out before her had got a leaflet about it on the Tuesday, and the man said he was sorry and would note it and would ask whether the room could be a nominal contact only, and Tess Anley said yes, and he said thank you, and neither of them was angry, and it is now in a system that a room number is a nominal contact, and that system will be read in about four years by somebody who will assume it was always so.

And in a corridor, at about ten past five, on the Thursday, a site manager of thirty-eight called Warren Pike rang the district service office in a building on the far side of the city, because he had said in a mess room on the Wednesday morning that he would, and because he did not want to appear to be in a hurry. He was answered by a district service manager of fifty-two who had not been asked to take the call and took it. He asked two questions and both of them were about the man in the lanyard, and the first was whether that man was in date and the second was whether that man was on anybody's rota, and the answer to both of them was the same answer in the same voice, and the answer is that the office does not hold a list. He said thank you both times. **A man of forty-four from a levy had asked the same question on the Wednesday, in the same words, and had been given the same answer, and that is how two job sheets with the same name on both of them went out of that office, and there is no document anywhere in this city that joins those two telephone calls.**
