# Chapter 113 — Eleven Seconds

The fault came in on a Saturday and the booking came in with it, and the booking said *attending contractor: district technician*, which meant that for the first time in twelve weeks a piece of paper in this city had used his job title as a reason for him to be somewhere.

He read it in a corridor at ten past seven on the Wednesday morning of week thirty-three and did not enjoy it, and he could not have told anybody why not.

The room is on the first floor of a building in Bower Street, in a room that was a store cupboard until four years ago and still has the shelving brackets in it, and it has one chair, a table, a plate on the wall, a rack of about four hundred consent leaflets in a plastic box with a rubber band on it, and eleven open narrow shares in a diary.

The woman who has it was thirty-one and her name was Ianthe Roke, and she had the diary open, and she had a girl of twenty-nine in the room with a mug, and she said, before he had the door shut:

"**It came up in four seconds this morning and I have written it down, so you are going to tell me it is nothing to do with the plate and I am going to say it again.**"

---

He put the bag down.

"**Tell me the number you wrote.**"

"**Eleven,**" said Ianthe Roke. "In the spring, on the fourteenth, at about nine in the morning, and I wrote it in the diary because the plate is slow and this room is small and a person has to stand in here and hold it, and eleven seconds is a fact about my working day and I have four hundred and eleven facts about my working day and I keep them because there is nowhere else to put them."

"How long have you known it was four."

"**Since twenty past seven this morning, and I did not sleep on it, and I want to say that before anything else, because I know what it looks like from the outside.**"

The girl of twenty-nine put her mug down.

"**Tess,**" said Ianthe Roke, "is twenty-nine and started in the middle of last term and has eleven things of her own to think about, and none of them is this, and I am going to use her anyway, because she is the only other person in the building and I have got forty minutes before the ward round."

"**Go on,**" said Tess Anley.

---

He did the maintenance, because that is what he is, and the maintenance took nine minutes and it told him everything and it told him nothing.

A pen-torch and a meter and a spare on the shelf. The plate is a mineral-fibre panel with copper, a label at the bottom left, two fixings at the top and two at the bottom, and it comes up in stages and holds for as long as a person stands in the room, which is the design and is not a fault and is a fact that a person in a hurry will treat as a fault.

The rise was four seconds. He timed it four times.

The label said **2-14** in a typeface that was not the building's.

"The room is two-oh-seven."

"**The room is two-oh-seven,**" said Ianthe Roke, "and that has been on my door since the Wednesday of the week thirty-one, and the plate has been on this wall for four years, and the plate came with the room, and I have never once looked at the bottom left of it."

He put the torch on the two top fixings and then the two at the bottom. All four had slots. Three of the four had about eleven years of oxide in the bottom of the slot and one of them had a bright edge in it, and the bright one was at the bottom left, and there was a clean rectangle in the dust on the wall behind the plate about two inches wider than the plate, and the rectangle was a different colour from the wall around it.

"**There has been a bigger plate on this wall,**" he said, "and it was there until recently, and it went off this wall, and the one that is here now came from somewhere in this building, because the dust says it came from about four inches to the left."

"How long ago."

"**I cannot say. The bright edge is hours, not days. The rectangle is months.**"

Ianthe Roke wrote both of those down in the diary, with the time, which is what she does, and then she put the pen down and said the thing that made the morning.

"**Say the other part.**"

"What other part."

"**You have been looking at a plate for nine minutes and you have not asked me the only question I have wanted answered since twenty past seven this morning, and you are not going to ask it, because it is a bad question and you have a rule about bad questions, and the rule is right, and I am going to ask it anyway.**"

"Go on."

"**Is it reaching further than it was.**"

---

He took the handheld off the shelf, which is the building's, and which lives in a drawer under the table with a battery that is always flat, and he put a fresh one in from his bag because he had one, because he carries them, and Tess Anley stood in the doorway and held the door open and Ianthe Roke stood in the corridor.

It was not a demonstration and it was not a practice and it was not a technique and it was not a share. It was a range test on a wall panel, which is a thing a technician does with a meter and a torch and a walk along a corridor.

"**Say what you are doing,**" said Ianthe Roke, from the corridor.

"**Checking how far the panel carries. Keep talking, both of you, it wants a voice.**"

He went out along the landing, and the plate in the room was two-oh-seven's door and the corridor is thirty feet, and then he went to the stairwell and down four steps, and then he went back up and round to the lift lobby and stood in it for nine seconds, and the meter in his hand did something at thirty-one feet that it did not do at forty-one.

"**Where are you standing.**"

"Lift lobby."

"**How many people go through that lobby.**"

"**nine a day,**" said Tess Anley. "Outpatients, visitors, the laundry at eleven and two, and a man from the offices upstairs who comes down at about ten and goes back up at three, and he is not staff, he is a tenant, and he has a key."

"And nobody in that room knows how far this goes."

"**Nobody in that room knew it was two-oh-four seconds ago,**" said Ianthe Roke.

---

The call to the district office took nine minutes and was made by Ianthe Roke, and Marek Senn stood in the corridor and listened to half of it.

"**The plate is on a schedule,**" she said twice, and the second time she said it more slowly. "And the schedule has got a room field, and the room field says 2-07, and the schedule is a form, and a form is not a record of what is on a wall."

Then: "**I am not accusing anybody of anything. I am telling you that the thing on my wall is not the thing on your schedule and I would like somebody to come and look at it in eleven weeks.**"

And then: "**Who is the person I ring if it is not eleven weeks.**"

There was a pause of four seconds.

"**That is the second person in a week who has asked me that,**" she said, when she put the phone down. "A foreman asked a dispatcher and a dispatcher said there is nobody, and now I have asked and somebody has said there is nobody, and it is the correct answer and it is the same answer, and I have had a week with it and I do not have anything to put it on."

"Then what do you do."

"**Nothing for eleven weeks,**" said Ianthe Roke. "Which is the correct procedure and is a procedure for a room where a plate belongs to a schedule, and not for a room where eleven people are connected to a wall."

---

**And that was the whole of the finding, and it took nine minutes, and it is the finding of this entire case in one cupboard.**

Eleven open narrow shares in this room, four of them past their review date, and one of those four past it by four months, and not one of the eleven people knew that the thing they were connected to had moved four inches to the left and thirty-one feet out of a room that was a store cupboard until four years ago.

"I am not going to ring eleven people today," said Ianthe Roke.

"No."

"**I have said that out loud so that you can hear me say it, and I would like it written down that I said it and did not do it, because I am not going to write it down in the diary, and a diary is a book, and a book is a thing that can be asked for.**"

"Then what is it in."

"It is in Tess," said Ianthe Roke. "And in a diary that is not going upstairs, and in my head, and a woman of thirty-one with a diary and a plate that is not hers is the whole of the arrangement, and there is no post."

"**I can put it in the fault ticket,**" said Marek Senn.

"**No.**"

"It is not a name and it is not a person and it goes in a system and the system keeps it."

"**I know it is not a name. That is not why.**" She was looking at the plate. "Because the moment it is in a system, then there is a thing in a system about this room, and then the thing in the system is the only version, and the version in the system will say a room and a date and a fault and nothing about a person, and in about four years somebody will read it and see a room and a date and a fault and conclude that nothing happened here, and something will have happened here, and it will have happened in a cupboard with a door that shuts."

"Then it stays off the system."

"**It stays off the system, and you are going to have to close a job on a system, and you are not going to be able to close it as fixed, and I am telling you now so that it is not a surprise at about eleven o'clock.**"

---

He closed it at half past ten in the yard, and he wrote on the sheet, in the box that says what was found:

*Plate in room 2-07 carries label 2-14. Rise time 4 s, previously 11 s. Range to lift lobby ~31 ft. Bigger plate evidenced by dust line. No work order, no name, no date. Placed with facilities, ref given, not quoted here.*

He read it twice. It was a fact about a wall and it was a fact about eleven people and it was a document about a room in a company's system, and there was no name in it, and the only mercy in the whole of that document was that it had no name in it, and a mercy of that kind lasts exactly as long as a tray.

Then he rang facilities and asked for the work-order number that had raised it, because a fault has to have come from somewhere, and the woman on the other end found it in nine seconds.

"**It came in on a booking. Saturday. The booking says *plate not holding, room 2-14, check and make safe.* Whoever raised that had a job to raise it and not a name, and there are nine bookings a week that say a room and not a person, and I can give you the booking reference and I cannot give you the man.**"

"Is the booking still there."

"**Bookings go at the end of the month. The reference goes in the system for a year and the booking does not.**"

"Thank you."

"**Do not thank me, I have given you a reference and not a man, and that is what I have.**"

---

Nobody was caught. That is not a story ending, it is a Wednesday.

Nobody was named. There was a district fleece on a hook in a corridor two floors down that a ward clerk had seen on a Tuesday in the middle of last term and had described to two people as somebody who had come in and not signed anything and had a battery tool, and the description matched a woman of thirty-one and a man of fifty-one and a fitter of twenty-nine in another district, and there have been four of these in a term — a plate, a lamp ballast in a yard office in Nine Bridges, a door reader on a customer site, and now this — and nobody in this city has ever put the four in a list, because the four are four things and a list of four things is still a list.

And the four are still four, and the things that are not the four are still the things that are not the four, and nobody counted anything today, and there was a plate in a cupboard and a number in a diary and a hand on a corridor wall at thirty-one feet and a nine-minute conversation with a woman who asked whether she should ring a postman and was told that there is no postman.

At half past four he went down two flights and out into Bower Street and stood on the pavement, and he did not go back up, and the tray in that room goes back to the district office at the end of the month whether the form in it is filled in or not, and a district nurse of thirty-four had been round twice asking whether they were going to fill it in, and Ianthe Roke had said no twice, and had not told her what happens to a tray that goes out empty.

---

*116. Wednesday of week 33, half past four on a pavement in Bower Street, and this entry is about a woman of thirty-one with a diary and a plate that is not hers, and a girl of twenty-nine who held a door open for nine minutes, and about eleven people who are connected to a wall they have never seen.*

*The plate.* **Two-oh-four seconds, timed about four times, and it used to be eleven, and the only person in this city who knew the old number is a woman of thirty-one who wrote it in a diary on the fourteenth, in the spring, in a diary nobody has ever asked to see.** The label says **2-14** in a typeface that is not the building's, and the room is **2-07**, and there is a clean rectangle in the dust two inches wider than the plate, so a bigger plate was on this wall until months ago and the one that is here came from four inches to the left. **All four fixings had slots. Three of them had eleven years of oxide in the bottom. One of them had a bright edge in it that is hours old and not days.** He is a technician and he can do maintenance and he did maintenance, and it took nine minutes, and it told him everything and it told him nothing about a person, and that is the correct output of a meter and a torch.

*And the range.* A handheld and a fresh battery out of his own bag, because he carries them, and a walk along a landing and down four steps and back up and round to the lift lobby, and the meter did something at thirty-one feet that it did not do at forty-one. About nine people go through that lobby a day, and one of them is a tenant with a key who is not staff. A wall plate does not read a person. It carries the shape of a share, and anybody standing in a corridor at thirty-one feet can be told that somebody in a cupboard is connected to somebody, and cannot be told to what, and can be told how, and that is worse. Eleven open narrow shares, four past their review date, one of them four months past, and eleven people who did not know. A range test is not a share and not a technique and not a demonstration and nobody asked me to do one: it is a technician with a meter and a corridor, and if it is on the page as anything else then I have done something this volume forbids.

*And what she did with it.* **She wrote two numbers and a time in the diary and did not sleep on it, and then she said the decision out loud so that I could hear her say it: eleven people, today, no. And the reason is not kindness. The reason is that a diary is a book and a book is a thing that can be asked for, and the moment the plate is in a company system then the version in the system is the only version, and in about four years somebody will read a room and a date and a fault and conclude that nothing happened in a cupboard with a door that shuts.** She put it in the girl of twenty-nine and in a diary that is not going upstairs and in her own head, **and she said the whole of the arrangement in about nine words: a woman of thirty-one with a diary and a plate that is not hers, and there is no post.**

*And the fault ticket.* Written in the yard at half past ten: label 2-14 on a plate in 2-07, rise four, range to the lift lobby, dust line, no work order, no name, no date, reference given and not quoted. A fact about a wall, a fact about eleven people, a document in a company's system, no name in it. The only mercy in that document is that it has no name in it and a mercy of that kind lasts exactly as long as a tray. I closed a job on a system I could not close as fixed, because a fixed job is a lie and an open job is a question somebody rings you about, and there is no third box. A booking that said a room and not a person raised it, on a Saturday, and bookings go at the end of the month and the reference goes in the system for a year. Entry 116. The book is at forty-three lines and a woman of sixty-one will not have it opened for three weeks, and four of these have happened this term and nobody has put the four in a list, and the four are still four, and I have not counted anything today and I have written the number of feet twice because feet are not people.
