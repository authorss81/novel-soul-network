# Chapter 118 — Do You Have A Partner

The gate of the depot on a service road in Nine Bridges opens at twenty to seven, and about eleven men work there, and nine of them are district and two of them are the customer's, and on the Wednesday morning of week thirty-four the briefing took twenty minutes instead of four because a man of thirty-eight called Warren Pike had come in with a leaflet and a six-sided handout and had asked to say something.

He was the site manager for eleven years and he was not a mechanic and he had never once been unpleasant to anybody who turned up with a torque wrench, and he stood at the front of the mess room with a leaflet in his hand and the hand on it was a stylised open hand with a thumb and a forefinger about a millimetre apart.

"**I am not making an accusation,**" he said. "I want to say that first and I want to say it in front of all of you, because I have been in a yard for eleven years and I have watched about nine people get taken apart for less than this and I do not intend to be the tenth."

Nobody said anything. Ozzie Wray was at the back on a crate with his hood down, because Ozzie Wray has been a foreman on that kitchen line for eleven years and has not put a hood up at a briefing since a man from Wales told him that a hood up at a muster is a man who cannot hear you.

"**What I have got is this,**" said Warren Pike. "Which came through my door with the quarterly levy note, like everything else that comes through my door with the quarterly levy note. And there is a session on it for the Thursday of the week of the thirty-six, and the leaflet names a job, and I am not going to read the job out because half of you are that job."

He turned the leaflet round and put it on the table face up.

"**And I have got this,**" he said, and put the handout down on top of it, "which is about a room in a hospital and a man in a bed, and it is a safe thing, the whole of that page is telling us it worked, and I am not here about the hospital."

"Then what are you here about," said a man of thirty-one near the door.

"**I am here about Friday.**" Warren Pike looked up. "Two of my men have told me that on a Friday, on a load, somebody in a lanyard says a word before it comes down, and that the word is the thing it turns on, and that if he does not say it the load does not move. And one of my men has been here for about eleven years and he does not think that is normal, and he asked me about it, and I said I would ask somebody."

He looked round the room and found Marek Senn standing at the back by the door with a job sheet in his hand.

"Are you the technician."

"Yes."

"**Then can I ask you a question and you can say no to, and I have got no problem with a no.**"

"Go on."

"**Is that you.**"

---

**What nobody in that mess room knew, except the man being asked, is what he had in his pocket.**

A leaflet with eleven session titles and a fee on it. A plastic sleeve on a lanyard round his neck with a single sheet folded twice, a reference in the corner, four fields, **the fourth field empty**, and a strip of tape across one corner that somebody else put there. A course exception in a company's system in a building on the far side of the city from his own bench, in a box that says *No reason given by holder*, written by a manager of fifty-two in a corridor who has undertaken in writing to write the same thing again in ten weeks. A rota board with eleven names on it, on which he was one of two names, and the board did not distinguish between grade two and grade three. About eleven people a week who had asked to be on a day when a person with a card comes out.

**Every one of those is in the leaflet. Not one of them has his name on it.**

He had four seconds and a job sheet in his hand and a man of thirty-eight in front of nine people, and he did the thing he had done eleven times on a Friday in about eleven rooms, except that on a Friday the eleven parts came out correct and in eleven seconds, and this was not a Friday and this was not eleven parts and this was the only time in a term and a half that anybody had ever asked him a question about the shape of himself in a room full of people who do his trade.

"Yes," he said.

---

"**I am sorry—**" said Warren Pike.

"No, go on."

"**I said is that you, and you have said yes, and I am going to say the rest of what I have got and then I am going to ring your district office, and I want to say both of those in front of them as well.**" He put his hand flat on the leaflet. "I read this on the Friday night. It says there is a danger in practices being handed round with nobody's name on them and in people being told they can act on their own judgement, and it has got a job title on the second one, and I have four people in this building who work Fridays for you, and one of them is a woman of twenty-nine who has been doing it for six years and who is better at it than anybody in this room, and I am not going to stand here and say I think she is dangerous."

"Then what do you think."

"**I think I would like somebody to tell me whether the thing my four people are relying on is a practice or a man, and if it is a man then I want to know what happens on a Friday when he is ill, and I am not asking you that as an accusation, I am asking you because there is nobody else in this city I can ask and I have looked at about nine leaflets.**"

---

**And that was the fairest question anybody has asked Marek Senn in a term and a half, and he answered it the way he has answered everything since the Wednesday of week thirty-two, which is to say that he told the truth about himself in a room and did it well.**

"That is exactly the right question," he said. "And I am going to answer it properly and I am going to answer it in one go because if I stop and start I will talk over somebody, and I have done that four times this fortnight."

"**Go on,**" said Warren Pike.

"**I am the fourth thing in eleven rooms a week. That is not a thing anybody gave me. Nobody decided that. I applied for the Friday release callout because it pays the release rate and I am twenty-two and it is a wage, and about eleven people a week put their name on a board because they asked to be on a day when a person with a card comes out, and I am the person with the card, and I am the reason eleven people wait for me.**" He put the job sheet on the table. "Eleven parts in eleven seconds, and the fourth of them is a word, and the word is on a company's card and in a company's system and it has my job on it, and I say it eleven times on a Friday, and it works, and it is correct, and a man with twenty-two years in a department has said on a stage that it is the safest piece of engineering he has ever been taught, and he is right."

Somebody at the back said "**that's the whole of it then**" and it was Ozzie Wray, and he said it quietly, and it was not a question.

"It is not the whole of it," said Marek Senn. "And the part that is not the whole of it is the part I have not been able to say in a room until this week, and I am saying it now because you have asked a fair question and because ninety people sat in a room on Friday and a man of fifty-one told them that the truth had been taken out of a sentence on its way through about four hundred institutions, and I have been sitting on the other half of that for eleven days."

"**Which is.**"

"**That the only reason I can say that word in those eleven rooms is that a form in a company's system lets me. Not a card. A form. There is a piece of A4 in a building on the far side of this city with a box on it that says *reason*, and I have not got a reason I am willing to give, and a manager of fifty-two wrote down *No reason given by holder* and said out loud that he was not going to invent one. And that is the entire difference between me and the about nine words on a laminated sheet behind a gate, and the entire difference is a form with a box on it and a manager who was decent about it.**"

He stopped there, and it was the best sentence he has ever said in a room, and about ten men in a mess room at seven-forty in the morning watched it happen.

---

Warren Pike took four seconds.

"**Right,**" he said. "Thank you. That is more than I expected and it is not an answer, and I am not going to pretend to you that it is."

"No, it isn't."

"**It is a form.**"

"It is a form."

"**And the form has got a name on it.**"

"No," said Marek Senn. "That is the entire point and I have been trying to say it for about four days. A form has a name on it and a laminated sheet behind a gate has not got one on it, and nine people in this city would trade the form for the sheet, and I have spent a term and a half proving that the sheet is the dangerous one, and the reason the sheet is the dangerous one is not what it says, it is what happens after about four years when nobody can ring anybody about it."

"Then I can ring somebody about the form."

"**You can ring the district service office, and there is a district service manager of fifty-two called Wyn Dunleavy, and he will tell you that it checked, and he will mean it, and he will not be able to do anything about it, and he has offered that to me twice this term and once in a corridor, and I have said yes to him both times and once in that corridor.**"

---

**And then the man of thirty-eight asked the question that has been waiting in a room in this city since a corridor on the Monday of week thirty-two, and it is nine words, and nobody has ever asked it.**

"**Do you have a partner.**"

The room did not go quiet. That is the thing Marek Senn has written about four times in a term and a half and had never once had the experience of. **The room did not go quiet. Eleven men in a mess room on a Wednesday morning made a noise, and three of them laughed, and one of them said *partner* the way a man says a word he has been saving.**

He had nine seconds.

He had been asked it once before, in a corridor, by a woman of forty-five with a tool braid, and she had not asked it as a question, she had told him what the answer had to be: *if anybody asks you on that Friday whether you have a partner, you say yes, and you say the name, and if you cannot produce a name then you are not a door, you are a hinge with a card on.*

"Yes," he said.

"**Name.**"

---

He could not produce one.

**That is the whole of the failure and it took four seconds and it is not going to get better.** A Working Share with seven headings and four named partners who may end it by word is a different thing and he has never pretended otherwise; what is being asked for is a person in a lift who can put the load down and go home, and there has not been one in eleven rooms a week since the Friday of week thirty-one, and the one candidate is a woman of twenty-nine who has a card and a bus to catch and who said in week thirty-one that nine seconds is a bus, and who is standing forty feet away in a doorway at about seven-forty this morning because her employer booked her in for a lift at nine.

"I do not have one," said Marek Senn.

"**Then you are the man in the leaflet.**"

"Yes."

---

Ozzie Wray got off the crate.

He did it the way a foreman of thirty-four does it after eleven years, which is slowly, and he came to about four feet from Warren Pike and said: "**You have asked him twice and you have got two answers and you are about to do the third thing, which is to go and ask somebody else, and I would like to say one thing and then I will go back to the crate.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I have worked with him for about four months and I cannot tell you whether he is a good technician, because he is not a technician, he is a man on a day rate with a lanyard, and I can tell you that in four months I have seen him do the safe thing when it was inconvenient twice, and once of those was on a Friday at half past four with a man on a load and nine people watching and a card in his hand that was not his.**"

"That is not the question."

"**No. It is the other thing. And the other thing is that I have said one true sentence out loud in a market in eleven years and I did not do it because I was brave, I did it because somebody put a microphone near me, and it did not help anybody, and I have never seen it again since, and I am not going to pretend to a customer this morning that I have been in a room about a technician before, because I have not, and if I have, it was not in this yard.**"

He went back to the crate.

Ozzie Wray is not the seventh of anything and was not counted today by anybody, least of all by the man who has spent a term and a half refusing to count anything, and the reason he is in this entry at all is that he was asked a question about another man in a yard and he answered it with the only true thing he had, and that true thing was about a market and a microphone, and it cost him nine seconds in front of a customer and eleven colleagues.

---

At about ten past eight, from a doorway, a woman of twenty-nine said:

"**Is the card I wait for on Fridays the one in that leaflet.**"

She had come in for a lift at nine and she had heard the whole of it because there are no walls in a mess room.

"**I am not going to answer that in here,**" said Marek Senn.

"**You are going to have to.**"

"**I am not going to answer it in here, and I am going to tell you why, and the why is worse than the answer. In here, the answer is that the leaflet is about a group of people handing practices round with no name on them and I am a job in a company's system with a form attached to it, and neither of those is true and both of them are what the room is holding, and if I give you the true one in a mess room with eleven men in it then the true one becomes a thing eleven men know about me, and then in about four years you are going to be asked about it by somebody with a clipboard.**"

Marguerite Okpara looked at him for four seconds.

"**You have been on a rota where I am the fourth one about nine times a week since the Friday of week thirty-one, and you have not got a partner, and this morning I have found out that you have not got a partner, and I am the only person in this building who has any use for that, and I did not choose to be told about it in a mess room at seven-forty in the morning, and I am not going to pretend that I am going to forget it.**"

"I know."

"**What are you going to do about it.**"

"**I am going to say the eleven parts on Friday and I am not going to say them better.**"

She went out to the yard and did not look back, which is correct, and he thought about it for the rest of the day.

---

Warren Pike rang the district service office at ten past nine with Marek Senn standing next to him, which was arranged, and which is the most generous thing anybody has done for him in a term and a half.

The office asked for the technician's course reference.

Marek Senn gave it. It is the reference of an exception, and the exception is in a company's system, and a district service office can read a course reference.

Then the office asked, in a perfectly ordinary voice, for the reason.

And that is where it stopped, in the middle of a Wednesday morning, on a bench in a mess room in Nine Bridges, in nine seconds.

"**I have not got a reason I am willing to give,**" said Marek Senn, "and I am not going to give one now, and I want to say in front of the site manager that I have said that out loud twice in a term and a man of fifty-two has written it in a box in a form, and the box says *No reason given by holder*, and the box does not say *reason if not held*, and there is no other box, and about nine hundred of those are signed a year and about eleven have got something in them that a person wrote for themselves."

The woman on the other end of the line said: "**I am going to have to note that.**"

"There is no reason," said Marek Senn. "Write that. That is the whole of it and it is half of what is in the box, and the other half is that a manager of fifty-two said out loud that he did not want anybody in about four years to read that form and think he got it out of a tired man on a Friday, and I would like that noted as well, and I would like somebody to ring Wyn Dunleavy and ask him whether he said it."

Nobody rang Wyn Dunleavy. Warren Pike said that he would, on the Thursday, and that he was not going to do it on the Wednesday because he did not want to appear to be in a hurry, and Marek Senn said that was fair, and both of them knew exactly what it was.

---

Nothing was cleared.

**He was not cleared by the man of thirty-eight, who had asked a fair question twice and had been refused a second answer both times, and who went back to his office and did not say anything to anybody for the rest of the day, and who has not said anything to anybody since, and who is not a villain and is not a friend and has never in his life been either.**

**He was not cleared by the district, which asked for a course reference and a reason and got both, and neither of which says anything about whether the arrangement is safe.**

**He was not cleared by a woman of forty-five with a tool braid, because he did not ring her, and the reason he did not ring her is that a technician in a lanyard telephoning a foreman in the same trade on the morning he has been asked in a mess room is a thing that looks exactly like what a frightened room is looking for, and there is no document in this city that would have done it better, and that is the sentence he has been avoiding for about a fortnight.**

**He was not cleared by the man of thirty-four on the crate, who said something true and went back to the crate.**

And a woman of twenty-nine went to work in a yard and kept a number in her head, and the number is nine and not eleven, and she had said in week thirty-one that she would say she did not know if she was ever asked, and she has not been asked, and this morning the only thing that has changed is that her *I do not know* has quietly stopped being a fact about a number and started being a defence of a man, and she never asked for that and cannot put it back, and she is twenty-nine and she has six years and a bus.

*121. Wednesday of week 34, half past four in the afternoon on a bench in a mess room in Nine Bridges, and this entry is about me, and it is about me because this is the one where I said it, and I want it in the book in the first person in the order it happened, and not improved.*
