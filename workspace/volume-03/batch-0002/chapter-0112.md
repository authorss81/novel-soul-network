# Chapter 112 — No Course On A Friday

The return had come back round twice before anybody had read it, because the district office puts a form through a tray and a tray does not know what a form is for.

It was one side of A4 on a Monday. It was due on the Tuesday. It had four lines and a guidance note and a box at the bottom for a name and a date, and Marek Senn read it standing up in the corridor outside the parts cage at ten past seven in the morning, because it had his job title on it and had had since the middle of last term, and a thing with his job title on it is a thing that is about him whether or not it says his name.

*Monthly activity return. Depot [ ]. Month ending [ ].*
*1. Calls attended.*
*2. Calls declined at dispatch.*
*3. Releases completed with an attending technician in date present.*
*4. Releases completed without one.*
*Guidance: line 2 is for information. Line 4 is reported monthly to the district office. A nil return on line 4 is a nil return.*

"**There is no box on this for a man who was there,**" said Sasha Pyle, who was twenty-three and had been doing returns for eleven months and had read line 4 about four hundred times.

"There is a box on it for a man who is in date."

"**There is a box on it for a man who is in date,**" Sasha Pyle agreed, "and the interesting thing about that box is that about nine people in this depot have never once been in it and never once will be, and the reason they never will be is on page four of the course pack and it is a Friday."

---

She had it on the wall. That was the thing Marek Senn had not known, and he had been in this building twelve weeks.

The district ran its two-day release course on a Monday and a Tuesday, in a training place in Lattice off the roundabout, on the clock and free to an employee and four hundred and forty pounds to anybody else. **It had been run the same way for eleven years. There has never been a Friday course. There is not a Friday course because a course is a two-day thing and a Friday is the day the district discovers what it did not check.**

"**So a man who only works Fridays cannot be in date,**" said Sasha Pyle, "and if a man only works Fridays and does the work, then either he does it on somebody else's card or he does it on nobody's, and the choice is not his, because the other option is a Tuesday he does not get paid for."

"How many."

"**On the rota? Four.**"

"How many men work Fridays in this depot."

"**Nine, and two of them are women, and four of the nine are not on the rota, and the four of the nine are the four on line 4.**"

---

The number for the month to the Friday was forty-one.

Marek Senn said it before anybody asked, which he noticed afterwards and did not enjoy noticing.

"**You have said that number out loud three times in two days,**" said Sasha Pyle, "and I am not complaining, I am telling you that in about two months forty-one is going to be a number in a system rather than a number in a cab, and a number in a system is a number somebody can do something with."

"Who confirmed them."

"**You did. Every one. It is in the file.**"

"Then line three is thirty-seven and line four is four."

Sasha Pyle put the pen down.

"**Say the other one.**"

"Which one."

"**Say the other one, because you have not said it and it is the one everybody in this building is going to say at about eleven o'clock this morning, and I would rather you said it first than heard it off Mervyn.**"

Marek Senn looked at the form for a while.

"Line three is forty-one and line four is nil," he said. "Because line four is *without one*, and one of the four was there. He held the other end of a bar for nine seconds and he talked a man of forty-four through a ramp he could not see, and if anybody in this district ever asks me what happened in this month I am going to have to say that four men were in the room."

"**And in date.**"

"**And in date. I cannot say that. I was not in the room and I have never been on a course and the box on my own form is empty.**"

---

Mervyn Hale came in at nine with the second cup of tea he had had that morning and had been a supervisor in this depot for eleven years and had a re-inspection in about eleven months, and he did not shout, and he was not a monster, and everything he said for the next hour and a half was true.

"**Right. Talk me through it.**"

Marek Senn talked him through it. He was at it for nine minutes and he gave the mechanism and the mechanism was correct in every part.

Mervyn Hale listened to all of it and then said: "**You are all wrong about what that box is for.**"

"Say more than that."

"**Line four is not a confession. Line four is a number that goes upstairs, and upstairs turns numbers into questions, and a question about a number of four at this depot is a question I cannot answer, because the answer is that the course is on a Monday and a Tuesday and the men who need it work on a Friday.**" He put the cup down. "And I have told that answer to a district office twice in eleven years and both times it has come back as a letter that does not contain the answer, and the letter that does not contain the answer is now the reason I cannot answer the question about a number of four, and I have eleven months and a re-inspection."

"So what do you want on the form."

"**Forty-one and nil,**" said Mervyn Hale, "and I want the four men to sign underneath it saying they were in date, and then the form is not a lie, it is a form signed by four people who have told me the truth about themselves in a room with me in it."

Nobody said anything for nine seconds.

"**Which four,**" said Sasha Pyle.

"**I am not writing anybody's name on a return. It is a count. That is the whole point of line four, it is a count and not a list, and there are about nine depots and nine of us in each one and I am not writing thirty-six names into a form because a form is a piece of paper and a piece of paper with thirty-six names in it is a piece of paper somebody can ask to see.**"

"Then line four is nil."

"**Then line four is four,**" said Mervyn Hale, "and I will write four, and I will fight the letter that comes back, because there is not a thing in that letter I cannot answer in about nine words, and the nine words are *the course is on a Monday*, and I have been saying those nine words for eleven years to nine people and I am tired of it."

---

Emrys Vaughan came in at ten past ten with his hood down and a carrier bag, and he was thirty-one, and he had done lifts for six years, and he had a card in a plastic sleeve that had expired in the middle of last term, and he was on this depot's Fridays and was not on the rota and had never been on the rota, and everybody in the corridor knew all four of those things and had known them for about a year.

"**Is it the form?**"

"**It is the form,**" said Sasha Pyle, "and Mervyn wants you to sign underneath it and say you are in date."

"**And if I say no.**"

"Then four."

Emrys Vaughan put the carrier bag down on the bench and stood there.

"**Right,**" he said. "Then I have got a question and it is not a stupid one. If I sign it, I am saying I was on a course on a Monday and a Tuesday and I was not, and I did the work, and both of those are true, and the form is now a document about me that is wrong in a box. And if I do not sign it, then there are four releases in this month that the district says nobody was in charge of, and the district says that about me, and not about the course, and about me."

"That is correct."

"**Then I have got two boxes and one of them is a lie and one of them is a complaint, and I have been doing this for six years and I have never once been asked which of the two I would rather have, and I am not going to stand here and be asked it at ten past ten in the morning on a Tuesday.**"

"**Emrys.**"

"**No,**" said Emrys Vaughan. He took the card out of the plastic sleeve and put it on the bench, face up, where the expiry was visible from four feet away and had been visible from four feet away for a year. "I have got a mortgage on a flat that is too small and an agency that pays me on Fridays and a Tuesday in Lattice that costs me a day's pay and a bus fare, and I have been not-in-date for eleven months, and I have been the fourth one in about four rooms a week for about four of them, and I have never once been asked where I learned the step, and I have never once been in trouble, and I have never once been asked to be in date."

He picked the card up and put it back in the sleeve.

"**I am not signing a lie and I am not signing a complaint, and I am going to go and do my day, and you can put four on it.**"

"**Emrys, sit down.**"

"**No,**" said Emrys Vaughan, and he was gone by a quarter past ten, and the carrier bag was still on the bench, and nobody picked it up for four minutes.

---

The return went at four o'clock with forty-one and four.

Mervyn Hale signed it. Sasha Pyle posted it. The name and the date went in the box at the bottom, which is the only box on the form that is about a person, and the person in it was a supervisor of forty-seven signing for the count, which is what that box is for and it is not a list.

Nobody in that depot said the sentence out loud, and nine of them thought it, and the sentence is the one a woman four miles away said to Marek Senn on a telephone on the Tuesday of week thirty-one, the second day of this term, in about nine words at the end of a call about a rota:

*In about four years a district office is going to write that unsafe practice in this district is performed by technicians who are not in date, and it is going to be true.*

**She had not known about the form and had not known about line four. She had known about the eleven parts, and the eleven parts say that if the technician does not say the word then you put the load down and report it, and a man who is not in date is not a technician, and so a man who is not in date is a man the card says must not release, and if he does release then the card has been got wrong, and the card is a company document, and the company is not the author.**

So he rang her on the Tuesday, from the corridor, at about ten past five, and it was a Tuesday, and she had told him in week twenty-four to ring her on a Tuesday and tell her afterwards.

She picked up on the second ring.

"**Tell me afterwards,**" she said.

"**I have got a form with four in it,**" said Marek Senn, "and a man of thirty-one who signed nothing and went out of the door at a quarter past ten, and a supervisor of forty-seven who signed it, and the four are the four men who are not on the rota because there is no course on a Friday, and you told me in week thirty-one that in about four years a district office would write that unsafe practice in this district is performed by technicians who are not in date."

There was a pause of four seconds.

"**I did say that.**"

"You did."

"**And it is on its way, and it is coming because of a form with four in it, and the form is correct, and the four men were good.**" He heard her put something down. "Say the next part."

"There is no next part."

"**There is a next part and you have had it since week twenty-one,**" said Talia Venn. "Which is that the sentence is going to be true and it is going to be fair, and both of those are the problem, and neither of them is anybody's fault, and there is nothing in this city you can do with a true and fair sentence."

"That is what I rang to say."

"**Then why did you ring.**"

"Because it is a Tuesday," said Marek Senn, "and you told me to, and I have been doing it wrong for six weeks and it turns out the only thing the Tuesday is for is that I can say a sentence to a person who already knows it."

He put the phone down and stood in the corridor with the form's carbon in his hand, and the carbon had forty-one on one line and four on the other, and the four were not a name, and there were nine depots, and four in each, and nobody anywhere could turn the number back into a man, and that was the good news, and the bad news was that a number of thirty-six in eleven months in a district that has written down *releases completed without an attending technician in date* is a rate, and a rate is a sentence, and a sentence in a document does not need anybody's permission.

---

In a repair shop in Lattice Ward that evening he did not open the book, because the book was at forty-three lines and a woman of sixty-one with a key on a string would not want it opened and he would not do it on a Tuesday either.

He wrote four lines on the back of a docket instead and put the docket in his pocket with the other one, and the two of them together were about a foreman of forty-four who wanted one name to ring and about four men who had gone out of a door.

And on the wall the list in the plastic sleeve had a fifth line on it and his initials at the bottom of the fifth line, and the initials meant *not done*, and there was no longer any document in this city that required that list to be there.
