- **The counting rule, restated so that a later writer can reproduce it, unchanged from batches 0001, 0002 and 0003:** count one occurrence for each literal *about* that is immediately followed, optionally through *a*, *an* or *the*, by a number word, a scale word (*hundred*, *thousand*, *half*, *dozen*, *couple*) or a counted unit or counted noun (*second*, *minute*, *hour*, *day*, *week*, *month*, *year*, *times*, *people*, *premises*, *rooms*, *words*, *pages*, *lines*, *names*, *men*, *women*, *days*, *weeks*, *years*). Count each occurrence once, across the whole file including the load book. A raw *about* count is not a hedge count: **176 of the batch's 370 raw instances are the preposition.**
- **How the reduction was made, so that the figure is reproducible rather than asserted. Three passes.** The batch went in at **455 true hedges** and came out at **194, a net removal of 261.** (1) *Semantic and total:* clock readings and ages lose the hedge, because a character who has just read a clock says ten past ten and a person is a year old — that removed **31 clock readings and 20 ages**. ### Bold emphasis, and the ceiling

**367 spans across 34,343 words, 10.69 per thousand, in a range of 7.50 to 12.36.** Volume 02 Movement V ran 7.8 to 12.4; batch 0003 ran 10.75 in a range of 7.17 to 14.90; batch 0002 ran 9.95. **The batch average is below the 10.75 ceiling it inherited and below batch 0003's own figure, and NO CHAPTER IS OVER 12.4.** The first draft of this batch was 485 spans at 14.06 per thousand with six of ten chapters over the ceiling, Chapter 136 at 19.87 and Chapter 134 at 17.71.

- **How the reduction was made, and the carve-out that batch 0003 got wrong.** The house convention is that a bold span marks the beat inside a person's speech. Where one line carries several spans, the extras are the elaboration *after a speech tag*, and **the elaboration loses the emphasis, not the lead-in** — so `"**That is not mine,**" said Marek Senn. "You have spent a week on it…"` keeps the emphasis on the refusal and drops it on the explanation. **In a load-book line the first and the last span always survive and only the middle ones go, so no load book lost a bolded thesis clause**, which is the specific fault the batch 0003 review recorded. A hand list of twenty-one single-span lines follows the same principle: each is a line whose emphasis was decorative, or a line that restates a beat the reply already carries — *Good.*, *Agreed.*, *No.*, *In about nine minutes.*, *You are saying no.*
- **Counting note that a reviewer should not repeat the batch's first mistake.** The obvious regex `\*\*[^*]+\*\*` cannot match a span that contains a single-asterisk italic inside it, so it silently under-counts. **Five bold spans in this batch contain an inner italic** — two in Chapter 139, two in Chapter 140 and one in Chapter 134, around *completed by* and *variant* — and the figure above is counted with a matcher that allows them. **The loose regex reports 362 spans at 10.54 and under-reports Chapter 134 by 11.90 against a true 12.23 and Chapter 139 by 11.72 against a true 12.36**, which is the difference between a chapter that looks like it has room and a chapter that is on the ceiling.
