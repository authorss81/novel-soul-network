# Chapter 138 — The Fifth Session

There were about forty folding chairs set out in rows in a room in Crown Terrace on the Thursday evening of the week of the forty and about nine of them were empty, and a microphone on a stand at the front with a chair beside it, and a trestle table by the door with about four hundred leaflets on it in a stack, and a man of fifty-eight in a fleece at the door with a list, counting, because that is what a committee sends a man to do.

The fifth of eleven sessions. *Supervised confirmation where the holder is not a named party to the share.* Admission free. **Fee paid.**

The panel was five and all five of it was on the front of the leaflet, listed by job, and three of the five were jobs and two of them were people printed as a job as well, and there was no way of telling from the leaflet which of them was which.

**Both of the two people on it are him again, chosen by a system out of two job sheets, and nobody chose him, and a man of fifty-two has stopped offering to check it.**

---

Corin Vasska read the fourth section of the specification out loud, in the room, to the people it is about, and then he read a third line that is not a section and is a line the committee minuted on the Wednesday of week thirty-seven in a room above a print works in about nine words, and he read it the same way, and about nine people in that room did not hear the difference and about nine did.

Then he asked for questions and nine people put their hands up and he took three.

The first was about the fee and he answered it with the number.

The second was a man of thirty-eight who wanted to know whether the third line changed anything, and the answer is that it does not change anything for about eleven weeks, and that is the honest answer and it cost the room nothing and it cost the officer about eleven years.

---

The third question came from the fourth row and it was not a question about the third line.

"**I have got a card,**" said Peter Amado, "and it has got a line on the back of it that says if the word does not come, withdraw and report, and I want to know whether that line is telling me the right thing about a room I am standing in."

"**That is a question for the panel,**" said the chair.

"**I know it is, and I have been coming to this room for five Fridays and I have read four lines out of a notebook on four of them and I have been paid a hundred and forty pounds and travel for the one I have not crossed out, and I have never once been asked a question by anybody in this room, and I have started to think that is deliberate.**"

"**It is not deliberate, it is a microphone,**" said the chair, "and if you have a question the panel will take it in order."

"**Then take it in order,**" said Peter Amado.

---

The panel took it in order and it took nine minutes, and the man of fifty-two passed, correctly, and the man of forty-nine from a firm in Ashfields passed again, correctly, and the independent practitioner said that the question is not about a card, it is about a room, and that a panel in a room in Crown Terrace has no standing in a room in Bower Street.

Which left two, and the chair said: "**District practitioner, release callout.**"

Marek Senn had four seconds and he had been in four hundred rooms in a term and a half and he had never once not known what the next sentence was.

He took nine.

"**I am not going to answer that as the panel and I am going to say why in forty seconds and then somebody else can do it, and I would like the chair to stop me if I am talking over somebody.**"

"**Go ahead.**"

---

"**There is no such thing as the right thing about a room,**" he said. "The same five words are the safest sentence anybody has handed nine men in a wet bay in the Saltmarket this term and the same five words took a thread off a woman of sixty-eight in a bay in Crown Terrace in four seconds on the Monday of week thirty-six, and the bay went off eleven minutes later, and nobody was hurt, and the card was right both times."

"**That is two situations,**" said the chair.

"**That is one sentence and it does not fit on a leaflet.**" He looked at the man of fifty-one. "The difference is not in the words. The difference is whether there is a person thirty feet away, and the only place in this city where anybody can find out whether there is a person thirty feet away is the load, and the load is a room in Bower Street with a rack of four hundred leaflets in it, and about forty-one people are on a thing that cannot be ended this week because a man with a booking came up with a screwdriver on the Wednesday of week thirty-seven and put a plate on the wall with the wrong number on it."

---

The room did what rooms do.

The man of fifty-eight in the fleece stopped counting for four seconds and then started again, and nine people turned round and looked at each other, and the chair said that the panel would take it in order, and the chair is a person whose office signs eleven of a year and who has now been in two rooms in five Fridays and has not been told anything about forty-one people.

Oren Vey stood up at ten past nine.

He is forty-one and he is not on the panel and he is owed nothing and he has been in this room once and gave a fact nobody asked him for, and he stood up and said four hundred and thirty-two in nine words and said that there is no mechanism in this city that can withdraw a document nobody has admitted to issuing, and said that the fact is a fact about a document and is not about the man in the lanyard, and that he has been in this room once and is not going to be thanked.

Then he said the other thing, and he said it to Peter Amado and not to the chair.

"**I have been asked about the man in the district jacket three times since the summer and I have not asked him anything, and I am going to say the reason out loud in a room because I am owed nothing and it is the only free thing I have got: a man who is not asking a question has a much better position than a man who has asked one.**"

---

Peter Amado stood up at twenty past nine and did not open the notebook, which he has done on four Fridays.

"**I have four lines in this and three of them are crossed out in my own hand in my own car and I know that room by now, and the room does not know me, and I have worked out that those two facts are the same fact and I am not going to pretend they are not.**" He put his hands on the front of the chair. "A man in a grey jacket came to a gate on a Saturday in the summer and read nine words out loud to nine of our men, and I have said since the middle of the summer that a thing cannot be safe because a lot of people have been given it, and a thing cannot be dangerous because a lot of people have been frightened of it, and there is no third thing and I have been looking for one for five Fridays."

"**And I have been asked for five Fridays by about nine hundred rooms' worth of people to be the voice of a committee of nine, and I have never been asked whether there is a person thirty feet away, and there was one in that gate, and he was thirty feet away, and he read the words out because he was standing next to the load and not because anybody sent him.**"

---

Then he said the sentence that is going to be true in about four years and that nine people in that room could not hear.

"**And I want to say the other half of it and then I will sit down. I have said a true thing into a microphone five times for a fee out of a levy, and the sentence has never once been in a room where somebody could check it, and this is the first room in five Fridays where a man has said out loud that the only place it can be checked is a room with a plate on the wall, and I am not going to be able to un-hear that, and I have got a wife and a heating bill and I have got nine weeks of series left and I am going to keep coming.**"

He sat down.

---

Corin Vasska stood up at half past nine and the man of fifty-eight in the fleece had not been told that the count was over.

"**There is a problem with this series and it is not the one on the leaflet and I am going to state it once and then I am going to stop, and I have thought about it for nine days and I have got four minutes of it.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**There is no mechanism in this city that can tell the difference between a man doing a thing and a man being asked about a thing in a room with a microphone.**" He put the leaflet down on the trestle table. "A man of twenty-two has a condition that says he may not demonstrate, teach, assess or assist at any practice in that department, on its premises, under its insurance, at any time, unlimited by hours. I told him in a corridor at half past six four weeks ago that this is not that, and I was right, and I am going to say the other half again and it is the half I have not said: the condition stops him doing the thing and it does not stop him being asked about it by a member of the public, and there is nothing anybody can do about that, because the mechanism would have to be a rule about who may be asked, and a rule about who may be asked is a rule about who is findable, and four of the five people on that panel are findable, and two of them are him, and a system chose them out of two job sheets in the week of the thirty-fourth and nobody chose them."

"**So the series is the problem.**"

"**The series is a budget and a specification and nine people and eleven thousand contributors and it is the only thing in this city that has paid for a sentence about a card in nine weeks, and I have been the officer of it for four years, and I am telling a room of forty people that I do not have a way of stopping it and that the reason I do not is that stopping it is exactly the thing I would have to be able to prove.**" He looked at the microphone. "I would like it minuted, and I have said that sentence in this series twice already and both times somebody wrote it down and one of those two was me."

---

The session ended at ten. Nine chairs were still empty. The fee was paid to two people and one of them had produced a true thing and one of them had produced the only question anybody has asked in five Fridays.

At the door a man of fifty-eight in a fleece was still counting, because he had not been told that the count was over.
