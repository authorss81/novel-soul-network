# Chapter 134 — Nobody To Wait For

The room did not go out with a bang and it did not go out with a fault, and there is no ticket, and there is no number, and no engineer has been because nobody has asked for one.

It stopped carrying twenty-nine people at ten past six on the Tuesday evening and it did it the way a room stops doing anything, which is that it simply did not do it, and everybody in the building found out on the Thursday morning from a woman of twenty-nine with nine forms.

---

Room **2-07** is a first-floor room in a building in Bower Street, and it is nine feet by eleven, and it has a bench in it with no key on it, and a corridor outside with a desk in it, and a plate on the wall by the door that says **2-14** in a typeface that is not the building's.

It carries about forty people.

Nine of them are in the long-stay ward on the floor above it, and thirty-one of them are in four buildings on the street outside it, and every one of the forty was on a narrow share that was routed through that room and had been for between five months and about four years.

Nobody in that building could tell you what a share was. There is no card in that building. There is no lanyard in that building. There has never been a card holder in that building or a man in a lanyard whose word anybody in it has been waiting for, and there is not one now.

---

Ianthe Roke is thirty-one and she is in charge of that room and has been for four years, and she has a diary, and the diary is in a drawer in the room, and there is no post above her whose job it is to make a telephone call.

She said the good news out loud, on the Wednesday morning, to about nine people in a corridor, before anything else, and she meant it, and it is the reason she is the one who said it.

"**I want to do the good news first and I want everybody to write it down, because I have been waiting eleven weeks for somebody in an office to tell me that a person was going to come up here and fix this room, and I have not had a telephone call, and nobody is coming, and I have had a plate on a wall for five weeks that says a room number that is not this room, and the reason nobody is coming is that the district's answer is a schedule with a room field in it, and the field says the other number.**"

Somebody at the back said that was not good news.

"**It is not good news, it is the absence of a thing that would have been worse,**" said Ianthe Roke. "**If there were a man in a lanyard for this room then in about four days there would be four hundred rooms waiting for him and a leaflet saying so, and I would not be able to walk into a canteen. There is no man in a lanyard for this room. I have got a bench and a diary and a wall with the wrong number on it, and none of those can be waited for, and I have wanted to say that sentence in this corridor for about five weeks and there has not been a reason until now.**"

---

Then she said the other half.

"**The bad news is that we found out on Thursday morning from a woman of twenty-nine with a clipboard, and not from anything in this room, and the reason is that the way you find out that a relay room has stopped is that somebody stops arriving.**"

---

Nkiru Obi is twenty-nine and she has been at the desk in that corridor for about four years and she has nine forms in a plastic wallet and a pen on a string, and the fourth field on all nine of them is not a name.

"**I do the intake for the ward and the paperwork for the street addresses, and the field is room and bed,**" she said. "**It wants a room and a bed. Not a name. I have been putting the room in for four years and the room is not the thing anybody wants and it is the only thing I am allowed to put in.**"

"How do you know the room has stopped."

"**I do not know it has stopped. I know that on Wednesday morning at about half past seven I went round the ward with the list of nine and I could find four of the nine doing the thing they do on a Wednesday morning, and the other five were not doing it, and they were all right, they were all awake and all right, and they had not been looked at since Tuesday afternoon.**" She turned the wallet over and did not open it. "**And then I went out to the street addresses and I could find twenty-four of the thirty-one. And then I came back upstairs and I did it again at about eleven and I could find twenty-five, because one of the four came back, and that is how I know it is a room and not a ward.**"

"How many can you find now."

"**Twenty-nine. All twenty-nine are all right. That is the part I am going to keep saying, because in about four days somebody is going to write a piece of paper that says a room in Bower Street stopped carrying twenty-nine people, and the two facts are going to be on the same piece of paper and the second one is going to be three lines long and the first one is going to be in it somewhere, and I have got nine forms and none of them has a field for it.**"

---

The eleven came off over nine days and every one of them came off correctly.

The first was on the Tuesday of the week of the thirty-six and the last was on the Friday before, and there is a card in about nine places in one district and four hundred and thirty-two of them, and eleven of the forty had read one, and eleven of the forty held a share for somebody.

**If the word does not come, withdraw and report.**

There is no word. There has never been a word on any of the forty, there is no load, no balance step, no lanyard, no card holder, and nobody has been standing in a corridor in that building for five months waiting to be asked. So the line applies, and the eleven who read it acted on it, and they were right, and the district's own one-page instruction says that a person holding a narrow share who no longer wishes to hold it does not require a reason and does not require a form.

It took four seconds each. There were eleven of them and they were on nine different days.

---

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Ianthe Roke.

"**The rest of it is that a share does not stop when a person stops, it stops when the room notices,**" said Nkiru Obi. "**And the room does not notice, the room is a bench and a corridor and a box, and the box is on the fourth shelf and it has got four hundred lines in it and eleven of them have got a date and a tick on them and none of them has got a name on it because there is no field for a name.**"

"**Then what happened on Tuesday at ten past six.**"

"**Nothing happened on Tuesday at ten past six,**" said Ianthe Roke. "**That is what I have been trying to say to about nine people since half past seven this morning. A room does not fail. It carries what is put through it. Eleven things stopped being put through it and on Tuesday evening the twentieth thing stopped going through and I found out yesterday, and there is no fault, and there is nobody to ring, and if you want a cause then the cause is a card with four correct steps on it and a line on the back of it, and the card is the right card and it is in a printer's shop in the Quiet Quarter and there are four hundred and thirty-two of them and nobody can withdraw one.**"

---

The only route out of a share is through the room.

That is not a design fault. That is what a relay is. A share in this city is a line that goes out through a room and comes back through a room, and the way a person gets off a line is by somebody at the far end saying so, and the somebody at the far end of about half of these has now said so, and the room is where the saying is heard, and the room has nothing to hear it with.

There is no second route. There has never been a second route. **The thing that has gone is not a service. It is the only way anybody had of getting off.**

---

"**How many people can I get off right now,**" said a voice from the door.

It was a woman of about forty-four in a district fleece and she had a telephone out and she was not asking Ianthe Roke, and Ianthe Roke answered her anyway.

"**Twenty-nine, and you cannot get off one of them, and neither can I, and neither can the woman on that desk, and the answer to who telephones a care-link review in this city is a person with a diary and the diary is in a drawer and there are eleven lines in it and ten of them have a tick and none of them has a name on it, and I am the person with the diary and I have been the person with the diary for four years and I am thirty-one and there is no post above me whose job it is to make a call, and I have said that sentence in this building twice this morning and both times to somebody who wrote it down, and this is the second one of those and I would like it noted that I know which one it is.**"

---

The standing exposure was never part of this and it is in the same room.

**Eleven open narrow shares in 2-07, four months past their review date.** They have been in that room since about the spring and nobody has looked at them and the diary has them in it with the same tick.

One of them is a man of fifty-one called Roy Amiss, who is at home with a frame and a stick and a leaflet he put on his own table, and he is not one of the forty, and he is not one of the eleven who came off, and he has a share that has been routed through a bench in a room in Bower Street for four months past its review, and if that room stops carrying things then it stops carrying him too, and he will not know, and there is no review, and the district does not ring.

"**They have been open four months past their review**," said Ianthe Roke, "**and the review is a date and a telephone number, and the telephone number is a firm, and the firm is a call centre, and the call centre is open, and nobody has called it, and if the room goes off today then those eleven are the same as the twenty-nine and they are the only other eleven in this city who are going to find out from somebody else.**"

---

Nobody asked for him.

He is on a panel of five, he is a man of thirty-one with a course exception in a company's system, and about eleven thousand premises pay a levy for a session series, and there was not one question in that corridor about the man in the lanyard, and there was not one face.

He noticed it at about half past eleven in the morning and he sat down on the bench in the room with the door open and the roller shutter half down, and he wrote nine words on the back of a docket, and the nine words are: **nobody in this building is waiting for me. It is the best news of the year.**

Then he wrote two more lines under it, and the second line is the one that has been in a corridor in a dozen rooms and in a docket in a cab and in nine words a woman of thirty-five made him say out loud, and the third line is the one nobody can withdraw.

**About eleven people a week are going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard.**
**About eleven thousand premises pay a levy that bought one line in the middle of last term and a second line a fortnight after it.**
**And about four hundred and thirty-two cards went out of a mailbox in the Quiet Quarter in six weeks into about nine places in one district, and there is no mechanism in this city that can withdraw a document nobody has admitted to issuing.**

He has not said the second and third lines out loud to anybody in four months and he has not put them on a wall and he has not put them in a document.

---

She came in at about twenty past five on the Wednesday evening and she had a coat on and a bag with about nine things in it and she works Fridays, and three of the forty are hers.

Marek Senn was in the corridor and had been since about eleven o'clock in the morning, and there is no form anywhere in this city that has a box for what he was doing there.

"**You are on the third corridor,**" she said.

"**You are on three of them and I did not know that until this morning and I have been sitting in this corridor for about six hours working out whether to tell you.**"

"**You have about four minutes and then I am going upstairs to do a round by hand, which I have been doing since about seven o'clock last night and which I can do until about six in the morning, and I am not going to ask you for anything, and I am not going to ask anybody in a district office for anything, and I have not asked and I am not going to.**"

"**Talia.**"

"**Do not.**" She was not sharp. She was moving. "**Say it here and then go upstairs and do your round, because I am not going to stand in this corridor at twenty past five and find out that you have said something to me.**"

---

"**About eleven people a week are going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard. And about eleven thousand premises pay a levy that bought one line in the middle of last term and a second line a fortnight after it, and a third is going to be minuted.**"

He said it in a corridor in a building in Bower Street, on the Wednesday evening, to a woman with a bag, at about nine words a minute.

He had said it in a print shop in the Quiet Quarter to a man of forty-one and had refused to say it in a corridor, and a woman of forty-five had refused him the same thing on the Friday before last in her own corridor with a tool braid in her pocket and had been right and had told him the cost: the day he says two figures in a corridor is the day there are three numbers in this city instead of two.

"**I know what that is,**" said Talia Venn. "**You have said it once in six months and it is in a docket in a cab and in nine people in a café in the Quiet Quarter, and I am the second person in this city to hear it and I am the first one who has not written it down, and I am not going to, and that is not a favour and I want you to hear that it is not a favour, it is that I have got nine of my own and one of them is on the third corridor of this building.**"

"**I know.**"

"**Then say nothing else about it.**" She went about four steps up the stair. "**I am going upstairs and I am going to do three people by hand for about ten hours and then I am going to go home and sleep, and I have not asked you for anything, and you are not going to be able to help me and I am not going to let you, and if you turn up in this building again before Saturday I will not open the door.**"

And then, from the landing, without coming back:

"**The thing you have not worked out, and I have, and you are not going to like it: it is not a relay problem. A relay is a thing that carries. What is broken is that about twenty-nine people in this city have got nobody to say so to, and the reason there is nobody to say so to is that the only people who ever said so to them were eleven strangers who read a card, and the card was right, and I cannot get twenty-nine of my own back by being right.**"
