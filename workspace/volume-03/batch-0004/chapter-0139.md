# Chapter 139 — Two Of Three

A door, in the sense this movement uses the word, is not a piece of paper and it is not a route and it is not a service. It is a practice that will take an ending off a person who wants one, and will either do it in four seconds or say no in about nine words, and the person whose share it is can refuse it, and there is no mechanism in this city that can tell a practice like that apart from a practice that is a front.

There are three of them and none of them belongs to Marek Senn and one of them is a nurse's, one is a repairer's and one is a courier's, and he went in the order a woman of twenty-four told him to go in, which was nurse first and repairer second and not in a corridor.

---

Sinead Corrigan is thirty-four and she is a district nurse and she filled in a form on the Friday of week thirty-three at about eleven in the morning on her own initiative because a woman in bay four could not answer and the office wanted the returns that day, and nobody asked her, and she put her own name in the box that says *completed by*, and the reason on page four of that form is now public under a heading about safety in about four thousand pockets, and there is a courteous note in a file with her name at the top of it asking the attending service what was understood by *if available*.

She has about eleven rooms on a list of her own that she keeps in a plastic wallet and the list is not a district list.

She heard him out in about nine minutes and then she did not say yes for about four minutes, and what she did in the four minutes was write on the clipboard.

"**I am going to write this down and then I am going to read it to you, and if you do not like it I am going to write it again differently, and you are not going to see either version twice.**"

---

She wrote for about two minutes and then turned the clipboard round.

"**One. Any person may bring an ending to this unit. Not a card. Not a form. A person, with a name on them, who wants something off, and I will do it or I will not, and if I will not I will say why in nine words and the reason will be about the share and not about the person.**"

"**Two. There are about eleven rooms on my own list and they are my rooms and I got them by being in them for four years and they are not the district's and I am not asking the district for anything and I am not telling the district.**"

"**Three. Nothing goes on anybody else's form. Not the nine-field one, not the assurance one, not the one with *if available* on it. There is a different conversation to be had about that form and it is not this one, and I have got a file with my name at the top of it and I am not going to make it longer.**"

She looked up.

"**Four. I am not putting your name on it, and I am not putting mine at the top of it, and if anybody asks me in about four years who started this I am going to say that a woman of thirty-five said no to a hire place in a café in the Quiet Quarter six weeks ago and that I read about it in about four thousand leaflets.**"

---

"**That is four sentences and I have not agreed to any of them.**"

"**No, and here is mine, and it is the only reason I have not agreed yet.**" She put the clipboard down. "You have a leaflet in nine hundred rooms with a job on it and no name on it, and four of the five people on that panel in Crown Terrace are findable, and a man of forty-four stood up in a room on Thursday and said that there is no mechanism in this city that can tell the difference between a man doing a thing and a man being asked about a thing. He is right. And on the same evening a man of fifty-one stood up in the same room and said that the only place the question works is a room with a plate on the wall, and nine people were taking notes."

"**And so the difference between me and a front is going to be that I am a person and you are a practice,**" he said. "That is the whole difference and there is nothing else and I have not got a better one."

"**Then say it the other way round,**" said Sinead Corrigan. "The difference between me and a front is that a front cannot say no in nine words about a share. I can. And the first time I do, to somebody who has been told I will help, is going to be the day this is worth having, and if that never happens then it is not a door, it is a poster, and I would rather be a poster that admits it."

"Agreed."

"**Then I will open it on the Thursday, and not before, and not in a district, and not on a form.**"

---

Civic Spine 7 is in Lattice Ward and it has a corridor and eleven men and a bench and a tool roll and a lead repairer's letter, and Sera Quill is forty-five and she has been in that building eleven years.

He went to her second, on the Friday of week forty-first, at twenty past four in the afternoon, on a day he had been on a callout since eight in the morning, and she was at the bench and she had a braid in her pocket and she had already known he was coming for two days, because a man of thirty-eight had been in her corridor on the Wednesday and had said a nurse's name out loud in a corridor and about nine men had heard it.

That was the seventh refusal of that kind in this case and it was a man of thirty-eight and he had not done it on purpose.

"**You have got four minutes and I have got a bay open,**" said Sera Quill, "and I am going to open it in the first thirty seconds so that you cannot turn it into a conversation."

"**Go on.**"

"**Any person may bring an ending to this building. Not a card and not a phone number. A person. And a mechanic will take it out with his hands and he will do it in about four seconds and he will write the name of the person who asked for it in his own hand on the back of his own job sheet, and his name underneath, and the job sheet goes in the parts cage and it stays there and it is not on a system and it has never been on a system and it is not going to be.**"

---

"**You can refuse it,**" she said, "and the refusal is the whole of it, and a man who refuses is not in trouble and is not written up and is not told about it, and I have put that in the lead repairer's letter in one sentence and I will read it to you and then I am not going to read it to you again."

She took a piece of paper off the bench and held it up and did not hand it over.

"*Any ending brought to this building is carried out or refused at the mechanic's discretion without reference to any other party, and the mechanic's refusal is final and is not a matter for review.*"

"That is a letter, not a policy."

"**It is a letter with one sentence in it and it has been a letter for eleven years about nine different things, and I have added nothing.**"

---

"**And I am not going to tell you why,**" said Sera Quill.

"**You do not have to.**"

"**I want to be clear that I am not going to, and I want you to notice that you did not ask twice, which is the first time in a term and a half that you have not asked twice, and I would like that written down somewhere and I know you cannot write it down, so I have said it.**" She put the paper in her pocket. "And if you ask me in a corridor I will say no, and if you ask me in a mess room on a Friday I will say no, and the reason I am not going to tell you is that the reason is a braid and a bench and eleven men and about eleven years, and four hundred people in this district would know the reason inside a week, and nine of them would be frightened, and there is nothing in that sentence that is for you."

---

"**One more thing and then I have got a bay.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**About forty-one people are on a link that cannot be ended, and a nurse of thirty-four is opening something on the Thursday for eleven rooms, and you are opening something here, and neither of you is doing it for me, and a courier is going to open the third.**" She said it fast. "And I am not going to say the third one's name and I do not know whether I am right about him and I have not asked and I am not going to ask, and I want it noted that a woman of forty-five in a repair shop has just talked about a third door and did not name it, and that is the first time in about four years I have done anything as clever as that."

"**It is not clever.**"

"**It is not clever at all, and that is why it is worth saying, and now get out of my bay.**"

He got out of her bay.

---

At half past four he sat on the bench against the long wall with the parts cage behind him and about nine men in the corridor, and a man of twenty-nine came past and said, in the tone of a man making conversation, that the nurse's name was all over the place this week and did he know she had been in a leaflet.

"**She put her own name in it,**" said Marek Senn. "On the Friday, in a box that says *completed by*."

"**Right,**" said the man of twenty-nine, and went and got a coffee.

And Sera Quill, from the far end of the bench, without turning round, said one sentence that nine men in that corridor heard and none of them wrote down.

"**He put a name in a box on purpose and she put a name in a box on purpose, and four hundred leaflets in this district have got a name in a box on purpose on them, and nine hundred have got a job title, and there is not one person in this city who can tell you which of those two is the thing that frightens a room.**"
