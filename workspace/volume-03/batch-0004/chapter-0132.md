# Chapter 132 — Thirty-One

The committee of the district practice assurance levy met on the Wednesday morning in a first-floor room above a shop of a kind that had gone out of business, and there were nine of them around a table and one of them had a minute, and the minute had been started before anybody spoke.

Corin Vasska is forty-four and he is the officer for the levy and he has been in the job four years, and he read the agenda out in about four minutes, and two items on it were not his and he said so.

"**The last two items came in from the district's own system and I did not put them there. One of them came in on Wednesday at ten past ten in the morning from a night shift and the other came in on Friday from a barrier contractor, and I am going to take them in that order and I am going to read them out in the words they arrived in, because the words they arrived in is about half of what I have been told this year.**"

---

Item one. A nine-field incident report on a company's system, filed at ten past five on the Wednesday of the week of the thirty-five by a night shift supervisor of forty-six with about nine people on nights, and the two fields at the bottom of it are drop-downs with no free text, and there are eleven recognised entries.

Ten of the eleven are a company, a training provider, an employee and a supplier.

The eleventh is a name that came out of a form into a district office about four years ago and has been on about four hundred leaflets and a levy note and a one-page circular ever since, and *other* sits in a free text field underneath, and a free text field is not a source.

Yasmin Okoye did not attend and had not been asked to, and her line in the report reads, in the third field: *source not established at time of completion. I picked the one that fitted.*

"**That is the whole of the first item and it is on nine pages**," said Corin Vasska, "**and eight of those pages are about the drop-down and one of them is that sentence, and I want the room to notice that the one sentence in a document is the one that got written down by the person who had to type something into a box with a thing in it that would not let her leave it empty.**"

---

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and then a woman of about fifty-one in a district fleece said that the drop-down was a reasonable design and that the free text field existed precisely so that a supervisor could record a source she could not identify, and that reading a typed name as an accusation of a group was not what the form was for.

"**Then what was it for,**" said Corin Vasska.

"It was for a person to record what the field could not hold."

"**Then the sentence in the third field is the field working,**" he said. "**And it worked, and it has been working for about four years, and about four hundred leaflets say the same name, and there is no document in this city that joins the leaflets to the field, and I have looked for one for about four days and I have now looked for about nine.**"

---

Item two was worse and it was shorter.

A four-hundred-line visitor record, four years, one barrier, one contract, in another company's building. Eleven of the four hundred lines carry four words in a visitor column. About two hundred and forty of the four hundred lines carry the same four words in a different hand, two or three times a week, on the days the contractors come, and the hand is different every time.

The record is intact. That is the finding. It has not been altered and there is no gap in it and it is the only document anybody has produced in this case that nobody can say was made up, and the reason it is intact is that it was never about anybody.

"**Eleven times out of about four hundred**," said Corin Vasska. "**And two hundred and forty of them are a contractor's day book written in four different hands by four people who were not hiding anything, and the reason this record is worth a committee's time and the other one is not is that nobody in four years put a name next to the eleven.**"

---

And then he asked the question, which he had told a room of forty people on the Thursday of the week of the thirty-six he would be asking.

He asked it to the table and not to anybody in it.

"**How many people in this district have heard the sentence from somebody who was paid to say it.**"

Nobody answered for about eleven seconds.

A man of about thirty-eight in a grey suit said the levy could not answer it and neither could the district and neither could the company, and that the question was unanswerable by definition because the whole of the mechanism is that nobody can produce a list.

"**That is right,**" said Corin Vasska. "**And I want it minuted that I asked it and that a member said that, and I want the reason minuted too, and the reason is that about four hundred leaflets carry a name and a fee and no person, and the one person in this city who has said that sentence for money is a man of about fifty-one with a wife's heating, and he has been to four of my sessions and he has been paid a hundred and forty pounds and travel, and if the honest answer is that we cannot count the people who have heard it then we have a specification that pays for coverage we cannot measure, and I do not know what to do with that, and I have not had that before in four years.**"

"Then what is the number you have got," said the woman of fifty-one.

"**The number I have got is that about eleven thousand registered working premises pay the levy and about nine hundred rooms have had a leaflet, and that is a ratio of about eighty to one, and I have been an officer of a levy for four years and that ratio is the single worst number in this city and I have never once been shown it on a piece of paper.**"

---

"**Then read the specification.**"

He read the specification out loud, which is what that office does and which is the only thing anybody in this city can say about it is that it is decent, and it is two lines long.

*Standing confusion in the trade between a supervised confirmation and a technician's own judgement.*

*The current position of the district's release callout arrangements.*

"**Two lines,**" said Corin Vasska, "**and the second one names no post and no person and no company, and I put it there, and the chair's office signs about eleven of those a year, and about eleven thousand premises pay for the line and about nine hundred rooms have got the leaflet.**" He put the paper down. "**And the answer to the sentence being right about the arithmetic is that the arithmetic is fine and what comes out of the arithmetic is what I do not have a procedure for. About eighty premises to every room with the leaflet in it is a coverage problem, and the only answer to a coverage problem in four years of this office has been more sessions, and I am not going to say that in a minute, so I am going to say it in a room.**"

---

Nobody moved the item.

That is the whole of what happened in about an hour and a half and it is the whole of what has happened in this city for about eleven weeks: the minute says *noted* in the second column, twice, and the levy continues, and the specification keeps its two lines, and the chair's office signs another one, and the district's drop-down keeps its eleven entries.

And then a man of fifty-eight in a fleece, who had been standing by the door for the whole of it with a clipboard, put his hand up, which nobody had invited him to do.

---

"I counted the heads," he said.

"**You are not on this committee.**"

"**No. I count the heads.**" He turned the clipboard round. "**Fourth session. Thursday of the week of the thirty-six. Forty chairs out and thirty-one people in them and about nine of the empties. Fifth of eleven, and we have done five.**"

"**Thirty-one,**" said Corin Vasska.

"**Thirty-one, and I have been counting them for five weeks, and on the second week of the series I had forty-four, and on the third I had thirty-eight, and the first one was forty-nine, and I did not know that was a fall until about two weeks ago and I have not said anything because nobody asked me and I am a man in a fleece and my job is a door.**" He put the clipboard on the table, which he was not supposed to do. "**It is the fourth number in this quarter and it is not yours and it is not a member of this committee's, and I would like it minuted because I am the only person in this room who has ever written anything down about whether this is working.**"

---

The man of fifty-eight in the fleece is Bryn Trethowan and he has counted heads at the door of a free session with a fee paid for five weeks, and he is not on the committee, and he is paid by the levy, and nobody has ever asked him whether the series is working, and the count of **thirty-one** is in the minute in the second column, under *noted*, with his name against it, and it is the only number in the document that was put there by a person who was not paid to have an opinion.

Corin Vasska looked at it for about four seconds and said, "**Thirty-one is not a coverage number, it is an attendance number, and attendance is not what the levy is for, and I have just spent an hour and a half proving that I cannot count the thing the levy is for, and a man at a door has given me the one number in this room that is a fact.**"

"I am not on the committee," said Bryn Trethowan.

"**No. And it is the fourth number in this quarter and it belongs to a man in a fleece and I am going to say the rest of it and I am going to say it once.**" He squared the minute. "**Nobody in this room is going to do anything about a fifth line for about eleven weeks, and eleven weeks is this week, and the reason there is nothing on the agenda is that every document that could have moved in eleven weeks moved on somebody else's authority, and a woman's telephone call on Friday morning, and a form on Wednesday at ten past five, and a contractor's four hundred lines that nobody altered.**"

---

At about half past eleven he came out into the corridor and stood in it for a while, on his own, out of habit, because he is a man who has learned to stand in corridors where the thing he has said can go into about nine people by Thursday.

Marek Senn was not in the corridor and was not in the building and did not know the meeting had happened until the minute was circulated at about four in the afternoon, on one page, with a reference and no attachment, and four of the five things in it he had been told about by somebody other than the man who wrote them.

*Item one: noted. Item two: noted. Attendance, session four: 31. Notes requested: none.*

**Nobody asked for notes.** It is the first document in this case with that sentence in it, and it is a sentence a man in a fleece put in a room by putting a clipboard on a table he was not allowed to put it on.

---
