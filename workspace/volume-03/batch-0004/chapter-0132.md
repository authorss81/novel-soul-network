# Chapter 132 — What Eleven Thousand Buys

The fund's committee meets on the first Wednesday it was due to meet and it meets in a room above a print works in Crown Terrace with nine chairs, a trestle table, a folder, and a projector that does not work and that nobody has tried to make work in about four sessions.

About nine people sit at the table and one of them chairs it, and the chair's office signs about eleven of a thing a year, and the specification the committee exists to administer has two lines in it.

---

Corin Vasska is forty-four and he is an officer of the levy and he is not lying about any number in the folder, and he has read every word of what he is paid to say, and he has wanted to be asked a question for about four sessions.

He was not asked one in the first eleven minutes, and he said so himself, twice, in different words, and one of the nine people at the table wrote it down, and Corin Vasska looked at the man who wrote it and did not object.

"**That is fair,**" he said. "**I said it. Write it.**"

---

The first thing the committee was told was a drop-down.

**Yasmin Okoye, forty-six, a night shift supervisor at a service address in Nine Bridges, about nine people on nights, filed a nine-field company incident report at ten past five on the Wednesday of week thirty-five, and the two fields at the bottom of it are drop-downs with no free text in them.** Eleven recognised entries. Ten of them are a company, a training provider, an employee or a supplier.

The eleventh is a name that came out of a form into a district office about four years ago.

*Other* sits in a free field underneath the two drop-downs, and a free field is not a source, and the form will not submit without a source.

The officer read the whole thing out including the field names, because that is the practice of his office and it is the only thing about his office anybody in this city has ever been able to say is decent, and then he put a hand on the folder and said: "**Nobody chose that. A person of forty-six chose the only one of eleven that was true, and the other ten were not available to her.**"

Nobody said anything for nine seconds and then a man of about thirty-eight asked who the name belonged to, and Corin Vasska said the name belongs to nobody, and the man said that is not an answer, and the officer said:

"**It is the only answer there is. It is on about four hundred leaflets and in about six thousand copies of a circular and it came out of a form about four years ago and after that it was a word. If I say who owns it then somebody in this room will write a name in a minute and in about four years there will be a document in this city with a person's name on it in a line about a group, and a group with a person's name in it is a thing you can point at a frightened room.**"

---

The second thing was a record of about four hundred lines that is intact.

**A barrier on a contract in another company's building. About four hundred lines of a visitor book going back four years. About two hundred and forty of the four words AWAITING RELEASE stamped in the visitor column, two or three times a week on the days the contractors come, in a different hand every time, in a rubber stamp anybody in that trade would recognise.**

The man at it is sixty-one. He has been at that barrier for four years. A man in a grey jacket came to the gate in the summer, wrote nine words out on a bit of pale card, was photographed, and was then told he did not need a copy.

**The record is the only copy. The man is not usable as a witness, and he said so himself, standing up, in about forty people, on the Thursday of week thirty-six, and refused the chair in front of all of them, and the reason he gave is that in about four years there is a document with nine words on it in it and a man of sixty-one is the only person in this city who can be found from it.**

The man who told the committee about the record is twenty-two years old and he works for a district service at grade two four days a week and at grade three on a Friday, and he is not on this committee and was not invited to it and was in the room for nineteen minutes on a pass that a man of thirty-eight gave him at the door without asking for anything.

He did not say which barrier. He was asked twice and he refused twice, and the second time he gave the reason, and the reason was arithmetic.

"**A sheet with two gates on it is a sheet about two men, and neither of them was asked.**"

---

The question Corin Vasska had wanted for about four sessions came at about eleven minutes to twelve and it was not the question anybody expected.

"**How many people in this district are holding a fifth line that nobody has issued.**"

He said he was asking it in the room because the officer had said in forty people on the Thursday of week thirty-four that there is no document in this city joining a leaflet with a job title on it to a drop-down in a company's form, and he had looked for four days, and the committee is nine people and a budget and this is the first question any of them had ever put to him.

There was no answer and he was told there was no answer three times by three different people, and one of them was him, and the third time he said the word *rate*, and a man of thirty-eight said that a rate is a number with a sample behind it, and Corin Vasska said yes, and said:

"**And I am the only person in this room who can produce a sample, and my sample is eleven thousand registered working premises, and about four thousand leaflets went out with the last levy note, and about nine hundred rooms have got a job title on one of them, and I can put those three numbers in a minute this afternoon and every one of them is true and comes out of my own ledger.**"

He put them in the minute. All three. And the three of them are in a document in Crown Terrace now and a man of forty-four is not thanked and a man of twenty-two is not thanked and nobody in the room said his name once.

---

And then he did the thing that made him the best argument this city has got against its own arrangements, which is that he was right.

"**Eleven thousand contributors. Four thousand leaflets. Nine hundred rooms. And a form that will not submit without a source, and a printer in the Quiet Quarter, and a Friday rota that is not my money and not my programme and not in my specification.**" He closed the folder. "**Eleven thousand people have paid for a sentence. The sentence is a line in a specification that asks for the coverage of a question that was asked in a market in the week of the twenty-four. And I can remove the programme on Thursday. I can take the series off the calendar in about eleven weeks and the leaflets stop going out and nobody in this city is ever asked that question in a room again.**"

"**That is true,**" said the man of twenty-two.

"**It is not what follows,**" said Corin Vasska.

---

"**About eleven people a week are going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard,**" he said. "**And that is a Friday. A Friday is not a series. A Friday is a card in date or an exception recorded and renewed, and it is renewed in an office by a man of fifty-two, and it is not on my calendar and it is not in my budget and it is not in the eleven thousand.**" He put his hand flat on the trestle table. "**Take the series off in about eleven weeks and the Friday is still a Friday. And the four hundred and thirty-two cards in about nine places are not a series either. I have looked for four days and there is no document in this city that joins my money to a piece of card printed off a photograph of a postage stamp, and I have stopped looking for one, because the reason there is no join is that the join is the whole difficulty and somebody has been earning a living from the gap for six weeks.**"

The man of twenty-two said: "**Then what do you want the committee to do.**"

"**I want the committee to minute that it has been told two things and has done nothing,**" said Corin Vasska. "**That is not nothing. In about four years somebody is going to say the committee was told, and the minute is the only thing in this city that will say it, and it is eleven words, and nobody has to be a hero in it.**"

---

The committee did that.

The minute runs to a page and about nine of its lines are about the specification and about nine people signed it and one of the nine is a chair whose office signs about eleven of a thing a year.

**A third line went into the specification and it was minuted, and it is the third line, and it is nine words, and it asks for the coverage of a supervised confirmation where the holder is not a named party to the share.**

That is the sentence this volume has been arguing against since the middle of the spring, and it was written by a person who did not want to write it, in about nine words, on a Wednesday afternoon, in a room above a print works, and it was minuted, and the money that pays for it comes from about eleven thousand premises and is honest and clean and goes where it says it goes.

**Nobody in the room said the number three out loud. Nobody noticed it. It is the third time this term a form or a minute has made a choice and it is the first time the choice has been made in a room with a chair in it.**

---

And there was a fourth number in that minute and it belonged to somebody else.

It came in on a stock return that a company attaches to a levy return every quarter, and it is the number of replies in a general enquiries box, and it is **four hundred and one**, and it is on the page because a stock clerk fills in the box on the return whether anybody has asked him to or not.

**Four hundred and one replies to a letter that went out on the Monday of week thirty-one to four hundred and six people, and about nine of the first forty-one got an answer from the company, and nobody in that building has read the other three hundred and sixty.**

Corin Vasska read it out, because he reads things out, and then said the thing that made a man of thirty-eight put his pen down.

"**I did not ask for that,**" he said. "**Nobody asked for that. That is a number about a company's postbox and it is in my minute because a man in a stock office fills in a box on a form, and if I leave it in then somebody in about four years reads a page of a committee minute and finds four hundred and one and thinks it means something about the district, and it means a woman in a building has not opened her own post since the spring.**"

He read it out, and he left it in, and he wrote a line under it that said the number was not sought and was not a rate.

That is the whole of the Wednesday and nothing happened in it.

---

A man of fifty-eight put a plate up in Bower Street on Wednesday afternoon with a booking that carries a job and not a name, and it says 2-14, and the room is 2-07, and the committee that would have been told about it was busy being told two things it could not do anything about.
