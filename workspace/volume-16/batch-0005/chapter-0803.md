# Chapter 803 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In That Second District Where A Woman Of About Sixty-One Who Sits On That Body Was Asked Whether She Was Resigning And Said Yes Before The Question Was Finished, And Gave A Reason That Was About A Room And Not About The District

**A Thursday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district, where a woman of about sixty-one said the word yes before anybody had finished asking the question. It took under a second. Nobody in that room asked her to stay and nobody thanked her.**

---

The board for that Thursday carried eleven names and eleven dates, and the last of the eleven was entered at ten to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Thursday, at the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days except one.

**That card in a rail that takes two is one thousand four hundred and seventy days, two hundred and ten weeks to the day. Line twelve is one thousand three hundred and eighty-six days, a hundred and ninety-eight weeks to the day.**

**Line thirteen is one thousand three hundred and thirty-seven days, a hundred and ninety-one weeks to the day. Line fifteen is one thousand two hundred and eighty-one days, a hundred and eighty-three weeks to the day. The hold is one thousand and ninety-nine days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks to the day, in the second of the four towns.**

**The thing that happened on that Thursday is that a body of about nine chairs lost one of them in about a second, and the woman who lost it had been on it nineteen years, and she gave her reason before she was asked for it.**

She is a woman of about sixty-one. She sits on that body. That body has about nine chairs and has had about nine chairs for as long as anybody in that room can remember and it has no staff and no permanent building and no money.

Nobody asked her whether she was resigning. A man of about thirty-eight began to ask her whether she was resigning and she said yes, and he did not finish the question.

---

**And she said in nine seconds that she was not resigning over the notice, and that she was resigning over the sentence, and that those are two different things and that everybody in that room would print them as the same thing.**

She said the notice is a district leaving and that she is not a district and has never been a district and that nobody had asked her to be anything about that notice.

She said the sentence is four words and a date. She said she has been in that room nineteen years and that in nineteen years the only sentences that have ever come out of that room are the ones four hundred people agree on, and that the four words are the first sentence to come out of that room that nobody agreed on and that nobody had to.

**She said that a body which prints the same reason as a district is a body which has stopped being able to say anything, and that she would rather it had a resignation on it than the sentence.**

Nobody in that room said that she had it wrong. Nobody in that room said the other thing either, and about two of them have said since that this was the correct thing to do and that they have been doing it for nineteen years.

A man of about fifty-two said that nobody agreed with her because a body of about nine people agrees with things on the basis of whether it can carry them, and that this one could not carry this one, and that he is not saying anything about the reason and is saying something about the body.

---

**And then she gave the reason she had actually come to give, and it took her nine seconds, and it was about a room and not about the district.**

She said she has been in that room nineteen years. She said that in nineteen years she has watched about nine chairs on that body be filled and emptied and filled again, and that she has watched a body of about nine people hand a thing to a body of about nine other people across a table and go home.

She said that in nineteen years she has never once seen anybody leave that body.

She said that nobody resigns from it. She said that people stop coming and are replaced and the replacing is done by two people who both of whom turn up, and that a body which cannot lose anybody is not a body, and that it is a set of chairs and a habit and a building somebody's rent.

**She said that she has been the second-oldest thing in that room for nineteen years and that she is now the oldest thing in it, and that this is not the reason, and that the reason is what was said before this and nobody in that room wrote it down.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-four asked her the question that four people in that room wanted answered, and she answered it in nine seconds and she did not soften it.**

He asked what happened to the thing when she went. She said that it would be carried by whoever was already carrying it and that in nineteen years it had never been carried by anybody who was not already carrying it.

He asked whether she would write down what she knew. She said that nobody in that room has ever written down what she knows and that she has not offered it in nineteen years and that she was not going to start on the day she left.

He asked whether there was anything she wanted said about her. She said no, and then she said that she wanted the chair taken off the table and not moved to a wall, and that a chair on a wall is a thing people put up and look at, and that she did not want to be looked at.

---

**Nobody in that room asked her to stay. That was checked afterwards by two people who were in that room and both of them said the same thing, and neither of them was asked to say it.**

A man of about fifty-two said afterwards that nobody had asked her to stay and that he had wanted to and that he had not, and that he had not asked because she had given a reason and a reason is a thing a person has earned.

A woman of about thirty-four said afterwards that nobody had thanked her and that she had wanted to and that she had not, and that thanking a woman for giving up a thing she has held for nineteen years is a way of making the people who did not ask her to stay feel better.

**Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Thursday, and the register of correct acts that made no consequence stood at four on that Thursday and stands at four.**

---

**And the body had about nine chairs on that Wednesday and about eight chairs on that Thursday, and the chair was taken off the table in the four minutes after she said yes and it was not put anywhere.**

A woman of about thirty-four carried it out herself and put it down in the passage and left it there, and said in nine seconds that a chair in a passage is a chair that has been removed and that a chair on a wall would be a different thing, and that she had been asked for a wall and had said no.

The chair is in that passage. It has not been taken out to a yard and it has not been put on a wall and two people have said since that they have walked past it in that passage and not gone round it.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about forty minutes and said nothing and was asked nothing and has said since that he was the only person there who had seen that chair carried out and that he had counted the chairs before and after and told nobody.**

He said that the count was eight and that the count is not a thing anybody printed and that he has not been asked for it and is not going to give it to anybody, and that a number one man keeps is a Tuesday and a number four hundred people have taken is a different object.

He was told a thing and did not repeat it.

---

**And the drawing of nine streets was on the table in that room on that Thursday with no names on it, and nobody put a name on it, and nobody asked for it, and it did not go out of the room.**

A woman of about forty-eight said in nine seconds that the day somebody asks for the drawing with names on it is the day the drawing stops being any use to anybody, and that this will be about the fourth person who asks and that the fourth person will be reasonable.

A man of about thirty-four said that the whole of the last fortnight had been two documents, one with four words on it and one with no names on it, and that he had come to think of them as the same kind of object, and that they are not, and that neither of them can be given away and for different reasons.

---

**The rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and about half an hour on a stair nosing in a second district, where nine of them had been painted over a metal edge and had gone soft.**

A nosing is the front edge of a tread where a foot goes down onto the riser, and a nosing on a wooden stair is a strip of beading let into the front, and a nosing on a fire escape is usually a strip of steel angle bolted to the front edge of a grating.

Nine of those angles had been painted over about four times by somebody who did not know what they were, and the paint had gone into the bolt heads and under them and had started to lift the steel off the edge in about nine places.

He scraped the heads, took the loose scale off with a wire cup, primed the bare steel under the paint edge, and put nine new bolts in with the heads proud so that the next painter cannot get a brush round them.

Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a nosing is not painted over, and that a nosing that has been painted over once is a nosing that will be replaced, and that about four fire escapes in that district have been painted over their nosings and that this is the first one anybody has stopped.

---

**And the second fault on that stair was not the nosing, which is the ordinary shape of a morning's work, and it was a handrail bracket that had been fixed into a hollow wall.**

A handrail bracket is a plate with two holes in it and it is fixed into something, and on that stair the second bracket from the bottom had been fixed into a hollow partition that runs under the plaster, with two plastic plugs and four-inch screws, and the whole bracket moved about a quarter of an inch every time anybody put a hand on it.

He took the bracket off, found a stud in the partition about two inches to the side, cut the plaster back, moved the plate over onto the stud, refixed with two four-inch screws and made good the plaster, and put the rail back on.

Thirty-one pounds was for the nosing and this was nineteen, and he told the man the order, because a stair with nine new bolts looks like a stair somebody has been at.

He said that four handrail brackets in that building are in hollow partitions and that a bracket in a hollow partition is a bracket that will come out one day with somebody's hand on it.

---

**And the fourth fault on that fire escape was not a fault at all, and he spent nine minutes of that Thursday proving that, and two people have since asked him about it.**

The middle flight of that fire escape had a handrail that moved about half an inch at the join between two sections, and everybody who used that stair had assumed for four years that it was loose where it met the newel.

It was not loose. The two sections of rail were joined with a lap plate and four bolts, and the bolts had been done up and the plate had been painted over, and the paint had gone hard in the gap between the two sections and was holding them a sixteenth of an inch apart at the weather end.

He took the paint off the lap with a chisel and a cloth, drew the two sections together, and re-drew up the four bolts. Nothing was replaced and nothing was bought and the repair took nine minutes and cost the man nothing.

He said that a handrail that moves at a join and a handrail that moves at a bracket are two different faults, and that a person who does not know which one they have will put a new bracket on and will still have a rail that moves in four months.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut. Behind her on the back shelf the eighth of eight has been sitting for two hundred and fifty days.**
