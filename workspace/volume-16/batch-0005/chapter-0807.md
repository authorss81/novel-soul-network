# Chapter 807 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road Where It Was Established That Nobody Is Holding The Line Because There Is No Line And That The Practice Is Being Held By About Four People Of Whom None Has Been Asked And Two Of Whom Have Said No Already, And Where A Man Of About Fifty-Two Gave A Figure Nobody Asked Him For

**A Tuesday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district, where four people were established to be carrying a thing that is not written down and where one of them said a number out loud that nobody had asked him for. It was on no page before that Tuesday and it is on no page now.**

---

The board for that Tuesday carried twelve names and twelve dates, and the twelfth was entered at ten to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Tuesday, at the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days except one.

**The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and seventy-seven days, a hundred and fifty-three weeks and six days. Line eighteen is one thousand one hundred and eighty-nine days, a hundred and sixty-nine weeks and six days. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and nineteen days, a hundred and forty-five weeks and four days.**

**Those four rooms and one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and seventy-one days, two hundred and ten weeks and one day. The card propped upright in the rail by that door is one thousand four hundred and seventy-five days, two hundred and ten weeks and five days.**

---

**The thing that got established on that Tuesday is that nobody is holding the line, because there is no line, and that four people are carrying a thing that nobody has asked them about, and that two of the four had already said no before anybody asked them anything.**

A man of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that this is what is happening and that anybody who says otherwise is describing a thing that used to exist.

He said that he had heard on the Monday that there was no line left and that he agrees with the finding and disputes the noun, and that what the four of them are doing has a name and that the name is not a line.

He said that they are not holding a line. He said that they are holding a habit, and that a habit is not a line and does not know it is being held, and that a habit stops on a Tuesday.

**He said that this is not an argument against anybody doing it and that he has not asked any of the four to stop and will not, and that he has said this out loud so that nobody in that room builds anything on the four of them.**

---

**And a man of about fifty-two said in nine seconds that a habit does not stop on a Tuesday, and then he gave a figure, and nobody had asked him for it and he had not shown anybody.**

He said the number is about nine weeks and that he has costed it himself and that he costed it in a notebook on a day off, in about eleven minutes and that the notebook is a private thing and is not on anybody's list because there is no list.

He said that the nine weeks is how long four people can keep doing it at the rate they are doing it, without anybody being asked and without anybody being paid, and that it is not a promise and it is not a plan.

He said that he does not intend to show the notebook and that he has said the number out loud on purpose so that if it turns out to be wrong then it is a number four people heard and not a number one man kept.

**Nobody in that room wrote the nine weeks down. About two of them have said since that this was deliberate and about two have said that it happened because nobody had a pencil, and that both of those are the same thing.**

---

**And the four are not a list, and there is no list, and nobody in that room could produce one, and two people in that district have said that a list would be the fourth sensible thing anybody has done in a fortnight.**

The man of about fifty-two is one of the four. A woman of about forty-eight is one of the four and said no on that Tuesday before she was asked and said that a no given in advance is cheaper than a no given after nine weeks.

A man of about thirty-one is one of the four and has said since that he said no before anybody asked him, and that a woman of about thirty-eight says she said it first, and that neither of them is going to be asked about it again.

A man of about thirty-eight is one of the four and has still not been asked, and has now had about a month, and has said twice in that room that being on a list of four is not the same as being asked.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that the nine weeks was the wrong number to say out loud and said so in the same nine seconds in which she said that it should not have been said.**

She said that a number said in a room of about nine people is a number nine people have, and that a number four hundred people have is a different object, and that four hundred people are going to be told about nine weeks by somebody before the nine weeks is out.

She said that this is not an argument against the man of about fifty-two and that he said the number on purpose for a reason and that the reason was decent.

She said that the second nine weeks is now in nine mouths and that it is not agreed by anybody and that nobody in that room agreed to it, and that about two of them said afterwards that a number nobody agreed to is the only kind this city has ever produced.

---

**Nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody on that Tuesday, and the register of correct acts that made no consequence stood at four on that Tuesday and stands at four.**

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about an hour and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that he has now been in that room five times in a fortnight and has been asked nothing on any of them.**

He said that a man of twenty-two is the only person in that room that everybody there met at a counter in a first district, and that this is a thing he chose when he was twenty-one and has never been able to give back.

He was told a thing and did not repeat it.

---

**The rest of that Tuesday was a counter until about six and about forty minutes on a cistern in a second district, where the seat had been loose and the fault was not the seat.**

A cistern is a box on a wall with a flush valve in it and a lever out of one side, and a loose seat is nearly always the hinge bolts, and on that cistern the hinge bolts were sound and the cistern itself was moving.

The cistern was hung on two wall brackets into a partition that had a stud about four inches behind one of them and nothing at all behind the other, and the bracket with nothing behind it had been put in with four-inch screws and a hollow wall plug and had been moving in the wall for about four years.

He took the cistern off, moved the bracket over onto the stud, refixed, and packed the fixing with a setting block, and put the cistern back on its flush and shimmed it level so that it does not rock when it is full.

Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a cistern that rocks when it is full is a cistern that is working against its flush every time, and that four cisterns in that building are on hollow partitions.

---

**And the second fault on that Tuesday was a fire door on a stair in the same building, which had been propped open with a length of batten since the summer.**

A fire door that is propped is a fire door that has been a door for nine months, and the fault underneath is usually a closer that is too fast or a hinge that has dropped, and the batten is the answer somebody found in about four seconds.

The closer on that door had been set to its fastest and the hinges had dropped about an eighth of an inch, so the door was closing onto the frame lip and jamming, and about four people had taken the batten out and put it back.

He took the closer off, reset it to its slowest setting, packed the hinge knuckles to lift the door a sixteenth of an inch, and took the batten away. Nineteen pounds.

He told the man that a door that jams on closing is a door that has been propped, and that four doors in that building have battens and that two of them were propped for the same reason as this one.

---

**And a manhole cover in a lane behind that building had a broken frame, which is the fault that gets a hole in a road for four months and then a temporary plate and then a hole again.**

A frame carries a cover and a frame that has cracked across one corner lets the cover rock, and a cover that rocks knocks the frame further in about four years, and the four months is how long a council takes.

He took the cover off, found the frame had been cracked for about two years and had a length of angle welded inside it by somebody, lifted the whole frame out, dug the bed down, and set a new one on a concrete collar.

Fifty-five pounds. He told the man that a welded frame is a repair that costs half as much and lasts a third as long, and that four lanes in that district have frames that have been welded, and that a temporary plate is what a council puts over a frame it has decided not to replace.

---

**And a yard tap in that lane had a washer gone at the spindle and had been left running all night on the Friday by somebody who did not know it was running.**

A running tap overnight in a lane in this city is about nine thousand gallons and it is on four people who live on that lane, and it is a fault that nobody has to be a plumber to find and everybody walks past.

He came back on the Tuesday because the man had told him about it, replaced the washer and the gland nut, and put a quarter-turn stop cock on the pipe below it so that the next time it goes the man can shut it himself.

Eleven pounds. He told the man that a stop cock below a yard tap is four pounds of brass and is the difference between a night and about four hours, and that nine taps in that district have no stop cock under them.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut. The eighth of eight has sat on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and fifty-five days and it is still not read.**
