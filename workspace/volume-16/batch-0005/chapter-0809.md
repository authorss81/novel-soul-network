# Chapter 809 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In That Second District Where A Man Of About Thirty-Four Was Asked To Write Down The Second Half Of A Sentence And Said In Four Seconds That He Did Not Know It, And Where A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Said That It Did Not Have To Be True, And Where He Wrote It On The Back Of A Docket Sheet And Gave It To Nobody

**A Thursday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in that second district, where a sentence was written down for the first time in a fortnight and given to nobody. Two people in that room said afterwards that it was nearly what was wanted and neither of them said how nearly. Nobody has asked either of them.**

---

The board for that Thursday carried twelve names and twelve dates, and the twelfth name was entered at ten to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Thursday, at the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days except one.

**That card in a rail that takes two is one thousand four hundred and seventy-seven days, two hundred and eleven weeks to the day. Line twelve is one thousand three hundred and ninety-three days, a hundred and ninety-nine weeks to the day, and line fifteen is one thousand two hundred and eighty-eight days, a hundred and eighty-four weeks to the day.**

**Line thirteen is one thousand three hundred and forty-four days, a hundred and ninety-two weeks to the day. Line fourteen is one thousand three hundred and nine days, a hundred and eighty-seven weeks to the day. The nineteen, last line in use, is one thousand one hundred and sixty-nine days, a hundred and sixty-seven weeks to the day.**

---

**The thing that happened on that Thursday is that a man of about thirty-four had the second half of the sentence in his head and did not write it down for about an hour, and then wrote it down, and gave it to nobody.**

He has had it since the Friday of that week, six days. A woman of about twenty-nine said on the Friday that the four words say when and not what, and that nobody has written the second half, and that the second half is the whole of it.

It was said on the Tuesday, in that room, that the second half is not one sentence and is not four words, and it was not said by him. He said on the Wednesday that four words to one man who locks a gate is not four hundred people, and he said on the Monday that a date which has already failed once is not going to mean anything the second time, and both of those were him.

**And on that Thursday he was asked directly whether he would write it down, and he said in about four seconds that he did not know it.**

He said that the four words are the only true sentence anybody has written in this city in about eleven years, and that a sentence that is true is a sentence that cannot be added to, and that adding to it is how four hundred people end up being told something that is nearly true.

**He said that nearly true is a thing four hundred people can be told and cannot check, and that this is the whole of what he has been afraid of since the Tuesday.**

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that the second half does not have to be true, and that it has to be the thing they would be told anyway, and that the difference between those two is four hundred people finding out on a Tuesday.**

She said that a true second half is a thing nobody in that room can produce and that they have been not producing it for four days, and that four days of not producing it has left four hundred people with a date and nothing.

She said that a person who is going to be told something on a Tuesday will be told whatever is to hand, and that what is to hand is the most ordinary version of it, and that the ordinary version is not a lie and is not the truth either.

She said that writing down the ordinary version and getting it in first is not the same as writing down what is true and it is the only thing available on a Thursday.

**He asked her what the ordinary version was and she said she did not know either and that they would have to find it between them, and that this was a worse thing to do than finding out what was true and about nine seconds longer.**

---

**And they found it in about forty minutes, and it is eleven words long, and it concerns what happens to the work rather than why it stops, and it was written on the back of a docket sheet and given to nobody.**

*A person is told what to do and does that instead.*

**He wrote it in his own hand on the back of a fresh docket sheet at about half past five and put it in the inside of the docket book, where the drawing of nine streets is not, because the drawing is on a different page and he would not have them read as a pair.**

Nobody in that room read it after he wrote it. He said that he had read it to himself once and that he was not going to read it to anybody else, and that a sentence he has read out loud to nine people is a sentence nine people have, and that about nine people is the problem.

**Two people in that room have said since that it is nearly what was wanted and neither of them has said how nearly, and nobody has asked either of them.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-eight said in nine seconds that the sentence does not concern the district, and that this is the only thing about it that holds.**

He said that whatever else it is, it neither says why a district leaves nor mentions four hundred people, and that this is the first sentence anybody has produced in a fortnight that concerns the work and not the people.

A woman of about twenty-nine said that this is what she said on the Friday and that nobody listened to her then, and that she has said it twice since and that the second time she was listened to.

**Nobody in that room said the sentence was correct. About two of them said it was the best of anything anybody had produced since the Tuesday and about two said that best and correct are two different things and that it is nine days old.**

---

**Nobody in that room could say what happens to a sheet that has been out of a building once, and this was asked because the docket book has been out of a building, and nobody in that room could answer it.**

A woman of about forty-eight said in nine seconds that a sheet that has been out of a building comes back with a person who has read it and nobody knows which person, and that this city has no arrangement about that and has never had one.

A man of about fifty-two said that the book of returns about hinges came out of a building in the week before last and came back, and that about two hundred lines of it carry one man's name in the middle of the line, and that nobody in that office has been told what a release line is.

**He said that a thing which comes back is not the same as a thing which has not been altered, and that this city has no way of asking, and that this is the fourth time in a fortnight that the answer has been the same two sentences.**

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about an hour and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that eleven words is a different number from four and that eleven words is a sentence somebody can disagree with.**

He said that four words cannot be argued with because there is nothing in them to argue about, and that eleven words has a subject and a verb and therefore has a person in it who is doing something, and that this is the first sentence in a fortnight that has somebody in it.

He was told a thing and did not repeat it. He has said that he has not been asked for it and is not going to ask.

---

**The rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and about an hour on a notice board in a first district, where a frame had been mounted with four screws and two of them had missed the wall.**

A notice board is a frame and a sheet of cork and a glass or a plastic face, and the frame is either let into the wall or fixed to it, and fixing it to a brick wall with four screws and no plugs is a frame that will come off in a hard frost.

Two of the four screws had gone into a joint and had found nothing. He took the frame off, drilled four new holes properly, fitted plugs, and re-fixed, and then found the board itself had been bowed by the weight of about nine years of paper on it and was not lying flat on the wall.

He took the cork out, flattened it by warming it and pressing it under boards for two hours, put it back, and trimmed about an inch off two edges that had been trimmed before and had been trimmed again. Nineteen pounds.

He told the man that a board that has been written on for nine years and has not been taken down has a bow in it, and that four boards in that building are bowed and that one of them is due to come off the wall.

---

**And the second fault on that board was not the frame, which is the ordinary shape of a morning's work, and it was a glass face that had been left in a wooden rebate on a putty line that had gone.**

A glazed board has a rebate, a putty line and a glass, and putty is a thing about four years old, and a glass on putty in the fourth year is a glass on nothing.

He took the glass out on a suction cup, cut the old putty out, put a new bedding on, and refitted the glass on a screw and bead at the top corner so that the next time it moves it moves about a quarter of an inch and not out.

Thirty-one pounds. He told him the order, because a man who has paid for a frame will say the glass was sound.

He said that about four glazed boards in that district are on putty and that three of them are on the same street, and that a glass on putty in a hard frost is a glass that goes into a corridor.

---

**And a counter in that same building had a till drawer that would not stay shut, which is the reason about nine counters in this city have a coin off the floor by the till every evening.**

A till drawer runs on a runner and is held by a spring catch, and a drawer that will not stay shut has either a catch that has gone slack or a runner that has dropped a quarter of an inch at the back.

It was the runner. He took the drawer out, took the two screws out of the runner, packed the back end up about a sixteenth of an inch, and refitted, and the catch came back with it and did not need touching.

Eight pounds. He told the man that a drawer that will not stay shut gets a strap or a weight put on it, and that both of those are ways of not moving a runner.

---

**And a display hook on the counter had come out of a chipboard shelf, and chipboard is the whole of that fault, and four counters in that district have display hooks out of chipboard.**

He filled the hole with a length of dowel set in glue, drilled the hook into the dowel rather than into the board, and told the man that the shelf is chipboard and that a hook in chipboard is a hook that will come out with the goods on it.

Eleven pounds. He told the man that four shelves in that building are chipboard where somebody expected a board, and that a person who wants a hook that holds has to have a batten behind it put in first.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut. Behind her, on the shelf, the eighth of eight has been sitting for two hundred and sixty-two days and nobody has opened the binder.**
