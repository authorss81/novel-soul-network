# Chapter 810 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Counter In A First District Where Eleven Words Were Said Out Loud Once In A Room By A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Had Been Asked To Carry Them And Who Said In Nine Seconds That He Is The Person Every One Of Four Hundred People Knows, And After Which Nobody Agreed To Any Of It

**A Friday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district, where eleven words were said out loud once by a man who had been asked to carry them and had said no first. Nine people heard it. Nobody agreed to it. Nobody thanked him and nobody forgave anybody.**

---

The board for that Friday carried fourteen names and fourteen dates, and the fourteenth name was entered at ten to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Friday, at the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days except one.

**Those four rooms and one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and seventy-four days, two hundred and ten weeks and four days. That card in a rail that takes two is one thousand four hundred and seventy-eight days, two hundred and eleven weeks and one day, and the post at that corridor end is one thousand and twenty-two days, a hundred and forty-six weeks to the day.**

---

**The thing that happened on that Friday is that four hundred people found out what the second half is, and that it was said out loud once in a room, and that the person who said it said no first and then said it.**

Nine people were in that room. A man of about thirty-four said eleven words into it. Nine of them can say the eleven words now. Four hundred people use that district. Nobody has counted any of those.

**The eleven words concern what happens to the work. They do not touch the district, or why it stops, or four hundred people.**

---

**And he said in nine seconds that he is the person every one of four hundred people knows, and that this is a thing a person can refuse, and that refusing is not a fault.**

He said that a man who carries a sentence into a district where he is known becomes the district's man in about four hundred days whether he meant to or not, and that he has been avoiding that since the Tuesday the four words were written.

He said that a man of about thirty-eight said the same thing to him fifteen days ago and got no thanks for it either, and that he had not thought of it, and that he has had about nine of those this year. He said that a woman of about thirty-four told that room on the same day the opposite, that nobody in this room is going to be known for saying four words, and that she was right about that as well.

**He said that he had come to that room to say no, and that four people in it had not believed he was going to, and that this was his fault and not theirs.**

Nobody in that room argued him back from it and nobody pushed him towards it.

---

**And she said the sentence has more than four words in it and a date, and that the point is still not the person, and that the second half is about a practice and not about the district.**

She said that the four words have a number of words in them that anybody can hold and that eleven words is a number that a person can be heard saying, and that this is arithmetic and not courage.

She said that the person who says it is not the point, and that she said this on the Thursday a fortnight ago in a room of about nine people and that four of them did not agree with her then and that two of them have agreed with her since.

A man of about thirty-eight said in nine seconds that this is the third time this fortnight that somebody has said the point is not the person, and that the third time is the first time anybody has said it and then done it.

---

**And he said the eleven words at about ten past five on that Friday, in about nine seconds, into a room with nine people in it, and he said them once and did not say them twice.**

He said them at the end of a table and not to anybody in particular and not to a face, and about two of the nine have said since that this was deliberate and two have said that he would have said them to a face if he had been asked to.

**Nobody wrote them down. Nobody asked him to write them down and nobody asked him to say them again, and four people in that room have said since that this was correct, and four have said that it was not, and that it should have been written down.**

A man of about fifty-two said that four hundred people finding out on a Friday from a voice in a room is exactly what happens to a sentence, and that the whole difference between this and a thing people are told is that they heard a man hesitate for nine seconds.

---

**And the register of correct acts that made no consequence stood at four on that Friday and stands at four, and a man saying a sentence out loud once in a room of nine people is not on that register and has not been counted on it.**

Nobody thanked him. He said in four seconds that thanking him would be a second thing to carry, and that this is the argument he lost on the Thursday and has not stopped losing.

**Nobody forgave anybody on that Friday and nobody was forgiven, and nobody in that room said that anybody had done anything to anybody.**

---

**And nobody could say on that Friday how many people can say the eleven words, and about two of the nine said nine and about four of the nine said a great many more and about two said that this city does not know things like that and has never known one.**

A woman of about thirty-four said that her figure of nine for the four words has not changed since the Thursday before last, and that a sentence with eleven words in it moves faster and further than a sentence with four, and that a number for the faster one will be wrong inside two days.

A man of about thirty-one said that he does not know the answer, that the reason is nobody has asked, and that the reason nobody has asked is that everybody who could ask has been busy saying it.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about fifty minutes and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that the sentence went past him at about four feet and that he could have said it and did not and that this is the whole of what he did.**

He said that he had offered to carry the four words on a Thursday three weeks ago and had been refused in four seconds for a reason that was true, and that the man who refused him has now done the thing he offered to do and refused it first.

He was told a thing and did not repeat it. He has said that he wrote nothing down and that nobody asked him to.

---

**The rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and about an hour on a counter in the same building where a coin tray had been fixed with four screws into a chipboard shelf and had worked loose.**

A coin tray is a shallow pressed tray with a rolled lip that stops a coin going over the edge, and it is fixed to a shelf about eleven inches above a counter, and four screws into chipboard is four screws into about four pounds of glue and wood.

He filled the four holes with dowel set in glue, drilled the tray's own holes out and fixed it into the dowel, and set it about a quarter of an inch further back than it had been so that a coin dropped in rolls towards the till and not towards the edge.

Eleven pounds. He told the man that the tray was not the fault and that the shelf is, and that four counters in that district have trays on chipboard and about none of them have been looked at.

---

**And the second fault on that counter was not the tray, which is the ordinary shape of a morning's work, and it was a lip on the counter itself that had been re-veneered in about four places with a strip of a different wood.**

A counter lip takes the wear of about four years of forearms and bags, and a patch in a lip is the repair that everybody makes and it works for about a year and then the patch moves.

The four patches had all moved about a sixteenth of an inch and two of them had lifted at one end. He took them out, matched new strips from the offcut, bedded them on the frame and not on the veneer, and pinned them at the ends with a fine cut nail.

Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a lip is not a shelf and is not a board, and that a patch bedded on veneer will move, and that four counters in that district have patches and one of them has nine.

---

**And a till counter in the same building had a key under the mat, which is the arrangement about four counters in this city have had since somebody could not remember which till they were on.**

A key under the mat is not theft and it is not a convenience either. It is a thing a person does on a Friday when the till key has gone home in somebody else's pocket and there is a queue.

He took the till key off its fob, cut a second one, put the second one in an envelope in the office drawer with the date on it, and told the man that a key under a mat is found by a person who did not come for it.

Eight pounds. He told the man that four counters in that district have a key under the mat and that at none of the four does anybody know whose it is or when it was put there.

---

**And a display of shelves at the end of that counter had been fixed to the wall through a length of plasterboard, which is the reason about four displays in this city come away with the goods on them.**

He took the whole display down, found the fixings had gone into plasterboard and not into studs, moved the fixings across onto two studs, and refitted it on the same holes where he could and on new ones where he could not.

Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a shelf fixed to plasterboard carries the goods and not the shelf, and that a display which has been leaning out for a year has probably been fixed to plasterboard, and that four displays in that building are.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the binder on the back shelf behind her is shut and the eighth of eight has been in it for two hundred and fifty-eight days.**
