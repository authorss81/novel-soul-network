# Chapter 805 — A Sunday, A Yard In A Second District That Opens At A Weekend And Was Already Open, And No Bench And No Counter Because There Is Neither On That Day, And A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Did Ordinary Work All Of It And Found A Thing He Has Been Carrying For Eleven Years Was Easier To Carry, And Said So In Four Seconds And Nobody Has Said Since That It Was Anything But The Cold

**A Sunday began at a gate in a second district rather than at a bench, went through a yard that opens at a weekend and was already open, and came back to a counter that was shut all day because there is no counter on that day. The shutter came down at about two. It is the only day of this stretch of days on which it comes down at that hour.**

---

The day before this one carried nothing at all, and the day after this one is a Monday, and this one is the only one of its kind in this stretch of days and everybody in this city knew that on the Friday.

The board for that day carried no names and no dates, which has happened about nine times in eleven years, and the shutter came down at about two and not at ten.

**Those four rooms and one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and sixty-nine days, two hundred and nine weeks and six days, and the card propped upright in the rail by that door is one thousand four hundred and seventy-three days, two hundred and ten weeks and three days.**

**The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand and thirty-five days, a hundred and forty-seven weeks and six days, one corner torn, eight unfinished, and not two of them have been laid against each other. On the back shelf behind a woman of about thirty the eighth of eight has been sitting for two hundred and fifty-three days.**

---

**The thing that happened that day was that a man of about thirty-four spent nine hours on ordinary work and found that a thing he has been carrying for about eleven years sat easier, and said so in four seconds to a man he was standing beside, and that two people have said since that it was the cold.**

It was a gate post. It had rotted at the ground on a gate that opens into a yard, and it was one of two posts and the other one was sound, and the gate had been leaning about a quarter of an inch for two years and everybody had stopped mentioning it.

He dug the rotted post out, cut it off below the rot, trenched the hole back to firm ground, set a new post in concrete with the top of the concrete below the ground line, and hung the gate back on it. Nineteen pounds and about an hour.

He was on that post from about half past six in the morning until half past four in the afternoon, and he did five more things that day, and he was paid for six of them.

---

**And the thing he has been carrying for about eleven years is a plate of iron with four slots cut in it, and it came up that day because he was standing in a yard with a shovel in his hand and nothing in his head.**

He did not say what it was. He said, in four seconds, to a man of about fifty-two who was there to buy six feet of wire, that it was easier, and that it had been easier for four months, and that he had not said so to anybody.

The man of about fifty-two said in nine seconds that he had carried a thing for nine years and that the day it got easier was the day somebody mentioned it, and that this is a thing people say to each other in a yard and mean nothing by.

**A man of about thirty-four said that the cold does that, and that it had been four degrees below what it ought to have been, and that two people in that district have said the same thing and that neither of them was him.**

Nobody agreed with either of them and nobody said that the man of about thirty-four was talking about a plate.

---

**And nine people in that district said the four words over that weekend, and nobody in this city knows which nine, and one of the nine was not asked and would not say.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that a figure of nine is the first number anybody has had for it since the Thursday before last and that it is the same figure, and that a weekend doubles the mouths in a district without changing the people in it.

A man of about thirty-eight said that the yard was open, and that a yard at a weekend is the only place in that district where a person who has decided nothing is standing next to nine people who have decided something, and that this is a fact about yards and not about sentences.

**Nobody counted anything. Two people in that district said afterwards that counting it would be the first sensible thing anybody does and two said it would be the end of it, and both of those have been said on every day of this stretch of days and neither has been acted on.**

---

**And the shop counter was not shut at ten because there is no shop counter on that day, and the bench was not open either, and the four rooms off that service road were shut and dark and stayed shut.**

A man of about thirty-four said afterwards that he had spent that day in a yard, on a gate post and on a length of wire, and that he had not thought about the second half of the sentence for nine hours, and that this was the first time in a fortnight it had not been in his head.

A woman of about thirty-four said that this is not a finding about anybody, and that a day off is only free for a person who has something to be off in front of, and that two people who have said that in this city have said it about other days and not about this one.

---

**And nobody said anything that day, and nobody told anybody anything, and four hundred people in that district did the ordinary work of a weekend in the ordinary way, and the day in the middle of the two carried nothing at all.**

A man of about fifty-two said in nine seconds that it is the day this city has the smallest amount of anybody in it, and that the smallest amount of anybody in a place is when a thing can be said to somebody, and that nobody used it.

He said that the two days at the end of a week in this city are for doing things that were decided on a Wednesday, and that nothing is decided on the second of them because there is nowhere to decide it, and that this is not a complaint.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that yard for two hours holding the length of post while it was set, and he did not know what else was in the yard and he was not told.**

He has said since that he spent that day carrying one end of a post and that this was the whole of it, and that a man of about thirty-four had asked him to come and he had come because he was asked.

He was not thanked. He has said that he was not thanked for two hours of holding a post and that he would have been uncomfortable if he had been.

---

**And the rest of that day was six feet of wire cut and measured and paid for, and a length of chain on a handcart with one link cut to size by somebody's predecessor.**

He put the six feet on the roll, measured it against the man's own hand span, and cut it with a proper cutter rather than a hacksaw, which puts a burr on wire and which is why wire cut with a hacksaw does not go into a hole.

He found the strained link while the man was counting out the money. That link had been put in about four years ago by a size under the rest of the chain and had been closed with a twisted wire, and the cart had been running on it for four years.

He took the wire off, took the light link out, put a proper joining link in and closed it with a shackle. Nineteen pounds was for the wire and this was eleven, and he told him the order.

He said that a strained link in a chain is one that has been stretched and not replaced, that four chains in that district have one, and that a twisted wire is what is holding the next one from being noticed.

---

**And a bus shelter in a third district had a bench in it loose at one end, which is the thing a person notices about a shelter and not the thing that is wrong with it.**

The bolts holding that end of the bench had gone round in the frame by about an eighth of an inch and the bench rocked. He took the bolts out, packed the frame with two thicknesses of slate, drilled the holes a quarter of an inch out, and refitted with longer bolts.

Eight pounds. He told the man that the packing will hold for four years, and that the fault is a bracket bolted into a hollow steel section and not into a plate, and that four shelters on that road have the same bracket.

---

**And a cold water standpipe in that same yard had a tap that would not shut off, which is the fault that gets a yard shut for a day and not the fault anybody plans for.**

The standpipe is a main fed from below ground with a drain off and a tap at the top, and the drain cock under it had been left a quarter turn open by somebody in the spring, which is not enough to show and is enough over a summer to put a puddle against the foot of a wall.

He found it by the wet patch, closed the drain cock all the way, took the top off the standpipe, and found a washer at each end that had gone hard. Eleven pounds and forty minutes.

He told the man that a drain cock left a quarter turn open is the commonest fault in this city and costs nothing to find and about a hundred pounds to find late, and that four yards in that district have one.

---

**And a handrail bracket in that yard had been welded to a rail instead of bolted, which is a repair that cannot be undone and which about nine people in this city have had done to them.**

Somebody had a rail come loose, could not find a drill, and had it welded. The weld is sound and it is on the wrong side of the bracket, and a rail welded on the wrong side cannot be adjusted and has to be cut off to be moved.

He cut it off with a grinder in nine minutes, cleaned both faces, re-bolted the bracket through the back of the rail, and dressed the cut. Twenty-two pounds.

He told the man that a weld on a handrail is a repair for a broken rail and not for a bracket that has come out of a wall, and that the bracket in that yard had been welded because the wall behind it is hollow and nobody had found out.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut. The eighth of eight is on the back shelf behind her and has been there for two hundred and fifty-three days.**
