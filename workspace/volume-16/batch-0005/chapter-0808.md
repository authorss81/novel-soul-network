# Chapter 808 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where About Nine People Were In A Room And Eight Chairs Were Used And The Ninth Stood Empty And Was Not Moved, And Where A Woman Of About Sixty Said That A Chair Is Not Removed On A Wednesday And Where A Man Of About Thirty-Four Said In Nine Seconds That An Empty Chair In That Room Is A Sentence

**A Wednesday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished on a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where nine people were in a room and eight chairs were used and the ninth stood empty against the wall from six until about seven. Nobody moved it. Two people in that room said it should have been moved and one of them said it should not.**

---

The board for that Wednesday carried eleven names and eleven dates, and the last name was entered at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Wednesday, at the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days except one.

**Line eighteen is one thousand one hundred and ninety days, one hundred and seventy weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and seventy-eight days, one hundred and fifty-four weeks to the day. What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and sixty-two days, one hundred and sixty-six weeks to the day.**

---

**The thing that happened on that Wednesday is that a chair stood empty in a room in which about nine people had been sitting about nine days earlier, and nobody moved it, and the room spent an hour on why.**

Nine people went up that stair between half past six and seven. Eight chairs were used. The ninth stood against the wall where it had stood for nineteen years and it stood empty for about an hour and it is still there.

**A woman of about sixty said in nine seconds that a chair is not removed on a Wednesday, and that it is noticed on a Wednesday and gone on the next one, and that nobody in that room is going to move a chair today.**

She said it standing at the window end and she said it to the room and not to the man who had mentioned the chair, and the man who had mentioned the chair did not say anything else.

She said that a thing that has been moved on a Wednesday is a thing that was moved, and that a thing that has not been moved is a thing that is still there, and that about four hundred people are going to hear about this room and none of them is going to be in it.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that an empty chair in that room is a sentence, and that about four hundred people are going to read it as one, and that this is the first time anybody in this city has said that and it is a thing about chairs.**

He said that a chair in a room in that district is read the way a line in a notice is read, and that nine people who walk past that room in a week will count the chairs and about four of them will notice the empty one and about two of those will be told about it by somebody else.

He said that he does not want that and that he cannot stop it and that he is not asking anybody to move the chair back, because putting it back is worse.

**She said that that was the first useful thing anybody had said about the chair in two days and that nobody had said it and that she had been waiting for it since the Thursday.**

Nobody in that room agreed with him about what should happen to the chair and everybody in that room agreed with her about what he had just said.

---

**And about four hundred people are not in this city and nine of them once had a network, and the chair says nothing about any of that, and nobody in that room tried to make it.**

A man of about fifty-two said in nine seconds that a chair is the cheapest thing in this city to put in a room and that it is the only thing in that room that nobody has to be in charge of.

A woman of about thirty-four said that the chair had been there nineteen years and that she did not know who put it there and that this was the correct state of it.

**Nobody in that room said whose chair it was, and two people in that district have said since that each of them has claimed it for a different chair.**

---

**And the ninth chair in that room is against a wall with its back to everything in it and it is not the chair that stood empty, and it did not move on that Wednesday, and its mover is named on no page of this stretch of days.**

A man of about thirty-one said in nine seconds that two chairs in one room that are both described by nine people as the ninth one is a thing about description and not about chairs.

He said that the chair with its back to the room has been there for as long as he can remember and that nobody has ever moved it and that nobody in this city has ever said who moved it there.

He said that the chair that stood empty on that Wednesday has been against that wall for nineteen years and that the other one has been against it for longer, and that this is the only difference and that nobody asked about it.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about half an hour and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that he counted the chairs twice on that Wednesday and told nobody and that the count is eight used and one empty.**

He said that a number one man keeps is a Tuesday and that he has had two counts this month and has not been asked for either.

He was told a thing and did not repeat it.

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about an hour on a stack of chairs in a hall in a second district, where one leg was loose and it was not the leg that was loose.**

A stack of chairs in a hall is a thing nobody looks at until somebody has to move the whole stack, and on that stack the loose leg was on the fourth chair from the bottom and the frame of that chair was sound.

He took the stack apart, put the frames out in a row, and found that about four chairs had been stacked one leg in and one leg out of line, so that every fourth chair carried the weight of the ones on it.

He re-stacked them with all the legs facing the same way, which is a thing about nine seconds of work, and then went round and re-glued and re-drew four loose legs, which is not.

Fifteen pounds. He told the man that a stack is a structure and not a heap, and that a stack put together with the legs crossed carries about four times the load on four chairs than it should, and that four stacks in that district are stacked like that.

---

**And the second fault on those chairs was not the legs, which is the ordinary shape of a morning's work, and it was the backs, which had been re-covered in a thicker material and had begun to tear where they went over the top rail.**

He took the two worst backs off, cut out the failed material with a knife rather than pulling it, and put a strip of the old material under the new so that the new had something to bear on.

Fifteen pounds was for the stack and this was eleven, and he told him the order, because a man who has paid for a stack will say the covers were sound.

He said that about four chairs in four halls in that district have been re-covered in something a quarter thicker than they were and that the tears will all be at the top rail inside a year.

---

**And a fire door in the same hall had been propped with a length of batten for four months and the closer on it had been removed entirely.**

That is not a repair anybody makes by accident. He put the closer back on the door from a bag of parts that had been on a shelf in that hall since the spring, and found that the spindle was bent and had been bent in the door, not in the bag.

Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a closer taken off a door is a closer somebody has decided not to pay for, and that the batten is what they put there instead, and that four fire doors in that district are propped and two of them have had their closers taken off.

---

**And a skylight over that hall had a rope and a weight on it that had been replaced with a length of electric cable, which works until the day it does not.**

A skylight that opens has a weight on a cord running over a pulley, and the weight is what closes it again, and somebody had taken the weight off and put a length of cable on in its place because the cord had frayed.

Cable does not sit still in a pulley and it does not balance, and a skylight on cable runs down of its own accord at about four inches a day, which is four feet a year.

He took the cable off, put a new cord on, rebalanced the weight on the pulley until the light opened and stayed open, and tied the cord off at about a foot. Nineteen pounds.

He told the man that a weight on a skylight is a mechanism and not a handle, and that nine skylights in that building have had a weight taken off them, and that four of them are on cable.

---

**And a bell wire in the same hall had been run round a corner without a pulley and was jammed, which is the reason that four bells in this city will not sound in the corridors.**

A bell wire going round a corner has to go over a pulley or it binds, and when it binds the wire stays where it was and the wire either side of it stretches instead, and the fault shows up in two rooms a long way from the corner.

He took the wire off the bracket at the corner, put a small pulley in, re-threaded it, and found that about nine feet of wire had stretched and had lost its shape, so he replaced that length too.

Eleven pounds. He told the man that a wire jammed round a corner does not break the wire and does not break the bell, and that it stretches a foot of wire a year, and that four corners in that building have a bell wire going round them without a pulley.

---

**And a cupboard door in that hall had been hung on two screws in the frame with the plates left off, which is why it has come off its hinges twice and been put back twice.**

A door is hung on three hinges and each hinge has a plate that goes into the door and a plate that goes into the frame, and a door hung on two screws and no plates is a door somebody did in about nine minutes.

He took the door off, fitted all six plates, put three new hinges in rather than reusing the two, and rehung it. Thirty-one pounds.

He told the man that a door on two screws is not a door anybody chose and is a door somebody could not find the plates for, and that nine doors in that building are on two screws.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six with her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and fifty-six days and has not been read.**

---
