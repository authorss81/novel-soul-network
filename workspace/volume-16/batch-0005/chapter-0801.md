# Chapter 801 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where A Room Opened At Six And A Question Was Put About What Four Words Do Not Say, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Said On The Friday Before That Nobody Had Written Down The Other Half Of It At All

**A Tuesday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished on a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where a room opened at six in the hour it belongs to the woman who holds it and about nine people were on the stair by seven. The bench and the counter were both open. The shutter came down at ten.**

---

The board for that Tuesday carried twelve names and twelve dates, and the twelfth name was entered at ten to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Tuesday, at the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days except one.

**That card in a rail that takes two is one thousand four hundred and sixty-eight days, two hundred and nine weeks and five days. The nineteen, last line in use, is one thousand one hundred and sixty days, a hundred and sixty-five weeks and five days.**

**The separation is eight hundred and forty-four days, one hundred and twenty weeks and four days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old. Those four rooms and one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and sixty-four days, two hundred and nine weeks and one day.**

---

**The thing nobody had said out loud, on that Tuesday, in that room or in any other room in this city, is what the four words are about.**

They are four words and they are on a sheet and on a wall in a corridor in a second district, and they say a day and they do not say a thing. It has been eleven days since they were written on the back of a docket sheet and they are in nine mouths in that district and nobody has counted the mouths and nobody has been asked to.

A man of about thirty-four said in nine seconds, standing at the window end of that room, that he had been waiting eleven days for somebody to put the question the way it actually goes, and that it does not go as what are they about, and that it goes as what is being taken off me.

He said the second of those was the question four hundred people would actually ask, and that the first one was the question the district was asking each other in shops, and that the two had been in the same corridor for eleven days with nothing on the wall about which of them was which.

Nobody in that room asked him which of the two he was for, because he had said both, and because a man who says both in nine seconds has already spent the whole of the nine.

---

**And then a woman of about twenty-nine, who had said the true thing about this on the Friday before, was in that room, and she had not been asked to be, and she said the rest of it in nine seconds.**

"**Nobody has written the second half,**" she said. "**That is the whole of what has happened in eleven days. Four words and a date and no second half, and four hundred people are going to read the four words and then ask the person they asked what it is that ends.**"

A man of about thirty-eight said that was the point of the four words, and that a date which carries a reason with it is a thing four hundred people can argue about on a Tuesday, and that this was argued about on the Tuesday it was written and had been argued about since, and that was the work.

She said that she had been in that room on the Tuesday it was written. She said she had not been in the room and had been told about it afterwards by a man of about thirty-four, and that a person who was not in a room does not get what the room decided.

Nobody answered her for about half a minute, and about two of the people in that room wrote something down during it, which is the first time anything has been written down in that room since the Wednesday before.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four said the thing that changed what the rest of the day was for, and he said it in nine seconds and it was about a name rather than about a sentence.**

He said that as of that Tuesday there was a second half, in the sense that anybody who asked about it could be told that it had been asked for and that nobody had written it. He said that was not the same as there being one and it was a great deal better than there not being one, and that four hundred people could be given the fact of the absence in nine words, and that the absence could be pointed at.

**The second half of the sentence now has a name on it in nine mouths and it is not a sentence and it is not four words and it is on no paper.**

Nobody in that room wrote it down. A man of about fifty-two said that was the correct outcome, and that the day the second half is written down without having been asked for is the day four hundred people are told a thing which is nearly true, and that he would know the day it happened because it would be a Tuesday.

A man of about thirty-one said that he had been in that room on the Tuesday it was written and that he did not remember any of this being discussed, and that a man who was in a room and does not remember it is the ordinary case and not the exception.

---

**And nine people went up that stair between six and a quarter past seven on that Tuesday and four of them stayed the hour, and two of the four said nothing at all.**

One of the other two asked whether a person could come on a Wednesday and find the room shut, which has been asked before, and one asked whether anybody had written down what the practice actually is, which has not been asked before.

The woman of about sixty answered the first in nine seconds, in the form she has used, and said nothing for nine seconds to the second and then said that nobody had, and that there was no form anywhere in this city for a person to put that on, and that she had looked for eleven years and had found two things written by accident.

**Two of the people who came up that stair have said since that the silence on the second question was longer than the silence on the first, and that two have said that the woman of about twenty-nine should have been asked it on the Friday and was not asked.**

A man of about fifty-two who was on the stair that evening said that the woman of about sixty was not being cold and that anybody in that room who said she was being cold had not been in eleven years of it, and that the woman of about sixty had answered the first question in the same words she has used for two months because the first question does not need a new answer.

---

**And the question of who was carrying the four words got answered on that Tuesday by nobody, and two people said afterwards that a room which has a question in it is different from a room which has an answer in it, and that neither of those is the same as a room with somebody's name on the thing.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that the four words belonged to nobody and that this had been established eleven days ago and that it was the only good thing anybody had done about them.

A man of about thirty-eight said that a sentence with nobody's name on it is a sentence four hundred people can be angry at, and that a named person's sentence is a sentence four hundred people have to be angry about somebody for, and that the second kind does not travel a street.

A woman of about forty-eight said that the drawing of nine streets on the back of a docket sheet had no names on it for the same reason and that she had been the one who said so, and that a person who puts a name on a line makes the line a claim and a claim can be argued with.

Nobody thanked anybody for any of those, and about two of the people who were in that room have said that four people arguing about who should carry a sentence is a better Tuesday than one person being handed it.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was on that floor for an hour and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that a question with nobody's name on it is a question four hundred people can all ask at once, and that he would not have wanted to be in the room when it was asked.**

He was told a thing and he did not repeat it. He has said that he wrote down nothing and that nobody has asked him to keep anything, and that the difference between the two is about nine weeks of his life and that he does not intend to collect on it.

He has also said that the four words in the corridor are the same four words he could have said in a gateway on a Monday, and that the reason he is glad the sheet went on a wall rather than into a mouth is that a wall cannot be argued with by the person who put it up.

---

**The rest of that Tuesday was a counter until about six and three quarters of an hour on a door closer in a first district, where a hanging weight had been shimmed with a length of card.**

A door closer is a box on the frame and an arm across the door and a weight hanging inside the box on a rod, and the speed it closes at is set by where that weight sits. Somebody had packed a folded length of card under the weight to slow it down, and the closer had been closing slower and slower for four months, and the fold had gone soft and the weight was sitting a sixteenth of an inch lower than it should have been.

He took the closer off the door, took the cover off, took the folded card out, put the weight back on its rod at the mark the maker had stamped into the side of the box, and refitted the cover. Nineteen pounds.

The man said the previous man had done it in four minutes with a screwdriver. He said a card under a weight is a repair that will be done again in about nine weeks because it is soft and it is there, and that four closers in that street had been done that way and all four of them were doing it again.

He said that the folded card had been put there by somebody who had a reason for it, and that the reason was usually a door frame that had dropped, and that the frame on that door had dropped as well and would still want a slower closer in about four months.

---

**And the second fault on that door was not the closer at all, which is the ordinary shape of a morning's work, and it was a screw in a plate on the frame that had been drawn into soft timber a quarter of an inch.**

He put a longer screw into the frame behind the plate rather than a longer plate over the frame, which is the repair anybody does first and which buys two years, and he told the man that the two years is how long a plate in soft timber takes to tell you.

Nineteen pounds was for the closer and this was eleven, and he told him the order, because a man who has paid for the first job will tell the next man the door was done.

He said that four plates in that street are on soft frames and that one of them has a longer plate over the frame and will be a longer plate over a rotten timber in two years, and that nobody has measured any of them.

---

**And the third fault on that Tuesday was in the same street and it was a floor spring in a shop door that had been oiled rather than adjusted, and the oil had got into the hinge.**

A floor spring is a tube set in the floor with a spindle coming up through the floor plate and an arm bolted to the door. Oiling one is the repair everybody makes when one begins to squeak, and oil goes down the spindle and collects in the hinge, and the hinge gets worse and the spring gets slower, and about four months later the spring is loose in its tube.

He took the plate up, took the arm off, cleaned out the hinge and the top of the tube, and adjusted the spring by turning the top of the spindle a quarter turn, and put the plate back down and set it to clear the frame by a sixteenth of an inch.

Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a floor spring wants adjusting and never oiling, and that the sound of a spring getting slower is a hinge and not a spindle, and that the second one is the first one nine months later.

---

And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six with her door shut. The eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and forty-eight days and has not been read.
