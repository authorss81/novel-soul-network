# Chapter 761 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Half An Hour Of A Stair In A Second District With A Door Closer Screwed To The Top Of A Step, A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Who Pinned A Sheet Of Card To A Wall With Two Drawing Pins And Said Nothing To The Man On The Floor, And Nine Pounds For A Part

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and about half an hour of it in a stair in a second district with a door closer in his hand. Nine jobs were dated for the day on a board by the till. A sheet went on a wall at about eleven while he was on the step.**

---

Nine names went on that Monday's sheet and nine dates went with them, the last taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten. The cage in the round-the-side had been going about nine times in the fortnight and the van was not needed.

**Those four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand four hundred and seven days, two hundred and one weeks to the day, and the card on end in the rail by that door is four days further on at one thousand four hundred and eleven, two hundred and one weeks and four days, and the rail takes two.**

**The nineteenth line on the board of two nails is one thousand one hundred and three days, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and four days. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and thirteen, a hundred and forty-four weeks and five days. The ask is one thousand and ninety-seven, a hundred and fifty-six weeks and five days.**

The separation is at seven hundred and eighty-seven days, a hundred and twelve weeks and three days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old.

---

**The stair is the top of a stair and it belongs to a building that lets out four rooms on a first floor, and the door that slams is the one at the bottom of it, and it has been slamming since before the spring because the closer on top of it is set to shut it as fast as the door will bear.**

He had it off in his bag before he went and he had the new one in a bag as well, and the adjustment screw on the old one was down as far as it will go and somebody had put a piece of tape round it to keep it there.

**About nine people came up that stair between ten and half past eleven and about four of them were going to the rooms and about four of the other five were not going anywhere in particular, which is what a stair in that building is at that hour.**

He had the old unit off and the back plate out and the dust in his hands and his knees on the step when she came up.

She was about thirty-four. She had a plastic box of drawing pins in one hand and a sheet of card in the other and the card was already creased down the middle from being in a bag, and she did not look at him and she did not step around him and she went to the wall at the turn of the stair as though the stair had always belonged to her.

---

**She pinned it up at about eleven with two drawing pins and about four seconds of adjustment between them, and neither of them said one word to the other.**

He read it upside down from the floor.

There were about nine words on it and a date at the bottom and under the words the name of a district written out in full because anybody could tell which one it was.

He read it three times.

He wanted to ask her what it was. **He had a sentence ready in about two seconds and he did not say it, and the reason he did not say it, which he worked out later and not at the time, was that she had not asked him what he was doing, and a person who is not asked does not ask.**

She came down past him going out and the plastic box went past at his knee and the door at the bottom went once and did not slam, because the closer was on the bench beside him in pieces.

---

**The building had paid him before she came up, in an envelope, at the door, by a man of about fifty-eight who said nothing beyond the amount and did not ask him to sign anything.**

He was on the step again by about half past eleven with the new unit bolted to the top of the door and the tape off the old screw and the old unit in the bag with the tape still round it, because he does not throw away a piece of tape that somebody had put there on purpose.

**Nobody in that building said one word to him about the sheet, and nobody in that building read it out loud to him, and about four people saw it and about five did not.**

He was back at the counter at about half past one and the shop had gone on without him and had not stopped.

---

**About nine jobs went out on that Monday and about nine came in and nothing escalated and nothing was handed back, and the till came up about four pounds out and he counted the tray twice.**

A woman of about thirty-eight brought in a bracket for a gate and wanted it the same afternoon because the gate was open on a lane and the lane was a bus route.

"**That's a bracket,**" he said. "**It'll be Thursday.**"

She said Thursday and it was Thursday and she came back on Thursday and it went on.

**He charged about nine pounds for it and the bracket cost him about two and he did not tell her that and did not charge her for the ten minutes it took him to explain that a gate bracket that has been open on a bus route for a fortnight is a bracket somebody has already been told to replace and has not.**

---

---

**About four of the people in that building read it. One of them is a man who works on the second floor and has been in that stair every weekday since before the spring, and he read it standing up, and he did not say one word about it to anybody, and he was in the shop on the Thursday and bought a washer and said nothing then either.**

That is the first thing about that sheet that is worth knowing and nobody has printed it anywhere: the people who read it are not the people it is about.

---

**The rest of that Monday was nine jobs and a counter and a till that does not add up, and he did all nine and charged for all nine.**

A man of about sixty came in with a lamp he had brought back because he had brought it back before.

"**That is a lamp,**" Marek said. "**You have had that lamp before.**"

"**Twice,**" the man said. "**It is not the lamp, it is the flex.**"

"**Then it is the flex,**" Marek said, and it was the flex, and it cost about four pounds, and the man said it had cost him eleven pounds the first time at a shop on a wide road, and Marek did not ask which shop and did not say anything about it and has thought about the shop on a wide road perhaps four times since.

A woman of about forty-one brought a bag of door handles she had taken off a door in one piece instead of eleven.

A boy of about fifteen brought a bicycle chain that fitted the bicycle and had been fitted by somebody who did not know what it was for.

**About four of the nine jobs on that Monday were jobs somebody had brought back, and that is not a bad day and it is not a good day and it is about what a counter is.**

---

**At about four o'clock a woman of about twenty-six came in and asked for a man and he said she had him and she said no, she had wanted to ask him something, and he said then ask him, and she stood at the counter for about nine seconds and then said it was a stupid question and went out.**

He thought about her for the rest of that week. She had come about the sheet on a wall in a second district because somebody who works with her works in that building and had said something at a shift, and she had wanted to ask a man who fixes things whether that thing was true, and she had decided in nine seconds that it was a stupid question.

**He did not know what the question was. He has not found out and he has not looked, and about nine people in this city have seen her come out of that shop and about four of them thought nothing of it at all.**

---

---

**The last job of that Monday was a gate at about a quarter to five and it is the one he thinks about.**

A man of about forty-three wanted it shut by dark and it cannot be shut by dark because the post is in and the hole has to be filled, and Marek said so in about nine seconds and then stayed for about forty minutes and did it badly and quickly.

They filled the bottom eleven inches with something that came out of a bucket and they put the gate on and it shut and it is shut now and about two of the four hundred yards in that district are shut on a Friday.

**He charged about thirty-one pounds for the Monday in total, and the gate was most of it, and the gate is the only thing on that day anybody will still be able to see in about four years.**

---

**At about ten he went up a stair near Civic Spine, about nine doors along from the tram stop, and about four minutes after him a woman of about twenty-four came up the same stair and went to a door about nine metres from his and shut it.**

They had rice and a stew that was three days old because that is what was in the pot, and bread, and there was no second thing.

She asked him what he had done on the day and he told her about the stair and the closer and the tape and the man of about fifty-eight with the envelope, and he did not tell her about the sheet, because he did not want to say the word *district* at a table on a Monday night and have it be the thing they talked about.

She said the gate was a stupid gate to have shut and he said it was, and she said the post was in and he said it was.

**Nobody said anything else about the sheet that night and neither of them has mentioned it since and it has been on that wall for six days.**

---

---

**There is a second sheet in that building and it went up in the autumn before the spring and nobody has taken it down.**

It is about a room that is to be shut for one day a week and it is the one that is still on the board at the building that receives the returns with a heading and no body.

The woman of about thirty-four pinned hers up about nine feet from a man on the floor and nobody in this city has ever asked her why she chose that wall and not another one.

---

**He wanted, all of that afternoon, for one person to come in and ask him what was on the sheet.**

Nobody came in and asked him what was on the sheet. Nobody came in and asked him about anything that was not on the ticket.

---

**What it cost was about two pounds and about four minutes of arguing with himself at about half past two, and the two pounds are the whole of it and he did not tell her that and told nobody, and the four minutes are not a figure anybody could use.**

**There are four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six on that Monday a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, in her chair, with the shut door of that room behind her.**

The eighth of eight is her page and it has been in a ring binder on a back shelf for a hundred and ninety-six. It did not come out on that Monday and nobody asked her one question about it and nobody is going to.

---

**He wanted the woman with the drawing pins to have asked him for something, and she did not, and the wanting lasted about two days and then stopped being a wanting and became a thing he kept looking for.**

He looked for it on the Tuesday and on the Wednesday and on the Friday and it did not come.
