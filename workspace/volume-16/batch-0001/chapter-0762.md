# Chapter 762 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Bottom Landing At About Half Past Six With About Nine People On It And A Question Asked Out Loud By Somebody Who Was Not A Member Of Anything, And Nobody In That Room Able To Answer It

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then a bottom landing in a second district from about half past six. Eleven jobs were dated. About nine people stood on a landing and one question was asked and no person on that landing had the answer to it.**

---

Ten names went on that Tuesday's sheet and ten dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**The rooms off that service road are at one thousand four hundred and eight days, two hundred and one weeks and one day, and the card on end in the rail by the door is one thousand four hundred and twelve, two hundred and one weeks and five days. The rail takes two.**

**The nineteenth line reads one thousand one hundred and four, a hundred and fifty-seven weeks and five days. The ninth hand copies of the front of one page read nine hundred and seventy-four, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks and one day.**

Seven hundred and eighty-eight days is the separation, a hundred and twelve weeks and four days.

---

**He did not go to that landing because anybody asked him to. He went because he had been in that building on the Monday and because it is four minutes' walk from the shop and because he has never in his life not gone.**

There were about nine people on it at about half past six, standing up, because there are about nine chairs on that landing and about four of the chairs work and nobody sits down in a room where a person is reading something out.

**A man of about forty-four was reading it out with a piece of card in his hand, and he read it as though he had been asked to and had not been asked to, which is a different thing and is not visible to the people standing nearest to him.**

She stood at the wall end. She had come straight from a sorting office on that road and she had her coat open and her hands in the pockets of the front of it and she did not look at the sheet while it was being read and she had clearly read it before.

---

**"Does a district have to keep a route open," said a woman of about twenty-six, "for the people inside it who have not given notice."**

Nobody answered her for about nine seconds.

Then the woman at the wall end said, in about nine seconds and without raising her voice, that she had looked in four places and it was not written down in any of them, and that she was not going to guess in front of nine people.

---

**A man of about fifty-two said that the sensible thing was that the district would keep it, and she refused him in about nine seconds with a reason that was about the work and not about anything else.**

"**A thing kept because somebody said it in a room is not a thing anybody can plan around,**" she said. "**You cannot put a shift on it. You cannot put a shift on something that lasts as long as somebody is feeling willing.**"

He said that was a lawyer's answer. She said it was a shift answer and that lawyers were not in the room.

---

**And then he did a thing he has done four times in eleven years and did not enjoy, which was to offer himself before he had been asked and to be told, in about four seconds, that he was not the person to do it.**

He said he could go and look at the route himself, in about four seconds, and she said no in about four seconds.

"**You do not hold it,**" she said. "**I am not putting it on you because you would hold it well. I am not putting it on you because it is not yours.**"

Somebody at the back laughed at nothing in particular and stopped.

---

**He wanted to be asked for the rest of that evening and he was not asked, and the wanting was worse on that Tuesday than it had been on the Monday, because on the Monday there had been about nine people in a stair and none of them had known who he was.**

The question got written on the back of a docket by a hand that was not his and not hers, and the docket went into a coat pocket, and nobody in that landing was told whose pocket.

---

---

**About four of the nine people on that landing work on that road and about two of them work in the building the sheet is on the wall of, and about nine of them had come because a woman of about thirty-four had put a sheet of card on a board and had not told any of them she was going to.**

She had told about four of them. The other five found out from the sheet.

**A woman of about twenty-six who had asked the question works on a gate at about five and has a bus to catch at about seven, and she had come on her break, and she went back for the shift.**

---

**The man of about fifty-two tried twice.**

The first time he said that a district would keep a route because keeping it was the ordinary thing and the ordinary thing did not need writing down, and she said the ordinary thing was what you had until somebody tested it.

The second time he said that the institution would respond inside a period it considered appropriate, and got as far as *considered* and then stopped, and looked at the floor, and she let him stop.

**Nobody helped him and nobody contradicted him and about nine people on that landing let the second attempt die where it died, and that is a thing about a landing and not about anybody.**

---

**At about seven the man of about forty-four who had read the sheet out said that he had read it because he was the sort of person who read things out, and that nobody had asked him to and that if nobody wanted him to he would stop, and about four people said no in four different ways in about nine seconds and he read the next one as well.**

He read three sheets that evening on the same landing, one of which was about a gate and one of which was about a bin, and nobody has ever worked out why those two were on the same board as the one about the district.

---

**He walked back. It is about four minutes' walk from that landing to the shop and it took him about eleven, and he did not go in and did not go home and stood for about nine minutes at the end of a street with a bag of door closers in it.**

He worked out during those nine minutes that he had offered to do a job he did not hold, to a person he had met once, about nine hours after a sheet went up on a wall he had been lying under. He worked out that the reason he had offered was not that he was useful and that he had known that at the time and had done it anyway.

**He has offered before. It has happened four times in eleven years and the other three were about a person who was ill and about two of those were about people he had met once.**

---

---

**The shop is about nine feet from that landing's building and he has walked between the two about four times in nine years and it never once occurred to him that the sheet was on a wall in between them.**

The sheet went up on the Monday. He read it on the Monday. He read it upside down from a floor.

**On the Tuesday about nine people asked each other a question on a landing about four hundred yards from that wall, a question that is written on no sheet at all, and he was on that landing and not one of the nine that had asked it, and about four of those nine have said since that they did not know there was a wall.**

---

**There is one more thing about that landing and it is the thing that has not been repeated.**

The building it belongs to lets out four rooms and about nine people use the landing because it has nine chairs and a light. It has been used as a landing for about eleven years and about four people have asked for it to be something else and been refused by a landlord in about four seconds each time.

**On that Tuesday, for about an hour and a half, about nine people used it for a different thing and the landlord was not there and did not know and has not been told and nobody in that landing is going to tell him.**

That is the only thing that happened on that Tuesday that anybody in the room would describe as new.

---

---

**A man of about thirty-one who was on that landing has said since that the question asked on that Tuesday was the first useful question anybody had asked in that building in about two years.**

He works on a gate and has been in that building since before the spring and about nine people there know him.

He has also said, in about four sentences in a corridor, that it was useful because it had been asked out loud and not because it had been answered, and that nobody has ever asked him a question in that building that he could not answer, and that on that Tuesday about nine people found out they all had the same one.

**Nobody wrote any of that down and he has told it twice and both times to about four people.**

---

**He went back to the shop at about nine and the shutter was down and the cage had been going about nine times in the fortnight and there was a woman of about twenty-nine waiting outside it with a bag of parts she had brought at about half past eight because she had been told to come in the morning.**

He let her in and did the work and charged her and she did not say anything about the landing because she had not been on it.

---

**What it cost him was about four seconds on a landing and about eleven minutes of walking afterwards that he has not charged anybody for, and the eleven minutes are the only minutes anybody has asked him for in this whole of it, and nobody did.**

**There are four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, at her chair, with her door shut.**

The eighth of eight is her page, and it has been on that back shelf for a hundred and ninety-two days, and nobody asked her anything about it, and nobody is going to.
