# Chapter 769 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Counter With A Docket Book On It And A Tin Beside The Book, And The Correct-Things-That-Change-Nothing Standing At Three At Ten In The Morning And At Four At Night, Which Is The Fourth Time In Four Movements

**A Friday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a counter with a docket book on it. About nine jobs were dated. A notice was correct in every particular and changed nothing at all, and that is the fourth instance on a board nobody in this city has counted.**

---

Nine names went on that Friday's sheet and nine dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**The rooms off that service road stand at one thousand four hundred and eighteen days, two hundred and two weeks and four days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand four hundred and twenty-two, two hundred and three weeks and one day. The rail takes two.**

**Line twelve is one thousand three hundred and thirty-eight, a hundred and ninety-one weeks and one day. Line seventeen is one thousand one hundred and ninety, a hundred and seventy weeks to the day. The post at that corridor end is nine hundred and sixty-six, a hundred and thirty-eight weeks to the day.**

Seven hundred and ninety-eight days is the separation, a hundred and fourteen weeks to the day.

---

**The register of correct acts with no consequence stood at three at ten in the morning on that Friday and stood at four at four in the afternoon and stands at four now.**

It is a board on a wall and nobody in this city has counted it in about nine years and nobody is going to.

What the third instance cost was a man of about fifty-two who stood at a junction for about nine minutes on a Friday, in a queue, on a Monday he cannot get, and whose wife's name is in a book at a clinic he has not been in the room of. It is on that board because nothing came of it.

---

**The fourth instance is a notice. It was correct in every particular and on that Friday it changed nothing.**

The form was the proper form. The date was a date four weeks out and it was in the form. The service was given and a copy went to the building that receives the returns and somebody wrote it in a book with a time against it. The woman of about thirty-four had not forgotten one step and had not added one.

**And the district was not leaving for four weeks, and nobody had answered, and a correct thing was done correctly and there was nothing to show for it by four in the afternoon.**

---

**A man of about fifty-two said the thing that a person says when a correct thing has not worked, and he said it in about nine seconds and he was not wrong.**

"**Then it is somebody's fault who could have done something,**" he said.

**She said no in about four seconds and what she said was about the work and not about him and not about anybody in the room.**

"**It is the fault of a rule with no number in it,**" she said. "**Nobody could have done anything. There was no day anybody was going to be able to do anything, because nobody knows when the day is.**"

---

**What it cost her was the first thing she has signed in eleven years that she chose.**

She has signed other people's names since before the spring — invoices, dockets, a gate register, a rota — and she has done it about four times a week for eleven years and she has never once been asked whether she agreed with any of it.

**On that Friday she signed one thing and she read it before she signed it, and she has said since that reading it took about eleven seconds and that the eleven seconds were the whole of the difference.**

---

**About nine people in that district have not been asked whether they agree with it. About four of them have not agreed with it and one of those four has said so out loud, in a queue, at about half past six, to a person who was not in the room.**

Nobody has told the institution which one.

**About two people in that district are named on the notice and neither of them has read it, and both of them signed in the same week as she did, and neither of them has asked what they signed.**

---

---

**The register is a board on a wall and the board is behind the counter and nobody comes behind the counter.**

It is a piece of hardboard with a list on it in a hand that is not his and that has not changed since about nine years ago, and every line on it is a thing somebody did properly and that made no difference, and there are four lines on it.

**The first is a gate opened early. The second is a rota worked correctly on a Monday that nobody could get. The third is a woman's name written in the column meant for it at a clinic, in a form that a man filled in for four years.**

The fourth is a notice.

---

**About four people in this city could name the four lines and not one of them has counted them, and two of the four have been asked about theirs and neither of them knew it was on a board.**

The man of about fifty-two whose gate was opened early does not know it is on a board. He has told the story about nine times and it has never once been in a room with a wall in it.

**That is the whole of what a register like that is: it is the only place in this city where doing a thing correctly gets written down, and it is behind a counter, and the people who did it correctly are not told.**

---

**He has read that board about nine hundred times in eleven years and he has never once written on it, and there is no pencil on the counter, and he has asked for a pencil twice.**

The second time he asked, a woman of about thirty-eight said that writing on it was for the woman of about sixty and not for him, and he said he understood, and did not ask again, and has not asked a third time.

---

---

**The docket book is the oldest object in that shop and it is not a shop book, it is the book a repairer writes in when a thing comes in and the person cannot wait.**

He started it about eleven years ago on the day he took the room and it is on a shelf behind the counter and it has about nine hundred lines in it and about four of the lines are from this week.

**Every line is a name, a thing, a date and a price. There is no column for a thing a customer said while the job was being done, and about four hundred lines in that book have a sentence written across them in his own hand that is not a name and not a price.**

He has never decided what to do about that. He keeps writing them.

---

**On that Friday a woman of about forty-four came in with a lamp and he fixed it in about nine minutes and charged about nine pounds and she said, on her way out, that she had heard about the notice.**

He said he understood. She said she did not agree with it. He said that was the arrangement.

**He did not ask her whether she lived in the district and about four people in this city have since told him that he should have and he has thought about it about nine times and has not worked out what he would do with the answer.**

---

---

**Nobody has counted that board in about nine years and about four people have stood in front of it and about two of them have read all four lines.**

One of the two is a woman of about thirty-eight who has been coming to that shop since before the spring and who buys a coil of cable about twice a month.

She read the four lines in about eleven seconds on that Friday, in about nine seconds of which she was looking at the third one, and she did not say anything about them and she has been back four times since.

---

**He wrote it down. That is the only thing he did on that Friday that was not work.**

He wrote the figure on the docket book at about half past three — about four hundred people, and one man, and a Tuesday about four seconds longer than he used to take — and he did not send it to anybody and it is in the docket book in his own hand and the docket book is on the shelf behind the counter.

**He wanted somebody to need it and nobody needed it on that Friday and it has not been needed since and he has not taken it out of the book.**

---

**There are four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, at her chair, with her door shut, and her page is the eighth of eight and it has been on that back shelf for two hundred and two days and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**
