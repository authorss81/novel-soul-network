# Chapter 768 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Caller Of About Twenty-Nine Who Does Not Give Her Job Who Asks One Straight Question At A Counter And Is Given The First Answer In About Four Seconds And Then The Harder Half

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until about six. Eleven jobs were dated. A caller of about twenty-nine who did not give her job asked one straight question at the counter and got an answer in about four seconds and then got the harder half of it, and neither of those was about a part.**

---

The board by the till carried eleven callers on that Thursday and a date against every one of them, and the eleventh was written at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**The rooms off that service road are at one thousand four hundred and seventeen days, two hundred and two weeks and three days, and that card in the rail by the door is one thousand four hundred and twenty-one, two hundred and three weeks to the day. The rail takes two.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand one hundred and eighty-nine, a hundred and sixty-nine weeks and six days. The nineteenth line is one thousand one hundred and thirteen, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and twenty-three, a hundred and forty-six weeks and one day.**

Seven hundred and ninety-seven days is the separation, a hundred and thirteen weeks and six days.

---

**She did not give her job and he did not ask, which is the arrangement they arrived at in about four seconds without either of them having decided it.**

She had a small thing in her hand — a bracket about the size of a fist with a plate on the back of it — and she put it on the counter and did not push it towards him and she said:

"**Is it true you can stop a district leaving.**"

---

**He said no in about four seconds. He did not put the bracket down, and he did not pick it up, and he said it to the bracket.**

"**No.**"

She waited. She had the look of a person who has asked something in about nine rooms and has been told no in about nine different ways and is waiting to find out which one this is.

---

**And then he said the harder half, which he had not planned and which took him about four seconds to get out.**

"**I could have. Once. About a year ago, in a different way, in about nine minutes, with about nine people in a room, and it would have worked. I did not do it and I would do it again and that is not the same as there never having been a version of it where I was the one who decided.**"

The shop had a bell going somewhere behind him and neither of them looked at it.

---

**She asked him whether that was going to happen again. He said he did not know.**

He did not say it would not. About four people who were in that shop in that week have each said that he could have said it would not and did not, and none of them has asked him why and one of them has, and he gave the same answer both times, which was that he does not know and that saying otherwise would be a thing he said to make himself easier and not a thing he could do in a face.

She wrote nothing down and she did not ask him to.

---

---

**The shop on that Thursday had nine jobs on the board and the cage in the round-the-side had gone about nine times in the fortnight, and about four people came in on that day for something that was not the thing they came for.**

That is a Tuesday and a Thursday and it is not a fault and it is the only thing about a counter that cannot be put in a form.

A woman of about fifty came in to ask whether the heat was on in a back room, which it is not, and which has never been on, and which is not hers.

A man of about twenty-eight came in with a meter and wanted to know whether a thing on a street of about eleven houses was reading high, and would not say which street, and Marek said he would not know either way and the man said that was not the answer he wanted.

**About two people on that Thursday were told something true about their own business by a man who fixes other people's things and neither of them has been back.**

---

**He worked out after about half past four that he had said the harder half of that exchange to a stranger because about nine of the people who might have heard it were not in the shop.**

He has thought about that for about eleven minutes and he has not worked out whether it was better in a face to one person than in a room to nine, and he is not going to be able to work it out, and about four people in this city would tell him it was better in the room.

**He has told it to a counter. There is no rule about which is better and there has never been a rule about which is better and he has been looking for one for about eleven years.**

---

---

**The till came up about four pounds out on that Thursday as it has on about nine of the last eleven days, and he counted the tray three times and the third count agreed with the first.**

He has a theory about the till and the theory is that the machine is correct and he is wrong, because the tray agrees with itself. He has had that theory since before the spring.

**About nine people have told him it is a known fault and about four of them have named a firm and about one of the four came out and looked at it and said nothing and went away.**

---

**At about half past five a man of about thirty-three came in with a drawer that does not run and did not want it to run, and wanted to know what was wrong with it, and Marek told him what was wrong with it in about nine seconds, and the man said he did not want it fixed and he paid about four pounds for the answer.**

That is a real thing that happens about twice a week and there is no line for it and it is not a favour and he has never described it as one.

---

**He has been the person somebody says a true thing to in a room about nine times in eleven years and every one of the nine was a person with a problem he could not fix.**

He has never worked out whether that is a coincidence or whether people can tell. He asked a woman of about twenty-four about it once, over about nine seconds, in a kitchen, at about eleven at night, and she said that anybody can tell, and then said that she could not always tell and did not know how she knew, and neither of them has returned to it.

---

---

**She was about twenty-nine and she came in at about half past three on a Thursday and she had a thing in her hand that she did not need, and about nine people have since worked out that she came in for the other thing.**

There is a thing people do at that counter and it has happened about nine times in eleven years and nobody has ever put a name to it and nobody has ever written it down, and it is that a person with a problem that cannot be fixed will find a reason to be in a room with somebody who listens to problems.

**He has never once said so to anybody and about four people in this city have said it to each other and about two of them have said it to him and he has not denied it.**

---

**The bracket was a gate bracket about the size of a fist and it was sound and she did not need it and she brought it in to have a thing to put on the counter.**

He looked at it for about nine seconds and told her it was fine and that she did not need a new one, and she said she knew, and she paid about nine pounds for four minutes of a man's attention that she had not come for and neither of them said one word about what those four minutes were actually for.

**He charged her. That is the whole of the end of it and it is on the docket and the docket says a bracket.**

---

**He wanted to be able to say yes. He has wanted to be able to say yes to one person in this city for about eleven years and about four people know that he wanted to on this day and one of them has said so to somebody else.**

He could not. He said no and then he said the other thing, and the other thing was true, and it was the first time he had said it in a face to a person who had not asked to be a witness to it.

---

**There are four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, at her chair, with her door shut, and her page is the eighth of eight and it has been on that back shelf for two hundred and six days and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**

---
