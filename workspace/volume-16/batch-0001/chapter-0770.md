# Chapter 770 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Room Above A Shop In A Second District From About Half Past Five With About Nine People At A Table, And The Second Half Of A Notice Which Names What A District Will Keep Doing And Then Names The One Thing It Will Not

**A Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a room above a shop in a second district from about half past five. About nine people were at a table. The second half of a notice was read out and it named what a district will keep doing and then it named one thing it will not.**

---

Ten names went on that Monday's sheet and ten dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**The rooms off that service road are at one thousand four hundred and twenty-one days, two hundred and three weeks to the day, and the card on end in the rail by the door is one thousand four hundred and twenty-five, two hundred and three weeks and four days. The rail takes two.**

**Line twelve is one thousand three hundred and forty-one, a hundred and ninety-one weeks and four days. Line thirteen is one thousand two hundred and ninety-two, a hundred and eighty-four weeks and four days. Line sixteen is one thousand two hundred and eleven, a hundred and seventy-three weeks to the day. The ninth hand copies of the front of one page are nine hundred and eighty-seven, a hundred and forty-one weeks to the day.**

Eight hundred and one days is the separation, a hundred and fourteen weeks and three days.

---

**She read it herself. Nobody read it for her, which was not the arrangement on the Tuesday on the landing and she had said so at the time.**

The first half was what the district would keep doing until it went: the returns would go up on the same days, the gate would open at five, the yard would be swept at six and not at seven, the bus would be let out of the bay at the same time it always is.

**About nine people at that table did not say anything about any of that, because all of it is ordinary and all of it is the reason a withdrawal is possible at all.**

---

**And then there was a second list and it had one thing on it and it was about four sentences long.**

*The district will not continue the holding of a release line at the yard.*

He read that twice and then a third time and then he put the sheet down on the table in front of him, which he had not done since he came in.

---

**A woman of about thirty-eight said, in about nine seconds and to nobody in particular, that it is not on a list.**

"**It is not on a list because nobody has ever had to write it down,**" she said. "**Nobody in this city has ever had to sit down and say who holds what. It has just been the man, and everybody has known it was the man, and everybody has known it because everybody has watched him do it.**"

---

**That is the first time anybody has needed to know, and the reason it is needed now is that if the district leaves, the practice leaves with it, because the practice is a man and the man is in the district.**

About four hundred people use it. Not four hundred in the district — about four hundred across three districts and a half of a bus route, and about nine of them are children and about four of them are on the register in a book that a woman of about forty-eight keeps and has kept for about nine years.

**It takes about eleven seconds to do. It is done about four times a day by about four hundred people and the man of about forty-seven does it about nine times a day and gets paid for about two of them.**

---

**Nobody in that room could say where it was written down and about four people looked for it on a phone and one of them found a photograph of a wedding and one of them found nothing.**

A woman of about thirty-four said the man had it in his head and that he would probably still have it in his head in four weeks when they had all gone.

"**He will,**" she said. "**He will hold the line for about nine weeks after we go because there is nobody to tell him not to. And then about four hundred people will find out on a Tuesday, all at once, and none of them will know why.**"

---

---

**About four hundred people is not a figure anybody can hold in the head in a room, and about four people in that room tried.**

A man of about thirty-eight said four hundred and then stopped, because four hundred people is not four hundred of anything anybody in that room has seen.

A woman of about thirty-four said it the other way round. She said that about four hundred people is four hundred people who have all, at some point, put a hand on a line for about nine seconds and not known whose hand it was, and that is four hundred people who have a habit and not an opinion.

**About four people in that room looked at her when she said that and about two of them wrote it down and neither of them wrote her name against it, which is what they did with the man of about sixty-one's sentence on the Monday and it is what they do with everything.**

---

**Nobody could find it. That is worth saying properly, because it is the finding and not the absence of a finding.**

Four people looked on about four things in about nine minutes. One found a photograph of a wedding with about nine people in it and one of the nine was holding a line. One found nothing. One asked a woman of about thirty-eight whether there was a book, and she said there was a register and not a book, and that the register had about nine names in it and about four of the nine had not used it since the spring.

The fourth asked the man of about forty-seven, who works on that line and who was in that room for about eleven minutes and said about nine words in them.

---

---

**The woman of about thirty-four read that second list twice and then put it face down on the table, which she has never done with anything in about nine years of signing other people's names.**

She said, in about nine seconds, that a district does not get to decide what about four hundred people outside it are going to do with their hands, and that whoever had written the second half of that notice had not thought about it, and that she had written it and she was not going to pretend she had not.

**Nobody in that room said she was wrong. About four people said nothing and about two of them wrote something down and about nine people have since described the second half of that notice in about nine different ways.**

---

**There is a version of this scene where he says he can hold the line and everybody looks at him.**

It is the version he runs about four times a week and it does not have a name and he has not told anybody about it and about nine people in this city would say he has told somebody.

**In the scene that happened, a woman of about thirty-four said that if he held it then the district would not have left, and he said no, and she said he had not heard her, and he said he had heard her, and she said that was not the same thing as hearing somebody.**

Nobody said anything after that and about nine people were in that room.

---

---

**He wrote it in the docket book at about half past six and then he put the book back on the shelf with the other nine and the other eight and the other seven.**

It is between two books about hinges. Nobody opens it for hinges.

**He has decided in advance that he will not take it out. That is a decision and it is about him and not about anybody in a second district, and about four people in this city would say that is the correct order and about two of them would say it is a way of not being in the room.**

---

**He wanted to say that he could take it. He has a scar and eleven years and two people in this city who would let him hold a line, and he has held a line in about nine rooms in about nine minutes in about a year, and he could do it again.**

He did not say it, because the last time he said it in a room about nine people heard him and about four of them were the ones he was speaking about.

---

**And what he did instead was write it in the docket book at the counter at about half past six, in his own hand, and give it to nobody.**

About four hundred people. One man. Nine seconds of his time on a good day and about four on a Tuesday. Three districts and a half of a bus route.

**It is the first thing he has done in this stretch of days that nobody asked him to do, and about nine people in this city do not know it exists, and he has not told them, and the docket book is on a shelf behind a counter.**

---

**There are four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, at her chair, with her door shut, and her page is the eighth of eight and it has been on that back shelf for two hundred and ten days and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**
