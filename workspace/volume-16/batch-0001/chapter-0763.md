# Chapter 763 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket From About Half Past Six Where The Book Does Not Open And No Number Is Said And Nobody Explains Why, And A Woman Of About Sixty Who Has Held That Room For About Nineteen Years

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and a first floor above a line in Saltmarket from about half past six. About nine people were in that room over about an hour. The book with a green cover did not open and nobody said a number, and nobody in that room asked anybody why.**

---

Twelve names went on that Wednesday's sheet and twelve dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and nine days, two hundred and one weeks and two days, and the nine hand copies of the front of one page are at nine hundred and seventy-five, a hundred and thirty-nine weeks and two days. Neither of those two comes out on a whole number of weeks and no figure on this page does.**

**Behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place, and it had been empty two hundred and eighty-seven days, which is forty-one weeks to the day.**

Seven hundred and eighty-nine days is the separation, a hundred and twelve weeks and five days.

---

**He has been going up that stair on Wednesdays for eleven years and he has never once had a reason written down for why.**

He went up at about half past six and there were about nine people in the room and about four of them came all the way to the table.

The woman of about sixty has held that room for about nineteen years. The table is the table. The ninth chair is hard against the wall with its back to the whole of it and it did not move.

**A man of about thirty-four sits in the eighth chair because from the eighth chair you can see the back of the ninth chair, and he was in it, and nobody asked him anything, and he has been in it four weeks running.**

---

**The book was on sixty-six lines when she came up and it was on sixty-six lines when he left, about an hour later, and nobody in that room asked her to open it and nobody in that room suggested it.**

The tin on the same table is on seventy-three with the lid down and there is a receipt from a shop in it as a page marker and the receipt is not hers.

**Nobody said a number on that Wednesday. That is the whole of that and it is not a thing anybody in that room finds strange, because a number is said at a sitting, and a sitting is a Wednesday of every fourth week, and this was not one of those.**

A man of about forty-four, who asks her one question a week and has for about four years, asked his.

She said it in about nine seconds without looking up from the table.

"**Nineteen years,**" she said. "**I have sat in this room nineteen years and I have never once watched anybody walk out of it. The only thing that has ever come through that door about leaving is a bill.**"

---

**About four people have stopped coming to that room and she has about nine other Wednesdays, and she has said that once, to him, and to nobody else in it.**

He wanted to say the number about the space behind her chair. It was two hundred and eighty-seven days and forty-one weeks to the day and he had it in his head and he did not say it, and he did not say it because she had not asked and because it was not his figure and because about four people in that room had known it before they came up the stair and about two of them had known it exactly and none of them had said anything either.

---

---

**About four of the nine people who came up that stair came to the table and about five did not, and the five who did not came because they come on Wednesdays and have been coming for about four years.**

Nobody arranges it. There is a list of nine names somewhere and it is not a list, and about four of the nine have not been for a month and about two of those four have been for six weeks and one of those two has not been for nine weeks.

**The woman of about sixty has not asked any of them and has said once, in about nine seconds, to a man of about forty-four who asked her directly, that about nine people have stopped coming to that room and that they were not coming because of what she said.**

---

**A man of about forty-four brought a list of about nine things and put it on the table and said they were the things he could not fix and that he wanted somebody to tell him which one of them was not a thing he could fix.**

She read it. It took her about eleven seconds and she read it twice.

"**The fourth one,**" she said. "**You can fix the fourth one. You have not fixed the fourth one because the fourth one is somebody else's and you have not asked.**"

He said that was not what he had expected and she said it was what she had, and he put the list back in his coat and did not ask her which was the fourth one out loud, and about two people in that room knew and neither said.

---

**The woman of about sixty does the books on a Wednesday in about eleven minutes and does them the same way every Wednesday, and it is the only part of that hour anybody watches.**

She writes the date at the top of a page. She writes what came in. She writes what went out. She puts a line under it and she does not rule anything and she does not use a ruler and the line is not straight.

**About four people in that room have said to one another over about four years that nobody in this city could do the books the way she does them, and none of them has ever said it in front of her, and she has never asked.**

---

---

**He goes up that stair on about nine Wednesdays out of eleven. The other two are the ones where something at the counter runs past about six and he is still standing there when the light in that room has already been on for about an hour.**

That has happened about four times in eleven years and on three of the four a woman of about sixty had seen him through the window from inside and had not come down and had not said anything.

**He has never worked out whether she saw him. She has never been asked. It is one of about nine small things in this city that nobody has ever put in a sentence to another person.**

---

**On that Wednesday, at about twenty past seven, a woman of about thirty-one said a thing to nobody.**

She said that she had been in that room in the autumn before the spring, twice, both times about a bill, and that she had sat in the sixth chair both times and had not been asked a question either time.

She said it in about nine seconds, to the middle of the table, and about four people heard it and about two of them looked at her and nobody asked her anything.

**She is a different woman from the one who said the thing about the chair on the Friday of that week, and the two of them have never been in a room together, and about four people in this city would confuse the two if anybody raised it.**

---

**He wanted to say something and he had about nine seconds to do it in and he used them on not saying it.**

---

**The room costs nothing and that is not nothing.**

Nobody pays to go up that stair. The building is a landlord and the landlord has never asked for anything for that room in about nineteen years and about four people in that room have thought about that at some point and about two of them have said so to the woman of about sixty and been told, in about nine seconds, that it is a room and a room is a room.

**Nobody has ever asked that landlord anything and he has never been in that room and about nine people in this city would say that is the most honest thing about it.**

---

**A man of about thirty-eight came at about a quarter past seven and said he had come about a bin, and the bin was not what he came about, and he had not come about it on the three previous Wednesdays either, and nobody asked him and nobody has asked him since.**

A woman of about thirty-one put her palm flat on the wood for the length of a breath and lifted it again and has said since that it was for nothing.

---

**What it cost him was one figure he had in his head and did not say, on the one day in this stretch of days on which the number of that room did not go up, and the figure is printed above and appears on no other page of this movement.**

**There are four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, at her chair, with her door shut, and her page is the eighth of eight and it has been on that shelf for a hundred and ninety-three days and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**
