# Chapter 771 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A First Floor In The Building That Receives The Returns From About Six With About Nine People In It For About Two Hours, A Man Of About Fifty-Two Who Is Handed A Pen At About Half Past Seven, And A Sheet That Goes Back Onto A Board With Its Middle Step Exactly As It Was

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then a first floor in the building that receives the returns. About nine people were in it for about two hours. A man of about fifty-two was handed a pen at about half past seven and there was a form on the table in front of him.**

---

Eleven names went on that Tuesday's sheet and eleven dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**The rooms off that service road are at one thousand four hundred and twenty-two days, two hundred and three weeks and one day, and the card on end in the rail by the door is one thousand four hundred and twenty-six, two hundred and three weeks and five days. The rail takes two.**

**Line twelve is one thousand three hundred and forty-two, a hundred and ninety-one weeks and five days. The nineteenth line is one thousand one hundred and eighteen, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks and five days. The hold on nine crates and the floor they stand on is one thousand and fifty-five, a hundred and fifty weeks and five days.**

Eight hundred and two days is the separation, a hundred and fourteen weeks and four days.

---

**The building that receives the returns is a landlord and a filing arrangement and about four people work in it, and about two of those four have worked there for about nine years, and the first floor has about nine chairs in it and about four of the chairs work.**

There is a board on the wall. It has about nine sheets on it. One of the nine is the withdrawal procedure and it has been printed since before the spring and it has never been run.

**The pin through the top left corner of it is still crooked. Nobody has taken it out and straightened it and about four people have looked at it on about four occasions and about none of them have said anything.**

---

He got to that room at about half past six and stood at the back for about an hour because that is where he stands.

At about half past five, in a shop in a district that is not this one, a man had brought in a gate closer that had dropped the last time it was thrown and had a bracket off, and it wanted a new arm and about four minutes of setting, and it had taken the four minutes and eleven pounds and the man had gone at about ten to six without saying anything beyond the eleven pounds.

**There were nine calls on the bench that day and two of the nine were the same closer in two different buildings, and about four people in this city maintain that closer and about nine of them maintain a different one and nobody has ever counted the closers.**

---

**The woman of about forty-four who keeps that room of lists gave the job to a man of about fifty-two in about nine seconds, in front of about nine people, and he took it, and nobody took it back.**

She said that the third step had been on that board since before the spring and that somebody had to put a period to it, and that she was not going to be the one holding the pen at nine o'clock on a Wednesday when the answer was not hers.

He said yes. He said it the way people say yes to a thing they have already been thinking about, and about four people in that room noticed and about two of them wrote nothing down.

---

**He uncapped the pen at about half past seven and put the point on the margin of the third step and wrote a figure and then stopped with the point still on the paper.**

The third step is nine words long and not one of the nine is a number.

*the institution shall respond within a period the institution considers appropriate.*

**About four people in that room had already read that step out loud on an earlier day and about four of them had read it differently and about two of them had read it the way they would read it if they were the one who had to decide.**

He had the pen on the margin. He did not write anything else for about nine seconds.

---

**And then the word did the thing it does, which is to be one word in four mouths and a different thing in every one of them, and it took about nine minutes to happen and nobody noticed it happening.**

A man of about thirty-one said the period is for the institution, and that the institution has about nine thousand returns a year and four people reading them, and that a period which is not about the institution is a period somebody has chosen for the institution without being in it.

A woman of about thirty-eight said the period is for the person who gave notice, and that a person who has given notice in writing is a person who is waiting, and that a person waiting is a person who cannot put a shift on next week.

A man of about sixty-one said that he has been coming to that room for about nine years and has never once been asked to hold anybody to anything, and that a period nobody names is a period nobody can be late for, and that the second of those is not a complaint.

**He said it to the floor. About four people heard it and about two of them wrote something down and neither of them wrote his name against it.**

A woman of about thirty-four said nothing at all.

---

**Four people wrote four figures in the margin of that step in four different hands, and no two of the four is the same figure, and none of the four said where it came from.**

Nineteen days. Eleven days. Six weeks. And one that is crossed out and cannot be read because the pen went through it.

**The woman of about thirty-four wrote nothing in the margin. She wrote on the back of a docket, which is what she has done four times a week for eleven years, and the docket went into a coat pocket and nobody has said whose pocket and there is no answer to that anywhere.**

---

**He read the third step out loud. He got as far as *the institution* and then he stopped for about nine seconds and asked the question that a man of about thirty-one had asked on a Monday in that room and that nobody had answered then either.**

He said, in about nine seconds, that he could not write a number on that line until somebody told him whether the number was for a person or for an office, and that it was the same question and that it had been asked in that room before, and that he was not going to guess in front of nine people.

**He put the pen down on the table at about ten past eight and it rolled about an inch and stopped against the sheet and he did not pick it up again.**

---

Nobody argued with him. That is worth saying properly, because the thing that usually happens in that room when a person declines to do a thing is that four people explain to him why the thing is easier if he does it, and none of that happened on that Tuesday.

**A man of about thirty-one said that he had asked the same question and had been told he would find out, and that it had been about nine days, and that nobody had found out and that he was not going to keep asking in a room where about nine people are sitting.**

A woman of about thirty-four said, in about nine seconds, that a man who will not write a number he cannot stand behind is cheaper than a man who will, and that she had been signing other people's names for about eleven years and that she had watched about four men do the other thing.

Nobody agreed with her out loud. About four people said nothing at all.

---

**At about twenty past eight the woman of about forty-four took the sheet off the table, squared it up against the edge of the table the way she had squared it up before, and carried it to the board and put it back in the same place.**

The third step was untouched. The four figures in the margin of it were not untouched, and nobody wiped them out, and nobody wrote under them, and they are on that board now and they are on it in four different hands.

**She put the pin back through the same hole it had been in and the hole is crooked and the pin is crooked and the two of them are now wrong together in a way that they were not wrong separately.**

---

**There is a hook on the back of that door for a coat and about nine coats go on it over about four months and there is always one that is somebody else's and about two people in that room have noticed that in about nine years and neither of them has said so.**

He left at about half past eight and was back on the stair at about ten.

**He wanted to be asked for the rest of that evening and he was asked for nothing, and the second time in about nine days that has happened is the time he has thought about it on a bench with a bracket in his hands.**

---

**And the thing he has not told anybody is the part after the pen went down, which is that he had a number ready before he came up and has not had it since about half past seven, and that he does not remember writing it down anywhere, and that it was nine days, and that he has not said nine days out loud to a living person in about two weeks.**

There were nine calls on the bench that day and eleven of them were dated and about two were declined and nothing was escalated and nothing was handed back.

---

**The rest of that Tuesday happened at about half past five in a shop and it took about two hours and a half and it is most of what he did that week.**

A woman of about thirty-six brought in a kettle from a back room off a road in a second district. It was not the kettle. The kettle came out of the shop at about four minutes past six working, and she had brought it because she had to walk past the shop anyway, which is the sentence she used and which was true.

The thing she had come with was a door closer off the same corridor, and it had been closing too fast since the winter and the man who fitted it had gone to the coast in the spring.

He took it off the rail in about nine minutes and put a washer in it that he had in a drawer since the summer, and the washer was not the washer for that closer and it was the size that fitted, and about four people in this city maintain closers and about nine of them would have told her to get a new one.

**She asked him what it would be in five years. He said he did not know and that he would not tell her, and she said that was the first time anybody in that street had said that to her, and neither of them said anything else about it.**

That was fourteen pounds and about nine minutes, and it came off the same sheet as the bracket, and both of them went on the sheet and both of them had a date against them and neither of them is the eleven names on the board.

---

**And at about half past two there was a chair strap, three pounds and about four words, and at about twenty past six a man of about twenty-eight came in with a bag of parts he had bought himself and asked whether it was worth fitting, and was told no in about four seconds for a reason about the part, and did not argue, and left with the parts.**

He put the bag back in the boot of the car and drove away. Nobody has seen the car again and nobody wrote down what was in the bag and about four people in this city have bought a bag of parts this year and carried it home.

---

**There are four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three, and from about half past six a woman of about thirty sat in the fourth of them, at her chair, with her door shut, and her page is the eighth of eight and it has been on that back shelf for two hundred and eleven days and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**
