# Chapter 777 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket From About Half Past Six Where The Fifty-Seventh Sitting Takes A Count And The Book Does Not Open, A Man In The Eighth Chair Who Is Asked Nothing By Anybody, And A Place Behind A Chair That Has Been Empty For Forty-Four Weeks And Is Printed Here And Nowhere Else In This Stretch Of Days

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then an hour on the first floor above a line in Saltmarket. A count was given there once and the green-covered book stayed shut. A man in the eighth chair was asked nothing by anybody at all.**

---

The sheet for that Wednesday carried fourteen names and fourteen dates, and the fourteenth name was taken at about twenty to five. At about ten the shutter came down, and it comes down at about ten on every day of this stretch of days.

**The rooms off that service road and one behind the other three are at one thousand four hundred and thirty days, two hundred and four weeks and two days, and the card on end in the rail by the door is one thousand four hundred and thirty-four, two hundred and four weeks and six days. The rail takes two.**

**Line eighteen is one thousand one hundred and forty-eight, one hundred and sixty-four weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and thirty-six, one hundred and forty-eight weeks to the day. What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and twenty, one hundred and sixty weeks to the day.**

Eight hundred and ten days is the separation, a hundred and fifteen weeks and five days.

---

**Behind the chair of the woman of about sixty there was an empty place and it had been empty three hundred and eight days, which is forty-four weeks to the day, and that figure is on this page and on no other page of this stretch of days.**

She has held that room for about nineteen years. The table is the table. The ninth chair is hard against that wall with its back to the whole of it and it did not move on that Wednesday and its mover is not named.

**About four people who came up that stair knew that figure before they came and about two of them knew it exactly and about none of them said anything to anybody, then or since.**

---

**The count was said once, in about nine seconds, in her face, by the woman of about sixty, and it was sixty-two of which fifty-seven, and she said it the way she says the date at the top of a page.**

Nobody said it again. Nobody wrote it down in that room. A man of about thirty-four who was in the eighth chair heard it and about four other people heard it and about two of those four have since said they heard it and neither of the two will say the number.

**A count is said at a sitting. A sitting is a Wednesday of every fourth week. About nine people in that room know that and about four of them have said so to themselves in a stair and about two of the four have said it out loud to a person who was not in the room.**

---

**The book was on sixty-six lines when she came up and it was on sixty-six lines when he left, about an hour later, and the last time it opened in that room a caller said a thing to her face and this time nobody said anything to her face at all.**

Which of the two is the reason is not known in that room and is not going to be said in that room. About four people in that room hold one opinion about it and about four hold the other and about one of the nine has not thought about it.

**Beside it on that table is a tin whose lid is down, and inside the tin is a receipt from a shop, and the receipt is doing the job of a page marker, and it does not belong to the woman holding that room. The tin stood where it was all evening.**

The two numbers are not added to each other in that room or in this city and about four people in that room have been asked which is the bigger and about none of them has said.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four sat in the eighth chair for the fifth Wednesday running and was asked nothing by anybody, and about four people in that room noticed that he was asked nothing, and about two of them noticed each other noticing.**

He sits in the eighth chair because from the eighth chair you can see the back of the ninth chair. He has never given a reason for that and nobody in that room has ever asked him for one.

**He came at about ten to seven and he left at about ten to eight and about fifty-one minutes of that was him and about nine minutes of it was a question he asked and about four people heard it and about two of them have described it since in about two different ways.**

The question was whether the about four people who have stopped coming to that room were on a list, and he had asked it once before, and this time he did not ask it, and the woman of about sixty said the thing about the door anyway, in about nine seconds, without being asked, and about four people in that room did not notice that she had not been asked.

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was at about half past two at a bench and at about four at a counter, and about four of the hours of that day were on a fire door in a second district and about nine minutes of it were on nothing at all.**

The fire door wanted a closer and got one, and the man who brought it said it had been catching on the frame since before the summer, and that the frame had swollen, and that about four people on that road had been lifting it.

He planed about a millimetre off the frame in about forty minutes and fitted the closer and charged twenty-one pounds and the man went at about six and said nothing beyond the money.


---

**What the woman of about sixty does in that hour is the only part of it anybody watches, and it takes her about eleven minutes, and she does it the same way every Wednesday.**

She puts the date at the top of a page. She writes what came in. She writes what went out. She rules a line underneath with the flat of a ruler she does not have, and the line is never straight, and about four people in that room have said to one another over about four years that nobody in this city could do the books the way she does them, and not one of the four has ever said it in front of her.

**She has never asked whether anybody can read them. She has never offered. About two people in that room have been able to read her writing since the spring and about four people believe they could not and have not tried.**

---

**A man of about forty-four brought a list of about nine things on that Wednesday and put it on the table and said they were the things he could not fix, and asked for one of them to be named as not his.**

She read it. It took her about eleven seconds and she read it twice and then she put it face down.

**What she said was four words long and about four people in that room heard them and about two of them wrote them down and neither of the two wrote her name against them, which is what they do with everything in that room.**

He did not ask her which one. He has not asked her in about nine weeks and he is not going to.

---

**And there was a woman of about thirty-four in that room for about nine minutes on that Wednesday, and she is not the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the books for a haulier on a road in a second district, and the two of them have never been in a room together, and about four people in this city would say they are the same person if anybody brought it up.**

She came in at about ten past seven because a woman of about thirty-one had told her the room was open, and she stood at the wall end, and she said one thing, and it was about a form, and it was that a form with a word at the top of a box and nothing under the word is a form that has been printed by somebody who expected a person to fill it in.

**Nobody asked her anything and nobody contradicted her and about four people in that room have since described what she said in about four different ways, and about two of the four have said she was talking about something else entirely.**

---

**He wanted to say something on that Wednesday and he had about nine seconds for it twice and he used them both on not saying it, and the two things he did not say were about a folded sheet in a different building and about a card on a wall that was nine feet from a board.**

He has not been in either building since the Monday and about four people in this city would say that is the correct order and he is not one of the four any more and has not been for about a week.

---

**And at about half past six a man of about sixty-one came in to that first floor, out of the rain, on his way to nothing, and stood at the back for about eleven minutes, and nobody asked him one question, and he left at about six.**

He has not been in that room since. Nobody in that room has ever said anything about that visit to anybody, and it is not on anything, and he does not know that it was noticed.

---

**A woman of about twenty-nine came in at about half past five and bought nine feet of cord and paid and did not say where it was going and did not need to, and about four people in that shop have noticed that she buys cord about once a fortnight and about none of them has ever asked her what for.**

---

**And at about half past six a man of about thirty-eight came into that shop and said he had come about a bin, and the bin was not what he came about, and he had not come about it on the three previous Wednesdays either, and nobody asked him and nobody has asked him since.**

A woman of about thirty-one laid her palm flat on the wood of the counter for about the length of a breath and then took it off again, and she has said since that it meant nothing at all.

**She is a different woman from the one who put her palm flat on the wood of a table in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket on a Wednesday about two weeks ago, and the two of them have never been in a room together, and about four people in this city would mix them up if anybody brought them both up.**

---

**In the fourth of the four rooms off that service road a woman of about thirty has been sitting since about half past six with her door shut. What is on the back shelf behind her is the eighth of eight and it has been there two hundred and fourteen days, and it is unread, and nobody is going to read it, and nobody has said one word to her about it in all that time.**

---
