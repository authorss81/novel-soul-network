# Chapter 776 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Room Off A Road In A Second District From About Five Where About Thirteen People Are Asked To Sign And Four Of Them Say No In Four Different Reasons, And A Gate At About Half Past Six Where One Of Those Four Says The Reason Out Loud In A Queue

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then a room off a road in a second district. About thirteen people were asked to sign a notice and four of them said no, in four different reasons and four different mouths. A district took the signatures it had and said what it could do with them.**

---

The sheet for that Tuesday carried eleven names with a date against each and the eleventh was taken at about twenty to five, and at about ten the shutter went down as it goes down on every day of this stretch of days.

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three are at one thousand four hundred and twenty-nine days, two hundred and four weeks and one day, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand four hundred and thirty-three, two hundred and four weeks and five days. The rail takes two.**

**Line thirteen is one thousand three hundred, one hundred and eighty-five weeks and five days. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and thirty-five, one hundred and forty-seven weeks and six days. What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and nineteen, one hundred and fifty-nine weeks and six days.**

Eight hundred and nine days is the separation, a hundred and fifteen weeks and four days.

---

**A notice went up on a Monday three weeks ago in about nine words and a date four weeks out, and about nine people read it and about four of them work in that building, and the notice is the second half of a thing a woman of about thirty-four has been putting up for about four years.**

The first half of that notice is what the district will keep doing until it goes. The second half is the one thing it will not keep doing.

**What was missing on that Monday was about nine signatures, and the signatures are not on the notice and the notice does not ask for any, and nobody in that district has ever written down who it is that a notice is for.**

---

**About thirteen people were asked on that Tuesday, which is everybody the district could name by about six o'clock that evening, and about nine of the thirteen signed and four of the four did not.**

The woman of about thirty-four did the asking. She had a sheet with thirteen lines on it and she asked with her finger on the line and she did not read the notice out again to anybody, because about nine of the thirteen had been in the room on the Monday and about four of the thirteen had not and both of those groups had already had it read to them by somebody else.

**She asked in about nine seconds each. About thirteen times. Nobody argued with her about any of the thirteen and about four of the thirteen asked her a question and she answered all four in about four seconds and none of the four questions was about the notice.**

---

**The four refusals are four different reasons in four different mouths and about four people who were in that room can give you all four and two of the four cannot remember which was which.**

A man of about forty-four said that he had a shift on the day and that a signature on a notice did not move a shift, and that he was not going to put his name under a thing that would be true on a Thursday and not true on a Wednesday.

A woman of about thirty-one said that she had signed nine things for that district in about four years and that eight of the nine had been about a bin, and that she wanted one that was not about a bin.

A man of about thirty-eight said that his brother worked at the yard and that he was not going to be the one who wrote it down, and that his brother had not asked him and that if his brother wanted to write it down his brother could write it down.

**A woman of about twenty-nine said, in about nine seconds, that she did not have anybody to ask, and that is the fourth reason and it is about nine words long and it is not about the notice.**

---

**And then the district did the thing that four people in that room had never seen a district do, which is that it did not ask why.**

There was no argument. There was no second asking. There was no meeting about the four. The woman of about thirty-four wrote four lines on the sheet, and marked them, and did not write a reason against any of the four, and turned the sheet round so that about nine people in that room could see it.

**A man of about thirty-one said, in about nine seconds and to the middle of the table, that the four were on the sheet, and that a line with a name on it and nothing after it was a line about a person and not about a view, and that nobody had to agree with it.**

Nobody agreed with him. Nobody disagreed with him either.

---

**And what the district did with what it had was said out loud in about four sentences, and it was said by the woman of about thirty-four, and it is the only part of that Tuesday anybody has written down since.**

About nine names go on the notice.

**Those nine names can hold a return, a gate and a sweep until the date the district gave. What those nine names cannot do is the yard. The yard is not a signature and the yard was never going to be a signature and about four people in that room had known that since the Monday the sheet went up and about nine had not.**

And a third thing was said, and it was about four hundred yards of yard behind a gate that opens at five, and it is eleven minutes at a walk and it cannot be crossed at all in the dark, and about nine people in that district know that and about four hundred people across three districts and half a bus route know the other half of it.

**Nobody in that room asked about the yard on that Tuesday. It is not on the notice and it is not on the sheet and it is not in the minute and about four people in that room have since written the word yard on a piece of paper at home and thrown the paper away.**

---

**The fifth reason was said out loud in a queue at about half past six and it was said to a person who was not in the room and it was said at a gate.**

One of the four is a man of about thirty-eight whose brother works at that yard, and he came at about half past six because he had heard about the four, and there were about nine people at that gate waiting to go through it, and he said his reason again, in about nine seconds, and about four of the nine heard it and about two of the nine repeated it inside the gate and about one of the two has said since that he did not know he was saying it.

**Nobody has told the institution which one of the four said it in the queue. Nobody has told the institution any of the four. The woman of about thirty-four has the sheet and the sheet has four names on it and nothing else and it is in a drawer in a room off that road.**

---

**And the last part of that Tuesday is that the man of about fifty-two was told at about half past seven that he has to write the period down, and that there are fewer people to write it for than there were on the Monday.**

He said nothing about that. He asked, in about nine seconds, a question about the form, and about four people in that room answered it, and about two of the four answered it differently, and about nine people left at about half past eight and nobody thanked anybody and nobody said a word about the four.

---

**A woman of about thirty-six came in at about half past five for a length of chain and paid and was not asked anything, and a man of about twenty-two came in at about six for a switch and paid and was not asked anything, and about four people in that shop that week have said that the two of them together are the quietest half hour the shop has had.**

---

**And what nobody did that Tuesday is as much of the record as what four people did.**

Nobody rang anybody. Nobody wrote to anybody. Nobody sent a second sheet out with a different wording on it. Nobody put the four names on any second page, and nobody copied the four reasons into the minute, and about two of the thirteen people who signed that afternoon asked the woman of about thirty-four whether the four had been spoken to and she said no in about four seconds and they left it there.

**A man of about thirty-eight who had asked for a list said, at about half past six, that a list would be about nine lines long and that the four of them were on it, and that a list of four on a sheet of thirteen is a list that can be read.**

She said that the sheet was a sheet of thirteen and that anybody who wanted a list of four could make one out of it in about four seconds, and that he was not to.

**He did not make one. About four people in that district have made one anyway, on the backs of about four different things, and about two of the four have thrown them away and about two have not.**

---

**The thirteen names on that sheet are the whole of what that district can be asked for, and about four hundred people use the thing the district is giving up, and those two figures have never been in the same sentence in this city.**

The nine hundred cards, the four hundred people, the thirteen names and the four reasons are four different counts and about four people in that room know all four and about two of those four have written any of them down and neither of the two has written more than one.

**Nobody has added them. Nobody in this city has a column that the four of them would go in, and about nine people in that district would tell you the four hundred is the important one and about four would tell you the thirteen is and they have all of them been in the same room.**

---

**At about half past six in the queue at that gate there were about nine people and a man of about thirty-eight said his reason in about nine seconds to a person who was not in the room.**

The person he said it to was a woman of about twenty-six on her way to a bus and she had not been asked for anything and did not know what the notice was. She heard about nine words. She has not given her name to anybody and about four people in that district have tried to find out what she said back and about none of them has found out.

**Nobody has told her since that a man said a thing about his brother in a queue. She has been asked about nothing by anybody in this city and she is not going to be.**

---

**And the rest of that Tuesday was a bench and a counter and about an hour of a gate closer in a third district that had dropped twice in a fortnight and that about four people in that street had been holding with a wedge.**

He took the closer off, cleaned it out, put a spring back that had gone soft, and told the man that a wedge in a fire door is how a building gets a fire. Nineteen pounds and about forty minutes, and the man paid and did not argue and has not put a wedge in it since.

**A woman of about thirty-six bought a length of chain at about half past five and was not asked anything. A man of about twenty-two bought a switch at about six and was not asked anything. Neither of them has been on any other day of this stretch of days and neither of them is anybody's.**

**In the fourth of the four rooms off that service road a woman of about thirty has been at her chair with her door shut since about half past six, and there is a fifth room behind the other three with a shelf in it. The shelf has held the eighth of eight for two hundred and thirteen days. No one has put a question to her about it and there is nobody in this city who could.**
