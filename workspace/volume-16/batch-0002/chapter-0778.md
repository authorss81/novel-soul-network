# Chapter 778 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A First Floor In The Building That Receives The Returns From About Six With A Form On The Table And A Box On It With Nothing In It, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Who Writes Twenty-One Days In It And Says In About Nine Seconds That It Is Wrong By A Fortnight Before Anybody Has Found Out

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then a first floor over a counter where returns are taken in. About nine people were in it for about two hours. A man of about fifty-two put a number into an empty box on a form and said out loud, before anyone had looked at it, that the number was wrong.**

---

Nine names went on that Thursday's sheet and nine dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**The card on end in the rail by the door is at one thousand four hundred and thirty-five days, two hundred and five weeks to the day. The rail takes two.**

**Line twelve is one thousand three hundred and fifty-one, one hundred and ninety-three weeks to the day. Line thirteen is one thousand three hundred and two, one hundred and eighty-six weeks to the day. Line fourteen is one thousand two hundred and sixty-seven, one hundred and eighty-one weeks to the day. Line fifteen is one thousand two hundred and forty-six, one hundred and seventy-eight weeks to the day. The nineteenth line is one thousand one hundred and twenty-seven, one hundred and sixty-one weeks to the day. The hold on nine crates is one thousand and sixty-four, one hundred and fifty-two weeks to the day.**

Eight hundred and eleven days is the separation, a hundred and fifteen weeks and six days.

---

**The form is a new one and it has been printed in the winter and about nine people in this city have seen it and about four of them have read it and it has a box at the bottom of it with a line ruled under the box and nothing printed above the line.**

The form says what a district will be told before it goes, and by whom, and it says when, and the when is the box.

**There is no number printed anywhere on that form. There is a line under the box and about four people in that room had looked at the line under the box before that Thursday and about two of them had said, in about nine seconds each, that somebody would have to write on it.**

---

**The man of about fifty-two had the pen because he had been the one holding the form, and about four people in that room had assumed he would and nobody had said so out loud.**

He uncapped it at about half past six. He read the whole form. He read the box. He put twenty-one days in the box, and he wrote it in about four seconds, and it was in the box for about eleven.

**And then he said, in about nine seconds and to the middle of the table, that twenty-one days is wrong, that it is wrong by a fortnight, and that the number that belongs in the box is seven.**

Nobody had checked it. Nobody had read it out. Nobody had a pen in their hand. **He said it before about nine people had finished blinking and about four of them have said since that the speed of it is the only part of that Thursday anybody remembers.**

---

**The room found out why in about nine minutes and the reason was not about the form and was about a gate and a yard.**

A woman of about thirty-one said that the rota at that yard is made seven days ahead, and that a person who works there finds out on a Friday what they are doing the following Friday, and that everything anybody in that district is told about a shift arrives seven days before the shift.

A man of about thirty-eight said that the gate opens at five and that a person told on the morning of a working day has already left the house, and that about four hundred yards of yard is eleven minutes at a walk and cannot be crossed at all in the dark.

**A woman of about thirty-four said that twenty-one days is a figure for a person who has time, and that about nine of the about four hundred people who use that line are children, and that a child is not told three weeks ahead.**

The man of about fifty-two wrote seven days in the box. He crossed twenty-one out first and he crossed it out with one stroke.

---

**It was corrected in the room in about nine minutes and about two people noticed, afterwards and not at the time, that he had not had to say anything at all.**

A man of about thirty-one pointed out that nobody had looked at the box. A man of about sixty-one said that a wrong number in a box on a form is a thing that gets found out four months later by somebody with no idea what it was for, and that saying it in a room is cheaper than that.

**Nobody said that to the man of about fifty-two. About four people in that room thought it and about two of them have said it since to people who were not in that room.**

He said nothing about the four seconds it took him to write twenty-one. He has not said anything about it to anybody, and about four people in this city would say he thought about it for about four days before that Thursday and about two of them would say he thought about it on the Monday.

---

**Nobody thanked him. That is worth putting in its own line because about four people in that room noticed at about half past seven that nobody had thanked him, and about two of them noticed each other noticing.**

The form went back into a folder with the box filled in. It is the first time that box has ever had anything in it and about four people in this city know that and about two of them know it from having been in the room.

**The form will go out with the answer to the district and nobody in that room has been told who will sign the answer, and the district has nine names on its notice, and the period is now a period for nine people and not for thirteen.**

---

**And the other half of that Thursday is that he was four feet from that table for about two hours and he was not asked one question and he did not say one thing about the number.**

He was four feet away and he had a scar and eleven years and about two people in this city who would let him hold a line, and the number in the box was about a gate and a yard and a rota, and he knew all three of those and said nothing about any of them.

**He wanted to be asked what the number should be and he was not asked, and that is the fifth time in about nine days, and it is the first one where he had an answer that he had worked out himself about four days ago and had to keep.**

A woman of about thirty-four who works four days a week in the shop was at that table and did not look at him once while the number was being corrected.

---

**At about half past seven he came down the stair and went to the counter and the counter took him until about ten, and about four people came in and about two of them wanted to know what had been decided in that room and he told them what had been decided in that room and nothing else.**

A man of about fifty-two came in at about half past five for a switch and paid and left and was not asked anything.


---

**And the part of that Thursday that nobody in that room argued about is the part where about four people found out what a period on a form actually is for.**

Nobody said it out loud. It came out of the nine minutes of reasons in about four separate pieces, and about four people in that room put them together at about ten past seven and about two of them said so and about two of them did not.

**A period on that form is not a period the institution takes. It is a period the person who is leaving has, before they are told, in which to put a shift on what they are told.**

Nobody wrote that down. About four people in that room have said that sentence since, in about four different rooms, and about two of them have written it down and about two of those two have put it in a form and about none of the two has sent it anywhere.

---

**There is a second box on that form and nobody filled it in on that Thursday and about two people noticed that it was still empty when the form went back into the folder.**

The second box is above the first one and it has four lines ruled in it and the heading printed over the top of the four lines, and the heading is a word about what the person is owed, and the four lines were for writing it down and nobody wrote anything in them.

**A woman of about thirty-four said, in about nine seconds and to nobody, that nobody can fill in that box until somebody says what the person is owed, and then about four people in that room looked at the third step of a printed sheet on a wall in a different building, and about none of them said that out loud.**

---

**And the man of about fifty-two left that room at about half past seven and went and stood on the stair for about nine seconds and then went down it, and a man of about thirty-eight who was still in the room said afterwards that he looked as though he had wanted to be asked and had not been, and about four people in that room agreed and about two of them said it was a cruel thing to say about a man of fifty-two.**

Nobody has asked the man of about fifty-two whether he wanted to be asked and he has not offered.

---

**The rest of that evening after about half past six was a counter, a bench and a drawer that was locked.**

A woman of about thirty-one came in at about half past five with a lamp from a stair and asked whether the bulb in it could be changed at the counter and was told it could not and that about four shops in this city would do it, and she asked which four and was not told, and she went out and came back at about six with the bulb already bought and it was changed in about four minutes for three pounds.

**A man of about fifty-two came in at about six for a switch and paid and left and was not asked anything, and the two of them together are the whole of the counter between half past five and about ten, and about four people in that shop have said that a quiet evening is a good evening and about two of them have said it twice.**

---

**A woman of about thirty-four came in at about six for a length of cord and paid and left and did not say where it was going, and about four people in that shop have noticed that she buys cord about once a fortnight and about none of them has ever asked her what for.**

---

**The fourth of the four rooms off that service road has had a woman of about thirty sitting in it with her door shut since about half past six. What is on the back shelf behind her is the eighth of eight and it has been on that shelf two hundred and twenty days, and it has not been looked at, and nobody in this city is going to look at it, and nobody has said a word to her about any of that.**

---
