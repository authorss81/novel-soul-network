# Chapter 775 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Wall Beside A Stair In A Second District From About Eleven Where A Card Goes Up Over A Paler Rectangle, Four People In That Building Who Look At The Gap And Two Of Them Who Say Something About It, And Two Lines Of Type That Neither The Building Nor Anybody In It Has To Obey

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then a wall beside a stair in a second district. A card went up on it at about eleven. Four people in that building looked at what was showing at the edge of it and two of them said something about it, and nothing was said to either of them.**

---

Thirteen names went on that Monday's sheet and thirteen dates went with them, the last at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three are at one thousand four hundred and twenty-eight days, two hundred and four weeks to the day. The card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand four hundred and thirty-two, two hundred and four weeks and four days. The rail takes two.**

**Line sixteen is one thousand two hundred and eighteen, one hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of the front of one page are nine hundred and ninety-four, one hundred and forty-two weeks to the day.**

Eight hundred and eight days is the separation, a hundred and fifteen weeks and three days.

---

**The wall is beside a stair and it is a corridor wall and the corridor goes past about nine people between seven and two on an ordinary day, and about four of those nine look at a wall on the way past.**

The card went up at about eleven on the Monday and it went up on a Monday because that is the day about nine people in that building are on that stair.

**Nobody in that building has to obey a line on a card, and nobody in that building did.**

---

**There was already something on that wall and the something was a rectangle about the size of a playing card, and the rectangle was paler than the wall around it, and the paint on the rim of it was not cracked.**

It had not been cleaned. It had been rubbed.

**A woman of about thirty-four who keeps the books for a haulier on that road has put a card in that spot for about four years and she took the old one down on the Friday and it went into her bag, and nobody in that building has ever asked her who asked for the first one and she has never been asked and she has not offered.**

The rim of the rectangle is rubbed back about a finger's width on the top edge and about a finger's width on the left edge, and the other two edges are not rubbed at all, and about four people in this city have looked at those two rubbed edges and worked out from them that a card has been lifted off that wall by the top left corner for about four years.

**Nobody has said that out loud in that building and Marek is not in that building and did not see the rectangle until about eleven on the Monday.**

---

**The new card is about the size of a playing card and it covers all of that rectangle except the two rubbed edges, and about a finger's width is showing at the top and at the left.**

That is the whole of what the new card did to the old of them.

**A card put exactly where a card has been put about nine hundred times hides the one thing about the spot that nine hundred cards made, and nobody in that building decided to do that and about two of the four who looked at the gap noticed it inside about eleven seconds.**

---

About four people in that building looked at it and about two of them said something.

One of the two is a man of about thirty-eight who has worked on that stair for about nine years and who said, to a woman of about thirty-four, that whoever put it up could have set it a finger's width lower and further in from the edge and the older one would have shown all the way round.

She said, in about four seconds, that she was not going to move it a finger's width to make an old card show.

**He said that was a strange thing to want and she said it was not a strange thing to want, and neither of them said anything else about it, and he went back to his work and she went back to the corridor.**

The other of the two is a woman of about twenty-nine who has worked at the counter at the bottom of that stair for about two years and who said nothing at all, and who stood looking at the gap for about nine seconds, and who has not mentioned it to anybody since and is not going to.

**Nobody told either of them that the gap was there on purpose and neither of them had asked and the person who put the card up had not said.**

---

**The two lines on that card are these.**

*A district may give notice in writing and state its reason.*
*The reply comes in a period that is written down.*

**They are about a process. They are not about anybody's mind and they do not say what anybody ought to think and about four people in this city have read them and about four of the four read them as instructions and about two of the four read them as a promise, and both of the two are wrong and neither of them has been told.**

Nobody in that building has to do either of those two things. Nobody has to give notice. Nobody has to wait for a period. The card is on a wall and about nine people a day go past it and about four of them look and about two of those four have said nothing.

---

**And there is a third thing about that wall which is not about the card, and it is that a printed sheet is about nine feet from it with two drawing pins in it, and about nine people have read that sheet and about four of them work in that building.**

The sheet went up on a Monday three weeks ago and it names a district's intention in about nine words and a date four weeks out, and it is a sheet of paper and it is bigger than a playing card and it is on a board and the board is not the wall the card is on.

**One of the about four people who work in that building and who has read the sheet is the man who read it standing up on the Monday it went up and said nothing then and has said nothing since, and he did not look at the card when it went up and he walked past it about nine times that morning.**

Nobody in that building has put the two things together in a sentence and Marek did not either and he was not there.

---

**The rest of that Monday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and about four hours of a door closer in a second district that had been slamming since the autumn.**

The closer was on a fire door and it had been slamming hard enough to be heard from a road, and the man who brought it said it had been like that since before the summer and that about four people in his building had stopped closing it and were holding it instead.

He took it off and put a new spindle in it that had been in a drawer since the summer and it took about forty minutes, and it cost nineteen pounds, and the man went at about six and said that was better than holding it.

**A woman of about thirty-four came in at about half past five for nine feet of cord and paid and left and did not mention the card and did not look at the wall on her way out, and nobody has asked her whether she read it and she has not said.**

---


---

**The corridor itself is about eleven feet wide and the wall is painted to about the height of a door handle in a colour that has been repainted twice, and the repainting is why the rectangle shows at all.**

A wall in this city that has been repainted gets a new colour and the old colour is under it, and anything that was against the wall for long enough leaves a shape where it kept the paint from drying.

**The rim of that rectangle is not a clean edge and it is not a crack and it is not a hole. It is the paint on the edge of the shape that has been gone over by hands, and about nine hundred hands in about four years is enough to do what it has done to about a finger's width of rim.**

Nobody in that building has ever cleaned it and nobody in that building has ever painted over it, and about four people in that building have wondered about it in about four years and about two of them have asked each other about it in a stair and neither has asked the woman.

---

**And the woman of about thirty-four was asked about it, and not by him, and she answered it in about nine seconds and she did not soften it.**

A man of about sixty-one who has a key to a cupboard in that building asked her at about half past four whether she knew the wall had been marked, and she said that she had known for about four years and that the first one was not hers either.

He said that was a strange thing to have been handed. She said it was a wall.

**He did not press it and she did not explain it and about four people in that building have since said the word inheritance out loud and about two of them have put it in writing and neither of the two has put her name next to it.**

---

**He heard the whole of it at about half past five on the Monday from a woman of about thirty-four who works four days a week in the shop, and she had been up that stair at about one o'clock and had seen it, and she told him in about eleven words and did not tell him what was under it.**

He asked what was under it. She said there was nothing under it.

**He asked her twice. The second time she said that she had not looked and that he was not to go up there, and about four people in that shop at that moment thought that was the wrong answer and about two of them did not think anything at all.**

He did not go up there that Monday and he has not been, and about four people in this city would say that is the correct order, and he has stopped saying that about himself because he does not know any more.

---

**The rest of that Monday after about half past five was a counter and a bag of parts and a kettle.**

A man of about twenty-eight brought the bag back. It is the same bag he brought about two weeks ago and about nine days ago, and it has about nine things in it and about four of them are the same four things.

A woman of about thirty-four looked at it for about nine minutes and told him that about two of the nine were the part the thing he had actually wanted and about four of the nine were the part for a different thing altogether, and that somebody in a shop in a first district had told him the second thing.

He said nobody had told him anything. She said that was not her doing, and he carried the bag away, and it is the third time, and about four people in that shop have noticed and about none of them has said anything to him about the count.

---

**And at about half past six a woman of about thirty-six came in for a strap and paid and went out and was not asked anything and has not been in that shop on any other day of this stretch of days.**

**The fourth of those four rooms has had a woman of about thirty sitting in it since about half past six with her door shut, and behind the other three is a fifth room and that is where the shelf is. On that shelf, for two hundred and twelve days now, has been the eighth of eight, and there is nobody in this city with a claim to it and nobody with the standing to ask her.**
