# Chapter 774 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, One Of The Four Rooms Off That Service Road From About Half Past Six With Two Lines Of Type Being Cut Down To One Of Them In About Nine Seconds, And A Two-Man Print Shop On A Road Off Saltmarket At About Half Past Seven Where Four Pounds Go Into A Tin And Nobody Asks Who Wanted It

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then one of the four rooms off that service road. Two lines of type were set for a card and one of them was too long. Four pounds went into a tin in a two-man print shop and nobody in that shop asked who had wanted it.**

---

Ten names went on the sheet for that Friday with ten dates against them, the last taken at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three are at one thousand four hundred and twenty-five days, two hundred and three weeks and four days. The card standing on end in the rail by the door is one thousand four hundred and twenty-nine, two hundred and four weeks and one day. The rail takes two.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven, one hundred and seventy-one weeks to the day. The post at that corridor end is nine hundred and seventy-three, one hundred and thirty-nine weeks to the day.**

Eight hundred and five days is the separation, a hundred and fifteen weeks to the day.

---

**The card is about the size of a playing card and it has two lines of type on it and nothing else on it, and it has been going about four years and somebody has been having it made for most of that time.**

What is on it is a process and not a thought.

*A district may give notice in writing and state its reason.*
*The reply comes in a period that is written down.*

**That is the whole of it. It is not a warning and it is not an argument and it does not say anything about anybody's mind, and about four people in this city have read it and about nine of them have read it standing up and one of the nine is the man who read it upside down.**

---

**The first line was settled in about four seconds and the second one took about nine minutes and then about nine seconds.**

The second line as it was written on the sheet in that room on that Friday had nine words on it after the first three.

*The reply comes in a period that the institution has decided is appropriate for that district.*

**Nobody in that room said that sentence was wrong. What happened is that a man of about thirty-four read it out loud twice and then said that it is the same nine words as the third step of a form he has read in a different building, and that a card with the form's own sentence on it is a card that says the form has no number in it.**

A woman of about thirty-one said, in about nine seconds, that the sentence had to go, and that what it had to become was six words shorter on the card than the sentence was on the sheet, and that the words it lost were the ones about the institution deciding, because the whole point of the card is that somebody has written a number down somewhere.

**She cut it in about four seconds and then read it out and about four people in that room agreed with the shorter one and about two of them did not and neither of the two said anything.**

---

**And the man of about fifty-two asked, in about nine seconds and to the middle of the table, what the card was for, since it does not order anybody to do anything.**

A woman of about thirty-four said that it was for people who cannot get to a room, and that about four of the nine people who pass the wall it is going up on cannot get to that first floor on a Wednesday, and that a card is not an alternative to a room and it is the only thing that exists for somebody who is not going to come.

**He wrote the two lines on a sheet and gave the sheet to nobody and about four people in that room copied them and about two of the two wrote them differently in the second line and neither of the two has been asked about it.**

---

**The man of about fifty-eight has had a two-man print shop on a road off Saltmarket for about nineteen years and he set those two lines himself, in one of the two faces he has, and the plate came into the shop in a box rather than through the door.**

He was not asked to set anything that Friday. **A woman of about twenty-six who works the counter in that shop and who has been there about two years carried the sheet in at about half past seven and paid for it and wrote a receipt and did not ask who wanted it.**

**He charged the going rate for four hundred cards and the money went into the tin under the counter, which is the tin that office's leaflet money went into years ago, and the day's takings came to nine pounds less than the order was worth and he has said so to nobody.**

---

**She asked him one question and it was about nine words long and it was the only question anybody asked in that shop about that card on that Friday, and he answered it in about four seconds and it was not the answer she wanted.**

She asked whether the card was going up anywhere he had been. He said he did not know and had not asked and that a printer does not ask.

**She said that a man who prints four hundred of a thing and does not know where they go is not printing, and that he is making paper, and that there is a difference and he does not care about it.**

He said, in about four seconds, that he had heard it before and agreed with it, and that he had printed a leaflet in this city once for money and had been asked the same question by somebody else in about nine seconds and had not answered it then either.

**She did not ask again. She has not asked anybody about that card since and she is not going to, and about four people who know she carried the sheet in have not asked her and are not going to.**

---

**The first four hundred went out of that shop at about half past seven on the Friday in a bundle, and about nine hundred went out, and nobody counted them and nobody has counted them since.**

**Four hundred was the number on the order. The press was already locked up and the paper was already cut and the man of about fifty-eight did the run and charged for four hundred and said nothing, and that is the whole of it and it is not a scheme and nobody is going to call it one.**

There is a receipt in the tin for four pounds and it is the only one and it has no name on it and it is not a membership and it does not go with anything.

---

**At about half past four on that Friday a man of about fifty-two came into the shop in Lattice Ward with a bracket off a gate and wanted it looked at and it was looked at and it was a bracket and it was about nine minutes and it was eleven pounds, and he was not the man who wanted the card and did not know there was a card.**

That is the last of the ordinary work on that Friday and it is about half of it.

**A woman of about thirty-six came at about ten past six for a length of cord and paid and left, and she was not asked anything, and she has not been in that shop on any other day of this stretch of days.**

---

**And there is a second thing about that card which nobody in that room agreed about and which is still not agreed about, and it is whether a card is a notice or an instruction.**

A man of about fifty-two said it was a notice, because a notice tells you what has happened and a card tells you what has happened. A woman of about thirty-four said it was an instruction, because it tells you a reply comes in a written period and that is telling somebody to do a thing by a time.

**Neither of them was answered and about four people in that room had views and about two of them said nothing, and the card went to a print shop with the disagreement still in it and nobody thought of that at the time.**

---

**About half an hour of that Friday was a bench and a gate and about two hours of it was the shop, and there was a stretch of about forty minutes at about half past four in which nothing at all happened, which is the only part of any day of this stretch of days that anybody in this city would describe as quiet.**

A man of about fifty-two came in with a gate on a hinge that had dropped twice in the winter. It wanted the same work a gate wanted nine days earlier in a first district and it took about an hour and a half because the screw holes had been redrilled by somebody else and the plate would not lie flat.

**He was paid eleven pounds for the gate and he said, at about half past six, that the plate had been drilled by a man who was in a hurry, and that there is no way of telling that from a gate when it comes off the hinge, and that about nine gates in this city are like it and about four of them are on the same road.**

A woman of about thirty-four who works four days a week asked him, while he was packing the tools, whether he was going to say that in a room. He said no. She said that was the first time in about four years he had said no to anything about a room, and neither of them said anything else about it.

---

**At about twenty past six a man of about sixty came for a length of chain and paid for it and went out again and was back at about half past six for a different length of chain and paid for that one as well and neither purchase was the chain he had come for.**

He has not been back since and nobody has asked him what the two lengths were for and the till has both of them on it and they are two separate lines and about four people in that shop remember the two visits and not the reason.

---

**In the fourth of those four rooms, and behind the other three as well, a woman of about thirty has been sitting since about half past six with her door shut. Her page is the eighth of eight, the shelf behind her has held it for two hundred and fourteen days, and there is nobody in this city entitled to ask her about it.**
