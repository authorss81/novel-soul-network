# Chapter 793 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where A Sheet Was Cut Down To Three Lines Because Nobody Could Think Of A Way To Stop A Person Reading It On A Bus, And Where Nobody Had Drawn The Streets

**A Thursday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district, where a sheet of paper was made smaller all evening by people insisting on what it must not carry. A person was found to carry it. The nine streets were not drawn, and nobody in that room could say where they began.**

---

The board for that Thursday took nine names and nine dates, the last of the nine at twenty to five. At ten the shutter was down, and it goes down at ten on every day of this stretch of days.

**The four rooms off that service road and the one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and fifty-two days, two hundred and seven weeks and three days.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand two hundred and twenty-four days, a hundred and seventy-four weeks and six days. Line eighteen is one thousand one hundred and seventy days, a hundred and sixty-seven weeks and one day.**

**The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand and eighteen days, a hundred and forty-five weeks and three days, one corner torn, eight unfinished, and not two of them have ever been laid against each other.**

---

**The job that Thursday was to get one person to walk one sheet across three districts and half a bus route, and the sheet was finished at half past five and the walking was not.**

What had to happen was that a person should be found who would carry a sheet of paper, on foot, from that counter to nine streets and back, and who would say one thing to anybody who stopped them.

Nobody in that room had ever asked anybody to carry anything on foot. About four people in this city have been asked in the last four years and about two of the four carried it and about two did not come back.

**A man of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that the carrying was the easy half and that the half nobody had a sentence for was what happens at the other end of the walk.**

---

**And then a man of about thirty-eight said the thing that stopped the evening, and it was about a bus, and nobody in that room had an answer to it.**

He had said nine times out of ten, in the last four weeks, that a sheet on a board is a thing a person walks past, and that a sheet in a hand is a thing a person can take and read, and that the difference between those two is the whole of what anybody is about to do.

"**I do not know how to stop a person reading a thing on a bus,**" he said. "**I have been telling people a date for a fortnight and I have not thought once about the nine minutes between the front door and the second stop. Somebody is going to sit on that bus and read it and take it in and be angry about it before they get to work, and then they are angry at me, and I will not have said anything.**"

**Nobody answered him. Four people in that room tried for eleven minutes and every attempt was a way of stopping people getting on buses, and the district is not going to stop people getting on buses.**

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-four said the thing that solved it, in nine seconds, and what she said was about how many things a person can carry in their head.**

"**Three,**" she said. "**A date and a street and where to put it. Three facts is a thing a person can hold in their head on a bus and they will say it correctly. Four facts is a thing they cannot hold, and so they will say it wrongly, and we will spend a fortnight explaining what we meant.**"

A man of about thirty-eight said that was the same as saying a sentence has to be brief, and she said that it was not, and that a brief sentence can still be wrong on a bus, and that what she was describing was a quantity and not a length.

---

**So the sheet was cut down on that Thursday evening in three passes, and every pass took something off it that somebody in that room had put on it, and nobody argued about any of the removals.**

It had carried the practice. That came off first, at nine seconds' notice, and nobody in that room said what the practice was while it was coming off.

It had carried a reason. That came off second, and the woman of about forty-eight said the thing about four hundred people and about agreeing, and she had said it before, and nobody stopped her.

**What was left on that sheet at half past five was three lines. A date. A street. And a line saying where a person can go and look at what has been said about them.**

A woman of about thirty-eight said, nine seconds later, that this is the first document in eleven years in this city that a person could actually carry, and that she did not know whether that was a good thing, and that nobody had asked her to have an opinion about it.

---

**And then the person who would carry it was settled, and it was not by a vote, and the man of about twenty-two was refused in four seconds when he offered.**

The woman of about thirty-four, who keeps the books for a haulier on a road in that district, said she would carry it. She said the reason in nine seconds and it was that she drives past that gate at a quarter to seven every morning and that about nine people come through it before half past seven, and that a person who says a thing on a bus says it wrong and a person who says it standing in a gateway at six in the morning does not.

The man of about twenty-two said that he could walk it and that he was free at seven. She said no in four seconds and gave a reason, and the reason was that nine people in that district know his face from a counter in a first district, and that four hundred people who use that line are not four hundred people who use that counter.

He has said since that she was correct and that he had not thought of it, and that a person being refused for a reason that is true is a thing that happens and that he has had about nine of those this year.

**Nobody thanked her. She is going to walk it on the Monday and about nine people in this city know that and about four of them have told somebody who was not asked.**

---

**And then somebody asked where the streets are, and it turned out that nobody in that room could say, and that is the whole of the rest of that Thursday.**

About four hundred people live on nine streets in that district. A half bus route goes past all nine of them. About nine hundred other people live on the same nine streets and have never been on that bus.

Nobody in that room could draw it. Four people tried in eleven minutes and each of them got as far as two or three names and then stopped, and one of them said that a street with two names is where it stops.

A woman of about forty-eight said, in nine seconds, that a street with two names is not a small problem and is the whole reason nobody has ever drawn it, and that about four hundred people use one of those two names and about nine hundred use the other and neither of those figures knows about the other.

Nobody drew anything on that Thursday. The sheet of three lines was rolled into a tube and stood on a shelf against the wall at half past six, and it was not written on, and it was not taken out of the room by anybody.

---

**And the sheet of three lines was read out in that room on that Thursday evening by four people in four different ways, and nobody in that room said anything about any of the four, and that was noted afterwards by somebody who was not in the room.**

Four lines. Three of them were the same line said by four mouths. The fourth person put the date before the words, so that anybody listening would get the day first and the fact second, and one of them has said since that this is the more useful order, and another has said the other.

**Nobody decided which order it should be in, because nobody noticed it happening. The most important arrangement made that week was made by four people saying a thing in the wrong order, and nobody in that room has said so and it is not on anything.**

A man of about thirty-eight asked whether a person could be given the sheet and be asked to sign for it. A woman of about thirty-eight said that a signature on that sheet is a person's name on a fact, and that there is no fact on it, only a date, and that nobody's name goes on a date.

Nobody wrote that anywhere. It is the second time this fortnight that something has been settled by somebody not writing it down, and the district's notice was settled the same way, and nobody has compared the two.

---

**The rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and forty minutes on a duct in a workshop in a first district, where an extraction fan had been taped rather than repaired by two men three years apart.**

The fan draws through a flexible duct and the duct had a length of eleven inches where two men had put tape over a tear, and tape over a tear in a duct does not stop a tear and does stop anybody seeing it.

He took the two lengths of tape off, opened the duct at the joint, found the joint had separated about half an inch, and put a proper band round it and re-taped it and told the man that the band was the repair and the tape was only there to keep the band warm.

Nineteen pounds and forty minutes. The man said he had a man in who comes on Fridays and that the last man had charged him for a strip of silver tape and said nothing else, and he was told that a man who does not open a joint cannot know what is inside it, and that silver tape is a promise about the outside of a thing.

---

**And the fan that duct came off had been running at half speed for two years because the damper in the fresh-air inlet had been wired shut and nobody in that building knew it had been wired at all.**

He found it by holding a strip of paper at the inlet and finding that it did not move, and then by opening the inlet cover and finding four turns of wire round the spindle and a padlock through the handle.

He cut the wire, took the padlock off, and told the man what had been holding the damper shut, and the man said he had put the padlock on because the damper had been open in the winter and the building had been cold.

**He said that a damper is a thing that is closed most of the year and open in the cold, and that a man who locks it shut in the winter to save the heat has also locked it shut in the summer to save the water, and that about two thirds of the running cost of that building had been going out of an open window for two summers.**

He charged nineteen and said that the fan is not the duct and not the duct tape and is not the motor, and that the motor had been blamed by two men and is the only part of it that was never touched. The fan ran at full speed for eleven minutes afterwards and he listened to it and said that the noise is the noise it will make for a month and then quieter.

**And the second job that Thursday was a compressor in the same building whose drain valve was seized and which had been emptying itself into a tray for four months without anybody opening the tray.**

The drain is a float valve and it had a tray under it and the float had stuck down, so the machine emptied itself all the time it was running and the tray had gone over the top twice and both times somebody had emptied it and told nobody.

He freed the float, cleaned the valve, and told the man that a machine which drains when it runs is a machine losing air and having to fill it again, and that this is four months of a compressor working to keep up with a leak. Twenty-two pounds and half an hour, and he told him to put a date on the tray so that the next person to empty it can see it is not a one-off.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six with her door shut, and on the back shelf behind her the eighth of eight has been sitting for two hundred and forty-one days without anybody opening it.**
