# Chapter 797 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where The Fifty-Eighth Sitting Took A Count And The Book Did Not Open And Nobody Asked It To, And Where A Man Of About Thirty-Four Arrived Late And Found Somebody Else In The Eighth Chair

**A Wednesday began at a bench, went through a counter, and went up a stair where a count was given once and a book stayed shut because nobody had said anything to the woman holding it. Nothing in that hour was the point of the week. The book did not open and nobody asked it to.**

---

The sheet for that Wednesday carried fourteen names and fourteen dates, and the fourteenth name was entered at twenty to five. The shutter was down at ten, which is the hour it goes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**The rooms off that service road and the one behind the other three are at one thousand four hundred and fifty-eight days, two hundred and eight weeks and two days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand four hundred and sixty-two days, two hundred and eight weeks and six days.**

**Line sixteen is one thousand two hundred and forty-eight days, a hundred and seventy-eight weeks and two days. Line seventeen is one thousand two hundred and thirty days, a hundred and seventy-five weeks and five days.**

---

**The count was said once, in nine seconds, in her face, by the woman of about sixty, and it was sixty-three of which fifty-eight.**

Nobody said it again. Nobody wrote it down. A man of about thirty-four, who arrived at about ten to seven and was eleven minutes late for the first time in five Wednesdays, heard it from the stair and not from her.

**A count is said at a sitting. Nine people in that room know that a sitting is a Wednesday of every fourth week. Four of them have said so to themselves in that stair and two of the four have said it out loud to a person who was not there.**

---

**And the book was on sixty-six lines when she came up and it was on sixty-six lines when the last person went down, and nothing at all was said to her face to open it.**

The last time it opened in that room a caller said a thing to her face. This time nobody said a thing to her face at all, and nobody asked her to open it, and nobody asked her not to.

Four hold one opinion about why and four hold another, and one has not thought about it and has said so.

Beside the book is a tin with its lid down and a receipt in it doing the work of a page marker, and the receipt does not belong to the woman holding that room, and the tin did not move on that Wednesday.

---

**And the eighth chair had a man of about fifty-three in it, which it had not had on any of the five previous Wednesdays, and nobody remarked on it and nobody asked him anything.**

He has been in that room four times. He came in at about ten past seven and left at half past seven and forty minutes of that was him. He has a grey coat on the back of that chair and he has had the same coat since before the spring and four people at that table have noticed the coat and not the man.

A woman of about thirty-four said, later, that the man of about thirty-four had sat in that chair for five Wednesdays running because from it you can see the back of the ninth chair, and that he had not given a reason and that nobody had ever asked him for one, and that on this Wednesday he was not there to be asked.

**The man of about thirty-four has not given a reason for that either. He has said that he was on a landing until ten to seven and that nine minutes is not long, and that the room was in his head for four days before that and was not in it on the stair.**

---

**And about nine people were in that room over that hour and about four of them were at the table, and nobody put a sheet on that table and nobody mentioned the practice and nobody mentioned the ninth street.**

A woman of about forty-eight, who keeps the register at a counter in a second district, came up that evening for the second time and was not asked anything and said nothing, and has said since that she came because she wanted to see whether the room still operated the way it does and that it does.

A man of about thirty-eight asked her on the stair, in nine seconds, whether the district had decided anything, and she said that a district does not decide things in a room like this one, and that he had said the opposite thing to about nine people in a fortnight and that she was not going to argue about it on a stair.

Nobody in that room mentioned what the count was for. Nobody in that room has ever asked.

---

**And for eleven minutes of that hour the woman of about sixty did the thing she does every Wednesday, and nobody in that room watched her do it and about four of them knew it was happening.**

She put the date at the top of a page. She wrote what came in and she wrote what went out and she ruled a line underneath. Nothing was on that page about a district, a release line, a gate, a yard, or four hundred people, and there has never been anything on that page about any of those things in nineteen years.

A woman of about twenty-nine looked at the page from where she was sitting and said in nine seconds that she had been in that room twice before the spring and both times about a bill and both times had seen that page being written.

She said that she had assumed for four years that it was a list of people who owed something. Nobody in that room told her that it was not and about two of them have said since that it never was, and about two have said that nineteen years of a page nobody can read is not a mistake, it is a wall.

**And the man of about fifty-three said one thing in that hour, and he said it to the door on his way out and not to anybody, and four people at that table heard it, and two of them have said what it was.**

He said, standing up, that there is a word people use for what is being done to that district and that the word is not the same as the word for what is being done to it. Nobody asked him for either word and he has not been in that room since and nobody has asked him for them.

About two people in this city know both words and about four know one of them, and neither set has offered either to anybody who would write it down.

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about half an hour on a folding table in a fourth district, where one leg had been screwed to the top with a plate that had been bent flat to reach.**

The leg had been loose for four years and the plate on the underside had been bent about an eighth of an inch to reach the rail, which means the leg had been loose when the plate was fitted.

He turned the table over, unbent the plate with two lengths of wood and a vice, drilled it square in eleven minutes, and refitted it. Seventeen pounds and half an hour.

The man said the leg had been wobbly since he bought the table and he said that this is the first time anybody has told him that a bent plate came before a loose leg, and that he has been blaming his own use of it for four years.

He charged for the plate and the drilling and not for the leg, and told him that a table with one leg out of square stands on three and a bit, and that a bit is where the wobble comes from, and that about eleven other tables in that building are the same and he has not looked at them.

---

**And the table had a second leg on it that was loose in exactly the same way, and he did the same thing to it, and the reason he did it was that doing one and not the other would have left a table with a leg that had been fixed for the wrong reason.**

He told the man that he could charge him for one leg or for two and that two would be the same money as one because the plate is the part that is bought, and that a man who has had one leg done properly will assume the other three are fine and will not have them looked at.

The man said he had had eleven of those tables in that room and had never thought about any of them. He said that he had thought about them, and that thinking about a table is not the same as measuring it, and that a room of eleven tables of one kind will all be wrong in the same way, and that this is true of about nine other things in that building.

He charged seventeen and said that the other three legs of that table were sound and would stay sound for years, and that the fourth will go in two years, and that he had written the date on the invoice so that whoever comes then can tell whether anybody looked.

**And the second job that Wednesday was a lectern in the same room, whose hinge had been let down so far by four years of leaning that the top would not stay open.**

He took the hinge off the frame, packed it out with two thin washers, and drilled one new hole into sound wood higher up, and refitted the hinge so that the top will hold anywhere between flat and vertical.

Thirty-one pounds and forty minutes. The man said that he had been holding the lectern with his knee for four years and that a person adapts to a lectern that will not stay open and that adapting is not the same as agreeing.

He said that this is the sixth thing this month where a person has been the part of the machine, and that he had started writing them down on the invoice for that reason and that about four other men in that building have started doing the same.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six with her door shut, and behind her the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf for two hundred and forty-two days and has not been read.**
