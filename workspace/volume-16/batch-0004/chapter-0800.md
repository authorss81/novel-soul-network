# Chapter 800 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Opened On A Wednesday Hour By The Woman Who Holds It Where About Nine People Came Up And About Four Stayed, And A Corridor In That District Where A Second Sheet Went Up Beside The First And Neither Was Taken Down

**A Monday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in two places at once: a first floor where a woman opened her own room in her own time, and a corridor where four words went on a wall next to a notice that had been on it since the spring. Neither sheet came down. Nobody has said which of them is the notice.**

---

The board for that Monday carried fourteen names and fourteen dates, and the fourteenth name was entered at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Monday, at the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**That card in a rail that takes two is one thousand four hundred and sixty-seven days, two hundred and nine weeks and four days. Line fifteen is one thousand two hundred and seventy-eight days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks and four days.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand two hundred and thirty-five days, a hundred and seventy-six weeks and three days. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and eleven days, a hundred and forty-four weeks and three days.**

---

**The first of the two places was a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, and it was a Monday, and the hour belongs to her, and that is the only reason the door was open.**

She said on the Friday that the room is hers and not theirs, and she said it with a condition attached, and on the Monday she opened it. Not for them. Because she wanted to know what about four hundred people do when there is a room and about nine people come.

About nine people went up that stair between half past six and half past seven and about four of them stayed the hour. Two of the four said nothing at all. One of the other two asked whether a person could bring their children, and was told that the stair is the only thing on the way up that is difficult.

**A woman of about thirty-four stood at the wall end of that room and said, in nine seconds, that this is the first evidence anybody has had of anything, and that what it is evidence of is that a room works when it is a room and not a notice.**

Nobody said she was wrong. Two of them have said since that this is the only finding of that week that was worth having. Two others have said that four people coming up a stair is not four hundred people, and that this is the whole difficulty in one sentence.

---

**And the second of the two places was a corridor in that second district, and there is already a sheet on that wall, and it has been on it since a Monday in the spring.**

It is a printed notice, held by two drawing pins, and the two pins are crooked a quarter of an inch in opposite directions and have been since before the spring, and about nine people a week walk past it and about four of them read it and one of those read it upside down from nine feet away on the floor.

A woman of about thirty-four said in four seconds that the new sheet goes beside it, and not over it, and that nobody was going to take a notice down that has been up since the spring.

Nobody argued with her. A man of about thirty-eight said in nine seconds, standing in front of the wall, that two sheets on one wall is a thing a person can be told twice by, and that a person told twice is a person who decides which of the two to believe, and that deciding is the part that goes wrong on a Tuesday.

**He said it about the wall and not about anybody who had put anything on it. Nobody in that corridor has suggested taking either sheet down and about two people have said that somebody will, in four weeks, and about two have said that this is the kind of thing nobody volunteers for.**

---

**And the new sheet went up on the Monday at about eleven, beside the other one, with two drawing pins of its own, and it carries four words and a date.**

*It ends on Monday.*

Nobody put a heading on it. Nobody put a name on it. Nobody put anybody's name on it, and four people in that district have said that a sheet with no name on it cannot be complained about, and four have said that is exactly why it will be complained about.

A woman of about twenty-nine came past at about ten past eleven and read both sheets, in that order, and stopped for nine seconds at the second one.

She said, in nine seconds, that she could say the four words, and that she had said them to about nine people over the previous week without anybody asking her to, and that she has never been asked where she learned them and has said where she learned them to nobody.

**Nobody has taken either sheet down. Four people in that district have read both and nine have read one and the rest of them walked past. Nobody has counted any of that. Two of them have said that counting it would be the first sensible thing anybody does, and two have said it would be the last.**

---

**And the date at the foot of the district's own notice is one week off, and it has not moved, and nobody has asked about it, and that sheet is still on that wall with its two crooked pins in it.**

The date at the top of that notice came and went on a Tuesday in the spring of this year and the district did not go and nobody wrote down what that meant. The date at the foot of it was added afterwards in the district's own hand and nobody has asked about it since it was added.

A man of about thirty-four said, in nine seconds, that it is a week, and that nobody in that corridor is going to do anything with a week, and that a person standing in a corridor on a Monday reading a date a week away has time to do nothing about it and that this is the correct amount of time.

Nobody in that corridor has done anything about it.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that corridor for nine minutes on that Monday and was four feet from the wall for nine minutes of it and was not asked to pin anything up.**

He put the pins in. He was asked to put the pins in by a woman of about thirty-four and he did it and it took four minutes and he was not thanked.

He has said since that he would have put it up whatever had been said, and that the reason he is not the person the sheet is put up to is not a rule and is not modesty, and is that a man who pins up a notice in a district about nine hundred people know by sight will be that district's man for about four hundred days whether he wanted it or not.

**Nobody thanked him. A woman of about thirty-four said the four seconds' worth of thing about it being the ninth chair all over again with a drawing pin and he said no in four seconds, and the reason he gave was about a chair and not about a notice.**

---

**And about four of the nine who came up that stair stayed that hour, and two of the four asked the same question in two different words, and neither of the two questions was about the practice.**

The first was whether a person could come on a Wednesday and find the room shut, and whether anything would say so in advance. The second was whether a person could bring somebody with them who does not want to come.

The woman of about sixty answered the first in nine seconds and said that the room is hers and that a person who wants to know whether it is open should come and see, and that about nine people in this city have asked her a version of this and about nine have not come afterwards.

**She answered the second by saying nothing for nine seconds, and then saying that she would rather that had been asked on Friday by somebody who had thought about it, and that nobody had thought about it.**

Nobody in that room spoke about the second question again. About two people who came up that evening have said since that the silence on it was the most informative thing said in that room that Monday, and about two have said that it was a woman declining to invent an answer.

**And at about eleven that morning, before any of this, a second sheet went onto a corridor wall nine feet from a man who was on the floor with a door closer nine weeks ago, and he was not in that corridor and has not been told by anybody that it happened.**

A man of about thirty-four put four drawing pins into a wall at about eleven and it took him four minutes and the pins went in first time, and he has said that this is the only piece of work he has done this year that went in first time and that he has told nobody else that.

Four people in that district have since said that the second sheet is crooked the same way as the first, and that this is a coincidence, and it has been repeated so often that two of them now believe it is not.

---

**The rest of that Monday was a counter until about six and about three quarters of an hour on a gate latch in a fourth district, where a padlock hasp had been fitted to a gate that opens inwards and has been worked loose over two years.**

The gate opens inwards and the hasp had been put on the wrong side, so the whole weight of the gate came down on the pin every time it was shut, and two years of that will work a hasp off a staple in nine weeks.

He took the hasp off, moved it to the other side, drilled two new holes about an inch along and refitted it. Twenty-two pounds and about three quarters of an hour.

The man said the hasp had been fitted by a man who came in two years ago and that the man had said it would last twenty years, and he said that it had lasted about two, and that the twenty years was true if the gate had opened outwards.

The man said the gate could not open outwards because of a wall two feet from it, and he said that a wall two feet from a gate is a thing a person finds out on the day and that about four people in this city find out things like that on the day and not before.

---

**And the gate that hasp was moved to the other side on that Monday, and the gate has been opened and shut nine times since, and there is now a mark on the ground where the pin went through the paving for two years.**

He drew the new hole position in the paving with a nail and took the gate off its hinges to do it, and the mark is a dark spot a quarter of an inch across and it is the only evidence in that yard that anything about that gate has ever changed.

He told the man to keep an eye on the mark rather than on the hasp, because a mark that gets bigger is a hole and a hole that gets bigger is the paving, and about nine hasps in this city are on gates that are moving because nobody looked at the ground.

**And the second job that Monday was a hoop and staple on a yard gate in a third district which was not broken but which was on the same gate and had been used to lift the same gate for four years.**

The hoop lifts a gate and the staple is what it goes into, and the staple had been set in a soft repair of eleven years ago and had leaned out by a quarter of an inch, which means the gate had to be lifted higher and further out every year to find it.

He reset the staple in the stone, set it plumb, and told the man that a gate which has been lifted four years without being noticed gets a quarter of an inch further away every winter, and that about four gates in that district have it and none of them has been measured.

Twenty-two pounds was for the hasp and this was twenty-six, and he told the man the order, and the man said that a man who comes once a year writes both of them on the same invoice and that this is the first time anybody has done that in four years.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six with her door shut, and on the back shelf behind her the eighth of eight has been sitting for two hundred and forty-seven days and has not been read.**
