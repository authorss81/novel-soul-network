# Chapter 791 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where Nine People Spent Two Hours And A Half On One Sentence And Put Four Words On A Sheet Instead

**A Tuesday opened at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district, where nine people spent two hours and a half on a single sentence about a thing nobody there can put on paper. The shutter came down at ten, as it comes down at ten on every day of this stretch of days.**

---

The board for that Tuesday carried eleven names and eleven dates, and the eleventh name was entered at twenty to five. The street outside that shop is quiet after about four in the afternoon for a reason nobody has ever measured.

**The four rooms off that service road and the one standing behind the other three are at one thousand four hundred and fifty days, two hundred and seven weeks and one day.**

**Line sixteen is one thousand two hundred and forty days, a hundred and seventy-seven weeks and one day. What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and forty days, a hundred and sixty-two weeks and six days.**

Eight hundred and thirty days is the separation, a hundred and eighteen weeks and four days, and it is in a one-line box sixteen months old, and it is the one figure in that paragraph with nothing to do with any of this.

---

**What the work of that movement was, in one sentence, is that somebody has to be told in advance what they are leaving, and nobody had yet worked out who.**

A district has given notice. When the district goes, what it takes with it goes with it, and what it takes is the holding of a release line at a yard. Four hundred people across three districts and half a bus route put a hand on that line nine times a day and have never once known whose hand it was.

Nobody in that room on that Tuesday had said the second half of that out loud before. A man of about thirty-four said it, in nine seconds, and then said that the first half was the easy half and that about four hundred people were about to find out what they were losing on a Tuesday, by turning up.

"**Telling them is not the hard part,**" he said. "**Not knowing what to tell them is the hard part, and we cannot tell them what we cannot say.**"

---

**So they started to write the sentence that says what the practice is, and that is where two hours and a half went.**

They wrote it eleven times on the back of a docket sheet and every one of the eleven was wrong in a different place, and the reason was not grammar. The reason was that the practice is not a sentence.

It is a man. It is a dial. It is eleven seconds. It is a right hand that has not been steady since a night a year ago, and there is no file on that, and nobody in that room had any business writing that down and none of them did.

A man of about thirty-four said the whole of the difficulty in nine seconds, and nobody disagreed with him, and two people in that room have said since that he was describing his own job and did not know it.

"**You cannot put that on a sheet,**" he said. "**A sheet can carry a day. A sheet can carry a street. A sheet cannot carry a man and a dial and eleven seconds and a hand that is not what it was. If you write it down you are writing down a quarter of it and the quarter is the part that will be argued with.**"

A man of about thirty-eight said that a person would read the quarter and believe it, and the man of about thirty-four said yes, and that is what a person does with a quarter, and that this was the argument they were already going to have on a Tuesday and were having it on a Tuesday in a room with the door shut.

---

**And then somebody asked the question nobody had asked, which was who was going to be told, and the room found out that it did not know either.**

There is no list of about four hundred people. There has never been one. A register with nine names in it hangs on a clipboard by that gate and is a record of a door being open on a Monday.

A woman of about thirty-eight said, in nine seconds, that this was not an accident and was not anybody's doing, and that a district is not four hundred people, it is a set of shifts and a gate and about nine people who have been asked to speak for it, and that the four hundred have not been asked anything.

Nobody in that room produced a name. Nobody has been asked to produce one.

**A man of about thirty-eight said that the four hundred were not missing, they were in rooms, and that a person who is in a room at six in the morning and on a bus at seven is not a gap in a record, he is a person who was not standing still while somebody was writing.**

---

**And a woman of about forty-eight, who keeps the register at the counter in that building, said the thing that turned the evening, and she said it in nine seconds and she did not raise her voice.**

She has kept that register for nine years. It is a hard-backed day book about four hundred yards long and it holds names and times and nothing else, and it has never once been asked to hold anything but a person arriving.

"**The sheet does not have to say what it is,**" she said. "**It has to say when it stops. That is all anybody can be given, and it is enough, and I will tell you why it is enough. You can be angry at a day. You cannot be angry at a man. If you put the man on the sheet you have given four hundred people something to be angry at instead of something to plan around.**"

She said the second half of that to the table and not to anybody at it.

"**And a person can be told a date in four words. Four words go in an ear and stay there. A paragraph goes in a person and comes out as an argument about a hand.**"

---

**And then somebody wrote four words on the back of a sheet, and it took four seconds, and the four words were a date with a verb on it.**

*It ends on Monday.*

Nobody changed a letter of it. A man of about thirty-eight asked whether it should say why, and the woman of about forty-eight said no in four seconds, and said that a reason on that sheet would be a reason four hundred people would all have to agree with, and that nobody in that room is going to be able to agree about it in a fortnight.

**That is the first sentence anybody has written in this city that four hundred people could be told, and it is four words long, and it says a day and not a thing.**

It has no date on it yet. That is a separate piece of work and it took another four minutes and it was done by a man of about thirty-four with a pen, and it is the day at the foot of that district's own notice.

Two people in that room have said since that the four words are too few and that this is the first thing about them that is true.

---

**And then the room tried to do the only other thing that had to be done that day, which was to say where four hundred people would go to read it, and it took twenty minutes and produced nothing.**

There is a board in a corridor in a second district with a printed sheet on it held by two drawing pins. About nine people walk past it between eleven and two on a Monday and about four of them read it, and one of those four read it upside down on the floor nine feet away.

A man of about thirty-four said that a corridor is not a place four hundred people go, and a woman of about thirty-four said that it is the only place anybody has ever put anything in that district, and that the answer being no is not a reason not to put it there.

**Nobody in that room could name a hall, a church, a school room, a pub back room, or a bus shelter in that district where four hundred people would go and stand. Four people tried for twenty minutes and every one of the four named a building that holds nine people.**

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for two hours and said nothing and was asked nothing, and has said since that he had the sentence ready at about twenty past six and did not say it.**

He has said that the sentence he had was worse, because it had a clause in it about what would still work, and that no person in that room had said what would still work, and that inventing that clause on a Tuesday in a room is how about four hundred people end up being told something that is nearly true.

He also said that he wanted to be asked what the date was and was not asked, and that this is the third week running he has not been asked.

**And then somebody in that room read the four words out loud, twice, and both times it went wrong, and what happened next is the reason the sentence is four words long and not forty.**

A man of about thirty-four read it. He read it the way a man reads a thing off a card in a shop, which is how he said he had read notices for eleven years, and nobody in that room said anything for nine seconds.

"**It ends on Monday,**" he said.

He said it twice. On the second reading he got a word wrong and stopped and started that word again, and two people at the table looked at each other, and one of them said that this is what it sounds like.

**The man of about thirty-four said, in four seconds, that this was the finding and that he had come in with a sheet and had not expected to leave with a demonstration. He said that four words is under the number of words a person can carry from a gate to a kitchen, and that four words in a pocket is four words in a pocket, and that four words in a mouth is a different object entirely.**

Nobody in that room contradicted him and nobody moved to make it easier. A woman of about thirty-eight said that a person who has to get a thing word for word in a gateway is going to stand in the gateway until they have said it, and that this costs a morning of somebody's week, and that a morning is a real thing.

**Then she said something that changed the shape of the rest of that week, and she said it to the sheet and not to anybody. She said that a sentence a person can get wrong in a gateway is a sentence that will get wrong four times in nine, and that every one of those four is a person who tells a household the wrong date and then is told the household had it correct.**

That is the whole reason the four words went out of that room on a Monday and not in that room on a Tuesday, and nobody in that room had known it before she said it.

---

**And the rest of that Tuesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and fifty minutes on the letterbox flap of a first floor in a third district, which had been packing out for four years.**

The flap had a return on it and the return had been packed with grease by two different men, four years apart, and a packed return is a flap that will not fall shut and a flap that will not fall shut gets packed again.

He took the flap off, took the packing out with a chisel and a cloth, and found that the hinge pin had been a quarter of an inch oversize for four years, which is why the flap had wanted packing in the first place. He put a washer behind the pin, refitted the flap and did nothing else to it. Fourteen pounds and fifty minutes.

The woman said she had had two men round about it and one of them had told her it was the cold, and he said that cold does not push a flap open, that the flap was held by a quarter of an inch of metal in the wrong place, and that a man who packs grease onto a moving hinge is buying eleven weeks.

The woman said she had been told eleven weeks before and had not known what it meant. He said that nobody knows what it means and that it is four years long, and that the only person who knew was the man who packed it.

---

**And the door that flap is on is out of square, and he found that after he had charged for the flap and not before, and he told her so on the way out rather than going back for his tools.**

A door frame carries four pounds of the door's weight on its top hinge and the rest on the jamb below, and if the frame is out of square the top hinge takes the whole of it and the door will be heavy at the top and light at the bottom.

He put a steel rule against the jamb at the top and at the bottom and read it twice and said the difference was a sixteenth of an inch. The woman said the man before had told her the door was the problem and he said that a man who does not measure the frame cannot tell, and that he has met that man twice before this week.

She asked what a sixteenth of an inch looks like to a person standing in a kitchen. He said it looks like nothing, and that it is why nobody believes anybody, and that a person is not wrong to think a door is heavy when a door is heavy.

**He wrote the two readings on the back of her own invoice with the dates on them and told her to keep the invoice, and she said the last invoice had gone in a bin in about a fortnight, and he said that is the eleventh door in this city this year that has been measured and written down and thrown away.**

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six with her door shut, and on the back shelf behind her is the eighth of eight, and it has been there two hundred and thirty-nine days, and it has not been read.**
