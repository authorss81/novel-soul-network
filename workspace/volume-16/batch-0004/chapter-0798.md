# Chapter 798 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where It Was Established That Somebody Would Have To Say A Date Out Loud To Whoever Came In, And Where Nobody Agreed To Any Of It And About Nine People Could Say It By Six O'Clock

**A Thursday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district, where a question was asked about a person and the answer was that the person did not matter. Nobody agreed to anything before the end of the day. By about six o'clock several people in that district could say four words without being asked.**

---

The board for that Thursday carried eleven names and eleven dates, and the last of them at twenty to five. Ten o'clock, and the shutter went down then as it goes down then on every day of this stretch of days.

**The four rooms off that service road and the one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and fifty-nine days, two hundred and eight weeks and three days.**

**Line seventeen is one thousand two hundred and thirty-one days, a hundred and seventy-five weeks and six days. The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand and twenty-five days, a hundred and forty-six weeks and three days, one corner torn, eight unfinished, and not two of them have been laid against each other.**

**Eight hundred and thirty-nine days is the separation, a hundred and nineteen weeks and six days, in a one-line box sixteen months old.**

---

**The job on that Thursday was to get one person to stand in a room and say a date out loud to whoever came through the door, and nobody had been asked and nobody had volunteered.**

There is a sheet with three lines on it and about nine people going to carry it on the Monday. There is a first floor above a line in Saltmarket with an hour of a Wednesday on it. There is a drawing of nine streets on the back of a docket sheet with no names on it.

None of those things is a person saying a thing to a face, and two of them said on the Tuesday that a piece of paper is not told to anybody.

A man of about thirty-four said, in nine seconds, that a sheet in a corridor is read by four people a week and a person at a gate is heard by nine a morning, and that this is the only part of the whole of it that does any work at all.

---

**And then a man of about thirty-eight said the thing that nearly stopped it, and it was about himself, and he said it in nine seconds and did not go quiet afterwards.**

"**I will say it,**" he said. "**I want that on the table before anything else is said, because I have been the man at the gate for a fortnight and four hundred people know my face in that district. When I say a date at that gate, somebody who does not agree with me is going to say that I am the district.**"

He said that he was not refusing and that refusing was not the word for it either.

"**A man can be a person who says a thing and a person can be the thing that a thing is about, and those are different and after four hundred people have heard my voice say a date they will not be different any more.**"

---

**And four people at that table told him it did not have to be him, and he said he knew, and that knowing it did not have to be him is not a reason for him to be relieved of it.**

Nobody in that room argued him out of it and nobody argued him into it. He said he would do it on the Monday. One of them has said since that he should not have said so in a room, and another has said that refusing in a room would have been worse.

**A woman of about thirty-eight said, in nine seconds, that a refusal in a room is a thing four hundred people never hear, and that a refusal at a gate on a Monday morning is a thing about nine people hear at once, and that the second of those is not less of a refusal and is more of one.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four said the thing that turned that Thursday, and it took her nine seconds and she said it to the table.**

"**Four words,**" she said. "**That is the whole thing. Four words and a date. Anybody in this city can say four words. I can say four words. The man who sweeps that yard at six can say four words. Nobody in this room is going to be known for saying them and the thing does not need anybody to be known for it.**"

A man of about thirty-four said that a thing anybody can say is a thing nobody will say, and she said that a thing nobody will say is what they have now, and that a thing about nine people say badly is a thing four hundred people can hear.

**Nobody argued with that. Nobody in that room had anywhere to argue it to.**

---

**And by six o'clock that evening nine people in that district could say four words, and nobody at that table asked any of the nine and nobody agreed to any of it.**

It happened in shops and at gates and on a bus and in a kitchen in a third district. The four words and the date. It is a very small thing and it is very easy to get slightly wrong, and about four of the nine got it slightly wrong and about four of those four corrected themselves before they had finished saying it.

A woman of about twenty-nine said it twice in one afternoon to about four people each time and was not asked to and said afterwards that she had said it to nine people and that two of the nine had asked her who she was.

**Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody organised any of the nine and nobody knew until the Friday who any of them were, and about two of the nine did not want to be found out and about seven of them did not mind.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-four wrote the four words and the date on the back of a docket sheet at half past five and did not give it to anybody, and that is the state of it at the end of that Thursday.**

He did not put it in the docket book on the shelf, because the drawing is in that book and he does not want the drawing read as a pair with a sentence, and he has said so in about nine words to a woman of about thirty-four and she has not repeated it to anybody.

He put it in the inside of the docket book instead, at half past five, and he says it will stay there until somebody asks him for it, and nobody has.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about an hour and a half and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he had offered to say the four words on the Monday and was not asked again.**

He has said since that he did not mind, and that the reason he is not the person is not humility and is not a rule, and that a man who stands at a gate in a district about four hundred people know is a man who will be asked about the district for the rest of his life, and that this is a cost and not a fault.

He has also said that he wrote down what he would have said and that it is the same four words, and that the four words are the same whoever says them, and that this was the whole of the argument and it took nine seconds and somebody else made it.

---

**And four of the people in that room on that Thursday said the four words to somebody who was not in the room, and not one of the four knew that any of the other three had said them.**

A woman of about thirty-four said them to a woman at the next counter. A man of about thirty-one said them to the man who books his van. A man of about fifty-two said them to a woman who was locking a gate at ten to seven and who did not stop walking. A man of about thirty-eight said them to his brother, who asked him what had happened to the yard.

Nobody organised it and nobody told anybody to and nobody in that room had any idea it had happened until the following Friday, when a woman of about twenty-nine said that nine people in that district could say it, which is the first number anybody had for it.

**The woman of about thirty-four has said since that this is the first thing in eleven years that has spread without anybody being a person who spread it, and that she has not decided whether that is the point of a sentence or an accident of a Thursday.**

A man of about thirty-eight said that a sentence that spreads by accident will stop spreading the moment somebody stops saying it, and that nothing is going to check it, and that this is the first thing in this stretch of days that nobody in this city is able to check.

Nobody answered him. One of them has said since that a checked sentence is a written sentence, and that the four words are the first ones anybody has here that cannot be checked, and another has said that this is the best argument anyone has made for them.

---

**The rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and about half an hour on a drinking fountain in a first district, where the push valve had been packed and had been packed twice before that.**

A drinking fountain push valve is a thing about nine people a day put a hand on and about nine hundred people a week look at and almost none of them touch.

The valve had a washer behind it that had gone soft and had been packed with grease to make it stop weeping, and grease behind a washer stops the weep for eleven weeks and then the washer is worse than it was.

He took the whole top off, took the washer out, cleaned the seat, and put a new one in, and told the man that the weeping was never the fault and that the fault was a washer four years old.

Eleven pounds and half an hour. The man said the last man had done exactly this and he said that four years ago a person could have done exactly this, and that the difference is that this time nobody has packed anything behind it, and that about two winters is what a washer gets.

---

**And the fountain had a second fault on it, which was the bowl, and which had been the reason two people in that building had stopped using it without saying so to anybody.**

The bowl was a stainless bowl set into a surround, and it had a fall that went the wrong way on one side, so that a third of what came out of the valve ran down the outside of the bowl and into the top of the surround.

He took the bowl out, found it had been packed under its far edge with a piece of card to hold it up, and packed in four years ago by somebody who had not wanted to remove it, and took the card out and re-levelled the bowl.

Eleven pounds was for the valve and the washer and this was nineteen, and he told the man the order so that the man could tell somebody which job was which.

**He said that about four faults in this city are a repair that was correct at the time and has become the fault, and that a card under a bowl is one of them, and that the person who put it there was solving a real problem on a different day.**

And the second job that Thursday was a urn tap in a canteen in a second district, which was weeping and had been weeping for two years and had been caught in a bowl on a trolley by whoever was nearest.

A weeping tap does not drip, it weeps from around the spindle, and a bowl on a trolley catches it and hides it and gets emptied by whoever notices. He took the head off, found the gland nut had been wound down hard by somebody in a hurry, and packed and refitted it.

Eight pounds and twenty minutes. He told the man to put a date on the bowl when it was next emptied, and that about four canteens in that district have bowls on trolleys for the same reason and that none of them has a date on anything.

---
