# Chapter 799 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where A Man Who Had Four Words Written Down Was Told In Four Seconds That They Had Never Been His To Hold, And Where A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Then Asked The Question Nobody Else Had Asked

**A Friday ran from a bench to a counter and into a room off a road in a second district, where four words that had been written down were handed out to about nine people in eleven minutes. Nobody thanked the man who wrote them. At the end of it a woman asked what the four words were about, and nobody in that room had an answer written down.**

---

The board for that Friday carried thirteen names and thirteen dates, and the thirteenth name was entered at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten that evening, at the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**The four rooms off that service road and one behind the other three are at one thousand four hundred and sixty days, two hundred and eight weeks and four days, and the card on end in the rail by that door is one thousand four hundred and sixty-four days, two hundred and nine weeks and one day.**

**Line eighteen is one thousand one hundred and seventy-eight days, one hundred and sixty-eight weeks and two days. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and sixty-six days, one hundred and fifty-two weeks and two days.**

---

**What was done on that Friday, and it took eleven minutes, was that four words on the back of a sheet were given to about nine people, and it was not done by the man who had written them.**

He had said on the Thursday that he was not giving them away. On the Friday morning he said it again in four seconds, and he gave the whole of his reason inside the four seconds and nobody interrupted him.

"**If I give this away it stops being mine,**" he said. "**And a sentence that belongs to nobody is a sentence nobody will say. I have watched that happen twice in four years in this city and both times it was because somebody gave it away.**"

He said it to the table and not to anybody in it, and then he said that he had been carrying it for a day and a half and that this was longer than he had expected to carry a piece of paper.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four answered him in nine seconds, and what she said was about the Tuesday, and about how many people were in the room when it was said.**

"**It was never yours,**" she said. "**It came out of a room of nine people on a Tuesday evening and it came out of a table and not out of you. You wrote it down. That is the whole of what you did and it is a smaller thing than carrying it.**"

He said that she was wrong about the room and she said that he had said about four words on the Tuesday and not the four words, and that the room had said nine people and not one.

Nobody argued with either of them. **Two of them have said since that she was correct and two have said that being the only person in a city who has the words is the only reason anybody has heard them, and nobody at that table has said both things out loud.**

---

**And he gave it away on the Friday, in eleven minutes, and he did it in a way that cost him the thing he said he was protecting.**

He wrote the four words and the date on nine pieces of the same size and gave eight of them away and kept the ninth. The ninth is the sheet from Thursday and it went back into the inside of the docket book at half past five.

He kept it and he has not given it to anybody and nobody has asked him for it, and two of them have said that keeping one is not holding on to them.

**He was not thanked and he did not ask to be. He said, in four seconds, that thanking him would make it his, and that this was the same argument he had lost on the Thursday and that he had worked out about half of it since.**

---

**And then a woman of about twenty-nine said the thing that nobody else in that room had thought of, and she said it in nine seconds, and it was about the content of the words rather than about who holds them.**

"**They say when,**" she said. "**They do not say what. Four hundred people are going to ask what it is that ends, and there is no answer written down anywhere in this city, and everybody in this room has been pleased for a week about how little of it there is.**"

Nobody in that room said she had it wrong. A man of about thirty-four said that the four words had been written on the Tuesday specifically so that nobody would have to agree about what it is, and she said that she had not been there on the Tuesday, and that a person who is not in a room does not get what the room decided.

**That is the first time anybody in this city has said that the four words were decided by about nine people and not by the district, and two have said since that she is the first person to say the true thing about it and two have said she is being hard on a Tuesday.**

Nobody wrote her sentence down. It was said at half past five and it is not on any paper, and one of them has said since that this is a habit and not an oversight.

---

**Nobody at that table answered her, and the four words went out to about nine people that afternoon without one, and the sheet in the docket book stayed where it was.**

A man of about fifty-two said that this was what a sentence does when it is only a day, and that a day can be repeated without anybody knowing what it is about, and that a Tuesday can be argued about only by people who were in the room.

The woman of about twenty-nine said that she was not going to be argued with about it and that she had said it once and would say it again in nine days and not before.

---

**A man of about twenty-two stood at the end of that table for forty minutes, said nothing, and was asked nothing, and has said since that the sheet in the docket book is now two things and he has been thinking about that for a day and a half.**

He said that a drawing of nine streets and four words are two different jobs and that a person who puts them in the same book will, in four years, read them as one thing and be wrong about both.

He has said that he has not asked anybody to move either of them and that he does not know whether he is protecting the words or protecting himself from having been in the room where they were written.

---

**And the ninth of the nine pieces was compared against the other eight by four people at that table, and it is not the same as the other eight, and nobody has done anything about it.**

The eighth pieces were cut on Thursday and the ninth was the one he wrote on Thursday as well, and the date on it is in a slightly different hand, because he wrote it first and the other eight afterwards and his hand does not repeat itself.

A woman of about thirty-eight found it and said in nine seconds that the date on that sheet is the date in a different shape, and that anybody reading the ninth will read it faster, and that this is a difference of a second per person who reads it.

**Nobody agreed to change it. A man of about thirty-four said in four seconds that a sentence written twice in the same hand by the same man on the same evening is a different sentence, and that this is a thing he would rather have found out now.**

Two of them have said since that there are now three kinds of that sentence, because the eight went out and were repeated from memory, and the ninth is in a book, and the eight that went out are not the eight anybody now says.

**And nobody in that room could produce a list of the about nine people who have it, and nobody has asked for one, and a woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that this is now the second thing in a fortnight that has spread past the point where anybody knows where it is.**

A man of about fifty-two said that the first thing was the sentence and the second thing is the sentence, and that this is a thing about four people do not have, and that it is not the four hundred, and that this is the difficulty and it is not the same difficulty as the first one.

---

**The rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and about an hour on a rolling shutter in a second district, where the bottom rail had been replaced twice and had been put on the wrong way round both times.**

The bottom rail of a rolling shutter carries the weight of the curtain and it drops into a channel. It had been fitted with the channel on the outside, and a channel on the outside puts a seal on the wrong face of the curtain, and nine years of wind has pushed it out of true.

He took the rail out, turned it over, refitted it, and shimmed the guides about an eighth of an inch. Forty-four pounds and about an hour.

The man said two men had done this and he said that a rail fitted upside down is the same rail fitted properly for two years, and that the two years is how long a curtain takes to tell you, and that four people in that building have each paid for that lesson in turn.

He charged forty-four and told the man to write the date on the back of his own invoice, and the man said he had three of those already and could not tell what any of them said, and he said that a shop that cannot tell what a note says is a shop that will pay again in two years.

---

**And the curtain on that shutter had a slat in it that was cracked, and it had been cracked since before the spring, and four people in that building have been closing the shutter by hand to avoid it.**

A curtain slat is a piece of profiled steel three inches deep, and a crack across the bottom of one of them spreads along the profile and then along the curtain, and the curtain will still run and will rattle differently on the way down.

He took the curtain off the bottom rail, took the one cracked slat out of its rivets, and put a new one in, and told the man that a curtain with one cracked slat is a curtain that is four months from being four cracked slats, and that he had found this one before the spread rather than after.

Forty-four pounds was for the rail and the guides and this was twenty-six, and he told the man the order because a man who has paid for the rail and not the curtain will assume the curtain is new.

The man said he had been closing that shutter by hand for four months and had told three people it was the wind. He said that the wind is why he heard it, and that a crack in a slat is not a thing the wind makes, and that about four faults in that street were put down to the wind this year and two of them were metal.

He wrote both dates on the back of the man's own invoice and said that a shutter curtain is replaced every eleven years or every four years depending, and that the depending is the whole of the trade, and that no sheet in any room in this city says which.

**And the second job that Friday was a chain on a roller door in the same yard, which had been a link light for a year and had been held on with a length of wire.**

He took the wire off, found the light link, and replaced it with a proper joining link, and told the man that a wire on a chain is a repair that tells the next person the door is fine, and that it tells them nothing at all about the one link that is stretched.

Nineteen pounds and forty minutes. The man said the wire had been put on by a man who came in quickly, and he said that a man who comes in quickly will use what is in the van, and that four people in that district have been the wire.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six with her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and forty-four days.**
