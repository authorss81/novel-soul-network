# Chapter 812 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Second Floor In A Building Off A Road Where A Second Attempt At One Sentence Was Read Aloud By Four People And Was Found By All Four Of Them To Be The Same Sentence With Different Furniture On It

**A Wednesday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished on a second floor in a building off a road, where a second attempt at one sentence was read aloud four times to nine people. Nothing was decided on that Wednesday and the sheet was still on the desk when the lights went on.**

---

That Wednesday's board took eleven names and eleven dates, the last name at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Wednesday, and ten is the hour it comes down at on all ten of these days.

**Line eighteen is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven days, a hundred and seventy-one weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and eighty-five days, a hundred and fifty-five weeks to the day. What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and sixty-nine days, a hundred and sixty-seven weeks to the day.**

---

**The sentence had been rewritten twice since the Monday and both times it had come back longer, and the reason both times was that the writer had tried to put a door count and a date count and a floor count in the same breath and the three of them would not sit still.**

The sentence ran to four lines. It said that the body was given things and it passed them on, and that in a period it had received a number and had passed on a number, and that the two numbers were not the same and that the difference between them was not written down anywhere.

A woman of about thirty-four read it and said in nine seconds that it was true, and that it was also the first half of an inventory, and that an inventory is a thing you read when you already know what the thing is.

A man of about thirty-eight read it and said that a person handed that on a Monday morning is going to work out in four seconds that it is a list of doors, and that the doors are the only number in it anybody will remember, and that a list of doors has been the thing on a wall in that district since before the spring.

**He said that four people have been asking in this city since the spring what happens to a sheet that has been out of a building once, and that four questions have been answered twice and two ways, and that this sentence would be the fifth question and would make it five.**

---

**And a man of about fifty-two read it twice and then said the thing that made the room go quiet for four seconds, and he said it in nine seconds.**

He said that a body which is described by what it has received and what it has passed on is a body whose whole existence is a series of handovers, and that a handover needs two ends and only one of the two ends is in the room.

He said that in four years of reading that body's papers in this city, the sentence on what is still owed has been written four thousand times and that nobody has ever asked which end of it owes it.

A woman of about forty-eight said in four seconds that the other end is not a room and that she has been the other end for about nine years and that she is a woman with a book and that the book does not know it is an end.

**Nobody in that room said that a register is not an institution and two people in it have said since that this is the sentence the whole of that Wednesday was about and that neither of them wrote it down.**

---

**And the man read it a fourth time and said that all four of them had read the same sentence and that two of them had said so and that he had not.**

He said that four people had sent him comments and that nine of those comments were on a word and that not one of them was on whether the sentence was the sentence to write.

He said that a sentence nobody can argue with is usually a sentence nobody is reading, and that the argument he was waiting for was on the doors, and that a man of about thirty-eight had given him that argument on the fourth second of reading it and then had gone back to the doors.

**He said that he was going to write the third version with the doors out of it and that he had not told anybody he was going to and that he was saying it out loud so that four people could stop him if they wanted to.**

Nine people in that room did not stop him and four of them said afterwards, on the stairs and in a corridor and in a car park, that they had wanted to stop him and had not, and that the not stopping had been because it was a Wednesday and that everybody was tired.

---

**And a man of about thirty-one said the half of it that nobody else had said, and he said it to the wall.**

He said that the sentence about handovers is the truest thing anybody has written about that body in about eleven years and that it is going to be read by about four hundred people as a description of a mechanism, and that a description of a mechanism tells a person exactly where to put their weight.

He said that a person who is told there are two ends of a thing and that one of the ends has a room will decide that the end with the room is the one to ask, and that the end with the room is the one that has been getting the sheets.

Nobody corrected him. A man of about fifty-two said in four seconds that he had said the thing nobody was going to say and that the reason nobody was going to say it was that the sentence on the desk had a date at the top of it and about four hundred people were going to read it on that date.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four said the one thing in that room that was said about that district and not about that sheet, and he said it standing at the back and he said it in about nine seconds.**

He said that four hundred people in that district have been told four times since the spring what is happening to them, and that every one of those four was somebody else writing something, and that on the date at the top of that sheet they will be handed a fifth.

He said that a fifth is not a number anybody in that district has a place for, and that there is no form for it and no column and no person in that district who is going to be the one to hand it over.

**He said that a person can refuse a thing four times and that on the fifth time a person stops refusing and starts waiting, and that nine people in that district have already started waiting and that four hundred have not and will not be told that the other nine have.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that this was the argument against putting a date on the sheet and that it was also the argument for it, and that a date is the only thing a person can carry and that a person carrying nothing stops carrying anything at all.

He said that was the best answer anybody had given since the spring and that he was not going to be the one to say so twice, and that four people in that room had heard him say it once.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about an hour and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that a sentence on two ends is a thing he could fix and that fixing it would not have helped.**

He has also said that he was told one thing and did not repeat it, and that the thing he was told concerned a door that a person could not open from the inside, and that he did not know which door and did not ask.

He has said one more thing about that Wednesday, which is that five people on that floor were carrying pieces of paper between tables and that he watched one of them carry the same piece of paper past the same table twice.

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where a corridor of office doors had been rehung on self-closers that had all been set to the same speed.**

A self-closer is a device in the head of a door with a piston in an oil-filled body, and the speed is set by turning a valve, and a valve set by somebody in about four minutes in the morning is the same on every door in a corridor whether the door is a fire door or a storeroom.

He went along the corridor with a clock and closed each door from a fully open position and timed it. Three of them took two seconds and four took five, and the four that took five were the ones that had to be closed with a shoulder.

He reset the four to two seconds, took the covers off two of the closers and found the oil down in both, and topped them up with the oil the maker had specified and not a thicker one.

Thirty-one pounds for the corridor. He told the man that a closer set to five seconds is a closer somebody has set to stop being a nuisance to the person who is not closing it, and that about four corridors in that district have their doors set that way.

---

**And the second fault on that corridor was a door that had been fitted with a lever handle on a door that somebody had bolted from the other side, which is not a fault and is a decision.**

Somebody had put a drop bolt through the leading edge and the floor on a corridor door in a building that had been bought in the spring. He took the bolt out, plugged the two holes in the edge and the hole in the floor, and fitted a surface bolt on the corridor side where it could be reached and not reached from the other side.

Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a door bolted from the far side is not locked, it is only a door that a person on the near side cannot leave, and that four doors in that building are like it and that nobody has ever said so out loud in that building.

---

**And a washbasin in the same corridor had been fixed through the pedestal instead of into the wall, so the whole thing rocked when anybody put a hand on it.**

He took the basin off, packed the two fixing holes in the pedestal, drilled through the back of the pedestal into the wall plate with a long bit, and refitted it on two new screws through the plate and not through the china.

Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a basin fixed to its own pedestal is a basin somebody can put a knee under, and that four basins in that building are and that in one of them the pedestal has already been through.

---

**And a length of trunking in an office in the same building had been clipped to a plasterboard ceiling with plastic clips.**

Trunking that carries cable is fixed along a ceiling with clips at four hundred millimetres, and a plasterboard ceiling moves with the building and the clips do not follow it. He took the clips out, put a line of solid timber battens under the trunking across six ceiling joists, and refitted it on screws into the battens.

Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that plastic clips into plasterboard hold a trunking up until the first warm week of the year, and that four runs of trunking in that office are on plasterboard and that one of them has come down onto a desk.

---

**And a radiator valve on the wall of that corridor had been painted three times without being shut off at the radiator, which is why four valves in this city are painted shut.**

He drained about two litres out of the radiator into a bowl, cracked the valve gently with a spanner and a folded cloth so it did not shear, and let it back in at about a quarter of a bar of cold.

Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a valve that has been painted shut is a valve that somebody decided was not a valve, and that four valves in that building have been painted shut and that in one case the radiator under it is still full and has never been bled.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and sixty-eight days and has not been read.**

---
