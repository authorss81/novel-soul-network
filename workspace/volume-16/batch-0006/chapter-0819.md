# Chapter 819 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Counter In A First District Where A Woman Of About Twenty-Four Put One Sheet Down In Front Of A Man And Said Four Words And Went Out, And Where A Woman Who Keeps A Haulier's Books Said Nine Seconds To A Man Of About Thirty-Four About A Working Day She Was Not In The Room For

**A Friday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished at a counter in a first district, where a woman of about twenty-four put one sheet down in front of a man and said four words and went out. A woman who keeps a haulier's books said about nine seconds to a man of about thirty-four and then went back to a gate.**

---

That Friday's board carried twelve names and twelve dates, the twelfth entered at ten to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Friday, at the hour it comes down at on every one of these days.

**Line seventeen is one thousand two hundred and eighty-one days, a hundred and eighty-three weeks to the day. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and fifty-seven days, a hundred and fifty-one weeks to the day. The separation is eight hundred and eighty-nine days, a hundred and twenty-seven weeks to the day.**

---

**She put one sheet down and she said four words and the four words were the date at the foot of that district's own notice and nothing else, and she was gone before he had turned it over.**

The date was in that district's own hand and it was the Monday the gate opened at five instead of six, and it was forty days old, and about four hundred people in that district had it and about nine of them had been told by a person walking past.

He said in four seconds that she had given him a date he already had. She said she had, and that this was the whole of what she had come to do, and that she had been asked to do something else and had not done it.

**Nobody asked her what she had been asked to do instead and nobody in that shop asked her to sit down and about four people in that shop have said since that she was in and out in about nine seconds.**

---

**And then a woman who keeps a haulier's books came in at about half past four and did not sit down either, and she said to a man of about thirty-four about nine seconds of something and then said nothing else.**

She said that he had used her working day as a fact in a room in a second district, on a Monday, and that she was not in that room and was not told and had not been asked.

He said in about four seconds that he had said that one man had been told three days before by a person walking past a gate, and that he had said it to five people in a room and that she had not been one of the five.

She said that this was correct and that the five people were not the problem and that the problem was that her day had been used to move a fact about somebody else across a room.

**He said that he was sorry and she said, in four seconds, that she did not want it said, and that an apology would put it back on her, and that she had said that to four people already this month.**

He did not apologise and he has said since that he wanted to and that she had told him in four seconds what the apology would do and that he has not worked out a way of saying it that does not put it back on her.

Nobody in that shop thanked him and nobody in that shop thanked her and about four people in that shop said afterwards that the smallest thing either of them said was four words.

---

**And what happened to the district on that Friday is in its own hand at the foot of its own notice and it happened on the Monday named there, and about four hundred people found out on that Monday morning that there was no work.**

It was not stopped. It was not argued with. It was not bought, because nothing was offered and no form was filled in and no period was put on it. It was not forgiven, because nobody said anything to anybody about anybody doing anything.

**The yard is still swept at six and not at seven and the bus is still let out of the bay at the hour it always was, and the gate still opens at six on a weekday, and about nine weeks of that is now in the past and nobody has said what it is.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that what came out of that yard on that Monday was the work and not the district, and that this had been the case for forty days and that this is the first time anybody has said it.

A man of about fifty-two said in four seconds that it was not the first time, that it is written down in the notice, and that what had been missing was somebody to read a notice in a room.

---

**And what four hundred people did in that district on the Tuesday after is known to about nine of them and to nobody in this city, and the whole of what they did was nothing, and this is not a criticism of them.**

A man of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that a Tuesday with no work in it is a Tuesday, and that about four hundred people who find out on a Tuesday are not going to do anything about it, and that this has been true of everything in this city for about eleven years.

He said that a thing nobody does anything about is not nothing and is a habit, and that a habit is the only thing this city has ever built that works, and that about four hundred people have just built one.

A woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that the habit is that the man still does it, and that the habit has a man's name in it and not a district's, and that this is why the district can withdraw and the thing cannot.

**She said that this is the whole of the answer and that it has taken this city about eleven years and one withdrawal to arrive at it, and that nobody in this city is going to be thanked for arriving at it.**

Nobody in that shop said that the man of about forty-seven had been thanked and nobody in that shop said that he had not been thanked, and about two people in that shop said afterwards that nobody had ever thought to look.

---

**And the district is still there, and the withdrawal took the work and not the place, and about four people in this city have said the second half of that out loud in a fortnight and nobody has given either of them a reason.**

A woman of about twenty-four said in about nine seconds that the yard gate opens at six and the yard is swept at six and the bus comes out at the hour it always was, and that these three things are the district and that they are still going.

She said that the thing that stopped is not any of them and has no gate and no sweep and no bay, and that nobody has ever written down which of the two ends of it belongs to the district and which to the person.

**She said that a person cannot withdraw a thing they cannot name and that the district has withdrawn everything that could be named and that this is the correct amount and that nobody has said so to them.**

**Nobody in that room said that the reading would have changed the date and about four people in that room said afterwards that they had wanted somebody to read it and had not wanted it read.**

---

**And what the man of about thirty-four said about that Monday afterwards is in his own words and he said it in about nine seconds in that shop and four people heard it.**

He said that four hundred people woke up on a Monday morning, and that the gate opened at five instead of six, and that nine of them had been told the date and the rest had not, and that nobody said anything to anybody.

He said that this is what happened at the date at the top of that notice in the spring as well, and that the only difference between that Monday and this one is that a woman said four words at a gateway three days before.

He said that nine people in this city have now said the sentence about what is lost and that four hundred people have not said it and will not, and that the sentence is about a line and not about a district.

**He said that he is not going to say it to them and that nobody is going to send him and that this is the correct arrangement and not a gap in it.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in four seconds that he had said that sentence in nine seconds four times in a fortnight and that each time it had been in a room and never at a gate.

He said that this was correct and that nine people in this city have worked it out and that none of them has said it to four hundred people, and that the not saying it is the whole of the difficulty.

---

**And nobody in that shop put a date in a diary on that Friday and nobody wrote it anywhere, and four people in that shop have since found out that the date is still on the notice and that the notice is still on a board.**

A woman of about twenty-four said in nine seconds that a date which has not been put in a diary is a date that will be read by nine people and no others, and that four hundred people read that notice in a fortnight without a diary and that they did not put it in one either.

**She said that this is the answer to a question nobody asked on that Friday and that it is the second time in about eleven weeks that the answer has turned out to be something nobody had been looking for.**

---

**And nobody in this city has condemned that district and nobody has endorsed it, and that is not a thing anybody decided.**

A man of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that the people who left are not the story and that the story is that nobody in this city had to write down who was holding what, and that about four hundred people were relying on a man they had never asked.

He said that this was the same thing he had been saying since the Monday and that nobody has given him a reason to stop and that nobody has given him one to go on with either.

**A woman of about twenty-four said in four seconds that nobody was going to be the story, and that the people who left would be talked about by people who did not go, and that this has happened in about nine other places in this city and is not a thing that can be stopped by anybody.**

---

**A man of about twenty-two was at that counter for about an hour and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that two women came into that shop in one afternoon and neither of them stayed and both of them had it correct.**

He has also said that he was told three things and repeated none of them, and that one of them was about a bay on a road in a second district, and that he now knows what a bay is for and has not been told which one.

---

**The rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district, where a set of about eleven metres of shelving in a storeroom had been put up without any wall fixings at the ends.**

A run of shelving that is fixed only in the middle is a cantilever at both ends and a cantilever in a storeroom is a cantilever that somebody will load the end of one day.

He took the run down, fitted uprights at each end into the wall with anchors, cross-braced the back, and refitted the run onto the uprights and the brackets.

Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that shelving fixed in the middle only is a thing that happens about four times a year and that four runs in that storeroom are like it and that one of them has a bracket pulled out of the wall.

---

**And the second fault in that storeroom was a wall which had been painted around a shelf that was still on it, and the shelf was holding about nine boxes off the floor.**

He took the boxes off, took the shelf off, cleaned the back of the paint line back to plaster with a scraper, and refitted the shelf about a hundred millimetres lower so the line would be covered.

Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a shelf painted round is a shelf that was never meant to be moved and that about four storerooms in this city have one and that in one of them the shelf is holding nothing at all.

---

**And a set of about nine boxes in that storeroom had been stacked so that the bottom box carried all of them and had been for about four years.**

He restacked them on a pallet off the floor, took the weight off the bottom box, and wrote the date on the side of the pallet with a marker so that the next person could see when it had been moved.

Eleven pounds. He told the man that a box on a floor carries the weight of everything on top of it and that about four stacks in that building are on floors and that on one of them the bottom box has gone soft.

---

**And a fire exit in the same building had a bar across it that had been painted over nine times and had swollen in the frame and would not lift.**

He took the bar off, cleaned the ends back to bright steel, planed the two pivot holes out to size, and refitted it on a new pin with a drop of oil on each, and worked it up and down four times.

Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a bar painted over nine times is a bar that nobody could tell was a bar, and that about four fire exits in that building have one and that one of them has had a notice put up over it.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and ninety-eight days and has not been read.**

---
