# Chapter 817 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road Where About Nine People Built The Reason For A District To Stay Out Loud And Found That There Was No Version Of It That Was Not A Trade, And Where The Only Person In That Room Who Could Have Made It Work Said No In About Four Seconds

**A Friday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road, where about nine people spent two hours taking a reason apart and putting it back together and it came apart the same way twice. Nobody was asked to stay and nothing was bought and a man of twenty-two said no in about four seconds.**

---

Thirteen names and thirteen dates went onto that Friday's board, the last of the thirteen at ten to five. On that Friday the shutter came down at ten, which is the hour it comes down at on every day of this stretch of days.

**Line seventeen is one thousand two hundred and seventy-four days, a hundred and eighty-two weeks to the day. The post at that corridor end is one thousand and fifty days, a hundred and fifty weeks to the day. The separation is eight hundred and eighty-two days, a hundred and twenty-six weeks to the day.**

---

**The reason was true and it was about four hundred people's hands and it took two hours to establish that there was no way of saying it that was not a trade, and about nine people in that room established it together.**

A man of about fifty-two put it in its best form first and it went like this. He said that four hundred people use a thing one man holds, that the man is losing the use of it, and that when he stops, four hundred people find out at once and none of them will know why.

He said that this is the only true reason anybody in that room has for asking a district to stay and that it has never been said to a district in about eleven years because saying it requires somebody to admit that the thing is held in one pair of hands.

**He said that the admission is the cost and that the cost is not money and is not a post and is nine people in a room saying out loud that a person they all know is carrying four hundred people in his left hand.**

Nobody in that room said it was untrue and nobody in that room said it was true and four people in that room said afterwards that the sentence worked and that four said it did not, and both of those were true.

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-four took it apart, and she took it apart in about nine seconds and she did it by asking who is it for.**

She said that a reason for a district to stay has to be a reason the district can use, and that a district cannot use being sorry for a man, and that a district cannot use a promise to pay for something nobody has priced.

She said that the only version of that sentence a district can use is the sentence that the thing will be paid for, and that nobody in that room has a price and cannot produce one this week, and that producing one this week would be the trade.

**She said that a reason which cannot be acted on is not a reason for staying and is a reason for feeling bad about leaving, and that this city has been given the second kind four times since the spring.**

A man of about thirty-eight said that she had it and that this was the same thing she said about a notice a fortnight ago with a different set of words, and that he was not going to be the one to say she had said it before.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four said the thing that made the room go quiet, and he said it to the table and not to the man of about fifty-two.**

He said that there is one person in this city that about four hundred people would take a thing from, and that this person has never said anything to them and has been in a room nine times this month, and that if that person said the reason was worth staying for then about nine of them would stay.

He said that this is not a threat and is not a plan and has not been suggested by anybody, and that he had worked it out at about the fourth minute of the meeting and had not said it for an hour and fifty minutes on purpose.

Nobody in that room said that he should have said it earlier and nobody in that room said that he should not have said it at all.

---

**And the man of twenty-two said no, and he said it in about four seconds, and the reason he gave was about his own hands.**

He said that he was the person, and that he was not going to say a thing like that to four hundred people, and that there is nothing anybody in that district would owe him afterwards and that he would not be able to do a repair for about two years without thinking about it.

He said that his hands do not hold steady for about two hours four times in eleven years and that this is the only thing he has ever been asked about that he could actually answer, and that he answered it and it was about himself and not about that district.

**He said that he does not know whether he refused it for a good reason, and that he has been in a corridor for about twenty minutes since and has not been able to make the reason be the district instead of his hands.**

Four people in that room said since that the reason was a true one and nine said it was not a good enough one and that the answer held anyway, and nobody in that room has put those two things next to each other.

---

**And the reason was not beaten and nobody argued him back, and a man of about thirty-eight said that the reason stands where it was put.**

He said that the reason is true and that it has been said out loud in a room and that a true thing said out loud in a room in this city is a thing that has never happened before and about four people will repeat it badly on purpose.

**He said that nobody in that room is going to say whether it was a defeat, and that nine people in this city will say which it was within about four days and that this room will not be one of the nine saying it.**

Nobody in that room asked him to put it in writing and nobody in that room offered to put it in writing for him.

---

**And a woman of about twenty-nine heard the reason for the first time on that Friday and heard it from a man of about thirty-eight in about nine seconds, and asked one question about it and did not get an answer.**

She asked whether the man of about forty-seven had been told that this was the reason. He said in four seconds that he had not and that he would be told on a Tuesday and that a Tuesday is when four hundred people find out.

She said that this is the shape of the whole thing and that she can draw it, and that the man does the work for years, and that nobody tells him he is the reason, and that on a Tuesday somebody else finds out and has to go and explain.

**He said that she had described a thing he had not described to anybody, and that he had described it to nine people in a room about an hour before she came in.**

Nobody in that room said that he was describing it to the wrong people and nobody in that room asked him who he was describing it to.

---

**And the last half hour of that Friday in that room was four people arguing about whether the price could be worked out at all, and it could not, and nobody pretended otherwise.**

A man of about fifty-two said that the only price anybody has ever heard of in this city is a sum of money and that the reason a person in that district cannot be given a price is that it is not a thing money buys and that he has no idea what it is.

He said that a man holds something nine times a day for eleven years and that at some point the holding is not a favour and is not a post and is a thing the four hundred have decided to take, and that this city has no word for that and has never needed one.

A woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that it has a word and that the word is about four hundred people relying on a man they have never asked, and that this is now the fourth time anybody has said it this month.

He said in four seconds that four times in a month is a habit and not a discovery, and that nobody in this room is going to be the first to say it out loud twice in one afternoon.

**Nobody in that room said it out loud twice and about four people in that room have said since that they came away from that Friday able to say it in the same words he used, which they had not had before the Friday.**

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about two hours and said about four seconds of it, and he has said since that he did not say the second thing he had come to say.**

He has also said that he was told two things and repeated neither, and that one of them was about a bay on a road in a second district, and that the other was about a person who had been told a thing in about nine seconds in an office a long way from here.

He said that he does not know whether refusing it was for a good reason and that he has not asked anybody and that he is not going to.

---

**The rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where a run of about eleven metres of handrail had been set out from a wall on brackets of the wrong projection.**

A wall bracket for a handrail is a plate and a projection. A bracket with the wrong projection sets the rail out from the wall, which is not a fault of the bracket and is a fault of the hand on it. Eleven metres at about eighty millimetres out is a rail nobody can take a full weight on.

He took the eleven brackets off, packed each one behind with two shims to bring the rail back to about fifty millimetres, refitted them, and re-fixed the rail onto the corrected line.

Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a handrail set too far out is a handrail a person uses with two fingers, and that four runs of handrail in that building are on brackets of the wrong projection and that one of them has come away at the top fixing.

---

**And the second fault on that run was a rail joint that had been made with a plate on one side only, which is a joint that works until the building moves.**

He took the plate off one side, cut a matching plate, drilled both through the rail with a brief bit, bolted them, and left the fixing holes for the wall brackets alone.

Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a plate on one side of a rail joint is a plate that takes the whole load and none of the shear, and that four joints in that building are plated on one side and that one of them has opened about a sixteenth of an inch.

---

**And four brackets on a fire escape in the same building had been bolted through a handrail flange and not through the flange's solid part.**

He packed the four flanges back out to solid, redrilled them and fitted longer bolts into the solid behind, and made good the paint on the four patches with a zinc-rich primer and one coat over it.

Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a bolt through a flange edge is a bolt that goes round the metal rather than into it, and that four brackets in that building are in flange edges and that on one of them the flange has started to tear.

---

**And a set of about nine metres of grating on a landing in that building had been cut about four millimetres undersize on one side and had been dropped in to fit.**

He lifted it, took about four millimetres off the bearing bar at one end with a cold chisel and a file, and refitted it so it bears on both ends and about half of its width.

Nineteen pounds. He told the man that grating dropped in to fit is grating carrying on one edge, and that four lengths of grating in that building are dropped in and that one of them has a bar bent where somebody put a trolley on it.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and ninety-one days and has not been read.**

---
