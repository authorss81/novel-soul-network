# Chapter 811 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Second Floor In A Building Off A Road Where About Nine People Were Sitting Down And About Four Of Them Were Working, And A Man Of About Forty-Four Who Had Been Employed Eleven Weeks To Write Down What That Body Is And Could Not Get Past The First Line Of It

**A Monday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished on a second floor in a building off a road, where nine people were sitting down and four of them were working. The man who had been put there to write something had one line finished and a date at the top of the sheet and nothing under the line.**

---

Twelve names went onto that Monday's board with twelve dates, and the last of the twelve was entered at ten to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Monday, at the hour it comes down at on every one of the ten days of this stretch of days.

**Those four rooms and one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and seventy-seven days, two hundred and eleven weeks to the day. Line sixteen is one thousand two hundred and sixty-seven days, a hundred and eighty-one weeks to the day, and the nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand and forty-three days, a hundred and forty-nine weeks to the day.**

---

**What came out of that yard on the Monday its gate opened at five instead of six was the work, and about four hundred people went out with the work and not with the district.**

Nobody on that second floor had said the word district in a fortnight and nobody put the two things together in a sentence, and the sentence that would have put them together was written nowhere and had no author and would have needed one.

---

**And the thing that was on that second floor on that Monday was a printed sheet with a date at the top and one line under it, and the man who had been employed eleven weeks to write the rest of it had been at that desk since about half past eight.**

A man of about fifty-two came up at about ten past nine and read the one line over the man's shoulder without sitting down.

He said in nine seconds that the line was a description of a thing that had happened and that nobody who reads it on the day it is handed to them will care, and that the people who will care are the ones who read it in a year when it has stopped being news.

The man of about forty-four said that the sentence above the line was the only part he had been able to write in eleven weeks, and that the sentence above the line was a list of what that body had received.

**He said that a list of what a body has received is not what a body is, and that he has known that since the fourth week and has not found a way to put the other thing on a sheet.**

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four came up at about half past ten and read the one line and then read the sentence above it twice and said it was not wrong and was not any use.**

She said that it was a list of doors and a list of dates and that every person who reads it would work out what it was for themselves and that four of them would have it and nine of them would have something else.

She said that the sentence a person reads on the day they are handed it is not the sentence they are handed it for. She said that the sentence they are handed it for is the one on what happens after, and that nobody in this city has ever written one of those because writing one means putting a date on somebody else's week.

A man of about fifty-two said in four seconds that she had described the difficulty and not the reason, and that the reason is that there are nine people on this floor and about four of them are paid and that none of the four is paid to be wrong.

**Nobody on that floor said that the man of about forty-four should be paid to be accurate, and two of the people who did not say it have said since that they thought about it on the stairs.**

---

**And the man of about forty-four said the thing that made the rest of that morning a Monday rather than a Tuesday, and he said it to the sheet and not to anybody.**

He said that he had been eleven weeks on that sentence and that he had got as far as knowing that he could not write what that body was, and that the reason he could not was that he had spent eleven weeks reading what it had done and there was not one word anywhere in what it had done about what it was.

He said that a thing that has only ever been described by what it has done cannot be described by what it is without somebody making a decision about which of the things it did mattered most, and that he was not going to make that decision on a Monday for nine people who had not asked him to.

**He said that if nine people in a room cannot say what they are then a sheet saying so is worth more to them than a sheet saying what they are, and that he would put that on the sheet if anybody would sign it.**

Four people in that room said nothing and five said something and two of the nine said the same thing, and the two of them who said the same thing were sitting on the same side of the table and had not spoken to each other since the spring.

---

**And five of the other people on that floor spent that morning doing nothing at all, and this was not a mood and it was not a grievance and it had been going on since before the spring.**

A woman of about thirty-one said in nine seconds that the nothing was the reason the man of about forty-four could not finish, and that the nothing was not caused by the sentence and would not be cured by it.

She said that about five people on that floor had been told to be there and about four of them had a job that consisted of passing a piece of paper to another person, and that in nine years nobody on that floor had been told to find out anything.

**She said that a floor where the only work is carrying a piece of paper from one table to another table is a floor that cannot find out what it is, and that this is not a fault in anybody on it and that it is a fault in the arrangement and that nobody on that floor is paid to say so.**

A man of about fifty-two said in four seconds that the arrangement had been made by somebody and that the somebody had gone, and that this was the same arrangement in four rooms in four districts, and that nine people in this city had noticed it and that the noticing had gone in nine letters and that nine letters is not a body.

Nobody on that floor said what the arrangement had been for and nobody on that floor said what it was meant to produce, and the sheet on that desk has neither of those on it either.

---

**And a woman of about forty-eight said the thing about the date at the top of that sheet, and she said it standing up, which she does not do.**

She said that a date at the top of a sheet is a promise about the day the sheet is read, and that nobody in that room had been asked to make that promise and that the date was already on the sheet because somebody had put it there before he came.

She said that if a sheet is read on the day at the top of it then whatever is under the line is said to about four hundred people at once and none of them has been asked, and that this is the arrangement everybody complains about, and that the complaint is correct and that it is being made to the wrong person.

**She said that the correct person is whoever chose the date, and that nine people in this city have said that this fortnight, and that nobody has written down the name of the person who chose it.**

A man of about fifty-two said in nine seconds that no person chose that date, and that the date at the foot of that district's own notice is in the district's own hand, and that this has been said before in a room and was not written down and is now being said twice in a fortnight.

Nobody on that floor said that this made the date a different kind of date.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about fifty minutes and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that he read the one line twice and that one line is a sentence about doors and that he has been on the other end of a door like that.**

He has also said that he wrote nothing down and that nothing was put in his hand, and that the sheet with a date at the top of it was lying on that desk face up for an hour with nothing on it under the line and that he looked at it for nine minutes.

---

**The rest of that Monday was a counter until about six and two hours of repairs in a second district, where a fire door on a stair had been closing on the frame and not into it.**

A fire door hangs on a frame and closes against a stop, and the stop is a strip on the frame that the door presses its seal against. A door that closes on the frame and not into it has a stop on the wrong face, or no stop at all with a seal doing the work of one.

He took the door off its butts, packed the hinge side with three washers so the door sat a sixteenth of an inch further into the frame, refitted it, and ran it closed and open six times with a card between the seal and the stop to feel for a drag.

Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a door seal is not a stop and that a seal that has to be the stop wears out in about four years and that four doors on that stair have seals doing the work of stops.

---

**And the second fault was a handrail on the same stair, which had been painted nine times over a loose bracket.**

A handrail bracket is screwed to a string and the string moves with the building and the screws do not follow it. He pulled the bracket, filled the two old holes with matched plugs, ran the screws back into sound timber an inch further in, and refitted the rail to the bracket.

Twenty-four pounds and half an hour. He told the man that nine coats of paint on a bracket is not nine coats of fixing, and that four brackets in that building have had one coat on them and came out of the wall in the winter.

---

**And a shop counter in a first district had a worktop that had been fitted with four screws straight into chipboard, which is the join four counters in this city have.**

He filled the four holes with dowel set in glue, drilled the worktop's own fixings out to the dowel, and refitted it about a quarter of an inch lower so that the front edge stood inside the carcass instead of proud of it.

Thirty-one pounds. He said that a worktop carried on four screws into four pounds of glue and wood is a worktop that moves when somebody leans on it and that nobody finds out for two years and then everybody finds out at once.

---

**And a length of handrail outside a building on the same road had been polished to a shine on the top face only, which is what happens to four handrails in this city every year.**

He took the top face back with a strip of abrasive under a block, took the corners off the ends so the shine could not come back on a curve, and left the underside alone because the underside is the part hands actually need.

Eleven pounds. He told the man that a shine on a rail is a warning and not a fault, and that four rails on that road have it and that a rail that has been polished on top and not underneath is telling the truth about which hand goes where.

---

**And a set of three steps at a shop in a second district had been painted round the nosings, which is what happens to four sets of steps in this city every year and which nobody has ever asked anybody to say.**

Paint on a nosing sits on the edge and not on the tread, and when it dries on the edge it lifts and takes the grit off the tread with it, and the grit goes onto the next step down and then onto the pavement.

He took the four coats off both nosings with a flat file under a block, ran a wire brush along the two treads, and left the rest of the paint where it was because the rest of it is on a riser and a riser does not get walked on.

Nineteen pounds and about three quarters of an hour. He told the man that a nosing with paint on it is a step that is wet in the autumn and greasy by the following spring, and that four steps in that district have it and that nobody in that district knows which four.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and sixty-six days and has not been read.**

---
