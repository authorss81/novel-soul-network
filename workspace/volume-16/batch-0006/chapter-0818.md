# Chapter 818 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road Where A Sheet Carrying A Date Was Read Out And Then A Reason Was Published On A Face In About Nine Seconds And Stood There, And Where Nobody Asked Anybody To Stay And Nothing Was Bought And The Sheet Went Into About Four Hundred Hands That Night By People Who Were Not Sent Anywhere

**A Wednesday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road, where the sheet was read out and then a reason was put in front of nine people and left there. Nobody asked anybody to stay, nothing was bought, and about four hundred people were handed it that night by people nobody sent.**

---

Fourteen names and fourteen dates were written on that Wednesday's board, the fourteenth at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Wednesday, at the hour it comes down at on each of these ten days.

**Line eighteen is one thousand two hundred and twenty-five days, a hundred and seventy-five weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand one hundred and thirteen days, a hundred and fifty-nine weeks to the day. What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven days, a hundred and seventy-one weeks to the day.**

---

**The sheet was read out in that room on that Wednesday in about nine seconds by a man of about forty-four, and it is now four lines long and it has a date on the top of it and it has no doors in it.**

It says what the body is given and what it passes on. It says that it holds no list of anybody. It says that it cannot hold a practice for anybody, and that it did not know it could not until a fortnight ago.

**Nobody in that room said one word about the four lines and nobody in that room put a fifth one on it, and the man who wrote it has said since that he was asked twice and that he said no twice.**

A woman of about twenty-four came into that room at about seven and read it standing up, and read it a second time, and put it down and did not say anything for about nine seconds.

She said that this is the first sentence anybody has written in this city that a person can be given before they are asked about anything, and that about four hundred people will be given it that night.

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-four published the reason, on a face, in about nine seconds, and it stands.**

She said that about four hundred people use a thing one man holds and that the man is losing the use of it and that when he stops, four hundred people find out at once and none of them will know why.

She said that this is true and that it has been true since before the spring and that nobody in this city has ever said it to a person who could do anything about it.

**She said that it is not a reason to stay and that she is not offering it as one, and that a reason to stay needs a price and there is no price and there will not be one this week.**

Nobody in that room said she had made it into something it was not. Nobody in that room said she had made it into something it was.

---

**And a man of about fifty-two asked her the question that was in the room, and he asked it in nine seconds and he asked it out loud.**

He asked her whether that body was going to do anything about the left hand.

She said no, in four seconds. She said that the body has no money and no way of finding out whether that man's left hand is the one that is going, and that the body has been asked about money twice this month and has not answered either time.

**She said that the reason is true and that the body cannot act on it and that a true reason which cannot be acted on is the exact shape of the thing this city has handed four hundred people four times since the spring, and that this is the fifth and that it is the first one anybody has said was a reason.**

Nobody in that room said that the fifth one was better and nobody in that room said that it was worse.

---

**And what the body is, in its own words, on the top of the sheet, with the date on it, is four lines, and they are these.**

*It is given things and it passes them on. It is not a register and it holds no list of anybody. It cannot hold a practice for anybody and it did not know that it could not until a fortnight ago. It does not decide anything by itself and nothing it has done since the spring was decided by anybody else either.*

---

**A woman of about twenty-nine read those four lines out loud twice in that room and stopped on the third word the second time.**

She said that the third line is the whole of what this city has been not saying for eleven years, and that it took four lines to say it because the two halves of it are about different people.

She said that the first half of that line says the body cannot hold a practice, and that the second half says it did not know, and that the second half is the one about nine people in that room and about nine hundred people in this city.

A man of about fifty-two said in nine seconds that this is the first time anybody has written down the other half of that line, and that the first half has been understood by everybody who has ever used that word, and that the other half is what nobody has ever said.

**Nobody in that room asked her which half she had expected to be in those four lines, and nobody in that room has asked her.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-eight said what will happen to those four lines, and he said it in about nine seconds and nobody argued with him.**

He said that four lines saying that a body cannot hold a practice is now a thing that four hundred people can hold against that body for about nine years, and that this is what happens to sentences and that he was not saying it to stop it.

He said that the sentence is worth more in the drawer of somebody who did not write it than in the room of the person who did, and that this city has been run that way for eleven years and that about nine people in this room have understood that this is the price of a sentence.

A man of about fifty-two said in four seconds that the price is the opposite of what people expect and that he has it, and that nobody in that room is going to say that out loud in a form with a date on it.

**Nobody in that room put it in a form and nobody in that room filled in a form, and about four hundred people were handed a piece of paper that night by people who had not been given a list and had not been sent anywhere.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-eight said what the body actually did that evening, and it is in its own words, and nobody wrote it down and it is set out here in full.**

It gave the sheet to about nine people who had asked for it and to nobody else. It did not ask a single person in that district to stay. It offered no money and no post and no period and no form of any kind.

It put the date on the sheet nine days before the date and it said so on the sheet, and it did not move the date when somebody said nine days was too few.

**It took the answer it got, which was that about four hundred people would be handed the sheet that night by about nine people who were not sent anywhere and who had not been given a list.**

---

**And a man of about fifty-two said the thing about what happens on the Wednesday after, and he said it before anybody had left the room and nobody stopped him.**

He said that four hundred people will have that sheet on the date and that nine of them will have read it and that the rest will not, and that this is not a failure of the sheet and is the ordinary arithmetic of a district.

He said that the body will find out which nine, and that in about eleven years nobody in this city has ever found out which nine, and that finding out is not the job of a body with nine chairs.

**He said that a second sheet would go out about four weeks after the first and that nobody has decided to issue it and that nobody is going to decide it in that room.**

A woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that a second sheet is how anybody finds out which nine, and that a first sheet tells a body nothing at all and that a second one tells it nine things.

Nobody in that room argued with her about the second sheet and nobody in that room committed anybody to issuing one, and four people in that room said afterwards that this is the argument that will decide it and that it will be decided by somebody who is not in that room.

---

**And the last twenty minutes of that evening was four people and a printer's proof of the sheet with the date on it, and nobody corrected a word of it.**

A woman of about twenty-nine found one thing, which is that the second line said it passes them on and that somebody asked in about eleven years what it passes them on to, and that the answer in about eleven weeks is that there is no next door.

She said that this is now on a dated sheet and that four hundred people will have read it and that no person in this city can un-read it for them.

**Nobody in that room asked her to take the line out and she did not ask to take the line out, and the sheet went out that night with the line in it.**

Nobody in that room counted the people who took it and nobody in that room has counted them since, and four people in that room said afterwards that the sheet says in writing a thing that body has been unable to say out loud for about eleven years.

---

**A man of twenty-two was in that room for about an hour and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that he stood four feet from a table for an hour while a true reason about four hundred people was standing in it.**

He has also said that he was told one thing and did not repeat it, and that the thing he was told was that the body had put a true reason in front of about nine people and had not asked one of them for anything, and that he was in the room and was not asked either.

He has said that he has been in a room about four times this month and has said about four seconds in all of them and that this is the only arrangement anybody in this city has ever offered him that he could actually keep.

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district, where a set of about eleven metres of palisade fencing had been replaced in the spring on posts that had gone into the same soft ground again.**

He pulled eleven posts, went down to the hard ground underneath, and refitted each post in a concrete collar, and re-tensioned the mesh, and told the man that it will need doing again in about four winters and that this is what four winters does to a post in soft ground.

Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that new fencing on old practice is the commonest repair in this city and that four other lengths on that road had been done in the spring and that nine of the posts on those four are already loose.

---

**And the second fault on that line was a gate on a hinge post that had been hung with a hinge that was too heavy for the post.**

He took the gate off, packed the post with a galvanised plate on the hinge side where the fixing is, refitted the gate on the same hinge, and eased it so it swings without a catch.

Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a heavy hinge on a post in soft ground works the post loose in about four winters and that four gates on that road have one and that one of them has a hinge bolt through a split.

---

**And a length of about nine metres of palisade along the same boundary had been set with its bottom rail about a hundred millimetres above the ground, which is the height a person can get a boot under.**

He took the whole length down, packed the ground along it to take the posts lower, and refitted the length with the bottom rail on the ground and the posts in collars.

Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a fence with a gap under it is a fence that a person can get a boot into and about four lengths on that road have one and that one of them has had a bag taken through it.

---

**And a yard gate on that boundary had been left off its latch and was being held shut with a length of wire, which is the ordinary shape of that fault and not the only one.**

He took the wire off, dropped both hinge bolts and picked them up about a millimetre, packed the hinge leaves with a shim to bring the gate back into true, and refitted a new barrel hinge on the closing side.

Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a wire on a gate is a latch that has been given up on, and that four gates in that yard are held by wire and that one of them has had a wire round the post and a lock on the wire.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and ninety-six days and has not been read.**
