# Chapter 814 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Second Floor Where A Third Attempt At One Sheet Was Brought In With The Doors Taken Out Of It And Was Put On The Table Face Up And Was Not Picked Up By Four People For About Eleven Minutes

**A Monday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished on a second floor in a building off a road, where a third attempt at one sheet was brought in and laid face up and left alone. Nothing was decided on that Monday and the sheet was still face up when the building shut.**

---

That Monday's board carried twelve names with twelve dates, the twelfth name entered at ten to five. The shutter came down at ten on that Monday, as it does on every day of this stretch of days.

**Those four rooms and one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and ninety-one days, two hundred and thirteen weeks to the day. Line sixteen is one thousand two hundred and eighty-one days, a hundred and eighty-three weeks to the day, and the nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand and fifty-seven days, a hundred and fifty-one weeks to the day.**

---

**The doors were out of it and it came to about half the length it had been on the Wednesday, and nobody on that floor could say where the other half had gone to.**

The man of about forty-four said that the doors had gone because a man of about thirty-eight had said on the Wednesday that four hundred people remember one number, and that a sentence with two numbers in it is a sentence with no number in it.

He said that he had gone through eleven weeks of other people's comments and that every one of them had been about a fact and that not one of them had been about whether the sentence was true.

A man of about thirty-eight said that the sentence was true and that a true sentence about four hundred people is not a description of anything and is a receipt, and that a receipt goes in a drawer.

**He said that the sheet on that table was no longer a receipt and that this was a decision and that nobody in that room had made it, and that he was not going to pretend he had not noticed it happening.**

---

**And the fourth person to pick it up was a woman of about twenty-nine, and she picked it up at about eleven minutes and read it and put it down and did not say anything for about four seconds.**

She said that it was the first sentence anybody had written about that body which could not be used against anybody, and that this was not because it was careful and that it was because it was about the body and not about anybody in it.

A man of about fifty-two said in nine seconds that this was the correct thing to have happened and that it had happened by accident, and that about nine people in this city had been waiting eleven years for an institution to describe itself and were going to get a sentence that describes a thing doing a thing.

She said that was not nothing, and that she was not going to say it was something, and that about four people in that room were disappointed and that she was one of the four.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four said the thing that nobody in that room had said since the spring, and he said it standing at the end of the table with his back to the window.**

He said that a sentence about a body doing a thing is the only kind anybody will hand to four hundred people and still recognise on the day they get it, and that about nine people in that district can tell you what the four words said and cannot tell you anything else anybody has said about them.

He said that a district can be told what is happening to it in about four seconds and cannot be told what it is part of, and that the second thing takes about nine years and that four hundred people are not going to live long enough for that to be cheap.

**He said that the sentence on that table is the first one anybody has produced in this city in about nine years that four hundred people would recognise, and that about two of the nine people who have said so have said it on a sheet and seven have said it in a room.**

Nobody in that room asked him which two had said it on a sheet.

---

**And a man of about fifty-two said what the date actually is for, and he said it to the window and not to the sheet.**

He said that the date is not a promise about the day the sheet is read. He said that the date is the day after the last day on which anybody in this city can add anything to what the sheet says, and that a sheet carrying that date is a sheet that is finished before anybody has read it.

A woman of about thirty-four said in nine seconds that this was not what was said on the floor above and that two people on this floor had understood it as something else entirely.

He said that it was not his sentence and that he had read it on the floor above and that the floor above had taken it the other way, and that nine people in this city do that every time a date is mentioned.

**He said that the whole of the argument for putting a date on that sheet rests on which day the date means, and that nobody has established which day it means, and that nobody on that floor is going to be asked.**

A man of about thirty-four said in four seconds that this was the most useful thing anybody had said on that floor since the Wednesday and that it was the wrong floor, and that the sentence would go out with the date meaning the first of the two days, because the first is the one that can be defended afterwards.

---

**And about four people on that floor asked the man of about forty-four a question nobody had asked him in eleven weeks, and it was the question about the sheet and not the question about the sentence.**

They asked him what he thought the sheet was for. He said in about nine seconds that he did not know, that he had taken it for granted that it was for the four hundred people in that district, and that a woman of about forty-eight had told him on that floor that a sheet with a date on it can be produced in a room in about four years.

He said that both of those are true, and that he had spent eleven weeks treating them as the same thing, and that this was the error and that it was his and not the floor's.

**Nobody in that room corrected him, and two people in that room said afterwards that they had wanted to and that the correcting would have been a kindness, and that a kindness was not available on that floor.**

---


**And then a woman of about forty-eight said the thing about the date at the top of it, and she said it standing up again, which is twice in a fortnight.**

She said that a sheet with a date at the top of it is a document and that a document is a thing that can be produced in a room in about four years by somebody who has found it, and that nobody in this room can stop that and should not try.

She said that a sheet without a date at the top of it is not a document and is a thing somebody said, and that things somebody said have never once in this city been produced in a room by anybody.

She said that she would not sign the sheet with the date on it and that she would sign the same sheet without it, and that about nine people in this city have refused to sign a second thing this month and that hers is the third.

**Nobody asked her to change her mind and nobody asked her why she would sign one version and not the other, and about four people in that room said afterwards that the reason was obvious and that they had not wanted it said out loud.**

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for about fifty minutes and said nothing and was asked nothing, and he has said since that the sheet lay face up on that table for eleven minutes and that four people walked round it.**

He has also said that he was told a thing and did not repeat it, and that the thing he was told was that a document can be produced in a room and a thing somebody said cannot, and that he had known that for about eleven years.

He has said that this is the first time in about eleven years he has heard a person say a thing he already knew in a room with nine people in it, and that he did not say so.

---

**And the last half hour of that Monday on that floor was four people and a sheet with a date on the top of it and no fifth line on it, and nine people had gone home.**

A man of about thirty-one said that the sentence is ready and that the only thing left is somebody to be the person who hands it over, and that this is the same problem the man of about forty-four has and that it is worse.

He said that a person who hands a dated sheet to four hundred people becomes the person who handed them the thing that happened, and that nine people in this city have been that person before and that two of them are in a room a long way from here.

He said that the man of about forty-four has been that person on paper since the Wednesday and does not know it yet, and that this was going to be said to him and that nobody was going to say it today.

**Nobody in that room said who was going to say it and nobody in that room said it to him, and the sheet went into a drawer on that floor with a date on it and nothing else in the drawer with it.**

---

**The rest of that Monday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a second district, where a set of shelves on a concrete wall had been drilled with a masonry bit straight into the fill and not into anything.**

A shelf fixed to a concrete wall has to be fixed into the wall and not into the hole that is in the wall. A hole drilled with a masonry bit and no hammer drill puts a cone of crushed block behind the plug and the plug holds about a fortnight.

He took the four brackets down, filled the old holes with cement mortar, redrilled through with a hammer drill and a proper bit, fitted chemical anchors, and refitted the brackets on the same centres.

Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a shelf on a concrete wall fixed into the fill is a shelf that comes down with the goods on it, and that four sets of shelves on that wall are fixed that way and that one of them has a crack through the plaster above it.

---

**And the second fault on that wall was a handrail bracket that had been bolted through a hollow block with a standard bolt and no sleeve.**

He pulled the bolt, found the void either side of it, fitted a hollow block sleeve so the bolt grips the far side of the block, and refitted it with a washer under the head the full width of the bracket.

Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a bolt in a hollow block with no sleeve is a bolt that is being squeezed and not pulled, and that four brackets in that building are in hollow block and that one of them has about a quarter of an inch of play in it.

---

**And a set of about nine metres of handrail on a stair in the same building had been carried out of a doorway and rehung across the frame, which is a repair and not a fault, and which had been done with four screws into a frame that is not solid.**

He took the rail off, moved the two brackets two hundred millimetres along the frame until they landed on solid stile, filled the four old holes, and refitted the rail on new holes through the stile and into a stud behind it.

Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a handrail carried out of a doorway and rehung is a handrail four years from coming off the wall, and that four rails in that building have been moved and that none of them has been moved back onto solid timber.

---

**And a set of coat hooks on a stair in the same building had been screwed into the plasterboard of a landing, where a landing has no stud and no batten and nothing at all behind it.**

He pulled the four hooks out, filled the holes, and fitted them through a length of batten let across the heads of the two joists above the landing and screwed to both.

Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a hook in plasterboard on a landing is a hook that comes out with the coat still hanging on it, and that four landings in that building have hooks in plasterboard and that on one of them somebody has used a plasterboard anchor.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and eighty days and has not been read.**

---
