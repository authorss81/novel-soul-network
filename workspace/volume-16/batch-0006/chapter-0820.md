# Chapter 820 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where A Green Book Was On Sixty-Seven Lines Before That Evening And On Sixty-Seven Lines After It Went Down, And Where About Nine People Came Up A Stair And Nobody Said A Thing To Her Face And The Tin Was Not Opened

**A Wednesday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished on a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people came up a stair and about four of them stayed the hour. No line went into the green book that evening and nobody in that room used a word for that, and the tin on the same table did not move.**

---

Thirteen names with thirteen dates went onto that Wednesday's board, and the thirteenth was entered at twenty to five. That Wednesday the shutter came down at ten, and ten is the hour it comes down at on all ten of this stretch of days.

**Line eighteen is one thousand two hundred and thirty-two days, a hundred and seventy-six weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand one hundred and twenty days, a hundred and sixty weeks to the day. What is still owed is one thousand two hundred and four days, a hundred and seventy-two weeks to the day.**

---

**Behind the chair of the woman of about sixty a place has stood empty since a week in the spring, and it is three hundred and ninety-two days old, which is fifty-six weeks to the day, and it is set down here and on no other page of this stretch of days.**

The ninth chair is against that wall with its back to the whole of the room and it did not move on that Wednesday and its mover is named on no page of this stretch of days.

Nobody in that room asked her about the place and nobody in that room has asked her about it in about fifty-six weeks, and four people in that room said afterwards that they had wanted to and that the wanting had not turned into a question.

---

**The count was said once, in about nine seconds, in her face, by the woman of about sixty, and it was sixty-five of which sixty, and she said it the way she says the date at the top of a page.**

Nobody in that room said a number back to her and nobody in that room asked her anything else, and the green book on that table was on sixty-seven lines when the room opened and on sixty-seven lines when the room emptied.

A man of about thirty-four said in about nine seconds that he had had four words in his mouth since about half past six and had not said them, and that the four words were the only four he had come with.

She said in four seconds that he had said that on the last two occasions as well, and that a person who says it three times has said it, and that about nine people in this city have said the four words and that this is not a room for the count.

**Nobody in that room said whether the book would open at some other Wednesday and nobody in that room has said so since, and this page does not say it either.**

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four said the only thing anybody said that evening about the green book, and she said it in about four seconds and she said it to the door.**

She said that a book that a person keeps is not the same object as a book that a room keeps, and that about four weeks ago somebody put a page into it without saying so and that the room has not worked out what it now owns.

A man of about fifty-two said in four seconds that the room owned the page and not the book, and that this is the distinction that a woman of about forty-eight has been keeping in a register for about nine years without being asked to.

**Nobody in that room disagreed with him and nobody in that room told the woman of about forty-eight, and four people in that room have said since that this was the correct decision and that it was still a wrong one.**

---

**And four people stayed the hour and about nine of them had come up, and the whole of what the four of them did with the hour was argue about about nine things that are not the four lines.**

A woman of about thirty-one said that the notice on the wall in that second district should come down, and that nobody in that room had the standing to take it down, and that four people have stood in front of it this month waiting for somebody with the standing.

A man of about fifty-two said that the notice is a notice, and that a notice coming down before the date on it is a thing a person does with a hammer, and that nine people in this city have wanted to and none of them has.

**He said that the correct thing is a second notice with a later date on it, and that a second notice is a thing that body has never issued in about eleven years, and that nobody in that room was going to issue one on a Wednesday evening.**

A woman of about twenty-nine said in nine seconds that the second notice is the answer, that it has been available since before the spring, that four people have worked it out, and that nobody wanted to be the one to sign it.

Nobody in that room signed anything and nobody in that room asked anybody else to sign anything, and nobody in that room said the word sign aloud after the second mention.

---

**And a man of about thirty-eight said one more thing about that evening which is about the last time anybody will be able to say it.**

He said that a body of about nine people with no permanent staff can be handed a thing and can pass it on, and that this is what it did tonight, and that this is what it has done for about eleven years.

He said that what it cannot do is hold anything for anybody, and that four hundred people in one district have found that out in about eleven weeks, and that this is the first time in about eleven years that the thing has been found out from the outside.

**He said that the woman of about sixty has been sitting in that room for nineteen years and has never once seen anybody leave it, and that a page went into her book in about eleven weeks without her asking for it, and that nobody in that room knows what that is.**

Nobody in that room said that it was a change and nobody in that room said that it was not one.

---

**And what four of the nine people said on the stair afterwards was about nine sentences, and about four of the nine said something and about four said nothing.**

A woman of about thirty-four said that the sentence on that sheet is the first one anybody has written in about eleven years which does not have a number in it, and that a sentence with no number in it cannot be checked and cannot be argued with.

A man of about thirty-eight said that nine people in this city will repeat it badly on purpose, and that he would say it to the first person who asked him and he has not been asked.

**Nobody on that stair said the eleven-word sentence and nobody on that stair asked anybody else to say it, and it is in nine mouths in this city and nobody knows how many can say it.**

---

**And the last thing on that Wednesday was a man of about thirty-one who had walked up that stair at about half past six, gone down it at about half past seven, and had four words in his mouth and did not say them anywhere.**

He has said since that he came up to give the count to somebody else and that he found out at the top of the stair that the count belongs to her and not to him.

He has said that this is the second time in about eleven weeks that he has arrived somewhere with a thing to say and found that the thing is somebody else's, and that he has not decided whether that is a good arrangement or a bad one.

**Nobody in this city has asked him which he thinks it is and he has not said.**

---

**And the rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where a set of about eleven metres of shelving in a storeroom had been put up without any wall fixings at the ends.**

He took the whole run off the wall, let an upright into each end, anchored both of those, braced the back across, and hung the run off the new uprights rather than off the brackets alone.

Thirty-one pounds. He told the man that a run carried on two brackets at its ends is a cantilever for as long as nobody leans on it, and that four runs in that store are carried that way and that on one of them an upright has begun to tear out of the plaster.

---

**And the rest of that Wednesday evening was a corridor on the same floor, in which two people who have a key to a room of their own each stood at about four feet apart for about nine minutes and neither of them said one word to the other.**

He has said since that she came out of that room at about half past seven carrying nothing and that she was not carrying anything and that he has known that she carries nothing since about four weeks ago.

He has also said that he had four sentences ready and did not say them, and that about two of the four were about a decision he made in a different city a long time ago, and that he did not say them because she had not asked.

She has said since that she did not ask because she was not going to, and that a person who does not ask does not get the answer, and that she had worked that out a long time before this corridor.

**Nobody thanked anybody in that corridor and nobody in that corridor said they were sorry and neither of them said one word to the other, and nine metres of corridor is the whole of what that floor has between their two rooms.**

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and about two hours of work in a first district, where a set of about eleven metres of roofing ladder hooks had been set into a fascia that had been replaced and not the hooks.**

He took eleven hooks off, found the old fixing holes in a fascia that was no longer there, filled them, and set the hooks into the rafter ends where they had been when the roof was framed.

Twenty-six pounds. He told the man that a hook in a replaced fascia is a hook in about nine years of weather, and that four runs of hooks in that district are in replaced fascias and that one of them has let a ladder go in about four winters.

---

**And the second fault on that roof was a ladder on the same building with two rungs of a different stock, which is a ladder repaired by somebody who did not have the timber to match it.**

He took the two rungs out, cut two from the same stock as the rest, and let them into the side rails with a scarf and a length of resin and a clamp, and sanded the treads true again.

Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that two rungs of different wood in a ladder is a ladder somebody has put back in service in about nine seconds, and that four ladders in that district have mixed rungs and that one of them has a rung that is not the same length as the one it replaced.

---

**And a gutter on that building had been fixed with a bracket screwed into a rafter tail that had been cut in two by a fitter.**

He replaced the bracket, filled the two old holes, cut a length of timber the size of the rafter tail and let it into the plumb of the rafter, and refitted the bracket through that into sound timber.

Twenty-four pounds. He told the man that a bracket in a cut rafter tail is a bracket in two half rafters, and that four buildings in that district have one and that on one of them the tail has separated about an inch from the wall plate.

---

**And a handrail on the stairs of that same building had been fitted with brackets that were set out from the wall far enough that the rail could be turned a quarter of a turn in its own fixing.**

He took the rail off, packed each bracket back so the rail stands square to the stair, and refitted it, and ran it along its whole length to be sure.

Nineteen pounds. He told the man that a rail that can be turned in its own brackets has been fixed to the wall and not to the stair, and that four rails in that building are like it and that one of them has loose brackets on both sides.

---

**And the fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from half past six, her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for three hundred and three days and has not been read, and nobody has asked her one question about it and nobody is going to.**

---
