# Chapter 789 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where Four People Said In About Nine Seconds Each That It Was Not Their Decision To Make, And Where A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Then Said The Thing Nobody Had Said, Which Is That There Is No List Of The Four Hundred And There Never Has Been

**A Friday started at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district. Four people said it was not theirs to decide. A woman of about twenty-nine then said the plainest thing anybody has said in that room in about eleven years, and it was an absence, and nothing was uncovered.**

---

Ten names went onto that Friday's board with ten dates against them, and the tenth of them at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten, as it comes down at ten on all ten days of this stretch of days.

**Line seventeen is one thousand two hundred and eighteen days, one hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day. The post at that corridor end is nine hundred and ninety-four days, one hundred and forty-two weeks to the day.**

**Eight hundred and twenty-six days is the separation, one hundred and eighteen weeks to the day, and it is in a one-line box about sixteen months old, and it is the only one of those three figures that has nothing to do with any of this.**

The four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand four hundred and forty-six days, two hundred and six weeks and four days.

---

**The question put to that room on that Friday was one question, asked in nine words, and it was not answered for half an hour.**

The question was: what happens to four hundred people on a Tuesday.

Nobody in that room answered it. Four people said in about nine seconds each that it was not their decision to make, and all four gave the same reason, which is that four hundred people are not theirs.

A man of about thirty-four who keeps the haulier books said that he carries for that district and does not decide for it. A woman of about thirty-eight said that she signs things for that district and does not arrange things for it. A man of about thirty-one said the arrangement belongs to whoever receives what the district stops doing, and that he does not receive it. A man of about fifty-two said that the form has a box on it and that the box is empty and that an empty box is not an answer.

**Nobody argued with any of the four. Nine minutes went past and four sentences started in that room and not one of them finished, and that is the fourth time in four years that has happened on a Friday.**

---

**And then a woman of about twenty-nine, who is one of the four who said no to that district's notice on a Tuesday three weeks ago, said the thing nobody in that room had said.**

She has said one sentence in this city in about three weeks and it was that she did not have anybody to ask. She was not asked anything since, and she was not asked anything on that Friday, and she said this unasked, in nine seconds, to the middle of the table.

"**They are not going to be told,**" she said. "**Not one of them. And it is not because anybody has decided not to tell them. It is because there is no list of them to tell, and there never has been, and there is not going to be one made now in three weeks.**"

**Nobody in that room said she had it wrong. A man of about thirty-four said, inside nine seconds, that a person can be told a thing without a list, and she said yes, and that he could stand at that gate at five in the morning and say it out loud to nine people at a time, and that four hundred people going through that gate is forty-five mornings, and that nobody has forty-five mornings.**

---

**Nobody in that room looked for a list on that Friday, and two people in that room had already looked, four years ago, and had found a register with nine names in it, and four of the nine had not used it since the spring.**

The register is not a list of the four hundred. It was never meant to be and four people in this city have said so in four different rooms. A man of about thirty-eight said that the nine names in it are the nine people who come through that gate on a Monday, and that four hundred people use the line across three districts and half a bus route, and that a Monday is not a place anybody keeps a record of.

**A man of about fifty-two said, in about nine seconds, that this was the only thing anybody had said in about eleven years that could not be answered with a form, and a woman of about thirty-four said that it could be answered with forty-five mornings and that nobody has forty-five mornings, and the two of them looked at each other for four seconds and neither of them said anything else.**

---

**And then the absence was on the page of that room, and the sentence that put it there is a sentence of nine words, and it is the only sentence about the four hundred that anybody has ever written down.**

Nobody wrote it at the time. It was written on the Friday after, by the man of about fifty-two, on a docket book page that is not the docket book on the shelf behind the counter, and he read it back to the woman of about twenty-nine in about nine seconds and asked her whether that was what she had said.

She said it was, and that she had said it better on the Friday and worse afterwards, and that people always say it better the first time.

**Two people in that room have said since that the missing list of four hundred people is the real problem, and four have said that the nine weeks is the real problem, and both of those statements are in this city and neither of them has been withdrawn.**

---

**And nobody in that room could say who would write a list of four hundred people, and nobody has been asked.**

A man of about thirty-four said the answer is whoever already has them, and four people in that room said that nobody already has them, and one of those four said the word nobody out loud and then said it again lower, as though it had not been said properly the first time.

A woman of about thirty-four said, in nine seconds, that a list would be a thing that gets used, and that a thing that gets used four hundred people without their being asked is worse than no list at all, and that two people in that room had thought that before and about two had not.

**Nobody asked her who the two were. She did not say, and four people in that room have since said they would not have asked her either.**

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for two hours, said nothing at the table, and was asked nothing at the table.**

He has said since that he could have put forty names on that sheet in nine minutes, and that he knows every one of the forty would have been somebody else's brother or somebody's mother, and that four hundred people who use a thing are not a set of people a person can carry in his head.

He has also said that he could name the man of about forty-seven, and that naming the man is the easy half, and that nobody in that room asked him for the other half and he did not offer it.

---

**The rest of that Friday was a counter until about six and forty minutes on a window catch on a first floor in a second district, and a shop that shuts at ten.**

The catch had been dropped into a sash that had swollen with the wet and had worked loose in four years. He eased the sash, replaced the catch, planed nothing and glued nothing, and told the woman that a swollen sash is a thing that happens in four winters and that he would rather come back in two years than put a new frame in now. Fifteen pounds and forty minutes.

The woman said the window had been in that frame before she was born and that nobody had ever told her the wood had swollen, and he said that wood swells and the telling does not make it stop, and that a person who has had a window for forty years is the only person who can tell how long it has been doing it.

---

**And the woman whose sash he had come for asked him a question about the other window, and he answered it, and neither of them thought of it as a question and both of them have thought of it as one since.**

The other window faces north and has not been painted since before the spring, and the woman said that she had been meaning to do it and had not, and that the reason she had not was that doing it would mean moving the plant table.

He said that a sash that swells is held by paint, and that a sash that cannot move at the bottom is held by a wet brushstroke and nothing else, and that a plant table is a thing a person can move for the length of a morning.

She moved the plant table that evening after he had gone, and did the window at the weekend, and the window works, and she has said since that the man who came about a catch in the other window got a window painted for nothing, and that she has not told him and is not going to.

**He has said since that he did not do the paint and that if he had done the paint she would have been told by two people that he had, and that the two people would have been the man who sold her the paint and herself.**

The catch he replaced is still working. The man has not been back about it and has telephoned nobody, and the shop has not telephoned him, and the file for that flat is a page with one line on it and the line is closed.

---

**And the register with nine names in it was looked at on that Friday by three people, in that room, and the looking took four minutes and produced nothing and was not the first time.**

The register is a hard sheet on a clipboard. Nine lines. Eight of them have a date against the last time that person came through that gate and one of them has no date at all, and that one has had no date since the spring.

A man of about thirty-four said that the line with no date is a man who came once and has not come since, and that the man came once because somebody asked him to come, and that a man who is asked to come once and does not come again is a man who decided something that day.

The woman of about thirty-four said that the eight dates are not a record of anybody and are a record of a door being open, and that the two are the same fact and that the city has spent about nine years calling it a register.

**The register has been on that clipboard for four years and nobody has ever taken it off, and about two people in this city have said that a clipboard is a thing a person carries and a list is a thing a person keeps, and that this one has been carried and not kept.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from about half past six with her door shut, and behind her the eighth of eight has been on a back shelf for two hundred and thirty days.**
