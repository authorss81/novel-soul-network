# Chapter 782 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six And A Room Above A Line In Saltmarket From About Half Past Six Where Nothing Whatever Was Decided, A Man Of About Forty-Four Asked One Question And Was Given An Address, A Woman Of About Sixty Said A Thing Unasked, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Came Down A Stair With Somebody Else's Handwriting In Her Hand

**A Wednesday opened at a bench, closed at a counter, and went up one floor above a line in Saltmarket at about half past six, where nothing whatever was decided. A man asked a question and was answered with an address. A woman said a thing nobody had asked her about, and nobody asked her anything else.**

---

The board for that Wednesday took nine names and nine dates, and the last name was written down at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten, and the ninth chair in the room above that line was against the wall before he got there and was against the wall when he left it.

**Line eighteen is one thousand one hundred and fifty-five days, one hundred and sixty-five weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and forty-three days, one hundred and forty-nine weeks to the day.**

**What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and twenty-seven days, one hundred and sixty-one weeks to the day, and it has been owed since before the spring and nobody has put a name against it and nobody is going to.**

The space behind the woman of about sixty's chair has stood empty three hundred and fifteen days, which is forty-five weeks to the day, and it is not on a form and it is not in a book and nobody in that room mentions it.

---

**Nine people came into that room between about half past six and a quarter to eight, and not one of them came about a Wednesday.**

Two of them came with a form and wanted to know where to put it. One came with a fault on a stair light and was told that it was the landlord's. One came in order to sit down, which anybody may do. One came because she has been coming for nineteen years. The rest came for the reason people come, which is that the room is above a line and the line runs where they are going.

**The woman of about sixty did not say a number. She has said one on four occasions that anybody in this city can remember and none of them was in that room, and nobody at that table wondered about it then and nobody has wondered about it since.**

---

**A man of about forty-four had been in that room before, about nine times, and on that Wednesday he asked the same question he has asked twice, and the reason he asks it is a shift and not a view.**

The procedure found on a Thursday four weeks ago has four steps. Three of them have a number in them. The fourth has a word where a number should be, and the word is the word the card in that second district is printed with.

"**I am not asking what the word means,**" he said. "**I have read it four times and I could tell you what it means. I am asking who the answer goes to.**"

The woman of about sixty said, in four seconds, that the answer went to the door at the end of the passage, and that the door at the end of the passage was a first floor and a set of shelves, and that the man who came to that door on a Tuesday and on a Thursday was not the man who came on a Monday.

**Nobody argued with him about the word. Four sentences went across that table about the door and the man and the days, and the man of about forty-four wrote the address on the back of his own hand, which he has done twice before, and he has never used the address for anything at all.**

---

**A man of about thirty-four sat in the eighth chair, which is where he sits, and was asked nothing and asked nothing, and that is the whole of what he did in that room on that Wednesday.**

He was told afterwards by somebody that it was the sixth Wednesday. He said he knew it was, and that there was nothing in the room for him to be the sixth of, and that he would keep coming anyway.

---

**And then the woman of about sixty said the thing, and nobody had asked her anything at the time and nobody asked her anything afterwards.**

She has been in that room nineteen years. She was in it before it was above that line. She keeps the book in the green cover and nobody has ever asked her to keep anything on behalf of anybody who is going.

"**A book is a thing people bring things to,**" she said. "**I have never once been asked to keep one for anybody who was going. In nineteen years nobody has come in here and said, we are going, and here is a book, and you will put the going in it.**"

It took her about nine seconds. She said it to the table and not to anybody, and then she went back to a sheet she had been looking at before anybody spoke.

---

**Nobody followed it up. Nobody asked her what a book kept for a leaving would have on its first page, and nobody asked her what she would do with one, and nine minutes later somebody asked her about a stair light.**

A man of about twenty-two was on the landing with a cylinder out of a lock for forty minutes and he heard the whole of it. He has said since that he wanted to ask her one question about it and did not, and went down the stair with the cylinder in his pocket and put the money in the box on the counter and went home.

**He has thought about it since. He has not gone back up. He has told one person in this city that he ought to have asked her, and that person did not ask him what the question was and he did not say.**

---

**A man of about fifty-two was in that room on that Wednesday with a form he had been given to fill in and had not filled in, and he asked the woman of about thirty-four, who was on the stair at the time, whether the door at the end of the passage was the same door the answer goes to.**

She said it was, and then said that it was not the same thing, and that one of the two was a room and the other was shelves and a woman who came in on a Tuesday, and that nobody had ever asked the shelves.

---

**At a quarter to eight a woman of about thirty-four came down that stair with a sheet of paper in her hand, and the writing on it was not hers.**

It was not a form and it was not that district's notice and it was not the sheet with thirteen lines on it, which is in a drawer in a room off a road. It was one sheet with a box at the bottom of it, a ruled line under the box, and nothing at all printed above the line.

She came down the last four steps without looking at it, went out into the street, and stood for about nine seconds in the doorway of that building. Then she walked toward the second district.

**Two people in that room saw her come down. One of them told the other. Neither of them has said since that they know what was on it, and one of them has said that he would not have looked if he had known.**

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and fifty minutes on a cylinder in a lock on a first floor, and a shop that shuts at ten.**

He had the cylinder out by ten past seven and the lock open by a quarter to eight. It took nine pounds and it took a key cut at a shop on a road off Saltmarket that was shut, and the key was cut on this counter in four minutes with a file and a vice.

The man who had let him in said that the last man through that door had done it in about nine seconds with a pick, and said it with no expression at all, and then said that he was not going to have that conversation twice in one evening.

He told the man, on the way out, that the strike was a worn one and not a forced one, and that a forced strike on that door would have taken four minutes and this took fifty, and that the difference between those two things is the difference between a door and a wall.

---

**And there was a second thing in that building that evening which took him longer than the lock did and which nobody in the world will ever hear about except the two people it happened to.**

The caretaker of that building had a bell on the first landing that had not rung since before the spring, and had been re-wired by a man who did not come back, and the wire was run outside the casing where it could be seen from the yard. He took the bell off, took the wire out of the wall in about six inches of plaster, found that the wire had been nicked in two places by a nail somebody had driven through the wall for a shelf, and dressed both nicks.

He told the caretaker, in the passage, that a bell is not a wire, and that whoever wired it had wired a wire, and that the two had got into the same hole in the wall and had been getting into each other's way for four years. The caretaker said that whoever wired it had been a man in a hurry and had said so at the time, and he said that a man in a hurry is not a reason and is a fact.

He rewired the bell inside the casing, refixed the button, and rang it once. Nothing happened on any floor. He said that was correct, because a bell on a first landing of a building where nobody answers a bell ought not to sound anywhere, and that a bell which sounds into an empty stairwell is a noise and not a bell.

**He charged fourteen pounds and did not put it on an invoice, because he was not asked to and because a man who puts a fourteen-pound line on an invoice for a thing nobody asked for is a man who is asking for something.**

The caretaker gave him a cup of tea out of a tin that had a spoon soldered to the lid, and asked him, not looking at him, whether the woman in the room at the end of that passage had said anything about the light. He said that the light was the landlord's and that he had not been in the room. The caretaker said yes, and that it was a shame, and let him go.

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from about half past six with her door shut, and behind her on the back shelf is the eighth of eight, and it has been there two hundred and twenty-one days and it is not going to be read this month either.**
