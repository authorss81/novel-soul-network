# Chapter 785 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where A Shop With Members Was Asked To Put One Of Its Own Behind A Line At Five In The Morning And Said Yes In About Nine Seconds And Then Said What It Would Cost In The Same Nine

**A Monday started at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district. A shop with members was asked a question it has no power to answer alone, answered it anyway, and named a price in the same breath. Nobody argued about either.**

---

The board for that Monday took twelve names and twelve dates, and the twelfth name was entered at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten, and the street outside that shop on a Monday is the same street as on a Wednesday except that two of the three buses stop before nine.

**The four rooms off that service road and the one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and forty-two days, two hundred and six weeks to the day.**

**Line sixteen is one thousand two hundred and thirty-two days, one hundred and seventy-six weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand and eight days, one hundred and forty-four weeks to the day, one corner torn, eight unfinished, and not two of them have ever been compared.**

The hold on nine crates and the floor they stand on is one thousand and seventy-five days, one hundred and fifty-three weeks and four days, in the second of the four towns.

---

**The shop is a cooperative. It is on a road in a third district, it has nine people behind a counter, and it has a back yard behind that counter with a gate of its own on the yard.**

It is not called anything in this city. It is referred to by what it is, which is a shop its members own, and it works because it was built with different rules from the shops around it. Four of the nine who stand behind that counter do the early shift, and they are on that road at five in the morning because they open it.

**About four of its members work shifts, and none of them can agree to anything on a Tuesday, and everybody in that room knew it before anybody asked. The reason is a meeting, and the meeting is on a Thursday, and the meeting is on the Thursday of next week.**

---

**The woman of about thirty-four asked, in about nine seconds, and she asked one person, and the person she asked has kept that shop's minutes for six years and has never once answered for it.**

The question was whether the shop could put one of its own behind the release line at that yard for a number of weeks.

The woman said, in four seconds, that the shop could not answer that in a room, and that what the shop could do in a room was say what the meeting would say, and that the meeting was on the Thursday of next week, and that four people in that room had not waited for a meeting in about eleven years.

**Nobody in that room argued with her about the meeting. A man of about thirty-four said that a meeting is a thing that happens and a Tuesday is a thing that does not, and the woman said that the shop would say on Thursday whatever it said on that Monday, and that she was telling them now so that nobody would have to sit through a meeting to hear it.**

---

**And then she said the two things, and she said them in the same nine seconds and in the same breath, and the first one was yes.**

"**We can hold it for nine weeks,**" she said. "**That is what we can hold it for. Not eleven and not ten, and I want to be exact about why it is nine.**"

**Nobody at that table liked the nine weeks and two of them did not like the price, and nobody argued about either, and four of them wrote nine down and two of them wrote the price down and nobody wrote both.**

---

**The reason it is nine is about the people who are already on that road at five, and she gave it in nine words.**

Four of the shop's members open that counter at five. In the ninth week of a run like this one, two of the four are on a different shift, because of a thing that happens every year in the autumn with two of their families, and a shop does not put a member on a gate at five in a week when that member has been up since four.

**She said it flatly and with no complaint in it, and she has said since that she is not complaining about the two families, and that she would think less of a shop that complained about two families in a room full of strangers.**

---

**The price is five pounds the morning, five mornings, and about nine weeks of it. She said the whole of it in nine seconds and did not say it kindly and did not say it unkindly.**

She said it the way a person reads out a rate. She said that the shop was not lending anybody and was not absorbing anything, and that the money has to come from somewhere, and that the only place in this city it could come from is the thing that has been asked for.

**A man of about thirty-four said, inside nine seconds, that nobody in that room had the authority to spend that, and that he was not going to be the one who wrote it down, and the woman of about thirty-four said that she was not going to write it down either and that it was not hers to write down.**

The five pounds a morning went into that room and stayed in it. About four people have since described it as a price. About two have described it as the answer.

---

**The woman of about forty-one who keeps that shop's minutes said four words before anybody thanked her, and nobody in that room had thanked her, and she said them to the door rather than to the table.**

"**Do not thank us.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody in that room has thanked that shop at any point since, and two people have said that they nearly did and did not, and one of them has said that the four words were the reason.

**She has said since that she said the four words because that shop has been thanked before, for other things, and that a shop that is thanked for a thing stops being able to name the price of the next one.**

---

**And then the difference between nine and eleven was named out loud, and it was named by the woman of about thirty-four, and she named it in about nine seconds, and it is two weeks.**

"**Eleven was a figure four people guessed at on a Friday and I have been saying for nine days that it was the wrong one,**" she said. "**Nine is a figure that four people here have agreed to, and one of them has said no and three of them have named a number, and the two weeks between the two figures is not a gap anybody is going to argue back to eleven.**"

Nobody argued back to eleven. A man of about thirty-eight said that the nine weeks is smaller than everybody wanted and larger than anybody costed, and the woman of about thirty-four said yes, and that both of those are true at the same time, and that she was not going to choose between them in a room.

---

**Nobody in that room thanked the shop. The shop did not expect to be thanked and said so in advance. The man of about twenty-two was four feet from the end of that table and said nothing and heard all of it.**

He has said since that he wanted to ask what happens in the tenth week, and that he did not ask it, and that the reason was that he had been in a room where a man said six and a shop said nine and a woman said eleven, and that those three numbers do not add up to anything, and that he did not want to be the one who pointed that out.

---

**The rest of that Monday was a counter until about six and an hour and a half on two hinges at a gate in a second district, and a shop that shuts at ten.**

The top hinge had been dropped into a soft wall for years and the pin had worn into the plaster. He packed the hole with a length of pipe, set the hinge, and told the man that it would want looking at in two years and not before. Twenty-two pounds and about ten minutes of the hour and a half in the hinge itself.

The man argued for about nine seconds and lost, and then asked whether the gate would swing true afterwards, and he said that it would swing as true as a gate in a wall like that swings, and that is a wall and not a hinge, and that anybody who tells him otherwise is selling a hinge.

He marked the wall with a pencil cross above the second hinge and wrote the date beside it, and the man photographed it with a thing he carries in a pocket, and he told him that a pencil cross lasts two winters and a photograph lasts longer and that neither of them is a repair.

---

**And the second hour of that Monday was spent on a thing that was not on any list, and the man who brought it in did not know it was unusual and would have said so if he had been asked.**

The gate on that second site has a frame of soft brick, and the top hinge had been set into it by a man who had a drill in about nine years and used it because it was there. The pin had gone round in a circle in the mortar and the mortar had gone round with it, so the whole hinge turned rather than the door.

He took the hinge off, cut the mortar out with a chisel and a hammer in about ten minutes, packed the hole with a length of pipe cut to the depth, set the hinge on the pipe, and told the man that the door would then swing on a hinge instead of turning on a hole.

The man said that the door had been like that for years. He said yes, and that a door that has been like that for years is a door that has been costing money every year it is like that, and that the man has been paying for a hole.

**Twenty-two pounds was the whole of it, and ten of those pounds were the two hours, and the man said that ten pounds for ten minutes was the most he had ever been charged for anything and he said it in a tone that had nothing in it at all.**

He wrote the date on the inside of the frame in pencil where it will not come off, and told the man that if the same man came back in about five years and the date was not there, then it had not been done and should not be paid for twice.

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from about half past six with her door shut, and behind her the eighth of eight has been on a back shelf for two hundred and thirty-one days.**
