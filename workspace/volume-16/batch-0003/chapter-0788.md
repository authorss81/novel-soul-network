# Chapter 788 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where Four People Explained Separately Why A Practice Had Never Been Written Down And A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Wrote It On The Back Of A Sheet In About Four Seconds And Gave It To Nobody

**A Thursday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district. Four people gave four separate reasons why a practice had never been written down. A woman turned a sheet over, wrote it down in four seconds, and gave it to nobody.**

---

The board for that Thursday carried nine names and nine dates, and the last name was taken at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten, and the four rooms off that service road stood at one thousand four hundred and forty-five days, two hundred and six weeks and three days.

**That card on end in the rail by the door of the fourth of those rooms is one thousand four hundred and forty-nine days, two hundred and seven weeks to the day, and the rail takes two and there is one thing in it and there has been one thing in it since before the spring.**

**Line thirteen is one thousand three hundred and sixteen days, one hundred and eighty-eight weeks to the day. Line fifteen is one thousand two hundred and sixty days, one hundred and eighty weeks to the day.**

The nineteen, last line in use, is one thousand one hundred and forty-one days, one hundred and sixty-three weeks to the day.

---

**The thing that had to be done on that Thursday was the smallest thing anybody in this city has been asked to do in about eleven years, and it took four seconds to do and an hour to get to.**

What had to be done was this: a man holds a release line at a yard. He has held it for about eleven years. Four hundred people use it. Nobody in this city has it written down anywhere, and the reason is not secrecy. The reason is that nobody has ever needed it in writing until the morning somebody needed it in writing.

**A man of about thirty-four said, in about nine seconds, that the reason is that there has never been a moment when not having it written down cost anybody anything, and that this is that moment, and that there is no more ceremony in it than that.**

---

**And then four people in that room gave four reasons, and the reasons were given separately, about nine seconds apart, and nobody in that room knew the other three had given one.**

A man of about thirty-four who keeps the haulier books said that the day it goes in writing is the day somebody asks him why it was not in writing for the eleven years before, and that he does not have an answer he would want to give.

A woman of about thirty-eight said that a name on a sheet is a thing that can be carried to somebody else, and that she has signed nine things for that district in four years and that eight of the nine were about a bin.

A man of about thirty-one said that the man would find out that he had been written down, and that he would then be, for the rest of his working life, a man who is in a drawer somewhere, and that four people in that room have thought about that for four years and have not said it out loud before.

**A man of about fifty-two said nothing, because he had said the whole of his position on the Wednesday in nine seconds, and four people in that room did not ask him for a fifth reason.**

---

**Nobody in that room organised those four reasons. They were not a decision and they were not a wall. Four people in that city have said since that they were four people arriving at the same place at different times for different reasons, which is a different thing, and it is not a conspiracy, and it has never been called one.**

The man of about thirty-one said the thing that has been said most about it since, and he said it inside nine seconds: nobody in that room had ever discussed it with the other three, and not one of the four has ever mentioned it to the other three since, and that is either four coincidences or one habit that nobody taught anybody.

**Nobody answered him. A man of about thirty-four said it was four coincidences and that he would rather it was, and that the difference did not matter this week.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four turned the sheet with thirteen lines on it over and wrote on the back of it, and it took her four seconds, and she did not read it out and she did not ask anybody to look at it.**

The sheet with thirteen lines on it is the sheet that district's notice went round on. Nine of the lines have a name and a tick. Four of the lines have a name and nothing after the name. It is in a drawer in that room and it has been in that drawer since a Tuesday three weeks ago.

On the back of it she wrote four lines. The first is a man of about forty-seven. The second is a yard in a second district. The third is that he has held it for about eleven years. The fourth is that it takes about eleven seconds.

**She did not write a date. She did not write a reason. She did not write a signature and she did not put anybody else's name on it, and there is no box on that sheet for any of those things.**

---

**Nobody in that room asked to see the back of it. Two people said afterwards that they had wanted to and had not, and both of them have said since that they are glad they did not.**

A man of about thirty-four said that the whole of the city now has the practice in writing on the back of a sheet in a drawer, and the woman of about thirty-four said yes, and that a drawer is a place, and that about eleven years is the first time anything in this city has been in a place.

**She gave it to nobody. It went back into the drawer with the rest of that drawer, which is a docket book and nine other papers, and she shut the drawer, and four people in that room watched her do it.**

---

**That is the whole of what was done. For the first time in about eleven years there is a piece of paper in this city that says in writing who holds a release line at a yard, and it is in a drawer in a room off a road in a second district, and it is not a document of anything, and it has no heading, and it is on the back of a list of names.**

A man of about thirty-four said, in about nine seconds, that the institution does not know about it, and the woman of about thirty-four said that it does not, and that it is the first thing she has written in four years that the body does not know about, and that she has not told him so in order to keep it.

---

**A man of about twenty-two was in that room for an hour and fifty minutes and said nothing and was asked nothing.**

He was told afterwards, on the landing, by the woman of about thirty-four, in about nine seconds, and he asked her one question, which was why she had not given it to him.

"**Because you would have given it to somebody,**" she said. "**Not to be clever with it. You would have given it to the man, because he is the man, and he would have told somebody, and then it would be a thing that four hundred people had been told about.**"

He did not answer that. He has said since that she had it wrong and that he would have taken it straight to the yard, and that he has not done it, and that he has not gone back up.

---

**And the man of about forty-seven was told about it on the Friday at that gate, by a man of about thirty-four, and he did not ask to see it.**

He was told in nine words at about five in the morning, with nine people going through the gate behind him. He said one thing, which was that it was about time somebody had, and then he said that he was not going to ask to see it, and that anybody who wanted him to ask to see it could come and ask him again in about a year.

**Nobody has asked him again. Four people in this city have said that they would, and two of them have said it to each other, and neither of the two has been to that gate at five in the morning since the spring.**

---

**The rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and an hour on a door frame at a shop in a third district, which was out of square by about a sixteenth of an inch, which is a thing he has now told three people about this month.**

He packed it, planed it, and told the man the door would shut properly and that the frame was the cause and that the door was not at fault. Nineteen pounds, and the man asked him to write it down somewhere so that it would not happen again, and he said he would put it in that shop's own book, and did.

The man read the entry and said that the last entry in that book was four years old and was about a different door, and he said that was the best possible news about a book and went back to work.

---

**And the hour on that door frame was two hours when he started counting, and the reason was a piece of wood the size of a thumbnail under the packing that nobody had put there on purpose.**

He had the frame out of square by about a sixteenth of an inch and he had the packing out and the packing was sound, and behind the packing there was a wedge of a hardwood offcut, driven hard, that had been there long enough to have gone hard and dark at the edges.

He did not take it out. He took about a quarter of an inch off the jamb in four places with a chisel, eased the frame back by driving the wedges out a sixteenth at a time, and shimmed it, and then he put the packing back and drove the same wedges back in to hold what he had moved.

The man asked why he had put the wedge back. He said that the wedge was the only thing in that frame that had ever held anything still, and that removing it would have left him a repair he could not promise, and that a repair nobody can promise is not a repair.

**He charged nineteen pounds and said, on the way out, that the frame was not the second person to try to make that door square, and that the wedge was the first, and that both of them had failed for the same reason and neither of them knew about the other.**

The man has told three people on that road about it, and all three of them had told him it was the door.

---

**And the man of about fifty-two was the one who put the four reasons on the record, and he has said that he wrote them down in the order they happened and that the order is the finding.**

He wrote four lines on a page of his own, in a hand that is not a hand anybody else in that room recognises, and he dated it, and he did not put a heading on it, and he gave it to the woman of about thirty-four and not to the table.

She read it standing up and gave it back and said that it was four reasons and not a list, and that a list would have wanted them in an order, and that these were in the order that people said them.

He has said that the order is the only part of it that is worth anything, and that in two years somebody will want to know which of the four reasons came first, and that the answer will be that it depends which table you were sitting at.

**The page is in that drawer as well. He put it in himself, in front of two people, and neither of the two said anything about that either, and four people in this city have said that a man who puts a page in a drawer in front of witnesses is a man who expects to be asked about it later.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from about half past six with her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and twenty-nine days.**
