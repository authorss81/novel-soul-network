# Chapter 786 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where Somebody Who Refuses This City Everything For A Living Was Asked For An Opinion And Gave One In About Nine Seconds That The District Is Not The Thing To Be Worried About

**A Tuesday went from a bench to a counter and into a room off a road in a second district. A person came from a house that has refused this city everything for a living, was asked one question, and answered it in about nine seconds in a way nobody in that room could use and nobody threw away.**

---

The board for that Tuesday carried nine names and nine dates, and the last name was taken at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten, and the rail of two by the door of the fourth of those rooms stood against a wall with one card on end in it and had room for one more.

**The four rooms off that service road and the one behind the other three are at one thousand four hundred and forty-three days, two hundred and six weeks and one day.**

**Line thirteen is one thousand three hundred and fourteen days, one hundred and eighty-seven weeks and five days. What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and thirty-three days, one hundred and sixty-one weeks and six days.**

The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand and nine days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and one day, one corner torn, eight unfinished, not two compared.

---

**The house sent a person, and the person gave no name, and nobody asked for one. That is the way that house has always done it and it is not a mystery and has never been one.**

The person came at half past five on foot, sat down at the end of that table, and took nothing out. The woman of about thirty-four had asked on the Friday before, in nine words, on a pavement, and they had said yes on the pavement in four seconds. They came, and they said nothing else about it.

**Nine people in that city have been to that house and asked for a thing and got a sentence. Four of the nine were asked for a name and did not get one, and none of the four has ever made a thing of it.**

Nobody in that room knew what the person did. Nobody asked. A man of about thirty-one said afterwards, in nine seconds, that it did not matter, and a man of about thirty-four said that it mattered and that it was not the kind of thing that mattered in a room.

---

**The question was one question, and the woman of about thirty-four asked it in nine words, and she made sure that it was one.**

She did not ask whether the district was entitled to go, and she did not ask whether it should be stopped. She said: we have four hundred people who use a line at a yard and about nine weeks of somebody standing at it, and we are going to say the truth about what it is before it goes, and we would like to know what you make of that before we say it.

**The person at the end of the table answered inside nine seconds, and the answer was not about the district.**

"**You are all looking at the wrong one,**" they said. "**The district is a set of people and a set of shifts and they have given a date and the date is theirs. Four hundred people are not a district and they are not anybody's, and they are the thing with no arrangement at all.**"

---

**Nobody in that room argued with it. Four people wrote it down, and two of them have said since that it was the first sentence anybody had said in that room that had a person in it who could not be argued out of it.**

A man of about thirty-four said that four hundred people are in three districts and half a bus route and that no body anywhere has all of them, and the person said yes, and said that this was the sentence, and that it had taken about eleven weeks to arrive at.

A man of about thirty-eight said that the district was leaving in three weeks and that those four hundred people were the reason it would not be allowed to, and the person said in four seconds that he had that wrong, and gave no reason for having it wrong.

---

**And then the person said the second half, and the second half is the inconvenient part, and it went into that room and stayed in it and was not ignored.**

"**Stop getting them ready,**" they said. "**The moment four hundred people are told what they are losing by somebody who is not their district, the leaving stops being that district's business and becomes yours. And then that district will be able to stand in a room and say that somebody is holding it in, and they will be able to say it out loud, and nine people will believe it.**"

A woman of about thirty-four said, in about nine seconds, that those four hundred people are going to find out on a Tuesday all at once anyway, which is what she said on a Monday three weeks ago in front of nine people. The person said that she had it exactly, and that it would be worse out of the wrong mouth.

**Nobody in that room could say what to do with it. A man of about thirty-one said the two things could not both be true, and the person said that they could both be true, and that four people in that city had been living in the middle of it for four years.**

---

**Two people wrote the opinion down and neither of them wrote down the name of the person who gave it, and four people in that room have described it in four different ways since.**

One of the two wrote it as an instruction. The other wrote it as a warning. The man of about thirty-four asked the second one which he thought it was, and the second one said he did not know, and that both were on the same page.

**The person at the end of the table stayed forty minutes, took nothing away, put nothing on the table, and said nine words in total, and four of those words are the ones that are still in that room.**

---

**And what came out of that room on that Tuesday as a fact is that four hundred people were named in it as the thing that has no arrangement.**

Not as a number. As the thing. Four people in that room have said since that nobody had ever put those four hundred on a table in that building as the subject, and two of them have said that the reason is that four hundred is nobody's business, and one of the two said that this is the same sentence twice and got no answer.

A woman of about thirty-eight said, in about nine seconds, that those four hundred are nobody's business until a Tuesday, and that on a Tuesday they will be everybody's at once, and that nobody in that room has ever written down one of their names.

**Nobody in that room could produce a list. A man of about thirty-four said there is a register with about nine names in it and that four of the nine have not used it since the spring, and that the register is not a list of the four hundred and was never intended to be.**

---

**A woman of about thirty-six who has costed a workshop's heat for three winters asked the woman of about thirty-four one question at ten past seven. The question was eleven words long and the answer was about a cost.**

She asked what it would cost to tell four hundred people the truth before the line stopped.

The woman of about thirty-four said, in nine seconds, that it costs a person walking with a sheet and a person in a room with a chair for whoever comes, and that neither of those can be done on a Tuesday by people who have a job, and that the honest number is not a number anybody in that room can produce this week.

**She was not asked whether it ought to be done. She has said since that she was not asked it, and that she had a sentence ready about it that she did not get to use, and she has not said what the sentence was.**

---

**The rest of that Tuesday was a counter until about six and about fifty minutes on a lock at a gate in a third district, which had been left open behind the last person out twice in about a fortnight.**

He took the keep out, dressed the top edge of the bolt, and put in a washer that cost him about nine pence, and charged four pounds for the hour. The man said that was cheap, and he said it was, and that a washer is nine pence and an hour is an hour.

He wrote on the invoice that the bolt was not worn and the frame was out of true, and the man said that the last man who came had said the bolt was worn, and he said that the last man who came had not had it out.

---

**And the person at the end of that table was gone before anybody noticed, and the thing that was left on the table was a chair that had been moved and had not been put back.**

Nobody saw them leave. The woman of about thirty-four said afterwards that she had been looking at a sheet and that when she looked up the chair was out at the angle a person leaves a chair at when they are not coming back, and that she is the only person in this city who can read a chair.

She left it out for a while and then pushed it in, and about four people noticed her push it in and two of them have said that pushing a chair back in is a thing a person does when a room is finished, and that the room was not finished and the chair was.

**The man of about thirty-four said, in about nine seconds, that the person had said nine words and had stayed forty minutes, and that the ratio was wrong, and that he had expected the opposite.**

The woman of about thirty-four said that the opposite is what people expect from a person who has refused this city everything for a living, and that what actually happens is that the refusal is done in a sentence and the rest of the time is the cost of having come.

She has said that she does not know whether that is true. She has said that it is the sort of thing that is true often enough that a person who works with people says it, and that a person who has not worked with people should not.

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from about half past six with her door shut, and the eighth of eight is on the back shelf behind her, and it has been there two hundred and thirty-two days and has not been read.**
