# Chapter 790 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where Four People Brought Four Figures To One Table And One Number Went In To One Book At About Half Past Six And Was Given To Nobody, And A Date Three Weeks Off On A Notice That Is Still On A Wall

**A Monday began at a bench, went through a counter, and finished in a room off a road in a second district. Four people brought four figures. One number went into one book at about half past six and was given to nobody, and it is smaller than the one everybody wanted and larger than the one anybody costed.**

---

The board for that Monday took thirteen names and thirteen dates, and the thirteenth name was entered at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten, and it comes down at ten on every day of this stretch of days, and the thirteen names on that board were the most any day has carried since before the spring.

**The four rooms off that service road and the one behind the other three stand at one thousand four hundred and forty-nine days, two hundred and seven weeks to the day.**

**Line sixteen is one thousand two hundred and thirty-nine days, one hundred and seventy-seven weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand and fifteen days, one hundred and forty-five weeks to the day, one corner torn, eight unfinished, and not two of them have ever been laid against each other.**

The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and fifty-five days, one hundred and fifty weeks and five days.

---

**Four figures came into that room on that Monday, and they were four different kinds of figure, and only one of them was a number of weeks that anybody had ever written down.**

The first was eleven weeks, and it came out of a docket book on a shelf behind a counter, in a hand belonging to a man of about twenty-two, and it sat between two books about hinges, and it was costed on a Friday four weeks ago by four people guessing.

The second was six, and it came from the man of about forty-seven, in about nine seconds, and it is what he says he has, and nobody has asked him to explain it and he has not explained it.

The third was nine weeks and a price, and it came from a shop with members on a road in a third district, and it was said in nine seconds, and the price was five pounds the morning.

The fourth was four hundred people, and it came from a woman of about twenty-nine on the Friday before, and it is not a number of weeks and cannot be made into one.

**Four people in that room had brought a figure of their own before they came in. Two of them had told nobody what it was, and two had told somebody in a car park and were told they ought not to have.**

---

**The eleven weeks is the figure that was the plan of record, and it is in a book, and being in a book is the only reason it had any standing at all.**

A man of about thirty-four said, inside nine seconds, that a figure that is in a book can be pointed at and a figure that is in a mouth cannot, and that about eleven weeks had been in a book for four weeks and had been believed in by everybody in that room including the man who wrote it.

The woman of about thirty-four said that it was four people guessed at, and that a guess in a book is still a guess, and that the reason it had been believed in was not the book and was the four people.

**Nobody in that room said she had it wrong. A man of about thirty-eight said that eleven had been the number for four weeks and that four hundred people had been planning around it, and she said yes, and that is what had happened, and that it is not the same as it being true.**

---

**The man of about thirty-four then did the thing nobody in that room had done in four years, which was to put the four figures side by side, and it took him four minutes with a pen and the back of a docket sheet.**

He did not add them up. A woman of about thirty-eight said, in about nine seconds, that she was glad he had not, and he said that adding them would have produced a number about thirty-five weeks, and that thirty-five weeks is a number about four people and not a number about four hundred people, and that the two have never been on the same line in a book in this city and she should not start it.

**The four figures stayed on the back of that sheet in four columns for the rest of the evening, and two people in that room have said since that the four columns are the most honest document produced in this stretch of days, and four have said it is a piece of paper with four numbers on it.**

---

**And then the number that was agreed came out of that room in a sentence of nine words, and the sentence said which two of the four had been asked, and it is the only sentence anybody said this week that says both at once.**

A woman of about thirty-four said it. She said it at about twenty past six and she said it to the table and not to anybody.

"**About nine weeks, of about four people, of whom two have been asked and two have not,**" she said. "**The two who have been asked are the man of about forty-four and the woman of about thirty-one. The two who have not are the man of about thirty-eight and the man of about fifty-two, and the second of those has already refused me once this week and is not going to refuse me twice.**"

**Nobody in that room asked her to say it again, and two people wrote it down exactly as she said it, and two of them wrote it down with the two names at the bottom instead of in the middle.**

---

**The man of about thirty-four asked her, in about nine seconds, why nine and not eleven, and she gave the whole of it in one sentence, and that sentence is the movement.**

"**Because nine is what a shop with members will put in writing, and two of my four have named a number, and eleven is what four people guessed at on a Friday,**" she said. "**Nine is two weeks under what everybody wanted and two weeks over what anybody costed, and both of those are true this evening, and I have stopped choosing between them.**"

Nobody argued with that. A man of about thirty-eight said, to the middle of the table, that he was one of the two who had not been asked, and everybody in that room turned round. The woman of about thirty-four said that she knew and had said so on purpose, and that a number which names the two who have not been asked is the only version of the number that is any use.

**He did not say anything else. He has said since that being named as one of the two who have not been asked was the closest anybody has come to asking him, and that he does not know what he is going to do about it, and that he has about a fortnight.**

---

**The number went into one book at about half past six, and it went in by the man of about twenty-two, in his own hand, on a line under a line, in a docket book on a shelf behind a counter between two books about hinges.**

There were two figures in that book before that evening and neither of them was his. There are three now and one of the three is.

He wrote the figure and the date and the four names, and he did not write a signature, and the book has no column for one, and he did not put anybody else's name on the line.

**He gave it to nobody. He put the book back on the shelf with the other nine and the other eight and the other seven, and it is not in a drawer and it is not behind a door, and it is on an open shelf behind a counter that a person can walk up to.**

---

**And the eleven weeks is still on that page, and it was not struck out, and that is the decision, and it is the reason the sentence about it is in this chapter.**

A man of about thirty-four said, in nine seconds, that a superseded figure ought to be crossed out. The man of about twenty-two said no in four seconds, and gave the reason himself, unasked, in nine words.

"**A figure one man keeps is not a figure people have taken,**" he said. "**If I take that line out then the eleven was never a figure anybody in this city believed in, and it was, and I costed part of it, and that has to be on a page somewhere with my hand on it.**"

Four people in that room said nothing and two of them have said since that this is the first time anybody has said out loud that a man costed part of a thing that four hundred people were relying on.

**Nobody has ever been given the eleven weeks. It is on a line in a book on an open shelf, and four people in this city know it is there, and not one of the four has taken it out.**

---

**And the date on that notice is three weeks off, and it has not moved, and the sheet is still on that wall in that second district with the same two drawing pins in it and the pins still crooked about a quarter of an inch.**

The date at the top of that notice came and went three weeks ago and nobody wrote down what it meant. The date at the foot of it, in the district's own hand, was added afterwards and nobody has asked about it, and about four people in that district have read it, and two of them have said that the district has finally written down the thing it was asked to write down.

Nobody in that room can say whether the district had any business adding it. A man of about thirty-four said that a date a district gives for itself is the only thing on that sheet nobody else in this city is entitled to move, and that the district has not moved it, and that is the whole of the position.

**Nine people in that district can read that sheet. Four of them work in that building. Nobody has told the five who have not read it that anything has happened, and four people in this city would call that a decision and two would call it an omission, and neither of the two has said which.**

---

**And the rest of that Monday was a counter until about six and an hour on a gate closer in a second district, and a shop that shuts at ten.**

The closer had been dropped again and he shimmed the pivot, and told the man that a gate on an old closer wants a new arm about every two years, and that he would rather do the arm in two years than put a new closer on now. Thirty-one pounds and an hour. The man said that was the same as last time, and he said it was, and that it will be the same next time.

He marked the shim with a pencil cross on the inside of the casing where it cannot be seen, and told the man that the cross is so that a man in two years knows that it has been done, and that a mark nobody can see is the only kind of mark that lasts on a thing like that.

---

**And the arm on that gate closer is a thing he has been asked about before, by two other men, on two other gates, and both of them were told the same thing and one of them came back and one of them did not.**

The closer on that gate has a pivoting arm and the arm has a slot in it and the slot has four positions, and the arm has been in the top position for four years, which puts the gate open further than the gate needs to be open and puts the full weight of it onto the hinge.

He did not change the position, because changing it would have altered how that gate had closed for four years, and a man who changes how a gate closes on a Tuesday has changed it for every person who uses it on a Wednesday. He told the man this in the passage and the man said he could not see the difference and he said that the difference is in what a person has to do to get through with a barrow.

**He shimmed the pivot instead, in nine minutes, with a strip of copper, and charged for the shim and not for the arm, and told the man that the arm is a thing he will do in two years when the man has had a chance to find out how the gate feels at the top position for a while.**

The man said that two years is a long time. He said that a gate closer is the only thing on that road that nobody argues with, and that arguing with it is how the man has had a gate on that road that works for four years.

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from about half past six with her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and thirty-eight days, and it has not been read.**
