# Chapter 781 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where Four People Are Asked A Straight Question And All Four Answer And Not One Of The Four Says Yes, And A Date At The Top Of A Notice On A Wall In That District Which Came This Morning And Was Not Moved

**A Tuesday began at a bench and went to a counter and finished in a room off a road in a second district, where four people were asked a plain question. All four of them answered it. The date at the top of a notice on a wall in that district arrived on that same morning, and nobody in the room had come to discuss it.**

---

The board for that Tuesday took thirteen names and thirteen dates, and the last name of the day was written down at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten, as it comes down at ten on every day of this stretch of days, and the street outside that shop is quiet after about four in the afternoon for a reason nobody has ever measured.

**Four rooms off that service road, and a fifth standing behind the other three, stand at one thousand four hundred and thirty-six days, two hundred and five weeks and one day.**

**Line thirteen is one thousand three hundred and seven days, one hundred and eighty-six weeks and five days. What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and twenty-six days, one hundred and sixty weeks and six days.**

Eight hundred and sixteen days is the separation, one hundred and sixteen weeks and four days, and it is in a one-line box about sixteen months old and it is the one figure of that paragraph that has nothing to do with any of this.

---

**The date at the top of that notice is the date by which the reply was to come, and it came on that Tuesday morning, and the district has not gone.**

The gate opened at five behind a wall four hundred yards long. The yard was swept at six and not at seven. The bus was let out of the bay at the hour it is let out at. A man of about thirty-four who keeps that district's haulier books drove past the gate at a quarter to seven and saw every one of those four things happen in the order they always happen in and has not written any of them down.

**Nobody in this city has written down what a date means when it arrives and nobody goes. There is no box on any form for it. The form in question is on a shelf in a room off that road with roughly half of it filled in and the other half of it is four ruled lines under a heading about what a person is owed.**

---

**What was done in that room on that Tuesday was the asking, and the asking took forty minutes and the whole of it was the asking.**

Nobody brought anything to that table. Nobody had prepared a paper. The woman of about thirty-four had the district's notice in her handbag and did not take it out, and the reason she gave, in about nine seconds, was that a person being asked whether she will do a thing for eleven weeks ought not to be looking at the reason for it while she decides.

A man of about twenty-two stood four feet from the end of that table with a coat over his arm for an hour and ten minutes. Nobody asked him anything. He has said since that he would have answered if he had been asked, and nobody in that room contradicted him, and nobody checked.

---

**A woman of about thirty-one stopped it nine minutes in, and she stopped it by saying that it was not the district's to do.**

"**You cannot ask them,**" she said. "**The district is the thing that is leaving. If the district asks four people to cover the mornings the district is gone for, then whatever they say, they have said it to the thing that is leaving and not to the four hundred people who are not in this room.**"

A man of about thirty-one said that this was a distinction without a difference, and she said that it was the whole difference between a person being asked and a person being told, and that four sentences went round that table after that and not one of them arrived anywhere.

**The asking went ahead anyway. It went ahead because nobody in that room had anything else to do with a Tuesday, and because the woman of about thirty-four said, in four seconds, that if it was the wrong thing to do then it was the wrong thing to do in a room with the door open, and the door was open.**

---

**The first of the four is a woman who sweeps that yard at six and has swept it at six for about nine years, and she was asked whether she would stand at the release line on the mornings she is not sweeping.**

She said no in nine seconds and then said the rest of it, which took longer.

"**I am on that yard at six. If I am on the line at six then somebody else is on the yard at seven, and the yard at seven is how the yard at eight gets cleared, and I know that because I have asked for six for about nine years and I have had six.**"

Nobody wrote it down. She has said since that she would say it again in the same words and that she is not sorry about any part of it, and that the man who wrote it down is the only person who did.

---

**The second of the four reads the dial on that line, and he has read it for four years, and the line is held by reading that dial and deciding inside a second.**

He was asked whether he would hold it and he asked one question back, which was how long, and he was told eleven weeks, and he said that eleven weeks was not a unit anybody can decide in.

"**I decide in about a second and then I am wrong for a day,**" he said. "**Nobody asked me whether I want to be wrong for a day for eleven weeks. You asked me whether I can be wrong for a day, and I can be wrong for a day until I am tired of it, and that is a different question and it is the only one of the two that matters.**"

A man of about thirty-four said that was the best sentence said in that room for four years. She said no, that was the man's own, about four hours earlier, and neither of the two has mentioned it since.

---

**The third of the four is a man of about thirty-eight whose brother works at that yard, and he is one of the four who said no to that district's notice, and he has not told his brother that he said no, and his brother does not know it.**

He was asked whether he would hold the line. He said that he would not be the second person in that family to find out about a Tuesday in a room.

"**I have not told him about the sheet and I am not going to stand in here and be asked about his hands while he is not in here.**"

The woman of about thirty-four said that his brother was owed an asking of his own, and he said yes, and that was the end of it, and nobody asked him a second question.

---

**And the fourth of the four is the man of about forty-seven, and he is the only one of them who answered with a figure, and the figure is not eleven weeks.**

He has held that line for about eleven years. He does it about nine times on an ordinary day and is paid for two of them. Four hundred people put a hand on that line across three districts and half a bus route, and the whole of it takes about eleven seconds.

He was asked whether he would hold it for eleven weeks and he said six.

"**Six,**" he said. "**That is what I have. I am not going to tell you what the six is made of, because you will want to do something about it and there is nothing to be done about it.**"

Nobody in that room asked him what the six was made of. A man of about thirty-four wrote six on the back of his own hand and rubbed it out four seconds later, and has said since that he does not know why he did either of those two things.

---

**A woman of about thirty-six who has costed a workshop's heat for three winters was in that room and heard all four answers, and inside nine seconds she said that the eleven weeks had been four people guessed at.**

"**That figure is mine and I have been telling people for four days that it is the wrong one,**" she said. "**It is four people guessed at. This is four people counted. A guess and a count are not the same kind of thing and the guess was the larger of the two by a long way.**"

The eleven weeks is still on a line in a docket book on a shelf behind a counter, between two books about hinges, in a hand that belongs to a man of about twenty-two who decided in advance that he was not going to take it out.

---

**Nobody in that room said the four answers were a refusal, and nobody said they were an agreement, and the woman of about thirty-four wrote the four of them down with no heading at all above them.**

The four are on that sheet with no reason against any of the four. That is the second sheet in this city in four years that carries a name and nothing after the name. The first one is in the same drawer and has thirteen lines on it and nine of them are ticked.

---

**And then the man of about thirty-eight said a thing that has been the reason for about nine days of somebody else's work, and he said it four seconds after the fourth answer, and nobody had asked him anything.**

"**It is written down,**" he said. "**The line, the man, the days, all of it. Four times. In four different places in this city, and every one of the four times it was written down by somebody who was writing down something else entirely.**"

The woman of about thirty-four asked him how he knew. He said he did not know. Then he said that two people had told him, and that he had not believed either of them, and that both of them had named the same four places and in the same order, which is the part he cannot account for.

**Nobody followed it up on that Tuesday. The man of about fifty-two, who has to write down what a period is, said he would ask four people one question each, and he did it in nine minutes on the Friday of the same week, and the four places are the whole of what that Friday was.**

---

**The rest of that Tuesday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and an hour and a half on a door closer in a third district that had been left standing open on a windy night for the fourth time in a fortnight.**

He took the spindle out of the case and found a burr on the inside that had been there since before the spring, dressed it with a strip of emery, put the spindle back, and told the man that the closer was not worn out and that the frame was out of square by about a sixteenth of an inch. Twenty-six pounds and fifty minutes. The man asked him whether the door would shut on its own now and he said that it would, and the man has since told three people on that road about it, and all three of them had told him it was the closer.

He wrote the measurement down on the back of the man's own invoice and not on a work card, and when the man said he would want it on a card he said that a card is for a thing that comes back and a frame out of square does not come back.

**A woman of about thirty-six came in at half past five for a length of chain and was not asked anything. A man of about twenty-two came in at six for a switch and paid and was not asked anything, and those two are the last half hour of that counter and the two of them together are the whole of it.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from about half past six with her door shut, and on the back shelf behind her is the eighth of eight, and it has been there two hundred and twenty-five days and it has not been read and nobody has said one word to her about any of it.**

---
