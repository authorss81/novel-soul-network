# Chapter 787 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where An Exception Was Asked For And Refused By The Only Person Who Could Have Written It Down, And Where The Reason Was A Right Hand And Not A Reason About Anything Else

**A Wednesday opened at a bench, closed at a counter, and went to a room off a road in a second district, where somebody asked for a named exception and was refused by the person it would have had to be written by. He gave his reason in about nine seconds. It was about a hand.**

---

The board for that Wednesday carried eleven names and eleven dates, and the last name was taken at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten, and the room above the line in Saltmarket stood open for an hour that evening, and nothing at all was said in it, and nobody remarked on that.

**Line eighteen is one thousand one hundred and sixty-two days, one hundred and sixty-six weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is one thousand and fifty days, one hundred and fifty weeks to the day.**

**What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and thirty-four days, one hundred and sixty-two weeks to the day, and the four ruled lines for it on that form in a room off that road were still empty on that Wednesday.**

The four rooms off that service road stand at one thousand four hundred and forty-four days, two hundred and six weeks and two days.

---

**What was being asked for was a piece of paper with a name at the bottom of it. The asking took nine minutes and the asking was not the hard part.**

A district has given notice. When it goes, what it takes with it goes too, and what it takes with it is the holding of a release line at a yard, and four hundred people across three districts and half a bus route use that line four times a day and have never once known whose hand it was.

The exception being asked for was this: a named arrangement, for a named number of weeks, in a named person's name, saying that the line at that yard is not one of the things that district is taking.

**Nobody in that room wanted a favour. A man of about thirty-four said, in nine seconds, that he did not want anybody let off anything and that he wanted a thing written down, and that those are two different requests and only one of them is available.**

---

**And then the hard part, which was that the exception could only be written by the man of about forty-seven, and everybody in that room knew it, and he was in the room for about eleven minutes.**

He does not say much in rooms. He was at the wall on the side away from the door and was not asked anything for nine minutes. A man of about thirty-four explained to that table what the form needs, which is a name at the bottom and a date and the hand of the person who holds the thing.

A man of about thirty-one said, inside nine seconds, that the man of about forty-seven could write his name on a piece of paper, and that a man who had done it for about eleven years could certainly write his name on a piece of paper.

**Nobody had asked the man of about fifty-two anything for four minutes, and then somebody did, and he answered in about nine seconds, and what he said was about a hand.**

"**I will not put my name to it,**" he said. "**I have watched that man put a right hand on that dial nine times a day for about eleven years and I am not going to be the one who writes down on a form that he can do it for eleven weeks. I have watched it. I know what it does. That is the whole of my reason and I am not going to give you a longer one, because you will use the longer one.**"

---

**Nobody in that room argued with him, and two of them understood exactly what he said and said nothing at all.**

The two are a woman of about thirty-one who has worked beside that man on a Thursday for four years and a man of about thirty-eight who has carried bar stock out of that yard four times a week since before the spring.

**Neither of the two has ever asked him about that hand. Four people in this city have noticed it and not one of them has said anything to him about it, and one of the four has said that he noticed it before the spring.**

Nobody gave a date out loud in that room. The date is known to nine people in this city and it is a night about a year ago, and four hundred people in this city still call those minutes the nine minutes, and it is not on any paper in that room and it did not go on any paper that day.

---

**The other people in that room argued about the reason and not about the hand, which is what a room does, and the man of about fifty-two let them.**

A man of about thirty-one said that a district is going to take a thing that four hundred people use, and that no arrangement ought to be allowed to stand in the way of that. The woman of about thirty-four said that those four hundred people are not going to be told and that a Tuesday with nobody told is worse. A man of about thirty-eight said that if it was about the hand then somebody ought to say so out loud instead of talking about a district.

**A man of about fifty-two said it again in about nine seconds, and what he said was that it was about the hand, and that they had just heard it said out loud twice, and that he was not going to say it a third time.**

---

**And the exception was refused, by the person it would have had to be written by, and that is the whole of what happened in that room at about twenty past six on that Wednesday.**

The form was not filled in. The box at the bottom of it is the box it has been since the winter, with a crossed-out figure and a seven written under it. The four ruled lines above it for what a person is owed are still empty, and four people in that room looked at them for nine seconds each and not one of them wrote anything on them.

**A man of about twenty-two stood four feet from the end of that table for an hour and fifty minutes, said nothing, was asked nothing, and has said since that he wanted to say one thing and did not.**

He has said that what he wanted to say concerned the seventh week, and that there is no seventh week in anything anybody has said, and that he did not want to be the man who introduced a seventh week into a room where a man had just refused on the grounds that he could not do it for eleven.

---

**Nobody thanked him. Nobody in that room said one word to suggest that he had been thanked by anybody at any point since, because he has not been and nobody in this city is going to.**

Two people in that room have said since that he was the wrong man to ask, and their reason is that a man who writes things down for a living ought never to be asked to write down a thing that is not true.

Four people in that room have said since that he was the only man who could have said it, and their reason is that anybody else would have said it about the district and about four hundred people and about a Tuesday, and that would have been a different sentence and would have been wrong.

**Both of those have been said in this city since, and neither of the two has said anything to the other, and two people here have put the two together and neither of them has said so to anybody.**

---

**The rest of that Wednesday was a counter until about six and half an hour on a gear in a mixer in a fourth district, which had been slipping for about a fortnight and which four people in that building had been adjusting with a spanner.**

He took the cover off, found the linkage worn, and put a new bush in, and told the man that the spanner was making it worse at about a millimetre a month and that it would want a new gear in two winters and not before. Twenty-eight pounds and half an hour.

The man asked whether he would be responsible for it, and he said that he would be, and that being responsible for a thing is not the same as being able to prevent it, and the man wrote that down on the back of his hand and has not asked about it since.

He set the mixer running for four minutes afterwards and listened to it, and said that the noise it made was the noise the new bush would make for about a month and after that it would be quieter than the old one had ever been, and that this is a thing he says and that nobody has ever come back to tell him he was wrong.

---

**And the second half hour of that Wednesday was a different fault on the same machine, and it turned out to be the same fault, and the man in the fourth district did not believe it for about nine seconds.**

He had the new bush in and the mixer running and he said that a linkage wears along a line, and that this one was wearing along a line, and that when a link wears along a line the line is a mark that the whole machine makes and not a fault of that part.

He showed the man the mark on the old bush, which was a bright band two millimetres wide and a duller one on either side of it, and he said that a bright band in the middle of a wear mark means the part has been rocking, and that a part rocks because the two holes are further apart than the two pins, and that the two holes are further apart because somebody drilled them on a Friday.

The man said that he had a man in who came on Fridays. He said that this was the first time in four years that anybody had told him anything about Fridays, and that he had a man in who came on Fridays.

**He put a reamer through both holes and took about a fifth off the diameter of the pin, which is a thing that can be done once and not twice, and he wrote both numbers on the back of the man's own invoice and told him to keep it.**

The man read them and said that he could not read them, and he read them to him, and then said that he would put them on the shop's own book, and then did not, and the numbers are on the invoice and the invoice is in a file on the fourth floor of a building in a fourth district.

---

**And the form with the box at the bottom of it has been in a drawer since the winter, and the drawer has other things in it, and one of the other things is the reason nobody picked the form up on a Tuesday.**

The drawer holds a docket book and nine other papers. Four of the nine are returns from that yard and three of those four are about hinges. The fifth is a sheet of graph paper with a rota on it for a week in the autumn before last, and the rota has a Thursday cell with two sets of initials in it, and one of the two sets is the man of about forty-seven.

Nobody in that room had looked in the drawer. The woman of about thirty-four had taken the form out of it on a Monday and looked at it and put it back, and she has said since that she had not looked at what else was in there, and that a person looking at one piece of paper in a drawer is not a person who has looked in a drawer.

The man of about fifty-two found the rota in the drawer an hour after he had said no. He has said that he did not take it out and that he shut the drawer and that he stood for about a minute with his hand on it, and that a man of about thirty-four asked him whether anything was wrong and he said no.

**He has said that the thing in the drawer is the same thing he refused over, written down by somebody else, and that he has not decided what he is going to do about that, and that he has had a fortnight and has not decided.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from about half past six with her door shut, and the eighth of eight has been on the back shelf behind her for two hundred and twenty-eight days.**
