# Chapter 783 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And A Room Off A Road In A Second District Where Four People Each Waited For Somebody Else To Post A Form, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Four Who Carried It Herself In The Cold To A First Floor At The End Of A Passage And Was Given A Receipt By A Landlord

**A Thursday ran from a bench to a counter and on into a room off a road in a second district, where nobody would post a piece of paper. A woman of about thirty-four picked it up, walked it herself, and handed it to a counter that has never heard of the thing it was for.**

---

The board for that Thursday took eleven names and eleven dates, and the last name of the day was entered at twenty to five. The shutter came down at ten, and the eleven callers were eleven people who wanted a thing mended, and not one of them wanted to be told anything at all.

**That card on end in the rail by the door of the fourth of those rooms is one thousand four hundred and forty-two days, two hundred and six weeks to the day. The rail takes two and there is one thing in it.**

**Line twelve is one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight days, one hundred and ninety-four weeks to the day. Line fifteen is one thousand two hundred and fifty-three days, one hundred and seventy-nine weeks to the day.**

The hold on nine crates and the floor they stand on is one thousand and seventy-one days, one hundred and fifty-three weeks to the day, in the second of the four towns.

---

**The form was printed in the winter. It has three boxes and a line ruled under the last one, and above the first box there are four ruled lines under a heading about what a person is owed.**

The heading has been there since the winter. Nothing has ever been written on those four ruled lines. A man of about fifty-two put twenty-one days in the bottom box on a Thursday four weeks ago in four seconds, and then said, in about nine seconds and before anybody had looked at it, that it was wrong by a fortnight, and that the number belonging in a box is seven.

He crossed the twenty-one out with one stroke. He is the only person in this city who has written on that form.

**Nobody has sent it. It has been on a shelf in a room off that road for four weeks with the twenty-one struck through and a seven written underneath it in the same hand, and about four people have looked at it and about none of them has picked it up.**

---

**The reason it was not sent is not a disagreement, and it is worth saying exactly, because that reason has held a form on a shelf for four weeks.**

A period is the answer. A district gives notice and states its reason, and the reply comes in a period that is written down, and the period is seven days, and the form is the form the reply goes on. It has to reach a person. There is no such thing as a form that finds its own way to the place where a reply has to be entered, and everybody in that room knew that, and every one of the four people who could have carried it said, in nine seconds each, the same sentence in four different ways.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps that district's haulier books said it was not on his round and that his round went to the fourth town. A woman of about thirty-eight said it was a thing for whoever had been talking to that district and that she had not. A man of about thirty-one said the form was not his to post, because the form was not about anything he touched. The man of about fifty-two said he had been handed the form to fill in and not to send it, and that filling in and sending are two jobs and only one of them had been given to him.

**Nobody said the district should post it. The district cannot post it, because the district is the thing the form is about, and four sentences went round that table about that and none of them ended in anybody standing up.**

---

**A man of about twenty-two said, in four seconds, that he could take it, and he said it to the woman of about thirty-four and not to the table.**

She said no in four seconds, and said that whoever carries a thing has to be able to say what it is when a woman at a counter asks her, and that he would not have been able to say what it was, and that she was not going to put him in a passage to find out.

He has said since that he would have been able to say what it was. Nobody in that room said he was mistaken and nobody said he was not.

---

**She went down at about half past six and she walked it. It is a mile and a bit on foot and it took her about twenty-five minutes in the cold, and she has said since that she thought about turning back twice.**

The building is a ground floor office and a first floor and a set of shelves. The ground floor belongs to a landlord. The shelves belong to the landlord. The filing arrangement belongs to the landlord. The man who comes on a Tuesday and a Thursday to collect from those shelves is not that body and never was. Nobody in that building has ever used the word the woman of about thirty-four uses, and the woman at the counter has been there about nine years and has not been asked.

**She put the form on the counter in four seconds, face down, with the seven showing, and the woman at the counter asked her what it was for.**

The woman of about thirty-four said, inside nine seconds, that it was a reply, and that a period of seven days had been entered on it, and that the district had asked the question and the answer was on the sheet, and that the sheet had to be entered somewhere or the period would run out with nothing on the other side of it.

---

**The woman at the counter said the words a landlord's office says about a piece of paper, which is that it would go on the shelf, and then asked whether it had a reference on it.**

It had not. The form is printed in the winter and it has three boxes and four empty ruled lines and no box anywhere on it for a reference, and four people have looked at that form and not one of them has read it as a form for somebody else to file.

**The woman of about thirty-four wrote a number on the top corner of it in her own hand and gave it back, and the woman at the counter read the number out loud to check it, and the number matched.**

Nobody has ever sent anything else to that address in this city's history, and that is now the only address in it that has a thing waiting on a shelf.

---

**What is still owed is one thousand one hundred and twenty-eight days, one hundred and sixty-one weeks and one day, and the four ruled lines under the heading on that form were empty when it went onto that shelf and they are empty now.**

A man of about thirty-four said, in about nine seconds, that those four lines are the part of it that will cost somebody, and that the box at the bottom is a formality and the four lines are a question addressed to a person.

The woman of about thirty-four said that the four lines are for what a person is owed, and that she does not know what anybody is owed, and that she is not going to invent it on a Thursday.

---

**Nine people in that building will see the form, because the filing arrangement works on nine, and four of them work in that building rather than in the office, and neither the woman of about thirty-four nor the woman at the counter knows which four.**

It will be seen on a Tuesday. It will be seen by somebody who will turn it over, look at the back, find it blank, and put it on a shelf. It will be on that shelf on the Tuesday after that. Nobody in this city has arranged for anybody to look at it twice.

**The woman of about thirty-four came back into the room off that road at ten past seven and sat down and did not say how the walk was, and two people in that room had counted the minutes and neither of the two said anything.**

---

**The rest of that Thursday was a counter until about six and an hour on a sash in a first floor in a second district, and a shop that shuts at ten.**

The cord had gone on the near side and the far side was sound, and he replaced the near one and left the far one, because he said that a new cord on both sides is a thing a person pays for twice and gets once. Eleven pounds and an hour.

The woman who owns that flat gave him tea she had not offered and then said she was sorry she had not offered it, and he said that the tea was the same tea either way and that the apology was the useful part.

He wrote the second cord's condition on the invoice as sound, and the woman read it and said that her mother had had the same window for about forty years, and that nobody had ever written the word sound on anything about it before.

---

**And the walk itself was longer than the thing she was carrying, and she has described it since in nine words and every one of them was about her feet.**

She went down that street past three shuttered counters and a place that sells nothing but keys, and at the corner she passed the gate that opens at five and looked at it, and the gate was shut because it was half past six in the evening and the gate is not a thing anybody leaves open after six.

She has said since that she did not know the gate was not open at that hour until she looked at it, and that she had known in her head for four years that there is a time after which a yard is only a yard, and that seeing it shut from a pavement in the dark is a different thing from knowing it.

She carried the form flat against her coat the whole way and a man coming the other way with a dog said sorry and she said sorry and neither of them slowed down, and the dog did not look at the paper and she has said that she is glad about the dog.

**She got to that building at about seven and stood outside it for four minutes before going in, and she has said that the four minutes were not for the cold and were not for her nerve, and that she was deciding what to say when a woman at a counter asked her what a piece of paper was for.**

She has never worked out what she would have said if the woman had asked. She has said that the answer she gave was a true one and that it was the answer she had been preparing for about a mile, and that those two answers are usually not the same thing.

---

**And the woman at that counter in that first floor has been doing that job for about nine years, and on the Thursday she asked the woman of about thirty-four a question back, and it was a good one and nobody answered it.**

She asked whether anybody at the other end knew the form had arrived, and the woman of about thirty-four said no, and then said that nobody at the other end was going to be told for about a week, and then stopped.

The woman at the counter said that in that case the number on the corner of the form was a number with nothing behind it, and that a number with nothing behind it in a filing arrangement is a number that a person finds again in four years and thinks is a mistake.

She wrote the same number on a card, about a quarter the size, and put the card in the tray the forms go into, and she said that a card in a tray is a thing a person looks at, and that a sheet on a shelf is a thing a person files.

The woman of about thirty-four looked at the card for four seconds and did not say anything, and has said since that the four seconds were the longest four seconds of that walk.

**The card is in that tray. The sheet is on that shelf. About nine people in that building handle the tray in a week and four of them handle the shelf, and the two systems have never spoken to each other in about nine years.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms has a woman of about thirty at her chair from about half past six with her door shut, and the eighth of eight is on the back shelf behind her, and it has been there two hundred and twenty-two days.**

---
