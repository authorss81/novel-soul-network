# Chapter 1061 — The Room Has Left The Room

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, one of them carrying heat until about nine, and the rain came over the whole of Nacre about nine and did not stop, and a shop that fixes what other people have fitted wrong was the only thing in it that had been on time.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Monday and nine names were written against that Monday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and sixty-seven days, two hundred and eighty-one weeks to the day. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is one hundred and forty days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and twenty-six days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district were one hundred and seventeen days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two and nobody asked him anything, and about four people were in it, and every one of those four had been in that room on another Monday.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not brought it back up.**

Marek said: **it is on a table now and not on that rail, and that is all it has done.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you put it on a table so that people can pick it up.**

Marek said: **I did not think about it that way and you have said it out loud now.**

---

**And then a man of about thirty-four came in at about ten past two with rain off him and asked at that counter whether that room was still taking people, and about four people in that shop have said since that nobody had told him anything about it before he got to the counter.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **my mother is in a home in Nine Bridges and she does not talk to anybody there. Somebody told me you can say things out loud in a room and that nobody writes them down.**

Marek said: **who told you.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **a woman at a counter in a first district. She cuts keys. I was at her counter on Saturday and she said it and she said it like she had read it somewhere.**

Marek said: **did she say a room.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **she said that shed. She said that shed on the road with the rails under the floor.**

Marek said: **and what else did she say.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **she said you can say three things in it and nobody writes them down.**

---

**And then he asked Marek to put it straight at that counter, out loud, in front of about four people, and about four people in that shop have said since that Marek did not do it and that he stood there for about nine seconds before he answered.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **you can tell me here that that is not what it is. You are stood at a counter and about four of us can hear you.**

Marek said: **To put that straight I would have to describe that room.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **that is not an answer.**

Marek said: **No. It is the reason there is not one. I have been in that room and I have said nothing about it and I am not going to start doing it in a shop with four people stood at my counter.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **so what do I tell my mother.**

Marek said: **that she would have to go and find out for herself, and that nobody can promise her what she will find.**

---

**And then Marek asked the only thing he could ask that was not a question about the room, and the man gave him the answer without being asked twice, and about four people in that shop have said since that the man did not want to give it and gave it anyway.**

Marek said: **what day was Saturday.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **I do not know what day it was. What day is it.**

Marek said: **Monday.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **and is that counter open on Monday.**

Marek said: **I do not know. It is not mine.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and four things were said out loud once each at that counter and none of them went on anything.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units had been finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road where one of them was carrying heat and where the bag was under the long bench where it has been since a Wednesday in the spring.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with a loop of cable behind the backplate doing nothing at all.

He took the loop out, shortened the cable at the terminal and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Nineteen pounds,**" he said. "**A loop of cable behind a backplate is somebody who could not reach the terminal and left proof that they had been there. Roses in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a shop about four of them have had to open up a wall for a lamp that was never on.**"

A switch in that yard had been put in with the cable going into the back of it through a hole made with a drill bit and no plate at all.

He made a plate, sleeved the cable and proved the switch from the gate.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**A switch with a hole in the back of it and nothing over the hole is a switch that somebody will pull hard one day and put a thumb on a bare end. Switches in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to hold a torch over their hands in the wet.**"

A light in that kitchen had been put in on a fitting with the earth sleeved back on itself and folded into the same terminal as the live.

He took the sleeve off, put the earth on the metal and proved the fitting with the switch off.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**An earth folded into the live is an earth that has been tidied away rather than connected, and the tidying is the fault, not the wire. Fittings in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to turn the oven off and hold a tester against it to see whether it was live.**"

A socket in that second unit had been put in on a box that had been pushed into a cavity too shallow for it and was standing proud of the plaster by about half an inch.

He took the box out, cut the cavity back and proved the socket with an instrument.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**A box standing proud of the plaster is a box that will be knocked by a knee carrying something. Boxes on that row are like that, and one of them has been a counter about four of them have had to tape a socket up and leave it until a wall came off.**"

**Seventy-one pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Monday or on any other, and no figure for the page that binder is holding is printed in this file.**
