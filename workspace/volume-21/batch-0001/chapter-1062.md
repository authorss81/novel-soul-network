# Chapter 1062 — The Question Goes To A Counter

**A Tuesday put him on the customer side of a counter in a first district for about forty minutes in the middle of the afternoon, in a shop with a bench down one side and about four people on it waiting, and the woman behind that counter cut keys and did not stop for him and did not look up more than twice.**

---

Eight callers came into that shop on that Tuesday and eight names were written against that Tuesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and sixty-eight days, two hundred and eighty-one weeks and one day. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is one hundred and forty-one days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and twenty-seven days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district were one hundred and eighteen days old.**

---

**He went down there at about half past two with nothing in his hands, and he stood at the customer side of that counter for about forty minutes, and the woman of about thirty-one cut keys the whole time, and about four people came and went off that bench.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **what do you want.**

Marek said: **nothing. I am stood here.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **there is a bench.**

Marek said: **I know there is a bench. I am not going to take any of it.**

---

**And then a woman of about fifty-five came in off the wet and sat down at the end of that bench and asked the woman of about thirty-one what happens in that shed, and about four people on that bench have said since that she had been carrying the question for about a week and a half and had not asked it anywhere else.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **I am told there is a room where you can say three things out loud. I would like to know what happens in it before I go and find it.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **I do not know. I have never been in it.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **somebody said it in here on Saturday.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **then it came from whoever said it, and it is not going to get better by me saying it again.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-five turned round on that bench and looked at Marek, and about four people in that shop have said since that she took about nine seconds deciding, and that she then asked a stranger in front of about four people.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **you are the one who has that room.**

Marek said: **I have been in it. I do not have it.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **that is the same thing in your mouth and not the same thing on paper.**

Marek said: **then we have got nothing here.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **I am asking you a question and you have not asked me one.**

Marek said: **No. I have got one that I am not allowed to ask and something short for yours, and I would rather you knew that than be given the other thing.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-one put a key down on that counter, and about four people have said since that she said the rest of it with the key still under her thumb, and that nobody stopped her.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **I am going to say something about that room to the next person who asks me, and I have worked that out over the weekend and it is not about you.**

Marek said: **you have not said it to anybody yet.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **not yet. I said Saturday to a woman who wanted to know and I gave her four words and she went off happy, and I have not been happy since, and I am not going to keep doing that.**

Marek said: **I could ask you not to.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **You can ask me. I am telling you now that I will not take it.**

Marek said: **I know that. I am asking anyway.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **I know you are. That is the whole of what I have got to say to you today.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people heard one stranger refuse to describe a room and then heard the woman behind the counter refuse to be asked anything about it.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units had been finished by about five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road, and the bag was under that long bench where it has been, and he did not touch it and nothing in that room was switched on but the one unit that carries heat.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with its old earth strap left on the incoming pipework and nothing at all on the appliance.

He took the strap off the pipe, made a proper bond and proved the cooker with the case off.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**An earth left on a pipe is somebody's job done to the nearest thing that would take a screw. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen about four of them have had to pull a cooker out to find out why the socket was warm.**"

A light in that second unit had been put in on a flex that went through a wall plug rather than a hole.

He took the plug out, bored the hole, sleeved the flex and proved the light with the plug in the socket.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A flex through a plug is a flex that somebody will one day yank at the plug instead of at the lamp. Fittings in that unit are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to rewire about four feet of their own ceiling.**"

A switch in that yard had been put in with the mounting screws going into a hollow block and no cavity cut for them.

He cut the cavity, put the screws into brick and proved the switch from the gate.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**A screw into a hollow block is a switch that is fine for years and then is not. Switches on that row are like that, and one of them has been a wall about four of them have had to have the same wall opened twice.**"

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a pendant that had been shortened by about four inches with a pair of snips and no connector.

He took the pendant back to length, made a proper flex and proved the lamp from a chair.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**A pendant cut short with snips is somebody who could not reach a ceiling rose and cut the wire instead. Pendants in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a shop about four of them have had to put a lamp on the counter because the one above it only worked if you stood sideways.**"

**Sixty-six pounds is the sum of those four on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Tuesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Tuesday or on any other, and the page inside it is given no figure anywhere in this file.**
