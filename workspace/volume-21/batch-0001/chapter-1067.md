# Chapter 1067 — The Asking Went Badly

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, one of them carrying heat until about nine, and that Monday was the one he spent asking people for something, and the asking took about an hour of a wet afternoon.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Monday and nine names were written against that Monday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten with about four people still behind that counter.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and seventy-four days, two hundred and eighty-two weeks to the day. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is one hundred and forty-seven days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and thirty-three days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district were one hundred and twenty-four days old.**

---

**He was at that bench until about two and nobody asked him anything and about four people were in the room and one of them was the man of about thirty-four, who had been in that room twice before and had still not gone, and who said out loud that he had not gone because of a sentence.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **I am still here.**

Marek said: **I know. I have seen you on three Mondays.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **I came on a rumour about a wall and I am staying for what a woman said at a counter on a Friday. That is a different reason and it is better.**

---

**And then at about half past two he said the only thing he had left to say out loud, and about four people in that shop have said since that it was the first time in this city he had asked anybody to stop saying anything.**

Marek said: **I want to ask every person I can reach who has heard what she said on Friday to stop saying it.**

The woman of about forty-one said: **you are asking me.**

Marek said: **I am asking you.**

The woman of about forty-one said: **No.**

Marek said: **I have not finished.**

The woman of about forty-one said: **You have finished, because you have said it once and I have said no and you have nothing after that which is not the same question again.**

---

**And then he asked the others one at a time and about four people have said since that he asked nine questions in about an hour and that six of the answers were no and that one man agreed and then went out and told his own counter about it.**

Marek said: **Will you not pass it on.**

A woman of about forty-one said: **No.**

Marek said: **Will you not say it at a counter.**

A woman of about forty-one said: **No, and I have said it twice in the last hour to two women in this shop.**

Marek said: **Then you have made it about four.**

The woman of about forty-one said: **Then it is about four and it was not before you started asking.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-four said the thing out loud that about four people who were in that shop have said since was the only thing anybody said that day that Marek wrote nothing down about and thought about for two days afterwards.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **You are asking the wrong people. I was told it and I passed it on because it was the only thing I had that was worth having this month. It is not hers to give away and it is not mine to hold back.**

Marek said: **That is two people saying that.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **You are stood at a counter. Ask somebody who got it out of a queue.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about nine questions were asked at one counter in about an hour and six of the answers were no and about four people who said no have said since that they did not enjoy saying it.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units had been finished by about five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road and put nothing up and wrote nothing down and did not tell anybody what had happened at that counter.**

A switch in that shop had been put in with a plastic faceplate over the front of it and no box behind it at all.

He took the plate off, cut a box in, fixed it and proved the switch from the floor.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**A faceplate with nothing behind it is a fitting somebody could have put on in about four minutes and thought about for none of them. Switches in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to hold a light on with their thumb because the switch was decoration.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in on a cable that had been cut long and folded back into the fitting and held with a grub screw.

He pulled the cable out, made a proper entry and proved the lamp from the gate.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**A cable folded into a fitting is a cable that has been shortened by somebody who did not have the length to hand. Fittings in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to reach in behind the lamp with two fingers to find out whether it was on.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in on a hob that had been wired from a junction box under the worktop with a spur that came out of the same terminal as the oven.

He put the hob on its own ring and proved it with four pans across it.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**Two things on one terminal is two things that share a fuse and one blown fuse takes both. Hobs in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to stop the oven while the frying pan was on because somebody put them on together.**"

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with a pull cord that had been tied to the door handle so the door could not open.

He un-tied the cord, put in a proper switch and proved the light with the door shut.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**A cord tied to a handle is a light that somebody will one day pull the door against. Switches in that unit are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to prop that door with a wedge for the whole of an evening.**"

**Sixty-nine pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Monday or on any other, and no figure for that page is printed here or on any of the sixty days of this volume.**
