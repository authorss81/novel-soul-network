# Chapter 1066 — The Doors Off Their Tracks

**A Sunday came down at about two in this city, which is what it does on that day, and the whole of that Sunday was short: a bench until about two, a shop that opens late and shuts early, and a road in a fourth district with a wet doorway on it.**

---

Four callers came into that shop on that Sunday and four names were written against that Sunday's book as each of them came in, and the shutter came down at about two, which is what it does on that day.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and seventy-three days, two hundred and eighty-one weeks and six days. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is one hundred and forty-six days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and thirty-two days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district were one hundred and twenty-three days old.**

---

**He walked past that shed on that road at about half past one on his way back from a shop in that district, and about four people have said since that they were stood in the doorway out of the rain and that they had been there about ten minutes and had not gone in.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **you are the one who had that room.**

Marek said: **I have been in it.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **We know. We were told.**

Marek said: **told where.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **At a counter. On Friday. She said what happens in there and she said you decide after, and about four of us were stood in a shop and we have all been telling people since and here we are.**

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-five said the other half of it, and about four people have said since that she said it to a stranger on a wet road and that she was about nine steps inside the doorway and did not come any further out.**

The woman of about thirty-five said: **We came on what somebody said last year. There was a wall and you could read it. That is how I heard about the room in the first place.**

Marek said: **there is still no wall.**

The woman of about thirty-five said: **No. There is not. So now there is a woman at a counter and a man of about twenty-seven and me, and that is how the three of us are here on a Sunday.**

---

**And then he asked them the only question he had that was not a question about the room, and about four people have said since that he asked it flatly and that nobody answered it straight.**

Marek said: **What were you two expecting to be standing here for.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **somebody to ask us something.**

Marek said: **No. There is nobody to ask you anything and there are no doors to close and I have not asked anybody to be here on a Sunday and nobody has any idea what you have come for except the three of you.**

The woman of about thirty-five said: **Can we go in and sit.**

Marek said: **You can go in and sit. I am not going to stop you and I am not going to stand at the front of it.**

---

**And then the three of them went in and sat down on that bench, and Marek went on up the road with about four people watching him go, and he did not come back that day, and about four people in that shed have said since that they sat there for about half an hour and that nobody came and that nothing happened.**

Nothing happened.

Nobody came.

Nobody asked them anything.

They went home in the rain about as they had come.

There was an envelope on the end of that bench with its flap up and nothing in it, and about four people have said since that two of the three sat looking at it for about nine minutes and neither of them said what it was for.

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and the shutter came down at about two and about half past six shut the fourth of those four rooms with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Sunday or on any other, and the page in that binder is not measured in this file.**

---

**What was left of that Sunday went into that shop and that yard and it took four jobs and not a fifth, and they were the four cheapest of any Monday through Thursday in a fortnight.**

A bell in that shop had been put on a bracket that had been screwed into a length of wood which had been screwed to the wall beside the door and not into it.

He took the wood off, cut two boxes and put the bracket into the wall.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A bracket screwed to a strip screwed to a wall is a bracket that is only as strong as the strip. Brackets in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a door about four of them have had to prop it open with a boot.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in on a holder with the ear left off because the ear had been bent.

He put a new ear on, wired it and proved the lamp from the gate.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A bent ear is somebody who has hung a bucket off it. Holders in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to change the whole fitting for a lamp that was never broken.**"

A socket in that kitchen had been put in at the wrong height for the worktop and could not reach it from above.

He took it out, moved it and proved it with a kettle standing on the worktop.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A socket behind a worktop is a socket that gets reached for with a wet hand and a hot pan in the other one. Sockets in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to unclip a kettle every single time they filled it.**"

A switch in that second unit had been put in on a plate that had been painted over twice and could not be earthed because it was on lath and plaster.

He brought the earth onto the box, took the paint back to the edge and proved the switch.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**Painting over a plate is not fitting anything. Plates in that unit are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to guess at which switch was which because they could not see the edges any more.**"

**Twenty-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Sunday, exact.**
