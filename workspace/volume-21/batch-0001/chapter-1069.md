# Chapter 1069 — Somebody Stood At The Front

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and then a counter until six and then a room on a road in a fourth district, and that Wednesday was the one on which the second half of it belonged to about nine people in a cold shed with the bench at the far end of it bare, and the rest of Nacre was asleep by nine.**

---

Twelve callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and twelve names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and seventy-six days, two hundred and eighty-two weeks and two days. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is one hundred and forty-nine days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and thirty-five days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district were one hundred and twenty-six days old.**

---

**He was at that bench until about two and about four people were in that room and the woman of about fifty-four said one thing out loud that she has not said before, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it while she was doing something else with her hands.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **If somebody starts it this time I am going to sit down.**

Marek said: **You can sit down whenever you want to.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have not sat down in about four years of this.**

---

**And then he went across at about half past six and about nine people were in that shed and about four coats were on that rail and the workbench was clear at about eleven feet long with nothing on it at all, and he did not stand at the front of anything and he put his hands in his pockets.**

A man of about forty-four said: **You again.**

Marek said: **Again.**

The man of about forty-four said: **I am not asking you anything tonight.**

Marek said: **Good.**

The man of about forty-four said: **I want to be here.**

Marek said: **sit down.**

---

**And then at about seven the man of about twenty-seven stood up at that bench and asked three people three things out loud, one at a time, in about nine voices' worth of trying, and about four people in that shed have said since that he had it wrong and that he had it wrong in the way a person has a thing wrong when they have had it described to them and not been in the room where it was said.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **What would you put in, and what would you keep out, and who could stop you.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **You are asking that as if there is a list.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **There is not a list. That is what I am telling you. There is no list and there is no form and you say it out loud and that is all.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **And who decides.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **You do.**

---

**And then he asked a woman of about forty-four who had never been in that room and she answered him, and about four people in that shed have said since that she answered him the way you answer somebody at a counter who wants a number.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **What would you keep out.**

The woman of about forty-four said: **All of it.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **That is not answering.**

The woman of about forty-four said: **It is the answer I have and you have asked me the same as you asked the others.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **Somebody said you can say three things here.**

The woman of about forty-four said: **Somebody said it to you and you have come and stood at the front of a room because of it.**

---

**And then about four people left that shed within about nine minutes of each other, and nobody stopped them and nobody said one kind thing to anybody as they went, and Marek stood with his hands in his pockets about eleven feet from that bench and did not say a word.**

Nobody said anything to anybody.

Nobody moved a chair.

The man of about twenty-seven put his hand into an envelope on that bench, found it empty, and said out loud that he had thought there was something in it and that nobody in that room knew what he meant.

The man of about forty-four said: **That is what you get.**

Marek said: **Yes.**

The man of about forty-four said: **And you are stood there saying yes.**

Marek said: **I am not going to say it is not how it goes. I am not going to say he is wrong either, because he asked it out loud in front of everybody and nobody in this room can tell him he does not know what he is doing.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and the room under a building in a first district was dark all evening and was not opened, and about four people walked out of a shed in Nacre that night saying nothing to each other.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units had been finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to that shed with the doors off their tracks and about nine people in it and nothing on the bench at the far end.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with a neutral and a line both landed on the brass plate behind it.

He took both off, landed them properly and proved the lamp and the tester on it.

"**Twenty-two pounds,**" he said. "**Two wires on a brass plate is somebody who has taken the plate off and put it back without noticing what is behind it. Roses in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a shop about four of them have had to take about nine of them out over one evening because none of them would hold a bulb.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate with the cover held on by a screw that turned into a bush and stopped.

He changed the bush, re-screwed the cover and proved the socket with the cover fitted.

"**Twenty-one pounds,**" he said. "**A cover that will not go back on is a cover somebody has taken off for a test and not put back. Covers in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to leave a socket with its terminals showing for the length of one summer.**"

A light in that kitchen had been put in on a rose where the earth was connected to a length of copper pipe that went into the water.

He took the pipe off, put the earth on the back of the rose and proved the fitting with the pipes full.

"**Twenty-four pounds,**" he said. "**An earth on a pipe is somebody's water, and a kitchen has more water in it than any room in a house. Fittings in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to have a new stopcock before they would let anybody touch the sink again.**"

A switch in that second unit had been put in with the metal box bonded to a length of water pipe instead of to its own earth.

He undid the bond, earthed the box and proved the switch with an instrument on the pipes.

"**Nineteen pounds,**" he said. "**A box on a water pipe is a box that has been earthed by whoever came first and nobody after. Switches in that unit are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to get an instrument out because the light was flickering and they could not work out whether it was the lamp.**"

**Eighty-six pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Wednesday or on any other, and there is no figure on that page in this file.**
