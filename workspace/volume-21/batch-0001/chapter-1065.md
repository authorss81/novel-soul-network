# Chapter 1065 — The Counter Where It Was Said Out Loud

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, one of them carrying heat until about nine, and the shortest walk he took that week was across a city to a shop with a bench down one side of it and a key machine behind a counter.**

---

Ten callers came into that shop on that Friday and ten names were written against that Friday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and seventy-one days, two hundred and eighty-one weeks and four days. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is one hundred and forty-four days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and thirty days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district were one hundred and twenty-one days old.**

---

**He was at that bench until about two and about four people were in that room and nobody asked anything of anybody, and then he went across the city to a first district and stood at the customer side of a key counter for about an hour.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **you again.**

Marek said: **I am stood here.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **you have been stood here three times and you have not had a key cut.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **then you are not stood here for a key.**

Marek said: **no.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-five came in off the wet and sat at the end of that bench for the second time in three days, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had been at that counter twice before that week and had asked the same question twice and been given four words each time, and that on this Friday she did not sit down.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **I have been back twice and I am back again and I am not going to say please.**

Marek said: **nobody has said please.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **the woman at that counter said please last time. That is what I have come back for.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-one put the file down, and about four people on that bench have said since that she said it once and that she said it the way a person reads an address to somebody at a door, and that it took about four seconds.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **Nothing happens in there, and after that it is you deciding.**

Nobody said anything.

The woman of about thirty-one said: **That is all of it. I am not going to say it again and I am not going to write it down for you.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-five asked her whether that was what happened in there, and about four people in that shop have said since that the woman behind the counter could have said one word and did not, and that what she said instead has been taken out of that shop by about four people and is now in this city.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **But is it that.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **I am not going to say that it is.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **That is not the same as saying that it is not.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **No. It is me not saying.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **You were in it.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **I have never been in it. Not once. I cut keys on a Friday.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **Then why are you the one saying it.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **Because you asked me twice and I had nothing else to give you, and I am sorry about the rest of it, and I am not sorry that I said it.**

---

**And then a man of about twenty-seven on that bench said thank you out loud to the woman behind that counter, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said it warmly and that she told him not to and that he did not take it back.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **thank you.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **Do not. I have not done anything and I do not know that it is anything.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **It got me down a stair in Ashfields.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **Then keep it and do not bring it back here.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-five stood up and said both halves of it out loud, and about four people on that bench have said since that she said them to Marek and not to the woman behind the counter, and that she was not complaining and was not asking for anything.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **Six weeks ago I would have gone on a Monday because somebody said there was a wall with a door on it.**

Marek said: **there was a wall and there is not now.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **I know. I would have gone on it and I would have taken my sister with me, and now I am on a Friday at a key counter because somebody told me what it is like to be in there.**

Marek said: **about four people have come to that room on a rumour in a month.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **And how many are coming now that a sentence is going round about it.**

Marek said: **I am not saying a number out loud in this city this week.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **You are stood in a shop with about four of us listening.**

Marek said: **Then you have all been counted by somebody else and not by me, and that is the only arrangement I have got.**

---

**And then Marek said the other half of it out loud to her on the step outside, and she wrote nothing down and repeated nothing back to him, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said it to a woman on a step and not to a room.**

Marek said: **They came on a rumour and now they come on what she said, and the rumour was never wrong about the room.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **So somebody is being paid for this.**

Marek said: **Nobody is being paid and nobody made it up and I cannot stop it.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **Then do not stop it. Do not go and tell her I said it was wrong.**

Marek said: **I am not going to.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **I know. I would not have come back for that.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and one woman said a thing out loud once and would not be thanked for it, and nobody in that shop repeated one word of it afterwards, and about four of them who heard it have not said since that they know whether it is so.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units had been finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road where one of them carries heat and where the bag is still under the long bench and nothing has been put up on any wall in Nacre this week.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with the lamp itself wired loose behind the gallery so that it swung.

He took the lamp out, made a proper gland, pinned the flex and proved it from a chair.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp that swings is a lamp somebody will put a hand through one day. Lamps in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a counter about four of them have had to move a paperweight that had been put underneath it for about two years.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in with a metal box buried under about four inches of concrete.

He cut a chase, brought the box up to the surface and proved the socket from the gate.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**A box under four inches of concrete is a box that was put in before the concrete was and has been there since. Sockets in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to run a lead from the house because the socket under the yard was never going to be found.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with an oven element wired straight across two terminals with no thermostat in the circuit at all.

He put a thermostat in, reterminated the element and proved the oven coming up and going off.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**An oven with no thermostat is somebody who wanted the oven on and did not mind when it went off. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen about four of them have had to stand and watch a pan for twenty minutes instead of ten.**"

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with a fuse holder that had been bridged with a piece of copper wire.

He took the wire out, put in a fuse of the rating that belongs on it and proved the fitting with the cover on.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**A bridge across a fuse is a person who has had three of them go and has taken the third one's advice. Fuses in that unit are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to unscrew a bulb before they could put the light on and have forgotten how to do it.**"

**Sixty-three pounds is the sum of those four on that Friday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Friday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Friday or on any other, and nothing in this file puts a number on the page it holds.**
