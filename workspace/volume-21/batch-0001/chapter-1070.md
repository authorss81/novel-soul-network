# Chapter 1070 — He Gave That Man A Door

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and that Thursday was the last day of a fortnight in which nothing was printed and about four people said a thing out loud, and the rain was still on.**

---

Eight callers came into that shop on that Thursday and eight names were written against that Thursday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and seventy-seven days, two hundred and eighty-two weeks and three days. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is one hundred and fifty days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and thirty-six days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district were one hundred and twenty-seven days old.**

---

**He was at that bench until about two and about four people were in that room and none of them asked him anything, and at about half past two the man of about forty-four came into that shop and stood at that counter in a dry coat, having clearly come a long way to be dry.**

The man of about forty-four said: **You have had a fortnight to think of an answer.**

Marek said: **I have.**

The man of about forty-four said: **Then have it.**

Marek said: **It is a shed on a road in a fourth district with the rails still under the floor. It is open on Wednesdays. Come at about half past six.**

---

**And then he stopped, and about four people in that shop have said since that he put one hand flat on that counter as though he had meant to go on and then did not, and that the rest of it took about nine seconds.**

Marek said: **That is a door and a day and nothing else.**

The man of about forty-four said: **That is not an answer to what I asked.**

Marek said: **No. It is what I have instead of an answer and I have thought about it for a fortnight.**

The man of about forty-four said: **I asked you whether it does anything.**

Marek said: **I have been in it once and I asked three things at that bench and I still do not know, and I am not going to find out for you at a counter on a Thursday.**

---

**And then a woman of about twenty-four came in at about four with her hood up and asked him, out loud, in front of about four people, whether the sentence was a thing she was allowed to pass on, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had it written on the back of a hand in a shop in another district.**

The woman of about twenty-four said: **A woman wrote it out for me on the back of my hand. Am I allowed to say it.**

Marek said: **I cannot give you that off a counter either.**

The woman of about twenty-four said: **You have not said no.**

Marek said: **No. I have said that I have got nothing to give you and that is not the same as no and it is the only thing I have got.**

---

**And then he gave her the one thing he did have, and it was in about nine words, and she wrote it on the back of the other hand and he did not look at what she wrote.**

Marek said: **It came off somebody's mouth. No office, no printer.**

The woman of about twenty-four said: **So I am not saying anything wrong.**

Marek said: **I have not heard you say it and I am not going to hear you say it.**

The woman of about twenty-four said: **That is the fourth time this week somebody has said something like that to me.**

Marek said: **Then there are about four of us.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people heard a man give one stranger a door and refuse two strangers an answer, and the day went on and about four people were still saying one sentence in this city when the shutter came down at ten.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units had been finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road and the bag was under the long bench where it has been since a Wednesday in the spring, and he did not open it, and he wrote nothing down anywhere, and nobody came to the door of that shop after eight.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with a dimmer module behind it that had never once been matched to the lamp in it.

He took the module out, put the plain switch in and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**A dimmer that was never matched to its lamp is a lamp somebody can dim to nothing and think has gone. Modules in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a bench about four of them have had to change a bulb and found the light was off.**"

A light on that landing had been put in on a fitting where the neutral and the line came in the same way round and were not separated at the terminal.

He separated them and proved the light from both landings.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**Two wires into one way round is a fitting that has been wired twice by two people who did not speak. Fittings on landings are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to blow the fuse and go downstairs to find the board.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a lamp inside the hood wired after the cooker and switched off with it.

He took the lamp off that cable and put it on the wall switch.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**A hood lamp wired in behind a cooker is a lamp that goes off when somebody boils a kettle. Lamps in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to hold a torch under their hands to find a pan.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate fixed with three screws into brick and one into a hole in a stone that went nowhere.

He took the fourth screw out, filled the hole and proved the socket from the gate.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**One screw into a stone is a plate that is one bad winter away from being on the floor. Plates in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a wall about four of them have had to put the lamp somewhere else until the wind dropped.**"

**Seventy pounds is the sum of those four on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Thursday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Thursday or on any other, and the figure for the page it holds is not on this page.**

---

*Conditions of the close.* **The book in the green binding was shut and the tin beside it had its lid down at ten that Thursday, and this file prints no figure for either and no difference between them and not one page of this volume remarks on any of it.

That register of correct acts that changed nothing said four at the start of that Thursday and said four at the end of it, nobody in this city is entered on it, and giving one man a door is not entered on it either.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named at the foot of this page and each one is named by itself. Two nicks cut into that rail. A sentence said once at a counter in a first district by a woman who had never been in that room is in about four mouths in this city, no page of this volume has said whether it is so, nobody has stopped it, one man has been given a day and an address, and the woman who said it is not thanked and is not forgiven and is not written down.**
