## 1. WHAT THIS MOVEMENT IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

**On the Monday five days after the last page of Volume 20, a man of about thirty-four is told at a counter in a first district that a person can say three things out loud in a shed on a road in a fourth district and that nobody writes them down, and he carries it across the city to the shop that pays for the printing and asks for it to be put straight out loud in front of about four people, and is told that putting it straight would mean describing that room.** On the Tuesday he stands at the counter where it was said and watches a woman of about fifty-five ask what happens in that shed for the second time and be refused twice. On the Wednesday a man of about forty-four puts both hands flat on a counter at about half past two and asks whether that shed does anything, and this file carries no answer to it and no account of what either of them said afterwards, and about nine people go into that shed that evening and nobody stands at the front of anything. On the Thursday a woman he loves notices on a half landing in a second district that he has not asked her for anything, and says out loud that her office would do it in about nine days and that she is not going to offer it twice, and neither of them says why. **On the Friday a woman of about thirty-one behind a key counter in a first district, who has never been in that room, says one sentence out loud, once, to a woman who has asked her twice, and then refuses to say whether it is so, and refuses to be thanked for it, and about four people on a bench down one side of that shop hear it.** On the Sunday about three of those people stand in the wet in a shed doorway for about ten minutes and go in and sit down and nothing happens. On the Monday he asks about nine people at one counter to stop saying it and about six of them say no out loud. On the Tuesday his hands go for about two hours and he prices two jobs at about a fifth of what they were worth. On the Wednesday a man who came on the sentence stands at the front of that shed and asks three people three things out loud and gets it wrong and about four people walk out. On the Thursday he gives one man a day and an address and refuses two women an answer, and the sentence is in about four mouths and nobody in this city has said whether it is so.

**AND WHAT IT IS NOT.** It is not a chapter about a villain, and there is no villain in it and there is none in this volume. It is not a chapter in which anything is decided about that room, because nothing is decided about it. **It contains no printed correction, no second notice, no form filled in, no column, no heading, no list of anybody who came, and no page on which anybody says the sentence is a true sentence — and it contains no page on which anybody says the opposite either, which is the harder half and the one the whole page turns on.** The one answer in it is not answered.
