# Chapter 1063 — Did It Do Anything

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and then a counter until six and then a room on a road in a fourth district with the rails still under the floor, and the whole of that Wednesday went into an ordinary day of work in an ordinary shop in the middle of a wet week, and about half of it was spent carrying stock.**

---

Eleven callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and eleven names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and sixty-nine days, two hundred and eighty-one weeks and two days. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is one hundred and forty-two days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and twenty-eight days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district were one hundred and nineteen days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and the place behind that woman's chair was empty and had been empty every day he had been in that room, and he stood with his hands behind his back for about as long as it took to decide not to say a number, and he did not say it and nobody in that room asked him for it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you are stood like a man waiting for a bus.**

Marek said: **I am.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there is no bus.**

Marek said: **there is one in about four minutes.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **No there is not. You have been stood there since before you sat down.**

---

**And then at about half past two he was behind that counter and a man of about forty-four came in off the wet, put both hands flat on the top of it, and asked him whether that shed does anything, and about four people in that shop have said since that the man had clearly been carrying the question a long way and had not slept on it.**

The man of about forty-four said: **does that shed do anything.**

Marek said: **what do you mean by anything.**

The man of about forty-four said: **anything. Anything at all. Does it change anything for anybody.**

Marek said: **I was in there on a Wednesday in the spring and I asked three things at that bench and about eleven people answered them, and I have not been able to tell anybody since then whether any of it did anything, and I am not going to answer you at this counter.**

The man of about forty-four said: **that is the first time in my life anybody has said that and not answered it.**

Marek said: **I know what it looks like from where you are stood.**

The man of about forty-four said: **so do not stand there.**

Marek said: **No.**

The man of about forty-four looked at the clock over that counter and did not say anything for about nine seconds.

Marek said: **I am going out to a call at three and I am not going to be back before half past four.**

The man of about forty-four said: **then I will come back.**

**And that is where this page stops carrying it, and no part of this file says what either of those two men said about it afterwards, or whether either of them said anything at all.**

---

**He went out to that call at three and came back at about five, and about four people have said since that he came back in and went straight to the shelf and did not look at that counter at all, and that whatever was on that counter had been put in a carrier bag by the woman who worked here and taken out to the yard.**

---

**And then he went across at about half past six to a room on a road in a fourth district where about nine people were standing about in the cold, and about four of them had been in that room before and about four of them had not, and nobody had told anybody what they were walking into.**

A woman of about forty said: **is somebody going to ask us three things.**

Marek said: **No.**

The woman of about forty said: **last Wednesday somebody did.**

Marek said: **Somebody did. I am not standing at the front of anything tonight and I have not asked anyone to stand at the front of anything.**

The woman of about forty said: **then what is the point of the door being open.**

Marek said: **that is the wrong question for me to be standing here answering and I am going to stop answering it now.**

---

**And then about nine people in that room went over what they would say and what they would not say, out loud, in about four voices at a time, and nobody kept anything, and about four people who were not in there have said since that it did not sound like anything and that nothing came of it.**

Nobody wrote anything down.

On the end of that bench there was an envelope with its flap up and nothing in it, and nobody in that room knew whose it was and nobody asked.

Nobody asked anybody to stop anything.

Nobody was the one who started.

The two who had come off a counter in a first district did not say so to anybody in that room.

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that room on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and the room under a building in a first district stayed dark all evening and was not opened.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units had been finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed with the doors off their tracks and a scarred workbench about eleven feet long at the far end of it and about nine coats on a rail.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with the neutral taken off its own terminal and onto the earth.

He moved the neutral back, proved the lamp from the floor and proved the tester on it as well.

"**Twenty-one pounds,**" he said. "**A neutral on an earth is not a fault you can see and it is not one anybody will find by looking. Roses in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a shop about four of them have had to take a whole ring out and put a lamp back on a table because they could not make it light twice running.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on an outdoor plate with the lid on the wrong way round and the gasket out of its groove.

He turned the plate, put the gasket back and proved the socket from the gate in the wet.

"**Twenty pounds,**" he said. "**A plate on backwards is a plate whose lid has been off in a hard winter and put back the other way round. Plates in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to stand under it holding a torch and their breath to see whether it was live.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in on a spur that came off the lighting circuit and not off a ring at all.

He took the spur off the lighting, put it on a proper ring and proved the cooker with two pans on at once.

"**Twenty-two pounds,**" he said. "**A spur off the lighting is somebody who wanted the cooker on and did not want the fuse off when it did not work. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen about four of them have had to go round the house switching lamps off to find out which lights the oven was on.**"

A light in that second unit had been put in on a rose with two cables into one terminal and a jumper across the other.

He doubled both ends onto their own terminals and proved the light with the other room dark.

"**Nineteen pounds,**" he said. "**A jumper is somebody who needed one more terminal and would not cut the cable. Roses in that unit are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to switch every light in the flat to work out which pair of them were joined.**"

**Eighty-two pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Wednesday or on any other, and not one figure for that page appears in this file at all.**
