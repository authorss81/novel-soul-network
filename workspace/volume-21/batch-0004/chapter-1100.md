# Chapter 1100 — Nobody Is Waiting For Anything

**A Tuesday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the whole of that Tuesday evening was nobody being asked anything at all in a shed on a road in a fourth district for about nine minutes, and nothing whatever happening in it.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Tuesday and nine names were written against that Tuesday's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten that night with the bar already across it.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and thirty-one days, two hundred and ninety weeks and one day. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and thirty-five days, two hundred and ninety weeks and five days. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is two hundred and four days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and ninety days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and eighty-one days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had not put the bread in since about Friday and the fly was on the sill again, and about four people in that room have said since that she has stopped trying anything and is going back to a cloth.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I am going back to the cloth.**

Marek said: **The cloth never worked.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **The cloth is only in the way of things being able to get at it.**

Marek said: **Then the cloth was doing more than I gave it credit for and I will take that back.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You are not going to take anything back. You have never once said anything I did not already have.**

Marek said: **That is not a thing I am going to argue about on a Tuesday.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-eight came into that shop at about half past four and asked for the sheet of paper she had taken out folded in two on the Friday before, and Marek told her that it went in that bin on that Friday afternoon, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had expected him to have kept it and that the whole of her reaction was one word and then silence.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **You threw it out.**

Marek said: **It went in the bin, and the bin goes out on a Thursday, and it went out on a Thursday.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **Good.**

Marek said: **You have said one word and you have not left.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **Nobody thanks anybody in this city, so do not wait for it.**

Marek said: **Not for a bin, not in this city, and I made my peace with it about four years ago.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that shop have said since that a sheet of paper went out in a bin on a Thursday and that nobody in that shop has said one word about it since.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about nine people were in it, and the man of about thirty was on the same seat he had been on the Thursday before, and the place behind that woman's chair was empty and had been empty for as long as anybody in that room could remember, and nobody at that bench looked at it.**

Nobody asked anybody anything at all.

Nothing whatever happened in it.

**And then at about seven the man of about thirty said the only sentence anybody said in that room for about nine minutes, and about nine people in that shed have said since that they counted it and that not one of them was waiting for the asking to start.**

The man of about thirty said: **Nobody has asked me anything.**

Marek said: **Nobody has asked anybody anything and that is not about you.**

The man of about thirty said: **I know that.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **Can I say something.**

Marek said: **You can say it and nobody in here is going to ask you what you were asked.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **I am not going to say it.**

Marek said: **Then you have had your about nine minutes.**

---

**And then that man of about thirty said out loud what has come back through two districts, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it to that bench and not to one person in it and that nobody in that bench asked him a question about it afterwards.**

The man of about thirty said: **What has come back is not the answer.**

Marek said: **No. It is not.**

The man of about thirty said: **It is what happened after it, and somebody has told it wrong and better.**

Marek said: **Then that is the part I cannot mend, and it is not about the man.**

The man of about thirty said: **I know. I am not asking you to.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who answered three things out loud in that room a week before said out loud on that Tuesday that what has come back through two districts is not his answer but what the room did after it, and that somebody has told it wrong and better, and that about four people at that bench put nothing in at all for about nine minutes on the Thursday before this one.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and he was still there at about half past ten.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a fitting with a lamp in it that had a loose connection inside it and it had been in that state for about four weeks.

He changed the lamp and proved the light from the floor with the shade on.

**Twenty-one pounds**, he said. **A loose connection is a lamp that somebody has been waiting for somebody else to notice.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate with two of the holes at an angle and a piece of tin behind them.

He put that plate out and put a proper one in and proved the socket from the gate in the wet.

**Seventeen pounds**, he said. **Two holes at an angle is a plate somebody has cut with the wrong thing.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the grill wired to the light and the light wired to the grill.

He unwired the two of them and proved the light and the grill separately with the oven off.

**Twenty-three pounds**, he said. **A grill and a light wired to each other is a cooker you cannot cook and cannot light.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with a shade that had been screwed into the ceiling about four inches off the lamp.

He took that shade down, put it back on the lamp and proved the light from the floor.

**Fifteen pounds**, he said. **A shade screwed to a ceiling four inches off a lamp is a shade that is about nine inches away from the thing.**

**Seventy-six pounds is the sum of those four on that Tuesday, exact.**

*Conditions of the close.* **That book in the green binding was shut on that table from the time that room opened until the time that room shut and it was shut at ten, and the tin beside it had its lid down at every hour of that evening, and no figure for either and no difference between them is printed in this file.
That ninth chair stood with its back to that room for every hour of that Tuesday and did not turn on a leg at any point, and the hand that last turned it by a leg is named on no page of this volume and on no page of any other in this city.
The room under that building in a first district was dark at ten on that Tuesday and is dark at this hour, and it does not get opened on any of the days this volume runs.
That register of correct acts that changed nothing said four at both ends of that Tuesday, nobody in this city is entered on it, and nine minutes in which nobody was asked anything is not an entry on it either.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named at the foot of this page and every one of them is in a sentence of its own. Two nicks cut into that rail. That card with its one crease and the three questions on it and nothing ruled over them. The lid on that tin. Nine torn corners off nine separate fronts. A board up on two nails. That shutter. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back of that shelf. An envelope on that bench with its flap up, and it is still empty. A pencil lying in a margin.
Six are named here outside those ten and none of them is counted with them: the cage down the long side of that counter, a carrier bag under a long bench with a hundred and fifty sheets in it, a plastic sleeve in a drawer in a woman's own flat with nine words and a date on it, a folded paper in a second district's drawer with one name struck through above another written under it, a leaflet taped under the lower of two clean rectangles on a wall in a fourth district, and a page with nine names standing on it and nothing else whatever on it.
A dated rule stands over anything filed behind it on a question this page does not print, and somebody who asks about one of those gets shown it.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody. A man who answered three things out loud in that shed on the Thursday before this one came back on that Tuesday and was asked nothing at all for about nine minutes; about four people at that bench put nothing in at all for about nine minutes on that Thursday and not one of them said a kind thing to anybody in that time; and the envelope on that bench is still empty and the card in that rail is still up and the light in that shed is still being paid for by one man out of his own money.**
