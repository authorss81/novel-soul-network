## 1. WHAT THIS MOVEMENT IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

**On the Thursday a man of about thirty who had been asked three things at that bench three weeks before and had answered two of them came back and was asked all three again, one at a time, out loud, with everybody in that room hearing every one, and he answered all three in his own words.** What he would put in was about a kitchen in a second district and about a woman in that kitchen he is not related to, and no page of this movement prints it and no page carries it out of the room it was said in; what he would keep out was four days and a bus and about four people; and who could stop him was his mother saying so. **About four people at that bench then put nothing in at all for about nine minutes and not one of those four said a kind thing to anybody in that time.** On the Friday two of the people who had been in that room said their own two halves at a counter without being asked, and a man who had been asking for nine weeks asked out loud how many people had been in the shed the night before and was told, out loud, that there has not been one number said aloud in that room since he sat down, and that this is not a rule and that the man in charge of nothing is not going to make it into one. On the Sunday a man stood outside that door for about nine minutes in the wet and did not knock, and nobody inside looked at the door and nobody sent him away, and he was inside by the time Marek got there and was asked nothing at all for about forty minutes. On the Monday a woman of about sixty-two who had walked past that door for about nine years came into it because there was a light on, sat at the back for about an hour and said four ordinary sentences, and the man who had two things to ask her asked her neither. **On the Tuesday the man of about fifty-one said out loud, for the first time in three weeks, that the light in that shed is his to pay for, and a woman asked out loud whether Marek had ever worked in a kitchen in a second district and was told he had not and goes past that door four days a week.** On the Wednesday the man who had been asking for nine weeks asked the last of the people he had not asked, got an answer back, and four people in that room each said out loud which of the answers already given that week they thought it was, and all four of them picked a different one and none of them picked the one that had been given, and he said out loud that eight answers to one question is a rhyme and not a record. On the Thursday the man of about thirty came back and nobody asked him anything at all for about two hours, and a woman who has been in that shed twice was told at that counter that what she has done is worth the same as a lamp and that the lamp is on a sheet. **On the Friday a woman of about forty-five brought into that shop the news that a man at a counter in another district has been talking about that shed, and could not repeat a word of it, and that the only parts about four people in that shop could get were a kitchen, and a woman, and about nine people not saying anything.** On the Monday about four people spent half an hour looking at an envelope with nothing behind it and a woman of about fifty-eight said out loud that she has money in her coat and is not going to put it in tonight. **On the Tuesday nobody asked anybody anything at all for about nine minutes, and nothing whatever happened, and the man of about thirty said out loud that what has come back through two districts is not his answer but what the room did after it.**

**AND WHAT IT IS NOT.** It is not a chapter about a villain, and there is no villain in it. **It contains no record, no form, no signature, no list of who came, no heading for the fifth column, no printed notice, and no page on which Marek corrects anything.** It contains no page on which the woman of about thirty-nine is asked anything by anybody, and none on which she is thanked, forgiven or written down, and the string `woman of about thirty-nine` does not stand on any of these ten files. **It contains no page on which the woman of about thirty-four appears at all, and none on which the third question is put to anybody but the man of about thirty, once, on Chapter 1091.** It does not join the two halves of the second sentence on any page, and it does not print the first sentence or quote it. **It spends no part of the envelope, which is still empty and is not counted by anybody; it puts no figure for the woman's page on any page; it prints no Exchange figure and no difference between the book and the tin and remarks on no sitting.** Talia is on no page of this movement at all, and nobody in this movement asks Marek anything, and he asks her nothing.
