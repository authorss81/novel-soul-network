# Chapter 1095 — Paying For A Light Nobody Asked Him To

**A Tuesday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the middle of that Tuesday was spent at that counter by a man of about fifty-one who had not said out loud for three weeks that the light in that shed is his to pay for.**

---

Ten callers came into that shop on that Tuesday and ten names were written against that Tuesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and twenty-four days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks and one day. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and twenty-eight days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks and five days. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is one hundred and ninety-seven days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and eighty-three days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and seventy-four days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had the cloth off the bowl and had put a saucer over the bowl instead, and about four people in that room have said since that it is the same bowl and it is not the same cover.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have covered it with a saucer.**

Marek said: **That is worse.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It is a saucer.**

Marek said: **It is a saucer and it will slide off at about nine o'clock and put the fly back on the sill, and then you and I will both be wrong about it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You can come back and put it on at nine.**

Marek said: **I can, and I will not, and the reason is that you would rather I did not.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That is the reason I said it.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-one came into that shop at about half past four and said out loud, in front of about four people, what he had not said for three weeks, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said it flatly to the counter and not to anybody near him and that nobody in that shop knew he was going to say it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **The light in that shed is mine to pay for and I am not going to tell you what it is.**

Marek said: **I am not going to ask you what it is.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I know you are not. I am saying it before somebody else says it.**

Marek said: **Somebody else said it three weeks ago and you have not said it since.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Then somebody has been saying it and I would like to be the one who says it.**

Marek said: **Noted and not repeated.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-eight came in about ten minutes after that and asked Marek, out loud, whether he had ever worked in a kitchen in a second district, and he said he had not and that he goes past that door four days a week, and about four people in that shop have said since that he answered that without a pause and that he did not ask her why she wanted to know.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **Have you worked in a kitchen in a second district.**

Marek said: **No.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **You go past it.**

Marek said: **Four days a week and I have not been inside it and I am not going to be.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **There is a woman in there who has been in about nine of these rooms.**

Marek said: **I know there is and I have not asked her anything and I am not going to start on a Tuesday.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **I am not asking you to start.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that shop have said since that a man who has been paying for a light in a shed for three weeks out of his own money said so out loud at a counter, and that a woman asked about a kitchen in a second district and got four sentences and no more than that.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, where about nine people were in it and nobody asked the man of about thirty anything because the man of about thirty was not in that room on that Tuesday.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a pendant drop of about nine inches from a rose that had been moved two inches across the ceiling from where it had been.

He took the drop back to the rose and proved the lamp from a chair.

**Sixteen pounds**, he said. **A rose moved two inches is nine inches of flex somebody has paid for.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate with the moulding split at one corner and the split left open.

He closed that split and proved the socket from the gate with an instrument on it.

**Twenty-three pounds**, he said. **A plate split at the corner is a plate that has been struck once and will be struck again.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the fan wired to the light so that the fan came on with the light and went off with the light.

He put the fan on a switch of its own and proved the fan running with the light off.

**Eleven pounds**, he said. **A fan on a light switch is a fan that only works in the dark.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with a length of flex inside it that had been cut short to make the fitting reach the hole.

He dropped that flex out to its proper length and proved the light from the floor.

**Twenty pounds**, he said. **A flex cut short to reach is a flex that is carrying more than it is meant to.**

**Seventy pounds is the sum of those four on that Tuesday, exact.**
