# Chapter 1091 — It Came Out In His Own Words

**A Thursday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and after half past six the whole of that Thursday went across a city to a shed on a road in a fourth district where a man who had been asked things there before sat down at a bench.**

---

Thirteen callers came into that shop on that Thursday and thirteen names were written against that Thursday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and nineteen days, two hundred and eighty-eight weeks and three days. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and twenty-three days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks to the day. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is one hundred and ninety-two days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and seventy-eight days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and sixty-nine days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had the flex lamp on the table in front of her with the flex wound round her hand and had not noticed she was doing it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **How long have you been watching that.**

Marek said: **Since you came in.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That is not an answer to the question.**

Marek said: **No. It is the answer to the question you are going to ask next.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You are going to be insufferable on a Thursday.**

Marek said: **I am going to be the same on every day and you get a Thursday of it.**

---

**And then at about half past four a man of about thirty-two came into that shop with a lamp on a lead and asked whether anybody there fitted lamps in a building that was not a shop, and Marek gave him the trade price for the same work and wrote it on that Thursday's line, and about four people in that shop have said since that the man did not argue and did not ask for anything to go on the sheet.**

The man of about thirty-two said: **It is a stair, not a shop.**

Marek said: **A stair is a room with a light in it.**

The man of about thirty-two said: **Then that is what I will tell them I was told.**

Marek said: **Tell them that and not the other thing.**

The man of about thirty-two said: **Which other thing.**

Marek said: **That it was a shop. That is the whole of the other thing.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about nine people were in it, and the man of about thirty was already standing inside the door with his coat still on, and about four people in that room have said since that he had not been in that room since the Thursday before the one he was stood in.**

Marek said: **Coat off or coat on.**

The man of about thirty said: **Coat on. I am not staying.**

Marek said: **I did not ask you to stay and I am not going to ask you to stay again.**

The man of about thirty said: **You said that last time as well.**

Marek said: **I did. And you came back, so whatever I said was not the reason.**

---

**And then he said the thing he had come to say, out loud, before he had taken his coat off, and about four people in that shed have said since that he said it to the middle of the room and not to one person in it.**

The man of about thirty said: **I have come back to say the same three.**

Marek said: **Say the same three.**

The man of about thirty said: **I have had three weeks to find out whether I would say them the same, and I will not, and I have known that for about four of those days and I have come anyway.**

Marek said: **Then that is the whole of what you came to find out.**

The man of about thirty said: **No. I came to find out whether I could say three things in front of about nine people.**

---

**And then Marek asked the three, one at a time and out loud, with everybody in that room hearing all three being asked, and about nine people in that shed have said since that nobody in it had said anything to him beforehand and that he answered all three.**

Marek said: **I am going to ask you three things. One at a time, out loud, with everybody here hearing it, and you can answer all three or none of them, and if you answer none of them you can sit down and stay and nobody in here will say one word about it.**

The man of about thirty said: **Ask.**

Marek said: **That is the first of three. What would you put in.**

The man of about thirty said: **Then take it, and do not ask me for the words back.**

Marek said: **Noted and not written.**

Nobody said anything.

The woman of about forty-five said: **I thought it would be about his mother.**

The man of about thirty said: **It is not about my mother and it never was going to be.**

---

**And then it came out of him in about nine seconds in that room, out loud, and it was about a kitchen in a second district and about a woman in that kitchen he is not related to, and about four people in that shed have said since that nobody in it had expected either half of it and that one of them had said on the way in that it would be about him and about his sister.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

The man of about fifty-one said: **I did not think that.**

The man of about thirty said: **I did not either. That is why I have not said it before.**

Marek said: **That is the second of three. What would you keep out.**

The man of about thirty said: **Four days, and a bus, and about four people.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **That was shorter than the first.**

The man of about thirty said: **It is four things and I am not padding it out so that it sounds like the first.**

---

**And then the third one was asked, and he answered it, and about four people in that shed have said since that they had spent three weeks assuming this was the one he could not do.**

Marek said: **That is the third of three. Who could stop you.**

The man of about thirty said: **Nobody can stop me except my mother saying so.**

Nobody said anything.

The man of about fifty-one said: **Your mother.**

The man of about thirty said: **She has not asked me once in three years and she is not going to start on a Thursday.**

Marek said: **That is three.**

The man of about thirty said: **That is three.**

---

**And then about four people at that bench put nothing in at all for about nine minutes, and about nine people in that room have said since that they counted those minutes against the clock on that wall and that not one of those four said a kind thing to anybody in that time, and that the fifth person who spoke in it was asked whether it had been about nine minutes and was told that it had.**

The man of about fifty-one put both hands flat on that bench and did not say anything at all.

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **I have not got one.**

The man of about forty-six said: **I have got one and it is not going to be better out of me than it was in.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **Mine is not either, and I have said that out loud twice this month.**

Nobody moved a chair.

The man of about twenty-two said: **Was that nine minutes.**

Marek said: **That was about nine minutes.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **Then nobody has put anything in.**

---

**And then a man of about twenty-eight came in about four minutes after it started again and asked what had been said, and about four people in that room have said since that three of them told him and one of them told him nothing at all, and that none of them told him what the first answer was.**

The man of about twenty-eight said: **Has he gone.**

The man of about thirty said: **I am stood here.**

The man of about twenty-eight said: **I am not going to pretend I did not know that.**

The man of about thirty said: **Then do not pretend it, and do not ask anybody else what I said either.**

The man of about twenty-eight said: **I was not going to.**

The man of about thirty said: **You were going to, and that is fine, everybody is, and it is easier if you say so.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man who had been asked three things in front of about nine people three weeks ago and had answered two of them came back and answered all three, and about four people at that bench then put nothing in at all for about nine minutes and not one of them said a kind thing to anybody in that time.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, where the rails were still under the floor and the doors were off their tracks and the card in that rail had been turned round so that the crease was on the other way up.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a pendant with the ceiling rose left hanging off two screws with a washer under one of them and not the other.

He took the rose down, packed the two screws properly and proved the lamp from the middle of that floor.

**Twenty-four pounds**, he said. **A rose hanging off two screws is a lamp waiting for a ceiling that has had a bad day.**

A socket in that yard had been put in under a lid with a gasket laid in dry and gone hard round the whole of the rim.

He lifted the gasket out, laid a new one with its face and proved that socket in the wet with the lid shut again over it.

**Seventeen pounds**, he said. **A gasket laid in dry is a lid that has already let the water in and is about to let it in again.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the grill element wired to the same terminal as the oven light and switched by the oven switch.

He separated them and proved the oven coming up, the grill coming up, and the oven going off when the oven goes off.

**Twenty-two pounds**, he said. **One switch on two elements is a cooker that cooks nothing the way the knob says it cooks.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with the earth tail left long and folded back under the fixing screw instead of made off.

He made the tail off at the terminal where it belongs and proved the fitting with the door shut and the instrument on the plate.

**Fifteen pounds**, he said. **A tail folded under a screw is an earth that a screw can undo without anybody noticing.**

**Seventy-eight pounds is the sum of those four on that Thursday, exact.**
