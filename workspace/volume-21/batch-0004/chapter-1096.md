# Chapter 1096 — It Sounds Like A Rhyme

**A Wednesday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the whole of the evening of that Wednesday went into a shed on a road in a fourth district where a man who has been asking for about nine weeks asked the last of the people he had not asked.**

---

Fourteen callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and fourteen names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about a quarter to nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and twenty-five days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks and two days. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and twenty-nine days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks and six days. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is one hundred and ninety-eight days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and eighty-four days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and seventy-five days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had the saucer still on the bowl and had put a second saucer on the first one, and about four people in that room have said since that neither saucer is holding and that nobody in that room has said so.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **There are two saucers on it now.**

Marek said: **That is a solution and not an accident.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then it is Wednesday.**

Marek said: **It is, and you are about nine days early with the second one.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then I will put them both back on Thursday.**

Marek said: **You will not, and I would like to know what you are waiting for.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That is between me and the saucers.**

---

**And then at about half past four a man of about twenty-two came into that shop to collect a cooker hood fan that had been in for about four weeks and had not been collected, and about four people in that shop have said since that he had been in that shed on the Wednesday three weeks ago and had put nothing in that night and had not been asked anything by anybody since.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **Is it ready.**

Marek said: **It has been ready for about four weeks.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **Nobody rang me.**

Marek said: **I have not got your number and I am not going to go through that book for it.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **I would have come back.**

Marek said: **You would have, and that is not a fault of yours or of mine, and it is one fan.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **It is about the only thing I have paid for this year.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about nine people were in it, and the man of about fifty-one asked the man of about twenty-two, out loud and in front of the whole of that bench, the same question he has asked everybody, and about four people in that room have said since that he asked it exactly as he has asked it all week.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **What were you asked.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **Three things.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **And what you said back.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **About half of it and about four people in front of me.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **What was the half.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **A thing I have not done in about four years and a person who was standing near me.**

---

**And then about four people in that room each said out loud which of the answers already given that week they thought that one was, and about four people in that room have said since that the four of them all picked a different one, and that not one of them picked the one that was actually given, and that one of them said so out loud.**

The man of about forty-six said: **That is the one about a hospital.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **That is the one about a car park at night.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **That is the one about a front step.**

The man of about twenty-eight said: **It is not any of them and that is the one I would put my name to.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **I have not told him anything else and I am not going to.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-one said out loud that eight answers to one question is a rhyme and not a record, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it to the bench and not to any one person in it and that nobody in that bench argued with him about it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Eight answers to one question is a rhyme and it is not a record and I have been asking for the wrong thing for about nine weeks.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **There were nine of us on that Wednesday and not eight, and you have never once counted us.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **There were eight when I started asking and I have not asked you.**

Marek said: **You have been asking for about nine weeks and nobody has told you that you cannot have it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You told me that on the first Sunday.**

Marek said: **I told you that you cannot have what you asked for. I did not tell you that what you have is nothing, and it is not nothing, and I have not got a way of saying the other half of that.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is twice now you have stopped halfway.**

Marek said: **It is, and you have counted it, and you did not have to be asked to.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty said the only thing he said in that room on that Wednesday, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it to the man of about fifty-one and not to the bench and that nobody else in that room heard it.**

The man of about thirty said: **You have got about nine answers and about four of them rhyme.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is the first useful thing anybody has said in that room.**

The man of about thirty said: **You have also got them out of order, and the order is the only part anybody can check.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You have never asked me for one.**

The man of about thirty said: **No. And I am not going to.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man who has been asking for about nine weeks asked the last of the people he had not asked and got an answer that four other people in that room recognised as somebody else's, and about nine people in that room have said since that nobody could tell them apart at the end of it.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, where the card in that rail had a pencil mark beside it that had been there about four weeks and had not been rubbed out.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a fitting with a fuse holder that had a piece of wire across it instead of a fuse.

He took that wire out, put a fuse in and proved the lamp from the floor.

**Twenty-seven pounds**, he said. **A wire across a fuse holder is somebody who has decided the fuse was in the way.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a length of conduit with a join in it outside and that join packed with a rag.

He cut that join out and made it properly and proved the socket in the wet.

**Fifteen pounds**, he said. **A rag in a join in the rain is a rag that will be a hole by Thursday.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with two elements on one terminal and the third one on the other side of the board.

He put all three on terminals of their own and proved the cooker coming up on each in turn.

**Eighteen pounds**, he said. **Two elements on one side and one on the other is a cooker that is tested half at a time.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with the flex run through a screw eye that had been screwed into the plaster.

He took the flex out of that screw eye and put a proper grip on it and proved the light with the shade on.

**Twelve pounds**, he said. **Flex through a screw eye is flex that somebody was pleased with for about four days.**

**Seventy-two pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**
