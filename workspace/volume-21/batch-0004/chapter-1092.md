# Chapter 1092 — What He Put In Was Somewhere Else

**A Friday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the middle of that Friday belonged to a woman of about thirty-eight and a man of about forty-six who had both been in that shed the night before and had both put nothing in at all.**

---

Eleven callers came into that shop on that Friday and eleven names were written against that Friday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about a quarter to ten, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and twenty days, two hundred and eighty-eight weeks and four days. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and twenty-four days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks and one day. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is one hundred and ninety-three days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and seventy-nine days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and seventy days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had the flex lamp back on the wall and had put a coat hanger under the flex where it hangs so that the weight comes off the rose.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I put a hanger under it.**

Marek said: **That is the correct thing to do about a flex.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Do not tell anybody that.**

Marek said: **I have not told anybody about the flex in about four years.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then you are the only person in this city who has not.**

Marek said: **I am not, and you know that I am not.**

---

**And then at about half past three the woman of about thirty-eight came into that shop with her coat wet through at the shoulders and asked for a dry sheet of paper for no reason at all, and Marek gave her one, and about four people in that shop have said since that she did not use it and carried it out folded in two.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **This is not for writing anything on.**

Marek said: **I did not ask what it is for.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **You never do.**

Marek said: **No.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **And you are told the same thing every time you ask me nothing.**

Marek said: **That is not a thing anybody can complain about.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **You can, but not to me.**

---

**And then the man of about forty-six came into that shop at about four o'clock and stood at that counter and said out loud what he would have put in and what he would have kept out, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said both halves without being asked and that neither of them was longer than the other.**

The man of about forty-six said: **A front step in a third district and my mother on it.**

Marek said: **And what you keep out.**

The man of about forty-six said: **That the two of us had not spoken for about four months.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **You told me that in a different order.**

The man of about forty-six said: **You asked me in a different order.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **I did not ask you anything.**

The man of about forty-six said: **Then you have been rearranging it in your own head and I would stop before it turns into a thing I said.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-one came into that shop at about half past four and asked Marek, out loud in front of about four people, whether he was going to ask that man again, and got back an answer that was not a promise in either direction, and about four people in that shop have said since that he asked it as though the answer were a date.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Are you going to ask him again.**

Marek said: **He comes on Thursdays and I ask on Thursdays.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is not an answer.**

Marek said: **It is the only one I have got that is not a lie, and you are welcome to the other kind.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I have been trying for about nine weeks to get one of those out of this room.**

Marek said: **I know what you have been trying for.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Then you know what I am going to do next.**

Marek said: **I know what you said you would do next, and so do about four people in this shop, and it has not been done.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that shop have said since that a man of about forty-six said two things about his mother without being asked, and that the man who wants an account of an evening did not get one word of it out of anybody in that room.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, where about nine people were in it and nobody asked anybody anything at all.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a fitting with the flex coming out of the bottom of it instead of the side and resting on the metal.

He made that entry at the side and proved the lamp from a chair with the shade on.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said. **A flex resting on the metal of its own fitting is a fitting that will be worn through from the inside.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on an enclosure with the lid on and nothing behind the lid at all.

He fitted a gland into that enclosure and proved the socket out in the wet with an instrument on it.

**Twenty-six pounds**, he said. **A lid on nothing is a lid that is there to be seen and not to be used.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a socket outlet on the splashback wired off the same terminal as the cooker.

He moved the outlet onto a terminal of its own and proved both of them with the oven on.

**Fourteen pounds**, he said. **An outlet sharing a terminal with a cooker is an outlet that gets tested with the cooker.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a plate with the screw heads left proud enough to catch a cloth.

He drew them back into the plate and proved the light from the floor with the door shut.

**Eighteen pounds**, he said. **A proud screw head on a plate is a cloth with a hole torn in it.**

**Seventy-seven pounds is the sum of those four on that Friday, exact.**
