# Chapter 1098 — It Is Going About Without Him

**A Friday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the middle of that Friday belonged to a woman of about forty-five who came into that shop and said that a man at a counter in a fourth district had said something about that shed which she could not repeat.**

---

Twelve callers came into that shop on that Friday and twelve names were written against that Friday's book as each of them came in, and the shutter came down at ten that night with nobody still in that shop.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and twenty-seven days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks and four days. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and thirty-one days, two hundred and ninety weeks and one day. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is two hundred days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and eighty-six days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and seventy-seven days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had the cloth off the bowl and the saucers washed and stacked on the sill, and about four people in that room have said since that she has given up on the fly entirely and is now feeding it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **There is bread in it.**

Marek said: **There is bread in it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You are not going to say anything about that.**

Marek said: **I am going to say one thing and then I am going to leave it alone.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Say the one thing.**

Marek said: **He will not be back for about nine days and that is a fact about him and not about the bread.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then it is about the bread after all.**

---

**And then the woman of about forty-five came into that shop at about half past three with her coat open and said that a man at a counter in a fourth district had said something about that shed, and about four people in that shop have said since that she said it standing at that counter and that she could not say it again afterwards when two of them asked her to.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **Somebody at a counter has been talking about your shed.**

Marek said: **About this shop.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **About a room on a road in a fourth district.**

Marek said: **Then that is not my shop and I cannot do anything about it.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **I know you cannot. That is not why I am here.**

Marek said: **Then say why you are here.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **Because I have been in that room four times and I never told you and I have only now found out what it is called.**

---

**And then Marek asked her, out loud, what the words were, and she said she could not repeat them, and about four people in that shop have said since that he asked her once and did not ask her again, and that the only parts about four people in that shop could get were a kitchen, and a woman, and about nine people not saying anything.**

Marek said: **Say the words.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **I cannot say them. I heard them about nine seconds and I have not got them.**

Marek said: **Then what have you got.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **A kitchen, and a woman, and about nine people not saying anything.**

The man of about thirty-two said: **That is not a sentence.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **It is what came out of it and there was a queue behind him.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty came into that shop at about half past five for a bulb and stood at that counter while it was got for him, and the woman of about forty-five told him what she had been told, out loud, with the man of about thirty-two still in the shop, and about four people in that shop have said since that he asked her three questions in about four seconds, that the shortest of them was one word, and that he did not look up from the bulb until she had finished.**

The man of about thirty said: **How many of them.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **Four that I have seen and two of them have said where they heard it.**

The man of about thirty said: **Where.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **One at a counter in a first district and one on a bus.**

The man of about thirty said: **A bus.**

The woman of about forty-five said: **That is what she said, and improving on it is not this woman's job.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that shop have said since that a man answered three things out loud in a shed nine days ago and the only parts of it that have come back through two districts are a kitchen, and a woman, and about nine people not saying anything.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, where about nine people were in it and about four of them had heard about the bus.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a fitting with the lamp itself twisted in the holder so that it sat on the thread.

He turned it down, seated it and proved the lamp from a chair.

**Twenty-two pounds**, he said. **A lamp sitting on the thread is a lamp somebody has got in and about two weeks to wait for.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on an extension with a pin bent back about a quarter of an inch.

He straightened the pin and proved the socket from the gate in the wet.

**Sixteen pounds**, he said. **A bent pin in a plug is a plug that somebody has already used and put back.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a lead out of the flex under the tap on the wall.

He took the lead out of that flex and put it in a proper gland and proved the cooker at the tap.

**Twenty-six pounds**, he said. **A joint under a tap is a joint that is wet about four times a day.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a rose with about two inches of flex hanging out of it below the ceiling.

He pulled that flex back up into the rose and proved the light from a chair.

**Fifteen pounds**, he said. **Two inches of flex below a ceiling is about two inches of something to hold.**

**Seventy-nine pounds is the sum of those four on that Friday, exact.**
