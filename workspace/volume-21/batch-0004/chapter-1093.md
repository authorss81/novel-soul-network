# Chapter 1093 — He Stood Outside In The Wet

**A Sunday was a bench until about two and the shutter came down at about two on it, and the whole of that Sunday after two was a man standing outside a shed on a road in a fourth district in the wet and four people inside it who did not ask him to come in.**

---

Four callers came into that shop on that Sunday and four names were written against that Sunday's book as each of them came in, all of them before noon, and the shutter came down at about two that day.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and twenty-two days, two hundred and eighty-eight weeks and six days. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and twenty-six days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks and three days. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is one hundred and ninety-five days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and eighty-one days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and seventy-two days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had a bowl of water on the sill and was taking a fly out of it every ten minutes or so and putting it outside on the sill of the landing.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It is the same fly.**

Marek said: **It is not the same fly and it is the same kind.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You have made that worse.**

Marek said: **I have made it a different conversation and it was a bad one before.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Put it in the water.**

Marek said: **It will drown and then it will be a different fly in the water.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then it can drown in peace.**

---

**And then that Sunday shut at about two and he went out into the wet at about ten past three and walked the length of that road twice with nothing to do until about half past six, and about four people who walk that road have said since that a man stood outside that shed for about nine minutes and did not knock once.**

A man of about thirty-six stood outside that shed with his hood up and his hands in his pockets.

Nobody inside that shed looked at the door.

About nine minutes passed with that door shut and nobody knocking on it.

The man of about thirty-six put his hand flat on the door and then took it off again and did not knock.

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about four people were in it, and the man of about thirty-six was inside it with his hood still up and had been for about four minutes, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody asked him how long he had been outside and that nobody asked him anything at all, and that not one of them has said since how long he was stood at that door.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **I was outside.**

Marek said: **I did not say you were not.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **I was about to knock and then I did not, and I cannot tell you why.**

Marek said: **Then do not tell me. It will keep until you can.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **It might not.**

Marek said: **Then it will not.**

---

**And then that man of about thirty-six said out loud that he had come on something he was told at a counter, and Marek asked him nothing at all about it, and about four people in that shed have said since that he said the words `I was told at a counter` once and that nobody in that room asked him which counter or by whom or when.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **A woman at a counter told me what happens in here and I have not got anything of my own to put in.**

Marek said: **Then you have not got to put anything in.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **I know that. I have come to see whether the room is like the sentence.**

Marek said: **I could not tell you, and I have not been told.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **You have been in here.**

Marek said: **I have, and that is not the same as being told, and I am not going to describe the place to you tonight.**

---

**And then nobody asked that man anything else for about forty minutes and he said nothing else, and about four people in that room have said since that they have been coming for years and have never once watched a person sit in that room and not be asked anything.**

The man of about thirty-six said, at about half past seven: **This is the quietest room in this city.**

Marek said: **It is the wettest.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **That is not an answer.**

Marek said: **No. But it is the one that took about nine seconds and it is about the room and not about anything in it.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **That is two sentences.**

Marek said: **It is, and I am aware of both of them.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-one came into that shed at about eight o'clock, and about four people in that room have said since that he did not say good evening, and that he went straight to that meter on the wall and put money into it, and that nobody in that room watched him do it except one person.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **It is still going.**

Marek said: **It is still going.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I have not been near it to check.**

Marek said: **I know you have not, and I am not going to say that to anybody who did not ask me.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is the first sentence in about three weeks that was for me.**

Marek said: **You did not ask me for it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **No. I have noticed.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that room have said since that a man stood outside that door for about nine minutes and nobody asked him to come in and nobody sent him away either, and that a man who has been putting money into that meter since a Tuesday did not say so once and was not thanked for it.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road where one of them carries heat, and he had not been inside any of them since about Thursday.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with a terminal block hanging out of it on about four inches of flex and tied to itself.

He re-made the entry, took the tie off and proved the lamp from a chair with the shade on.

**Twelve pounds**, he said. **A block tied to itself is somebody who did not have the correct length and would not say so.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on an enclosure with two of its screws out and lying in the bottom of it.

He found both screws, made good the enclosure and proved the socket in the wet with the lid shut.

**Twenty-one pounds**, he said. **Screws in the bottom of their own box are a box that has been opened and put back without being made good.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a flex that went behind the cooker and came back out through the same hole.

He took it back out through the hole it should have gone through and proved the cooker with the door open.

**Nine pounds**, he said. **A flex that goes in behind a thing and comes out through the same hole has been shortened by somebody with a knife.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with a lamp in it that had a different holder on the end of it.

He took that lamp out, put one in that fits and proved the light from the door with the shade on.

**Sixteen pounds**, he said. **A lamp with somebody else's holder on the end is a lamp that will be in about nine weeks.**

**Fifty-eight pounds is the sum of those four on that Sunday, exact, and it is the smallest day's work on that sheet since about four weeks ago.**
