# Chapter 1094 — She Came And She Was Not Asked For It

**A Monday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the middle of that Monday was spent in a bus shelter in a fourth district with a woman of about sixty-two who came to that shed for the first time in about nine years and said nothing.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Monday and nine names were written against that Monday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and twenty-three days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks to the day. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and twenty-seven days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks and four days. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is one hundred and ninety-six days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and eighty-two days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and seventy-three days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had two pans of water on the sill and had given up on the fly entirely and had put a clean cloth over the bowl.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have covered it.**

Marek said: **It will be there when you take it off.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That is not why I covered it.**

Marek said: **Then tell me why you covered it and I will stop guessing about the fly.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I did not say I would tell you.**

Marek said: **No. You said you had not.**

---

**And then at about half past three he was in a bus shelter in a fourth district with a woman of about sixty-two who had been in it since about two o'clock, and she said out loud that she had walked past that door for about nine years and had never gone in, and about four people who have walked that road have said since that she was stood at the end of that shelter with a bag in both hands and that she had not been stood there for anybody else.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **I have walked past that door nine years and never gone in.**

Marek said: **Is that a complaint.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **It is not anything. I am telling you where I have been.**

Marek said: **You have told me. Do you want me to ask you anything.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **I did not come for anything and I have not brought anything.**

---

**And then he walked about four hundred yards with her to that shed and did not tell her anything about what was in it, and about four people on that road have said since that she asked him twice on the way what it was and that he gave her the same two sentences both times and that neither of them told her anything.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **Is there a list at the door.**

Marek said: **There is not.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **Then how would anybody know.**

Marek said: **They would not, and that has not stopped anybody for about nine years.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **Somebody at a counter told me about a woman and a room.**

Marek said: **There are about four of those in this city.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **Then you will not tell me which.**

Marek said: **No.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **Good. Neither will I, and that is not going to change tonight.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about nine people were in it, and the woman of about sixty-two came in about four minutes after he did and sat down at the back with her bag on her knees, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody spoke to her for the first half hour and that she did not look at anybody.**

The woman of about sixty-two said, to Marek: **There is a light on in here that somebody has paid for.**

Marek said: **There is.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **I have known about that light for about four years.**

Marek said: **You have not told me and I have not asked you.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **I am telling you now because I have seen it and I would rather somebody had heard it from me.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-one came in at about seven and looked at her for about four seconds and then looked away, and about four people in that room have said since that he had two things to ask her and asked her neither, and that one of the two was not about the light.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You have come out on the bus.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **I have not. I have walked from the shelter.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **It is a long way from the shelter in this.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **I have done about nine years of it.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **Nobody has asked me for anything and I would like to keep it that way for about an hour.**

Marek said: **You have about an hour.**

The woman of about sixty-two said: **Then I will have had a look at the floor and I will go home.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who had walked past that door for about nine years sat at the back of it for an hour and said four ordinary sentences and not the one anybody in that room was in there for, and that nobody asked her for it, and that she left at about eight and somebody walked out with her to the end of that road and said nothing on the way.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and then at about eight he walked a woman of about sixty-two to the end of that road and went back across the city and worked until about ten.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a switch plate with a four amp fuse behind it and a thirteen amp fuse in it.

He put the four amp fuse in and proved the lamp from the floor.

**Twenty-five pounds**, he said. **A big fuse behind a small plate is a plate waiting for somebody to change it back.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a metal box with a cable coming in through the lid and clamped by the lid itself.

He brought the cable in through a gland in the wall of that box and proved the socket in the wet.

**Thirteen pounds**, he said. **A cable clamped by its own lid is a lid that has to come off every time you look at it.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the clock wired so that it ran whether that cooker was on or not.

He took the clock off that supply and proved the clock running off the cooker alone.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said. **A clock that runs on the supply and not on the oven is a clock that lies about your oven.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with its earth left off entirely and a green and yellow conductor coiled inside the metal.

He brought that conductor out and made it off to the fitting and proved the fitting from the floor.

**Twenty-three pounds**, he said. **An earth coiled inside the metal is an earth that a fitter put there so that he would not be asked about it.**

**Eighty pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Monday or on any other day, and no figure for what it holds is written anywhere in this file.**

The day's four jobs came to eighty pounds, exact.**
