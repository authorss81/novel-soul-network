# Chapter 1097 — Nobody Asked Him Anything At All

**A Thursday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and that Thursday evening was the second Thursday a man stood at that bench in a shed on a road in a fourth district and came away from it having not been asked anything.**

---

Eight callers came into that shop on that Thursday and eight names were written against that Thursday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about eight, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and twenty-six days, two hundred and eighty-nine weeks and three days. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and thirty days, two hundred and ninety weeks to the day. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is one hundred and ninety-nine days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and eighty-five days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and seventy-six days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had taken both saucers off the bowl on Wednesday night and had come in on the Thursday morning and found the fly on the sill, and about four people in that room have said since that she said nothing about it and put the cloth back over the bowl.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It was there all night.**

Marek said: **It was.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **And you told me it would be.**

Marek said: **I told you a saucer would slide off at about nine o'clock. I did not tell you which nine o'clock.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You knew what you were about.**

Marek said: **I knew what the saucer was about. The fly has been about nine years and it has never once been about a saucer.**

---

**And then a woman of about fifty-eight came into that shop at about four o'clock and told Marek, out loud and unasked, that she had been in that shed twice and had put nothing in either time, and asked him to make a note of the two visits, and Marek told her that no note is made of that, and about four people in that shop have said since that she came in expecting to be refused and left as though she had been offered something.**

The woman of about fifty-eight said: **I have been twice and I have nothing.**

Marek said: **The twice is not going on anything.**

The woman of about fifty-eight said: **You note everything else.**

Marek said: **I note what somebody pays for and what somebody has done wrong in their own house. That is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it for about four years.**

The woman of about fifty-eight said: **So the two times I went is worth less than a lamp.**

Marek said: **It is worth the same as a lamp, and the lamp is on a sheet.**

The woman of about fifty-eight said: **That is the first thing anybody has told me in this city that made it better.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about nine people were in it, and the man of about thirty was on the same seat, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody said one question to that man for about two hours and that two people came and sat beside him and then left without saying anything.**

The man of about thirty said: **Nobody has asked me anything.**

Marek said: **No.**

The man of about thirty said: **You were not going to.**

Marek said: **No. And I am going to ask you again on Thursday, and I have not asked you whether I should, and if you want me to ask you whether I should, you can ask me for that on Thursday and I will think about it.**

The man of about thirty said: **You have told me the answer to that already.**

Marek said: **You have not asked me the question.**

---

**And then a man of about forty-nine asked him, out loud and in front of the whole of that bench, whether he was coming back the next Thursday, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the only question anybody asked that man all evening and that he answered it in about four words.**

The man of about forty-nine said: **Are you coming back next Thursday.**

The man of about thirty said: **I have not decided and I am not going to say.**

The man of about forty-nine said: **That is a no with extra words on it.**

The man of about thirty said: **It is a no with a date on it and the date is not mine yet.**

The man of about forty-nine said: **You answered it faster than the other man answered his.**

The man of about thirty said: **That one had been asked about nine weeks. This one had been asked about four seconds.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-one said out loud that he was not going to ask that man anything because he had got about nine answers already and was not going to waste one, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it to the bench and not to Marek and that Marek did not answer it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I am not going to ask him. I have got about nine and I am not going to waste one on the only man in here who has given me all three.**

Marek said: **That is the first sensible thing anybody has said in that room in about nine weeks and it took you that long.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Do not build on it.**

Marek said: **I am not building on anything.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You are thinking of a Thursday.**

Marek said: **I am thinking of a Thursday.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who answered three things out loud in that room a week before was asked one question about a Thursday and nothing else, and that a woman who has been in that shed twice was told at a shop that what she has done is worth the same as a lamp.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, where about nine people were in it for about two hours and about four of them did not say a whole sentence.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a fitting with the moulding filled half way with plaster and the other half left open.

He took that open half out, packed it and proved the lamp from the floor.

**Fourteen pounds**, he said. **Half a moulding filled is a fitting somebody stopped working on.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a box with an earth taken to the door hinge instead of to the box.

He brought that earth onto the box itself and proved the socket with an instrument on the lid in the wet.

**Twenty-four pounds**, he said. **An earth on a hinge is an earth that stops being an earth when the gate moves.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the timer wired so that it ran the oven on its own overnight.

He took the timer off the oven supply and proved the oven on the timer alone.

**Seventeen pounds**, he said. **An oven running on its own overnight is an oven nobody has to think about and everybody pays for.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a batten fitting with two holders on it and only one lamp in the other.

He put a lamp in the empty holder and proved both lamps from the door with the shade on.

**Twenty pounds**, he said. **One lamp on a batten is a batten somebody fitted and then decided was too much.**

**Seventy-five pounds is the sum of those four on that Thursday, exact.**
