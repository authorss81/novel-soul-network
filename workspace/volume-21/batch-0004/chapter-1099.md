# Chapter 1099 — Somebody Had It In A Coat

**A Monday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the whole of that Monday evening was spent looking at an envelope lying on a bench in a shed on a road in a fourth district with its flap up and nothing at all behind it.**

---

Seven callers came into that shop on that Monday and seven names were written against that Monday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about half past seven, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and thirty days, two hundred and ninety weeks to the day. The one card in that rail, once creased across its middle, stood at two thousand and thirty-four days, two hundred and ninety weeks and four days. What is in a carrier bag under a long bench is two hundred and three days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is one hundred and eighty-nine days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are one hundred and eighty days old.**

---

**He was in that room off a line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had a saucer on the bowl again and had left the corner of it up, and about four people in that room have said since that she has been experimenting and that nobody in that room has asked her what she is after.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It is one saucer and it is on a corner.**

Marek said: **Then it will be there in the morning.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It will be there until I am in it.**

Marek said: **That is about nine o'clock on about nine days out of ten and you have got it wrong twice this week.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then I will stop saying the time and say only the nine.**

---

**And then a man of about thirty-six came into that shop at about half past four with a sheet about an awning outside that shop, and asked Marek to put his name at the bottom of it, and was refused, and about four people in that shop have said since that the man did not know that shop and had walked past it on the way in and that Marek read the whole of it before he answered.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **It is only about the awning.**

Marek said: **It is about the awning and my name under it, and I do not put my name on things.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **Everybody puts their name on things.**

Marek said: **Everybody puts their name on things they have done. I have not done that awning.**

The man of about thirty-six said: **You have looked at it.**

Marek said: **I have looked at it from the road and I am telling you what I have and am not about it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that shop have said since that a man walked past a shop he knew nothing about and put his name to nothing.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about nine people were in it, and about four of them spent the first half hour of that evening looking at that envelope without touching it, and about four people in that room have said since that two of them took a hand out of a coat and put it back and that neither of them has said why.**

The woman of about fifty-eight said: **I have got it in this coat.**

Marek said: **You have not said what you have got in this coat.**

The woman of about fifty-eight said: **And I am not going to tell you, and that is not why I have told you that.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Then it can wait.**

The woman of about fifty-eight said: **It can wait as long as I like it to, and I am saying so out loud so that nobody else in this room has to.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **Is anybody going to say what that is for.**

Marek said: **No. And he is the one who asked us for about nine weeks and he does not know either.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody has put anything in that envelope, that two people in that room said out loud that they were not going to tonight, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to be the first one has not been asked to change his mind.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, where about nine people were in it and about four of them spent the first half hour looking at an envelope on that bench without touching it.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a fitting with a lamp in it that had come loose in its holder and was hanging by one contact.

He reseated it and proved the lamp from the floor with the shade on.

**Eighteen pounds**, he said. **A lamp hanging by one contact is a lamp that has already been on about nine hours.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate with the lid held on by a single screw and that screw going into a plug.

He put a proper box behind it and proved the socket from the gate in the wet.

**Twenty-five pounds**, he said. **One screw into a plug is one screw and about nine weeks.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a flex that had been taped where it went through the splashback.

He took the tape off, sleeved it and filled the hole and proved the cooker at the splashback.

**Fifteen pounds**, he said. **Tape where a flex goes through a wall is a repair that lasts about nine days.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a batten with one lamp in it and the other holder facing the wall.

He turned that holder round and proved the light from the door.

**Sixteen pounds**, he said. **A lamp for a batten that does not go in a batten is a lamp somebody carried in on the bus.**

**Seventy-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**
