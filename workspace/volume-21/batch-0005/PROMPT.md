## 1. WHAT THIS MOVEMENT IS GIVEN, AND WHAT IT MAY NOT INVENT

**The woman who cooks, who took the other end of a bar of steel about four feet long off a man at that bench in about four seconds on one Wednesday and said one word that had not been agreed with anybody, is asked on four different evenings to do it again.** She can stop it and she does stop it, on the second time, in about four words, and nobody apologises to her, **because nobody asked her the first time either, and that reason is stated out loud once on one page of this movement and on no other.**

**Invent no rule about who may do what in that room, no role, no rota, no post, no description of what she is for, and no page in which she is thanked, praised or written down.** **Invent no record, no form, no signature, no list of who came, and no page on which anybody protects anybody from the asking.** **The cost is on the bench and not on her: after she stops it, the man at that bench has nothing in his hands and about four people at that bench that night put nothing in at all, and both halves are said out loud.**

**AND THE THINGS MOVEMENT IV LEFT IN THE ROOM, WHICH CARD 5 DOES NOT MENTION AND WHICH A PASS READING ONLY CARD 5 WILL DROP.** A man of about thirty answered three questions out loud in that room one week before this movement's first day and about four people at that bench then put nothing in at all for about nine minutes. **What he said he would put in is about a kitchen in a second district and about a woman he is not related to, and it is going about this city by mouth, and the only parts that have come back through two districts are a kitchen, and a woman, and about nine people not saying anything. No page of this movement prints his three answers, no page repeats any fragment of them as a sentence, and no page asks him any of them again.** A woman of about sixty-two came into that room for the first time in about nine years because there was a light on, said four ordinary sentences, and was asked nothing; **nobody asks her for anything in this movement either.** **Nobody has put anything in that envelope and about four people spent half an hour looking at it, and a woman of about fifty-eight has said out loud that she has money in a coat and is not going to put it in tonight.** A man of about fifty-one has asked everybody and has about nine answers of which about four rhyme, and has said out loud that he is not going to spend one of them on the only man in that room who gave him three, and he has said out loud that the light in that shed is his to pay for. **A man of about thirty-six stood outside that door for about nine minutes and came in on his own and was asked nothing, and he asked Marek out loud to put his name at the bottom of a sheet about an awning and was refused.**

**AND THE ANSWER MOVEMENT IV PROVED CANNOT BE HANDED TO THIS MOVEMENT.** Nobody kept a count of anything in that room, nobody stood at the front of it, about nine people in it could not say what time they arrived, and one of them has never been through the door. **A movement in which about four people put nothing in at all must not be written as though a count of that four were available either: put the four on the page, name what each of them did not do, and let the figure stay a figure.**
