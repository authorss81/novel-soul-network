# Chapter 1071 — Somebody Asked Her In A Shop

**A Monday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and that Monday was wet from first light with the front of that shop propped open at about eleven because the rain was coming in under that door, which meant everybody who came through that day came through fast.**

---

Eleven callers came into that shop on that Monday and all eleven of them were written against that Monday's line as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and eighty-one days, two hundred and eighty-three weeks to the day. Under that long bench there is a carrier bag, and what is inside it is one hundred and fifty-four days old, the printing that has never gone on the wall of any of those four rooms is one hundred and forty days old, and the four words once said at a desk in a first district were one hundred and thirty-one days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four asked him one thing about a lamp and then asked him nothing else, which is the way that room goes.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Is that lamp on the outside wall.**

Marek said: **It is. It has been rewired twice and both of those times by somebody who wanted it on that side.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **And is it safe.**

Marek said: **It is now. I have not proved it yet.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then do not go telling anybody it is.**

Marek said: **I do not.**

---

**And then at about half past two a woman of about thirty-nine came in off the wet with a table lamp under her arm and put it down on that counter, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had not stood in that shed since the spring and had not been through that shop door at all until that afternoon.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **It goes on and it goes off and it is not the plug.**

Marek said: **It will be a switch somewhere you do not expect it to be.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I have had it about four years.**

Marek said: **Then it has been getting itself worse for about four years and that is the part you would not have noticed.**

---

**And then a man of about thirty-four who had been stood at the other end of that counter for about nine minutes asked her, out loud, in front of about four people, to say it again, and about four people in that shop have said since that he had been in that shed four times, had never once been in the room where she said it, and had not been told once by anybody that he could not have it.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **You are the one who said it.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I am.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **I have had it four times this month and I have never heard you say it.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **No. I am not going to say it again.**

**And that was nine words, and about four people in that shop have said since that they have heard her refuse to say one sentence four times in a month and that they can remember all four without anything else in between.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **There are people stood at that counter who have only had it second hand.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Then they have it second hand and they can keep it.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **You do not know what your sentence has done.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **No. I do not. And I am not going to find out by saying it twice.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **Nobody has ever refused me twice.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Nobody had ever asked me twice.**

---

**And then she would not put that lamp down, so he worked on it where she was standing, and about four people in that shop have said since that it took him about nine minutes and that she stayed in the middle of a wet floor the whole of it and that nobody thought to ask her to move.**

Marek said: **There is a lamp holder in the base and somebody has put a switch on the flex. That is all it is.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Somebody put a switch on my flex.**

Marek said: **Somebody did. It goes on that sheet at the going rate and you can argue with me about it after.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I have not got anything to argue with you about.**

Marek said: **You have got an argument. You have not got the interest in it yet either.**

---

**And then after she had gone the man of about thirty-four asked him how many people have asked her, and Marek said the thing out loud that he has been saying all month, and about four people in that shop have said since that the man of about thirty-four did not like any part of it.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **How many have asked her.**

Marek said: **I have not got a number and I am not going to invent one for you.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **You have got a shop. You could count them off that counter.**

Marek said: **I could. And then there would be a figure with that woman written beside it in a drawer, and she has not asked to be in anything.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **So it is not that you cannot.**

Marek said: **It is that I will not, and that is a different thing, and I can tell you are not going to like it.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **You are stood behind a counter telling me about it.**

Marek said: **I am, and I have been since about eleven o'clock, and it is the only place I have got.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that room heard a woman refuse to say one sentence twice and heard a man afterwards say out loud that he will not put a figure on her.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road, where one of them was still carrying heat and where the bag was under the long bench and had been since a Wednesday in the spring.**

A pendant in that shop had been put in on a flex that had been shortened with a crimp and a length of sleeving pushed over the cut.

He took it back to its length, made a proper flex and proved the lamp from a chair.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said. **That crimp is under a rose and it will be under that rose until somebody takes the rose off, and pendant flexes in that shop are like that.**

A socket in that yard had been put in with a fuse of twice the rating that belongs on it, and the holder was warm to the back of his hand before he had taken the plate off.

He put in the rating that belongs there and proved the socket with an instrument.

**He priced that one at seventeen pounds and said: I am not charging you for the chasing. Chases behind that yard wall are mine to pay for.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a terminal block sitting where the heat from the oven element reaches it.

He took the block out of the heat, put it back on the skirt of the cooker and proved the oven coming up and going off.

**Twenty-one pounds**, he said, **and that is the one that will not come back on me. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to hold a torch inside their own oven.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a plate with the screws tightened by somebody who had a short screwdriver and had not said so.

He put a longer one in the toolbox, re-screwed the plate and proved the light with the door shut.

**Eighteen pounds**, he said. **Somebody drove that in and somebody will take it out one day, and it will be whoever is nearest.**

**Seventy-five pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact, and the ninth minute of that lamp was done for nothing and put on that sheet at the going rate anyway.**

---

**Half past six on that Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms with a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Monday or on any other, and no page of this file puts a count on the paper it is holding.**
