# Chapter 1072 — He Came On The Wednesday He Was Given

**A Wednesday was a bench until about two and then a counter until six and then a room on a road in a fourth district with the rails still under the floor, and that Wednesday was the first clear one in a fortnight, with the frost still standing in the shadow of that shed from about half past four.**

---

Twelve callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and twelve names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and eighty-three days, two hundred and eighty-three weeks and two days. The carrier bag under that long bench is holding one hundred and fifty-six days of paper, the printing that has never once been on a wall in this city is one hundred and forty-two days old, and what a woman said at a desk in a first district is one hundred and thirty-three days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and the woman of about fifty-four opened the book in the green binding on the table at about ten past two in front of about four people, and nobody in that room said one word about the fact that she had done it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You can sit down.**

Marek said: **I have been sitting here since about eleven.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then sit down again.**

Marek said: **I will.**

---

**And then about half past two he was at that counter and the frost was still on the glass outside it, and a woman of about twenty-six came in off it and stood at the customer side and would not sit down, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had been in two shops before that one.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **I have come on a bus for this and I would rather not do it in front of anybody.**

Marek said: **Then it is not going to be in front of anybody.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **It is, because there are about four of you.**

Marek said: **There are. I cannot make that be different and I would not tell you it was nobody if there were.**

---

**And then the woman of about twenty-six went down that corridor in a second district at about half past three with a woman of about thirty-nine who had been waiting on a step by the door, and the corridor was the long one with a fire door at the end of it, and about four people have said since that the asking happened about nine feet from that fire door with the door shut, and that the woman of about thirty-nine refused her in about nine words and did not stop walking while she did it.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **I came on a bus for this.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Then take it back with you.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **I have never heard you say it and I have heard it about four times.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I am not saying it twice for anybody else.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **You would not have to say it to me.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I am saying it to nobody. Ask me something else.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **What else is there.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Nothing. You have got the same afternoon I have got.**

---

**And then Marek, who had been at the far end of that corridor for about nine minutes with his back to a window, said the one thing he had, and about four people in that corridor have said since that it did not help at all and that she asked him what anybody was supposed to do with a shut door.**

Marek said: **She is not going to say it and I am not going to ask her.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **You are asking her for me.**

Marek said: **I have asked nobody anything. I have told a stranger in a corridor that a door is shut, and that is all I have done.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **Then what is anybody supposed to do with a shut door.**

Marek said: **Go and stand in front of it, the same as you would with any other one.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed and about nine people were stood about in the cold, and the man of about forty-four was one of them, and about four people in that room have said since that he had come on the day he was given and had not said so to anybody when he walked in.**

The man of about forty-four said: **You gave me a day.**

Marek said: **I gave you a day and an address and nothing else.**

The man of about forty-four said: **I know what you gave me. I have come on it.**

Marek said: **Nobody has said anything about how it went.**

The man of about forty-four said: **Nothing has gone on at all. You are stood at the back of it with your hands behind you.**

Marek said: **I am.**

The man of about forty-four said: **I have been told a woman said no to somebody at a counter on Monday.**

Marek said: **I was there.**

The man of about forty-four said: **Then you know I could go and ask her.**

Marek said: **I know you have not and I am not going to tell you why that is, because I do not know why it is.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about nine people stood in a shed that evening with nobody at the front of it, and one of them had come on a day he had been given and had asked nothing.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed with the doors off their tracks, and the bag was still under that bench and nothing was put up on the wall of any of those four units.**

A rose in that shop had been put in with a terminal block behind it that would not take a conductor of the size that was in the box behind it.

He took the block out, put one in that would take it and proved the lamp and the tester on it.

**Twenty-three pounds**, he said. **That is the third time I have had to put a bigger block in for the same fault and I am not charging you for the first two.**

A socket in that yard had been put in with the lid left off and the earth sleeved back onto itself and folded into the terminal it came from.

He made a lid, took the earth off its own conductor and put it on the box and proved the socket from the gate in the frost.

**He priced that one at seventeen pounds and said: I am not charging you for the box. That box is the only good thing in that wall and I have left it exactly where it is.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in on a supply that came off the lighting circuit by somebody who had extended it under a floorboard and not told anybody.

He took that supply off, put the cooker on a ring of its own and proved the cooker with two pans on at once.

**Twenty-one pounds**, he said, **and if that floorboard ever comes up there will be a cable under it and I would take money for telling you now where it is.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with the earth connected to the gas pipe and nothing else.

He took it off the gas pipe, put it on the fitting's own metal and proved it with the pipe full.

**Twenty pounds**, he said. **A gas pipe is not an earth. It is a thing somebody can put a spanner on.**

**Eighty-one pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty was behind that door, and the binder on the back shelf of that shop did not come out on that Wednesday or on any other day of this volume, and nothing whatever is set down here about the paper in it.**

---

*Conditions of the close.* **The green-bound book lay open from about ten past two until about half past six on that Wednesday, and the tin standing next to it on that table had its lid on it the whole time, and this page carries no figure for either of those two things and no difference of any kind between them.
The chair that has faced that same wall since before this volume opened turned by no leg and was touched by no hand in any hour of that Wednesday. Whoever last turned it is not named on one page of this volume.
The room under the building in that first district stood dark and shut at ten that Wednesday as it has stood dark every day of this volume so far, and it is not opened again on any of the sixty.
That register of correct acts that changed nothing said four at both ends of that Wednesday and says four now, no person in this city is entered on it, and a woman saying no to a stranger in a corridor is not an entry on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **This page finishes with ten things, each of them named on its own and none of them named with another. Two cuts into that rail where the card used to lie. The card itself, with its crease and its three questions and no line ruled under any of them. A board hanging by two nails with a hole at each nail. Nine torn corners belonging to nine separate fronts of a thing. The lid of that tin. That shutter and its bar. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back of that shelf. An envelope flat on that bench with nothing behind the flap. A pencil in a margin with nothing written in the margin.
And these six are outside that ten and stand apart from all of them: the cage standing down the long side of that counter, a carrier bag under a long bench with a hundred and fifty sheets in it, a plastic sleeve in a drawer in a woman's own flat holding nine words and a date, a folded paper in a second district's drawer with a name struck through and another written under it, a leaflet taped under the lower of two clean rectangles on a wall in a fourth district, and a sheet carrying nine names and nothing besides the names.
Whatever stands filed behind a dated rule over a question this page will not print can be read by any person who asks to see it.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody. A woman of about thirty-nine said no twice in a month to two people in two buildings, and neither no has been repeated by anybody, and the second one has already been carried out of that corridor by somebody.**
