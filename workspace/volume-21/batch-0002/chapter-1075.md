# Chapter 1075 — A Stair In Ashfields

**A Monday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and that Monday was the first one in three weeks that nobody came into that shop in a wet coat, and about four people came in and looked at the same thing at the same counter before two o'clock.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Monday and nine names were written against that Monday's line as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and that shutter came down at ten.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and eighty-five days, two hundred and eighty-three weeks and four days. The bag under that long bench is a hundred and sixty-one days old and holds a hundred and fifty sheets, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is a hundred and forty-seven days old, and one sentence said at a desk in a first district is a hundred and thirty-eight days into the city.**

---

**He was at that bench until about two and about four people were in that room and one of them asked him whether a landlord can put a heater off a shared wall, and he answered that, and then nobody asked him anything for the rest of that morning.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Can he take the heater off the wall we share.**

Marek said: **He can take his own heater off his own wall and he can be on that side of it with a hose.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That would cost me money.**

Marek said: **It would cost about nine degrees and it would cost her nothing, and those are not the same thing.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Say that again.**

Marek said: **No. I have said it once and it was enough.**

---

**And then a man of about fifty-eight came into that shop at about four with his hands still cold from outside and said he had asked her on a stair in Ashfields at about eleven that morning, and about four people in that shop have said since that he had the whole thing planned before he came through that door and that he said it anyway.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **I asked her on a stair this morning.**

Marek said: **You asked her.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **I went up a stair in Ashfields at about eleven where I knew she was going through, and I asked her for it, and she would not give it to me.**

Marek said: **That is the fourth time somebody has asked her this month.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **Is it.**

Marek said: **A counter, a corridor, a bench outside a clinic, and now a stair, and you are the fourth and I have met none of the other three.**

**And about four people in that shop have said since that they have now heard her refuse three people in three weeks and that nobody in that shop has heard her say the sentence at any hour of any of them.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **She said, you are asking me now and I am going up. That is what she said and I have been carrying it down about nine steps.**

Marek said: **Say it again.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **No. I will not.**

Marek said: **That is hers and not yours. You have been holding it for about an hour now.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **I have been holding it since about eleven, and I have not put it down once, and I came in here to put it down.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-eight said the thing that about four people in that shop have said since was the only reason he had come into that shop at all, and Marek could not answer any part of it.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **My daughter has it. Not heard it. Has it.**

Marek said: **From a woman of about thirty-nine.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **From her and off a woman at a counter, and she thinks the two of them are the same thing and they are not.**

Marek said: **They are not.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **Then what is the second one.**

Marek said: **I have been in three of the four places today and I could not tell you where it started.**

The man of about fifty-eight said: **You are stood behind a counter and you cannot tell me where it started.**

Marek said: **And I would not correct it if I could, and I would rather tell you that than let you find out in a month.**

---

**And then at about half past five he went across a first district and stood at the bottom of a stair in Ashfields for about nine minutes, and there was nobody on it, and about four people on that street have said since that a man stood at the bottom of that stair looking up at it as though he expected somebody to come down.**

Nobody came down that stair.

The stair had a fire door at the top of it that had not been on a closer since about four years ago.

Somebody had put a carrier bag on the third step and taken it away again by the time he got back to the shop.

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people heard a man say out loud that he would not correct a sentence even if he could find out where it started, and that he was telling them now so that they would not find it out later.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was over by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road and put nothing up on any wall and wrote nothing down anywhere.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose where the earth had been left hanging out of the back of it in a loop of bare copper.

He took the loop in, put the earth on the bracket and proved the lamp and the tester on it.

**Eighteen pounds**, he said. **That copper has been hanging there long enough to have a colour of its own.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate that has been screwed to a fence panel and the panel itself moves in the wind.

He took the fence panel out of it, cut two boxes into the brick and proved the socket from the gate.

**Twenty pounds**, he said. **The whole of that is a thing about four of them will lean on a fence for.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the earth strap on the oven door hinge instead of on the oven.

He took it off the hinge, put it on the frame and proved the oven with the door shut.

**Seventeen pounds**, he said, **and I would have said about nine for that one if I had not seen the hinge.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with a lamp in it that was a fitting lamp and not a lamp, and it had been getting warm for about four years.

He took it out, put in one that goes there and proved the fitting with the cover on.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said. **That one has been telling somebody in that flat for years that something in it is wrong and nobody has listened to it.**

**Seventy-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty was behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf stayed where it is on that Monday and on every other day of this volume, and not one thing written inside it is measured here.**

---

*Conditions of the close.* **That green-bound book lay shut all through that Monday with the lid of the tin beside it down the whole time, and this file gives no figure for either one of them and no difference of any kind between them.
That ninth chair faced the wall it has faced for this whole volume and did not move by a leg or by anything else on that Monday, and nobody's hand went near it. **Whoever last turned that chair is not named in this file and is not named in any other of this volume.**
Nothing was lit under that building in a first district at ten on that Monday and nothing is lit there now, and it is not opened again in this volume.
That register said four at the start of that Monday and four at the end of it, nobody in this city is on it, and a man standing at the bottom of a stair is not on it either.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named at the foot of this page, each by itself and none of them named with another. Two nicks out of that rail. The card, with one crease and three questions on it. That board on two nails. Nine corners torn off nine fronts. The lid on that tin. That shutter with its bar. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back of that shelf. An envelope on a bench with its flap up and nothing in it. A pencil lying in a margin.
Six objects are named outside those ten and stand clear of all of them: the cage down one long side of that counter, a carrier bag under a long bench with a hundred and fifty sheets in it, a plastic sleeve in a drawer in a woman's own flat with nine words and a date in it, a folded paper in a drawer in a second district with a name struck through above another name written under it, a leaflet taped under the lower of two clean rectangles on a wall in a fourth district, and a page carrying nine names with nothing written underneath them.
Anything filed behind a dated rule over a question that this page does not print can be read by anybody who asks to read it.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody. About four people in this city have been told that there are two things going about and about four of them have been told it by a woman who said neither of them, and nobody in this city has said whether either of them is so.**
