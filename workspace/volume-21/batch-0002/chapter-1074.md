# Chapter 1074 — A Different Thing Going About

**A Sunday in this city comes down at about two, and this Sunday was one of the bright cold ones, with hard light lying on the rails under the floor of a shed on a road in a fourth district and about four people through the door of it at about half past twelve.**

---

Four callers came into that shop on that Sunday and four names were written against that Sunday's line as each of them came in, and the shutter came down at about two, which is what it does on that day.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and eighty-four days, two hundred and eighty-three weeks and three days. There is a hundred and sixty days of paper in a carrier bag under that long bench, a hundred and forty-six days of the printing that has never been anywhere is in the same count, and a hundred and thirty-seven days is all that a woman at a desk in a first district has been carrying one sentence.**

---

**He went across at about half past twelve and stood outside that shed for about nine minutes and then went in and sat down at the far end of that bench, and about four people in there had not seen him come in and did not look up, and he did not say one word to anybody in that room.**

The woman of about forty said: **Is somebody going to ask us three things.**

Marek said: **No.**

The woman of about forty said: **Nobody asked us last Sunday.**

Marek said: **I was in this room last Sunday and nobody asked me three things either.**

The woman of about forty said: **Then what are you here for.**

Marek said: **To sit at that end of that bench, same as everybody else.**

---

**And then about nine minutes after he had sat down the woman of about forty turned round and said the thing out loud that about four people in that shed have said since was the reason about four of them had started walking to the end of that bench, and that she said it to the floor and not to him.**

The woman of about forty said: **There is another one going about.**

Marek said: **Another what.**

The woman of about forty said: **Another thing people say. It is about her and it is not about that shed.**

Marek said: **You have heard it.**

The woman of about forty said: **Two women on a bus. I did not ask them where they had it.**

Marek said: **And they did not tell you.**

The woman of about forty said: **One of them said she had it off a woman in a queue, and I did not ask which queue.**

---

**And then Marek asked the only question he had that was not about the room, and about four people in that shed have said since that he asked it flatly and that nobody answered it and that he waited.**

Marek said: **Which queue.**

The woman of about forty said: **I said I did not ask.**

Marek said: **I know you did not. I am asking whether you remember.**

The woman of about forty said: **No.**

Marek said: **Then we are at the same place.**

The woman of about forty said: **Which place is that.**

Marek said: **The place where about four people have got a piece and none of them has got where it came from.**

---

**And then nobody in that shed said anything else about it for the rest of that afternoon, and about four people in there have said since that two of them tried it out on each other and got two different versions back and would not say whose version was nearer.**

Nobody asked anybody anything.

Nobody stood at the front of anything.

Nobody in that room repeated one word of what the woman of about thirty-nine had said in the spring.

The envelope on the end of that bench was still lying there with its flap up, and about four people in that shed have said since that nobody looked at it once that afternoon.

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and about half past six shut the fourth of those four rooms with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf did not come out on that Sunday or on any other, and no figure for what is in it appears anywhere in this file.**

---

**What was left of that Sunday went into that shop and that yard and it took four jobs and not a fifth, and they came to the smallest figure that shop has had on a working day since the spring.**

A bell in that shop had been put on a bracket that had been set into a plasterboard panel and not into anything behind it.

He cut a box behind the panel, moved the bracket into brick and proved the bell from the middle of that floor.

**Five pounds**, he said, **and I have put that up on every shop on this road for nothing.**

A lamp in that yard had been put in on a holder with the thread cut about four threads short so that it had to be held on to stay in.

He made a new ring, turned it down and wired the lamp and proved it from the gate.

**Six pounds**, he said. **That lamp has been hanging on by about nine threads and one good wind.**

A socket in that kitchen had been put in at about knee height behind a chair where somebody keeps their feet.

He moved it up to the worktop and proved it with a kettle standing where it should stand.

**Seven pounds**, he said. **A socket at the height of a chair is a socket somebody has to reach under a table for.**

A switch in that second unit had been put in on a plate that had been painted round rather than over, so that a ring of every colour under it was showing.

He took the paint off back to the edge and proved the switch with the cover on.

**Five pounds**, he said. **They have painted round forty of these and over the rest, and I have stopped asking why.**

**Twenty-three pounds is the sum of those four on that Sunday, exact, and it is the smallest single day's work on that sheet since about nine weeks ago.**
