# Chapter 1076 — It Was Not Hers To Give Again

**A Thursday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the middle of that Thursday was spent in a launderette in a second district on a plastic chair with one machine at the end of it stopped and all the rest of them going.**

---

Eleven callers came into that shop on that Thursday and eleven names were written against that Thursday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and eighty-six days, two hundred and eighty-three weeks and five days. What is in a carrier bag under that long bench is one hundred and sixty-four days old, the printing that has never been on a wall is one hundred and fifty days old, and the four words once said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and forty-one days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and nobody asked him anything, and the woman of about fifty-four said the only thing she said that day, which was that one of the machines in the launderette had stopped and had not started again.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **The one at the end has stopped.**

Marek said: **Which one.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **The second one from the door. It has been going since about four and it stopped at about twenty past one.**

Marek said: **A machine that stops at twenty past one has a load in it and a door lock on it. Do not go back in there tonight.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I was not going to go back in there tonight.**

---

**And then at about half past two he was across the city and in a launderette in a second district and the woman of about thirty-four was on a plastic chair with a basket on her knees, and about four people in that room have said since that she had been on that chair for about an hour before he came in and that she had two machines going and had been watching them.**

Marek said: **You have been waiting.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I have been waiting about an hour and I would have waited about nine more.**

Marek said: **You knew I was coming.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I did not. I knew somebody would.**

---

**And then he asked her the third one, which is the one she has never answered in this city and which he had now put to her twice, and about four people in that launderette have said since that he asked it exactly as he had put it the first time, word for word, and that she heard it coming before he had finished it.**

Marek said: **Who could stop you.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I heard you.**

Marek said: **I know you heard me.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **Then you know what happens now, and so do the machines.**

Nothing said anything for about nine seconds.

Marek said: **I am not going to ask it a third time. I am telling you that before you have to wait for me to be sure I mean it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I have never thought you meant anything else. That is not the difficulty.**

---

**And then she said the thing out loud that nobody else in this city had been willing to say in about nine words, and about four people in that launderette have said since that she said it to him and not to the machines, that her hands did not move on that basket while she said it, and that she has not said it since to anybody including him.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **She said it once. It was hers to say.**

Marek said: **Say the rest of it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **There is no rest of it. She said it in a room, to about nine people, and one of those people wrote it down and threw it away, and what is walking about this city is the copy.**

Marek said: **And the copy is at a counter in a first district.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **The copy is wherever a person says it, and there are about four of them and none of them is her.**

---

**And then Marek said the only question he had left that was not about the room, and about four people in that launderette have said since that he put it out loud in a room full of noise and that she answered the first half of it before he had finished asking.**

Marek said: **Then what am I doing here.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **Nothing.**

Marek said: **I could write it out.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **You cannot, and I am the reason you cannot, and I am telling you that for nothing. I am telling you because you will not find it out anywhere else.**

Marek said: **I have not got another reason.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **You have got a rule and a rule is a reason that does not have to be nice about it.**

---

**And then he left that launderette at about half past four and did not say where he was going, and about four people in that room have said since that he stood in the doorway for about nine seconds afterwards and then went out and up the road and not back down it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **You will stop asking.**

Marek said: **I stopped this morning and did not tell anybody I had.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **Then somebody else will stop for you.**

Marek said: **Then they will be correct and I will not be able to stop them, and that is the whole of it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that launderette on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people there heard a woman refuse a question for the second time in this city and say out loud that a sentence said once by somebody else is that somebody else's to keep, and not hers to hand about.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road and worked there until about eight and did not put anything up on a wall.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a switch plate that had been painted round twice, so that there was a colour showing under it and over it.

He took the paint back to the edge of the plate and proved the switch with an instrument on it.

**Fifteen pounds**, he said, **and I have stopped explaining that one and I do it now without saying anything about it.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a box that had been chased into a wall and then packed round with expanding foam.

He cut the foam out, filled the chase and proved the socket with an instrument in the wet.

**Twenty-one pounds**, he said. **Foam is a good way of holding a box in and a very poor way of letting anybody get to it again.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the oven element connected to a terminal that was two sizes too small for the conductor.

He made the terminal the size it should have been and proved the oven coming up and going off twice.

**Fourteen pounds**, he said. **Somebody has put a whole oven behind a terminal meant for a lamp.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with the earth taken off its metal and put on the plate holding it.

He put the earth back on the metal and proved it in the wet with an instrument on the plate.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said, **and that one is the commonest thing I do and I have never once got used to it.**

**Sixty-nine pounds is the sum of those four on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Thursday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty was behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Thursday or on any other day of this volume, and no figure for what it holds is written anywhere in this file.**
