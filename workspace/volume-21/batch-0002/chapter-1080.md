# Chapter 1080 — He Was Not Going To Describe It

**A Wednesday was a bench until about two and then a counter until six and then a room on a road in a fourth district with the rails still under the floor, and that Wednesday was the last of that fortnight and about nine people were in that room at about half past seven and nobody was at the front of it.**

---

Thirteen callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and thirteen names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and ninety-seven days, two hundred and eighty-five weeks and two days. The bag under that long bench is a hundred and seventy days old and has been since a Wednesday in the spring, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is a hundred and fifty-six days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and forty-seven days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and the place behind that woman's chair was empty and had been empty every day he had been in that room, and he did not say anything about it, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody there has ever asked him about it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You have got that look.**

Marek said: **I have not got a look.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You have got the one you had the time the machine stopped.**

Marek said: **That was about a machine.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It was about whatever you were not saying and you are back on it.**

Marek said: **I am.**

---

**And then at about half past three a woman of about thirty-four came past that counter on her way out and stopped, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had come in for nothing and that she stopped for about nine seconds and then carried on.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **You are not going to ask anybody.**

Marek said: **I am not going to ask anybody.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **You have not stopped being asked, though.**

Marek said: **No. And I have stopped being the one that stops it, which is a smaller thing and it is the only one I have got.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **Then you will be asked by me on a Wednesday at some point and I will not answer you.**

Marek said: **I know. I will still be here.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed and about nine people were stood about in it, and the man of about forty-four was there and the man of about twenty-seven was there, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody said anything about a woman of about thirty-nine at any point of that evening.**

The man of about forty-four said: **I have not asked her.**

Marek said: **I know that. You told me you had not and I did not ask why.**

The man of about forty-four said: **I have been waiting to find out why myself.**

Marek said: **You will not find it out from me, and I have thought about that for about nine days and it is not a thing I am being clever about.**

The man of about forty-four said: **Then what have you been thinking about.**

Marek said: **About two things I have decided, and both of them are about me and neither of them is about her.**

---

**And then he said them out loud to the man of about forty-four and not to that room, and about four people in that shed have said since that about four of them were close enough to hear it and that about four of them have each said something else about it on the way home.**

Marek said: **I am not going to describe that room to anybody. And I am not going to ask anybody to stop talking about it.**

The man of about forty-four said: **You asked me to stop.**

Marek said: **I asked you once, in a shop, and I will not do it again, and that is the first one being taken back and it is being taken back by me and not by you.**

The man of about forty-four said: **And if it goes on.**

Marek said: **Then it goes on, and I have been told about four times this week that I cannot stop it and I have believed all four of them.**

---

**And then about half past eight two people left that shed together and about four people in that room have said since that one of them was saying a thing to the other one on the road about nine steps outside the door, and that it was not the sentence, and that nobody in that room interrupted either of them.**

Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about half past six shut the fourth of those four rooms with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Wednesday or on any other.

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed with the doors off their tracks and nothing on the bench at the far end of it.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a pendant that had been joined in with a block connector inside the rose and the connector was the wrong size.

He took the connector out, made a proper joint and proved the lamp from a chair.

**Twenty-one pounds**, he said. **A block connector is a joint that is designed to be pulled apart and that has been buried under a ceiling.**

A socket in that yard had been put in at a height where somebody could reach it only by standing on a bucket.

He brought it down to where a person can reach it standing up and proved it with a meter held the way a meter is actually held.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said. **I have proved that socket in four positions because in none of them can a person see it.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a flex that went through a hole in a tiled wall and the hole had been filled afterwards with about four inches of grout.

He took the grout out, sleeved the flex and proved the cooker with the oven on and the door shut.

**Twenty-four pounds**, he said, **and that is the most I have charged for one job this month and I would not want it to be.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with a pull cord that had been tied round a pipe.

He untied it, cut a switch into the drop and proved the light with the door shut.

**Eighteen pounds**, he said. **Somebody wanted that light off and did not think of a switch.**

**Eighty-two pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact, and it is the largest single day on that sheet since about four weeks ago.**
