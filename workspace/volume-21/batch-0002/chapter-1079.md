# Chapter 1079 — Nobody Knows Where It Started

**A Tuesday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and that Tuesday was spent asking four people the same short question at one counter and getting four different answers that all said the same thing.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Tuesday and nine names were written against that Tuesday's line as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and ninety-six days, two hundred and eighty-five weeks and one day. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is a hundred and sixty-nine days old, the printing that has never once been on a wall in this city is a hundred and fifty-five days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and forty-six days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four asked him whether a card can be taken off a counter and put back, and told him the answer before he gave it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Can a card go back where it was.**

Marek said: **It can go back anywhere.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That is not the same as it going back by itself.**

Marek said: **No. And I am not going to be one of the people who puts it back for her.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I did not ask you to. I asked whether it can.**

---

**And then between about three and about half past five he asked four people one question each, and about four people in that shop have said since that it was the same question four times and that he wrote nothing down between them.**

Marek said: **Where did you first hear that she will not say it again.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **Somebody on a bus.**

Marek said: **Who on the bus.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I do not know and I have stopped trying.**

Marek said: **And you.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **Off a woman who was fed up.**

Marek said: **Which woman.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **I could not tell you her colour.**

Marek said: **And you.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **Off my sister.**

Marek said: **And your sister.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **Off a woman in a queue, and she would not say which queue, and I have not asked her twice.**

---

**And then a woman of about fifty-five came in at about half past five and put her hand on that counter and asked Marek the one question he has no way of answering, and about four people in that shop have said since that she had been asking it in other shops for about nine days.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **Somebody has told me she is saying something else now.**

Marek said: **About what.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **About herself. And I want to know whether that is so.**

Marek said: **I have been asking four people since about three where they first heard it and I have got four answers and not one of them has a person at the end of it.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **Then you could go and ask her.**

Marek said: **I am not going to ask her a fifth time this month and neither is anybody else, and if I do it is going to be me doing it because I want an answer and not because she owes me one.**

---

**And then he said the thing out loud that is the closest he comes all week to saying what he will not do with that room, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said it to one woman at a counter and that about four other people heard all of it.**

Marek said: **I am not going to describe that room to you at this counter, and I am not going to print anything this week, and I am not going to tell you whether it is so or not, because I do not know.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **You are stood behind a counter and you have been in that room.**

Marek said: **I have, and I asked three things at that bench and I have never been able to tell anybody what any of it did, and that has not changed in a fortnight and I am not going to pretend it has.**

The woman of about fifty-five said: **Then what good are you.**

Marek said: **None this afternoon. Come back when somebody has actually answered something.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people heard a man say out loud that he had no answer and that he was not going to invent one, and about four people left that shop not knowing any more than they came in.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road where one of them carries heat and where the bag was still under that long bench.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with two neutrals landed together and a spare terminal left empty beside them.

He separated the neutrals, left the terminal empty and proved the lamp from the floor.

**Seventeen pounds**, he said. **Two neutrals together is a lamp that works on its own and not on anybody else's.**

A socket in that yard had been put in with the earth going to a screw into a wooden batten and nothing else at all.

He cut the batten out, put the earth on the box and proved the socket from the gate in the wet.

**Twenty pounds**, he said, **and a screw into a batten is the same mistake as a screw into a stone and it takes the same amount of weather.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the element leads going to a terminal block that was mounted on the back of the cooker where the heat gets.

He remounted the block on the skirt and proved the oven coming up and going off.

**Sixteen pounds**, he said, **and that is one of those jobs where the man before you has put it exactly where you would not put it.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with the flex going through a hole that had been made with a brace and not a hole saw.

He enlarged the hole, sleeved the flex and proved the light with the door shut.

**Twenty-two pounds**, he said, **and I have opened that same brace hole in four flats on this row this month.**

**Seventy-five pounds is the sum of those four on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Tuesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty was behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Tuesday or on any other day of this volume, and no figure for what that binder is holding is written here.**
