# Chapter 1083 — The Only Thing Anybody Could Write Down

**A Tuesday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the middle of that Tuesday was spent standing at the customer side of a key counter in a first district while two people who wanted the same thing argued about what it was.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Tuesday and nine names were written against that Tuesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and three days, two hundred and eighty-six weeks and one day. The bag under that long bench is a hundred and seventy-six days old and has not been opened, the printing that has never been on a wall in this city is a hundred and sixty-two days old, and the four words once said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and fifty-three days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four said that the lamp over that door had gone out somewhere between about seven and about nine and that she had put it back on with the switch by the machine.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It went out on its own.**

Marek said: **Those do not go out on their own. They go out when somebody has had at them.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Nobody has had at it.**

Marek said: **Then it was at the supply.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That is what I am telling you and you are telling me it is at the supply.**

---

**And then at about half past two he was across the city at the customer side of that key counter, and the woman of about thirty-one was on that machine with a blank on the cutter, and the man of about fifty-one was already standing at the end of that bench with his coat still buttoned.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **He has been in twice this week.**

Marek said: **I heard.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **He wants it written down.**

Marek said: **So he has told you what he wants it for.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **He told me. He wants it so a sentence can be checked against it.**

Marek said: **That is not the same as a man wanting an evening written down.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **It is what he is going to walk away with.**

---

**And then the man asked her to say it once more, out loud, in that shop, so that he could put it down afterwards, and about four people on that bench have said since that he asked her standing up and that the machine was still running when he asked.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Say it once more and I will put it down after.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **I am not saying it again for a page.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You said it at this counter. I heard you.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **And I told you then that I would not say whether it is so.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Then write that down instead, and that is one thing settled.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **That is the one thing anybody in this city could write down.**

---

**And then Marek did not ask her anything at all on that counter, and about four people in that shop have said since that he stood there for about nine minutes and did not ask her one question, and that she looked at him once and then went back to the machine.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **You have come to ask me something.**

Marek said: **No.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **You have come and stood here.**

Marek said: **I have come and stood here.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **Then say the reason out loud, because he is listening.**

Marek said: **There is no reason. That is not a trick and there is nothing behind it.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-one said out loud what he was going to do about the rest of it, and about four people on that bench have said since that he said it while she was still on that machine and that nobody answered him and that the machine went on the whole time.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Then I pay for the light in that shed now.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **Say that again in front of the people who were in there.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **No. I have said it once.**

The woman of about thirty-one said: **Once is not the same as told.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people on that bench have said since that a man of about fifty-one said out loud on a Tuesday that the light in that shed was his to pay for, and that about four of them have said since that they did not hear him say it a second time.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road and worked there until about eight and put nothing up on a wall.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a ceiling rose with the terminals left with about a quarter of an inch of bare copper standing out of them.

He took the copper back, made the joint and proved the lamp from the floor with the shade on.

**Twenty-one pounds**, he said. **Copper standing out of a terminal is copper somebody will touch one day for no reason at all.**

A socket in that yard had been put in behind a wooden batten that somebody could put a nail into without knowing what was behind it.

He took the batten out, ran the conduit properly and proved the socket from the gate with the gate shut.

**Seventeen pounds**, he said. **Put a batten over a socket and you have put a fence up in front of it.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with an oven that came on at about half heat and stayed there whatever it was set to.

He changed the thermostat and proved the oven coming up to heat and going off.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said. **An oven that will not go past half heat is an oven that has forgotten what it is for.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with the earth connected to the centre contact of the lamp itself.

He took the earth off the lamp, put it where it belongs and proved the light in the wet.

**Twenty pounds**, he said. **The earth belongs on metal that stays put and not on a piece of glass somebody can unscrew.**

**Seventy-seven pounds is the sum of those four on that Tuesday, exact.**
