# Chapter 1090 — He Came Back For A Lamp

**A Thursday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and that Thursday was spent on a lamp left behind a till ten days earlier, on a woman who said one sentence about her own office, and on a hand laid flat on an envelope.**

---

Twelve callers came into that shop on that Thursday and twelve names were written against that Thursday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and twelve days, two hundred and eighty-seven weeks and three days. The bag under that long bench is a hundred and eighty-five days old and is the oldest thing in that room, the printing that has never been on a wall in this city is a hundred and seventy-one days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and sixty-two days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four told him that the frame of that window had been painted over at some point and that whoever did it had not opened it, and he told her that was the usual way of it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It has been painted shut.**

Marek said: **Then it was shut before that and the paint only made it official.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That is a strange thing to say about a window.**

Marek said: **It is a strange thing to paint. The window is only carrying it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You have not said whether you will open it.**

Marek said: **I will not open it on a Thursday afternoon with you standing in the cold.**

---

**And then at about half past four the man of about fifty-one came into that shop and asked for the lamp he had left behind that till, and about four people in that shop have said since that he asked for it by name and that he did not say anything else at all when he got it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **The lamp.**

Marek said: **It is behind that till where you left it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **It has been there ten days.**

Marek said: **It is a lamp. It does not go anywhere.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I have been told that about a woman and I did not believe it.**

---

**And then Marek told him what it wanted, out loud, and the man paid him for it at the trade price and said nothing about any of it, and about four people in that shop have said since that neither of them raised their voice and that the sum was the same as any other sum on that sheet.**

Marek said: **A new base plate, about four inches of flex and a lamp that goes in a fitting.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **How much.**

Marek said: **Twenty-six pounds.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is the same as anybody.**

Marek said: **It is the same as anybody because it is the same job.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I thought it might not be.**

Marek said: **There is one price and I have never found a way to move it.**

---

**And then a woman he loves came through that shop at about half past five on her way out and stopped at that counter, and about four people in that shop have said since that he did not ask her one thing while she was standing there, and that she stayed about nine seconds and then carried on out.**

Talia said: **My office has a form for that and the form is the problem.**

Marek said: **I know it has.**

Talia said: **You have not asked me for anything this month.**

Marek said: **No.**

Talia said: **I noticed that on the Thursday and I have not said anything about it since.**

Marek said: **I noticed that you noticed.**

Talia said: **That is the whole of what I came in for.**

---

**And then in that shed the man of about fifty-one put his hand flat on that envelope with its flap up and nothing behind it, and said out loud two things about it, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody in that shed had touched that envelope in about nine months and that he put his hand on it and took it off again inside about four seconds.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I am not going to be the first one to put anything in that.**

Marek said: **What made you think about it tonight.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Nine days of paying for a light and not saying so, and a woman who will not be thanked, and nine people who will not sign a thing that does not exist. That is three of us putting something into it and about four of them who would.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I do not know what the next one would be and I am not going to be the one who starts it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop or in that shed on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people at that counter have said since that a man came for a lamp and paid a trade price for it and said nothing about a shed, and that the woman who loves the man behind that counter said one sentence about her own office and did not say one word about either of them.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road, and then across the city to a shed on a road in a fourth district where about four people were in it and the man of about fifty-one was already there.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a fitting with the flex knotted inside the fitting before it reached the terminal.

He unknotted it, made the entry and proved the light with the door shut.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said. **A knot inside a fitting is somebody making the flex shorter instead of the hole bigger.**

A socket in that yard had been put in with a terminal block full of copper strand pushed back into the moulding.

He pulled the strand out, re-made the termination and proved the socket in the wet.

**Seventeen pounds**, he said. **A strand pushed back into a block is insulation that has given up.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the oven lamp wired into the element circuit so that it burned all night.

He took the lamp off that circuit and proved the oven lamp coming on with the door open and going off when it shut.

**Twenty-three pounds**, he said. **An oven lamp on an element circuit is a light somebody will leave burning for years.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting held up by two cables because the ceiling above it had fallen in and been patched.

He fixed it to the joist above it and proved the light with the shade on.

**Fifteen pounds**, he said. **A fitting hanging on two cables is a fitting waiting for a bad night.**

**Seventy-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Thursday, exact.**

*Conditions of the close.* **The book in the green binding was shut and the tin beside it had its lid down at ten on that Thursday, and no page of this file prints a figure for either of those and no difference between them.

A man of about fifty-one has paid for a light in that shed out of his own money for nine days without saying so once more, nine people in that shed have said they will not put a name to a thing that was never written, and nobody in this city has told him that there is nothing left for him to buy.**
