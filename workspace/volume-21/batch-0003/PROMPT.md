## 1. WHAT THIS MOVEMENT IS GIVEN, AND WHAT IT MAY NOT INVENT

**A man of about fifty-one who has run a kitchen wants a record of what happened in that shed, and he is right, and a record is the only defence any of them has against a sentence that is not theirs.** A record about a room is the thing four volumes have refused, and they refused it for the reason this volume's guardrail forbids the word `true` on every page: **a record is a description.** Both sides are right and the room has no way to hold two things that say different things about the same evening. **The seventy-eighth sitting falls on Chapter 1089, which is also the one day in this volume on which the place behind that woman's chair comes to a round whole number of weeks, and no figure for it is printed on that page or on any other page of this volume.** The cost is that about nine people at that bench say they would not sign anything, one of them says so out loud, and the man of about fifty-one pays for that shed's electricity out of his own money and says nothing about it for about nine days.

**Does anybody else answer:** the woman of about thirty-four answers him, once, in about nine words, **and it is not a refusal to sign and it is not an agreement to sign.** **What stops him:** the room cannot hold two things that say different things about the same evening, and one of those things is a record and the other is about nine people who would not sign one.

**Invent no record, no form, no signature, no list of who came, no heading for the fifth column, and no printed figure for the place behind that chair on any page of this movement except Chapter 1089, which is the one page of the volume allowed one.** Invent no page on which Marek corrects anything. Invent no Exchange figure and no difference between the book and the tin and no remark on the sequence of sittings. Invent no woman of about thirty-four's third refusal: **she is asked the third question in this movement if the card's single answering is what the pass means by asking, and she must not refuse it a third time, because that refusal is at Chapter 1113 and is Movement VI's.**

**AND THE INHERITED DISAGREEMENT THAT WILL BITE THIS MOVEMENT, STATED SO THAT A PASS READING ONLY CARD 3 DOES NOT FALL INTO IT.** Two women now hold this volume's sentences and neither of them is the woman of about thirty-nine. **The woman of about thirty-one put a sentence into the world on Chapter 1065 and on Chapter 1077 took the hours card off her own counter and gave one half of a second sentence away. The woman of about thirty-four said on Chapter 1076 that the other woman's sentence was hers to say and not to give.** A record in this movement has to be a record about an evening and a bench and about nine people, and **it must not become a record about a sentence, because a sentence is not a thing that anybody in that room has agreed the shape of and two of the women who hold it do not agree with each other.** That is the movement's engine and it is the one thing a man with a record cannot get around.
