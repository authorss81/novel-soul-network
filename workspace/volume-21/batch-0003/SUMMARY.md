## 1. WHAT THIS MOVEMENT IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

**On the Sunday a man of about fifty-one who has run a kitchen for about nine years asked Marek, out loud on a step at the door of a shed on a road in a fourth district, for one written account of a Wednesday in that room, and was refused twice, and said out loud that in about nine years of a kitchen he has never had it in writing that nobody came off that floor hurt.** On the Monday he carried a lamp he had taken out of that kitchen four years ago into a shop to be looked at, was offered the mend for nothing, refused the kindness, and said that a woman who has cut his hair for twenty years said that sentence to him on a bus without knowing he had been in that room. On the Tuesday a woman of about thirty-one at a key counter in a first district was asked to say one sentence a second time so that it could be written down afterwards, and refused in nine words, and told him that the only thing anybody in this city could write down is that she would not say whether it is so, and the man then said out loud that the light in that shed was his to pay for from that Tuesday. **On the Wednesday he asked Marek, out loud at a counter in front of about four people, how many people had been in that shed, and was refused a figure in nine words and then told out loud why every one of those records has to begin with a number.** On the Thursday a woman of about thirty-four answered him once, at a canteen table in a second district, in nine words, and it was neither a refusal to sign nor an agreement to sign. On the Friday a woman of about forty-four asked him out loud whether he was still paying for that light and got back that he had not said anything else about it since about Tuesday. **On the Monday of the next week he asked three people at that bench what they were asked and what they said back, and each of the three answered him with a question instead, and one of them told him out loud that he had never been through the door of that room.** On the Tuesday he asked two people whether a woman of about thirty-nine had said anything on that Wednesday and was told that she said nothing at all for about an hour and a half, and that she said she would not be thanked for it, and that nobody is going to hand it to a man with a sheet. **On the Wednesday he asked about nine people in that shed, one at a time and in the order they came in, and got nine answers out loud of which not two were the same length, and a man who has never been through that door told him he had not been asked to sign anything, and a man he had never met said out loud that he would not sign it and neither would the rest, and by the time that man had got to the end of his own sentence about writing it down every one of that bench had said no to it in their own words.** On the Thursday the man came back for the lamp and paid the trade price and said nothing about a shed, and a woman who loves the man behind that counter said one sentence about her own office, and in the evening his hand went flat on an envelope with nothing behind it.

**AND WHAT IT IS NOT.** It is not a chapter about a villain, and there is no villain in it. **It contains no record, no form, no signature, no list of who came, no heading for the fifth column, no printed notice, and no page on which Marek corrects anything.** It contains no page on which the woman of about thirty-nine is asked anything by anybody, and none on which she is thanked, forgiven or written down. **It contains no page on which the woman of about thirty-four is asked the third question**, and she is asked nothing at all on any of the other nine. **It does not join the two halves of the second sentence on any page.** It contains no Exchange figure, no difference between the book and the tin, and no remark on where any sitting falls.
