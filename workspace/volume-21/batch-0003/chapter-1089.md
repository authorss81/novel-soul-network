# Chapter 1089 — Every Answer Came Back A Different Length

**A Wednesday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and then a room on a road in a fourth district where nine people were asked one thing each and the thing being asked of them was not the thing any of them had come for.**

---

Fifteen callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and fifteen names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and eleven days, two hundred and eighty-seven weeks and two days. The sixteenth of those ruled lines, never once written on, stood at one thousand eight hundred and one days, two hundred and fifty-seven weeks and two days. The bag under that long bench is a hundred and eighty-four days old, the printing that has never been on a wall is a hundred and seventy days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and sixty-one days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the place behind that woman's chair was empty and had been empty for eight hundred and eighty-nine days, which is one hundred and twenty-seven weeks to the day, and nobody in that room had ever asked him about it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **You have gone quiet since about two.**

Marek said: **I have been listening to the frame of that window.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It is the same frame.**

Marek said: **It is the same frame and it has moved about a quarter of an inch at the bottom, which is nothing, and it has moved in about nine years.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then the thing I told you about is real.**

Marek said: **The thing you told me about is real and it is not a burglar and that is the whole of what I have.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about nine people were in it, and the man of about fifty-one was stood at the end of that bench with both hands flat on it before anybody had sat down, and about four people in that room have said since that he had been in that shed before half past six and had not touched a thing in it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Nine questions and I am asking them one at a time.**

Marek said: **In the order they came in.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **In the order they came in.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **Nobody is going to ask her, then.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I know she is not here and I am not asking her.**

---

**And then he asked the first one, out loud, and the answer came back out loud in two pieces, and about four people in that room have said since that the first piece was short and the second one was three times as long and that nobody in that room told her how long her own had been.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **What were you asked.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **Three things.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **And the other two.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **I have not said to anybody this week and you are the one who said it out loud first, so you can have the first one and not the other two.**

---

**And then the second one, and the third one, and the fourth one, and about four people in that shed have said since that those three answers were all different again and that one of them was about what somebody else had said rather than about what the person had said themselves.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **I was asked what I had done before I came.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **I have never been in that room and you asked me what I was asked in it.**

The man of about fifty-five said: **What I was asked is a thing about you and not about me, and I am not going to do it in front of eight people.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Then I have got four of nine.**

Marek said: **You have got four, and one of them is about what somebody else said, and that is the one that will be the only one anybody repeats.**

---

**And then the eighth one said out loud what seven of them had already said in their own words that evening, and about four people in that shed have said since that it was the only refusal of it said in a voice the rest of the room could hear.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **I would not sign that and neither would the rest.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You have never been asked to sign anything.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **You have been asking us what we would put in it for about nine days and that is asking, and I have answered it, and this is the answer.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I did not mean it as a trap.**

The man of about thirty-eight said: **I know you did not, and you will write it down as a trap anyway, and that will be the first line on it.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-one said out loud that he was going to write all nine of those down and put his own at the top of them, and about four people in that room have said since that every one of that bench said no to that in their own words before he had got to the end of his own sentence, and that not two of the nine of them said it in the same words, and that not one of them said it about anybody but themselves.**

---

**And then the book in the green binding lay shut on that table through the whole of that evening, and about four people in that room have said since that it was on that table where it was on the last Wednesday and that not one of them looked at it twice and that nobody at that bench said anything about it.**

Nobody said anything about the book.

Marek said: **It is shut.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I know it is shut. I did not come for that.**

Marek said: **Nobody comes for that.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people on that bench have said since that nine answers were given out loud and that not two of them were the same length and that a man of about fifty-one has not written any of them down.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, where the rails were still under the floor and the doors were off their tracks.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with a terminal block that had been pushed in behind the plate and could not be reached to be checked.

He brought the block forward, checked both terminations and proved the lamp from a chair.

**Twenty-one pounds**, he said. **A block you cannot get at is a block you have not fitted, you have hidden.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate with the lid held on by two screws and one of them was in a slot that had been used before.

He put in a screw that goes into solid metal and proved the socket from the gate in the wet.

**Eighteen pounds**, he said. **A screw in a used slot holds until the first cold night.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the element leads made off to two terminals and the earth tail left under the clamp of one of them.

He moved the earth tail off that clamp and proved the case with an instrument on it.

**Twenty-six pounds**, he said. **An earth under a live clamp is an earth that stops being an earth.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting that had been earthed to the plate screw and nothing else.

He put an earth on the fitting itself and proved the fitting with the door shut.

**Seventeen pounds**, he said. **The plate is thin and the fitting is not, and the fitting is what a person can touch.**

**Eighty-two pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**
