# Chapter 1081 — He Wanted It Written Down

**A Sunday was a bench until about two and the shutter came down at about two on it, and then a room on a road in a fourth district with the rails still under the floor, and the whole of that Sunday after two was one conversation standing up between two men on a step.**

---

Five callers came into that shop on that Sunday and five names were written against that Sunday's book as each of them came in, all of them before noon, and the shutter came down at about two that day.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and one days, two hundred and eighty-five weeks and six days. What is in a carrier bag under that long bench is a hundred and seventy-four days old, the printing that has never gone on a wall in this city is a hundred and sixty days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and fifty-one days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four told him that a tap at the back of that room had been dripping since Friday and that she had put a cloth under it and had not told anybody.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Since Friday.**

Marek said: **A cloth under a tap is the correct thing to do about nine days out of ten.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **And on the tenth.**

Marek said: **On the tenth you want somebody in here before about seven in the morning.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have been putting up with the tenth since Friday.**

Marek said: **Then it is mine to mend on a weekday and you need not carry it.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about four people were stood about in it, and the man of about fifty-one, who has run a kitchen for about nine years, was on that step with his coat open and there was flour still in the creases of his knuckles.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I want it written down.**

Marek said: **Say what you want written down.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I want one account of that Wednesday.**

Marek said: **There is one.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Then put that one down.**

Marek said: **No. And I am not going to write that room down for anybody.**

---

**And then he said out loud the reason he wanted it, which is a thing about himself and not about that room, and about four people in that shed have said since that he had been asked why he wanted it three times that month and had not said it until that Sunday.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Without it, the only thing anybody has got is that sentence.**

Marek said: **Yes.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **And you will not tell me it is wrong.**

Marek said: **No. And I will not tell you it is so either, and you have had both refusals out of one man in about nine seconds.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is not the same as not knowing.**

Marek said: **It is not the same as knowing and it is the only place I have got to stand.**

---

**And then he said what he would have in it, and about four people in that shed have said since that nobody else in there said anything at all while he said it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **In nine years of a kitchen I have never had it in writing that nobody came off that floor hurt.**

Marek said: **Then you are asking me for a thing I cannot give you and not for one I could.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You could give me a sheet.**

Marek said: **I could. And it would be about half of what happened and none of what you are asking for, and you would be the one who found out which half later, and I am not going to do that to you.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Then I will ask the others myself.**

Marek said: **Ask them.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people on that bench have said since that a man who has run a kitchen for about nine years asked a man to write a Wednesday down and was told no twice, and that he went out into the wet without his coat buttoned over that answer.**

---

**That day's work at that counter was finished by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road where one of them carries heat, and he had not been inside any of them since about Friday.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a pendant that had been shortened at the ceiling rose and left hanging out about four inches below it.

He took the pendant back up to the rose, made the joint and proved the lamp from a chair with the shade on.

**Sixteen pounds**, he said. **A pendant hanging under a rose is a lamp that anybody carrying something through will hit.**

A socket in that yard had been put in under a metal lid with the lid fixed down over the whole of it.

He took the lid off, made a proper gland and proved the socket out in the wet with the lid shut again over it.

**Twenty-two pounds**, he said. **A lid over a socket is a lid over the thing that keeps the rain out.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a flex that went through a hole in a tiled wall with the hole left the width of the flex and nothing else.

He sleeved it, filled the hole and proved the cooker with the oven on and the door shut.

**Fourteen pounds**, he said. **A hole the width of a flex is a hole that water has already found.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting with its flex stapled to the ceiling at two points about a foot apart.

He un-stapled it, put a proper grip on it and proved the light with the shade on.

**Nine pounds**, he said. **Two staples are not a fixing. They are a promise somebody made and did not keep.**

**Sixty-one pounds is the sum of those four on that Sunday, exact, and it is the smallest day's work on that sheet since about four weeks ago.**
