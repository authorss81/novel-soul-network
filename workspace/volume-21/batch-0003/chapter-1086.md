# Chapter 1086 — The Thing He Said Once

**A Friday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and that Friday evening was spent in a shed on a road in a fourth district by about nine people who had all come for nothing in particular and stayed about forty minutes.**

---

Ten callers came into that shop on that Friday and ten names were written against that Friday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and six days, two hundred and eighty-six weeks and four days. The bag under that long bench is a hundred and seventy-nine days old and has not been touched since about Wednesday, the printing that has never been on a wall in this city is a hundred and sixty-five days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and fifty-six days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four asked him whether that lamp is supposed to stay on all night and he told her what he would be able to do about it on a weekday.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Is that lamp supposed to be on all night.**

Marek said: **No.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Then somebody is leaving it.**

Marek said: **Somebody is leaving it, and it is not the woman of about fifty-four.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That was not a question and you have answered it anyway.**

Marek said: **It is a thing you can get an answer to by asking me.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and about nine people were stood about in it, and the man of about fifty-one was on that step again where he had been the week before, and he came in without saying anything about the lamp or the sheet or the number.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Nine of them.**

Marek said: **Nine of them.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is a figure.**

Marek said: **That is how many are stood in here and I did not go round the outside of it.**

---

**And then a woman of about forty-four who had been in that room on that Wednesday said, out loud, in that shed, the thing about the light, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it to him and not to Marek and that it was the only thing anybody said about it that night.**

The woman of about forty-four said: **You said on Tuesday that the light in here was yours.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I did.**

The woman of about forty-four said: **Are you still paying for it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I have not said anything else about it since Tuesday.**

The woman of about forty-four said: **That was not what I asked you.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **No. It was what I said.**

---

**And then Marek said out loud the reason he is the wrong man for that sheet, and about four people in that shed have said since that he said it standing at the back where he stands, and that nobody came nearer the front for it.**

Marek said: **If I put a Wednesday down I would be putting down a Wednesday I remember. That is the whole of what I have got on it and it is not the same as what happened, because what happened belonged to about nine people and I was one of them for about two hours.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You would remember it well enough.**

Marek said: **I would remember my own part of it well enough, and then there would be my part of it on the top of theirs with my handwriting on it, and after about nine days nobody could tell which half came off that bench.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Put it down as mine.**

Marek said: **Then it is a page about what a man in a kitchen remembers about an evening, and that is worse and not better, and I have thought about that since about Tuesday.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that room have said since that the light over that bench had been out of somebody's pocket for three days and that nobody had said so out loud again after about Tuesday.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was done by about half past five, and at about half past six he went across to four converted units off that service road and worked there until about eight and put nothing up on any wall.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with a piece of paper folded and wedged behind the terminals to hold them apart.

He took the paper out, set the gap and proved the lamp from the floor.

**Twenty pounds**, he said. **Paper behind a terminal is a gap somebody measured once and will not measure again.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate that had been screwed down over the cable so that the entry was pinched between the plate and the box.

He took the plate off, released the cable and proved the socket in the wet.

**Twenty-two pounds**, he said. **Screw a plate down on a cable and you have made a fault with a lid on it.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the grill element wired across the oven element instead of through it.

He put it through its own terminal and proved the grill alone and then the grill with the fan.

**Fifteen pounds**, he said. **A grill across an oven is an oven that will not get to the top on its own.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting that had been carried in from another room and had a different hole pattern in its base.

He made the entry to suit the fitting and proved the light with the door shut.

**Twenty-seven pounds**, he said. **Bring a fitting from another room and it brings its own screws and none of its own holes.**

**Eighty-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Friday, exact.**
