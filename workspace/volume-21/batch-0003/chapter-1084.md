# Chapter 1084 — It Has To Begin With A Number

**A Wednesday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and that Wednesday was spent at one counter being asked for a number by a man who had begun to build something in his head and wanted the first line of it settled.**

---

Fourteen callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and fourteen names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and four days, two hundred and eighty-six weeks and two days. The fourteenth of those ruled lines, ruled below the thirteenth and blank, stood at one thousand eight hundred and forty days, two hundred and sixty-two weeks and six days. The bag under that long bench is a hundred and seventy-seven days old, that printing which has never been on a wall is a hundred and sixty-three days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and fifty-four days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four had put that door lamp on a long flex so that it could be carried to the far end of that room, and she told him that she had done it herself and had not asked anybody.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I carried it over to the far end.**

Marek said: **How.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It is on a long flex and I picked it up at the other end.**

Marek said: **Then you have picked it up by the flex and not by anything made to be picked up.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It came up in my hand all that time.**

Marek said: **A lamp comes up all that time until about the third.**

---

**And then at about half past three the man of about fifty-one came into that shop and asked Marek, out loud, at that counter, in front of about four people, how many people had been in that shed on the Wednesday, and about four people in that shop have said since that he asked it in the ordinary voice a man uses when he asks for a thing he expects to be given.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **How many were in there.**

Marek said: **On which night.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **The Wednesday. The one you were at.**

Marek said: **I never counted them and I am not starting.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You were stood at the back of it.**

Marek said: **I was stood at the back of it and I was not stood there with a pen.**

---

**And then Marek told him out loud why the number would not come, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said it in the same voice he prices work in and that the man did not sit down while it was being said.**

Marek said: **Every one of those starts with a number. How many came, how many were asked, how many put something in. That is the first line and there is no way to leave it out, and if you have a first line you have a record, and if you have a record you have a set of things that were left out, and the things that were left out are the ones that come back to me at about four in the morning for the rest of my life.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **So it is a number you will not give me.**

Marek said: **It is a number I do not have, and it is a number I will not invent, and those are two different reasons and you only needed the first one.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You have given me two reasons to be careful with and one answer.**

Marek said: **Take the two reasons and leave the answer.**

---

**And then the man said what he had been doing with his own Tuesday, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said it while he was taking a coat off a hook and that about four of them have said since that he asked four people in a kitchen about eleven and got nothing he could write.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I asked four people in a kitchen about eleven.**

Marek said: **And what did they say.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **They said what they would have said if I had asked them the time of day.**

Marek said: **Then they have told you what you have got.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is four people who were there.**

Marek said: **That is four people who were there and a man with no idea which four, and put those in that order and it is not an account, it is a list with the wrong order on it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people at that counter have said since that a man of about fifty-one asked a man who had been in that room how many people were in it, and did not get a figure, and told about four people that his kitchen would not give him one either.**

---

**That day's work at that counter and in those four units was done by about half past five, and he did not leave that service road again until about eight that night.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a pendant that had been wired straight through with no switch anywhere on the whole of it.

He cut it, put a switch in the drop and proved the light from the door with the door shut.

**Twenty pounds**, he said. **A lamp with no switch is a lamp somebody carries a chair for.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate that had been painted over twice with the lid stuck down under the paint.

He freed the lid, made the earth and proved the socket from the gate with an instrument on it.

**Twenty-four pounds**, he said. **Paint on a lid is only paint until somebody wants that socket.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the oven door catch bent so that the door would shut and not latch.

He took the catch out, made a new staple and proved the oven door shutting and latching twice.

**Sixteen pounds**, he said. **A door that shuts and does not latch is a door somebody will force one day.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a fitting that had been painted round with two colours and a third under it.

He took the paint back to the edge of it and proved the light with the shade on.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said. **Three coats on one fitting is three people who did not like the first one.**

**Seventy-nine pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact, and it is the largest of the four sums on that sheet this week.**

*Conditions of the close.* **That green-bound book and that tin beside it were both shut at ten on that Wednesday, and no figure is printed here for either one of them and no difference of any kind between the two of them.
That ninth chair faced what it faces through every hour of that Wednesday and did not turn on a leg, and nobody in that room looked at it, and whoever last turned it by a leg is not named on any page of this volume.
That room under a building in a first district was dark at ten on that Wednesday and is dark at this hour, and this page does not open it and no other page in this volume does.
That register of correct acts that changed nothing said four at both ends of that Wednesday and is not added to by anything said at that counter, and a man refusing to invent a number is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named at the foot of this page one after another, and no two of them are brought together in a sentence. Two nicks cut into that rail. That card, creased once across the middle. A board up on two nails. Nine torn corners, one off each of nine fronts. The lid on that tin. That shutter and the bar across it. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back of that shelf. That envelope on that bench with its flap open and empty. A pencil lying in a margin.
Six are named outside those ten and stand separately from all of them: the cage down the long side of that counter, a carrier bag under a long bench with a hundred and fifty sheets in it, a plastic sleeve in a drawer in a woman's own flat with nine words and a date on it, a folded paper in a second district's drawer with a name struck out above another one written under it, a leaflet taped below the lower of two clean rectangles on a wall in a fourth district, and a page carrying nine names and no second line under them.
Whatever is filed behind a dated rule over a question this page will not print is opened to any person who asks for it.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody. One man in this city has now been refused a figure, a page and an answer by three people in three days, and about nine people in that shed are about to be asked what four people in a kitchen could not be asked, and nobody in this city has stopped any of it.**
