# Chapter 1087 — Each Of Them Asked Him Back

**A Monday was a bench until about two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and that Monday evening was spent in a shed on a road in a fourth district by a man asking three people the same question one at a time and being asked three back.**

---

Thirteen callers came into that shop on that Monday and thirteen names were written against that Monday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away by about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten that night.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at two thousand and nine days, two hundred and eighty-seven weeks to the day. The eighteenth of those ruled lines, second up from its foot, stood at one thousand seven hundred and twenty-seven days, two hundred and forty-six weeks and five days. The bag under that long bench is a hundred and eighty-two days old, the printing that has never been on a wall is a hundred and sixty-eight days old, and the four words said at a desk in a first district are a hundred and fifty-nine days old.**

---

**He was in that room off that line in Saltmarket until about two, and about four people were in it, and the woman of about fifty-four said that somebody had been in that room over the weekend and had left a window open, and that she could not tell whether that was the man who comes on Mondays or somebody else.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **A window was open when I got in.**

Marek said: **On which floor.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **This one.**

Marek said: **Then somebody has a way in I have not got.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I am telling you because it is cold.**

Marek said: **You are telling me because you have already decided it was not me and I have not had a chance.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went across the city to that shed, and the man of about fifty-one had got there before him and was standing at the near end of that bench with about four people already sitting on it, and he asked his question to the first of them out loud and about four people in that room have said since that he asked it the same three times in three different voices.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **What were you asked, and what did you say back.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **What time did I get here.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is not what I asked.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **It is the first thing anybody asks when they ask me that.**

---

**And then he asked the second one, and about four people in that room have said since that she let him get a whole answer out before she asked him anything back.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You were there. What were you asked.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **Who was already in when you came.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **I do not know who was already in.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **Then that is the answer to your question and you have had it for about nine seconds.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is not an answer.**

The man of about twenty-seven said: **You have not got a better one off me.**

---

**And then he asked the third one, a man who had been coming to that room on and off for about four years, and about four people in that shed have said since that the man of about fifty-one asked him what he was asked as though they had all been there together, and that the answer was one line and it stopped the asking for about a minute.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You. What were you asked that Wednesday.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **I have never been in that room in my life.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You come up here.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **I come up here. There is a stair and a landing and a door on that landing and I have never been through that door, and you have put me on a list of people who were in that room when nobody in this city made a list.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **You said you came up here.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **And I have. That is not the same thing and if you cannot hold the two of them apart you will not hold about nine people apart either.**

---

**And then Marek said out loud where he stands in it, and about four people in that shed have said since that he said it from the back and that it was the shortest thing he said all that month and that the man of about fifty-one did not turn round when he said it.**

Marek said: **I will not stop you and not help you.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **That is the first thing you have said to me this month that I can write somewhere.**

Marek said: **It is not for writing. It is so that you stop asking me what I am going to do about it.**

The man of about fifty-one said: **Will you be at it on Wednesday.**

Marek said: **I will be in this room on Wednesday and you know that already.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shed on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people on that bench have said since that three people were asked one question and answered it with three other questions, and that the man of about fifty-one wrote none of them down because he had nothing to write.**

---

**He finished that counter and those four units at about half past five. At half past six he was across the city on that service road, and he was in the last of those four rooms until about eight.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a switch that had been wired into the line instead of the neutral and had been getting away with it for about four years.

He put it onto the neutral and proved the lamp from the floor twice.

**Sixteen pounds**, he said. **A switch on the line works until somebody touches the fitting while it is on.**

A socket in that yard had been put in at a height where the lid could not be opened without a spoon.

He brought it down to a height a person can open standing up and proved it in the wet.

**Twenty-five pounds**, he said. **A lid that needs a spoon is a lid that will be pried off with a knife.**

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with an earth strap left hanging inside the casing and not made off to anything.

He made the strap off to the casing and proved the case earthed with an instrument on it.

**Nineteen pounds**, he said. **An earth strap hanging loose is a strap somebody has already cut to length and not thought about.**

A light in that second unit had been put in on a rose with two cables into the plate and nothing to tell them apart.

He separated them at the plate and proved the light with the door shut.

**Thirteen pounds**, he said. **Two cables into one plate with nothing to tell them apart is two cables waiting.**

**Seventy-three pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**

*1090.
Monday of week 355, at ten. That is the three hundred and thirty-second day of this stretch of days. Thirteen callers came into that shop on that Monday and thirteen names were written against that day's line, the last of them let away by about half past eight.
**A man of about fifty-one asked three people at that bench what they were asked and what they said back, and each of the three answered him with a question instead, and one of them told him out loud that he had never been through the door of that room; Marek told that room out loud in about nine words that he will not stop the man and will not help him, and the man said he would be there on Wednesday. The day's four jobs came to seventy-three pounds, exact.**

*Conditions of the close.* **The green-bound book and the tin beside it were both shut through that Monday, the book from the moment that room opened and the tin at every hour, and no figure is given here for either one of them and no difference between them.
That ninth chair faced that wall for every hour of that Monday and no leg of it went round, and the hand that last turned it that way is not named in these pages.
The room under that building in a first district was dark at about ten on that Monday and is dark now, and it stays shut across every day of this volume.
That register of correct acts that changed nothing said four at both ends of that Monday, nobody in this city is entered on it, and a man being answered with three questions is not an entry on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named at the foot of this page, each on its own and with nothing named beside it. Two nicks cut into that rail. That card, creased once across the middle. A board up on two nails. Nine torn corners off nine separate fronts. The lid on that tin. That shutter. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back of that shelf. An envelope lying on that bench with its flap up. A pencil lying in a margin.
Six are named outside those ten and stand clear of all of them: the cage down the long side of that counter, a carrier bag under a long bench with a hundred and fifty sheets in it, a plastic sleeve in a drawer in a woman's own flat with nine words and a date on it, a folded paper in a second district's drawer with a name struck through and another written under it, a leaflet taped under the lower of two clean rectangles on a wall in a fourth district, and a page that carries nine names and stops there.
Whatever has been filed behind a dated rule over a question not printed here can be read by any person who asks to read it.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody. Three people have now been asked one question in that shed and answered with another, and one of the three has never been in the room at all, and five of about nine have not been asked, and the man of about fifty-one has begun to understand that a list of questions is not a list of answers, and nobody in this city has told him he has understood it correctly.**
