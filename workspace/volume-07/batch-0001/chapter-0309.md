# Chapter 309 — The Fifth Part

The Wednesday of week one hundred and ten was the day a man of twenty-two went to four buildings with a woman of thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors, and in the third of them two men who have done the same thing at a barrier for eleven years could not stop doing it, and nobody in that building was frightened and nothing was wrong; and the same day four hundred was said out loud four times in one stairwell by four people and it came out as four different numbers and no two of the people who said them knew about the others.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Six jobs attended and none of them was in the third building and none of them was in any of the four.

The word was at nine and ten and it was nine and ten in about four hundred rooms this morning and nobody in this file knows how many.

The room off the service road is three hundred and three days old and is warm and about four people came in for the temperature on the Tuesday and about four came in on the Wednesday.

---

The four buildings are in two districts and they are ordinary: flats over shops, a workshop block, a small college, and a building with a gate in it. They are four of the four hundred and eleven premises a district keeps a list of, which is a fact nobody in this file would have known a week ago and is now a fact four people know.

Every one of those four buildings has about nine hundred plates between them, in the sense that each has plates on its doors, and every one of those plates carries a number that belongs to a different line of the same company's list, and the numbers are the numbers they should be if the list had been written the way the building is.

"**They are not wrong numbers. That is the thing people get wrong. They are right numbers for a different building.**"

He asked what she could actually do about it.

"**Nothing. There is a man at the company who could tell you how many of his own plates are on the wrong line and he has told about four people and it is about nine hundred and he cannot change any of them, and he is not a villain and he is owed nothing and he has not slept.**"

---

They went in at about eleven. The man of twenty-nine who works on lifts came out of the third building at about twenty past eleven on his own lunch and was surprised and stayed.

He works on about nine buildings a week. He is one of the four people in this case who can get into a building a person cannot get out of, and he has said so in about nine seconds twice.

The third building has a barrier and a gate and a man of about forty on the gate in the morning and a man of about thirty-five on it in the afternoon, and between them they have done one thing at that barrier for eleven years and it is a handover and it takes about nine seconds.

He asked the man of about forty if he could do it.

The man of about forty did it. Four times. It was fine. He said it was fine in the flat voice of a person reporting on a machine.

The man of about thirty-five did it, and stopped, in the middle of it, with his hand where it was.

"**Again.**"

He did it again and stopped in the same place. It is the same place both times, and it is about four seconds before the end. He was asked how long he had been doing that, and said since Tuesday. He was asked what he had done on Monday, and said that he had done it.

---

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and then the man of twenty-nine said the thing that this entry is for, in about nine words, and he is a person who has said the true thing twice in about four years and this is not one of those and he said so.

"**I have not got the words for this one yet. I have got the shape. It is that the thing they are doing needs a person to tell them where it stops, and this week the telling has stopped, and they cannot find the end of it on their own, and they are not frightened, and they are not ill, and they are not stupid.**"

Somebody behind them said that then it was the thing they were taught.

"**It is the thing they are taught and it is not being taught any more this week and it has not been untaught. It has been taken away. I have been the man in the lanyard in about nine buildings a week for four years and I have never once thought about what happens on the day I am not there.**"

He said that last part to the man of about thirty-five and not to anybody else and did not explain it.

---

The practice on a card in a company training room has four of the five parts of a technique in it. Everybody in that stairwell knows the shape of that, or would, and a clerk of forty-four pulled about nine hundred of those cards out of use in about four days in an earlier volume by invoking a printed standard correctly, and it changed nothing, because the cards were never the thing anybody was using.

The fifth part is the boundary-release beat. The beat that performs the boundary. The one that says, in a body, *I have got this far and I am putting it down and I am the one who decides that.*

Nobody in that stairwell said that sentence and one of them has said a version of it in a different context.

What the man of twenty-nine said instead was: **they have been taught to wait to be told, and this week nobody is telling them, and waiting is not a fault, and a person can be damaged by the absence of something they were never supposed to learn to do without.**

---

The woman of thirty-four went up first and he went second and they came down at about half past two.

At the third landing she stopped, and it was a landing, and there was nothing in it.

"**Do not say anything.**"

"**I am not going to say anything.**"

"**I am going to say one thing and I am not going to say it again, and I am asking you not to write it down, and you are going to agree, and I want the agreeing to be quick.**"

"**I agree.**"

"**I have stood on a step four times in about four years and I could not move, and I have not told anybody, and I told you instead, and you are a person who writes things down for a living and you have just agreed not to and I would like you to know that I noticed how fast you did it.**"

Neither of them said anything else about it on that stair. It is recorded here because this is a file no character in this case can read and because a refusal that is not written down anywhere is a thing that has to be written down somewhere or it does not exist, and the whole of this case is about which of those two it is.

She said it on a stair and not in a room because a room is where things in this case are said so that other people can be in the room, and she did not want anybody else in this one. He did not tell Talia Venn, and she is four miles away and was in a room with nine chairs in it that afternoon, and he has not been asked why and there is a question he has not answered and is not going to ask her.

---

They were in the car park for about four minutes and the man of fifty-one with a tool bag was there because he had a stair door in that building, and the man of twenty-nine was there because he had not gone back in.

Then four hundred was said four times and it came out as four numbers.

He said it first, because it was his to say and it had been sitting in him since an earlier volume.

"**About four hundred competent people in this city can still invoke a printed rule. It is printed, it is numbered, it is dated, it has never been withdrawn, and it restricts rather than repeals, and about four hundred people in this city are competent to use it and none of them is on a list and the number cannot be produced by anybody.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Four hundred buildings I can get into that a person cannot get out of.**"

That was the man of twenty-nine, in about nine seconds, as he has said it twice before.

"**Four hundred lines.**"

That was the man of fifty-one, and then he stopped, and then he said the sentence that is the reason this entry has a paragraph of its own.

"**That is not mine.**"

"**No.**"

"**Then I have been saying the same number as about four hundred other things for about four years, and so has he, and so has about nine people in a room, and the four of us have never once been in a room.**"

---

The woman of thirty-four asked the only question anybody asked in that car park and she asked it of him.

She asked whether anybody could produce theirs, pointing at each of them in turn, and all three of them said no.

"**Then we are three people in a car park holding the same number for three different reasons and none of us can prove it, and about four hundred competent people in this city could each tell you a completely different thing if you asked them, and about four of them would tell you the truth and the other about four hundred would tell you a different truth without knowing it.**"

"**That is right.**"

"**Then I am not going to be a list of it. I am not going to be four hundred anything, and if anybody in this city comes to me and says we need everybody who knows about this, I am going to say no, and I have never refused anything in my life and I am going to start this week.**"

She said it in the car park and about four people heard it and none of them improved on it and she was not thanked and she has never been thanked and she did not say it to be thanked and said so.

*312. Wednesday of week 111, at about four in the afternoon in a car park in a second district, and this entry is four buildings, two men at a barrier, one refusal on a stair, and one number said four times.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Six jobs attended, none of them in any of the four buildings, and the four buildings were not jobs and were not docketed and were not paid for. One stair door in that building, attended by a man of fifty-one with a tool bag. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and no days-off figure is carried. The room off the service road is three hundred and three days old. A card in a rail is three hundred and seven. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and twenty-three, thirteen one hundred and seventy-four, fourteen one hundred and thirty-nine, fifteen one hundred and eighteen, sixteen ninety-three, seventeen seventy-five, eighteen twenty-one. Wall unchanged, eighteen lines in twelve hands and thirteen names. None of the four buildings has been added to any list in this case and the four hundred and eleven premises a district keeps is a fact about a district and not about this case.**

*Four buildings, and the plates.* **Composition: flats over shops, a workshop block, a small college, a building with a gate. Four of the four hundred and eleven premises a district keeps, a fact nobody in this file knew eight days ago. Plates: about nine hundred between the four, and every plate carries a number belonging to a different line of the same company's list, and the numbers are correct for a different building rather than incorrect. Corrected, in that stairwell, in about nine seconds, by a woman of thirty-four: they are not wrong numbers, they are right numbers for a different building. Company position, unaltered: a field service engineer can say how many of his own company's plates are on the wrong line, is about nine hundred, cannot change any of them, is not a villain, is owed nothing, and has not slept. What a person can actually do about nine hundred plates: nothing. Actions taken on the four buildings: none by anybody, and this is recorded because it is the fourth time in about four years that a competent person has correctly done nothing.**

*The barrier.* **Site: a gate in a building, one handover shape, roughly nine seconds, two men, eleven years between them. Older of the two: four consecutive attempts, all four completed, reported in the flat register of a man describing a machine. Younger: two attempts, both halted at an identical point roughly four seconds short of the end of the shape, hand unmoved. Onset: Tuesday. Status on Monday: completed. Nothing in that building was broken, nobody in it was frightened, and no fault was found by anybody. What the man of twenty-nine who works on lifts said, in about nine words, having said first that he had not got the words for it and only the shape: that the thing needs a person to tell them where it stops; that the telling has stopped; that they cannot find the end of it on their own; that they are not frightened, not ill and not stupid. His addition, addressed to the man of about thirty-five and to nobody else: that he has been the man in the lanyard in about nine buildings a week for four years and has never once thought about what happens on the day he is not there. **The fifth part of a technique is the beat that performs the boundary. The cards in a company training room have four of the five parts and the fifth is absent, and a printed standard said so, and about nine hundred of those cards came out of use in about four days in an earlier volume and it changed nothing. Nobody in that stairwell said the sentence about a boundary-release beat. The man of twenty-nine said the sentence that carries it, and it is a different sentence, and it is the reason this entry exists.**

*One refusal, on a landing.* **Asker: a woman of thirty-four, in about four seconds, with no preamble, on a bare landing. The request was silence. The grant was immediate and worded. One statement followed, given once and declared not to be repeated: a count of about four over about four years, an inability to move, no prior witness of any kind, and the choice of him as the one person told. The accompanying ask: that it not be written. The agreement: given inside a second, and its speed was observed aloud by the person who had asked, and that observation is hers, is not a criticism, and was not improved on. Venue for the whole of it: a stairwell, deliberately not a room, on her stated and true grounds that a room is where this case says things so that other people may be present, and she did not want a second person present for this. **Recorded in this file because a refusal that is not written down anywhere does not exist, and the whole of this case is which of those two things a refusal is. It is on no surface in this case. He is the only witness. He has not told anybody and is not to be asked to.**

*One number, four times, in about four minutes.* **First, the man of twenty-two: about four hundred competent people in this city can still invoke a printed rule; printed, numbered, dated, never withdrawn, restricted and not repealed; none of them on a list; the number cannot be produced. Second, the man of twenty-nine who works on lifts: about four hundred buildings he can get into that a person cannot get out of, said in about nine seconds, as he has said it twice before. Third, the man of fifty-one with a tool bag: four hundred lines, being the number of lines in this city carrying one cook's four words, in a different hand every time, over about four years, in about nine buildings. Then, immediately, and this is the finding: **that is not mine.** Recognition, by him, of a fact he had not had for about four years: that he, and the man of twenty-nine, and about nine people in a room, have all been saying the same number for different things and have never been in a room together. Can anybody produce theirs: no, and no, and no, said by him, in that order, about his own first, and the man's second, and hers last. Her statement of the shape of it, in about nine seconds: three people in a car park holding the same number for three different reasons, none able to prove it, about four hundred competent people in this city able to give a completely different answer each, about four of them telling the truth and the rest telling a different truth without knowing it. **Three of the three referents in this manuscript are now separated on the page by four people in a car park. The first was said by him and he has never said it in a room off a service road. The second is the man of twenty-nine's. The third is the man of fifty-one's and the cook is not in that car park and has not been told and is not going to be.** Her last act of the day, unasked for, in about nine seconds: she is not going to be a list of it, and if anybody in this city comes to her and says we need everybody who knows about this she is going to say no, and she has never refused anything in her life and is starting this week. Not thanked. Not improved on. She did not say it to be thanked and said so and that sentence is hers and is in nobody else's mouth.**

*What the day did not touch.* **He taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted nothing, and went to four buildings as a second pair of eyes with a bag, and no practice was run in his presence in any of them. No panel, no marker, no relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded. The printed rule was named in a car park and not in a room, and its number is in no file of this case and is in no document in it. The Exchange did not sit and the next sitting is a Wednesday seven days from today. The secondment is unchanged. The heating is on, costs more, and the fortnight is unpaid and unresolved. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page in the ring binder is unread and the refusal on that landing is not that page and is not in that binder and will not be. The standing question is not stated here. The question written on a service road is not answered here. No message arrived from anybody. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 312.**
