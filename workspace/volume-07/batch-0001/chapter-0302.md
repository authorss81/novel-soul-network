# Chapter 302 — Nine In One Morning

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and nine was the morning a woman of twenty-four counted nine people in one morning in a mobile clinic in the Lower Wards who each described the same thing in their own words, which is that a practice they have done for years ended before they were ready for it to end; she wrote all nine down in her own words in about eleven minutes, telephoned none of them, told nobody, and did not write down the tenth, and the tenth was herself.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Four jobs attended and one address in Nine Bridges not attended and not chased, and a stair fire door is on tomorrow's list for the tenth time.

The word was at nine and ten and it reached eleven on no hour of it. Nothing in this entry is about that and nothing in this entry is stopped by it.

The room off the service road is two hundred and eighty-nine days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Tuesday evening for the temperature, and the clinic that this entry is about has a consent clipboard on its wall that no institution has ever asked her for.

---

The clinic is one room above a shuttered shop with about nine chairs in it, a sink, a screen, a trolley, and a window that faces the yard and not the street, and it opens at half past seven and closes at four, and Talia Venn has run it for about six years and is a resonance nurse and holds no post and is owed nothing, and on the Wednesday of week one hundred and nine there were eleven appointments and about nine people came and two of the eleven were people who had been before.

There is a woman of about forty-three who drives it and does the venepuncture and has been doing both for about nine years, and she is not a resonance nurse and is not a mechanism and holds no post, and she is named in this file by a count.

---

The first account came at about twenty past eight and it was not a complaint.

A woman of about sixty-one who sews at a kitchen table on a second floor had been doing a stitch-and-pull on a bias binding for nineteen years and had felt the release arrive at about the wrong moment, which is a phrase this case has no better word for, and she described it as *the row finishing itself while my hands were still on it*, and she was not frightened and she was not unwell and she wanted to know whether it was her eyes.

The second was a man of about thirty-one who drives a van, and he said that the turn he has made at the same corner for six years arrived early, and that he has been in an accident because of a turn, and that he did not want to talk about the accident and did not.

The third was a girl of about nine with her mother and neither of them could say it in practice words and both of them said it in the words they had, which were *it went faster than the song*.

The fourth and the fifth and the sixth were three women who work at the same table on the same afternoon in a first-floor room over a launderette and who all three said the same shape of thing and did not know about each other.

The seventh was a man of about forty-four who asked whether it was the new one and was told, in about four seconds, that there is no new one.

The eighth said nothing and put her hand flat on the table and opened it and closed it.

The ninth was a man of about seventy who had come for a blood pressure and who said the word *stopped* and then said that he meant the opposite of stopped, and corrected himself in about four seconds, and both halves are in the record.

---

She wrote all nine in her own words. Not summaries. The words.

She wrote them in the order they came in, in the seat they came in, with the time on each, and she wrote nine accounts in about eleven minutes, and nobody has ever read them and they are in a drawer in a room above a shuttered shop with about nine chairs in it, and that drawer is a clinical drawer and it can be asked for by anybody with a reason and by anybody without one, and she put them in it anyway.

The woman of about forty-three read the first one over her shoulder while she was writing the fourth and said one thing.

"**Those are tiredness, Talia. Nine people in a wet week is nine people in a wet week, and I have been in this room for nine years and I know what tired sounds like and that is what it sounds like.**"

"**How many of them would you have written down a month ago.**"

"**None of them. I would have written down a blood pressure.**"

"**That is not the same answer and you know it is not.**"

"**It is not. It is my answer.**"

---

At about a quarter to twelve the woman of forty-three went out to the yard for a cigarette and the woman of twenty-four did a resonance practice, which is a thing she does four or five times a day and has done for six years, and it consists of putting her thumb on somebody's pulse and listening for a shape and taking her thumb off.

She put her thumb on her own wrist.

The shape arrived early. She took her thumb off about four seconds before she would have and she sat on the edge of the trolley with her hand in her lap and looked at the trolley for about a minute, and then she did not write it down.

**That is the tenth account and it is the only one that is in a body and not on paper, and it is hers, and she is the only person in this case who knows it happened, and she has not told anybody and is not going to this week.**

---

She did not telephone him.

He was on a job in a second district until about two and then he was in a van for forty minutes, and she knew both of those things without asking, which is one of about four things about him that she has never asked for and has always had.

She wrote the nine accounts out again on a separate sheet at about half past three, in the same words, because she wanted to know whether the words survived being written twice, and they did not quite: four of the nine had changed tense and one of the nine had lost a clause, and the clause was the part about wanting to know whether it was her eyes, and she put that one back in by hand.

Then she put both sheets in the drawer, and locked the drawer, and wrote the clinic's own count of the day on the board on the wall — eleven appointments, nine people, two of them previous, one of them a repeat blood pressure — **which is true, and which is about a quarter of what happened in that room, and which is the only record of that day that anybody in this city can see without asking.**

She did not tell the man with the tool bag, who would have understood the smell and who was not there, and she did not tell the person of sixty whose job it is to ask questions, and she did not tell the room, and the not telling is her decision and not his and it is not a thing she is going to consult him about.

*305. Wednesday of week 109, at about four in the afternoon, and this entry is nine accounts, one of them deliberately not written, and a board on a wall with a quarter of it on.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven appointments and nine people; two of the eleven were people who had been before; one was a repeat blood pressure and one was a man of about seventy who corrected himself in about four seconds and both halves are in the record. Nine accounts in about eleven minutes in the sitter's own words, in seat order, with times. A second copy written out at about half past three: of the nine, four had changed tense, one had lost a clause, and the clause was restored by hand. Both copies in a clinical drawer in a room with nine chairs, locked, and no copy is on paper anybody outside that building can see without asking. The board on the wall carries eleven, nine, two, one, and that is about a quarter of the day. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating in the room off the service road is on; no figure of days off is carried. The room is two hundred and eighty-nine days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is two hundred and ninety-three. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and nine days old, thirteen one hundred and sixty, fourteen one hundred and twenty-five, fifteen one hundred and four, sixteen seventy-nine, seventeen sixty-one, eighteen seven. Wall unchanged: eighteen lines, twelve hands, thirteen names. No accounts in that drawer have been read by anybody and no request for them has been made.**

*The nine, by source, seat and time, with what each one is evidence of.* **One: a woman of sixty-one, a bias binding at a kitchen table, nineteen years of the same stitch and pull, the release arriving early; asked whether it was her eyes. Two: a man of thirty-one, a van, the same corner for six years, the turn arriving early; declined to discuss a prior accident and did not explain. Three: a girl of nine, present with a mother, no practice vocabulary, the words used were that it went faster than the song. Four, five and six: three women at one table on one afternoon over a launderette, same shape, mutually unacquainted. Seven: a man of forty-four, asked whether it was a new thing, told in about four seconds that there is no new thing, and did not accept it. Eight: a woman who said nothing and demonstrated with one hand. Nine: a man of about seventy, used the word stopped, corrected himself to the opposite, both retained. Common elements across nine: early completion, in a body that is not unwell, in a practice with a long history, in a person who is not frightened. Common element absent from all nine: any fault found, any second party, any device. What nine accounts are evidence of: that nine unrelated people in one district on one morning noticed the same shape. What nine accounts are not evidence of: why, how widespread it is, whether it happened to anybody else that week, and whether the woman of twenty-four's own thumb on her own wrist is a tenth account or a coincidence between one observer and one observer.**

*The objection, and the part of it that holds.* **From: a woman of about forty-three, nine years in that room, at about half past ten, over a shoulder, unprompted. Substance, in three claims: that nine accounts in a wet week is nine accounts in a wet week; that nine years of clinical contact makes her the competent judge of what tired sounds like; and that nine is not a finding. The first two claims are not refuted anywhere in this entry and are not improved on by anybody. The third is correct and is not a reason, and the reason it is not a reason is that the same competent person wrote none of the nine down a month ago, and said so, and said *that is not the same answer*. Weight carried by each: the first two are a competent person's opinion formed over nine years in a room and are unweighted by anything on this page; the third is arithmetically true and does not bear on cause. Not resolved in this entry. Not to be resolved by anybody who was not in that room.**

*The tenth, which is not on paper.* **Practitioner: the woman of twenty-four, in the same room, alone, at about a quarter to twelve, with the woman of forty-three out of the room. Practice: her own, a thumb on a wrist, four or five times a day for six years. Result: the shape arrived about four seconds before she would have released. Duration of the pause afterwards: about a minute. Recorded: nowhere. Not on either sheet, not on the board, not to him, not to the room, not to the woman of forty-three, not to a person of sixty whose job it is to ask questions. Reason she would give if asked, and has not been asked: that it is hers, and that a number a practitioner writes about herself in her own building is a number that is in her building. Whether the ninth account and the tenth are two is not decided in this entry and no writer may decide it by counting.**

*What the day did not touch.* **He was not in that room and did not know any of it until the Monday of week one hundred and eleven, twelve days later, and was told about all of it in a workroom four miles away and did not get a copy. She did not ring him and did not ring anybody. No consent was obtained and none was needed and no share was offered and none was made, and the words *offer*, *agreement* and *withdrawal* do not appear in the record because the day did not contain one. No panel and no marker and no relay. No message arrived from anybody. The printed rule was not mentioned and about four hundred competent people in this city can still invoke it. The Exchange did not sit. The secondment is unchanged. The heating is on and costs more and the fortnight is unpaid and unresolved and was not touched by any of this. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page in the ring binder is unread and this entry is not the reason. The standing question is not stated here. Nobody thanked anybody and about nine people in this city said a true thing to one person who wrote it down and one person said a true thing to herself and put it in a drawer and the difference between those two things is the whole of this entry. Entry 305.**
