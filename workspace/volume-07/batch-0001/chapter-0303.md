# Chapter 303 — The Standing Instruction

The Thursday morning of week one hundred and nine was the morning a lead repairer of forty-five told about nine crews that the standing instruction was wrong, and then told them she was not changing it, and both halves were said in the same four minutes and about four of the eleven crews did not send anybody out; and a man of twenty-two carried a true thing about a lift to that room and was told in about four seconds that a lift is not a practice, and he was right and she was right, and two crews sent nine people into a building in the Lower Wards where nothing at all was wrong.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Three jobs attended and a shift change at a workshop in the middle of this city that is not a job and is not on the docket and has not been on one in about nine years.

The word was at nine and ten. Four names on a lease cost more than they did and the room they pay for is two hundred and ninety days old and is warm.

---

That workshop is a set of four bays with a yard behind it and about eleven crews who go out in the morning and come back in the afternoon, and a lead repairer of forty-five with broad hands and a clipped voice and a metal braid of tools she wears under her shirt, who writes warnings on the backs of forms because she does not trust neat records that fit on one page.

She said it at about half past six, standing at the middle bench, with about nine people in it.

"**The standing instruction is wrong for this week.**"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"**I said it is wrong and I am not changing it, and both of those are true, and I would like about four of you to notice that I have said a thing in that shape before and to notice also that it has never once turned out to be both.**"

---

A man in the second bay said, from the middle of it, that he had heard the first half and not the second, and asked her for the second half in words she could put on the back of a form.

"**The second half is that the standing instruction is: if a release does not come, you hold, and you telephone, and you hold until a person answers, and if no person answers you hold until one arrives on site, and the person who arrives is the one who owns the release, and you do not finish somebody else's practice for them.**"

Somebody said that was correct. She said it was correct, and that it was correct in every week of the year, and that she had taught it to about nine crews a week for about nine years, and that she was not stopping this week because of a bad week.

He asked what was wrong with it, then. She said that it was correct, and that it was going to be expensive.

---

A woman of about thirty-eight at the end of the bench said the thing that the rest of that bay had been thinking since about a quarter to six.

"**Sera. On Tuesday a man in Nine Bridges rang his own building's maintenance number and got a recorded notice and it said the service was operating normally. On Wednesday the same thing in three buildings. On Thursday nobody rang anybody because everybody had heard the notice and believed it. That is not a practice stopping. That is a phone not working, and it is a phone, and phones come back.**"

Somebody in that bay said that phones come back in about four hours. She said phones come back in about four hours, and that these had been off since Tuesday. The fault, she said, was somebody else's, and she was not standing in a bay about it on a Thursday.

Somebody at the back said, in about nine words, that about eleven people a week in this city were still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and said it the way you say something you have said before.

He said that he had been one of the eleven for about four years, and that he had never once known what the man in the lanyard was going to do, and that on Monday the man in the lanyard had not been in that building at all, and that a lift in it had gone up and down all morning and had stopped where it always stops.

He said that if the lanyard had been in the building it would have been the same.

Nobody improved on it. Sera looked at the man for about four seconds and then looked at the bench.

"**That is the correct shape of the problem and it is not the problem.**"

"**It is not the problem.**"

"**It is a real thing and I have been fighting about it for four years and I am not going to stop on a Thursday because it is the first week it has cost anybody anything. What it costs is a person standing in a stairwell at eleven o'clock at night deciding whether he is allowed to finish. That is the whole cost. If he is not allowed, he is a person who was told to wait. If he is allowed, somebody's pump runs. I would rather be wrong about a person than right about a pump, and I have been asked which of those two I would rather be by about four people in about four years and my answer has not changed and it is not going to change on a Thursday.**"

---

About four of the eleven crews did not send anybody out that morning.

That is the count and it is not a mutiny and there was no vote and nobody took a vote and about four of the eleven crews are crews whose people have been with them for more than about four years, which is not a coincidence and is a thing about crews that no document in this city records.

Sera let them go. She said one sentence about it and it was about the reporting, not about the going.

"**If you go out you go out on a job number like anybody else and you bring back what you find and you bring back the thing you did not find as well, because the second one is the one that gets written down, and if I have to ask you for it you will make something up and I will not be able to tell.**"

That is the whole of what she said and it is correct and it is the only correct thing anybody in that bay said all morning and nobody thanked her for it.

---

He had the lift on the bench from about ten past seven, written out on the back of a docket-sized piece of paper in four lines, and he had it there for about nine hours and he offered it to two people and both of them were right.

The first was a woman of about thirty-four who came in about eleven, and he said the lift stopped between five and six four times and he watched it four times, and she said, in about four seconds, that that was a lift.

"**That is a lift.**"

He said that it was a fault in a lift in a building in Nine Bridges, that it had happened four times in about an hour, and that there was no door on that floor in that shaft. She said, *and?* He said that nobody knew.

"**Somebody at that building knows. Somebody has a book of what that lift does and you have written in it that it stopped somewhere it has never stopped, and the next time it does that, somebody will read that you wrote it, and you are telling me you did not put it on a docket.**"

"**No.**"

Then it is a piece of paper in a bag, she said, and it is worth nothing, and you were right about the docket and you are wrong about the rest of it.

---

The second was Sera, at about half past five, and she took the four lines and read them standing up and gave them back.

"**A lift is not a practice.**"

"**No.**"

"**I have not got a place to put that. I have got about nine crews and a bay and a yard and a thing I have to write on the back of a form because I do not trust a page, and if you bring me a lift I will get nothing out of it, and I will be able to say nothing about it, and the nothing will be on me.**"

"**Then what would be a practice.**"

"**I do not know. If I knew it I would not be standing here.**"

He put the four lines back in his book and she went to the door and he stood there for about four seconds and then she said, without turning round, that about four of her people had not gone out that morning, and that two of the other crews were in the Lower Wards, and that if he wanted to be useful he could go and be useful to them and not to her.

He said they did not need him. She said then do not go.

---

The two crews that went out to that building came back at about half past four.

About nine people in one building in the Lower Wards had finished their shifts early and had been on the pavement since about half past eleven with nothing to do, and the building has about four tenants, and one of them is a workshop and one is a kitchen and one is a stairwell with a bench in it, and the bench was full at about two in the afternoon.

Nothing in that building was broken. The lift worked. The plant ran. The meters were all where meters are. Two of the nine had a headache and one of them had not slept and one of them was crying outside a shop and one of them was about sixty-two and had walked to the wrong district and was still there.

There is no fault. A crew lead of about thirty-four said, on the yard at about half past four, in about nine seconds, that she had gone in to find a fault because that is the job and there is no fault and that is the finding, and that she had found nine people on a bench instead.

Sera heard it at about five. She said two things.

"**Do not write it down.**"

"**It is nine people,**" she said.

"**Nine people on a bench is a Thursday. In about four days it will be ninety people on a bench and in about four days this room will want a piece of paper about it, and the piece of paper will be about ninety and by the time anybody reads it the nine will have gone into it and the nine are the only ones anybody did anything for.**"

"**And the four crews.**"

"**The four crews were right and I was right and I am going to be asked to say which one of us was rightest by somebody in a building neither of us is in, and I am not going to answer, and neither are you.**"

Nobody thanked her. Nobody thanked the crew lead. The two of them argued about the bench for about four minutes and the older of them lost and said so and was not thanked either.

*306. Thursday of week 109, at about half past five at a yard behind four bays, and this entry is one instruction, four crews, nine people on a bench, and a piece of paper that did not get written.*

*Conditions and docket.* **About eleven crews; about four did not send anybody out and there was no vote and no vote was taken and about four of the eleven are crews whose people have stayed with them more than about four years, which is no document's business. Two crews attended one building in the Lower Wards, job numbers issued and reported. Nothing is broken at that building and that is the finding. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and no days-off figure is carried. The room is two hundred and ninety days old. A card in a rail is two hundred and ninety-four. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and ten, thirteen one hundred and sixty-one, fourteen one hundred and twenty-six, fifteen one hundred and five, sixteen eighty, seventeen sixty-two, eighteen eight. Wall unchanged, eighteen lines in twelve hands and thirteen names. A four-line record of a lift exists on the back of a docket-sized sheet in a bag and in the back of a book and on no docket, and two competent people have declined it, one of them in about four seconds and one of them after reading it standing up.**

*The instruction, and both halves of it, in the order they were said.* **Speaker: a lead repairer of forty-five, standing at a middle bench, about six thirty, to about nine people. First half, verbatim in substance: the standing instruction is wrong for this week. Second half, four minutes later: it is correct and it is not being changed. The instruction itself, as she states it: if a release does not come, you hold, and you telephone, and you hold until a person answers, and if no person answers you hold until one arrives on site, and the person who arrives owns the release, and you do not finish somebody else's practice for them. Her stated reason for not changing it: that it is correct in every week of the year and she has taught it to about nine crews a week for about nine years, and that she is not stopping because of a bad week. Her stated cost, in about nine words: that a person standing in a stairwell at eleven o'clock at night deciding whether he is allowed to finish is the whole cost, and that she would rather be wrong about a person than right about a pump. Objected to by about four people, in four objections, none of which was met with an answer and none of which was wrong. Kept: unchanged, in writing as well as in practice, which is the thing she is good at.**

*The four crews, and what they were actually.* **Not a mutiny: no meeting, no vote, no list, no petition, nobody instructed anybody, and no crew was told by anybody in management. Composition: the four are crews whose people have stayed with them more than about four years, and no document in this city records that fact and none was created. Cost to the person who gave them permission: she issued job numbers and required the second half of the report, which is what they did not find, on the stated ground that a crew asked afterwards for it will invent it and she will not be able to tell. Cost to the four crews: none recorded and none claimed and about four of them will be told in about four days by somebody in a building none of them is in that they went anyway. Refused by the same person, in four words, in the same four minutes: a piece of paper about it. Reason given: that in about four days this room will want one, that the paper will be about ninety, and that the nine are the only ones anybody did anything for. That reason was given twice, at about five and again on the yard, and the second time it was improved on by the person it was about and the improvement was worse and she was thanked for it and was not thanked.**

*Nine on a bench.* **Building: four tenants in the Lower Wards — a workshop, a kitchen, a stairwell with a bench in it, and a fourth not described. Time observed: from about half past eleven. Count: nine. Composition: one of about sixty-two who had walked to the wrong district, one who had not slept, one with a headache, one crying outside a shop, and five undescribed. Faults found: none. Lift: works. Plant: runs. Meters: where meters are. The finding of the two crews, given on the yard in about nine seconds by a crew lead of about thirty-four: she went in to find a fault because that is the job, and there is no fault, and she found nine people on a bench instead. Nobody in this case has been told anything by any of the nine, and the nine are not a case and are not to be converted into one. What the two crews brought back was a count and a shape and nothing else, and they were right to bring it and it was not enough.**

*The lift, offered twice, refused twice, in about four seconds each.* **Offered to a woman of about thirty-four at about eleven: that it stopped between five and six four times, that there is no door on that floor in that shaft, and that nobody knows. Answer: that is a lift, and somebody at that building has a book of what that lift does, and the next time it does that somebody will read that he wrote it, and the piece of paper in a bag is worth nothing, and he was right about the docket and wrong about the rest of it. Cost of that answer: none stated by her and not a small one, because it is the first time in about four years that anybody has told this case that the correct procedure and the useful one are not the same object, and it is going to be in three more chapters in a harder form. Offered to the lead repairer of forty-five at about half past five, on four written lines, standing up: answer, in about four seconds, that a lift is not a practice, that she has no place to put it, that she would be able to say nothing about it, and that the nothing would be on her. Followed by an offer of help to the two crews, refused in four words and then reinforced in about four: do not go. He did not go. Where the four lines are: the back of his own book, and not a docket, and not this bay's records, and not any other surface.**

*What the day did not touch.* **No practice was taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted by anybody, and a bay behind a workshop is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception. The standing instruction stands, in practice and in writing, and will stand for four more days. The printed rule was not mentioned. The four hundred competent people who can invoke it are not a number in this entry and are not on a list. No panel, no marker, no relay, no share offered, made or withdrawn. No message arrived from anybody. The Exchange did not sit. The secondment is unchanged. The heating is on, costs more, and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid and unresolved and four crews went out into a bad week without any of that being mentioned to any of them. The hardboard was not written on. The standing question is not stated here. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 306.**
