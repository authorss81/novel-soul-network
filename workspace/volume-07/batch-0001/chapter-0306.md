# Chapter 306 — It Is Not A Fault

The Wednesday of week one hundred and ten was the day a ventilation plant in a second district refused to restart and a maintenance record said it had been done in the spring, and both of those were true, and a man of fifty-one with a tool bag took the plant apart and put it back together and it ran for about nine hours, and a woman of thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors was in that plant room with him, and about four hundred was said out loud in that room by two people as two different numbers and neither of them knew the other one existed.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Five jobs attended and one address in a second district where a plant will not restart and a record says it was done.

The word was at nine and ten and it was nine and ten in about nine rooms this morning and nobody said so. The room off the service road is two hundred and ninety-six days old and is warm.

---

The plant is on the roof of a building of about eleven flats in a second district, which is not one of the four the case has been in and is not in the city this case has a name for, and it is an extract plant for a stairwell and a landing and it is not a civic thing and nobody has ever called it one.

It had been running since about ten past six and it stopped at about half past six and it would not start, and the man who rang the building's own maintenance number at about seven got a recorded notice that said the service was operating normally, and the man who rang it again at about eight got the same recording, and about four other people in that building rang it between seven and nine and every one of them got the recording.

The record on the wall in the plant room is a card in a plastic sleeve with a spring in it and a month on it and four ticks on it, and it says the work was done, and the month is four months ago, and the person who did it wrote his own initials on it and no surname.

---

A woman of thirty-four came in first, at about ten, and she had a company lanyard on and a bag of about nine hundred door numbers in it in a printout, and she is not a mechanic and holds no post and has been going to those doors on her own time for about four years because nobody asked her to.

A man of fifty-one came in at about half past ten with a tool bag, and he had been on the road since about six.

They looked at the card first. Both of them. It took about a minute and neither of them said anything while they did it.

She said it was his handwriting, and then that he had done it, and she said both in about four seconds each and neither of them said it as a discovery.

"**He did it and it is not a lie and I would like that on whatever you write.**"

---

The part is about the size of a fist and it is a switch that has been replaced with one from a different manufacturer, and the different manufacturer is on about nine hundred doors in four buildings as a plate number and is on a company's list on a different line from the one that applies, and about nine hundred of those plates carry a number that is wrong for the door it is on and a room in this city has never had its own number on the outside of it and a company field service engineer can say how many of his own company's plates are on the wrong line and cannot change any of them.

The man of fifty-one had the part in his bag because he has had one since about the spring.

He put it in at about eleven and the plant ran at about ten past twelve.

---

They came down at about half past three and sat in a van.

"**It will go again.**"

"**It will go again in about nine hours or it will not, and if it does not then it is a motor and I will come back on Tuesday.**"

She asked whether it was a fault. He said it was a switch.

"**Then the building was not in a fault for four months.**"

"**No. And I want to say the next part properly because I have been trying to say it to about four people in about two years and I have never had anybody write it down. The building was in a fault for four months and the fault was doing nothing at all, and it began to matter on a Tuesday, and the thing that changed was not the building.**"

Nobody wrote that down. The woman of thirty-four looked at the printout on her knees.

"**I have about nine hundred doors on that sheet and about four hundred of them are in buildings like this one and every one of those four hundred has a plate on it that came off the wrong line of somebody's list. Four years. And I have never once thought that was connected to anything, because it is a number, and a number is not a thing that does anything.**"

"**It is not connected to anything.**"

"**I know. I said it was not.**"

---

Then he said the other one, and he said it to a printout and not to a person, and the woman of thirty-four wrote it down afterwards and did not tell him she had written it down.

He said that the cook whose four words they are has been on about four hundred lines in about nine buildings in about four years, in a different hand every time, on about two hundred and forty of them on one sheet the size of a piece of kitchen paper, and that the sheet is in a room off a service road and that he has read it and that nobody else in this city has read it including the woman whose four words they are, and Talia Venn was told about nine accounts on the Monday and has not asked him for them once since, which is a thing he has noticed and has not mentioned to anybody including her, and that this case has been carrying the number about four hundred for a term and a half and has not once put it next to a number of buildings.

"**Four hundred lines.**"

"**Four hundred lines.**"

She said that was not the same four hundred as nine hundred doors, and he said no, and then:

"**Then it is not the same four hundred as anything and there is a third one somewhere and I have not met it.**"

He looked at her and said, in about four seconds, that this was the first time in about four years anybody had said anything to him about that number at all, and that she was not going to be thanked for it, and that he was going to have to think about it and that he was not going to do that this week.

---

At about four she asked him the question that made it a Wednesday.

"**Is it going to be four hundred lines everywhere.**"

"**I have no idea. I know a man in a yard behind a workshop who was asked the same question on Thursday about nine people on a bench, and she said do not write it down, and she was right, and I have not written it down.**"

He said that so nobody knew.

"**So about four of us know four different halves of it and there is nobody whose job it is to hold the halves.**"

She said, in about nine seconds, that she had been going to about four hundred doors on her own time for about four years and that about nine people in this city had told her that was not her job, and that on Wednesday it had stopped being not her job, and that she was the only person she knew who could stand in a plant room and answer that question and she had answered it by not knowing.

"**You will not be thanked for that.**"

"**I have never been thanked for that and I have four years of evidence and I did not say it for that.**"

She said it as a joke and about four people who were not there would have improved on it.

*309. Wednesday of week 110, at about four in the afternoon in a van in a second district, and this entry is one switch, one card in a plastic sleeve with a spring in it, and one number said twice in two senses by two people who did not know there was a third sense.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Five jobs attended, one of them a plant in a second district this case has not had before and which is not a civic asset and has never been called one. Two job numbers for two people, one of them a company employee on her own time with a lanyard and a printout of about nine hundred door numbers. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and no days-off figure is carried. The room is two hundred and ninety-six days old. A card in a rail is three hundred. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and sixteen, thirteen one hundred and sixty-seven, fourteen one hundred and thirty-two, fifteen one hundred and eleven, sixteen eighty-six, seventeen sixty-eight, eighteen fourteen. Wall unchanged. The plant ran for about nine hours and will run again or it will not and if it does not then it is a motor and the man of fifty-one will come back on a Tuesday. He wrote it as: it will go again. None of that is a repair and all of it is a correct action performed by a competent person and it changes nothing about the weather.**

*The plant, the card, and the part.* **Object: an extract plant serving a stairwell and a landing, roof-mounted, about eleven flats, in a second district. Behaviour: ran from about ten past six, stopped at about half past six, would not restart on nine presses between about seven and about half past nine, and the building's own maintenance line returned a recorded notice on at least four separate calls from at least four separate people that the service was operating normally. Record: a card in a plastic sleeve with a spring in it, a month four months old, four ticks, and one set of initials and no surname. Both people in that room read the record first, for about a minute, in silence, and both concluded the work had been done, and one of them asked for that to be written down and it is written down. Part: a switch about the size of a fist, from a different manufacturer, a part that man of fifty-one had in his bag since about the spring. What the wrong number is: about nine hundred plates in four buildings carry a number off a different line of the same company's list, and a room in this city has never had its own number on the outside of it. Correct action: the part was replaced at about eleven and the plant ran at about ten past twelve. Consequence: nine hours, and a promise of a Tuesday. **That action is correct, is owed nothing, was performed by two people who are not villains, and it reaches nothing.** The building was in a fault for four months and the fault was doing nothing at all.**

*Two four hundreds, in one van, in about four minutes.* **First: a man of fifty-one with a tool bag, to a printout and not to a person, in about nine seconds. That number, said before, is the number of lines in this city carrying one cook's four words, in a different hand every time, over about four years, in about nine buildings, and about two hundred and forty of them are on one sheet the size of a piece of kitchen paper in a room off a service road. He has read that sheet. Nobody else in this case has read it. The cook has not been told it exists. This case has carried the number since an earlier volume and has never put it next to a number of buildings. Second: a woman of thirty-four, that about nine hundred doors are on her printout and about four hundred of them are in buildings with a plate off the wrong line of somebody's list, and that in four years she has never once thought it connected to anything, because a number is not a thing that does anything. Recognition, in about four seconds, by the man of fifty-one, that two things she and he both call four hundred are not the same four hundred. Her next three sentences: that there is a third one somewhere, and that she has not met it. His answer: that this is the first time in about four years anybody has said anything to him about that number at all, that she is not going to be thanked for it, that he is going to have to think about it, and that he is not going to do that this week. **Two referents of three are now separated on the page by two people who do not know that a third exists. The third is the number of competent people in this city who can invoke a printed rule, and neither of these two people has been told about it and neither of them is in a room where it has been said. The thread is not closed here and is not to be closed inside a fortnight.**

*The man's own sentence, which nobody wrote down.* **Said at about four in a van in a second district, to nobody, and it is the reason this entry exists: the building was in a fault for four months and the fault was doing nothing at all, and it began to matter on a Tuesday, and the thing that changed was not the building. Not written by him. Not written by the woman. Not written by anybody in this file. Carried into a later entry and to be attributed to him when he is next on the page, and he has not agreed to that and has not been asked.**

*What the day did not touch.* **He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing, and a plant room is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception. Nothing was run and nothing was forwarded and no relay was staged. The printed rule was not mentioned by anybody in that van. The Exchange did not sit and its next sitting is a Wednesday a fortnight from today. The secondment is unchanged and the chair has still not been told. The heating is on, costs more, and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is unresolved, and a man of fifty-one has been driving since six in the morning and did not eat and nobody in that van noticed and one person in this case will. The hardboard was not written on and no line has been added to it in about two weeks. The woman's page in the ring binder is unread. The standing question is not stated here. The question written on a service road five days ago is not answered here and is not in anybody's mouth. No message arrived from anybody. Nobody thanked anybody, and the one sentence in this entry that anybody would have thanked him for is the one he did not say twice. Entry 309.**
