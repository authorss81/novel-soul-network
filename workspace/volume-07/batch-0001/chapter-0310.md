# Chapter 310 — The Nineteenth

The Thursday evening of week one hundred and eleven was the evening a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts wrote the nineteenth line on a hardboard on two nails in a room off a service road, after naming the cost out loud to about nine people, which is that the only man in this city who has said in public that he will not take the middle is now findable as that man; and about half an hour later a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip asked a question that had no technician's answer and a man of twenty-two said yes, and nobody authorised it, and it is the first thing he has ever governed.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Three jobs attended, one of them a stair fire door in Nine Bridges for the eleventh time, and the eleventh was the same as the ten before it.

The word was at nine and ten and it reached eleven for about two hours in the early afternoon and on no hour since. The room is three hundred and four days old and is warm and there were about eleven people in it by half past six, which is more than there have been on a Thursday.

---

He came in at about half past six with a coat on and had been in the room about four times in about four years and had never once written on anything in it.

He said the cost first, out loud, to the room, in about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody stopped him and nobody asked him whether he was sure, which is a question this case has asked a man of thirty-four about once and got a different answer.

"**I have said the true thing about this twice in about four years and nobody has improved on either of them and nobody has thanked me for either. There is one line on that wall about being written down, the newest but one, and I have read it about four times, and it says that in about four years about four people are going to be able to find a person because of a piece of paper. I am about to do that to myself on purpose, in a week when everybody in this city is looking for somebody to be in the middle of it, and in about four years somebody in a building I have never been inside is going to be able to find one man who has said out loud that he will not do it, and I am telling you that now so that it is my choice and not a surprise.**"

"**Why.**"

That was about four people at once.

"**Because I was the fourth row and I was right there for four years and nobody came and got me, and if I say nothing then in about four years there is going to be a week in this city where four things are going wrong and the four people who can do them are four different people, and the only people who know all four names will be in this room, and on the Tuesday nothing happens. That is mine. I said it. And I would like it to be on a wall and not in a head.**"

---

He wrote it in about nine seconds with a pen off the counter and signed it.

*I was fourth. I am not going to be the middle.*

Eleven words. There is no space left where a name would go, because he wrote his name.

The wall is nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names. No line has ever been removed and none has been rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line. The newest is eleven words long and it is a promise, and it is the first line on that wall that is a promise, and every other one of the eighteen is a record of something that had already happened.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody said it was welcome. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag read the whole board standing up afterward, which he does, and said nothing.

---

The cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and had been since about half past five, and she came out of the sink area at about half past seven and stood at the end of the counter with a cloth.

"**A woman wrote the line above this one and walked nine miles to be in this room, and she put her name on it, and it took her nine seconds, and it took you nine seconds, and neither of you has been thanked, and they are just over three weeks apart and about four people in this room have noticed that and not one of us has said so.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Well. I have said so.**"

---

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and had read all nineteen lines and had not said anything for about half an hour, and at about a quarter to eight she looked at the board and then at the man who had written the line, and she said nothing, and the reason is not recorded in her mouth and has not been given to anybody and is not to be asked for.

She is nineteen. She is the only nineteen-year-old in this case. She removed a man of twenty-two from the only thing he owns, in nine words, in a room, and she has not improved on those nine words since and has not been asked for a better set.

---

The man of fifty-eight with a bad hip was in the corner and had been since about half past five and had not had a cup of tea, as he does not.

He asked at about a quarter to eight and it took about nine seconds and there was no preamble to it and nobody had been talking.

"**You are the only person in this city I can ask.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Not the only. I have got a hardboard on a wall with a woman's name on it, the line above the one he has just written, and I have a district office four miles away with a woman of sixty in it who has never once lied to me. And you are the only one of the three who can be in a stairwell at eleven o'clock at night and make a decision about whether a stranger is allowed to finish what they started. And I have got about four buildings in a district I have never worked in where somebody's release is not coming and nobody local knows it. And I have got a man of fifty-one with a tool bag who will not take my telephone calls, and a woman of thirty-four who told three people in a car park on Wednesday that she will not be a list of this, and a cook who will not be on a telephone.**"

He stopped, and about nine people in that room waited, and he went on, and it is the second and last thing he has said in about four years.

"**Are you going to be the one who is asked.**"

---

He did not stand up. He had a docket in his other hand.

"**Yes.**"

He said it in about four seconds and nobody repeated it and nobody improved on it and nobody said *you did not have to* and nobody thanked him for it, and about four people in that room wanted very much to say something and did not, and one of them has wanted to for about four years, and she is in another room in another part of the city at about nine o'clock on a Thursday doing work that gets paid for, and she is Talia Venn and nobody in this one knew and neither of them telephoned the other.

"**Right.**" The man of fifty-eight with a bad hip put his hands on the arms of the chair. "**Then I have told a stranger where to find a man and I have never done that in about four years and I am not going to be thanked for it either, and that will be all right.**"

He had not been thanked. He has never been thanked. He has said two things in about four years, and he has refused one, and the refusal is older than either of the two and nobody has asked him about it again.

---

There is no form and no list and no minute and no notice of a notice. There is no permission and no appointment and no authority and no stamp, and nobody in that room can point to the thing that made him say yes, and in about three weeks a Continuity Office will write down a name on a piece of paper in a building he has never been inside, and the piece of paper will not be wrong, and the reason he said yes on a Thursday evening to a question from a man with a bad hip is that a question with no technician's answer got asked in a room with about nine people in it and he was the only one who could answer it.

The shutter came down at about ten. The room is warmer than it was three weeks ago and there is a sheet of paper with a district office's mark on it face up on the counter, and about four people in this city came into that room this week for the temperature, and the cheapest thing any of them has used is another person's money spent on gas, and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door from about two until ten and was not asked anything.

*313. Thursday of week 111, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one promise, one question, and one yes.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Three jobs attended, one a stair fire door in Nine Bridges at its eleventh occasion, same fault number, same description, no fault findable. About eleven people in the room by half past six, which is more than on any previous Thursday. The rate reached eleven for about two hours in the early afternoon and on no hour since. The heating is on and no days-off figure is carried. The room is three hundred and four days old. A card in a rail is three hundred and eight. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and twenty-four, thirteen one hundred and seventy-five, fourteen one hundred and forty, fifteen one hundred and nineteen, sixteen ninety-four, seventeen seventy-six, eighteen twenty-two, nineteen zero. Wall before this evening: eighteen lines in twelve hands and thirteen names. Wall after: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names. Lines removed: none. Lines rubbed off: none. First line: a cook's. Newest: eleven words, about nine seconds, a pen off the counter, signed, with no space left where a name would go. A sheet with a district office's mark on it is still face up on the counter and has been there since Friday.**

*The line, and the cost before the mark.* **Writer: a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, on a coat, in about four seconds of speech and nine seconds of writing. Cost, named aloud before the mark, in about nine seconds, to the room and not to a person, in four items: he has said the true thing twice in about four years and nobody improved on either and nobody thanked him; he has read the eighteenth line about four times; in about four years about four people will be able to find a person because of a piece of paper; and the person they will find is the only man in this city who has said in public that he will not take the middle, in a week when everybody is looking for one. Consequence he accepted, in his own words: that it will be his choice and not a surprise. Why he did it, asked by about four people at once, in about nine seconds: that he was in the fourth row for four years and nobody came and got him; and that in about four years there will be a week when four things are going wrong and the four people who can do them are four different people and the only people who know all four names will be in that room; and that he would like it on a wall and not in a head. **The cost is not the signing. The cost is that a refuser is findable, which is a thing this case has spent six volumes arguing against and has not once looked at from this side.** Text: eleven words. Signature: his own name, written out, no space left. Wall after: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, fourteen names. Nobody thanked him and nobody said it was welcome and nobody asked him whether he was sure.**

*What the cook said, unasked, at about half past seven.* **A cook of twenty-seven, from the sink area, with a cloth, standing at the end of the counter, in about nine seconds: that a woman of sixty-one wrote the line immediately above the one about to be written, and walked about nine miles to be in that room, and signed it; that both lines are signed and both were written in about nine seconds; that neither has been thanked; that the interval between them is just over three weeks; and that about four people in that room have noticed the interval and not one of them had said so. Then: well, I have said so. Nobody said anything in reply. Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. **This is the second occasion in just over three weeks on which a person has named two things in a room out loud that nobody else was going to name. The first was a woman of sixty-one who walked about nine miles to be in a room where nobody knew her. The two women are not the same and have not met, both of them are owed nothing, and neither of them has been told anything about the other.**

*The silence, and the person who kept it.* **Reader: one, aged nineteen, in that room from about half past six. She read the whole board. She said nothing for about half an hour, and then said nothing again, and looked at the man who had written the line without speaking to him. **Her name is in nobody's mouth in this movement and is on no surface in it. The reason for the silence is not recorded in her mouth, has not been given to anybody, and is not to be asked for.** The nine words she said some months ago, to a man in a room, about a page on a shelf — *nobody should have to find you to stop me* — have not been said since and were not said tonight, and she did not improve on them and nobody asked her for a better set.**

*The question, and the yes.* **Asked by: a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip, from the corner, having been in the room since about half past five, without a cup of tea, at about a quarter to eight, in about nine seconds, with no preamble, to nobody. Preamble, in substance: three people in this city can be asked and it is not one of them. A hardboard on a wall, carrying a woman's name one line below the newest. A district office four miles from that room, with a woman of sixty in it who has never once lied to him. And the man of twenty-two, who is the only one of the three who can stand in a stairwell at eleven at night and decide whether a stranger is permitted to finish what they started. Four facts carried with the question: about four buildings in a district he has never worked in where a release is not coming and nobody local knows it; a man of fifty-one with a tool bag who will not take his telephone calls; a woman of thirty-four who refused on Wednesday, in a car park, to be an index of anything; a cook who will not be on a telephone. The question, in full: are you going to be the one who is asked. Answer, in about four seconds, seated, with a docket in the other hand: yes. Cost named by the asker, unprompted, in about four seconds: that he has told a stranger where to find a man, which he has never done in about four years, and that he is not going to be thanked for it either, and that that will be all right. Nobody repeated the answer. Nobody improved on it. Nobody said he did not have to. Nobody thanked him. About four people in that room wanted to say something and did not. **He had said one other thing in about four years, on a Friday, about a sheet of paper that nobody answered him on, and this was the second and the last, and the third of the five things he has been asked about is one he has refused and is not going to be asked again. There is no form, no list, no minute, no notice of a notice, no permission, no appointment, no authority and no stamp, and nobody in that room can point at the thing that made him say yes.**

*What the day did not touch.* **He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing and is the subject of nothing in this entry. No panel, no marker, no relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded. The printed rule was not mentioned by anybody and about four hundred competent people in this city can still invoke it, and one of them is asleep. The Exchange did not sit and its next sitting is a Wednesday six days from today. The secondment is unchanged and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. The heating is on, costs more, and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is unresolved, and about four people came into that room this week for the temperature and one of them is a woman of about thirty who was not asked anything. The master's fifth line is blank and the drawer was not opened. The pencil box in Bower Street is untouched. The plate in a service cupboard is not explained, not removed and not blamed on anybody. The woman's page in the ring binder is unread. The standing question is not stated in this entry and carries no number. The question written on a service road in week one hundred and eight is not answered here, is not in anybody's mouth, and nobody in that room referred to it. No message arrived from anybody. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 313.**
