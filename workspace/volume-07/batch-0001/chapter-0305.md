# Chapter 305 — People In The Middle Of A Morning

The Monday morning of week one hundred and ten put about nine people on a pavement in the Lower Wards in the middle of a working day with nothing to do and no way of getting home, and a courier of twenty-one moved them, and the moving was not his job and nobody had asked him to do it and he did it in about four hours; and a woman of about eighty-four on the fourth floor of a block in Nine Bridges would not come down four flights on a stranger's back, and a man of twenty-two was telephoned about her at about eleven, and the telephone was a letting agent's number on a plate with no name on it.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Four jobs attended, one of them a stair fire door in Nine Bridges for the tenth time, and the tenth was adjusted and went back the way it had come and nobody chased it.

The word was at nine and ten. Four names on a lease cost more than they did. The room off the service road is two hundred and ninety-four days old and is warm.

---

The depot is four bays and a yard and about nine carts and a smell of wet cardboard, and the first thing that happens in that yard every morning before anybody has said anything is that somebody starts counting, and this is not on a form and is not on a docket and nobody has ever written down who does it.

About nine people were in the shelter at about half past ten. They were not lost. That is the thing to be said first and it is the thing nobody got right for about four days. They knew where they were. They had left work early because their shifts ended early, and the shift ending early left them standing in a place where they had been since six in the morning and where they were due back at four, with eleven hours in front of them and nothing in them.

Nine people. A man of about thirty-four in overalls who had a shift and a bus pass and no bus. Two women of about forty who worked a till. A man of about nineteen who worked a kitchen and had come out in his working clothes and had not thought about a coat. A woman of about fifty-eight who had walked. A man of about twenty-seven. A woman of about thirty-one and a woman of about thirty-one, sisters, who had both stopped at the same moment and had compared notes and had not been able to explain it.

---

Rafi Pell is twenty-one and broad-shouldered and does not trust a confident explanation and carries two tools he does not own. He was in that yard at about half past ten because he collects from four kitchens on that run and one of the four kitchens had not opened, which is not the same as being shut.

He did the only thing anybody in that yard could think of, which was to start moving them, and it is worth being exact about what moving them was: it was four vans going in four directions to four addresses, and it was about four telephone calls made from a mobile phone to four houses where somebody was at home, and it was eleven hot drinks out of a machine that works.

He is not a social worker and has not been trained as one and the second of those is not a thing anybody in this case has said about him.

---

The fourth-floor woman came at about eleven and she was the only one of the nine who could not be moved, and the reason she could not be moved was not her age.

Her lift had been stopping between floors since about half past eight and had come back each time she pressed the button, and she had been in it that morning and had got out at five and had decided, on a stair, at the age of about eighty-four, that she was finished with it.

Rafi went up. Three flights, because the lift was being used by two other people at that moment and he could hear it.

He got to the fourth-floor landing at about a quarter past eleven and knocked and a woman of about thirty-one answered and said the old woman was not coming down on anybody's back and had said so twice to a neighbour.

"**Tell her I am not carrying her. Tell her I am walking her down at her speed and she can sit on every landing and it will take about forty minutes and I will not do anything she does not do first.**"

"**She says no.**"

"**Then tell her I am going to sit on this landing until about one o'clock and after that I have to go and collect from four kitchens, and I am telling you that so that you can tell her, because I am not telling her anything she can use against me.**"

---

He sat on the fourth-floor landing of a block in Nine Bridges for about an hour and a half and came down with her at about twenty past twelve, at her speed, with a rest on every landing, and she was carrying her own bag and he was not carrying anything except a folded blanket he had taken off a chair in her flat without asking and returned before they left the ground floor.

Nobody in that building thanked him. The woman of about thirty-one at the top of that stair said one sentence to him at the door, which was that the old woman had said no to two men from a service in the week before last and had got down the stairs with them anyway, and that this time she had not had to be asked twice, and that he should not build anything out of it.

He said he would not.

---

He was telephoned at about eleven, and the number that was telephoned is on a plate outside a converted shop unit off a service road, and the plate carries a letting agent's number and no name, and the call went to a letting agent, and the letting agent put it through to a room because a letting agent who rents four units and has had about four hundred enquiries about one of them over about four years knows a voice.

He was on a job until about two. He got there at about half past two. He had had four numbers in his phone all morning and he had rung three of them about a building, a plant and a door, and the fourth was not a number at all and it was a woman of twenty-four four miles away and he had not rung it, and he has not said that out loud to anybody including her.

About nine people were on a pavement in the Lower Wards and had been all day and about six of them had gone home by then, and one of them was still there because his bus pass was in a coat in Nine Bridges.

"**You are the only person in this city I can ring.**"

Rafi said it standing up, in about four seconds, with his bag still on.

"**I have known that for about four years and it is not a compliment.**"

"**I know.**"

"**It is not a compliment because the day there is one of us is a day somebody is going to use it, and I am not asking you not to be it, and I am also not going to be the one who thanks you for it, because the second one is how you end up being it.**"

He said that in about nine seconds and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked anybody and about six people got on a bus and one of them did not because his pass was in a coat.

---

They sat on a wall for about half an hour. He did not have a technique and neither did anybody else. What he had, and said out loud once, in one sentence, to a man of twenty-seven who was asking what he was supposed to do, was the following: find out what a person is doing this evening before you find out how they are getting home, and the second one does not matter nearly as much as it looks like it matters, and about four people have said that out loud in this city in about four years and it is on no form.

"**You have said that before.**"

"**I have said it twice.**"

"**To who.**"

"**To a woman of thirty-four who checks doors and to a man of fifty-one with a tool bag and neither of them improved on it and one of them is not on a rota.**"

He said it and then he said the other thing, which he said because he had been on the wall for half an hour and had nothing else to do with it.

"**I am not the man for this and I am the man for it, and I have known both of those since about half past ten this morning, and I am going to keep doing it, and if that makes me a post then I am a post and I will tell you so and you can write it down and I will not sign anything.**"

Nobody wrote anything down. A man of about twenty-seven said that his shift starts at four and he had missed it, and that that was fine, and that his foreman was going to be fine about it as well, and that he had decided that in about four seconds on a wall.
