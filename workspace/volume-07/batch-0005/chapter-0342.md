# Chapter 342 — Nine People And No Order

The Wednesday afternoon of week one hundred and twenty-two was the second time in nineteen days that the light went out of four buildings in this city, and **the question that everybody in this case has about it is not how to prevent it and is how a thing that was done by hand once is done by hand twice without anybody copying the first time**, and the nine people who have been asked about it this evening gave nine answers and not one of the nine was a first step, and about four of the nine in that room came in not knowing that the other five had been asked.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Ten jobs attended and one not attended, in a building in the second of the four districts, and it is chased.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is three hundred and eighty days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Monday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The light went at about four in the afternoon and it went the way it went nineteen days ago, which is from the top down, floor by floor, on a timer that is old and honest, about nine seconds apart, and about nine people in four buildings watched it happen from their own landings and did not ring anybody.

There is no stairwell lit in any of the four this time. The count of four hours was on a piece of paper in a building in Crown Terrace and it expired nineteen days ago and nothing has replaced it, and no office has written anything since, and a stairwell that somebody had put a light in for four hours and had taken a crew out of one building to do it was not lit on the Wednesday afternoon, and about nine people in four buildings found that out by looking.

**Nothing about that is anybody's doing and no name is attached to any part of it and nobody in this case can catch the weather.**

---

Four hours. From about four to about eight, and the season has turned far enough that four o'clock is now dark, and the buildings were not dark for four hours by accident. They were dark for the length of a working afternoon and most of an early evening.

About nine people in the second group have been asked since how they heard about it and about four of the nine gave the same answer, which is that somebody in a stairwell had said it, and about four of those four could not remember which somebody, and one of them named a man who has lived in that building for about nine years and does not know that he has been the answer to a question in about two buildings.

The man has not been told and is not going to be. He was in a stairwell on a Friday night in the dark and said one sentence to somebody on the way past and has not thought about it since.

The chapter does not know most of their names and has not asked. About nine people in two of the four buildings did something and about nine in the other two did something else, and the two groups have not met and are not going to, and about four of the people in the second group had been in the same stairwell as somebody in the first group on the Friday night nineteen days ago and had said one sentence to them, in the dark, on a landing, in passing, and have not said anything about it since.

That is the entire mechanism and there is no other one.

---

A room off a service road was warm from about half past six and about nine people were in it, and the man of about thirty-three had asked them to come, one at a time, over three days, and had not told any of them what the others had said, and had not written anything down at any point, in this room or anywhere else.

He said why, at the start, in about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it.

"**I asked nine people to tell me what they did this afternoon and I have not told any of them what the other eight said, and if you have all come here expecting to agree on something then you are in the wrong room, because nine people did nine things and I am not going to turn it into a way of doing it, because I do not know how to turn it into a way of doing it and neither does anybody in this room.**"

Then he asked them, one at a time, in the order they came, and did not interrupt, and did not go back over anybody.

---

A woman of about sixty-eight on the fourth floor of one of the four said that she went out on her landing at about half past four and asked four people what they were doing, and then did what the third of them was doing.

A man of about thirty-eight said that he put a torch in the pocket of a coat belonging to somebody who was going to be out and did not tell him, and that the man found it on the Tuesday and has not mentioned it, and that the man of thirty-eight is not going to bring it up.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors, who was in that building nineteen days ago and read about nine numbers in the dark with a light held low, said this:

"**I did the same nine numbers because I had about four hours of nothing to do and they are the only nine I have. The wrong one is still wrong. I did not learn anything doing it the second time, and I want that said out loud in here before somebody writes it down as a practice.**"

A man of about forty-one who keeps a gate in a different district said that he did the floor rota, which he does on paper on a Sunday, and that he did it in the dark, and that he could not read it, and that he did it from memory, and that it was wrong by two, and that the two were corrected on the Thursday by a person who did not know that a rota had been done in the dark.

A woman of about twenty-seven said that she sat down, and that that is the whole of what she did, and that she would like it recorded that she did nothing and is not going to be asked why.

A man of about fifty-two said that he knocked on nine doors, and that he does that anyway, and that on this occasion nine doors were opened.

A woman of about forty-four said that she had four hours of nothing and slept for two of them, and that she has not slept in a chair since the Tuesday before last, and that nobody rang her to ask whether she was all right, and that she has not mentioned it and is not going to.

A man of about thirty-one said that he asked a man of fifty-one for nothing at all and that the man of fifty-one came into his building and stood on his landing for about two hours, and that he wanted to be asked why and was not going to be told.

A man of about twenty-two with a badge on a lanyard who works for a company in another part of the city and is not in this case and is not going to be, said that he did nothing that helped anybody, and that he would like that said first and not last, because about eight other people in that room are going to describe a thing they did and he is going to describe the absence of one, and both of those are Wednesday afternoons.

**Nobody improved on any of the nine. Nobody thanked any of the nine. Nobody asked any of the nine a second question, and the man of about thirty-three did not ask the man of about twenty-two with the badge anything at all.**

---

The man of fifty-one with a tool bag came in at about seven and put the bag against the wall and was asked nothing, and asked nothing, and then said one thing, and it took about nine seconds, and it is about what he cannot do.

"**You have all just been asked what to do and about four of you have asked me that without noticing that you asked it. I can tell you what I did and I cannot tell you how, and those are two different questions and I have only got an answer to the first one. I have been in about four hundred buildings in this city in the dark and I have not once done it the same way twice and I was not faster this afternoon than I was on the Friday and I was not slower. If you want it written down I cannot do that. If you want somebody to show it to somebody else you have the wrong man. And if you want it to be the same next time it will not be the same next time, and that is not modesty, that is what it is.**"

He was not asked a second question. He has not been asked a second question about it and is not going to be.

---

**A way of doing a thing exists. It is not a technique, it is not on a sheet, it is not in anybody's head as a step, and nobody in that room is going to give it a name tonight.** It got out of one stairwell and into about four people on one Wednesday afternoon, and about five of the nine in that room had no route into it at all and were told by somebody who did, and the telling took about four seconds and happened at a counter and a door and a landing and not in a room, and nobody called it anything.

Nobody in that room knows who will do what on the next dark afternoon. Nobody has written it down. Nobody is going to, and the man of about thirty-three said so in about four seconds at about ten past seven and nobody improved on it.

"**I am going to go on asking people one question and it is not what to do, it is whether they will do it again. I am not going to ask them twice. I have not asked anybody yet and I am not going to on a Wednesday.**"

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
