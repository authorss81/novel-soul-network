# Chapter 347 — The First Knot

The Friday night of week one hundred and twenty-three was the tenth Friday this volume has put in a company training room in Crown Terrace, and it was also the night a man of twenty-two cut the oldest thing he has, with the agreement of the man on the other end of it, and then walked about nine people through the Lower Wards on his own. **He was not one of the nine, he was not in it, and no part of that night was easier for him than it was for anybody else in it, and about four of the nine people he walked were better at it than he was and one of them said so.**

---

The room off a service road is three hundred and eighty-nine days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Thursday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

Nine folding chairs and about eleven people in a company training room in Crown Terrace, from about seven until about nine, and seven of the eleven are on the company's list and four are on no list at all.

The run went at about half past seven.

**She began it.** The woman of thirty-six who calls it stood up and it took her about nine seconds and she said the wrong thing first and then the right thing, and about nine people did the floor in about nine minutes, and about four seconds came off the end of it that nobody in that room counted and nobody has counted since.

Nobody said why. Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody has said one word about it to anybody outside that room, and the man of thirty-four who began the last one was not in this one and has not been asked about either.

---

At about nine the nine doors opened.

Nine people, nine places, four hours, and each of them able to stop, and nobody knew who was where and nobody was going to find out. **What holds that night together is not a person and is not a technique. It is nine local practices that were already good, nine people who had each said yes at a door, and one old arrangement that a man of twenty-two cut at about eleven o'clock with the agreement of the man on the other end of it.**

---

The first one closed at about ten.

A man of about forty-one at a door in a building in the second of the four districts said no, and he had said on the Monday that he would, and nobody had asked him to and nobody knew he had. He had a person behind that door who could not be moved and a person behind that door who was seventy-eight and had been asleep since about nine.

He did not explain it to anybody, because the promise was that nobody would be asked, and he kept the promise and he did not explain it.

He gave the door to the woman of thirty-one with a key in her pocket, who was at a door in the same building and who is one of the nine, and she took it without being asked, and she did not ask him why either, and the two of them have not spoken about it since.

**Nobody in that building rang anybody. Nobody in this case knew for about nine minutes. The door was shut for about nine minutes and it was the first time a door had been shut that night and it did not go down the street.**

---

At about eleven he made the telephone call, and it is the only telephone call he made that night, and it lasted about four minutes, and the man on the other end of it agreed before he was asked.

"**You are going to ask me to stop.**"

"**I am going to ask you to stop.**"

"Then I have got about four minutes before I have to be somewhere else, so say the rest of it, and say it fast, and do not apologise, because I have been doing this since I was seventeen and I have never once asked you for anything and I am not going to start at one in the morning."

He said it. It took about nine seconds and it was that a man of twenty-one was the only person in this city who could get across the whole of it in under an hour, and that two of the nine doors are at the far end of it, and that the only way he had ever reached him in under an hour in about four years was an arrangement that neither of them had ever put into words and that he had never once asked him about.

"**Then I am going to do it myself, and it will take me about three hours, and it will be slow, and you are not to help me.**"

"**I am not going to help you. I want that said and there is nowhere to say it, and I am going to the far end because about nine people are not going to open a door for a man of twenty-two, and I have been on about four hundred runs for people who did not ask me since I was seventeen and this is the first one I have done for a man of twenty-two and I am going to enjoy it.**"

He said: "**There is nobody to thank.**"

The man of twenty-one said: "No. And do not tell anybody I said any of this, and I am not going to tell anybody, and neither of us is going to know what the other one did tonight."

**That is the oldest thing he has and he cut it with his own hands in about four minutes in the middle of a working night and the man on the other end of it agreed first, and neither of them has said one word about it since and neither of them is going to, and nobody took a vote about him and nobody asked him to stop.**

---

Then he went and got about nine people off a landing.

There were about nine of them on the fourth floor of a building in the second of the four districts and about four of them could not get down four storeys in the dark without somebody going ahead of them, and the bottom of that stair is the only way out of that building that works in the dark, and the one at the bottom has been locked since before anybody in this case was born.

They had to get to a canteen in a bus depot in the first of the four districts, which had its own power and had about nine people in it and was open, and it is about two miles on foot and there was no bus.

**He walked them. He is twenty-two and he is not a strong man and he had done eleven jobs that day and he had been awake for about twenty hours and he had not eaten since about seven in the morning and he has not said so to anybody and is not going to.**

He got the count wrong twice. He counted nine on the second floor and there were eight. He counted eight on the ground floor and there were nine. About four of those nine were better at it than he was and one of them, a woman of about thirty-four who was counting her own family, told him so at about half past midnight in four words.

"**You are the slowest thing in this group.**"

He said: "**I know.**"

Nobody has improved on that sentence and nobody has repeated it and she is not in this case again.

He sat down on a step at about eleven and could not get up the first time. A man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts, who was in that building on the earlier night and who came with them, got him up with a hand under his arm and said nothing about it and has said nothing about it since.

---

The second door closed at about half past eleven.

A woman of about fifty-eight at a door in a building in the third of the four districts said no, on purpose, having said on the Monday that she would, and she did not say why to anybody and nobody asked her, and the promise was kept in front of nine people who did not know it had been made.

She gave the door to a man of about twenty-seven who lives in that building and who has read and replaced the numbers on the doors there for about four years, and who was in that stairwell on the earlier night because it is where he lives, and who is not one of the nine and has not been asked to do anything by anybody in this case.

He took it and he could not read the number on the plate, because there was no light and there was never any light, and he said so to about four people in about four seconds and nobody improved on it.

"**I cannot read it and I do not need to read it. It is not the number that matters tonight, and I have known that for about four years and this is the first week it has been any use to anybody.**"

Nobody thanked him. He has not asked. About nine minutes.

**Two people put that night back together and they have not been in the same room, and neither of them is one of the nine, and neither of them was asked by anybody, and the two of them have not spoken to each other and are not going to.**

---

The load came down between about half past twelve and about one in the morning.

Nobody in this case knows the moment. A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip was told at about nine the following morning and wrote nothing down then or since. There was no fault in any of the six buildings and there was nobody to arrest and nobody to name and nothing to write a report about.

**Four hours and about nine minutes, and about nine people went through the Lower Wards on their own feet, and nine doors were open and two of them were shut for about nine minutes each on purpose, and nobody in this city can say afterwards that a person did any of it, and there is not one thing in this case that anybody can say that about.**

A cook of twenty-seven washed a kettle and went home at about two. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag went home at about one and was at his own door by about half past one and has not been asked about any of it.

Nobody thanked anybody.
