# Chapter 322 — The Second Piece Of Paper

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and fifteen was the morning four people with ordinary jobs each did the correct thing with a copy of a Continuity Office instrument in their own building, and every one of the four was right, and every one of the four discovered that doing the correct thing had produced a second piece of paper, and that the second piece of paper asked them a question they were not authorised to answer, and that the cost of it was named out loud in one of their own words and by nobody else.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Five jobs attended and one of them was in a building in the third of the four districts, on a stair, for about nine minutes, and nothing about that job is in this entry.

The word was at nine and ten and reached eleven for about half an hour in the middle of the afternoon and on no hour after that. The room off a service road is three hundred and thirty-one days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Tuesday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The instrument is two sides. It carries the mark of a Continuity Office at the top, a reference under the mark in a form only that building can read aloud, a date on the letter, a count, and a reason, and the reason is a count.

In the fourth line of the second side it says that the point of coordination for a resonance event is one person, and the person is named at the foot of that side.

**It is not a form and it is not a minute and it is not a notice and it is not a report and it is not a committee paper and it is not a leaflet and it is not a registration and it is not a constitution, and it is not a hardboard on two nails, and the difference between the last two is the whole of what a later part of this case is about.**

Four copies of it reached four buildings this case has never been inside, and nobody in this case put them there.

---

One. A woman of about forty-one, estates officer, first of the four districts, in her own building, at about ten past nine in the morning.

A document arrives in a building. The correct thing is to log it. That is her job and she has had it for about six years and she does it in about four minutes, standing at a counter, with a pen.

She wrote the reference in her own register, which is a book, which is not a ring binder and is not a hardboard. She wrote the date. She wrote the address it came from. She wrote the count, because the count is on it and a count goes in the register.

That is all correct. All four steps. Nobody in that building could have done it better and about four people have since told her it was the right thing to do.

It produced a second piece of paper.

---

The second piece of paper came back at about half past eleven, from the same building, on the same letterhead as the register's own, and it is about half the size, and it is a form, and it is the first form in this case that anybody has been sent rather than gone and got.

It says that the reference is noted. It says that the count is noted. And it says, in a single line across the bottom:

**Please confirm that the person named is aware of this appointment.**

That is the whole of it. There is no second question and no third and nothing to sign at the foot of it and no name of anybody who will read it.

---

She read it standing up. She did not sit down and she did not go and get anybody, and there were about four people in that room and about four of them watched her read it.

**She is authorised to write what she is authorised to write. The register is authorised. A confirmation that a named person is aware of an appointment is not a thing she is authorised to write, and there is no version of her job in which she is, and there is no manager in that building she could ask, because there is not a thing to ask about: she cannot answer the question, so the question is not routed anywhere, and it is still on her counter at about one o'clock with nobody else's initials on it.**

She said nothing for about four minutes.

---

Two. A man of about thirty-three, duty supervisor, second of the four districts, same morning, about ten past ten.

The correct thing, where he works, is that a document that arrives is put in the folder for the next shift and read out at the handover. He has done it about four times a week for about four years and about four people have never once asked him whether he thought it was a good idea.

He put the copy in the folder. He read the first line of it out at the handover at about ten past seven that morning, which is before the copy arrived, and then again at about ten past one, which is after, and he read all of it both times because that is the procedure and the procedure is not stupid.

**Correct. Every word of it. Nobody in that building has ever had a fault with anything he has done at a handover in about four years.**

The second piece of paper is a query. It came back on the folder's own paper with a line through it and a note in the margin, and the note asks for the number of people on shift who had read the copy by the time it was read out, and it asks for that number to be given per person rather than in total, and it says it needs it before Friday.

**Nobody in that building keeps that number. There is no column for it. He could count the room on a Friday and he could not say who had been standing where on a Tuesday.**

He wrote *not held* in the margin in about four seconds, which is the correct answer, and sent it back, and the second piece of paper came back a third time at about four o'clock with the same question on it and nothing added.

---

Three. A woman of about twenty-eight, fourth of the four districts, in a district office, at about half past ten in the morning.

The correct thing where she works is that a document from outside is not actioned by the person who receives it. It is passed to the person whose desk it is about. She passed it. It took her about two minutes and she wrote a covering note, which is what she is supposed to write, and the covering note is four lines and is correct and says which desk it is going to and why.

**She has done this about nine times a day for about six years. Nobody in that office has ever told her that she does it well and nobody has ever told her that she does it badly.**

The second piece of paper came back to her at about one o'clock, from the desk it had gone to, with a question on the bottom of her own covering note in a different hand.

**Which of the two people in this document is to be asked the question at the foot of it.**

There are not two people in the document. She read it twice. There is a count, a reason, a reference, a date, and one name.

**She is not authorised to answer it. The desk it went to is not the desk that wrote the question, and there is no procedure that says who tells the person who sent it that the question cannot be sent back, because in about six years nothing has ever come back that could not be answered.**

She put it in a tray. The tray is on a desk in a building this case has never been inside. She did not chase it and she was right not to and she was not thanked for either.

---

Four. A man of about fifty-four, in the third of the four districts, in a building with a door carrying a district's own name and a number that is the district's number and is not a date and is not a count, at about ten past eleven in the morning.

He is on a gate. His correct thing is that anything pinned up stays up. He had a spare pin and about four minutes and he put the copy on the board by the door where about nine people a day walk past it, and he did not take anything down, and nobody asked him to take anything down.

**Nobody asked him to pin it up. He has never had a conversation about it and he never will.**

He got no second piece of paper. Nobody wrote back to him, because nobody knew he had done it, and nobody will know, and there is no form in that district that records that a copy went on a board.

**He was right. It is the only one of the four that was free and he did not know that it was the only one and nobody told him and nobody is going to.**

---

And the cost, named out loud, in her own words, by the first of the four, at about one o'clock, in about eleven seconds, to about four people in her own building, and it is the only cost named out loud in this entry:

"**I have got a piece of paper that asks me whether a man knows he has been named. And I am a person who writes things in a book. And I do not know. And I am not going to ring him, because that is not my job and because if I ring him then I have found him, and about four people in this city can find a person who has been found once. So I am going to write down that I do not know, because that is the true answer, and the true answer is the one that is going to be read by somebody as a refusal, and it is not a refusal, and there is no way on that paper to say that it is not.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. Nobody told her she had done the right thing, because about four people in that room had done the same correct thing in the same week and not one of them had been told either.

---

A fifth thing happened on that Wednesday and it is not a document and it is not a thing anybody in this case arranged.

The cook of twenty-seven has a cousin who works in a building in the second of the four districts, and the cousin mentioned in a doorway on the Sunday that somebody's job had changed because of a piece of paper, and the cook did not ask which piece of paper, and was not asked, and has not asked since.

She said one thing about it at about seven that evening, in a room off a service road, in about nine seconds, to nobody:

"**They have all got a copy of the same piece of paper and every one of them has done the right thing with it and every one of them has got something back they cannot answer, and none of them can tell the other three, because there is nowhere to tell them.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. She went back to the sink.

---

And a man of twenty-two was told about one of the four on the Wednesday, by a person with an ordinary job who had heard about it and had not been asked and had not been going to keep it, and what he was told was that a question of the shape *does he know* is now on paper in four buildings in this city.

He said nothing about it. He has not repeated it, improved on it, or written it down.

**What he has, and it is new, and he has not said it out loud to anybody, is that there are now about four ways in this city for a piece of paper to arrive at his name, and he was named on one of them before anybody thought of the other three, and he cannot get any of the four back, and none of the four is a thing he did.**

He teaches, demonstrates, assesses and assists nobody, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*325. Wednesday of week 115, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is four correct actions in four buildings, four second pieces of paper, one of them free, and one cost named out loud in about eleven seconds.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Five jobs attended, one in a building in the third of the four districts, on a stair, about nine minutes, and no job number in this case covers anything in this entry. About four people on a board by a door in the third district that nobody has recorded. The rate held at nine and ten and reached eleven for about half an hour in the middle of the afternoon. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off a service road is three hundred and thirty-one days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is three hundred and thirty-five days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and fifty-one, thirteen two hundred and two, fourteen one hundred and sixty-seven, fifteen one hundred and forty-six, sixteen one hundred and twenty-one, seventeen one hundred and three, eighteen forty-nine, nineteen twenty-seven. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread. The master's fifth line is blank and the drawer was not opened. The Exchange did not sit and its next sitting is in a fortnight and it is the eighteenth sitting and the book does not open there.**

*The four actions, four hands, four buildings, all four correct, as four records.* **One: a woman of about forty-one, estates officer, first district, about ten past nine; log a document in a register, which is a book and is not a ring binder and is not a hardboard; reference, date, address, count; four minutes standing at a counter; about four people in the room watched and none of them said anything. Two: a man of about thirty-three, duty supervisor, second district, about ten past ten; a document that arrives is put in the folder for the next shift and read out at the handover; about four times a week for about four years; read in full twice, once before it arrived and once after, because that is the procedure. Three: a woman of about twenty-eight, fourth district, about half past ten; a document from outside is passed to the desk it concerns with a four-line covering note, which is what she has done about nine times a day for about six years. Four: a man of about fifty-four on a gate, third district, about ten past eleven; anything pinned up stays up; spare pin, about four minutes, a board by a door with about nine people a day past it. **All four correct. None asked to do any of it. None thanked for any of it. None has ever been told they were right by anybody, and none of the four is a villain and none of the four is owed anything.**

*The four second pieces of paper, three of them, and one absence.* **One, at about half past eleven, on the letterhead of the register's own building, about half a sheet, and the first form in this case anybody has been sent rather than gone and got: the reference is noted, the count is noted, and **please confirm that the person named is aware of this appointment.** No second question, no third, nothing to sign, no name of a reader. Two, returned on the folder's own paper, a query with a line through it and a note in the margin, asking for the number of people on shift who had read the copy by the time it was read out, per person and not in total, before Friday. **No column for that number exists in that building, and the correct answer *not held* was written in the margin in about four seconds and sent back, and the same question came back a third time at about four o'clock with nothing added.** Three, returned to a desk at about one o'clock, in a different hand, on the foot of a covering note: **which of the two people in this document is to be asked the question at the foot of it.** There are not two people in the document; she read it twice; there is a count, a reason, a reference, a date and one name; the desk it went to did not write the question; there is no procedure for telling a sender that a question cannot be sent back, and in about six years nothing had ever come back that could not be answered. It is in a tray on a desk in a building this case has never entered and was not chased. **Four, none. A man on a gate in the third district pinned a copy to a board and no second paper came, because nobody knows he did it, and no form in that district records that a copy went on a board, and nobody is going to find out.**

*The cost, as a record.* **Speaker: the first of the four, at about one in the afternoon, about eleven seconds, to about four people in her own building. Items in her order: a paper now asks her whether a man knows he has been named; her job is writing things in a book; she does not know; she will not ring him, because that is not her job, and because if she rings him then she has found him, and about four people in this city can find a person who has been found once; therefore she will write down that she does not know, because that is the true answer. **And the cost, which is the last clause and the one that matters: the true answer is the one that will be read by somebody as a refusal; it is not a refusal; and there is no way on that paper to say that it is not.** Not improved on. Not thanked. **Not one of the four has been told by anybody that they did the right thing, and each of the four did it.**

*What it did to the man named, and the fourth thing in a head that was not written down.* **Informed on this Wednesday by a person with an ordinary job who had heard about it and had not been asked and was not going to keep it: that a question of the shape *does he know* is now on paper in four buildings in this city. He said nothing, has not repeated it, has not improved on it and has not written it down — not on a service road, not in the back of his own book, not on a surface. **What he has and has not said: there are now about four ways in this city for a piece of paper to arrive at his name; he was named on one of them about three weeks before anybody thought of the other three; he cannot get any of the four back; and none of the four is a thing he did.** This is not a fourth item in his head and is not to be described as one: the four lines about a lift and the folded sheet and the sentence with a stamp are three and are recorded where they are recorded. It was not spoken aloud, was not put on a surface, and is not to be asked about.**

*What the day did not touch.* **He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing and is the subject of one line of the last paragraph above and nothing else. No panel, no marker, no relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded. **No name is attached to anything that happened on this Wednesday, and no cause was named for any of it.** The printed rule was not invoked. The Exchange did not sit. The secondment is unchanged and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. The heating is on, costs more, four names on a lease cost more, and the fortnight is unpaid and unresolved. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page is unread. The standing question is not stated in this entry and carries no number. The question written once on a service road in week one hundred and eight is not answered here, is not in anybody's mouth, and its sentence is not printed in this entry. No message arrived from anybody, and four second pieces of paper in four buildings are not a message. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 325.**
