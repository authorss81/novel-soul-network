# Chapter 333 — A Count Of Doors

The Thursday of week one hundred and eighteen was the day this case worked out which four buildings a count produces, and it turned out that the four it produced were the four with the most doors on a list, and that a man of fifty-one with a tool bag could name four other buildings in about nine seconds and was right about every one of them, and nobody thanked him, and the four on the sheet did not change, and **he is right about the load and wrong about four buildings.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Nine jobs attended and one address not attended, not chased, and it is the one that has been not attended for about a fortnight.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty-three days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Wednesday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The four buildings on that sheet carry about nine hundred doors between them. Two hundred and forty, two hundred and ninety, two hundred, a hundred and seventy.

**Nine hundred doors on one company's maintenance list, in four buildings, and the four buildings on the sheet are those four buildings, and about four people established that on the Thursday morning of week one hundred and eighteen inside about nine minutes and nobody had to be told how to do it.**

---

The test was the woman of about twenty-nine's, and she ran it herself, in her own building, to about four people who had not asked her, on a list she keeps for her own reasons and has kept for about four years.

She put the four addresses next to the four numbers on the sheet. Then she put next to those the four numbers her own list carries, and the four numbers her own list carries are the same four numbers, and about nine hundred is what four buildings come to when the doors on them are added up, and the four buildings on the sheet are four of the four on the list.

Then she said the part that is the finding, in about nine seconds:

"**So the count did not go wrong. The count did exactly what a count does. Somebody counted the doors they could get at, and there are about nine hundred of those in four buildings, and a count of about nine hundred is about nine hundred doors, and no part of that is anybody's fault and none of it is a person.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her.

---

The man of forty-one came to the fourth district at about eleven in the morning because he had been told, correctly, that four buildings were being discussed, and he is Oren Vey, who is an operations director of the company that holds the maintenance contract and prints the plates, and he did not bring a product and did not offer one and was not asked to bring anything.

He had a sheet of his own. It is not a form and it is not a minute and it is not a notice and it is not a report and it is not a committee paper and it is not a leaflet and it is not a registration and it is not a constitution, and it is a schedule of what his company maintains and in which buildings, and it runs to about four pages and about nine hundred lines.

He said three things in about nine seconds each, and all three of them were correct.

"**My company maintains a plate. That is the contract. It does not cover a night, it does not cover a person, and it does not cover what is behind a door, and I could not change what is on a plate in four buildings this morning if somebody gave me the week.**"

"**I did not choose the four buildings. I have never chosen the four buildings. They are the four with the most doors in them, because they are the four where I have the most to lose, and I would like that recorded as a thing I said and not as a thing I did.**"

And then the third one, which he said to a person with an ordinary job and not to the four people in the room, in about four seconds:

"**If you want to know why a city is dark in the buildings where it is darkest for a person, you will not find out from a list of doors. I have looked. I have four pages.**"

Nobody improved on any of it. Nobody thanked him. He left at about half past eleven with his four pages and he is not in this case again and nothing of his was used by anybody in this case afterwards.

---

And the man of fifty-one with a tool bag said his part in a doorway at about half past twelve, in the second of the four districts, to nobody, having been asked nothing.

**He has been in buildings in this city for about nine years and he can get into a great many of them. He can say which four.**

He said, in about nine seconds:

"**Nine flats and about nine people. Eleven doors and about eleven people. Four doors and a room over a shop. Nine doors and a woman who does not leave her flat before six in the evening. That is four buildings and that is where four hours in the dark costs a person something in this city and I have been in all four of them in the last month and I have not been asked and I am not going to be asked.**"

Then he said the other half, and it is the half that is the finding, and he said it without slowing down:

"**Those four have about thirty-three doors between them. The four on that sheet have about nine hundred. A count cannot tell the difference between a door that gets maintained and a person who is in the room behind it, and it is not going to learn to, and I have been trying to think of a way of writing it down and I have not got one, and I am fifty-one and I am not a post and nobody has asked me to be one.**"

**He was right about all four. Four people checked three of them over the following fortnight and the fourth was checked by a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts who was standing in it on the Friday night. Nobody thanked him for any of it and he has not asked and is not going to ask.**

---

And a woman of about thirty-eight in the first district put the other half of the same thing on the table at about half past two on the Thursday, to about four people, out loud, in about nine seconds, and it is about a file and not about a list of doors.

She has about four hundred and eleven premises in her own file. She has had them for about four years. Every one of them came from somebody else's count.

"**A premise is a word for a building. I have four hundred and eleven of them and I have never once been asked what a building is for, and neither has anybody who put one in my file, and a count of premises is a count of what somebody counted first, and if you count what somebody counted first for long enough you have got a city made entirely of things that were counted.**"

Nobody improved on it. She is not a villain and she is not a fool and she is owed nothing and nobody thanked her.

---

**He is right about the load.** The four hours have to come off somewhere and they are coming off the four buildings with the most doors in them and not the four buildings with the most people in them, and if the load is real and it is real and four competent people and one Continuity Office man with a bad hip have all said so in four different buildings, then something has to come off and this is what came off.

**And he is wrong about four buildings.** He read the sheet on a counter on the Wednesday in about nine seconds and took it, and he has not been to any of the four and has not been asked to go, and the four on it are the four with about nine hundred doors on them, and the four with about thirty-three doors on them are not on it and are not going to be, and about four people in this case can name them and about nine hundred doors cannot.

Nobody has said this to him. Nobody has told him about the doorway, or the four pages, or the nine minutes, or the thirty-three doors. **He is behind, and he does not know that he is behind, and about four people in four buildings know it and none of them is going to tell him.**

---

A room off a service road was warm at about half past six that evening and about nine people were in it.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all for about an hour. **A man of fifty-one with a tool bag put the bag down at the door, which he has not done on any other evening of this volume, and stood at the back and said nothing at all, and nobody asked him about the doorway.**

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one thing, in about four seconds, to nobody, and it was about a queue, and it was not about any of this, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
