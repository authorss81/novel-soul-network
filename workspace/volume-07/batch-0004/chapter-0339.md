# Chapter 339 — The Nineteenth Sitting

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and twenty was the nineteenth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and the book opened, and a person telephoned at about ten past ten and said one thing to the holder's face and it went in in about nine seconds, and no word of it is in this entry, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her, and the telephone moved from sixty-three to sixty-six and the book moved from forty-nine to fifty and the count was announced for the twenty-fourth time and she did not give anybody the rule and nothing was given in her place and the ninth chair was against the wall with its back to the room and it was not moved back.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Six jobs attended, one of them in a building in the first of the four districts, on a stair, at about half past seven in the morning, in the dark, because a light in that building comes on at about eight.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is three hundred and sixty-six days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Tuesday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The shutters were up in a room above a line in a building in Saltmarket and the holder of the room said so at the start, as she says at the start of every sitting.

Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in. The ninth was against the wall with its back to the room and it stayed where it was and nobody looked at it and nobody had to. There is a table, a tin that has never had a lock on it, and a book with four columns and forty-nine lines in it when the morning started.

Nine people. A man of about forty-four, ninth attendance, who asked for something four weeks ago and was given nine words that were not it. A young man of twenty-one in attendance since he was seventeen, who keeps a count he has never given her. A young woman of twenty-one in attendance since she was fifteen, who has never been asked anything and was not asked anything this morning. And about five others undescribed.

**Nobody wrote down that she was there. Nobody writes it down.**

---

The telephone rang at about ten past ten and she answered it in about two seconds and said her own name, which she has said on that telephone for nineteen years.

A person said one thing to her.

**She did not ask them to say it again. She did not ask them a question about it. She did not write while they were talking and she did not repeat it to anybody in the room. It took about nine seconds and then she said thank you and then she was quiet for about four seconds and then she wrote a line in the book with a pen she took out of a bag, and the line is in the book and is not in this entry.**

Nobody in that room asked what it was. Nobody asked who it was. About four people in that room understood that there was a thing to be done about a person and about four people in that room did not, and the four who did not have not said anything about it before or since.

**The book opened. It is at fifty lines. It is at fifty lines because somebody said a thing to her face at about ten past ten on the Wednesday morning of week one hundred and twenty, and there is no other way a line gets into that book and there has not been for nineteen years.**

---

The count came at about ten past ten, unasked, as it comes, about four minutes after the line went in.

"**Fifty to sixty-six. One and three in four weeks, to the day. The book is up one and the telephone is up three, and the telephone was ahead of the book, and it was ahead four weeks ago, and it was ahead the time before that, and I am telling you the direction and not the difference, and I am not going to look into it in front of nine people.**"

"**You have announced that count how many times.**"

"**Twenty-four. Nineteen of those are the nineteen Wednesdays this book holds and five predate it, and there is a sheet with the five on it that I have not shown anybody in nineteen years and am not going to on a Wednesday.**"

Nobody said anything. **Nothing was converted. A call did not become a line and a line did not become a sitting and none of the three became either of the other two, and the numbers one and three were not explained and were not asked about, and the direction was given and the difference was not, and she has said the direction in six sittings running and has not looked into it once.**

The telephone is sixteen ahead of the book, where it was fourteen ahead four weeks ago and eleven ahead eight weeks ago, and the direction has not changed sign since before a word was written down, and the difference is not a number of sittings and is not converted by anybody in that room, and she has not offered it and nobody has asked her for it.

---

Nobody asked her for the rule, and she gave nothing in place of it, and that is the whole of it.

She gave it for about the ninth time in nineteen years, to people who did not need it, and in the last week of an earlier volume she said she had said it for the last time and that anybody in that room who needed it after that morning could do without her. Nine people were in that room. Four of them are in this one. **Four weeks ago, in this room, one of the nine asked her for it again, having said out loud that he wanted to be able to tell somebody he had asked, and she gave him nine words that were not it, and the promise was kept, and about four people noticed the keeping and not one of them said so. This morning nobody asked.** A man of about forty-four, ninth attendance, sat down at about a quarter to ten and did not ask, and about four people in that room noticed that he had not asked, and he was not asked about that either, and about four people in that room noticed the noticing.

**There is no beat available here and none was taken. She is not asked, and she does not say it, and she does not refuse it, and she does not paraphrase it and does not allude to it and does not give anything in its place, and anybody who has been waiting nineteen years to be told what it is will not be told it this morning, and this entry does not say what it is either.**

---

At about half past eleven she said one more thing, and it was unasked, and nobody had asked her for anything since about ten past ten.

"**A person telephoned me this morning and got a sentence out in about nine seconds and I have written it down and there is nothing else I am going to say about it, and I would like about four of the nine of you to notice that I have not explained myself and I am not going to.**"

Nobody said anything. A young man of twenty-one said one word, which was that he had noticed, and she said that was not the same thing and he said he knew.

He has not said another and has not been asked.

---

Nobody in that room said anything about the seven days that had gone before it.

There was a building in the third of the four districts that had been dark for four hours on the Friday night, and a count on a piece of paper in a building in Crown Terrace that said four hours, and a man of fifty-eight who had said one sentence in a room a week before this, and about nine people in this city who had stopped telephoning a man of twenty-two, and the man of twenty-two himself. **None of it was raised in that room. None of it was asked about. Nobody in that room knows about any of it, and this entry places the four facts side by side and joins them in no way.**

The printed rule is still named and restricted rather than repealed and about four hundred competent people in this city can still invoke it, and that number is not a conspiracy and is not on a list and is not reducible, and nobody can produce it.

A Continuity Office still holds an emergency authority. A man of twenty-two was shown a page in a building in Crown Terrace about a month ago and found that his own appointment carries a reference, a count, a reason and no date, and the page is still in a file on the second side of that building with no date on it, and nobody in this room knows any of that and it was not raised.

The ninth chair was against the wall at the close and was not moved back, its mover is not named, and the answer about furniture was given for the ninth time and was not confirmed, because confirming it is a minute and she does not take minutes.

---

A room off a service road was warm at about half past six that evening and about nine people were in it and about nine of them do not know that a line went into a book in another part of the city this morning, and the cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all for about an hour, and a man of fifty-one with a tool bag said nothing, and a first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one thing, in about four seconds, to nobody, and it was not about any of this, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*342. Wednesday of week 120, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one book that opened, one telephone call of about nine seconds, a line in a book and nowhere else, three numbers, and a rule nobody asked for.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Six jobs attended, one a stair in a building in the first of the four districts at about half past seven in the morning, in the dark, because a light in that building comes on at about eight. One room above a line in a building in Saltmarket, shutters up, nine chairs, the ninth against the wall with its back to the room and not moved back at the close, mover not named, a tin that has never been locked, a table. Nine people: the holder, sixty-one; a man of about forty-four, ninth attendance, who has not asked; a young man of twenty-one, in attendance since seventeen, keeping a count he has never given her; a young woman of twenty-one, in attendance since fifteen, unnamed, unasked, and not the first-year of nineteen of another building; about five others undescribed.  The book opened. One line, at about ten past ten, from a caller who said one thing to her face. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off a service road is three hundred and sixty-six days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is three hundred and seventy days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and eighty-six, thirteen two hundred and thirty-seven, fourteen two hundred and two, fifteen one hundred and eighty-one, sixteen one hundred and fifty-six, seventeen one hundred and thirty-eight, eighteen eighty-four, nineteen sixty-two. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line and the newest is eleven words and is the only promise on it. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread. The master's fifth line is blank and the drawer was not opened. The four figures at the close of this entry, printed and not converted: fifty lines in the book, sixty-six on the telephone, the count announced twenty-four times, and nineteen years. And his appointment, which carries a reference, a count, a reason and no date. Its next sitting is a Wednesday twenty-eight days from today.**

*The sitting, the ordinal, and the open-and-shut pattern of the volume.* **Number: nineteenth.  Third of the five sittings of this volume, and the second of the three at which the book opens. Gap since the last: four weeks, to the day. Day: a Wednesday, and she has not opened that book on a Friday in nineteen years and nobody asked her about that this morning. Present: as recorded above. The book opened. Owner: the case.***

*The call, and the line, and what was and was not done with either.* **The telephone rang at about ten past ten; she answered in about two seconds and said her own name, as she has for nineteen years. A person said one thing to her.  She did not ask them to say it again, did not ask them a question about it, did not write while they were talking and did not repeat it to anybody in the room. About nine seconds. Thank you. About four seconds of quiet. Then a pen out of a bag and a line in the book. No word of it is in this entry. Nobody in that room asked what it was or who it was. About four people understood there was a thing to be done about a person; about four did not, and have said nothing about it before or since. The line is in the book because somebody said a thing to her face, and there is no other way a line gets into that book and there has not been for nineteen years. Not improved on. Not thanked. Owner: the case, and nineteen years.***

*The four figures, and the direction.* **Book: forty-nine to fifty, up one. Telephone: sixty-three to sixty-six, up three in four weeks, to the day. Announced count: twenty-three to twenty-four, of which nineteen correspond to the nineteen Wednesdays this book holds and five predate it on a separate sheet not shown to anybody in nineteen years. Spoken at about ten past ten, unasked, as it comes, about four minutes after the line went in.  Four figures, printed and not converted: fifty, sixty-six, twenty-four, and nineteen years. Direction given, difference not, and she said she was not going to look into it in front of nine people. The telephone is sixteen ahead of the book, where it was fourteen ahead four weeks ago and eleven ahead eight weeks ago; the direction has not changed sign; the difference is not a number of sittings. Nobody gave an interval for the line and nobody asked for one. The hardboard in a converted shop unit in another part of the city is a different object, is not in that room, has never been read in that room, and its newest line is sixty-two days old; the two are not to be added to one another by anybody. Owner: the case.***

*The rule that was not asked for.* **Given in nineteen years about nine times, to people who did not need it, and promised at the end of an earlier volume to be said for the last time, with nine people present and told they could do without her. Four of those nine are in this room. Four weeks ago, in this room, one of the nine asked again, having first said aloud that he wanted to be able to tell somebody he had asked, and she gave nine words that were not it; the promise was kept in front of the same nine and about four people noticed and none of them said so.  This morning: no ask. A man of about forty-four, ninth attendance, sat down at about a quarter to ten and did not ask, about four people noticed, he was not asked about not asking, and about four people noticed the noticing. Not said, not refused, not paraphrased, not alluded to, nothing given in its place, and this entry does not say what it is either. Owner: the case, and the not taking is the whole of the beat and there was no beat.**

*The unasked thing at about half past eleven.* **That a person telephoned her and got a sentence out in about nine seconds and she has written it down, and there is nothing else she is going to say about it, and she would like about four of the nine to notice that she has not explained herself and is not going to. A young man of twenty-one said one word, that he had noticed; she said that was not the same thing; he said he knew; he has not said another and has not been asked. Owner: her, and she is owed nothing and is not thanked.***

*What was not in that room and was not raised.* **A building in the third of the four districts that was dark for four hours on the Friday night. A count of four hours on a piece of paper in a building in Crown Terrace. A man of fifty-eight who said one sentence in a room a week earlier. About nine people in this city who stopped telephoning a man of twenty-two, and the man of twenty-two. The printed rule: named, restricted, not withdrawn, about four hundred able to invoke it. The Continuity Office, and a man of twenty-two's appointment, which carries a reference, a count, a reason and no date.  None of it was raised, none of it was asked about, nobody in that room knows about any of it, and this entry places the four facts beside each other and joins them in no way. No name is attached to anything that happened in that room. The secondment is unchanged and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. The heating is on, costs more, four names on a lease cost more, and the fortnight is unpaid and unresolved. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page in the ring binder is unread. The standing question is not stated in this entry and carries no number. The question written once on a service road in an earlier week is not answered here, is not in anybody's mouth, and its sentence is not printed in this entry. No message arrived from anybody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 342.**
