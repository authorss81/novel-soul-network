# Chapter 331 — Four Hours, And A Reason

The Monday morning of week one hundred and eighteen was the morning a Continuity Office man of fifty-eight with a bad hip wrote down an instruction, in print, with a count and a reason and his own name at the bottom of it, and sent it to four districts, and it named four buildings, and about four people in those four buildings did the correct thing with it inside about four hours, and every one of the four found that the correct thing was available to them and the reason for it was not, and about nine people in this city can now see exactly where it will land, and nobody thanked the man who wrote it, and he has not asked to be thanked and has said he will not be.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Eight jobs attended, two of them in the same building on the same morning, and the day was in the middle of the week and nothing about the day was unusual in it.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Sunday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

It is a sheet of paper with a reference on it and four lines of print and a signature, and it is the first thing this office has written in this case that carries a count of hours, and it went out at about eleven in the morning.

He did not write it in a room with nine people in it. He wrote it at a desk in a building in Crown Terrace with a bad hip and a pen that had to be held at an angle, and it took him about nine minutes, and the second thing he did was read it, and the third thing he did was send four copies to four districts and keep the fifth.

**A count of hours has not existed in this city before, in this case. It exists as of about eleven o'clock this morning and about nine people know where it lands.**

---

The order says four things and stops.

It carries a reference in the form that office uses. It carries a count, and the count is four hours. It carries a reason, and the reason is that about nine sites in four districts have a condition in them this week and cannot all be carried at once, and four of the nine have to come off first, and the four have been named. And at the bottom of it there is a person.

**It is not a form and it is not a minute and it is not a notice and it is not a report and it is not a committee paper and it is not a leaflet and it is not a registration and it is not a constitution, and it is not a hardboard on two nails, and the difference between the last of those and the first of those is the whole of what the rest of this volume is about and it is not settled by anybody in this case.**

There is no column on it for what a person is to do with an hour. There is no column for who decides. There is no date on it, and the condition it is issued under has no beginning and no end printed anywhere on it or on the page it came off, and that is not an oversight, because the man who signed it said out loud in a room in Crown Terrace a fortnight ago that a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it runs until somebody with a stamp puts a date on it, and **nobody in this case has ever improved on that sentence and nobody improved on it this morning and it is not repeated here.**

---

The four buildings are named by street and by the number of doors in each, which is the way that office counts, and the four numbers are about two hundred and forty, about two hundred and ninety, about two hundred and about a hundred and seventy.

A building in the first district with about two hundred and forty doors and a shop on the ground floor and a parade of units above it. A building in the second district with about two hundred and ninety doors and a car park behind it and a gate on the front. A four-storey building in the third of the four districts with about two hundred doors, of which about nine are flats and the rest are rooms that people go to in the daytime. And a building in the fourth district with about a hundred and seventy doors, most of them in use between about nine and about five.

**The four are the four that came out of a count, and the count is a count of doors, and nobody in this case has yet asked what a door is for.**

---

And about four people did the correct thing with it on the same morning, inside about four hours, and each of the four was right.

A woman of about thirty-eight in the first district, whose job is a counter and a file with about four hundred and eleven premises in it, read it twice, put a copy in her own record where anybody in her building could get it, and told about four people in her own building before about two o'clock. She asked one question, correctly, at about half past eleven, and it was a question about the count and she got a count.

A man of about thirty-three in the second district, whose job is a desk and a gate and a shift, read it out twice on a shift, pinned a copy to a board by the gate, and telephoned about nine people in that building by hand, from his own telephone, in about forty minutes. He did not go through anybody.

A woman of about sixty in the third of the four districts, whose job is to ask questions, read it in about four minutes and asked two questions about it, both of them correct, and got answers to both of them, and neither answer was a reason.

And a woman of about twenty-nine in the fourth district, who had looked at about nine buildings in a fortnight for her own reasons, put a copy in a drawer and said nothing to anybody about it until about half past four, and then said about four seconds of it to about four people who had not asked her.

**Not one of the four of them did anything wrong. Every one of the four of them did the whole of what the paper gave them to do, and not one of the four of them can say, out loud, in their own words, why their building and not the one next to it, and every one of the four of them found that out about the same time on the same morning and about four other people in this city found out the same thing in other buildings.**

---

Nobody in the four districts wrote back to say no. Nobody in the four districts wrote back to say yes. **A piece of paper with a count on it arrives in a building and the building cannot decline it, and the building can do the whole of what the paper says, and the building is left holding about four hours it has not been given any way of carrying.**

The man of fifty-eight knows all of that. He was told about three of the four in about nine words each by about half past four, from a person with an ordinary job who answers a telephone for a body that is not the office and is not a company and has no name anybody in this case uses, and he did not reply to any of the three, and the not replying is correct, and there is no form for a reply and none was created.

---

A room off a service road was warm at about half past six that evening and about nine people were in it, and there is a sheet of paper on the counter that four people in four buildings in four districts have, and there are four of them in four buildings and one of them is in a room in a different part of the city and is not a protagonist of anything.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all for about an hour. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag read the whole board standing up and said nothing, and had not been asked anything and was not asked anything.

**A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one thing, in about four seconds, to nobody, and it was about a bus, and it was not about any of this, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.**

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door from about two and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*334. Monday of week 118, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one printed instruction with a count of hours on it, four buildings, four correct responses, and about nine people who can see where it lands.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eight jobs attended, two of them in one building on one morning. Four copies of one sheet sent to four districts at about eleven in the morning and a fifth kept. About nine sites in four districts with a condition in them this week, of which four are named.  Four correct responses, each inside about four hours, each by a person with an ordinary job, none of them wrong. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is three hundred and fifty-four days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and seventy, thirteen two hundred and twenty-one, fourteen one hundred and eighty-six, fifteen one hundred and sixty-five, sixteen one hundred and forty, seventeen one hundred and twenty-two, eighteen sixty-eight, nineteen forty-six. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line and the newest is eleven words and is the only promise on it. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread. The master's fifth line is blank and the drawer was not opened. The Exchange sat on Wednesday twelve days ago, its book did not open, its telephone is at sixty-three, its count has been announced twenty-three times, and its next sitting is a Wednesday sixteen days from today.**

*The object, and the four things on it, and the four classes of thing it is not.* **Sheet, one, printed, four lines, a reference, a signature, a person at the foot of it. The count is four hours. The reason is that about nine sites in four districts cannot all be carried at once and four of them have to come off first, and the four are named.  The count is a count of sites and the count on the sheet is a count of hours, and the two are different objects and the sheet does not say which is which. It is not a form, a minute, a notice, a report, a committee paper, a leaflet, a registration or a constitution. It is not a hardboard on two nails. There is no column on it for what a person is to do with an hour and there is no column for who decides and there is no date on it and the condition it is issued under carries no beginning and no end. The difference between a printed instruction with a reference and a person at the foot of it and a hardboard on two nails is the whole of what the rest of this volume is about and is not settled by anybody in this case. Owner: the case.**

*The four buildings, as the count produced them.* **Named by street and by doors: about two hundred and forty in the first district, about two hundred and ninety in the second, about two hundred in the third of the four districts, of which about nine are flats, and about a hundred and seventy in the fourth.  The four are the four that came out of a count, and the count is a count of doors. About nine people in this city can say which building on the sheet is theirs, and about four of them are the people who have to carry it and the other five are not. The nine who can see where it lands and the nine who live in the third building on this list are two different nines and are not to be added to one another. Nobody asked the question of what a door is for. Owner: the case, and the question is not answered in this entry.**

*The four responses, each correct, and the thing all four of them found.* **One, a woman of about thirty-eight in the first district, counter and a file of about four hundred and eleven premises: read it twice, a copy into her own record, about four people in her own building told before about two, one question about the count, answered. Two, a man of about thirty-three in the second district, a desk and a gate and a shift: read it out twice on a shift, a copy on a board by the gate, about nine people telephoned by hand in about forty minutes, through nobody. Three, a woman of about sixty in the third district, whose job is to ask questions: read it in about four minutes, asked two questions, both correct, got answers to both,  and neither answer was a reason. Four, a woman of about twenty-nine in the fourth district: a copy in a drawer, nothing said until about half past four, then about four seconds to about four people who had not asked her. None of the four is a villain and none of the four did anything wrong and all four did the whole of what the paper gave them; not one of the four can say out loud why their building and not the one beside it, and all four found that out on one morning. A building cannot decline a piece of paper with a count on it and can do the whole of what the paper says, and is left holding about four hours it has not been given any way of carrying. Owner: the case.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nothing was taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception. Nothing was run by him, forwarded, or compared.  The printed rule was not invoked in any of the four buildings and was named in no mouth. The Exchange did not sit. The secondment stands and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. Heating on, costs more, four names on a lease cost more, the fortnight unpaid and unresolved. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page is unread.  His appointment carries a reference, a count, a reason and no date, and no date was put on it here, and the sentence about a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it was not said and was not needed. The standing question is not stated and carries no number. The question written once on a service road in an earlier week is not answered, is in nobody's mouth, and its sentence is not printed here. No message arrived from anybody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody, and the man who signed the sheet has said he will not be thanked and has not been asked to be. Entry 334.**
