# Chapter 335 — One Stairwell Stays Lit

The Monday of week one hundred and nineteen was the day the cost arrived, four days before it happened, and it arrived on about nine people who live in a four-storey building in the third of the four districts and who were not asked and are not going to be asked, and in that building the things that keep people well are done at a table twice a week by a person whose work gets paid for, and **those things cannot be done in the dark, and the reason they cannot be done in the dark is that somebody has to be able to see a face, and a face in a stairwell with a torch held over it is not a face, it is a lit patch, and a lit patch is not the same object and a person who has been asked a question in a lit patch has been asked it in the dark.** And a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip did the one thing he was right about on that Monday, and he is not yet at the point where he says the sentence.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Seven jobs attended, three of them after seven in the evening, which is not unusual in this volume and is not unusual in the job.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty-seven days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Sunday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The third district's building has about nine flats and about two hundred doors and about nine people live in the flats and the rest of the doors belong to rooms people go to between about nine and about five.

At a table in the ground floor of that building, twice a week, a woman of twenty-four does work that gets paid for. About nine people come. It is a table, a chair on the other side, a piece of paper, and about eleven appointments in a morning.

**The thing that is done at that table cannot be done in the dark. Not because it needs a machine. Because a person is asked to say yes to something about themselves, and being asked and being watched are the same act, and one of them without the other is not a smaller version of the appointment. It is a different appointment and it is cheaper and everybody in that building knows it and nobody has written it down.**

So the four hours on the Friday land on about nine flats.

---

And the cost was named out loud, in somebody's own words, on the Monday morning, by a person with an ordinary job who is not a protagonist of anything and is not going to become one.

A woman of about thirty-one answers a telephone in a building in Crown Terrace for a body that is not the office and is not a company and has no name anybody in this case uses. She has about four hundred calls a week. She has no procedure for any of this and she has not created one.

She said it in about nine seconds, to a person with an ordinary job who had come about forty minutes on a bus and had asked correctly, and she did not say it to the visitor.

"**I take about four hundred calls a week and in nineteen years of doing it I have never once had anybody ring me at seven in the evening about a person. On Friday I will take about nine, and about nine of them will be about a person, and I am going to be the person who decides which of them gets a stairwell with the light on and which of them does not, and nobody has told me how to do that, and there is nobody in this city who has.**"

Nobody improved on it. The visitor wrote it down in her own words and read it back before she left and did not summarise it, and that is correct.

Then, after about four seconds, to nobody:

"**And I want the other half of it said, because the man who wrote the sheet is right and the sheet is right and I have read it twice. Four hours off a building that has two hundred doors in it is four hours of load taken off a thing that is breaking. That is not a punishment. I have just spent four hours being asked what the cost is and I have got about nine names' worth of cost and no count at all, and the count is the part I have.**"

---

And on the Monday morning the man of fifty-eight with a bad hip did the thing he is right about.

He did not change the count. The four hours stand in four buildings. He did not move a building and he did not move an hour and he did not put a date on anything.

**He wrote a second instruction, in print, with a reference on it, with a count on it, with a reason on it, and with a person at the bottom of it, and the count is four hours and the reason is that in one of the four buildings there are about nine people who sleep there and one stair between them and the ground floor.**

**He put emergency light in the stairwell of that one building for those four hours, and nowhere else, and the instruction went to one district, and it is not a form and it is not a minute and it is not a notice and it is not a report and it is not a committee paper and it is not a leaflet and it is not a registration and it is not a constitution, and it is not a hardboard on two nails, and the difference between the last of those and this one is the whole of what the rest of this volume is about.**

It took him about nine minutes. It cost that office a crew and about nine hours of somebody's evening and a night's sleep for a person with a bad hip, and he paid all three and has not mentioned any of them.

**He is right about the load. The load is real and four hours have to come off somewhere and about nine hundred doors is not a thing anybody in that building can argue with, and he is the only person in this case who has to be right about it every day and is.**

---

And the cost of the second instruction landed on a person too, and that person was not asked, and it lands four days before the first one.

**Somebody has to be in that stairwell for four hours on a Friday evening with a light on and a piece of paper.** A woman of about twenty-eight in the third of the four districts, whose job is to pass a document to the desk it concerns with a four-line covering note, was the person who worked out who, and she did it in about nine minutes on the Monday morning, and she did not have to be told to and she was not asked whether she would.

She said, in about four seconds, to about four people in a corridor, that a lit stairwell is not a light, it is a person standing in a stairwell, and that the person standing in it for four hours on a Friday evening is somebody who is now doing something on a Friday evening that they were not doing, and that the four hours of the table work still do not happen because a person holding a light cannot also ask anybody anything.

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. Nobody has asked her to hold the light and nobody is going to and it is not in this entry that she will or will not.

---

And on the Monday afternoon, at about half past two, a person asked him a question and he did not answer it.

She rang him. She said she had about nine seconds and used about four of them, and she asked whether the count of four hours is a count of hours or a count of people.

He said that he would look at it. He did not say when. He did not say that he had an answer. He did not say that the answer was going to be given in a room. He said, in about four seconds, that the reference on the sheet was the correct reference to quote, and that was the end of it.

**He has not been asked the question again. He will be, and the asking is not in this entry.**

---

Nobody told the man of twenty-two any of it. He was on a job in the second of the four districts on the Monday and finished all seven of his jobs and got to the room off a service road at about half past six, and about four people in that room knew something they were not going to tell him, and he did not know there was something, and he has not asked, and nobody has offered.

---

A room off a service road was warm at about half past six that evening and about nine people were in it.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all for about an hour. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag said nothing and looked at the counter for about four seconds, which is more than he usually does, and he has not been asked about the four seconds.

**A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one thing, in about four seconds, to nobody, and it was about a queue, and it was not about any of this, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.**

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*Why light is a condition of the practice and not an convenience of it.* **Premises, fourth floor of a building in the third of the four districts, about nine; doors, about two hundred; the daytime rooms used between about nine and about five. The requirement is not illumination. Consequence for the Friday: the appointments did not run on the Friday and did not run on the Saturday, and were not moved into the dark, for the reason above. Consequence for the stairwell: one person in it, holding light, unable to ask anything. Owner: the case, and this is the volume's account of why four hours of a count is not four hours of a person's life.**
