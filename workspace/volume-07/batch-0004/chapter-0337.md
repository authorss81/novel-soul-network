# Chapter 337 — Four Hours With The Lights Off

The Friday evening of week one hundred and nineteen, from about seven to about eleven, was four hours in one stairwell of a four-storey building in the third of the four districts, and about nine people were in it who had not all met, and there is no man of twenty-two in this chapter and no part of this chapter is about him. There are four torches and none of them was given to anybody. There is a voice and most of what was said in that building between about seven and about eleven was voice. There is a door on the ground floor that had to be open all night and was. There is a bell that went at about half past eight and nobody in that building knows now whether it was the alarm or the wind. **There is a kettle that went on at about ten for somebody on the fourth floor who had a hot water bottle and a fever, and the person who carried it up four storeys in the dark did it because she was going to anyway, and nobody asked her to, and nobody thanked her.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Nine jobs attended, and the ten o'clock one is a stair in a building in the first of the four districts, and none of his jobs that evening was in the building this entry is about.

The word was at nine and ten and reached eleven for about nine minutes at about half past eight, which is unusual and which he has not mentioned to anybody. The room off a service road is three hundred and sixty-one days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Thursday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The light went at about five to seven.

Not all at once. That stair is on a circuit with a timer and the timer is old and the timer is honest, so the building went from the top down, floor by floor, about nine seconds apart, and the people in it watched it happen from the landings, and there was nothing anybody could do about it and nobody tried to do anything about it.

**A count of hours went onto a piece of paper in a building in Crown Terrace on a Monday morning and the piece of paper went to four districts and four of them came back with nothing, and on the Friday evening the count arrived on about nine people in this building and it arrived exactly as it was written, and that is the first fact about that night and it is the only one anybody has written down.**

---

There was one stairwell in that building with the light on for those four hours, and about nine people in the building knew which one it was without being told, and the reason they knew was that somebody had put it in writing with a count on it, and nobody in that building had been asked to be happy about that and about four of them were not and about four of them were.

The rest of the building was dark from the top down. The stairwell that was lit had a light the size of a hand and a stair that goes up four times and comes down, and about two hundred doors off the landings, of which about nine are flats and the rest are rooms that are shut at seven on a Friday because that is what they do at seven on a Friday.

**Nobody decided anything that night. That is the other fact, and it is a fact about about nine people and not about a plan, and there was no plan, and there is nobody to this day who could tell you who was in charge of that stairwell between about seven and about eleven, and if you asked any of the nine they would tell you that there was not anybody, and they would be right.**

---

The man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in that building at about ten past six because a gate motor in the same district is on the same supply and somebody telephoned him, and he came with the bag he has carried for about nine years and he had been in that building once, in daylight, about four years ago.

He said three things in about nine seconds each, on the ground floor, to whoever was nearest, and all three of them were correct and none of them was an instruction.

"**There is one way out of this building that works in the dark and it is this stair. The other one has been locked at the bottom since before I was in this trade and it is not a second way out, it is a wall with a door in it, and I am telling you that so that nobody counts it.**"

"**The riser is behind the panel on the half landing and I can get my hand round it, and I am not going to, because I have not been asked to and because the man who put the light on did not put it on for a water leak.**"

And the third one, which is the one everybody in that building repeated afterwards, and he said it standing up, with the bag on the floor:

"**Four hours is what it said it would be, and I have been in a great many buildings in this city in the dark, and the ones that go wrong are never the ones where somebody had to work. It is the ones where somebody decided to work it out. Nothing has to be decided in here. It is a stair and a rail and about nine people and it is going to be over at eleven.**"

Nobody thanked him. He has not asked. He put the bag against the wall and stayed on the ground floor for the whole of the four hours and did about four things in that time and none of them was more than a minute.

---

A woman of thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors had come into that building at about half past six on her own account, with a list she keeps for work, and about two hundred doors of that building are on it and about nine hundred of them are on it in four buildings.

She did the only thing in that building on Friday night that anybody who was in it had never seen anybody do, and she did it because she could and because there was four hours of nothing to do with.

**She read about nine door numbers in the dark.** Not out loud, and not to anybody. With a light held low in one hand, one number at a time, from the bottom of the stair to the top and back down, saying nothing, checking what was on the plate against what she had, in about an hour and a half.

Seven of the nine were right.

One was wrong, and she had known for about two years that it was wrong, and the person whose door it is lives on the third floor and was not in the building and does not know. And one was right that she had believed was wrong.

**She has not told anybody. She has not written it down. There is no sheet and there is no form and there is nothing on a counter in that building about it, and she told one person in that stairwell at about nine o'clock, in about four seconds, and the person she told it to did not know what to do with it and has not done anything with it and is not going to.**

---

A man of about twenty-seven who has read and replaced the numbers on doors for about four years was in that building because he lives in it, and he has a torch of his own and he did not go up with anybody and he did not go down with anybody.

He could not read a number in the dark. He said so, out loud, to about four people, in about four seconds, on the ground floor, at about half past seven, and it was the plainest thing anybody said for two hours.

Then he said the other half of it, and it was also plain, and about four people heard it:

"**I can do the ones I know by heart and I know about nine of them and I have not needed to see anything for nine. The rest of them I have to have a number in front of me and there are about two hundred and there is no sheet.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked him to do anything about it. He went up at about half past eight and came down at about nine and went up again at about ten, and nobody sent him and nobody stopped him and he has not said why and was not asked.

---

A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was in that building at about a quarter past six because a lift in it had stopped between two floors, and there was no fault, and there was nothing to be done about it with the power off, and he was the person the building rang and he came and could not leave.

The car sat where it had sat since about half past four. The button in the car did nothing. The gate on the landing was shut and the gate is worked by a person and there was no person in the car.

**He was on the second-floor landing from about seven until about eleven with a torch he had not switched on, and he stood at the rail for about nine minutes at a time, four times, and on two of the four times somebody came up the stair and he went, and on the other two times nobody came and he did not.**

He did four things in that four hours. He read out, twice, from memory, the make and the year and the load of a lift that is not the lift in that building, to a man of about twenty-eight who was writing on the back of a hand, and he was right both times. He put his hand on the landing gate once, and a woman of about forty-one put her hand where his was and took it off him, and it closed, and he put it back, and neither of them said a word about it and neither of them has said a word about it since.

**Nobody in that building has ever said one word to him about any of this and nobody is going to, and he has not said one word to anybody about what it is like to stand at a rail for four hours with a torch he has not switched on, and that is the last thing that happened in that building and it is not the end of anything and it is not to be written down by anybody as a finding.**

---

A woman of about forty-one on no list lives on the first floor of that building and had been coming to a company training room in another part of the city for about four years, and had not left a room when nothing told her to for about a month, and had not spoken in one.

On the Friday night she came down at about seven and got to the ground floor and sat down on the bottom step with her back to the wall and did not go out of the front door.

The door was open. The door was open the whole four hours because a man of fifty-one put a coin under it at about ten past six and it stayed.

**Nobody asked her to go out and nobody asked her why she did not and about four people noticed and about four people in that building did not know what they had noticed and one of them knew and has not said anything to anybody and is not going to.**

---

The bell went at about half past eight.

It is the alarm on the car and the car has a battery and the battery is about four years old. It went for about two minutes. Nobody in that building knows to this day whether it was the car or the wind in the stairwell or a plate moving on a landing.

**About nine people went down four storeys.**

Some of them had already been on the ground floor and went out into the yard and stood there. Some of them were on the fourth floor and went down in about ninety seconds with a hand on a rail and a torch in the other hand, and one of them went down holding the rail with both hands and no torch at all and got all the way and has told nobody which of the two she did.

They came back up. It took about eleven minutes all told. Nobody counted them, because counting nine people in the dark with four torches is not a thing anybody does, and about four of the nine thought, at the time, in about the same words, that there were about eleven, and there were nine.

Nobody has said the alarm was nothing. Nobody knew.

---

The kettle went on at about ten.

There is a cook of twenty-seven who works in that building's ground floor between about nine and about two and there is a kettle on that is hers and there is a person on the fourth floor who had a hot water bottle on since about six and had a fever by about eight and had told nobody, and who is not important and is not a plot and is not going to be in anything else in this case.

She knew about the fever at about seven. Nobody told her. She had asked at about seven, in the corridor, in four seconds, whether anybody up there was all right, and four people had said yes.

**She put the kettle on at about ten and she took it up four storeys in the dark and she held the rail with her elbow and she was at that door in about two minutes and she was there for about nine minutes and she came back down with the empty and she washed it and she went home.**

Nobody asked her to. Nobody thanked her. Nobody has asked her about it since and she has not brought it up and is not going to.

---

A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a gate in another part of that district was in that building because the gate motor is on the same supply and because there is a man on that gate at night who has to be told, and he had come at about ten to seven to say it.

He stayed. He had a torch and he knew where the second stair was, which is to say he knew where the bottom of it was, because it is locked and he had tried it about two years ago.

He did one thing that mattered and he did it at about nine without being asked and without telling anybody he was going to do it, and about four people found out about it afterwards.

**He went up to the second-floor landing in the dark and sat down on the step with his back against the wall about a metre from a man who was standing at the rail, and he stayed there about nine minutes, and he did not say one word.**

He has not said that he did it. He was asked about it once, by a person with an ordinary job, and he said that he had been in the building, which is true, and nothing else, and he has not been asked again.

---

The light came back at about eleven and about ten minutes past, from the bottom up, floor by floor, about nine seconds apart, and the last floor was at about ten past eleven.

**Four hours and about ten minutes, and the count had said four, and the man who wrote the count was in a building four miles away and was not told about any of this and has not been told about any of this since, and nobody rang him, and there is nobody in this city who thinks he would have wanted to be.**

Nobody thanked anybody. Four people went back up. A door stayed open all night that had been shut for about a fortnight, and a coin is still under it, and a kettle was washed, and two numbers on two doors in that building are known to one person.
