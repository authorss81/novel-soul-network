# Chapter 334 — The Seventh Friday

The Friday evening of week one hundred and eighteen was the seventh of the Fridays this volume has kept, and it was a seventh thing and not a second version of any of the six, and about eleven people were in the same company training room in Crown Terrace, and for the first time on any of the seven Fridays nobody asked the man who owns the room whether he was going to write it down, **and the room started a run off a number read out of a piece of paper, which it had never done, and nobody in that room has said why that worked.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Four jobs attended and neither of them was in the building this evening is about. A shift in a company training room is not a job and has never been on this docket.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty-four days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Thursday for the temperature and one of them is a woman of about thirty who was not asked anything.

---

This is the seventh of them and it is a seventh thing. The four Fridays before the middle of this volume and the two after it were six different things, and the six are not joined to one another by anybody and are not to be joined without a reason and a record, and there is no reason tonight and there is no record.

The room is the same room. About nine folding chairs, a counter, a telephone on the company line, a run that is a hold with a count in it, and a release that is supposed to arrive when somebody in the room says so.

**The post at the back of that room is not described this week either. That is the third Friday running and it is not a habit and it is not a decision anybody has taken today, it is a decision this case has taken three times, and a writer who decides what is on one company's post has decided a fact about one company, and this entry does not decide it.**

---

One. The woman who runs the room is thirty-six and has a lanyard and holds no post and is owed nothing. She has run that room for about four years on her own voice.

On this Friday she began the last run of the evening and could not begin it. She looked at the man of about twenty-seven, who does not know that she looks at him, and waited, and about nine people waited with her, and nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

Then the man of about twenty-seven turned a sheet of the company's own over, found the line with the count on it, and read the number out. Nothing else. Not the sentence, not a name, not a word.

**The run went. It went because a number came out of a mouth with a piece of paper in front of it, and the woman of thirty-six did not say the word, and about nine people in that room have not said why a number will do what a voice will not, and one of the nine has been coming for about four years and is not going to say it either.**

Nobody wrote it down. There is no sheet. There is no form and there is no report and there is no register and there is no column, and the number on the company's own sheet was in front of about eleven people and none of them copied it.

---

Two. And then, about four minutes later, in about nine seconds, the man of about twenty-seven said the other thing, having been asked nothing by anybody.

He reads and replaces the numbers on doors for a living. He has been inside two of the four buildings on that sheet.

"**I have been in that building in the third district about nine times and there are about nine flats in it and about two hundred doors, and the number on the door of the third-floor flat at the back is not that flat's number, it belongs to a flat on the other side of the city, and it has been there for about two years, and I have read it nine times and said nothing nine times because it is not my plate and it is not my job and I get paid on Fridays.**"

Then he stopped, and after about four seconds he said the rest of it, and nobody had asked for the rest of it:

"**I am not saying that to get the four hours stopped. I am saying it because I have been in that building and I have counted its doors and I could tell you which four the count got and I could not tell you which four it should have got, and I have been carrying the second one about a fortnight and there is nowhere to put it down.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked him. The woman of thirty-six said one word, which was *right*, and went back to the counter.

---

Three. The man of twenty-nine who works on lifts is on no list and has been in that room for about four years and has said nothing on any of the seven Fridays of this volume.

He came in at about half past five, which he has done about four Fridays out of four, and stood by the door for about nine minutes before he sat down, and nobody asked him about it, as on the other six, and nobody is going to.

He sat down. He asked, in about nine seconds, whether anybody would be in the city on Friday of next week past about seven in the evening. He was told that about nine people would be, in four buildings, in the dark, and that it was four hours and it was written down.

He said, "**Right,**" and said nothing else, and that is the ninth of those and it is the only thing he has said in that room in this volume.

**Nobody in that building has asked him what he intends to do on Friday of next week. Nobody asked him on the Monday and nobody has asked him since. He has not said what he will do and he has not been asked and he is owed nothing.**

---

Four. The woman of about forty-one on no list has been in that room for about four years and has not spoken in it since the week she said a word she was never asked to say.

On this Friday she left at about nine, at the end, in about four seconds, without sitting down again, which she has not done for about a month.

One person noticed. She has not been asked about it. It was not asked about in a corridor and it was not asked about at the door and the reason is not recorded in her mouth and is not to be asked for.

---

And nobody asked the man of thirty-four who buys the training, pays for the room and keeps it open, whether he was going to put any of it in a report. He was asked four times in one week about a fortnight ago and said no four times and gave no reason and said he would keep saying no.

**Nobody asked him a fifth time. About four people in that room noticed that nobody had asked him a fifth time, and about four people in that room had been one of the four who noticed something and said nothing the last time, and nobody has said anything about that either, and the asking stopped and nobody stopped it.**

He said, in about four seconds, at the counter, to nobody, that he had noticed as well.

---

A room off a service road was warm at about ten that night. A cook of twenty-seven said nothing at all. **A man of fifty-one with a tool bag sat down at the back with the bag between his boots and said nothing, and had said nothing in a doorway the day before and has not been asked about either.**

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one thing, in about four seconds, to nobody, and it was about a lift, and it was not about any of this, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*337. Friday of week 118, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is the seventh Friday, a run started off a number, a plate read out nine times and not said nine times, and a fifth time nobody asked.*

*Conditions and docket.* **A shift in a company training room is not on this docket and has never been on one. About eleven people in the room, seven on the company's list, a card in a box on a table, unchanged for about four years, and about four on no list at all, which is the standing figure and is on the back of one woman's own card and is on no form.  The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty-four days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is three hundred and fifty-eight days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and seventy-four, thirteen two hundred and twenty-five, fourteen one hundred and ninety, fifteen one hundred and sixty-nine, sixteen one hundred and forty-four, seventeen one hundred and twenty-six, eighteen seventy-two, nineteen fifty. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line and the newest is eleven words and is the only promise on it. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread. The master's fifth line is blank and the drawer was not opened. The Exchange sat sixteen days ago on Wednesday, its book did not open, its telephone is at sixty-three, its count has been announced twenty-three times, and its next sitting is a Wednesday twelve days from today.**

*The run, the number, and the silence about both.* **Instructor, thirty-six, lanyard, four years on her own voice, holder of no post, owed nothing: could not begin. Looked at a man of about twenty-seven who does not know she looks at him. Silence: about nine seconds, in a room of about eleven.  The man of about twenty-seven turned a sheet of the company's own over, located the line carrying the count, and read the number. Nothing else: no sentence, no name, no word. The run began. The instructor did not say the word. The sheet was in front of about eleven people and was not copied by any of them, and there is no sheet, form, report, register, note or column anywhere in that company for any of it. About nine people in that room, one of them a four-year attender, have not said why a numeral will do what a voice will not, and the four-year attender is not going to say it. Nothing was named and nothing was written and this record is an observation, not a finding. The term this room sells is not said in this record and is not printed in it. Owner: the case, and it is to be tested again and not to be answered in this volume by anybody in a narrator's voice.**

*Two of the four, by hand.* **A man of about twenty-seven, company's list, four years in that room, reads and replaces door numbers for a living, has been inside two of the four buildings on the sheet. Two statements, the second unasked and about four seconds after the first. First, about nine seconds: nine visits to the third district's building; about nine flats among about two hundred doors; the plate on the third-floor flat at the back carries a number belonging to a flat in another part of the city; about two years in place; read nine times, said nothing nine times, because the plate is not his and the work is not his and he is paid on Fridays. Second, in four seconds: not said to stop the four hours; able to name the four the count produced and unable to name the four it should have produced; carrying the second about a fortnight; no place to put it. One word back, from the instructor: *right*. Not improved on, not thanked.  A man of twenty-nine, lifts, no list, four years, silent on all seven Fridays of this volume: nine minutes by the door at half past five; asked in about nine seconds whether anybody would be in the city on Friday of next week past seven in the evening; told about nine people, four buildings, the dark, four hours, in writing; said *right*; nothing further. That is the ninth such utterance of his in that room and the only one he has made there in this volume, and nobody in that building has asked him what he means to do on the night, nobody asked him on the Monday, and he is not to be asked. Owner: him, and he is owed nothing.**

*The other two of the four, and the fifth non-asking.* **Forty-one, no list, four years in that room, silent since the week she spoke once unasked. This Friday: left at about nine, in about four seconds, without sitting down again, after about a month of sitting down again. Observed by: one person. Asked: not in the room, not in a corridor, not at a door. The reason is hers, is not on the record of this entry, and is not to be sought.  Thirty-four, buys the training, pays for the room, keeps it open. Asked four times in one week about a fortnight ago whether he would place any of it in a report; four refusals; no reason given; a statement that he would continue refusing. Asked a fifth time: by nobody. About four people in that room registered the non-asking. About four of those four had registered something in an earlier week and said nothing, and nothing has been said about that either.  Nobody stopped the asking. He registered it himself, in about four seconds, at the counter, to nobody, and gave no reason then either. No form, report, register, note or column exists in that company and none was created. Owner: the case, and the asking stopped by nobody and that is the whole of how it stopped.**

*What the day did not touch.* **He was not in that building and is the subject of no word of it. No practice was taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted by anybody in it, and a company training room and a room off a service road are both outside that department and neither is in it because of him.  Nothing was run by him, forwarded, or compared. No name was attached to anything that happened and no cause was named for any of it. The printed rule was not invoked and was named in no mouth. The Exchange did not sit. The secondment stands and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. Heating on, costs more, the fortnight unpaid. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page is unread.  His appointment carries a reference, a count, a reason and no date, and no date was put on it, and the sentence about a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it was not said and was not needed. The standing question is not stated. The question written once on a service road in an earlier week is not answered, is in nobody's mouth, and its sentence is not printed here. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 337.**
