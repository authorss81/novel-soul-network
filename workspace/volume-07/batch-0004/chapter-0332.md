# Chapter 332 — The Hour Is Not A Person

The Wednesday of week one hundred and eighteen was the day a count of hours stopped being a sentence on a piece of paper and became a thing four competent people in four buildings in four districts were carrying, each of them correctly, each of them with their own arithmetic, each of them saying out loud in their own words what it cost, and **the reason their response was right and the reason it was wrong turned out to be the same reason, and the reason is the load, and the load is a real thing and it is the reason the order exists.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Six jobs attended, one of them a stair fire door that has been attended about eleven times and behaves the same every time.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty-two days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Tuesday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

Two days after the sheet went out, the four hours are in four buildings and about four people are holding them, and none of the four of them is holding a document. They are holding an amount of time, and the time does not go away when the sheet is put in a drawer or pinned to a board or read out on a shift.

**A count of hours is the first thing this case has produced that a person cannot put down, and nobody in this city has a practice for putting one down.**

---

One. The first district. About two hundred and forty doors and a parade of units above a shop, and the man who owns four of the units is about fifty-nine and has had them for about nine years and is not a protagonist of anything.

He opened on the Wednesday as he opens every Wednesday. He said, to a person with an ordinary job who had come to check a count, in about nine seconds, that four hours on a Friday evening is not four hours, because a Friday evening in his four units is about two hundred and forty people arriving in a period of about four hours and about two hundred and forty people leaving in a period of about four hours, and the whole of his trade is the two of those.

Then he said the other thing, which nobody had asked him for:

"**I have got no way of telling four of them that. And I am not going to stand in a doorway and say it four times to two hundred and forty people, and the man who sent the sheet is right that the building has to come off, and I have not got a better idea than him and I am not going to pretend I have.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked him. He opened on the Thursday as well.

---

Two. The second district. A building with about two hundred and ninety doors and a gate on the front and a car park behind it, and a man of about thirty-three whose job is a desk and a gate and a shift.

He has it on a board by the gate and about nine people a day go past that gate. He had telephoned about nine people on the Monday and he had not slept differently and he says so.

His cost, named out loud, in his own words, on the Wednesday afternoon to a person who had not asked:

"**It is not the two hundred and ninety. It is that I can hold a gate in the dark and I cannot hold two hundred and ninety doors, and the sheet has one number on it and both of those things are in the number.**"

Then, after about nine seconds, without being asked and to nobody in particular:

"**And I want to say the other half, because I have been thinking about it since Monday and I am the only one in this building who has to think about it: the load is real. I have seen what it does. The four hours are not a punishment and the man who wrote it is not a fool, and if the building does not come off then it is the two hundred and ninety that come off, and I know which of the two I would rather happened and so does he.**"

Nobody improved on either half. Nobody thanked him. He went back to the gate.

---

Three. The third of the four districts. A four-storey building with about two hundred doors of which about nine are flats, and a woman of about sixty whose job is to ask questions, standing in her own corridor with about four people, holding a copy of the sheet she asked two questions about on the Monday.

She named her cost in about nine seconds and it was not the one anybody expected.

"**Two hundred doors and about nine people. The two hundred go home at about five. The nine are there now, on a Friday, at about seven, with the light off, and there is nothing in that building that can be done at night that was not done before about seven, so the four hours do not cost that building anything anybody can put on a piece of paper, and I have been trying since Monday to work out how to say that they cost something, and I have failed, and I am not going to say a thing to be interesting.**"

A person of about twenty-eight in that corridor, whose job is to pass a document to the desk it concerns with a four-line covering note, said one thing back in about four seconds, and it was the only improvement anybody made on anything that day, and it was not an improvement:

"**It is not that nothing happens. It is that nine people do four hours of what they do on Fridays in a room with no light in it, and that is a thing that happens to them and not a thing that happens to a building.**"

Nobody thanked either of them. The woman of about sixty wrote nothing down and has not written it down.

---

Four. The fourth district. A woman of about twenty-nine with a copy in a drawer, who has now said the thing twice, and this time she said it in about nine seconds to about four people who had asked her for it.

"**The four buildings on that sheet are the four with the most doors on a list. That is how the four were made. A door is on a list because somebody maintains it and somebody maintains it because there are a lot of them, and the count that came out of that office is a count of doors and doors are what that count is, and I do not think anybody in that building knows it and I do not think anybody here did before today.**"

Then, in about four seconds, and this is the part she is not asked about and does not repeat:

"**And there are four buildings in this city where four hours in the dark would cost a person something, and none of them is on the sheet, and I have not got a way of saying that in a form anybody can act on and I have looked.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. It was in a corridor at about half past four on a Wednesday and about four people heard it and about nine hundred doors in four buildings are on a maintenance list that has nothing to do with it.

---

And the reason all four of them are right is the load.

**The load is real. It is what happens to a building that is left on when about nine other buildings in four districts are already carrying what they can carry, and four competent people in four buildings know it is real, and the man of fifty-eight with a bad hip knows it is better than the four of them know it, and it is the only thing in this case anybody in this case can agree about without being asked to agree.**

And the reason all four of them are carrying it wrong is the load as well, because a load is measured in the thing it is made of and this one is made of hours, and **an hour is a different thing to a person, and a count of hours is a correct answer to a question about a load and is not an answer to any question about anybody, and the sheet does not say which of those two things it is answering.**

**Every one of the four of them is carrying the hour correctly. That is the finding of that day and it is not a finding anybody had to be taught and nobody in any of the four buildings is doing it badly.**

---

He was in one of the four on the Wednesday morning, on a job that was on his docket, and the sheet was on a counter with about four other things on it, and he read it in about nine seconds, and he did not take it and he did not ask anybody a question about it.

He has read it. Nobody has told him what any of the four of them said about it, and nobody has told him about the woman of about twenty-nine's finding, and he has not asked, and about four people in four buildings have noticed that he has not asked.

---

A room off a service road was warm at about half past six that evening and about nine people were in it.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag stood at the hardboard with his back to the room and said nothing. **A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one thing, in about four seconds, to nobody, and it was about a price of something in a shop, and it was not about any of this, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.**

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*335. Wednesday of week 118, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is four costs named out loud by four people, one improvement that was not one, and the finding that an hour is not a person.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Six jobs attended, one a stair fire door for the eleventh time, unchanged. One of the four buildings attended on a job that was on the docket at about ten in the morning, where a copy of the sheet was on a counter with about four other things on it and was read in about nine seconds and not taken.  Four costs named out loud, in four buildings, in four districts, in about nine words each, by four people with ordinary jobs, none of whom is owed anything and none of whom is thanked. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty-two days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is three hundred and fifty-six days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and seventy-two, thirteen two hundred and twenty-three, fourteen one hundred and eighty-eight, fifteen one hundred and sixty-seven, sixteen one hundred and forty-two, seventeen one hundred and twenty-four, eighteen seventy, nineteen forty-eight. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line and the newest is eleven words and is the only promise on it. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread. The master's fifth line is blank and the drawer was not opened. The Exchange sat fourteen days ago on Wednesday, its book did not open, its telephone is at sixty-three, its count has been announced twenty-three times, and its next sitting is a Wednesday fourteen days from today.**

*The four costs, as a record of speakers and counts.* **First. Fifty-nine, four units, first district, sheet count two hundred and forty. Nine seconds, then nine more unasked. Content: arrival and departure both run in periods of about four hours, and the whole of his trade is the two of those; no way of saying it four times; not going to stand in a doorway; the man who sent the sheet is right; no better idea and not pretending otherwise. Second. Thirty-three, desk and gate, second district, two hundred and ninety. Two parts, the second unasked and nine seconds after the first. Content: a gate can be held in the dark and two hundred and ninety doors cannot, and the sheet carries one number containing both; the load is real; the four hours are not a punishment; the writer is not a fool; if the building does not come off then the doors come off, and he knows which he would rather and so does the writer. Third. Sixty, questions, third of the four districts, about two hundred of which about nine are flats. Nine seconds, refused to be interesting. Content: the two hundred leave at five, the nine are there at seven in the dark, nothing in that building can be done at night that was not done before seven, and she has failed since Monday to find a way to say the four hours cost that building something. Written down: nothing. Reply, from a woman of about twenty-eight, documents, four seconds,  not an improvement: it is not that nothing happens, it is that about nine people do four hours of a Friday in a room with no light in it, and that is a thing that happens to them and not to a building. Fourth. Twenty-nine, fourth district, a hundred and seventy. Nine seconds to four people who asked, then four more unasked. Content: the four on the sheet are the four with the most doors on a list; a door is on a list because it is maintained and it is maintained because there are many; the count is a count of doors; four buildings exist where the dark would cost a person something, none of them is on the sheet, and she has looked for a form and has not got one. Said in a corridor at about half past four. No improvement by anybody. No thanks to anybody. Two of the four costs had a second half that was not asked for and one of those four is a statement that the sheet is right, which is the only place in this movement that anybody defends it out loud. Owner: the case.**

*Why all four are right and why all four are wrong, and the identity that does it.* **Ground of the rightness: the load is real; it is the failure of a building left on when about nine others in four districts are already at their limit; four competent holders know it; the writer of the sheet knows it better than the four; it is the only proposition in this case that anybody accepts without being asked to.  Ground of the wrongness: the same load, measured in the material it is made of, which is hours. An hour is not a person. A count of hours answers a question about load and answers no question about anybody, and the sheet does not say which question it is answering. Consequence on the ground: all four holders carry the hour correctly; no holder in any of the four buildings is carrying it badly; nobody required instruction to do so and none was given. Owner: the case.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nothing was taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception.  He read the sheet on a counter in about nine seconds, took it, put no question about it, and has been told none of the four costs. Nothing was run by him, forwarded, or compared. The printed rule was not invoked and was named in no mouth. The Exchange did not sit. The secondment stands and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. Heating on, costs more, the fortnight unpaid. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page is unread.  His appointment carries a reference, a count, a reason and no date, and no date was put on it, and the sentence about a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it was not said and was not needed. The standing question is not stated. The question written once on a service road in an earlier week is not answered, is in nobody's mouth, and its sentence is not printed here. No message arrived from anybody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 335.**
