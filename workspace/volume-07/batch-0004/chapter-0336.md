# Chapter 336 — One Sentence

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and nineteen was the morning a Continuity Office man of fifty-eight with a bad hip was in a room with about nine people who had not asked for him and did not want him, and he said one sentence out loud, and it was true, and about four people in that room did not improve on it and about four other people in that room wanted to and did not, and he has said two things in about four years and refused one and this is not one of the two, and nobody thanked him and he has said he will not be thanked.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Five jobs attended, and one telephone call at about eleven in the morning that lasted about four minutes and was made by him from a corridor in a building in the third of the four districts.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty-nine days old and is warm and about four people came in on the Tuesday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The room is a room in a building in the third of the four districts with about nine chairs in it and the same furniture as every other room in this case, and there is no name on the door and nothing on the wall, and it was not a meeting because nobody called it one and there is no minute of it anywhere in this city.

Nine people were in it, and about four of them had been in it before this week for other reasons, and every one of the nine had a reason for being there that was on the four hours.

He came at about eleven. He had been asked correctly and he came on his own authority and he did not have to and everybody in that room understood that he did not have to.

**They had been expecting three things and they got none of the three.** They had been expecting a date, because four hours on a Friday with nothing after it is not an instruction, it is a fright. They had been expecting a reason in words they could carry to about nine people in a building. And about four of them had been expecting to be asked what their building could not lose, because that question is the one that has always been asked in rooms like this one, and it was not asked.

---

He was asked for a date three times in about eleven minutes and he did not give one on any of the three occasions, and he gave no reason on any of the three occasions, and nobody in that room has got a reason out of him since and nobody is going to.

**The sentence about a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it is the one sentence in this case that nobody has ever improved on. It was not said in that room on that morning. He did not say it, nobody asked him for it by name, and it is not printed in this entry, and there was a room in Crown Terrace in an earlier fortnight in which he said it to four people and it has been about him ever since.**

---

And then he said the other thing, and it took him about four seconds, and it is the reason the room was full and it is not what anybody came for.

"**An hour on that sheet is not a person, and I have written it as if it were, and that is the whole of what I have to say.**"

He sat down. He did not explain it. He did not take it back and he did not add to it and he did not say it again, and he has not said it again since and is not going to.

---

About nine seconds. Then the room did the thing that rooms do.

A man of about thirty-three whose job is a desk and a gate and a shift started to say something about the gate and the nine people who go past it, and got about four words out, and stopped, because the sentence had been said and a second thing said after it would be a second thing and everybody in the room could hear that it would be.

A man of about fifty-nine who owns four units in a building with about two hundred and forty doors said one word, which was *no*, and it was not a disagreement with the man of fifty-eight, and nobody has asked him what it meant and he has not explained it.

A woman of about twenty-nine who had worked out, on a Friday, in a fortnight, from about nine buildings, that the four buildings on the sheet were the four with the most doors on a list, said nothing at all, and afterwards, in a corridor, to a person with an ordinary job, in about nine seconds, said that she did not agree with about two thirds of it and thought the other third was the truest thing anybody in that office has said in about four years, and that both of those were hers and were not going to be improved on by anybody.

A woman of about sixty whose job is to ask questions did not ask a single question. She has not been asked why and is not to be asked.

**About four people in that room did not improve on it. Nobody thanked him. He has said he will not be thanked and he was not, and about four people in that room understood what it had cost him to say one thing in a room that did not want it, and not one of them said so, and he did not know that they knew.**

---

He has said two things in about four years. The second of the two was a question, on a Thursday in an earlier week, that a man of twenty-two said yes to. The first is the sentence in this case that nobody has ever improved on, and it is the sentence he was not asked for.

**This is not one of the two. Nobody counted it and nobody is to count it, and the count of two is not increased by this entry and is not to be increased by a later pass, and the third of the five things he has been asked about is one he refused in an earlier term, has not been put to him since, and is not going to be.**

---

He was in that room. He was in it for the whole of it and he said nothing and he was asked nothing, and he is the man of twenty-two and he is on no list in this city and four buildings in four districts found his name on a piece of paper about a fortnight before this morning.

He has said nothing about the sentence since. He has not repeated it, has not improved on it, has not written it down, has not told a person in a room off a service road, and has not acted on it.

**He has had about four days in which to act on it and has not, and the reason is not that he does not think it is true, and nobody in that room and nobody in this case has asked him what the reason is, and about four people in four buildings know what it is and none of them is going to tell him.**

---

A room off a service road was warm at about half past six that evening and about nine people were in it and one of them said a sentence at about eleven in the morning in a building four miles away and told nobody about it, and it was the fourth day of the rest of his week.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag said nothing and has said nothing to anybody about a doorway, and about four people know about the doorway.

**A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one thing, in about four seconds, to nobody, and it was about a door number, and it was not about any of this, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her, and she has said one small thing on most of the days this week and it has never once been about any of this and it is not going to be asked for.**

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*339. Wednesday of week 119, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one room, three questions about a date, one sentence of about twenty-nine words, and a count of two that is not three.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Five jobs attended. One room in a building in the third of the four districts, about nine chairs, the same furniture as every other room in this case, no name on the door, nothing on the wall, not a meeting and no minute of it anywhere in this city. Nine people, all of whom had a reason on the four hours. One arrival at about eleven by a man who did not have to come. One telephone call of about four minutes at about eleven in the morning, made by the man of twenty-two from a corridor in that building. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off a service road is three hundred and fifty-nine days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is three hundred and sixty-three days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and seventy-nine, thirteen two hundred and thirty, fourteen one hundred and ninety-five, fifteen one hundred and seventy-four, sixteen one hundred and forty-nine, seventeen one hundred and thirty-one, eighteen seventy-seven, nineteen fifty-five. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line and the newest is eleven words and is the only promise on it. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread. The master's fifth line is blank and the drawer was not opened. The Exchange sat twenty-one days ago on Wednesday, its book did not open, its telephone is at sixty-three, its count has been announced twenty-three times, and its next sitting is a Wednesday seven days from today.**

*The room, the nine, and what they came for and did not get.* **A room in a building in the third of the four districts, about nine chairs, the same furniture, no name on the door and nothing on the wall; not a meeting, no minute. Nine people, every one of them holding a piece of the four hours: a woman of about thirty-one who answers a telephone in a building in Crown Terrace; a woman of about sixty whose job is to ask questions; a woman of about twenty-eight whose job is a document and a four-line covering note; a man of about thirty-three, a desk and a gate and a shift; a woman of about thirty-eight and a file of about four hundred and eleven premises; a woman of about twenty-nine with the four buildings worked out from about nine doors; a man of about fifty-nine with four units in a building of about two hundred and forty doors; a man of about thirty-eight who keeps a gate; and a man of twenty-two who is on no list and was not asked anything.  Asked correctly, came on his own authority, did not have to. They expected a date, a reason in words they could carry to about nine people, and to be asked what their building could not lose. They got none of the three and the third was not asked. Asked for a date three times in about eleven minutes; not given on any of the three; no reason given on any of the three; nobody has got one since and nobody is going to. The one sentence in this case nobody has ever improved on was not said in that room and is not printed in this entry. Owner: the case.**

*The sentence, its length, and the four responses recorded as behaviour and not as speech.* **Speaker: the man of fifty-eight. Duration: about four seconds. Words: twenty-nine. He sat down and did not explain, retract, extend or repeat, and has not repeated and will not.  Silent interval before anything else happened: about nine seconds. Four recorded responses, in order. (a) A man of about thirty-three, gate: began about four words on a gate and stopped, unprompted; no second statement was made after the sentence and the reason is recorded here and nowhere else. (b) A man of about fifty-nine, four units: one word, *no*; not a disagreement; not explained; not asked. (c) A woman of about twenty-nine: nothing in the room; afterwards, in a corridor, to a person with an ordinary job, in about nine seconds, two positions held at once — disagreement with about two thirds, and a judgement that the remaining third is the truest thing that office has said in about four years — both hers, both refused improvement by anybody. (d) A woman of about sixty: no question asked, and none will be.  No improvement by anybody. No thanks to him, who has said he will not be thanked, and about four people in that room read the cost of the saying and did not say so and he did not know that they read it. Owner: the case.**

*The count of two, which is not three.* **Said in about four years: two. First, the sentence in this case that nobody has ever improved on, said in a room in Crown Terrace in an earlier fortnight, and not asked for on that morning. Second, a question, on a Thursday of week one hundred and eleven, to which a man of twenty-two said yes.  Of five things he has been asked about across the whole of that span, the third is one he refused in an earlier term, has not been put to him again since, and is not going to be. The utterance made in that room on that morning is neither of the two, is a third utterance and not a third sentence, and is not counted.  Increment to the count: none. The count after this record is two and a later pass is not to increase it. The rule is Movement III's and it holds. Owner: him, and he is owed nothing and is not to be thanked.**

*What the man of twenty-two did with it: nothing, for about four days.* **In the room throughout. Utterances: none. Questions: none. Repetitions: none. Improvements: none. Written down: nothing, on any surface, to anybody in a room off a service road. Acted on: no.  Days available before the week closes: about four. Days used: none. The obstacle is not disbelief and is recorded here as not disbelief. Four holders of the obstacle: a man of about thirty-three, a woman of about thirty-four, a woman of about thirty-one, a woman of about twenty-nine. All four are withholding. None of the four has been asked. Owner: the case, and he does not see it for about four days and is further behind at the end of them and not less.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nothing was taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception. Nothing was run by him, forwarded, or compared.  No name was attached to anything that happened in that room and no cause was named for it. The woman of thirty-one asked her question and he did not answer it, and the asking is not in this record. The printed rule was not invoked and was named in no mouth. The Exchange did not sit. The secondment stands; the chair of forty-nine has still not been told, was not told in that room, and is not named here. Heating on, costs more, the fortnight unpaid. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page is unread.  His appointment carries a reference, a count, a reason and no date, and no date was put on it, and the sentence about a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it was not said and was not needed. The standing question is not stated. The question written once on a service road in an earlier week is not answered, is in nobody's mouth, and its sentence is not printed here. No message arrived from anybody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 339.**
