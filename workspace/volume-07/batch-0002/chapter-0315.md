# Chapter 315 — On A Door

The Monday morning of week one hundred and thirteen was the morning a copy of a Continuity Office instrument was taped to the inside of a street door in the Lower Wards by nobody anybody can identify, and about nine people read it before eleven, and a woman of about thirty-one who delivers copies and collects signatures went round about nine doors with a clipboard and had to write a number at the top of her own sheet, and four people signed and four did not and one told her to go away, and the number she has to write is nine, and in a building she has never been inside that number is going to be read as a count of people in this city who agree.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Six jobs attended and one of them was a stair fire door in Nine Bridges for the twelfth time, and the twelfth was the same as the eleven before it and nobody chased it.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off the service road is three hundred and fifteen days old and is warm and costs more.

---

The street door is a door of a four-tenant building in the Lower Wards, one of the tenants being a workshop and one a kitchen and one a stairwell with a bench in it, and the bench was full at two o'clock on the Thursday of last week and there is nothing wrong with that building and nothing has been wrong with it.

The copy is one sheet, folded once, held at the top corners with two strips of tape from a roll that is not that building's.

**Nobody in that building put it there. Nobody in that building has admitted to putting it there and nobody in this case has asked anybody, and there is no form in this city that says who puts a copy of an instrument on a door.**

About nine people in that building read it before eleven, including a man of about forty who is on that gate in the morning, and one of them was the courier of twenty-one, who came past at about half past eight on his way to collect from four kitchens.

---

The courier read it standing up and then said one sentence to nobody.

"**I said in a room off a service road that I would not sign anything, and that was a fortnight ago, and I have not been asked to sign anything yet, and I would like that noted before anybody hands me something.**"

Nobody handed him anything. He went to collect from four kitchens.

---

A woman of about thirty-one came round about nine doors between about half past nine and about one. She has an ordinary job and an ordinary coat and a clipboard with her own sheet on it and a stack of copies. She is not a post and she is not a mechanism and she is owed nothing and she is not a villain.

At each door she said the same four sentences, which she had been given, and which are correct, and which are: this is a copy of a document that has been issued; you may read it; nobody has to sign anything; and here is my sheet and I am recording who has read it and not who has agreed.

Nobody improved on them and nobody thanked her and she is not going to be thanked.

---

**The cost, in her own words, at about one o'clock, in a doorway in the Lower Wards, to a man of about twenty-two who had come out of a building he was not called to and who asked her one question and did not ask her a second:**

"**There is one line at the top of my sheet and I have to write a number in it before I go back this afternoon, and the number is the number of people who have read the copy, and the line above that says what the reading was for. And when that sheet is in an office in a building I have never been inside, nine is going to be a number of people in this city who agree, and there is no column on my sheet for the four who did not, and I was not going to write one, and if I am not sure about that in about four years somebody else will.**"

He said that was the correct thing to say and would not be improved on. She said nobody thanks a courier either and she did not say it to be thanked and had said that in a car park in a different district a fortnight ago and did not know that anybody had heard it.

---

**Three people in this case were asked that day whether the order was right, and all three answered, and the three answers are not the same answer.**

**The man of twenty-nine who works on lifts**, in a stairwell at about half past ten, having been asked by a person of about thirty-one with a stack of copies and no authority to ask anything:

"**The count is right. The name at the bottom of it is not mine to do anything with, and I am not going to treat a page as a man in a room, and that is two sentences and I am not going to add a third.**"

He said he would be asked again in about four days by somebody in a building he has never been in, and that he would give the same two sentences, and that nobody is to tell him about the fourth sentence in advance.

**The man of fifty-one with a tool bag**, on a yard at about eleven, asked the same question by the same person, in about four seconds, and he had a stair door in that district and had not been asked to be there and had come:

"**It is.**"

One word. About four people in that yard heard it and nobody improved on it and nobody asked him for the second half and he did not offer it, and later, at about half past two, unprompted and to nobody, he said the other half and it was about nine lines in about nine buildings and one cook who has not been told.

"**That order does not mention them and that is correct and I am not going to tell her.**"

**The woman of thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors**, at about one, in a doorway, asked the same question for the third time that morning:

"**It is right and I am not going to help with it, and those are two different sentences and about four people are going to hear them as one.**"

She said that she said no to being a list of this in a car park a fortnight ago and has not been thanked and is not going to be, and that she is not going to be handed a list of it either, and that the difference between refusing a list and refusing a sheet of paper is that one of them takes an afternoon and the other one is somebody else's handwriting, and that she is going to carry on going to the doors, and that there is no column anywhere in this city she has ever seen for the doors she did not get to.

Nobody improved on any of the three. Nobody thanked any of the three.

---

**About nine people in this city who would have answered a telephone in the last fortnight were told what to do on Monday by a piece of paper, and every one of the nine has an ordinary job.**

A letting agent who rents four units and has had about four hundred enquiries about one of them in about four years. The maintenance line of a building of about eleven flats whose recording says the service is operating normally and has been saying it since Tuesday. A stair fire door on a docket, which is a company's and has the same fault number on it for the twelfth occasion. A woman of about forty-three who drives a mobile clinic and does the venepuncture. A crew lead of about thirty-four who went into nine sites this week and came back with the right count. A woman of about thirty-four with a printout of door numbers. A district office that keeps a count of buildings. A depot of four bays. And a room off a service road with a plate on the outside of it and no name on the plate, which was found on Monday by a document and not by a person.

**None of the nine has refused anything and none of the nine has signed anything and one of them has said out loud, in advance, that he will not sign anything, and that was said a fortnight ago and nobody has asked him since and one person has noted that it should be noted before anybody hands him something.**

---

A room off a service road was warm at half past six that evening and a first-year of nineteen read the copy that had come to that room, which is not the copy on the door in the Lower Wards and not the copy in the bay behind a workshop, and said one sentence about the arithmetic of it in about nine seconds, to the room and to nobody in it.

"**One woman wrote nine at the top of a sheet this afternoon and had to, and it is going to be read somewhere as nine people in this city who agree, and about four of the nine of us have been saying a number out loud for a fortnight that means something else, and she is the first person in this city who has had to make one.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. She sat down.

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

*318. Monday of week 113, at about half past six at a counter in a room with two pieces of paper on it, and this entry is one door, about nine readers, one number, and three answers that are not the same answer.*

Sheet: one numbered line at the top, a descriptive line above it, no column for the people who said no. **Cost, named aloud by her, unasked, at about one, in a doorway, to a man of twenty-two who asked one question and did not ask a second: that she must write a number before she goes back; that the number is the number who have read it; that nine in an office she has never entered will be read as nine people in this city who agree; that there is no column for the four who did not; that she was not going to write one; and that if she is not sure of that in about four years somebody else will be.** He said it was the correct thing to say and would not be improved on. Not thanked. **Second instance this fortnight of a person declining thanks, in a doorway and in a car park; the two have not met and are not to be introduced.**

One, a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, in a stairwell, at about half past ten: the count is right; the name is not his to do anything with; he will not treat a page as a man in a room; two sentences, no third.
