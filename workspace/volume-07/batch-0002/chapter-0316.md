# Chapter 316 — Four Correct Steps

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and thirteen was the morning a care-link in a first-floor flat in the Lower Wards, in place about two years, ran the way it is supposed to run, and could not be ended, and none of the people in the chain of it refused anything, and every one of them was doing their job, and there are four steps between a woman of thirty-one and a Tuesday morning with no charge and nobody coming, and all four are correct, and a woman of twenty-four wrote the whole of it down in about eleven minutes and nobody authorised her to.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Four jobs attended, one of them in a first-floor flat in the Lower Wards where a person of about eighty-one lives on her own, and the docket is a company's and the company is not the company that sends anybody.

The word was at nine and ten. The heating in the room off a service road is on and it costs more and the room is three hundred and seventeen days old, and four people came in for the temperature on the Tuesday and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

A care-link is a thing a person pays for and a company sends somebody to do. It is a telephone answered at night by a person who is not in the house, and who comes if it is rung, and who cannot be rung after about eleven at night on a Sunday.

**This one works.** It has rung about four times in about two years and somebody has come every time, and the woman it is for is at home in a first-floor flat and is not alone at night, and everybody in the chain of it knows that, and nobody in the chain of it is owed anything and nobody in the chain of it is a villain.

---

The woman is about eighty-one. She sews nothing now and she did nine years of it. She has a telephone on a table by the chair, and a card in a drawer with a number on it, and the number is the company's, and she has never had to use it because her daughter lives about nine streets away and has a key and comes in on a Sunday.

The daughter is about thirty-one. She pays about nine pounds a month, which is the current figure and was less than that when it started. She has paid for about two years. She has never missed one and there is no mechanism by which she could.

They came into a mobile clinic above a shuttered shop at about twenty past ten on the Wednesday, together, in the daughter's car, and the mother came because she is asked to come in the spring and it is in the diary on the card.

---

The woman of twenty-four is a resonance nurse and has run that room for about six years and holds no post and is owed nothing, and there were eleven appointments and about nine people, and the mother and the daughter were the fourth and the fifth.

Talia Venn asked one question, at about half past ten, in about nine seconds, and it was the only question she asked them.

"**What happens if you stop paying it.**"

The daughter said she did not know. She said that was not true and that the first half was true, and then she said the rest of it, in about four minutes, with the mother in the room, and the mother did not correct her once.

---

"**I ring the company on a Tuesday. That is the first step and it is correct and they would be within their rights if it were a Friday. And then they ask me whether she is safe at the moment, and she is standing in this room, and they ask me whether she is safe at the moment, and I have to say yes, because she is. And then it is not a cancellation. It is a request for a review. A review takes about four weeks.**"

"**And the review is about her.**"

"**The review is about her. It is not about the arrangement. It is not a question about whether the money is being spent well, it is a question about her, and it is nine questions and a person of about forty-four asks them, and they are good questions and I have not got anything wrong with them.**"

Her mother said one thing in that whole four minutes, and it was at about the end, and it was that she was not lonely.

"**I know that,**" the daughter said. "**I have known that for about two years. That is not what the form wants.**"

---

**The second step is the visit, and it is the step the whole thing turns on, and it is the step nobody in this case can argue with.**

The review is carried out on a Wednesday morning in the middle of a working day because that is when the reviewer is at that address, and the daughter is at work then and has been for about two years, and the mother is at home, alone, with the door open, waiting for somebody from a company.

The nine questions are about her. She answers them. On the two previous occasions in about two years she has answered them the way a person answers nine questions from a stranger on her own front step, which is politely and briefly and with an answer that is true and is not the answer a form wants, and on both occasions the record said *continue*.

**Nobody has lied in that record. Nobody has written anything that is not true. The record says that a woman of about eighty-one living on her own was asked nine questions by a competent person and that the arrangement is appropriate, and both of those are correct.**

The daughter has been told that twice. She has never been told anything else and has never been asked to appeal and has never been told that she could.

---

**The third step is a form, and the fourth is the number of reasons on it.**

To change anything, a person has to put a reason in a box. There are about four reasons available and one of them is that the person has died and two of them are about the person being unsafe and the fourth is that the arrangement is no longer required by the person who arranged it.

**There is no box on that form for a person of about eighty-one who is not lonely, has not fallen, has not been admitted, has not stopped eating, and whose daughter has not stopped paying.**

The daughter said that sentence in the room in about nine seconds and then said that nobody had put her there and that she had got there herself, and that she had got there because it is the only place the reasoning goes.

"**And the thing I am not going to be,**" she said, "**is a person who stopped looking after her mother. Nobody has said that to me. It is not on the form. It is in the four reasons and it is in my head at about four in the morning about twice a month and I have not said it out loud to anybody in about two years and I have said it out loud to you this morning because you asked me one question in a clinic and not in a corridor.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. The mother said the word *lonely* twice more and did not correct herself.

---

**And the cost of the whole arrangement, named aloud in her own words at the end, and it is the only cost named out loud in this entry:**

"**Nobody has refused me anything. That is the whole of it and I have been trying to say it since half past ten. Everybody has been correct. The company can be reached. The company answers. The person who comes comes. The questions are good. The form is the form. And there is no route from a Wednesday morning in this room to a Tuesday with no charge and nobody coming, and the reason there is no route is that the one person who is safe enough to make that call is the one person the call is about.**"

She said that and then she said, in about four seconds, that she had found out what a person intends to do this evening before she had found out how she is getting home, and that she had been getting it wrong for about two years.

The woman of twenty-four said nothing. She wrote it down after they had gone.

---

**Talia Venn wrote the whole of it down and nobody authorised her to.**

She wrote it in about eleven minutes at about half past three, on a sheet of her own, in the same words as the mother and the daughter, in the order it was said, and she did not summarise it and she did not put it on the board on the wall, which that morning carried eleven, nine, two, one, and which is the only record of that day anybody in this city can see without asking.

She put it in the clinical drawer in that room, which locks, and which now holds three sheets, of which two are the same kind of thing to each other and the third is not, and which is the single point of failure that a man of twenty-two named in a workroom four miles away a week ago to her face, and which she did not concede and has not conceded and has not told him has got a second thing in it.

**She did not telephone him. He was on a job until about two and then he was in a van for forty minutes and she knew both of those things without asking, which is one of about four things about him she has never asked for and has always had. She has not told him that there is a second sheet in the drawer and she is not going to this week, and the reason is not recorded in her mouth and is not to be asked for, and the reason is hers and the argument about the drawer is hers and unfinished.**

---

She said one clause out loud, at about four, to nobody, in about nine seconds, and it was about a cold room in an earlier winter and two women and a mother of seventy-nine and it was a clause and not more.

"**There were two of them and they were right and nobody thanked them, and it is not the same thing, and I am not going to use them to make a point about a form.**"

Nobody in that room heard her and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her and the two of them are not a case and are not to be gone and got and she has not gone and got them and is not going to this week.

---

A room off a service road was warm at half past six. About nine people were in it and there were two pieces of paper on the counter, one with a district office's mark on it and one with a reference in a form nobody in that room can read, and a cook of twenty-seven said one sentence about the two of them and went back to the sink, and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nobody thanked anybody.
