# Chapter 312 — The Seventeenth Sitting

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and twelve was the seventeenth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and the book opened, and one line went in, and the telephone moved from fifty-seven to sixty, and the book stands at forty-nine lines, and a man of about forty-four said a thing to the holder of the book to her face and it went in in about nine seconds, and about four minutes later he asked her for a rule she has given for about the ninth time in nineteen years and said on the last morning of the last volume that she had said it for the last time, and she gave him something else in nine words, and it was not the rule, and nobody in that room mentioned the promise.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Two jobs attended, neither of them in a building this case has a name for, and the day was in the middle of the week and nothing about the day was unusual in it.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off the service road is three hundred and ten days old and is warm and about four people came in for the temperature on the Tuesday and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The shutters were up in a room above a line in a building in Saltmarket and the holder of the room said so at the start, as she says at the start of every sitting.

Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in. The ninth was against the wall facing away from the room and it stayed where it was and nobody looked at it and nobody had to. There is a table, a tin that has never had a lock on it, and a book with four columns and forty-eight lines in it.

Nine people, and the man of about forty-four was the seventh time he has come, and there is a young man of twenty-one in attendance since he was seventeen who keeps a count he has never given her, and a young woman of twenty-one in attendance since she was fifteen who has never been asked anything and is not asked anything this morning, and about five others undescribed.

**Nobody wrote down that she was there. Nobody writes it down.**

---

She had not opened the book at the twelfth or the fourteenth or the sixteenth, and she said nothing about that this morning, and nobody in that room raised it, and the reasons for those three sittings were hers and two of them are not hers and none of them were in the room and none of them were asked for.

At about a quarter to ten the man of about forty-four sat down and said that he wanted to say a thing out loud to her and not to the room, and she said, in four words, to say it to her then.

Nobody else heard it. It took about four minutes and there is not one word of it in this entry, because the holder wrote it in the third column of a four-column book and nobody read it and nobody has read it since, and it is in this case only as a line, and a line is a line.

---

What it was about, in the room's hearing afterwards, in about eleven words, was that he has been the person people go to for about four years, and that he has said yes to all of it, and that last week he said no to one, and that the one he said no to was to somebody who had nowhere else to go.

**The holder of the book wrote the line in about nine seconds and did not read it back and did not summarise it and did not thank him.**

About four people in that room knew what the line was about and not one of them said.

---

Then, about four minutes later, he asked her for it.

"**You have given me that rule about nine times in nineteen years and you said last month that you had said it for the last time, and I have thought about it for a week and I am going to ask you for it anyway, and I would like you to know that I am asking so that I can tell somebody I asked.**"

Nobody said anything. The young man of twenty-one said nothing and did not give her the count he keeps.

She did not say it. She said, in nine words, and she said it to him and not to the room:

"**You already know it. You are asking for company.**"

Nine words. **It is not the rule. It is not a refusal of the rule. It is not the rule said a tenth time, and nobody in that room improved on it, and about four people in that room understood all three of those things at the same moment and did not say any of them.**

He said that was fair and that it was what he had wanted and that he was not going to say anything else about it. Nobody thanked her. Nobody thanked him. The young man of twenty-one said nothing, and she did not ask him, and she has not asked him in nineteen years and is not going to.

---

**AND THE THING NOBODY IN THAT ROOM MENTIONED, AND THE READER IS ENTITLED TO NOTICE IT, IS THAT SHE KEPT A PROMISE SHE MADE OUT LOUD IN FRONT OF NINE PEOPLE.** She said in the last week of a volume that she had said that rule for the last time and that anybody in that room who needed it after that morning could do without her. Nine people were in that room. A man of about forty-four was in that room and was one of the people who heard it, and this morning he asked and she gave him nine words that were not it, and the promise was kept, and it was kept in front of the same nine people, and not one of them said anything about the keeping.

She did not mention it afterwards. She has never once mentioned a promise.

---

The count came at about ten past ten, unasked, as it comes.

"**Forty-nine to sixty. Eleven in four weeks. The book is up one and the telephone is up eleven and the telephone was ahead of the book, and it was ahead four weeks ago, and it was behind the book before that, and I am telling you the direction and not the difference, and I am not going to look into it in front of nine people.**"

"**You have announced that count how many times.**"

"**Twenty-two. Seventeen of those are the seventeen Wednesdays this book holds and five predate it, and there is a sheet with the five on it that I have not shown anybody in nineteen years and am not going to on a Wednesday.**"

Nobody said anything. **Nothing was converted. A call did not become a line and a line did not become a sitting and none of the three became either of the other two, and the number eleven was not explained and was not asked about and the direction was given and the difference was not, and she has said the direction in four sittings running and has not looked into it once.**

---

**The last line in that book went in four weeks ago, at the sixteenth sitting, and it is twenty-eight days old. Nobody in that room gave an interval and nobody asked for one.**

In a converted shop unit in another part of the city there is a hardboard on two nails carrying nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names. It is a different object from that book, it is not in that room, and it has never been read in that room in nineteen years. Its newest line was written six days ago and is eleven words long and is a promise.

**The two objects are not to be added to one another by anybody. One of them holds what callers said to a woman's face and the other holds things people wrote down in a room and signed, and the difference between those two is the whole of what this case is about, and it is not settled in this entry and is not to be settled by anybody who was not in this room.**

The ninth chair was against the wall at the close and was not moved back, its mover is not named, and the answer about furniture was given for the ninth time and was not confirmed, because confirming it is a minute and she does not take minutes.

---

Nobody said anything in that room about either of the two things that were decided in a room in Crown Terrace in the last fortnight, and one of them was decided on Monday, and both of them are still true, and neither was asked about.

The printed rule is still named and restricted rather than repealed and about four hundred competent people in this city can still invoke it, and that number is not a conspiracy and is not on a list and is not reducible, and nobody in this case can produce it.

A Continuity Office man of fifty-eight still holds an emergency authority. **On Monday a name went onto a page in that office. Nobody in this room knows it, nobody in this room was told it, and no name of his is on anything in that room and none was read out and none was asked about, and this section records the two facts side by side because they are the two things that happened in that fortnight and neither of them is in the other.**

---

At about half past eleven she said one more thing, and it was unasked, and nobody had asked her for anything since about half past ten.

"**I am sixty-one and I have run this in a room above a line for nineteen years and I have never once been on a list, and I am not going to be on one in about four years either. And I said four weeks ago that I had said the rule for the last time, and I did not say it this morning, and about four of the nine of you noticed and not one of you has said so and I have noticed that all of you noticed, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it.**"

Nobody said anything. A young man of twenty-one said one word, which was that he had noticed, and she said that was not the same thing and he said he knew.

He has not said another and has not been asked.

---

A room off a service road was warm at half past six that evening and there were about nine people in it, and there are two pieces of paper on the counter, and one of them is a sheet with a district office's mark on it and a ruled line under the mark carrying no characters, and the other one is a document with a reference in a form nobody in that room can read, and the two of them are not the same kind of object.

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door from about two and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said one thing about the two papers and it was that a room with a piece of paper in it is a room that can be asked what it thinks, and she had said that in about nine words in a room in a different street about a fortnight ago, and nobody improved on it and she did not say it twice.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*315. Wednesday of week 112, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one book, one line, three calls, and nine words that were not the rule.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Two jobs attended, neither in a building this case has a name for. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off the service road is three hundred and ten days old. A card in a rail is three hundred and fourteen days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in the room above a line, and the two are not to be confused in one sentence. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and thirty days old, thirteen one hundred and eighty-one, fourteen one hundred and forty-six, fifteen one hundred and twenty-five, sixteen one hundred, seventeen **eighty-two**, eighteen twenty-eight, nineteen six. The seventeen is the oldest line on the Exchange hardboard in a room in Saltmarket by a distance and is a different hardboard from a different board and is not the one in a converted shop unit. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread and is not this entry's business. The master's fifth line is blank. There are two documents on one counter in that room and they are not the same kind of object and one of them is about him.**

*The four figures at the close, and the rule about them, and the thing that was said in place of the rule.* **Number: seventeenth. Gap since the last: four weeks, to the day. Day: a Wednesday, and she has not opened that book on a Friday in nineteen years and nobody asked her about that this morning. Room: above a line in a building in Saltmarket, shutters up, the holder announcing it at the start. Furniture: nine chairs in a curve, both ends turned in, the ninth against the wall with its back to the room and not moved back at the close; mover not named; the answer about furniture given for the ninth time and unconfirmed, because confirming it is a minute. Tin: never locked. Present: the holder, sixty-one; a man of about forty-four, seventh attendance; a young man of twenty-one, in attendance since seventeen, keeping a count he has never given her; a young woman of twenty-one, in attendance since fifteen, unnamed, unasked, given no reason, and not the first-year of nineteen of another building; about five others undescribed. The book opened. One line went in. The holder wrote it in about nine seconds, did not read it back, did not summarise it, did not thank him. The line is in the third column of a four-column book and no word of it is in this entry and it has not been read by anybody since. Book: forty-eight to forty-nine. Telephone: fifty-seven to sixty, eleven in four weeks. Announced count: twenty-one to twenty-two, of which seventeen correspond to the seventeen Wednesdays this book holds and five predate it on a separate sheet not shown to anybody in nineteen years. Four figures, printed and not converted: forty-nine, sixty, seventeen, and nineteen years. Direction given, difference not, and she said she was not going to look into it in front of nine people. The no-conversion rule was not given. About four minutes after the line went in, the man of about forty-four asked for it, having first said that he had thought about it for a week and was asking so that he could tell somebody he had asked. What she gave instead, in nine words, and it is not the rule and not a refusal of the rule: **you already know it, you are asking for company.** Not improved on. Nobody thanked either of them.**

*What was asked for and what was given, in the order, and the promise.* **Ask: the rule that no call becomes a line and no line becomes a sitting and none of the three becomes either of the other two, given for about the ninth time in nineteen years, given every time to somebody who did not need it, and given for the last time in the last week of a volume in front of nine people, with the holder adding that anybody in that room who needed it after that morning could do without her. Nine people were in that room. This morning, in a room of nine people, the same man asked for it again and said out loud why. Given: nine words, addressed to him and not to the room, and they were not the rule. Not improved on by anybody. **The promise was kept in front of the same nine people who heard it made, and not one of the nine said anything about the keeping, and she did not mention it, and she has never once mentioned a promise, and the young man of twenty-one who keeps a count he has never given her said one word afterwards and it was that he had noticed, and she said that was not the same thing.** Owner of this fact: the case. The reader is entitled to notice it and nobody in that room will.**

*The two things nobody in that room raised, and both of them are still true.* **One: the printed rule. Named, restricted rather than repealed, not withdrawn, not destroyed, about four hundred competent people in this city able to invoke it, that number not a conspiracy, not on a list, not reducible, and not producible by anybody in this case. Not mentioned at this sitting and not asked about. Two: the Continuity Office. Emergency authority retained and unchanged and held by a man of fifty-eight who agreed to nothing, was not defeated, was not turned, and is right about one thing, which is that a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it runs until somebody with a stamp puts a date on it. **On Monday, between the fifteenth and the seventeenth of this volume, a name was written onto a page in that office. It is not in this entry, no name of his is in that room, nobody in that room was told it, and this record places the two facts beside each other and joins them in no way.** Neither was raised. Neither was on a list, because there is no list.**

*What the day did not touch.* **He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing. No panel, no marker, no relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded. **No name was attached to anything that happened on this Wednesday.** The instrument is a document about a person and not about a cause, and no character in this case has connected it to a smell, a lift, a plant, a switch, a barrier, nine people on a bench or any of the nine accounts in a locked drawer, and no such connection is available to anybody who was not in four rooms this week. The printed rule was not invoked. The secondment is unchanged and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. The heating is on, costs more, four names on a lease cost more, and the fortnight is unpaid and unresolved. The hardboard was not written on and the nineteenth line is six days old. The woman's page in the binder is unread. **The standing question is not stated in this entry and carries no number. The question written once on a service road in week one hundred and eight is not answered here, is not in anybody's mouth, and its sentence is not printed in this entry.** No message arrived from anybody. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 315.**
