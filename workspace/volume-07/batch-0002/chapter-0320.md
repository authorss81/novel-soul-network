# Chapter 320 — No Date On It

The Thursday afternoon of week one hundred and fourteen was the afternoon a man of twenty-two went into a building in Crown Terrace that he has never been inside, on a telephone call from a person with an ordinary job, because on a Thursday evening in a room off a service road he had been asked whether he was going to be the one who is asked and had said yes, and he found that his own appointment has no date on it and said so out loud, and a Continuity Office man of fifty-eight said he knows, and then said a sentence this case has been carrying since a room in another street, and the sentence is about him now and it is true and nobody improved on it.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Three jobs attended and two of them were in the same building and one of those was a stair fire door for the fourteenth time and the fourteenth was the same as the thirteen before it.

The word was at nine and ten and reached eleven for about an hour in the middle of the afternoon. The room off the service road is three hundred and twenty-five days old and is warm and about four people came in for the temperature on the Wednesday and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved.

---

The building is a building in Crown Terrace with about nine desks on two floors and a counter at the bottom of the stairs and a smell of paper that has been warmed. He had been past it. He had not been inside it and he has never been in any building this office is in.

He was asked to come by telephone on the Monday by a woman of about thirty-one whose job is to arrange appointments and who read the request off a piece of paper, and she gave him a floor and a time and said that if the time was wrong she could move it about nine days forward, and he said the time was wrong and she moved it about four days forward, and she was correct and efficient and owes nothing and was not thanked and he did not thank her.

**Nobody made him go. There is no form requiring him to go and no list on which he appears and no minute of the asking. He went because of a yes, said on a Thursday evening in a room with about nine people in it, in about four seconds, to a man with a bad hip.**

---

The man of fifty-eight is in a coat that has never been in a workshop. He did not stand up. He said one thing, which was that he had about nine minutes, and he had a document in front of him that was not the instrument, and it was about a page and about a stamp and there was nothing in the room that looked like a meeting.

He is a person with an ordinary job and a stamp. **A chair of forty-nine is a person with an ordinary job and a stamp is a piece of metal and neither of them is a mechanism.** He agreed to nothing, he was not defeated, he was not turned, and he has never once been a post.

---

He was shown the record.

**The appointment is a paragraph on a page in a file that is not a form and is not a minute and is not a register and is not a list.** It is about nine words long. It says that the person named in the instrument is appointed as the point of coordination, and it says for the duration of the condition, and it says nothing else.

There is no date of appointment on it. There is no date of review on it. **There is no date on it at all, and the condition it runs for has no date on that either, because the condition is described in nine plain words with no beginning and no end and nothing on the second side of any sheet in this building that says when anybody will decide that it has finished.**

He read it standing up in about four minutes. He did not touch it and did not ask to and was not offered it.

Then he said it out loud, in about four seconds, to the room.

"**There is no date on my appointment.**"

"**I know.**"

---

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. Then the man of fifty-eight said the thing, and he said it in about nine seconds, and it is a sentence this case has been carrying since a room in another street in an earlier volume and it is the one sentence in this case that nobody has ever improved on, and he had said it before in a room in Crown Terrace and he has not been asked to say it again and said it now because a man of twenty-two said four words and they were the four words it is about.

"**A standard adopted as temporary with no date on it runs until somebody with a stamp puts a date on it.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked him. Nobody said it had already been said.

---

**And then he said the rest of it, which is about the man standing in the room and not about the sentence, and which is why this entry is the last entry in this movement and not a notice about a piece of paper.**

"**That is about me. It has been about the rule for about eleven years and it was about a rule on a wall in a binder and everybody in this case knew it and nobody improved on it, and it is about a person now, and there is a difference and the difference is that a rule can be invoked by about four hundred competent people in this city and a person can be asked about himself by about four of them.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**The rest of it is that I have a stamp and I am not going to use it, and I am not going to give you a reason, and the reason I am not giving you a reason is that the day somebody with a stamp puts a date on a thing then the date is the date the thing was noticed and not the date it ended, and about four hundred competent people in this city can invoke a printed rule about somebody who has not come back yet, and a date on a piece of paper is a date a person can be asked about.**"

Nobody improved on that either. Nobody thanked him. He said he had about nine minutes' worth and had used about four of them, which is the only joke anybody in that building has made in about eleven years and it was not received.

---

**And the cost, in his own words, in about eleven seconds, standing up, with his hands at his sides, and it is the only cost named out loud in this entry:**

"**On Thursday I was asked whether I was going to be the one who is asked and I said yes in about four seconds and I did not know what I was being asked for, and I have spent the fortnight being the answer to a question, and the answer has turned out to be nine words in a file with a condition on them and no date. So the thing I have is not the authority. I cannot make anybody do anything and I have not been asked to and I would not know how. The thing I have is that sentence, and it is pointed at me, and it is true, and it is the only true thing anybody in this case has said to me in a fortnight, and I am not going to improve on it, and I am not going to be able to put it anywhere.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody thanked him and he did not ask. Nobody asked him to sign anything and nobody offered him anything and he was not asked a single question in about nine minutes, and he has never been asked a single question in that building and was not asked one then.

---

He went down the stairs and out into Crown Terrace at about ten past four.

**He did not write it on a service road. He did not write it in the back of his own book. He did not telephone the woman of twenty-four four miles away, who is not in this entry and who is not to be asked about, and there is a reason and he has not got it and has not tried.**

That is the third thing he has put in his head instead of on a surface. The first was a lift, four lines in a book he carries. The second was a sheet with two places at the foot of it, folded once, in an inside pocket. **The third is a sentence that belongs on a hardboard on two nails in a converted shop unit off a service road, and the hardboard is nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, and no line is to be added to it this week, and the first line is a cook's and is still the first line, and the newest is eleven words and is a promise, and he did not go and put it up, and about four people in that room have noticed that he did not and not one of them has said so.**

Nobody thanked anybody.

---

A room off a service road was warm at about half past six that evening and about nine people were in it and there are two documents on the counter and the fact of an answer, and a cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all for about an hour, which she has done before and has not done for about a fortnight, and a first-year of nineteen looked at the door when he came in and said nothing, and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

The shutter came down at about ten.

*323. Thursday of week 114, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one appointment with no date on it, one true sentence pointed at a person, and a third thing kept in a head.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Three jobs attended, two in the same building, one a stair fire door in Nine Bridges at its fourteenth occasion, same fault number, same description, no fault findable, not chased. One appointment made by telephone on a Monday and moved about four days forward because he said the time was wrong, by a woman of about thirty-one whose job is to arrange appointments and who read the request off a piece of paper and who is correct and owes nothing and was not thanked. One visit to a building in Crown Terrace that he had never been inside, arriving at about ten past three, of which about nine minutes were in a room with a man of fifty-eight, and out into Crown Terrace at about ten past four. **The rate held at nine and ten and reached eleven for about an hour in the middle of the afternoon. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off the service road is three hundred and twenty-five days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is three hundred and twenty-nine days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The secondment is unchanged and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. The Exchange sat on Wednesday and its next sitting is in a fortnight.**

*The appointment, and what is on the page it is on.* **Page: one page in a file in a building in Crown Terrace. It is not a form and is not a minute and is not a register and is not a list and is not a report and is not a committee paper and is not a leaflet and is not a registration and is not a constitution, and the wall on two nails in a room off a service road is none of those either and the two are not the same object and have not been confused by anybody. Contents: about nine words. That the person named in the instrument is appointed as the point of coordination; **and that the appointment is for the duration of the condition.** Nothing else. Not present: any date of appointment; any date of review; any date at all. And the condition it runs for has no date either, because the condition is described in nine plain words with no beginning and no end, and no page in that building says when anybody will decide that it has finished. Read standing up, in about four minutes, by the man named, who did not touch the page and did not ask to and was not offered it. **He said it out loud in about four seconds: there is no date on my appointment. The man of fifty-eight said, in about four seconds: I know.**

*The sentence, the rest of it, and the difference, as a record.* **Speaker: a Continuity Office man of fifty-eight, in a coat that has never been in a workshop, seated, with a document in front of him that was not the instrument, in a room that did not look like a meeting. He holds a stamp; a stamp is a piece of metal; he is not a post; he agreed to nothing, was not defeated, was not turned, and is none of those things today, and has been asked to agree to nothing and has refused nothing and says those are not the same. The sentence, about nine seconds: **a standard adopted as temporary with no date on it runs until somebody with a stamp puts a date on it.** It is the one sentence in this case nobody has ever improved on. He had said it before, in a room in Crown Terrace, was not asked to say it again, and said it because four words had just been said that it is about. Then, unasked, about nine seconds, three claims: the sentence has been about a rule for about eleven years; everybody in this case knew it and nobody improved on it; it is about a person now. And the difference, which is the finding of this entry: a rule may be invoked by about four hundred competent people in this city and a person may be asked about himself by about four of them. Then, on being asked for the rest of it, nine seconds, and he gave no reason and said he would not: he has the stamp; he is not going to use it; the day somebody with a stamp dates a thing, the date is the date it was noticed and not the date it ended; about four hundred competent people can invoke a printed rule about somebody who has not come back yet; a date on a piece of paper is a date a person can be asked about. Not improved on. Not thanked. He said he had about nine minutes and had used about four, which nobody received. **The four figures at the Exchange, printed and not converted: forty-nine, sixty, twenty-two, nineteen years.**

Therefore: **what he has is not the authority** — he cannot make anybody do anything, has not been asked to, and would not know how. What he has is the sentence; it is pointed at him; it is true; it is the only true thing anybody in this case has said to him in a fortnight; he is not going to improve on it; and he is not going to be able to put it anywhere. Nobody said anything. Nobody thanked him and he did not ask. **He was asked no question in about nine minutes, and has never been asked one in that building. Nine were not asked: why he had said yes; what he thought the count was; whether he had read the condition; what he would do; whether he would refuse; what he wanted; who had told him; and what he would say if a person rang him. None of the nine is to be asked by anybody in this case.**

One: four lines about a passenger lift, written in the back of a book he carries, on a Monday in the early hours, not on a docket, for a reason given out loud in a room and not improved on. Two: a sheet with a reference, a count, a condition and two places at the foot of it, folded once, in an inside pocket, since Wednesday, on no surface of any kind. Three: a sentence of about nine words, from a man with a stamp, pointed at him, which is not on any surface. **He wrote none of the three down after leaving at about ten past four: not on a service road, not in his own book. He did not telephone the woman of twenty-four four miles away, who is not in this entry and is not to be asked about, and he has not got a reason and has not tried.** Destination not used: a hardboard on two nails in a room off a service road, which is nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added this week, none removed in nineteen years, the first line a cook's and still the first, the newest eleven words and the only promise on it. Not written on. Noted by about four people in that room and said by none of them. **The Exchange's four figures at the close of this movement, printed and not converted: forty-nine lines, sixty on the telephone, the count announced twenty-two times of which seventeen are the seventeen Wednesdays this book holds and five predate it on a sheet not shown to anybody in nineteen years, and nineteen years. No conversion of any of the four by anybody in this entry. The appointment: a reference, a count, a reason, and no date.***

*What the day did not touch.* **He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing, and was shown a page and said four words. No panel, no marker, no relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded. **No name is attached to anything that happened on this Thursday, and no cause was named for any of it, and the condition in nine plain words is a condition and not a person.** The Exchange did not sit and its next sitting is in a fortnight and it is the eighteenth sitting and the book does not open there.
