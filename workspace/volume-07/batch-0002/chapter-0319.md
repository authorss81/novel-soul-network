# Chapter 319 — The Place For A Name

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and fourteen was the morning a woman of about thirty-six with a shoulder bag went up the stairs of a building in the Lower Wards with about nine documents in it and gave one of them to a man of twenty-two on a landing, and he read it standing up in about four minutes, and it had a reference on it and a count and a condition and a place at the foot for a name and a place for a date, and he did not sign it and did not say that he would not, and she wrote down that she had given it and that he had not signed it, which is what she is required to write down, and he put it inside his coat, which is the second thing he has put in his head instead of on a surface and the first was a lift.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Six jobs attended, one of them a stair fire door in Nine Bridges for the thirteenth time, and the thirteenth was the same as the twelve before it.

The word was at nine and ten and reached eleven for about two hours in the early afternoon. The room off the service road is three hundred and twenty-four days old and is warm and costs more.

---

The woman of thirty-six has an ordinary job and an ordinary coat and a shoulder bag with about nine documents in it, and she is going up the stairs of four buildings in one district and down the stairs of two in another, and the work is correct and takes about four minutes a building and nobody has ever thanked her for it.

She does not know him. He does not know her. There is no list.

**She had about nine documents and about four of the nine people she has already given one to have signed, and one of those four signed with a date on it, and there is a place for a date on every one of them and about four of them have a date on them and about five do not and the reason is that the date is the date of the signing and not a date anybody is given.**

---

She was on the second landing of a building in the Lower Wards at about eleven, going up, and he was coming out of a flat with a tool bag of his own, and she said, in about four seconds, that she had one of these for whoever it was for, and that it did not have a name on the front of it and had a name on the back of it, and that she had been up and down this building twice and this was the last of the nine.

He said he would read it there and she said that was fine and stood on the landing, and a man of fifty-one with a tool bag was on the landing at the same time about a stair door and did not leave and said nothing, as he does.

---

It is one sheet. It is a copy of the same document with the same mark and the same reference in the same form that nobody in this case can read aloud correctly, and it repeats the condition in nine plain words and the count of about nine sites in four districts, and it says the point of coordination is the person named at the foot of the second side, and his name is at the foot of the second side, and it is the same name as on Monday of last week.

And at the foot of that sheet, below the name, there are two things.

**A printed line, and at the end of the printed line a space, and under the space a line that says a name. And a second line that says a date.**

It does not say by when. It does not say what happens if it is not signed. It does not say whether anybody will ask again.

---

He read it standing up against the wall on the landing. It took about four minutes and he read it twice and he did not sit down and he did not go into the flat.

"**There is a place for my name and a place for a date and nothing on this that tells me what the date is for.**"

"**No.**"

"**Do you know what it is for.**"

"**No. I am not authorised to know what it is for. I am authorised to give it, and to write down who I gave it to and whether they signed it, and to go to the next one.**"

"**And if I do not sign it.**"

"**Then I write that it was given and not signed, and I go to the next one, and I do not ask why, and I am not going to ask why, and that is not me being careful. That is the whole of what I have been given to do.**"

Nobody improved on it. The man of fifty-one with a tool bag read the sheet over his shoulder for about nine seconds and said one thing and it was that he had a door to do in that building and would be glad if the two of them were finished with the landing.

---

**He did not sign it, and he did not say that he would not, and the two of those are not the same thing and about four people in this case have been confusing them for about four years.**

He said what he was going to do, in about nine seconds, to the woman and to the man with the tool bag and to nobody in particular.

"**I am not signing it here and I am not telling you I will not sign it, because I have not got anything to sign it with and because the second of those is a thing I would be saying to a woman on a landing and you have nine of them and I have met about four of the people they went to.**"

She said that was the most she had been told by anybody that morning and she wrote none of it down, because she is not authorised to write down anything except the two things.

---

Then she said the thing that is the cost, in her own words, unasked for, while she was putting the other eight away in the bag, and it took about nine seconds and she said it to the stairs.

"**I am going to write down that it was given and not signed, and that is the correct thing to write down and it is what I was given to write, and I am not going to put anything else in that space. And in about four years somebody in a building is going to read a line that says a man did not sign a thing, and there is no column anywhere in this for the reason, and the reason is that nobody is allowed to ask me for it, so the line is going to be a line about a man who was asked and said nothing, and about four people are going to read it that way, and he did not say nothing.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. She said nobody thanks a person with a bag either and went down the stairs.

---

The sheet went inside his coat, in the inside pocket, folded once, and it is not in his book and it is not on a docket and it is not on a service road and it is not in a room off a service road and it is not in a ring binder and it is not in a drawer in Lattice Ward.

**That is the second thing he has put in his head instead of on a surface. The first was a lift that stopped between two floors with no door between them, in the early hours of a Monday a fortnight and a bit ago, four lines in the back of a book he carries and not on a docket, because a docket is a company's and a company can be quoted and a record with a name at the bottom of it can be read four ways by four people who have none of the context, and he said out loud in a room that the only thing he is frightened of is a piece of paper and has been for about four years.**

He has now got two. The first one is on paper. The second one is not on anything.

---

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag did a stair door on the second landing at about half past eleven and came back down past the two of them at about ten past twelve and said one sentence to nobody on the way past, which was that he had read the sheet over his shoulder and that it is a sheet with a place for a date on it and that he has been working for about nine years and cannot think of one job he has ever done where the date was the important part.

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked him.

---

**Four miles away, on the same Wednesday, a woman of twenty-four had a document of her own on her desk, and it had a place at the bottom of it too.**

It came from a district office and it was not an instrument and it had no reference in a form anybody can read and it asked the clinic to note that a condition had been described and to acknowledge that it had been noted. It was about nine lines and it was correct and it was the second one and there is a rule in that office about acknowledging the first one and not the second.

She put it in the drawer with the other two things, and the drawer locks, and there are now three sheets in it, and one of them is a single point of failure that a man named two weeks ago in a workroom and that she has not conceded and has not told him has got a third thing in it.

**She did not telephone him. He did not telephone her. Neither of them wrote to the other and there is no record anywhere in this case of the two of them having spoken since the Monday of week one hundred and eleven, and on that Monday she said that if it is a Thursday thing then it is a practice, and he said then he will not come on Thursday, and she said that was the first thing he had got right that week. Neither of them has been wrong since. Nobody has put either of them right and nobody is to.**

*322. Wednesday of week 114, at about ten at night on a service road, and this entry is nine documents, one unsigned, one correct line, and a place at the foot of a sheet for a date.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Six jobs attended, one a stair fire door in Nine Bridges at its thirteenth occasion, same fault number, same description, no fault findable. A stair door in a building in the Lower Wards, by a man of fifty-one with a tool bag, at about half past eleven, which is not on the docket and was not a job anybody asked for. A shoulder bag with about nine documents in it, four buildings and two others in two districts, about four minutes a building. **The rate held at nine and ten and reached eleven for about two hours in the early afternoon. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off the service road is three hundred and twenty-four days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is three hundred and twenty-eight days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and forty-four, thirteen one hundred and ninety-five, fourteen one hundred and sixty, fifteen one hundred and thirty-nine, sixteen one hundred and fourteen, seventeen ninety-six, eighteen forty-two, nineteen twenty. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread. The master's fifth line is blank and the drawer was not opened. There are now three sheets in a clinical drawer in a different building and a fourth document has not been put anywhere. The Exchange sat on Wednesday and its next sitting is in a fortnight.**

*The sheet, and the two places at the foot of it.* **Carrier: a woman of about thirty-six, ordinary job, ordinary coat, shoulder bag, about nine documents, four buildings in one district and two in another, about four minutes a building, correct, never thanked. Given on a second landing of a building in the Lower Wards at about eleven by a woman who does not know him and whom he does not know, and there is no list. Object: one sheet. Carries the same mark and the same reference in the same unreadable form as the document of the Monday of week one hundred and twelve; repeats the condition in nine plain words; repeats the count of about nine sites in four districts; states the point of coordination as the person named at the foot of the second side, and his name is there and is the same name. At the foot, below the name: a printed line ending in a space, a line beneath it reading *name*, and a second line reading *date*. Not on it: any date by which it is to be signed; any consequence of not signing it; any statement that the person named has been asked; any statement that anybody will ask again. Read standing up against a wall on a landing, twice, in about four minutes. **No second copy of this sheet is known to this case and it is not in any office this case has been inside.**

*What was written, and what was not, and by whom, as a record.* **Her wording, about four seconds, at the foot of a stair: that she has one of these for whoever it was for; that it has no name on the front and a name on the back; that she has been up and down that building twice and this was the last of nine. Standing figures: about nine documents, about four signed, one of those with a date on it, and a place for a date on every sheet, of which about four are filled and about five are not, **because the date is the date of the signing and not a date anybody is given.** Asked what the date is for: no. Asked whether she knows: not authorised to know; authorised to give the sheet, to record the name and whether it was signed, and to go to the next one; and if it is not signed, to write that it was given and not signed, to go to the next one, to ask no why, and that the not-asking is not care and is the whole of the instruction. Not improved on. Not thanked. When asked what she was told at the time, about four seconds: that it was the most she had been told by anybody that morning, and she wrote none of it, being authorised to write two things. She wrote the second of them. One of the nine buildings in this case has no copy of this sheet and this case has seen it once.**

*The not-signing, and what he said about it, as a record.* **Two acts, kept apart in this file and kept apart on the page. One: he did not sign. Two: he did not say that he would not. They are not the same act and about four people in this case have been confusing them for about four years.** His statement, about nine seconds, to the woman, to a man of fifty-one with a tool bag who was on the landing about a stair door and did not leave, and to nobody in particular: that he is not signing it there; that he is not telling her he will not; and his two reasons, in his order — that he has nothing to sign it with, and that the second of those would be a thing said to a woman on a landing, of whom there are about nine, of whose destinations he knows about four. Her answer, in about four seconds: that this was the most she had been told by anybody that morning, and that she wrote none of it down, being authorised to write two things, of which one is a name and one is a yes or a no. **She wrote the second.** Where the sheet then was: an inside pocket, folded once. Where it is not: his book, a docket, a service road, a room off a service road, a ring binder, a drawer in Lattice Ward. **Second instance in this case of a thing kept in a head rather than put on a surface. The first was four lines about a lift, in the back of a book he carries, not on a docket, for the reason he gave out loud in a room a fortnight ago — that a docket is a company's, a company can be quoted, a record with a name at the bottom of it can be read four ways by four people holding none of the context, and that the only thing he is frightened of is a piece of paper.**

*Her cost, in her own words, to the stairs, as a record.* **Unasked, while putting eight documents into a bag, about nine seconds, to the stairs: she will write that it was given and not signed; that this is the correct entry and the whole of what she was given to write; that she will put nothing else in the space. Consequence, as she named it: in about four years a line in a building will read that a man did not sign a thing; no column exists anywhere in her work for the reason; the reason is that nobody is permitted to ask her for it; about four people will read the line as a man who was asked and said nothing; **and he did not say nothing.** Not improved on. Not thanked. She said nobody thanks a person with a bag either and went down the stairs. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag, coming down past both of them at about ten past twelve, said to nobody that he read the sheet over his shoulder, that it carries a place for a date, and that in about nine years of work he cannot think of one job where the date was the important part. Neither of those two sentences is a fifth thing anybody has said and improved on, and neither is on any surface.

*Four miles away, and what neither of them did, as a record.* **On the same Wednesday, in a workroom in a different part of the city, a woman of twenty-four held a document from a district office with a place at the foot of it. Not an instrument; no reference in any readable form; about nine lines; correct; it asked a clinic to note that a condition had been described and to acknowledge the noting. Second such document that office has sent that clinic. There is a rule in that office about acknowledging the first and not the second. **Placed in a drawer that locks, which now holds three sheets, one of them a single point of failure named in a workroom a fortnight ago to her face, not conceded, and not disclosed to him.** Contact: none. She did not telephone him; he did not telephone her; neither wrote. **No record exists anywhere in this case of their having spoken since the Monday of week one hundred and eleven. On that Monday she gave the reason a Thursday thing is a practice and he undertook not to come on a Thursday, and she said it was the first thing he had got right that week. Neither has been wrong since. Nobody has put either of them right and nobody is to.** No scene, no declaration, no kiss, no milestone paid, and the words *we*, *our* and *us* do not occur in this chapter above the entry line.**

*What the day did not touch.* **He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing, and read a sheet and did not sign it. No panel, no marker, no relay, nothing run, nothing forwarded. The printed rule was not mentioned and about four hundred competent people in this city can still invoke it and that number is not on a list. The Exchange did not sit and its next sitting is in a fortnight. The secondment is unchanged and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. The heating is on, costs more, and the fortnight is unpaid and unresolved, and a room off a service road now holds two documents and the fact of an answer. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page is unread. **The standing question is not stated in this entry and carries no number. The question written once on a service road in week one hundred and eight is not answered here, is not in anybody's mouth, and its sentence is not printed in this entry.** No message arrived from anybody and a sheet with two places at the foot of it is not a message. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 322.**
