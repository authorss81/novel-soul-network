# Chapter 317 — The Second Sheet

The Friday evening of week one hundred and thirteen was the evening a second laminated sheet was taped over the face of the first one on a post in a company training room in Crown Terrace, with a mark on it that is not the company's, and a woman of thirty-six with a lanyard read it out because she is the one who reads things out in that room, and about four people in that room then had to decide whether a piece of paper from a building they have never been inside outranks a word they have been given in person for about four years, and all four decided differently, and the telephone did not ring, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. A shift in a training room is not a job and has never been on this docket and about four hundred of these Fridays are on no surface in this city.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off the service road is three hundred and nineteen days old and is warm and costs more.

---

The sheet is the same size as the first one. It has been taped over the face of the company's own laminated sheet on the post at the back, four corners, with tape from a roll that is not the company's, and the mark in the corner of it is not the company's, and there is no form in this city that records who taped a second sheet over a company's own post.

Nobody knows who put it there. A man of thirty-four who buys training for the company looked at it at about half past five and said two things about it, which were that it had been there before he arrived and that he had arrived at half past four.

---

The sheet is nine lines. It says that a condition described in nine plain lines is present across about nine sites in four districts, and it gives a count, and it says the point of coordination for it is a named person, and it says the following instruction, and the instruction is the whole of the document:

**That the end of a practice of this kind is to be confirmed by a call from the office named, and is not to be given in the room by an instructor.**

It does not say what happens if the call does not come. Nobody in that room asked and the sheet is not going to be amended and there is nobody in that room whose job it is to amend it.

---

A woman of thirty-six with a lanyard read it out at about ten past six, standing up, in the flat voice of a person reporting on a machine, and she read all nine lines, and she read it in a room of about eleven people and she has run that room for about four years and is not a mechanism and holds no post and is owed nothing.

She has asked four times in about four years for the word to be written down on that post and has been refused four times. The word is not on the first sheet and never has been.

She put the second sheet down and then said the first of the four answers, in about nine seconds, to the room and not to one person.

"**I have asked four times for one word to be written on that post and been refused four times, and I have run this room for about four years on my own voice, and there is now a sheet on that post with somebody else's mark on it and it is going to tell me when a practice ends. I am not going to do that, and that is not a refusal of the sheet, it is a statement that I have been here for the whole of the time it has not existed and I am not stopping.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. She did not give the reason she gave the last time she was asked for one, because nobody asked her for one.

---

**The four people, in the order they decided, and each of them is a decision and not an opinion.**

**First, a man of about twenty-seven**, on the company's list for about four years, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular:

"**A word is a person and a paper is a record. If the record says it, then in about four years a person can be asked what happened and there is something to ask about, and if it is my voice then there is nothing to ask about and I am the only copy. I am going to do what the sheet says.**"

**Second, the woman of thirty-six with a lanyard**, who had already said hers, and who was not asked again.

**Third, a man of thirty-four who buys training for the company**, at the counter, at about half past six, to about four people:

"**I said in this corridor a week ago that I am not going to be the man in the lanyard and I am not going to stop buying the training, and I have found out in about nine seconds that I was half right, and I am going to do what the sheet says for as long as the sheet is on that post, because I pay for the room and the room is mine to keep open, and that is not a different reason from the one I gave you and I am not going to pretend that it is.**"

**Fourth, a woman of about forty-one**, on no list, four years, who said the word in that room last Friday without knowing she was saying it, at about a quarter to seven, in about nine seconds:

"**I am not going to decide anything this week. Last Friday I said a word I had never been asked to say and I did not know I was saying it, and there is no way to do that on purpose, and I have thought about it for six days, and if the sheet says the end comes from a building in Crown Terrace then that is where I will take the end from, and if nobody rings then I will sit here.**"

Nobody improved on any of the four. Nobody thanked any of the four. **The four answers are: the record can be asked about later, so obey it; the sheet is less specific than the word it replaces, so refuse it; I pay for the room, so comply, having said I would not; and I cannot do a thing on purpose that I did by accident, so I will wait and see. They are four different reasons and about four people in that room could give a fifth and none of them did.**

---

The last run of the evening is the one everybody does at the end, and it is a hold with a count in it and a release that is supposed to arrive when somebody says so.

It went at about half past seven.

The telephone in that room is on the wall by the counter and it is a company telephone on a company line and it rang once, at about a quarter past six, and it was about a man's daughter and it took about two minutes.

**It did not ring at the end of the run.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A woman of about forty-one looked at the instructor. The instructor looked at the sheet on the post for about four seconds. About nine people in that room waited, and nobody has the words for what they were waiting for, and the woman of forty-one did exactly what she said she would do and sat there.

Then the instructor said the word.

She said it at the same point she says it every Friday, at the same speed, in the same voice, and about four people in that room did not expect that and about four did, and the run was over and about nine people got up.

---

A man of thirty-four who buys training said one thing at the door, at about half past seven, and it was not to anybody, and it is the last thing said in that room that week.

"**Nobody rang. That is not going to be in a record anywhere, there is no column on any sheet in this building for a call that did not come, and I am the only person in this room who knows it and I am not going to be the person who says so twice.**"

He put a card in the box on the table. He was in it about nine seconds. Nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him and he is owed nothing.

---

Nobody thanked anybody. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was in that room and has been in it for about four years and is on no list and said nothing at all and had said he would say nothing at all, and has not improved on that.

A room off a service road was warm at about ten that night and a cook of twenty-seven said one sentence about a piece of paper on a post four miles away and nobody in that room could check it and nobody asked her to and she was not thanked.

*320. Friday of week 113, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one sheet over another sheet, four people deciding four ways, and a telephone that did not ring.*

*Conditions and docket.* **A shift in a company training room is not on this docket and has never been on one. About eleven people in the room. Seven on the company's list, a card in a box on a table, unchanged for about four years; about four on no list at all, which is the standing figure and the number is on the back of one woman's own card in a flat in Nine Bridges and is on no form. **The identity of anybody who was not in that room is not established in this entry and no attempt beyond about four minutes is described.** The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off the service road is three hundred and nineteen days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is three hundred and twenty-three days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a room above a line. The hardboard: twelve lines two hundred and thirty-nine, thirteen one hundred and ninety, fourteen one hundred and fifty-five, fifteen one hundred and thirty-four, sixteen one hundred and nine, seventeen ninety-one, eighteen thirty-seven, nineteen fifteen. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread. The master's fifth line is blank. The Exchange sat on Wednesday and its next sitting is in a fortnight.**

*The second sheet, and what is on it, as a record.* **Object: a sheet the same size as the company's own laminated sheet, taped over the face of it on the post at the back, four corners, tape from a roll that is not the company's. Mark in the corner: not the company's. **Present before the man of thirty-four who buys training arrived at half past four. No form in this city records who taped a second sheet over a company's own post, and none was created, and nobody has been asked.** Contents: nine lines. A condition in nine plain words. A count of about nine sites in four districts. A named person as point of coordination. One instruction, and the whole of the document: **that the end of a practice of this kind is to be confirmed by a call from the office named, and is not to be given in the room by an instructor.** Absent from it and not going to be added: what happens if the call does not come; any date; any question at the foot; any signature; any reference. **Read out in full, standing up, about two minutes, by the woman of thirty-six with a lanyard, in the flat voice of a person reporting on a machine, to a room of about eleven people.** The company's first sheet is underneath it, its word is not on it and never has been, and the woman who has run that room for about four years had asked four times in about four years for one word on that post and been refused four times. Two sheets on one post; neither has the word on it; that is the whole of the difference between them.**

*The four decisions, in the order, as four records with four hands and four reasons.* **One, a man of about twenty-seven, on the company's list four years, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular: a word is a person and a paper is a record; a record can be asked about in about four years, and his voice cannot, and he is the only copy of his voice; he is going to do what the sheet says. Two, the woman of thirty-six with a lanyard, in about nine seconds, before the others, unasked, and framed as a statement rather than a refusal of the sheet: four requests over about four years for one word on that post, four refusals; about four years of running the room on her own voice; a sheet now on that post with somebody else's mark on it that will tell her when a practice ends; she is not going to do that; she has been there for the whole of the time it has not existed and is not stopping. **Nobody asked her for a reason and she was not asked for the one she gave the last time she was asked, and that one is not repeated in this entry.** Three, a man of thirty-four who buys training, at the counter, in about nine seconds: a statement made in that corridor a week earlier, that he is not going to be the man in the lanyard and is not going to stop buying the training; a finding in about nine seconds that he was half right; compliance for as long as the sheet is on that post, on the ground that he pays for the room and the room is his to keep open; and a refusal to pretend that is a different reason. Four, a woman of about forty-one, on no list, four years, at about a quarter to seven: no decision this week; last Friday she said a word she had never been asked to say and did not know she was saying it; there is no way to do that on purpose; if the sheet puts the end in a building in Crown Terrace then that is where she will take it from; and if nobody rings, she will sit there. **Four reasons, four decisions, about four people in that room could have given a fifth and none of them did. None of the four was improved on. None of the four was thanked.**

*The last run, and the call that did not come, as a record.* **Run: the last of the evening, a hold with a count, an end that is supposed to arrive when somebody says so. Time: about half past seven. Telephone: a company line, on the company telephone by the counter. It rang once, at about a quarter past six, about a man's daughter, about two minutes. **It did not ring at the end of the run.** Silence: about nine seconds, with about nine people in it. The woman of forty-one looked at the instructor. The instructor looked at the second sheet on the post, about four seconds. Nobody else moved. **The woman of forty-one did exactly what she had said at about a quarter to seven that she would do, and sat there.** The instructor then said the word, at the point she says it every Friday, at the speed and in the voice she uses every Friday. About four people did not expect it; about four did. Nine people got up. At the door, about half past seven, one sentence from the man of thirty-four who buys training, to nobody: nobody rang; this is not going to be in a record anywhere; there is no column on any sheet in that building for a call that did not come; he is the only person in that room who knows it and he is not going to be the person who says so twice. Card returned to the box: one, about nine seconds, unobserved. Not thanked. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was in that room, is on no list, said nothing at all, and has not improved on that. **One telephone, one ring, not at the end. Nothing was recorded, because there is nothing to record and about one person in this city knows it.**

*What the day did not touch.* **He taught, demonstrated, assessed and assisted nothing; he was not in that room and does not appear in this entry. No panel, no marker, no relay, nothing run by him, nothing forwarded. No practice was taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted by anybody in that room, and a company training room is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is the exception. The printed rule was not mentioned and nobody in that room knows the number of people in this city who can invoke it. **The four decisions were taken by four people in a company training room and no narrator recorded them and no load book of a practice exists in that company, and there is no form, no minute, no register, no report and no committee paper anywhere in this entry, and the second sheet is the only object in this case that has been put on a surface by an institution and is not a wall and is not a card in a box and is not a ring binder.** The Exchange did not sit. The secondment is unchanged and the chair of forty-nine has still not been told. The heating is on, costs more, and the fortnight is unpaid and unresolved. The hardboard was not written on. The woman's page is unread. The standing question is not stated here. The question written once on a service road in week one hundred and eight is not answered here. No message arrived from anybody and a sheet on a post is not a message. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 320.**
