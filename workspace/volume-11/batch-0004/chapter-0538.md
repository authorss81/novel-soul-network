# Chapter 538 — A Woman Of About Fifty-Eight Puts A Piece Of Paper In A Bin In A Town Four Days Of Bus Travel Away, And Nobody In This City Is Told What Was On It

**The Friday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a warm room from about half past six, and its shape was a bin at the back of a shop in a town four days of bus travel away taking a piece of paper out of the world in the ordinary way that paper leaves the world, and nobody here knowing what was on it.**

---

On the Friday, eleven were written down, eleven were given a day to be written against, and eleven arrived, the last of them at about half past five. The day was given over to a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and then to that counter until about six, and afterwards to the four rooms off the service road. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, and was not communicated to anybody. **The heat in that flat is on, dearer than in the winter, four names on the tenancy carrying the increase, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own money is owed to nobody and is not going to be paid at the end of this month.** There are four rooms behind that service road and a fifth behind them, and the whole of it is nine hundred and twenty-one days old, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and four days, and the heat is on.

A card has been in the rail on that counter front for nine hundred and twenty-five days, one hundred and thirty-two weeks and one day, four days beyond the rooms, and the rail holds two.

---

The woman of about fifty-eight has a shop and a fridge and a freezer and about nine hours of no cold behind her and about nine hours of that ahead of her, and she rang about four people on the Wednesday, and one of them is a man of about thirty-three who is not answering, and her number is not on anything in this city and it is not in a job ticket and it is not in a flat file and it is not in the head of any person who could carry it four days.

She was not rung on the Thursday. Nobody in this city was told about her on the Thursday and nobody has been told about her on the Friday, and that is not a decision anybody took and it is the fourth of the four things and the only one with no number on it at all.

**The piece of paper was about a third of a sheet, folded once, and it had been folded once for about nine years, and it had four lines on one side and nothing on the other, and it came out of a drawer in a shop that sells curtains on a street that does not go anywhere anybody goes on purpose, in a town four days of bus travel away.**

It is not the hardboard on two nails. It is not the card in a rail. It is not the room off the service road. It is not the master's fifth line, which is blank and is on a sheet of a woman's own in a second-floor room in another district. It is not a page of a book of four columns. It is not a page out of a ring binder. It is a third of a sheet of paper in a drawer in a shop that sells curtains, and it was folded once, and about nine years is how long it had been folded once.

**And on the Friday afternoon in that town a woman of about fifty-eight who has a shop and a fridge and a freezer and about nine hours of no cold behind her was clearing a drawer because the drawer had a runner in it that had come away, and the paper was in the drawer because the drawer had been used as a drawer, and it went into the bin behind the shop with about four other things, and the bin goes out on the Friday night, and that is the whole of how.**

---

Nobody knew what was on it.

A man of about thirty-eight in a town four days of bus travel away has a card in a flat file in a flat in a street where the bins go out on a Wednesday, and that is a different bin in a different town on a different day of the week, and the two bins have never been in the same street and no person has ever stood in both of them.

A man of about fifty-eight who has been the man behind a counter for about nine years kept a list of removals and gave nine sheets of it to about fourteen people in a room in another district on the Wednesday, and about four of the fourteen wrote on the back of a hand, and not one of them wrote a page reference, and the paper that went in the bin is not one of those nine sheets and does not carry the same hand.

A man of about fifty-three who fits plumbing, four days of bus travel away, has told one person in one cellar about nine words and has not told anybody else anything, and what he has not told anybody about is not on a piece of paper in a drawer in a shop that sells curtains, and if it were it would not be in the same town.

---

The man at the counter did not know any of this and was not told any of it and has not since been told any of it.

What he was told on the Friday afternoon, at about half past four, at that counter, was a thing that has nothing to do with any of it and that he will remember.

A woman of about twenty-nine came in with a box of about nine things and a piece of paper of her own, and she put the paper face up on the counter, and the paper was a printed form with about nine lines on it and a box at the foot of it, and she said that she had been given it by a man of about forty-four from a firm, and that he had said it was standard, and that she had signed it, and that she wanted it off her name, and that she was told at a counter four days of bus travel away that a man would be behind that counter on Friday and not the man who gave it to her.

**"What is on it."**

**"Nine lines about what we do not share with anybody, and a box at the bottom, and my name is in the box."**

**"Then it is not a form about not sharing. It is a form about the box."**

**"That is what I have been told by a woman of about twenty-four in a room and I did not understand one word of it and she did not explain it twice."**

**"Do you want it off your name."**

**"No."**

**"Then say why you are here."**

**"I am here because I signed it in a room where there were about nine other people and a woman asked me whether I wanted a copy and I said no, and it is a form and about nine people in this city can each say a paragraph out loud and none of them can put a thing anywhere. I am here because a man gave me a form in a shop and a woman gave me a room and I have two pieces of paper and about nine days' work between them and I have not been asked a question by anybody in either place."**

A man of about thirty-three came in at about a quarter to five with a bag of about nine things for a shelf he has not built yet and wanted to know whether the shop took work in on a Friday, and was told that it does not, and that the shutter comes down at about ten and has for about nine years, and that this is the fourth Friday he has asked and the answer is the same, and he said that is fine and he will stop asking, and he did not stop asking, and the man at the counter has a note about that man in his own head in the same place he keeps about four other things about four other men and it is the only note in this case that is not on paper.

Nobody at the counter said anything for about four seconds, and then he said the sentence, and he has said it in this city in about nine rooms now and it is the same every time and he has never found a better one.

**"I am not the person who can take a thing off your name and I am not going to invent a person for you, and what I can do is tell you that the sheet is smaller than what it is trying to hold and that is not a fault in your handwriting."**

**"Whose handwriting."**

**"Mine. On the back of that form there is a paragraph that any of about four hundred competent people in this city can say out loud, and not one of them has anywhere to put an objection to it, and that paragraph is the only thing anybody has ever written down about any of it, and it is on the back of your paper in a font."**

---

**What he wanted, and did not say, on that Friday, was a Saturday.**

There is one Saturday in the month and it is not his and about four people a week knock on the door of a shop front off a wide road and about one of the four asks him for a lift, and he does not have a car and has not had one since he was about seventeen, and on the Friday he worked out that a bus in each direction is about eleven hours and that a town four days of bus travel away cannot be reached and looked at and come back from inside one Saturday, and that if he went on the Saturday he would be a man who has gone somewhere for no stated reason and could not say why, and a man who cannot say why is a man somebody asks, and he has been somebody's question for about four months and is tired of it.

**So he did not go, and the cost is that a man of about fifty-two who does maintenance on four blocks in that town is still doing a job he cannot prove he was allowed to do, and a man of about thirty-eight with a bus ramp is still waiting to be told whether he may take the bus out, and a woman of about thirty-one is out of pocket seventy-eight pounds, and not one of those three things is his, and he has been in this city for about two years and has not once been in any of them and is not going to start on a Saturday in about four days.**

He did not take the form and he did not keep a copy of it and he did not write her name on anything, and she took it away in the box with about nine things in it, and he has not seen her since and will not ask after her.

**The one thing he decided on that Friday, and it cost him, was to tell her the truth about what a thing written on the back of a form in a font actually is, which is nothing at all, and which she then had to carry four days of bus travel back with, and she said thank you for it, and it was the only thank you on that Friday and it was for the worst thing he said all week.**

---

A man of about twenty-four came in at about ten past five and asked whether the shop had ever had a lift in a wall, and was told that it had not, and that the man he is asking for is the one who wrote down the addresses and is not the man who knows what is behind them, and the man of about twenty-four said that this is the third person in this city who has said some version of that sentence to him this month, and the man at the counter said that this is true and that in about four years there will be about four thousand people in this city able to say it and about nine of them will have a reason, and the man of about twenty-four said right and left with the bag.

At about half past six there were about four people in the warm room and the man of about fifty-one was at the north wall with his bag on the floor at his feet, five hundred and twenty-seven days, seventy-five weeks and two days, and he had not said anything.

A man of about thirty-three who works a weighbridge in the third of the four towns is in this city for four days and was not in that room, and he has been in this city once before in about a fortnight and was in a canteen off a market on the night of it, and a woman of about thirty-five with a bench off a court said one sentence to him the second time and has not said a second one.

The room off that service road is nine hundred and twenty-one days old, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and four days, four rooms and a back one, and it is warm. The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and twenty-five days old, one hundred and thirty-two weeks and one day, four days past the room, and it has not been moved and has not been turned over and the rail holds two.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines. The twelfth is eight hundred and forty-one days, the thirteen seven hundred and ninety-two, the fourteen seven hundred and fifty-seven, the fifteen seven hundred and thirty-six, the sixteen seven hundred and eleven, the seventeen six hundred and ninety-three, the eighteen six hundred and thirty-nine, and the nineteen six hundred and seventeen days, eighty-eight weeks and one day, and it is the only line of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. Not a line was put on that board on the Friday and not a line was taken off it, and a twentieth has still never been begun.

The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and sixty-nine days, sixty-seven weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one at the wall is five hundred and twenty-seven days, seventy-five weeks and two days. The man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and fifty-four days into a hold of four buildings and about nine hundred doors, seventy-nine weeks and one day, and nobody has asked him anything. The ask is six hundred and eleven days, eighty-seven weeks and two days, from the day it was asked. The distance is three hundred and one days, forty-three weeks to the day, and it is not shorter and it is not ended.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
