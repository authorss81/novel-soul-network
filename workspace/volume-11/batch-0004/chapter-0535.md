# Chapter 535 — A Man Of About Fifty-Eight Who Has Made A Question Smaller Four Times A Week For Nine Years, Sitting In A Room And Giving It Back To About Eleven People

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a first floor above a shop in another district from about half past six for one hour, and its shape was a man who has never been asked anything in nine years telling about eleven strangers exactly what he did with each of their questions, one at a time, in a face, and nobody thanking him for it.**

---

The Monday's page carries eleven names, a date against each of the eleven, and eleven people through that door, the last at about half past five. The bench until two, that counter from two to six, the four rooms off the service road from about half past six, and one hour on a first floor in another district after that. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, and did not go to a living person. **The heat in that flat is on, dearer, four names on the tenancy carrying the increase, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own money is owed to nobody and is not going to be paid this month.** The rooms off that road are nine hundred and seventeen days, one hundred and thirty-one weeks to the day, four rooms and a back one, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and twenty-one days, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and four days, four days more than the room, and the rail holds two.

---

The room is above a shop that sells hose and couplings and has been there for about forty years, and it is reached by a stair at the back and it holds about sixteen chairs and it was not hired for anything in particular. A man of about fifty-eight had paid for the hour on the Friday, in cash, in a note of a fixed sum, and had told nobody else in advance what the hour was for, and about eleven people had come.

They had come because each of them had been told by somebody else that there would be a room, and not one of them had been told what the room was for, and when they got there the man of about fifty-eight was sitting at the front with a sheet of paper and about eleven chairs and eleven people and nobody else.

**"I am going to say what this hour is and then I am going to do it, and I am not going to ask anybody a question, because I have spent nine years asking people questions that were not the right size and this hour is the other thing."**

**"Then what is the hour."**

**"It is me giving back."**

He was about fifty-eight and he had a soft voice and a coat that had been brushed that morning, and everything he said that evening he said once and did not improve the second time, and about nine of the eleven people in that room had spoken to him before and got less than they came for.

---

**"I am going to go down the room and I am going to say the thing I did to your question and I am going to say the date if I know it, and if I do not know the date I am going to say that I do not know the date, and I am not going to apologise, because an apology is a smaller question and I have had enough of those."**

The first person was a man of about forty-four who had asked, in a corridor, in a week that is not this one, whether a certain sheet stayed where it was.

**"You asked me whether a sheet stays. I asked you whether you had read it. That is a smaller question and it is four words and it is about nine seconds long, and I have asked about four hundred people that question and I have asked it four times a week for about nine years."**

**"Why."**

**"Because a sheet that a person has not read is a sheet I can put back, and a sheet a person has read is a sheet they own, and if they own it I have to be the sort of office that can be argued with. I did not want to be that. I want to say that out loud in a room with about eleven people in it and then never again."**

The man of about forty-four said nothing, and the man of about fifty-eight said the date, and it was wrong by about a week, and he said so, and moved on.

---

The second was a woman of about thirty-four who had asked in a first-floor corridor on a Wednesday whether a thing she had been asked to do could be undone, and had been asked, in reply, how long she had been doing it.

**"I did not say how long you had been doing it. I said how long it had been since you were asked. That is not the same question and it is four words shorter, and I have never once been asked to explain the difference and I have wanted about four hundred people to ask me to explain the difference."**

**"What is the difference."**

**"How long you have been doing it is about you. How long it has been since you were asked is about me. I made it about me and you went away with a fact about me in your hands instead of a fact about you."**

**"That is what happened."**

**"Then it happened about four times a week for nine years and I am telling eleven people at once because I have worked out that saying it to one of you at a time is how it got to nine years."**

---

The third, fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, tenth and eleventh were the same shape and none of them was the same, and the shape was this: somebody had asked a question, and a smaller question had been put back, and the smaller question had been made about the office rather than about the person, and the person had gone away with something that was true and was not what they asked for.

The man of about fifty-eight did not soften any of them and did not defend any of them and did not once say that he had been protecting anybody. He gave dates where he had them and said he did not know them where he did not, and about four of the eleven had written nothing and about seven had written something on the back of a hand.

**"That is the whole of the hour. You have got it before I have said any of it to anybody, which is nine years earlier than anybody else in this city has got it, and that is the only thing I have ever wanted out of an hour and I have never once been able to ask for it because asking for it is a question and I make the questions."**

---

At about ten past seven he stopped, and a woman of about thirty-one who had not said anything for the whole hour said one thing, and it was not kind.

**"You have just told eleven people that you made the questions smaller on purpose for nine years. In a room with no minutes and no list and nobody's name on anything. And if one of us tells anybody, it is your word against ours, and you know that, and you have known it for nine years, and you have just done it in a room for an hour on a Monday."**

**"Yes."**

**"Why."**

**"Because I am fifty-eight and I have a coat and a room and a job, and in about four years I will have none of the three, and I would like one thing to have been said by me while I still had all of them, and I have thought about that for about four days and I have not found a better reason and I am not going to invent one."**

Nobody thanked him. The man at the counter had said on the way in that nobody was going to thank him, and nobody did, and he was not thanked afterwards, and the eleven people left between about ten past seven and about half past seven and did not stay to talk to each other in the stair.

---

The hour had cost the room over a pound and change and had cost him nothing else that anybody could put a figure on, and he paid it in a note and he went down the back stair of a shop that sells hose and couplings, and the man at the counter walked with him to the bottom of it because they happened to be going the same way, and neither of them said anything for the whole of the stair.

At about half past seven there were about four people in the warm room off the service road and the man of about fifty-one was at the north wall with his bag on the floor at his feet, five hundred and twenty-three days, seventy-four weeks and five days, and he had not said anything on the Monday and had not been in the other building and had not been told about the hour.

The room off that service road is nine hundred and seventeen days old, one hundred and thirty-one weeks to the day, four rooms and a back one, and it is warm. The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and twenty-one days old, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and four days, four days past the room, and it has not been moved and has not been turned over and the rail holds two.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines. The twelfth is eight hundred and thirty-seven days, the thirteenth seven hundred and eighty-eight, the fourteenth seven hundred and fifty-three, the fifteen seven hundred and thirty-two, the sixteen seven hundred and seven, the seventeenth six hundred and eighty-nine, the eighteenth six hundred and thirty-five, and the nineteenth six hundred and thirteen days, eighty-seven weeks and four days, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. Nothing was added to that board on the Monday and nothing was taken from it and there is no twentieth line.

**What he wanted, and did not say, on the Monday, was to be the eleventh person in that room, and to have asked a question, and to have had it made smaller.**

He has been a person in about nine rooms in this city in about four months and every time he has opened his mouth somebody has said the sentence, and on the Monday he stood on a back stair of a shop that sells hose and couplings and worked out that this was the one room in the city where nobody was going to make his question smaller, and that the reason was a man of fifty-eight with a brushed coat who has spent nine years making other people's questions smaller and paid for the room out of a note in order to be the one who did not.

He did not go back up. He had already asked the man for nothing in a shop on the Friday and had been given nothing, and the man told him on the stair that nobody was going to thank the man of fifty-eight, and he said that he knew, and there is a second thing a man could have said in that stair and he did not say it, and it would have been the sentence he says at the counter to about four people a week, and that sentence is the wrong sentence for a stair, and he knows that it is the wrong sentence for a stair, and he said nothing at all instead, and got a bus.

The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and sixty-five days, sixty-six weeks and three days. The man of about fifty-one at the wall is five hundred and twenty-three days, seventy-four weeks and five days. The man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and fifty days into a hold of four buildings and about nine hundred doors, seventy-eight weeks and four days, with no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority, and nobody has asked him anything. The ask is six hundred and seven days, eighty-six weeks and five days, from the day it was asked. The nine copied fronts of a page are four hundred and eighty-three days, sixty-nine weeks to the day, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found. The distance is two hundred and ninety-seven days, forty-two weeks and three days, and it is not shorter and it is not ended.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
