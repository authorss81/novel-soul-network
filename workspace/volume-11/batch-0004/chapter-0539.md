# Chapter 539 — The Thirty-Ninth Sitting Of A Room Above A Line, And A Stranger Asked Her A Question And She Answered It And It Was Not A Line

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and an hour in a second-floor room in another district, and its shape was nine items at four minutes each and a tenth thing that was not an item, in which a stranger asked the woman who keeps the book a question in a face and she answered it and did not write it down.**

---

Eleven jobs are listed for the Wednesday, eleven of them have a date against them, and eleven people came into that shop, the final one of them at about half past five. Bench work in Lattice Ward to two, the district counter two to six, an hour above a line in another district in the morning, and the service-road rooms in the evening. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, and went to nobody. **The heating in that flat is running, dearer than in the winter, with four names on the tenancy carrying the rise, and a fortnight that a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and will be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that road are nine hundred and twenty-six days, one hundred and thirty-two weeks and two days, four rooms and a back one, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and thirty days, one hundred and thirty-two weeks and six days, four days more than the room, and the rail holds two.

---

About eleven people came up the stair. Six had been in the same chair on the last Wednesday and five had not, and one of the five had not been in that room in nineteen years and had come for a reason he gave.

Nine chairs went into a curve with the two ends turned in, and the tenth chair was hard against the wall with its back to the whole room. It did not move in the hour. Nobody looked at it more than once. Whoever moves it is not named here and is not going to be.

There was a table, an unlidded tin with no lock on it and never one, and the book face up inside her right hand's reach from about ten until about eleven.

Nine items, four minutes each, and eight of them went to the chairs the way eight of them had gone for nineteen years.

**The tenth thing was not an item. It was a man of about twenty-eight who came up that stair with a coat he did not take off, and who had not been in that building before and had not come for a Wednesday, and who stood at the end of the table for about nine minutes and then asked her a question in a face, and she answered it, in about nine seconds, out loud, and she did not write it down.**

---

**"Are you the one who keeps the book."**

**"I am."**

**"Then I have not got a thing for the book. I want to be straight about that before I start, because I can see there are about nine people here with about nine things and I have not got one."**

About four people in that room looked up and about nine of them did not.

**"Say the question."**

**"Last month a man of about forty-four came round my door with a form about a thing in my wall, and I signed it, and my name is in a box at the bottom of it, and he said it was standard."**

**"Was it read to you."**

**"No."**

**"Did anybody ask you whether you understood it."**

**"No."**

**"Then say the rest of it."**

**"I have brought it down here four days on a bus because I did not know what to do with it and because a man at a counter told me to bring it here and then told me a fortnight ago that he is not the person who may take a thing off a name, and I have been carrying it for about four days and I have not opened it once and I brought it up a stair to be in a room with about nine people in it."**

**"Where is the form."**

**"In a bag, downstairs, and I have not got it with me because I was told not to bring paper into this room and I have obeyed that without knowing why."**

**"That was not me."**

**"I know it was not you, which is why I am asking you."**

---

**And she answered him, out loud, in a face, in about nine seconds, and about nine people in that room heard it and it went in no book.**

**"You were not asked. A person was asked and you were not asked, and there is nothing in this city that can put that right today, and the person who gave you that form is not a villain and is doing the correct thing for the company that pays him, and both of those are true at once and I am the only person in this city who is paid to say both of those out loud and it takes nine seconds and it does not change your name."**

The man of about twenty-eight looked at her for about four seconds and then said, and it was the only thing he said in that room:

**"That is the first time anybody has said the second part."**

**"What second part."**

**"The part about him not being a villain. Everybody who has said anything to me about it has made him into somebody."**

Nobody in that room said thank you. The man of about twenty-eight went down the stair at about eleven and did not speak on the landing and did not speak in the street, and the form is still in a bag in a town four days of bus travel away, and the name is still in a box at the foot of it, and nobody in this city is going to take it off for him, because there is nobody in this city who may.

---

**The book did not open, and the reason is printed and it is not a rule and it is not evidence of anything: nothing was said to her face. Nine things went to nine chairs and the tenth thing was said to her and it was answered by her and it was a question and an answer and neither of them was a thing anybody came with.** She has said twice in nineteen years what a thing said to her would sound like, and on both of those mornings the book also stayed shut, and nobody on that landing has ever been told in advance which mornings it will be in, and it is not written on the landing, and it is not in the four columns, and that sequence of sittings is not a rule and is not going to be described by anybody as a change in her.

**The count was announced at the end and it is announced at a sitting and on no other day, and it came out at forty-four, of which thirty-nine correspond, and thirty-nine of those correspond to the thirty-nine Wednesdays this book holds and five of them predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody. On the nine days before this one the count was forty-three of which thirty-eight, and the two states are two states and not one state, and the difference between them is a count and not a distance.**

**No number went into the tin, and the tin stands at seventy-three, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it has not moved. The book stands at fifty-nine lines, which is what it stood at before the morning, and fifty-nine is what it will stand at on the nine days after this one until a sitting, and the book is a number and the tin is a number and the count is a number and none of the three turns into either of the others, and the difference between the first two is not a number and was not said out loud in that room on that Wednesday and is not said here.**

Nobody asked her why it stayed shut. Nobody asked her whether she was all right, and that has not been asked in that room in nineteen years.

---

**What he wanted, and did not say, on that Wednesday, was the second chair.**

There was a chair beside her at that table and nobody sat in it, and it is the same chair a man of about twenty-nine sat in four weeks ago after saying twenty-one words across it, and the man at the counter had a piece of paper in his coat that was the other half of a receipt and he did not think about the chair at all, and then he thought about the chair for about nine seconds on a stair and about four seconds at the counter and about nine minutes at a bench in Lattice Ward, and in all three of those places he decided not to go up the stair and sit in it, and in none of the three places was there anybody to see him not do it.

**The cost, named exactly: if he sits in the second chair then the room is a room with a second thing in it, and a room with a second thing in it is a room that is waiting for him, and a room that is waiting for a man of twenty-two is a room that will hold for him whatever the woman in it says at about eleven, and the woman in it has said out loud on a Tuesday and again on a Thursday that she will not allow that, and the only way to allow it is for him to be somewhere else, and he is somewhere else.**

He was at the bench until about two and at that counter until about six, and about four people came in between two and about two and a half and about four of them were told no.

A woman of about thirty-four who reads meters and works nights came in at about ten past five on the Wednesday and asked him one question, and it was not about a form, and it was whether the man of about fifty-eight was all right, and he told her that he had put nine sheets on a table in a room in another district on the Wednesday evening and had named what it cost him out loud and that the cost was a job on the Friday, and she said that she had heard, because a woman of about thirty-four who had been in that room said it on a bus and has been saying it in her own way ever since, and he said that he knew, and she said that she was not asking about the sheets.

**"Then ask me about the sheets."**

**"I asked whether he was all right, and he is a man of about fifty-eight who liked a job and said so in front of about fourteen people, and that is the first thing anybody has said about him in this city in about four years that was not a small question, and I am not going to be the person who makes it one."**

**"That is the second time this month somebody has done that for him."**

**"It is and I have not decided whether it is kind."**

The first of the four was a man of about forty-four who wanted to know what the room off a wide road would do with a form of his own, and had heard about it from a woman of about fifty-eight who had been in a town four days of bus travel away and had come back with a different story, and he had a piece of paper in his hand about the size of a postcard with a box at the foot of it.

**"I have not been inside it."**

**"I have not either and I write things down for people sometimes."**

**"Then I am going to be a person who asks a stranger to read a form for him and I want you to understand that I know what I am doing."**

**"I understand it and I am going to say the thing I say, which is that the person who may read that for you is a woman of about twenty-four and she is not behind this counter and there is nothing I can do about that."**

**"Then can you write on the back of it."**

**"No. The last time somebody wrote a thing on the back of a form in this city it went in a font and it was not hers and it did not put anybody anywhere, and I would rather you carried it four days than that I did it in about four seconds."**

**"I have carried it four days already."**

**"Then carry it four more and do not sign anything on the way."**

The man of about forty-four said right, and put the card back in his pocket, and did not say thank you, and it is the same card that a man of about thirty-eight has been carrying in an inside pocket in a coat since the spring, and it is not that card, and neither of those two men has ever been told about the other.

**He did not write the address on a job ticket for the man of about forty-four, and the reason is that the fourth piece of paper he wrote in about a fortnight was handed over by hand on the Friday before last to a man of about thirty-four who mends pumps and was not put in a drawer and was not read out in a room, and he could not do the same thing twice in a week and call it the same decision.**

---

At about half past six there were about four people in the warm room for the temperature and one of them was the man of about fifty-one, who had been at the north wall for five hundred and thirty-two days, seventy-six weeks to the day, and who had not said anything, and whose bag was on the floor at his feet.

The man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors is five hundred and fifty-nine days into a hold, seventy-nine weeks and six days, in another city, with no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority, and nothing asked of him and nothing given to him, and he was not in this city on the Wednesday and will not be on the Friday.

The ask is six hundred and sixteen days, eighty-eight weeks to the day, counted from the day it was asked. The newest of the nineteen lines on two nails is six hundred and twenty-two days, eighty-eight weeks and six days, and it is the one line of that board that has ever ended on a zero. The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and seventy-four days, sixty-seven weeks and five days. Nine copied page fronts are four hundred and ninety-two days into the run, which is seventy weeks and two days, and the three hundred and six days between him and the office in another district are forty-three weeks and five days. He knew both figures to the day and was in another district and did not go up the stair to sit in the second chair, and there was a man of about twenty-eight in that chair at about eleven and the man did not know he was there.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
