# Chapter 533 — A Third Thing, And A Number Nobody Chose To Make

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a warm room from about half past six, and its shape was a third of the four things happening at once in four towns, and one person in this city working out what the interval between asking and being told actually is, and not rounding it, and reading the result to a man with a pencil.**

---

Eleven on the docket, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last at about five forty. The morning at the bench in Lattice Ward, the four hours at that counter, and the warm rooms off the service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, and was not said to a living soul. **The heat in that flat is on, dearer than in the winter, four names on the tenancy are carrying it, and a fortnight a cook put out of her own money in the winter is owed by nobody and will be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** Behind that service road there are four rooms and a fifth behind them, and they are nine hundred and thirteen days old, which comes to one hundred and thirty weeks and three days, and the heat in them is on.

The card across the front of the counter has been in its rail for nine hundred and seventeen days, which is one hundred and thirty-one weeks to the day, and that is four days past the age of the rooms, and the rail takes two.

---

The man of about forty-four with the pencil came in at about a quarter to four on the Thursday and he had asked for a card of paper four days ago and had been given one, and this is the whole of the arrangement between them.

**"I have got three."**

**"You said two on Monday and one on Tuesday and now you have got three."**

**"I have got three and they are not the same three. There is a lift in a block in the first town, a ramp on a bus in the second, and a man of about twenty-nine who is not answering."**

**"Say the third one."**

**"A woman of about fifty-eight who runs a shop in the third town. She has had a fridge and a freezer and no cold for about nine hours and she has rung about four people and nobody has come, and she has not rung you because she does not have your number and she does not have anybody's."**

**"Then it is not in your three. It is in your four, and you have never met her."**

**"I have never met her and I have written her down, which is more than you have done, and I would like to say so exactly once and then never again."**

Nobody at the counter said anything for about four seconds, and the man with the pencil waited, and then he said the thing he had come to say.

---

**"I want to give you a number and I want you to write it down, and I want it to be the number and not a round thing, and I am going to be about nine minutes and you are going to hate the first four of them."**

**"Give me the number."**

**"I cannot, because the number is a sum of six numbers and none of the six is the number. A person asks at one time and somebody in this city is told at another time, and the difference is the thing, and the difference is different every time and I have three of them and I want the fourth."**

**"Then give me the three."**

He put the card on the counter and turned it round, and it was the back of a job ticket and there were three lines on it in pencil, and the man at the counter read them.

**"First one. Asked in a town four days of bus travel away at about nine minutes past seven on Monday morning by a man of about fifty-two with a bar. Somebody in this city was told at about four o'clock on the Tuesday afternoon. That is fifty-six hours and fifty-one minutes and I have counted it four times because I did not believe it the first three."**

**"Fifty-six hours and fifty-one minutes."**

**"Second one. Asked at about ten past six on the Monday evening by a man of about thirty-eight with a ramp and a form. Somebody in this city was told at about half past two on the Thursday afternoon. That is ninety-six hours and twenty minutes, and that is the bad one, and the bad one is not because anybody was slow. It is because the answer had to come back four days before anybody could be told it."**

**"And the third."**

**"Third one. Asked at about ten past ten on the Tuesday night by a man of about thirty-three who is not answering. Told at about a quarter past one on the Thursday afternoon. Fifty-nine hours and five minutes."**

**"Then the shortest is fifty-six hours and fifty-one minutes and the longest is ninety-six and twenty, and that is two numbers and you have said you have one."**

**"I have not got one. I have got three and I have got a feeling, and the feeling is that about nine hours of all of those is somebody sitting on a piece of paper, and I would like to be wrong about that and I am not going to be."**

---

The man at the counter wrote the three on the wall list under the plastic sleeve in his own handwriting, which he does not do, and it took about four seconds, and then he stopped and looked at what he had done for about nine seconds.

**"Do not do that."**

**"It is the wall list. Everybody reads it."**

**"I know it is the wall list. That is why do not do it. About eleven people a week read that and one of them will copy a figure onto a job ticket and take it four days on a bus to a person who has never heard of a bus ramp."**

He took the pencil back and rubbed the three lines off the wall list in about four seconds and the sleeve went back over the marks and there is nothing on that wall today that was not on it yesterday except a man of about thirty-three who is not answering.

**"Then where does it go."**

**"On a card in a drawer under the counter, and the drawer is emptied on a Friday."**

**"That is worse."**

**"It is honest and it is where everything in this case goes, and I would rather a number died in a drawer on a Friday than travel four days on somebody's initiative."**

---

**The second half of that was said out loud at about five, in a face, and it was a claim and not a plan, and the man who said it has an ordinary job and is not on anything.**

**"I am the man who lifts at a market. I am not the man who decides anything. I am going to say one sentence and then I am going to go and do my work and I would like it written down that I said it in this shop and not in a room."**

**"Say it."**

**"A response that has to be agreed on beforehand does not arrive in time, and about nine weeks is the number I have for how long it takes here, and if somebody in this city told a man with a ramp on a Monday evening that a plan existed for Mondays then that is a better sentence than the one I am saying and it would still be ninety-six hours and twenty minutes."**

**"Are you asking for a plan."**

**"No. I am not asking for a plan. I am a man who lifts things and I am telling you the shape of the thing and I would like somebody in a room to hear it and do nothing with it and I would like to be able to say afterwards that I said it here first."**

Nobody wrote it down, including him.

---

A woman of about twenty-five came in at about a quarter to six with the job ticket in her hand and said she had been past the room again and had not been in and that the ticket was still no use, for the fourth time, and the man at the counter said that it was, and that he had had about four days again to look for the thing that is use and had not found it, and that he was not going to apologise for it this time because an apology from a counter is a way of ending a conversation and she had not come to be apologised to.

She said that, and he said right, and she said that she would keep the ticket and that she was not angry and that she had been saying the same thing to about four people for about two years, and one of the four was herself, and then she said the thing she had come to say.

**"I have been offered a second thing. Same room, same company, same form, and about nine people from that office have asked me three times in a fortnight whether I would sign it and I have said no three times and on the third time one of them said, in a corridor, that I am making it difficult for people who have a job.*."**

**"Did you say anything."**

**"I said that making it difficult is the whole of what I am for and that I would like that in writing.*."**

**"Did you ask for it in writing."**

**"I did and she said she would put it in a folder.*."**

**"Then that is a yes to a folder and not to a piece of paper, and a folder goes in a drawer and the drawer is emptied on a Friday.*."**

**"I know. I have been doing this for about two years and I know what a folder is and I said it anyway, because what else is there."**

She kept the ticket and went and the man at the counter did not write anything on it and has not got a better sentence than the one he says to everybody, and he said it to her, and she said she had heard it.

At about half past six there were about four people in the warm room and one of them was the man of about fifty-one, who had been at the north wall for five hundred and nineteen days, seventy-four weeks and one day, and who had not said anything on any of the three days this week.

A man of about thirty-three who works a weighbridge in the third of the four towns is in this city for four days and was not in that room, and he is a different man from the man of about thirty-three who is not answering in the third town, and the two of them have never been told about each other, and the man with the pencil did not know that on Thursday and will not know it on Friday.

The room off that service road is nine hundred and thirteen days old, one hundred and thirty weeks and three days, four rooms and a back one, and it is warm. The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and seventeen days old, one hundred and thirty-one weeks to the day, four days past the room, and it has not been moved and has not been turned over and the rail holds two.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines. The twelfth is eight hundred and thirty-three days, the thirteenth seven hundred and eighty-four, the fourteenth seven hundred and forty-nine, the fifteenth seven hundred and twenty-eight, the sixteen seven hundred and three, the seventeenth six hundred and eighty-five, the eighteenth six hundred and thirty-one, and the nineteenth six hundred and nine days, eighty-seven weeks to the day, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. Nothing was added to that board on the Thursday and nothing was taken from it and there is no twentieth line.

The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and sixty-one days, sixty-five weeks and six days. The man of about fifty-one at the wall is five hundred and nineteen days, seventy-four weeks and one day. The man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and forty-six days into a hold of four buildings and about nine hundred doors, seventy-eight weeks to the day, and nobody has asked him anything. The ask is six hundred and three days, eighty-six weeks and one day, from the day it was asked. The nine copied fronts of a page are four hundred and seventy-nine days, sixty-eight weeks and three days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line still not found. The distance is two hundred and ninety-three days, forty-one weeks and six days, and it is not shorter and it is not ended.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
