# Chapter 532 — A Second Thing In A Second Town, And A Rule Nobody Broke And Nobody Can Blame

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a warm room from about half past six, and its shape was two people at a counter finding out that the first of two things had been answered before the second of them was known to anybody, which is not anybody's fault and is the worst shape there is.**

---

Eleven on the docket, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last at about a quarter to six. A bench in Lattice Ward until about two, the counter of that district on to six, and the rooms behind the service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate and it is wrong and it went to nobody. **The heat in that flat is on, dearer than in the winter, four names on the tenancy are carrying the increase between them, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own money is owed to nobody at all and is not going to be paid in this month.** Those rooms are nine hundred and twelve days old, which is one hundred and thirty weeks and two days, and there are four of them with a fifth at the back, and the heat is on in all of them.

Standing in the rail across that counter front is a card of nine hundred and sixteen days, which is one hundred and thirty weeks and six days, and it is four days older than the rooms, and the rail has held two cards for eight years.

---

The man came in at about ten past four on the Wednesday and he had been in this city since the Sunday and he was going back on the Friday, and he had a bus driver's timetable in his coat and a look on him that the man at the counter had not seen on a person before and hoped not to see again.

**"I drove a bus on the Monday with a ramp on it."**

**"Then say the part where it is a problem."**

**"I had a woman of about forty in a wheelchair on it and the ramp would not go down, and I held it down with my knee and with a man's hand on the end, and we got her off at the third stop, and it took about four minutes, and I have driven that route for about nine years."**

**"Then what."**

**"Then I filled a form out in the book in the cab, which is the correct book and the correct box, and I put the ramp on the bus as not working, which is the correct thing to do, and a man from the depot came at about four and looked at it for about nine minutes and could not tell me whether I was allowed to take the bus out again with a part that had not come."**

Nobody at the counter said anything for about four seconds.

**"That is not anybody being at fault."**

**"No,"** the man said.

**"That is the correct answer and I have known it since the Monday and I cannot make it fit anywhere."**

---

The woman of about forty-four came in at about half past four and she had not been in that shop since a fortnight before, and she had spent the Monday and the Tuesday and the Wednesday arranging for the woman of about eighty-one to have her shopping brought up four floors, and she works in a supermarket in a town four days of bus travel away and is the person who is told things.

**"There is a second one."**

**"Say where."**

**"A different town. A bus, a ramp, a woman in a chair, and the man is the one who was in here twenty minutes ago and he has not met the woman of eighty-one and has never heard of her."**

**"How long has that one been going."**

The man of about thirty-eight said it, because she was looking at him and not at the counter.

**"Since Monday morning. The same morning. My bus was the same bus as the lift."**

**"Right,"** the woman of about forty-four said, and put her hand flat on the counter, and did not say anything else for about nine seconds.

---

**And then, in a face, in a shop, with two strangers in it, a person with an ordinary job said the second half of an argument that a woman of fifty-three had not finished in a room, and nobody in the shop agreed with him and one person in the shop wanted very much to.**

**"I am going to say a thing and I am not going to argue for it and you can both forget it by Friday."**

**"Say it."**

**"I drive a bus. A bus is a thing that has to be agreed on before anybody can get on it, and the timetable is agreed, and the route is agreed, and the ramp is agreed, and about six hundred and forty people get on that bus in a week because of things that were agreed by people they have never met. And when one of the agreed things breaks, we do not stop the bus and hold a meeting about it. We go round it."**

**"You went round it."**

**"I went round it, and I filled the form in, and I am still sitting in this city four days after I went round it, and the reason I am sitting in this city is that a part is on its way and the part is on its way on a bus, and that bus is doing about nine miles an hour on a road that is not the one I am used to."**

**"Then the slowness is the road."**

**"No. The slowness is that I could not put a woman in a wheelchair on a bus with a ramp on it and not tell anybody, and I could not do that for four days either, and those two things are the whole of my life as a driver and I would not have either of them the other way."**

The man at the counter did not agree with him. He had a sentence, it was eleven words, and it was about four days old, and it was true, and he was not going to say it in a shop to a bus driver and a supermarket manager because the second it left his mouth it would be a thing two people agreed, and about nine weeks later there would be a man in a room who said it, and every frightened person would go to him.

**"The ramp man is in the depot yard,"** he said instead.

**"Which depot."**

**"The one on the far side of the market in that town."**

**"Then I can tell you the thing that will happen to him and it is not a comfort. He will be suspended for about nine days pending a part and a check, and then he will be back on and nobody will say anything about it, and the check will be on a form in a book, and the book will go in a drawer on a Friday."**

**"All right."**

**"I am telling you because you will be asked and I would rather you had the true thing than a kind one."**

---

A man of about twenty-three came in at about five and asked whether a thing that had happened to his sister in another district four days of bus travel away counted as a thing that had happened to him, and was told in about four seconds that he is not the person who may say that, and that his sister is not the person who may say it either, and that if she wants somebody to ask her what she agreed to there is a room with a sheet of paper on its door and he does not have the address and has not asked for it, and the man said he had heard of that room, and the man at the counter said that four people a week knock on that door now and cannot read what is on it, and that this is a consequence and not an accident, and the man of about twenty-three said right and went.

The woman of about forty-four said her piece before she left, and it was not about the ramp and it was not about the lift.

**"I have been the person they tell things to in that town for about six years. I am not staff. I am the woman who is at the counter at eight in the morning. On Tuesday morning a man came and told me about the ramp and I said the same words I said to you this afternoon, and then I went back to the floor and did my job."**

**"What words."**

**"I said, wait for it, and I want you to hear that I have said it about forty times and I have never once said anything else: wait for it."**

**"That is not wrong."**

**"It is not wrong and it is not enough, and I have known that for about four days, and I have not got anything to put in the place of it, and I am not going to stand in a shop and produce one at half past four on a Wednesday."**

She picked up her bag and went out into the street, and nobody said anything at the counter after that which anybody would have wanted to say.

---

At about half past six there were about four people in the warm room for the temperature and one of them was the man of about fifty-one, who had been at the north wall for five hundred and eighteen days, seventy-four weeks to the day, with his bag on the floor at his feet, and who had said nothing on the Monday and had said nothing on the Wednesday either.

The woman of about thirty-four who reads meters and works nights was in that room on the Wednesday and she was the only person in it who had said anything, and what she said was about a woman of about fifty-three and a room off a wide road and a paragraph on the back of a sheet of paper, and she said that the woman had been right about the four hundred and wrong about the nine chairs, and that both of those had been said to her face and neither of them had been taken back.

Nobody in that room agreed with her and one person in that room did not hear the second half of it, and she knew which person and did not say the second half twice.

The room off that service road is nine hundred and twelve days old, one hundred and thirty weeks and two days, four rooms and a back one, and it is warm. The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and sixteen days old, one hundred and thirty weeks and six days, four days past the room, and it was not moved and it was not turned over and the rail holds two.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines. The twelfth is eight hundred and thirty-two days, the thirteenth seven hundred and eighty-three, the fourteenth seven hundred and forty-eight, the fifteenth seven hundred and twenty-seven, the sixteen seven hundred and two, the seventeenth six hundred and eighty-four, the eighteenth six hundred and thirty, and the nineteenth six hundred and eight days, eighty-six weeks and six days, and that one is the only line of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. Nothing went on the board on the Wednesday and nothing came off it and there is no twentieth line.

The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and sixty days, sixty-five weeks and five days. The man of about fifty-one at the wall is five hundred and eighteen days, seventy-four weeks to the day. The man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and forty-five days into a hold of four buildings and about nine hundred doors, seventy-seven weeks and six days, with no stamp and no reference and no date, and nobody has asked him anything since a question was put to him in a doorway and answered. The ask is six hundred and two days, eighty-six weeks to the day, from the day it was asked. The nine copied fronts of a page are four hundred and seventy-eight days, sixty-eight weeks and two days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found. The distance is two hundred and ninety-two days, forty-one weeks and five days, and it is neither shorter nor ended.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
