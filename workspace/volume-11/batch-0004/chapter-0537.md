# Chapter 537 — A Man Of About Forty-One Offers A Room And A Friday Morning And A Right To Say No In Nine Seconds, And Is Refused In About Nine Seconds By A Man Who Mends Pumps

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a room off a wide road from about half past six with about eleven people in it, and its shape was a company man putting an address and a number and a Friday morning on a table, and a man who mends pumps saying no in about nine seconds before anybody had agreed that anybody was allowed to.**

---

The Thursday's docket runs to eleven, the dates against the eleven run to eleven, and eleven turned up, the last of them at about ten past five. A bench until two, a counter until six, a room off a wide road in between, and the service-road rooms from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, and went to nobody at all. **The heat in that flat is on, dearer than in the winter, four names on the tenancy carrying the increase, and a fortnight a cook put out of her own money is owed by nobody and is not going to be paid at the end of this month.** The rooms off that road are nine hundred and twenty days, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and three days, four rooms and a back one, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and twenty-four days, one hundred and thirty-two weeks to the day, four days more than the room, and the rail holds two.

---

**Two figures on this page are each other's age and each other's day, and they are the fourteenth line and the man of about fifty-one.** The fourteenth line on the hardboard on two nails is seven hundred and fifty-six days old, and seven hundred and fifty-six is the day the man of about fifty-one first sat down against that north wall with a bag at his feet. That man has been at the wall five hundred and twenty-six days, seventy-five weeks and one day, and five hundred and twenty-six is the day the fourteenth line was written.

Neither of those two figures is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and no figure of days off for the heating exists anywhere in this case, and a man who mends pumps being asked to answer a question about what he may say is not that heating.

---

The man of about forty-one came at about half past six with a folder and did not open it for about four minutes, which is a thing a man does when he already knows the room is against him and wants the room to have tried.

There were about eleven people and nine chairs and four of the nine were used twice. A woman of about twenty-four was at the trestle and said nothing, because it was Tuesday and the room is for a thing a person was asked to do and did not understand, and what was on offer was not that, and she said so in one sentence in about four seconds and then sat down.

**"Then this is not my room tonight."**

**"Then it is a room with nine chairs in it, and I have rented the hour, and there is a kettle."**

**"Say the next part."**

**"An address. A room of ours on a Friday morning with a door that opens at eight. A number you can give anybody."**

He turned the folder round and there was a printed sheet on it with a table of about nine columns and the columns had names under them of the kind of thing that gets counted.

**"And one of yours, and I am going to be exact because nobody else in this room has been exact with me."**

**"Go on."**

**"Every thing that is fitted, worn or read by the thing in your wall ends up in a file in a room on a Friday, and about four hundred people in this city can each say a sentence about their own consent out loud and not one of them can see what is in the file. I want the right to say yes or no to a decision before it is taken, in a room, in a face, and I want it in writing, and I want it in about nine seconds."**

About nine people in that room heard that and the room did what rooms do, which is to go quiet for about four seconds and then to ask about money.

---

**The man who said no is about thirty-four and he mends pumps, and he had come because a woman who works in a kitchen in this city asked him to come, and he had not read anything and had not brought anything, and he was the only person in that room whose job is to be told no.**

**"No."**

That is about nine seconds from the end of the sentence about four hundred people.

**"Right, that is — sorry, say the rest of it."**

**"I mend pumps. I go to about nine houses a week. I am the person who comes when the water stops and I am the person who cannot say why it stopped to the person whose water it is, because I was not in the house when it stopped and the person who was in the house has gone to work."**

**"And you think that is a reason not to have a number."**

**"I think that is the only reason a number is worth having, and you have offered it to a room that does not work in the houses."**

Nobody in the room had said anything for about four seconds and the man of about forty-one said the thing that is the whole of his method.

**"Then let me put it more carefully. I am not asking to override anybody. I am offering to be the place where the answer comes from, at eight in the morning, on a Friday, in about nine seconds, and every household in this city that has one of these in it would be told what it is being asked to agree to before anybody asks them."**

**"And what does the household get."**

**"A decision in about nine seconds instead of a question that goes away for four days."**

**"Yes."**

**"Then what are you refusing."**

**"I am refusing the second half of that sentence and you have said it twice and the second time you said it as though it were the first half."**

---

**And then he said the thing that cost him, in a face, to about eleven people, and he said it before he was asked what it was, and he is not on anything and is not a committee and is not an office.**

**"On Tuesday night there was a man of about thirty-eight in this city with a ramp on a bus that would not go down and a woman in a wheelchair on it, and he held it down with his knee and got her off at the third stop, and he filled the form in the book in the cab, which is the correct book, and he has not been able to find out whether he is allowed to take the bus out again. He is not in this room. He is in a town four days of bus travel away and he is one of about nine people I have been to about nine houses of this week with, and I know about it because a woman in a kitchen told me on the Tuesday and I did not write it down and I am saying it here because you have asked me a question in a room and I have not got a better place to say it."**

**"That is not a refusal of me."**

**"No. It is a reason, and I have not got a better one, and I would rather give you a bad one in a room than a good one in a corridor."**

**"Then say the refusal."**

**"You have got it. I said it in nine seconds and then I explained it for about four minutes, which is nine seconds for the answer and a long time for the reasons, and that is the arrangement you are offering me and I am telling you I do not want it."**

Nobody in that room said he had refused the right thing and nobody said he had refused the wrong thing, and the woman of about twenty-four said nothing at all, and a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts said one sentence and it was not addressed to anybody in particular.

**"He is right about the nine seconds and he is wrong about who should have them, and both of those are true and I have not got a way of holding both."**

**"Then say the other half."**

**"I do not think a Friday morning at eight is a bad thing. I think a Friday morning at eight is a good thing and I think about eleven people in this room do not want it and are not going to say so, and the ones who are going to say so are going to say it in about four years when one of them has a bad morning."**

---

The man of about forty-one did not withdraw the offer, and he did not take the folder away angry, and he closed it and said that he would be in touch in about a fortnight with the same address, and he said that to a man of about thirty-four who mends pumps and not to the room, and then he said it to the room.

**"I would like it minuted that I was refused and that I am still offering."**

**"There is no minute."**

**"I know there is no minute. I said it for the people who were not in the room."**

**What he wanted, and did not say, on that Thursday, was to be asked by one of the eleven whether the right to say no in nine seconds is the same thing as the right to say no at all, and he had an answer ready that took about four minutes and it was the truest thing he has thought since the spring, and about nine people in that room were saying the same sentence to each other in about nine different ways and not one of them said it out loud.**

He has wanted, for about four months, to be the man who says the thing nobody in the room will say, and he has been told by a woman of about twenty-four in a corridor and by a woman of about thirty-four on a pavement and by himself on a Sunday that this is a thing he may not do, and on the Thursday a man of about thirty-four did it in about nine seconds in front of eleven people and got nothing for it, and the man at the back wanted to put a hand on that man's shoulder and did not, because a man who puts a hand on a shoulder in a room is a man who has said something about the shoulder and there was nothing to say about the shoulder.

**The cost, named exactly: for about four years he has carried the only road in this case to four buildings and about nine hundred doors in a town four days of bus travel away in his own head, and a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts is in this city for four days and will be in it again in about a fortnight, and he did not ask the man of about twenty-nine on the Thursday whether he knew of a fourth thing, and did not ask him on the Friday, and will not ask him in about a fortnight, because asking a man of about twenty-nine in a canteen whether he knows of a fourth thing is asking him to be the person somebody has to be thinking about, and that is the one job in this case he has refused four times this year and the fifth time is in about four days.**

Nobody applauded. Nobody shouted. About four people asked him questions about the Friday morning afterwards in the stair and he answered all four and gave the address to none of them, and that was not a decision anybody made, it is just what happened.

---

At about half past six on the Friday before, the man who mends pumps had been at the counter in this city for about nine minutes and had not asked for anything, and a man of about twenty-two had given him a job ticket with the address of a shop front off a wide road on it, in his own handwriting, and had not said what the room is for.

**"You have written this before."**

**"I have."**

**"Then you know it is four words and a door and it does not tell anybody anything."**

**"I know. I have been told by about four people this year that an address is no use, and one of the four was the woman who asked me to come tonight, and she said it to me in a kitchen and I did not have a better answer then and I have not got one now."**

**"Then why did you write it."**

**"Because you asked me to come and you did not have an address to give me, and I would rather be the wrong kind of help for one evening than not turn up."**

The man of about thirty-four took the ticket and did not put it in a drawer and did not read it out in a room full of people and did not give it to anybody else.

The room off that service road is nine hundred and twenty days old, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and three days, four rooms and a back one, and it is warm. The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and twenty-four days old, one hundred and thirty-two weeks to the day, four days past the room, and it has not been moved and has not been turned over and the rail holds two.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines. The twelfth is eight hundred and forty days, the thirteenth seven hundred and ninety-one, the fourteen seven hundred and fifty-six, the fifteen seven hundred and thirty-five, the sixteen seven hundred and ten, the seventeen six hundred and ninety-two, the eighteen six hundred and thirty-eight, and the nineteen six hundred and sixteen days, eighty-eight weeks to the day, and it is the only line of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. Nothing went onto that board on the Thursday and nothing came off it and there is no twentieth line.

The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and sixty-eight days, sixty-six weeks and six days. The man of about fifty-one at the wall is five hundred and twenty-six days, seventy-five weeks and one day. The man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and fifty-three days into a hold of four buildings and about nine hundred doors, seventy-nine weeks to the day, and nobody has asked him anything. The ask is six hundred and ten days, eighty-seven weeks and one day, from the day it was asked. The nine copied fronts of a page are four hundred and eighty-six days, sixty-nine weeks and three days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found. The distance is three hundred days, forty-two weeks and six days, and it is not shorter and it is not ended.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
