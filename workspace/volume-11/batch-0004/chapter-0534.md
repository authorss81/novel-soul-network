# Chapter 534 — A Fourth Town Where The Only Thing Anybody Has Is A Room With Nine Chairs In It, And Nobody Says So

**The Friday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a shop front off a wide road from about ten past two, and its shape was a man handing a piece of paper to a woman of twenty-four and being told in nine seconds what he had agreed to, and nobody remarking on it afterwards, including the man.**

---

The Friday's sheet has eleven jobs on it, the date column has eleven against them, and eleven people came, the last of them arriving at about half past five. Bench work until about two, the counter two to six, a shop front off a wide road in the middle of it, and the rooms off the service road from half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, and it went to nobody. **The heat in that flat is on, dearer, four names on the tenancy carrying the increase, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own money is owed to nobody and will be owed to nobody at the end of this month.** Off that service road are four rooms with another behind them; they are nine hundred and fourteen days old, one hundred and thirty weeks and four days, and nobody in them is cold.

A card has stood in the rail on that counter front for nine hundred and eighteen days, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and one day, which is four days more than the rooms behind the road, and that rail holds two and has held two for eight years.

---

The man of about thirty-seven came on the Sunday for a funeral and he went back on the Friday, and he had a piece of paper in his coat that he had been carrying for four days and had not shown to anybody, and it was folded in four and it had been folded in four on the Wednesday and it was soft along the fold.

He came in at about ten past two and the door was open and there were six people in nine chairs, and the sheet on the door is in a small hand that leans about four degrees to the right and nine people out of ten cannot get through it, and he stood in front of it for the length of about nine lines and then came in anyway.

**"I want to be asked what I agreed to."**

**"You have come to the right room and you have not come to the right hour. Sit down. I have got four people ahead of you and the last person leaves at about half past five."**

**"I have got a bus at about six."**

**"Then you have got about four minutes with me and not the whole afternoon, and I am going to use them, and I am going to read that paper myself and not ask you a single question about it first, and if you have the wrong room you will find out in about four minutes and it will not have cost you anything."**

---

She read it standing up, and it took her about nine seconds, and the room was doing the other thing it does on a Friday afternoon, which is nine items at four minutes each, and the six other people were doing their items and were not listening.

What was on the paper was not a contract and was not a form in two columns and did not have a signature box. It was a printed sheet with a heading about a supply arrangement and it had eleven short paragraphs and the eleventh said that the arrangement continued for as long as the apparatus remained on the property.

**"You agreed to a thing that keeps going as long as the thing stays, and the thing stays because you agreed."**

**"That is what the man said. He said it was standard and it was on page eleven and it was the same one they put in everywhere."**

**"It is not the same one they put in everywhere, and I cannot prove that to you this afternoon, and what I can do is tell you the four things that are wrong with it and give you a sentence you can say to any man in any room."**

**"Say the four."**

**"One: there is no way in it to end it. Two: ending it is the same as removing the apparatus, and removing the apparatus is your decision and nobody else's, but the sheet does not say that, and a person who has not been told that is a person who thinks the company decides. Three: it does not say what happens to the water if it stops working, and the water in that block of yours has already stopped once, and if it stops twice in a year the company will write to you and not to the people upstairs. Four: there is a person at the top of the first page whose job is to make sure nothing is put in front of anybody who has not understood it, and that person's name is not on this."**

The man of about thirty-seven looked at the eleventh paragraph for about nine seconds.

**"How long did that take you."**

**"It took about nine seconds to read and about four minutes to be sure of, and you have been carrying it for four days."**

**"Right."**

**"Say the sentence."**

**"I have not understood what I agreed to and I am not taking it away today, and I will take it away in a week, and until one of us has looked at it the water is yours and the apparatus is on a piece of paper that has my name at the bottom of the first page and not on the eleven."**

**"Now say it back to me and do not say it better than I said it."**

He said it back worse than she said it and she let him, and then she put a hand out for the paper and he gave it to her and she folded it in four along the folds that were already there.

**"You are not keeping that."**

**"I am keeping it for a week, and then it goes back in an envelope, and if he asks you for it before then you say seven days and you do not give him a reason."**

**"Nobody has ever said seven days to me about anything in my life."**

**"Then say it tomorrow, and say it on Monday, and say it every day until it stops being frightening, because the frightening goes after about the third time and not before."**

---

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted on that Friday, and did not say, was to be asked by one of the fourteen how he had known which four faults those were, and he was not asked, and he stood against a wall for about nine minutes working out that he would have had to answer it by saying he had read the sheet, which is true and is not a qualification and is not what he would have said.**

A man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts came in at about half past four and had been in four of the four towns in his life and had driven past a shop that sells curtains in one of them in a van at about six in the morning, and he said, to nobody, that the shutters were half up and there was a light on behind them, and then he said that a man who was in there at half past six in the morning with the light on and the shutters half up is either up or has not been to bed, and that both of those are ordinary.

**"Is that worth saying."**

**"It is worth saying to the man on the counter and to nobody else, and you are the man on the counter, and I have not told the room and I am not going to, and you will not either, because a room of about eleven people does not need to hear that a man was up at half past six.*."**

**"Then I will not."**

**"You will not. And in about four days it will not matter, because in about four days the man of about thirty-seven is going to say seven days to a man from a firm, and that is the thing that survives this week, and a man being awake at half past six is not a thing anybody should carry four days on a bus.*."**

About eleven people went through that door between two and about half past five and the room took nine and about five of the eleven were on the pavement, and about four of the five were told what the room would not do, in about four seconds each, by a woman who can read her own door and nobody else's.

**Nobody in that room said that the man of about thirty-seven had been answered in about four minutes after carrying a thing for four days, and nobody in that room said it afterwards, and he did not say it, and the woman of twenty-four did not say it, and it is not written down anywhere in this case that anybody found that room quick, and this is the fourth of the four things and it is the only one of the four where the answer arrived before anybody had stopped waiting for it.**

The third of the four is a woman of about fifty-eight who runs a shop in a third town who has a fridge and a freezer and about nine hours of no cold behind her, and about four people she rang have each said a different thing, and the fourth man she rang was a man of about thirty-three who is not answering, and nobody in this city has her number and the number is not on anything, and the three intervals written on the back of a job ticket on Thursday do not contain her.

---

At about half past six there were about four people in the warm room and the man of about fifty-one was at the north wall with his bag on the floor at his feet, five hundred and twenty days, seventy-four weeks and two days, and he had not said anything.

**Two figures on this page are each other's age and each other's day, and they are the fifteenth line and the hold.**

The fifteenth line on the hardboard on two nails is seven hundred and twenty-nine days old, and seven hundred and twenty-nine is the day the man of about thirty-three took on four buildings and about nine hundred doors. The hold of that man is five hundred and forty-seven days, seventy-eight weeks and one day, and five hundred and forty-seven is the day the fifteenth line was written.

Neither of those two figures is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and no figure of days off for the heating exists anywhere in this case, and a woman in a shop in a third town with a fridge and a freezer and no cold behind her is not that heating, and a man in a shop front off a wide road who was handed a paper he had carried for four days is not that heating.

The room off that service road is nine hundred and fourteen days old, one hundred and thirty weeks and four days, four rooms and a back one, and it is warm. The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and eighteen days old, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and one day, four days past the room, and it has not been moved and has not been turned over and the rail holds two.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines. The twelfth is eight hundred and thirty-four days, the thirteenth seven hundred and eighty-five, the fourteenth seven hundred and fifty, the fifteen seven hundred and twenty-nine, the sixteen seven hundred and four, the seventeenth six hundred and eighty-six, the eighteenth six hundred and thirty-two, and the nineteenth six hundred and ten days, eighty-seven weeks and one day, and it is the only line of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. Nothing went onto that board on the Friday and nothing came off it and there is no twentieth line.

The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and sixty-two days, sixty-six weeks to the day. The ask is six hundred and four days, eighty-six weeks and two days, from the day it was asked. The nine copied fronts of a page are four hundred and eighty days, sixty-eight weeks and four days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found. The distance is two hundred and ninety-four days, forty-two weeks to the day, and it is not shorter and it is not ended.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
