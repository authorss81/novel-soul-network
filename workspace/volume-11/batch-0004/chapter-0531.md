# Chapter 531 — A Question Asked At A Counter In A Town This City Hardly Reaches, And Two Days Named In A Face

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a warm room from about half past six, and its shape was a question asked at a counter in a town this city hardly reaches, and an answer on the only road there is, and a man here deciding what to do with the waiting, and not deciding it well.**

---

Eleven on the docket, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last at about ten past six. The bench in Lattice Ward until two, that counter from two to six, the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, fixed, and said to nobody. **The heat in the flat above that road is on, dearer than in the winter, four names on the tenancy carry the increase, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own money is still owed to nobody and will not be paid this month.** The rooms off that road are nine hundred and ten days, one hundred and thirty weeks to the day, four rooms and a back one, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and fourteen days, one hundred and thirty weeks and four days, four days more than the room, and the rail holds two.

---

She came in at about twenty past two with a laundry's badge still on and a carrier bag with her working things in it, and she put the carrier down and stood at the counter for about nine seconds before she said anything, which is longer than most people take and she knew it was.

**"You are the one who writes the addresses."**

**"I write some of them. I do not decide which."**

**"Then I have come to the wrong end of it and I have come anyway, because there is only one."**

She was about thirty-one. She had been four days in a town where a woman about eighty-one had been in a lift between the second and third floors since about seven that morning, and a man of about fifty-two who does the maintenance on four blocks had got her out in about four minutes, and had then spent two days finding out whether he was allowed to have done it.

**"Say the four minutes first."**

**"He had a bar and he had a lamp and he brought the car up to the landing and he talked to her the whole time, and he did not open the doors until he had said to her that he was going to, and then he dropped the brake and brought it down to the second and she got out onto the landing and he went back up and shut the car."**

**"That is four minutes and it is good work and I am not going to argue with it."**

**"I am not asking you to. I am telling you it, because the four minutes is the part that is not the problem and everybody keeps talking about the two days."**

---

A man of about twenty-six came in at about a quarter past two with a bicycle wheel under his arm and wanted it mended by Friday and was told it would be Tuesday, and asked whether anybody had told him that, and was told that nobody had and that the wall list has no wheel on it and never has, and he said that his sister had told him and he had not believed her.

The rota man was in the shop from about half past two until about four, with a plastic sleeve buttoned into the inside pocket of a coat, and he had asked for the same thing twice before and been refused twice before, and he came to the counter and asked a third time.

**"Is there a person here who can tell me whether a thing I was asked to do counts."**

**"No."**

**"You have not heard the question."**

**"It does not matter what the question is. The answer is no, and I will give it to you in four seconds so that it costs you nothing, and you can go back up the green stair with the sleeve buttoned."**

**"The inspection is at the end of the month."**

**"I know what the inspection is. I have never been able to help you with an inspection and I have never once pretended otherwise."**

**"I have four of nine."**

**"Then you have four of nine and the other five are not going to arrive because they are waiting for a person from this city to tell them what the sheet is, and there is no such person and there never has been, and the only thing that has ever happened in this case is that you have a sheet in a sleeve."**

The rota man said that the fifth person was a woman of about thirty-one on the fourth landing who worked nights, and that she had said the sheet was fine in a voice that meant the opposite, and that he had not asked her a second question, and that this was the fourth time he had named that out loud to a person who could not do anything about it. Then he went and stood at the shelf with his back to the room for about nine minutes and left.

Nobody at the counter was thanked for that and he did not ask.

---

**The two days were named in a face at about a quarter to three, and the wage was in the same breath, and neither of them was softened.**

**"The man with the bar asked the counter in that town on the Monday morning. The counter is not yours and the counter is not anybody's. The counter wrote it on a piece of paper and put the piece of paper in an envelope and the envelope went on the bus, and the bus is four days, and so the answer came back on the Friday morning and he had already done it on the Monday."**

**"Then he did not need the answer."**

**"He did not need the answer on the Monday and he needed it on the Wednesday, because on the Wednesday a man of about forty-four from a firm in a different town came to that block with a card and asked him to sign something about the lift, and he could not say yes because he did not know, and he could not say no because he had already opened the doors."**

**"What did he say."**

**"He said he would send word, and he has nobody to send, and he said he would write, and writing is four days, and he said he would ask the man at the counter, and the man at the counter had already told him the same thing you have just told me, and he said fine."**

**"Say the rest of it."**

The words came out flat and in order and she did not stop in the middle of them, which is a thing a person does when they have said this to themselves more than once.

**"The rest of it is that I work at a laundry on the morning shift and the lift has not been signed off, and so I have been at my sister's flat since Wednesday morning, and the laundry has sent me a note on Thursday that I am not needed for the rest of the week, and there is nothing at the end of the month, and I have a car that has a thing wrong with the clutch."**

**"That is two days and it cost me seventy-eight pounds and I have never once seen anybody get that money back out of anything."**

**"Who is the man of about fifty-two."**

**"He has been doing those four blocks since before I was at that laundry. He is not a bad man. He is a careful one and careful is what he is and it cost him the same two days because he could not go back to his own work on the Wednesday either."**

**"What did it cost him."**

**"He did not tell me. I asked him on the bus and he said he would work it out and he has not told me and I am not going to ask him twice."**

---

The man at the counter did not write the town-one question on a job ticket.

He has a half ticket in a plastic sleeve under the wall list with four words in his own handwriting on the back of it, and a second half in a woman's hand on a hook, and he has watched both of them arrive late, and he decided, in about four seconds, with a pen in his hand, not to make a third.

**"If I write it on a ticket and give it to you, you will carry it back four days, and it will arrive on Friday, and by then it will be a question about a lift that has been running for three days."**

**"Yes."**

**"That is the whole of my reason and it is not a good one and I am giving it to you anyway."**

**"It is better than not giving me one."**

She picked up the carrier bag and stood there for about four seconds longer, and then she said a thing that was not a request and was not a complaint and that the man at the counter wrote nothing down and has thought about since.

**"Do not tell me it was not my fault. Everybody has told me it was not my fault. It was not my fault and I have got seventy-eight pounds."**

She went. Nobody thanked her and she did not ask and the second half of a job ticket is still on a hook under a wall list and has been since a week in which a woman of about twenty-four was appointed to something.

---

**What he wanted, and did not say, on that Monday, was to be the man who drove the four days.**

He has been a person who gets asked for about ten years and there has not been one road out of this city he has ever needed to take, and on the Monday afternoon he stood at the counter and did the arithmetic of a bus in both directions and worked out that it is about eleven hours each way, that it comes back on the Saturday, that a bench and a counter and a room from half past six are not available on the Saturday or on the Thursday or on the Friday of the next week, and that a woman of about thirty-four who reads meters and works nights has told him that there is a person in that town with a bar and a woman of about eighty-one in a lift, and that he has exactly one address in the world and it is on a job ticket in the pocket of a woman of about twenty-five who washes up who has told him it is no use four times.

He did not ask her for the ticket. He did not ask for the address. He asked the woman of about thirty-one for the name of the man, and she said she did not have it, only the shop and the age of him, and he wrote nothing down, and that was the decision of the day and it cost him the four days and it will cost him the four days again in about a fortnight, and he has already worked out that it will be about a fortnight and has not told anybody that either.

**The cost, named exactly, is this: a man of about fifty-two is doing a job he cannot prove he was allowed to do and there is one person in this city who could go and stand in that town and ask him, and the man at that counter will not be that person, and the only thing he has instead is a number on the back of a job ticket that is in a woman's pocket in another district of this city, and that number is an address of a door and not the name of a man.**

---

At about half past six there were four people in the warm room for the temperature and one of them was the man of about fifty-one, who had been at the north wall for five hundred and sixteen days, seventy-three weeks and five days, with his bag on the floor, and who had not said anything.

A man of about fifty-three who fits plumbing is four days of bus travel away in a town where a plate of iron with four slots in it is under a floor, and he has told one person in one cellar about nine words, and he has not told anybody else anything at all, and the not-telling is now the only thing about the plate that is true anywhere in this case.

The room off that service road is nine hundred and ten days old, one hundred and thirty weeks to the day, four rooms and a back one, and it is warm. The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and fourteen days old, one hundred and thirty weeks and four days, four days past the room, and it has not been moved and it has not been turned over and the rail holds two.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines. The twelfth is eight hundred and thirty days old, the thirteenth seven hundred and eighty-one, the fourteenth seven hundred and forty-six, the fifteenth seven hundred and twenty-five, the sixteen seven hundred, the seventeenth six hundred and eighty-two, the eighteenth six hundred and twenty-eight, and the nineteenth six hundred and six days, eighty-six weeks and four days, and that one is the only line of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. Nothing went onto that board on the Monday and nothing came off it and there is no twentieth line.

The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and fifty-eight days, sixty-five weeks and three days. The man of about fifty-one at the wall is five hundred and sixteen days, seventy-three weeks and five days. The man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and forty-three days into a hold of four buildings and about nine hundred doors, seventy-seven weeks and four days, with no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority, and he has not been asked anything again. The ask is six hundred days, eighty-five weeks and five days, from the day it was asked and from nothing said since. The nine copied fronts of a page are four hundred and seventy-six days, sixty-eight weeks to the day, eight of nine unfinished, and the first disagreement in the fourth line is still not found. The distance is two hundred and ninety days, forty-one weeks and three days, and it is not shorter and it is not ended.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
