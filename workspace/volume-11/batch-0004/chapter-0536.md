# Chapter 536 — A Man Of About Fifty-Eight Puts A Thing On A Table In Front Of Fourteen People And Says Out Loud What It Has Cost Him

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and then an hour in a ground-floor room in another district with about fourteen people in it, and its shape was a man who has spent nine years behind a glass handing the glass to about fourteen people and putting his own job on the table with it, and naming the price of that out loud in a face.**

---

The list for the Wednesday holds eleven, the dates against them hold eleven, and eleven came through the door, the last of them at about five fifty. Lattice Ward to two at the bench, the district counter from two until six, a ground floor in another district in the evening, and the service-road rooms after that. The rate is nine and then it is ten, and it has been wrong for years, and nobody at that counter has ever been told so and nobody outside it cares. **The heating in the tenement over that road is running, and costs more than it did, and four people on one lease are between them finding it, and a fortnight that a cook found out of her own pocket in the winter is on nobody's list and will be on nobody's list at the end of this month.** The four rooms off that service road, and the one at the back of them, stand at nine hundred and nineteen days, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and two days, with the heat on in all five.

What has been standing in the rail along that counter front for nine hundred and twenty-three days, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and six days, is a card, and the rooms are four days older, and the rail is built for two.

---

The room is on the ground floor of a building with a counter on it that has been closed to the public since a Tuesday, and it was not hired and it was not borrowed, because the man of about fifty-eight who came into it at about half past six had been the one who decided who used that room, and he had spent the day deciding it in a way that four different people have since described as the only sensible thing and one person has described as the end of something.

About fourteen people were in it. There was a table at the front and there was a chair at the back against the wall with its back to the whole room, and it did not move in the hour and nobody looked at it more than once, and whoever moves it is not named here and is not going to be.

There was a tin on the table with no lid and no lock, and there was a book of four columns face up in front of the woman of about twenty-four, and there was a second chair beside her that nobody sat in.

---

He put it down at about ten past seven and he did not put it down gently, and the sound of it on a table is in about nine people behind him, and about four of them were on a list and about nine of them were not.

It was a bundle of about nine sheets, folded, and the first thing on the top sheet was a list of what had been taken out of a room and when and by whose hand, and the second thing was that the hand was the same on every line.

**"I am going to say four things and then I am going to stop talking, and I want the four of them said in this order and I do not want to be asked anything until the fourth."**

Nobody asked him anything.

**"One. That is a list of removals. Two. It is genuine. Three. Every line on it is about fourteen years old and every line on it is about a place called Marrow Falls, and about eleven of the eleven places the material went from are in this city, and the city is on the list as the place it went to and not only as the place it went from."**

Nobody said anything for about four seconds and then a man of about fifty-three said the word *fourteen* and the man of about fifty-eight said, yes, and that he was aware of the arithmetic, and that he had done it himself in about four minutes the night before and had got the same answer twice.

**"Four."**

**"That list is the reason this city did what it did fourteen years ago, and it was in a building with a counter on the ground floor that was open to anybody who walked in, and for about nine years I have been the man behind that counter, and I have answered about four hundred questions with a smaller question."**

---

**And then he named what it cost him, out loud, in a face, to about fourteen people, and nobody wrote it down and he did not ask anybody to.**

**"I am fifty-eight. I have a coat, a room above a lock-up and a job, and the job is the only one of the three that is worth anything and I have known that since I was about thirty. On Friday I am not behind that counter. Not moved, not promoted, not consulted. The office has not asked me for anything and I have not been dismissed and I will not say I was dismissed, because it would not be true and because a man who says he was pushed out of a job he was pushed out of is a man asking a room to feel something on his behalf."**

**"Then what did happen."**

**"What happened is that I put nine sheets on a table in a room I chose, in front of about fourteen people, and I did it on a Wednesday evening instead of a Monday morning, and on Friday there will be a person behind that counter who is not me and who has not been told anything by me because I was not going to put a note through his door the night before and make it a smaller thing."**

**"Say the rest of it."**

**"The rest of it is nine years of being the man behind the glass. I liked it. I want that said by me and not by anybody else and I am saying it now. I liked being the man behind the glass, and about four hundred people asked me a real question in that time and I gave about four hundred of them a small one, and about four hundred of them went away holding something true about me and nothing about themselves."**

A woman of about thirty-one said that a man who has just handed over a thing is not the same as a man who has been relieved of it, and that the difference is worth about nine years and does not belong to him.

**"It does not belong to me."**

**"That is the first thing anybody has said in this hour that I have not already thought and I would like it recorded that I have not written it down either."**

Nobody thanked him then and nobody thanked him afterwards and he was not thanked in the street, and he walked to a stop and got a bus, and on the bus a woman of about thirty-four who had been in that room said out loud, once, to nobody, that she had been in this city for about nine years and had never once been asked a question by anybody in it, and then she said nothing else for the rest of the journey.

---

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted, and did not say, on the Wednesday afternoon, was to be in that room and to be the fourteenth name on a list of the fourteen, and to be asked whether the list was genuine, and to be the person who said yes.**

He wrote nine lines for a board once and was asked again for them twice and did not write them twice, and the reason he has given for that is restraint and the reason he has not written down is that he does not know which of the two it is, and this is the first week in about four months in which the not-doing and the not-knowing have come apart and he has watched them come apart from the other side of a counter.

He had the address of that room. He had it for about nine minutes on the Wednesday afternoon, in a shop, from a man of about fifty-eight who was not in a hurry to be anywhere, and he did not use it, and the reason is that a man who arrives in a room where a list has just been read out is a man who has been in the room, and a man who has been in the room is a man somebody in the room has to be thinking about, and about four people in this city have spent about four months being the person somebody has to be thinking about and not one of them would have thanked him for it then and none of them thanked him now.

**"You are not coming."**

**"I am not coming."**

**"Say why, once, and then do not say it to me again."**

**"Because about four hundred competent people in this city can each say a paragraph out loud and not one of them has anywhere to put an objection to it, and I have said that sentence to about four people this year and each of the four went away believing I could put one there, and I cannot, and I have known that since a Monday in the spring."**

**"That is the best of the about four you have given me."**

**"It is the truest one and it is the one where I am the subject of the sentence, so I have been saving it."**

At about half past six, before any of that, the man of about fifty-eight had been in a shop in this city for about nine minutes with a man of about twenty-two who does not decide anything, and what was said there was about the same four minutes from the other side.

**"You are going to say that list in a room and about nine people in this city will hear it and about four hundred people who could each say a paragraph out loud will not hear it, and that is the correct arrangement and I am not arguing with it."**

**"I am not going to bring it to a counter."**

**"Good."**

**"I am going to say the thing that is actually the hard part and it is one sentence, and then I will stop."**

**"Say it."**

**"I have had about four hundred chances in nine years to be asked the question that would have had this out of me, and about nine people in this city have been close to it, and I made every one of those questions smaller on purpose, and I am not going to be forgiven for that in a room tonight, and I am not asking to be."**

**"Then what are you asking for."**

**"I am not asking for anything. I have never asked for anything. That is the whole of the problem and I have just said it to you on a Wednesday afternoon in a shop and I would like you to notice that I noticed it."**

---

The room off that service road is nine hundred and nineteen days old, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and two days, four rooms and a back one, and it is warm. The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and twenty-three days old, one hundred and thirty-one weeks and six days, four days past the room, and it has not been moved and has not been turned over and the rail holds two.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines. The twelfth is eight hundred and thirty-nine days, the thirteenth seven hundred and ninety, the fourteenth seven hundred and fifty-five, the fifteen seven hundred and thirty-four, the sixteen seven hundred and nine, the seventeen six hundred and ninety-one, the eighteen six hundred and thirty-seven, and the nineteenth six hundred and fifteen days, eighty-seven weeks and six days, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. Nothing went onto that board on the Wednesday and nothing came off it and there is no twentieth line.

The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and sixty-seven days, sixty-six weeks and five days. The man of about fifty-one at the wall is five hundred and twenty-five days, seventy-five weeks to the day. The man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and fifty-two days into a hold of four buildings and about nine hundred doors, seventy-eight weeks and six days, and nobody has asked him anything. The ask is six hundred and nine days, eighty-seven weeks to the day, from the day it was asked. The nine copied fronts of a page are four hundred and eighty-five days, sixty-nine weeks and two days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found. The distance is two hundred and ninety-nine days, forty-two weeks and five days, and it is not shorter and it is not ended.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
