# Chapter 545 — About Nine Hundred People Are In A Hall And A Clerk Rules On A Sheet Of Paper And The Second Count Comes Out Four Hundred And Thirty-Six Against And Four Hundred And Seventeen For, And The Woman Who Says It Aloud Is Not On Anything And Nobody Thanks Her

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and the middle of a public hall with nine hundred people in it from about nine until about half past eleven, and its shape was two shows of hands taken on one sheet of paper by a woman whose whole work is counting and who said the two numbers out loud and was not thanked for either of them.**

---

Eleven jobs for the Monday, eleven dates, eleven people at the counter, the last of them at about ten past five. Bench in Lattice Ward until two, the counter two to six, the service-road rooms from about half past six, and a public hall off the market street from about half past eight. Nine, and then ten, is what the bench charges and it is wrong and it did not reach a person. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer, four names on the tenancy are carrying it, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and will be owed to nobody at the end of this month.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and thirty-eight days, one hundred and thirty-four weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail on the front of that counter is nine hundred and forty-two days, one hundred and thirty-four weeks and four days, four days more than the rooms, and the rail takes two.

**At about half past eight the hall had about four hundred and eleven people in it and the queue outside had about two hundred and sixty and both figures are here because both were on two separate pieces of paper on the same morning.**

The man of about thirty-four who opens that door was at the north door with a folded sheet and a torch in his pocket and the word MONDAY written at the top of the sheet in the woman's own hand and nothing at all under the two columns, and he had been there since about ten past eight and he had not sat down once.

**He let about nine people in before the general queue on the word of a woman of about twenty-nine who was carrying chairs, and one of the nine was a woman of about twenty-five who washes up at a canteen on a corner, and her name is Odile Marsh, and a woman she was carrying a chair for said it out loud in the middle of that doorway on the Monday morning at about nine, and the man at the door heard it and wrote it nowhere, because there was nowhere on that sheet to write it.**

The doors opened at about nine and stayed open until the business began, which is a thing they have never done at that building in about nine years, and about four hundred people came in between nine and ten and about nine people were still outside at ten past ten.

**He heard the name at about ten past ten, about nine rows from the back, in about nine hundred people, and he did not know what to do with it and did not do anything with it, and the cost of that is that a woman who has asked him for one thing twice and not been given it now has a name he could have used, and he has used it in his own head about four times since and nowhere else, and there is no version of this Monday on which a man of twenty-two stands up in that hall and says a woman's name into a microphone that does not work.**

At about ten, from a chair at the front that had a microphone on a stand in front of it and the microphone did not work, the woman of about fifty-two who chairs the standing committee said that the business was begun, and the man of about thirty-four at the north door shut two doors on about two hundred and sixty people, and it took him about eleven seconds, and he had no way of knowing it was the right eleven seconds and he has not been told since and has not asked.

---

**The first thing was put to that room in about four minutes and it is the eleven sentences of the Wednesday with a twelfth added, and the twelfth was that no firm in this city may enter a person's name here by default and no firm may read silence as agreement, and that the process does not cover how a thing is done to a body, and does not cover a person's own care, and does not cover two people arguing about money.**

**"Hands up if you are for. Not if you have not thought about it. If you have not thought about it, hands down, and that is not against me, that is with me."**

They went up together. It took about four seconds for about nine hundred arms to reach about shoulder height and it made a noise like a person moving a long heavy thing along a floor and not like anything else.

The woman of about fifty-two who chairs read the rule out loud before she read the number: that a thing is carried by more hands for than against and not by more than every hand in the room, and that a person is one hand and that is the whole of what a person is here.

---

**The woman who took the numbers is called Doreen Teel, she is about fifty-nine, she is a clerk, and she has counted at about four meetings in this city in about nine years, and she is not on anything, and she was given the sheet and a pencil at about ten past nine by a woman of about twenty-two who did not explain it and did not offer to stay.**

She ruled the first column in about four minutes and she said the first number out loud, once, and then she said it again because about four hundred people in that hall had not heard the first time.

**"Four hundred and eighty-one in favour. Two hundred and nine against."**

About nine hundred people in that hall did not move and about eleven of them put their hands up after she had finished saying it, and she said the second number again, and did not add the eleven, and said why.

**"I have said it twice because about four hundred of you did not hear the first time and I would like the record to show that I said it twice and not that the number changed. I am not adding the eleven. A person who puts a hand up after a number has been read out loud is not part of that number and I am not going to pretend they are."**

The first thing was carried. It was not close and she said so in the same breath as the number and nobody argued with her about it.

---

A woman of about twenty-nine stood up at about a quarter past ten, on the aisle side, and asked a question and got an answer in a face and was not thanked for asking.

**"Does the office survive this."**

**"No."**

**"Right."**

**"I have not finished. That office is not in front of this room and was never in front of this room and it is not on either of the two things you have just voted on, and I am the only person in this city who may remove the person who holds it and I am not going to do it in a room with nine hundred people in it, and its eleven weeks run out on a day that two people in this city know and neither of them is me."**

**"That is two answers again."**

**"It is one answer and one piece of housekeeping, and if you would like to ask me in a room of nine I will come, and I will come to that room and not to this one, and about four of you know where it is."**

Nobody thanked her. She sat down and about eleven people who had been sitting with their hands up put them down, and it was not clear whether that was agreement or relief, and she did not look round to find out.

---

**The second thing was put to that room at about twenty past ten and it is the thing a woman of about forty-four who keeps a register for a court in this city had put to about four hundred people on the Friday, and she was in that hall on the Monday and she was not on the platform and she did not want to be on the platform, and she said on the Friday that asking anyway was the honest end of the evening.**

A man of about forty-four who drains was in that hall and he was at the back on the aisle side and he did not put his hand up and he told two people afterwards, in about nine seconds each, that he had not, and that he had been right.

**"Hands up if you are for. If you do not know, that is against, because the thing in front of you needs people to know."**

That was not the same rule as the first one and the woman of about fifty-two who chairs said so out loud before the hands went up, in about eleven words, and about nine hundred people heard the difference and the difference is why the number is what it is.

They went up in two waves and the second wave was smaller and slower and about a hundred of the people who had them up on the Friday had them up on the Monday, and the hall made less noise than it had for the first one.

---

**The clerk ruled for about four minutes. She put the pencil down in the middle of it, which she had not done on the first column, and she said, before she read anything, that the two columns did not come to the same total and that she was going to say the two totals and the difference and that nobody was to think the difference was a fault.**

**"Six hundred and ninety on the first and eight hundred and fifty-three on this one, and the difference is a hundred and sixty-three, and those are people who had a hand up for one thing and had it down for the other, and I am not going to ask any of them which of the two they meant, because they have told me already and they have not told me the same thing twice."**

**"Four hundred and seventeen in favour. Four hundred and thirty-six against."**

The hall did not make a noise. That is in the record because it is true and because about four hundred people in that room have described the moment since and every one of them has described the noise and there was not one.

**There was one moment in the middle of the second count that the clerk dealt with, and she dealt with it in about nine seconds, and it is in the record because a woman in that hall has since described the ruling as the fairest thing that has happened to her in about nine years, and she is about thirty-one and was holding a child of about two on her left arm and could not get a hand up and would not put the child down.**

**"I am not asking you to put that child anywhere. I am asking whether a person holding a person is counted as a person."**

**"You are counted as not knowing, because the rule I read out said that if you do not know that is against, and you have not told me what you know, and I am not going to guess at it in a room of about nine hundred."**

**"Then I will tell you what I know and you will write it in the other column, and I have watched you write in two columns all evening."**

**"No, and here is the reason and it is not a rule anybody made: a column I write on your word is a column anybody can be argued out of by anybody who argues well, and I am a woman with a pencil and I am not going to hand that over to about nine hundred people who are all tired and who will all be asked about it by somebody tomorrow."**

**"That is a good answer."**

**"It is the correct answer and it is not a good one, and I have said the difference four times today."**

**The man of about twenty-two was about nine rows from the back and he saw the whole of that from where he was standing and he did not write one word of it down, and that is the fourth time this month he has been about nine feet from a true sentence that somebody wanted kept.**

**"The thing is refused. It needed more for than against and it has got less, and the number against is four hundred and thirty-six and I have said it once and I am not going to say it twice, because the second time would be for me."**

The woman of about fifty-two who chairs said, in a face, in about nine seconds, that the Commons in this city kept its own process, and that a person in it was asked and answered, and that the answer stayed where the person had put it, and that nothing in that room had changed any of that, and that about four hundred and thirty-six people had made sure of it, and that she was not going to use the word *defeated* about anybody.

**Nobody thanked the woman of about fifty-nine with the sheet. About four people said the word thanks to her in the general noise within about a minute and she has said that she does not remember one of them and that she is not owed one and that a count is not a favour.**

---

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted, and did not say, in that hall, was to be the man who carried the numbers in, and he was asked to, in a face, at about ten past ten, by a woman of about twenty-nine who has nine people to go and stand with on a pavement.**

**"Will you take the sheet in at the end. There is no answer, there is only a man carrying a piece of paper across a room, and I have to go outside."**

**"No."**

**"You are quick."**

**"I have been quick about this for about four years and it has not got better."**

**"Then I will ask the man who runs the door, and he has been on his feet since about six and he has not sat down once, and I do not know his name either."**

**"You could ask him and he would do it."**

**"Yes. Thank you."**

**The cost, named exactly, and it is his and not the woman's and not the man at the door's: the sheet went in at about four minutes later than it should have, and about four hundred and thirty-six people in that hall stood and waited those four minutes for a number, and that is the cost of the four minutes and it is real and it is not going to be anybody's fault, and what it bought was that he was a man who was not in that room doing something, and there is a version of the rest of his life in which he can say that he was there, and he gave that up on purpose at about ten past ten on a Monday, and a man of twenty-nine will go and ask a man he does not know.**

He was at the back of that hall, and the doors were shut at about ten, and about two hundred and sixty people were outside them, and the man who shut them had been standing since about ten past eight and had a torch in his pocket and a sheet with two empty columns and a word at the top of it and no name anywhere on it, and not one of the four decisions a man of twenty-two took in the four days before that Monday was the right decision and not one of them was about that door.
