# Chapter 547 — Four Words Are Said Out Loud In A Face In A Hall For The First Time In This City By A Person Who Is Entitled To Say Them And About Nine Hundred People Hear Them And Nobody Explains Them And Nobody Repeats Them Afterwards

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and the same hall with more people at its doors than had been at its doors on the Monday, and its shape was four words said out loud at about a quarter to one and the whole of the rest of the afternoon spent on the discovery that nobody in the building could say what any of them meant.**

---

Bench in Lattice Ward to two, the counter two to six, the rooms off the service road from about half past six, and a public hall off the market street from about ten past nine until about half past four. The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and forty-five days, one hundred and thirty-five weeks to the day, four days on from the rooms, and the rail takes two.

**About four hundred of the people who had been outside on the Monday came back on the Thursday, and the doors of that hall were not shut, and about two hundred and sixty people stood in the doorway and on the step and in the road for about an hour and a half and heard everything, and that was a decision taken on the Wednesday evening in about nine minutes by a woman of about thirty-six who lets the hall out, and she gave the reason to the man of about thirty-four who opened that door and who did not argue with it and who said he was glad and then said that he was not glad and that both of those were true.**

**He stood at the north door from about ten past nine until about four o'clock with the same folded sheet in his hand and he did not use it, and about two hundred and sixty people looked at it as they came past and about nine of them asked him what the two columns were for and he said that they were for a Monday and that Monday has been and go in.**

**"Who is speaking."**

**"That is the chair. Her name is Ilse Rooker and I have only had it since Wednesday, because a man with a lanyard said it within nine feet of me on the forecourt and I heard it, and I have not said it back to anybody who asked me before today because I wanted to see whether it was a name or a job."**

**"And is it."**

**"It is a name. It is a woman's name and she is on a platform at the front of a room and about nine hundred people are in it and nobody has said it out loud before."**

---

The business on the Thursday was to enter a result against a name, and the entering was done by a man of about thirty-three with a clipboard who had been a minute-taker at four meetings in this city, and the result was on two lines and the two lines were the first thing carried and the second thing refused and the two numbers were the numbers of the Monday and the count of the Monday was in the record and was not a name.

**And the man with the clipboard said, at about twenty to one, that he could not enter it, and that he was sorry, and that he had entered it that way about nine times in this city in about nine years and that every one of those nine had been against a description, and that a description is not a thing you can put a decision against, and that he had never before been asked to.**

**"Then somebody had better tell me what to put it against."**

**"I am going to say the name of the body that decided it, and I am not going to explain it, and if anybody in this room explains it afterwards I will say out loud that they did not have the office to, and I will say it however senior they are, and I have thought about that for two days and I have decided that is what the office is for."**

The man with the clipboard said that about eleven people in that hall said something at the same time and that the room heard it as one noise.

**"The Commons Standing Committee."**

That is the whole of it. Four words, in a face, at about a quarter to one on the Thursday of the second week, said by a person who is entitled to say them, and heard by about nine hundred people in a hall and by about two hundred and sixty in a doorway and on a step and in a road.

**Nobody said a word afterwards. The man with the clipboard wrote it down and spelled it wrong on the first attempt and she made him spell it right, and then she said, to about nine hundred people, that she was not going to explain it, and that there was nothing in it to explain, and that about four people in this city were going to be asked to explain it within the hour and that they should not.**

---

**About four people did try, in the hour after that, at the back of that hall and in the road outside it, and every one of them was stopped by a person who had been in the room, and what they were told was the same four words and the fact that there was nothing else, and about nine hundred people left that building knowing a name and not knowing anything at all about it.**

**About two hundred and sixty of those people were in the doorway and on the step and in the road, and they got it through a wall of bodies, and a man of about fifty-eight who had come into this city four days ago for a funeral and had been in that hall on the Monday in the front nine rows stood at the back of the step and said, to a woman of about twenty-nine beside him, that he has been in this city about nine years and has never once heard a body in it say its own name out loud, and that he did not think he has ever heard one in any city, and the woman of about twenty-nine said that she had not either and that she had voted.**

**"Then you have done something I have not done."**

**"About four hundred other people have done it too and I have been told the number twice and I have not written it anywhere, because I do not write things down."**

**"You have it exactly."**

**"I have it exactly and I am not going to say it to anybody and you are not going to say it to anybody, and that is a strange thing for two people to be doing on a step outside a hall."**

They stood on that step until about two o'clock and neither of them said one word about the other one again that afternoon.

**At about a quarter to two a man of about thirty-one with a notebook came out through the crowd, and he had been trying for about an hour to get to the front of that step, and when he got there he asked two people the same question and got the same answer from both of them, which is four words, and the four words were that the name had been said once and not explained, and he wrote that down, and then he asked a third person, who was about thirty-four and had not been in the hall, and the third person said that he had heard a woman with a dead microphone say a name and that he did not know anybody's name, and the man with the notebook crossed out about nine words and did not look up again.**

**"You are going to leave that in."**

**"I am going to leave that in because it happened, and because a man who crosses out only the parts that are wrong ends up with a piece that is right and useless, and I have written about four hundred of those and I have not printed one."**

**"Then print this one."**

**"I will print this one, and it will have no names in it, and about four hundred people who were in that hall will be in it, and the four words will be in it once, and that is the whole of what anybody will know on Monday morning."**

A woman of about thirty-four who works in a launderette in another district said to a man of about forty-four who repairs boilers, in the road, in about nine seconds, that she thought it meant the council. He said that he had been in the room and that the council had a name with a word on it that people could look up, and that the four words were not that, and that he was sorry and that he could not help.

A man of about twenty-six who is a bin man said to a woman of about twenty-nine that it sounded like the thing that had a chair with a dead microphone on it, and the woman of about twenty-nine said that was the same thing and that she was in the room and that it had not been explained in the room and that she was not going to explain it outside the room, and he said right.

**At about ten past two, at the north door, a man of about thirty-four who has run that door on two of the last three days asked a man of about twenty-two standing in the doorway what the four words meant, in a face, the way you would ask anybody.**

**"I do not know."**

**"You are the one everybody comes to."**

**"I am the one everybody comes to and I learned that woman's name on Wednesday and I have been in this room once in eleven years and I have not read anything about any of it, and I am not going to stand in a doorway at half past two on a Thursday and make up four words for a man who has been shut out of two meetings in one week because I would sound useful."**

**The cost of that, named exactly, and it is his and there is nobody to carry any of it: it is the first time in about four years he has said those two words to a person who wanted something off him, and the man of about thirty-four who ran a door for four hours on the Monday and did not sit down and shut two doors on about two hundred and sixty people in about eleven seconds has told about nine people in that road that the man who writes things down for people does not know what it means, and there is no correcting it and he did not try and he has not been asked to and will not offer.**

---

The result was entered at about half past two and read out at about ten to three in about nine words, and the office of the woman of about twenty-four was not on the minute and she said so herself, in a face, to about nine hundred people, and it is the third time in four days that she has said it and nobody thanked her for saying it either time.

**The doors of that hall were opened at about ten past nine and they were shut at about four o'clock by a man of about thirty-four who shut them himself and who did not have to be asked, and about two hundred and sixty people were still on the step and in the road when they shut, and he did not tell them to move and he did not apologise, and about four people asked him why the doors were shut when the meeting had not finished, and he said the same four words each of the four times, and the four words are not a name.**

**"The meeting has finished."**

**"It has not, he is still reading."**

**"The meeting has finished and the reading is not the meeting, and I have shut about nine hundred doors in this city in about nine years and I have never once known which of those two things was true, and today somebody told me at about ten past nine and I have believed her and I would like to be left alone."**

The man of about thirty-three with the clipboard was asked, afterwards, by a man of about thirty-one with a notebook, whether the name had a history, and he said that he had been a minute-taker at four meetings in about nine years and that he had never had to write it, and that he did not know, and that he was not going to find out on a Thursday afternoon because it was not his office.
