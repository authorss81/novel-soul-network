# Chapter 544 — The Second Of The Two Things Is Put To A Face In A Hall With The Doors Open, And It Is True, And A Man Who Drains Says No In About Four Seconds And Is On Nothing

**The Friday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and the floor of a public hall with its doors open from about half past seven, and its shape was a woman of about forty-four putting one true thing to about four hundred people and a man of about forty-four answering it before the sentence was finished, and afterwards nobody in that hall said a number.**

---

Eleven jobs for the Friday, eleven dates written in beside them, eleven people at the counter, and the last of them at about ten past five. Bench in Lattice Ward until two, that counter two to six, the service-road rooms from about half past six, and the floor of a public hall off the market street from about half past seven. Nine and then ten is what the bench charges an hour and it is wrong and it reached nobody. **That flat's heating is on and dearer, four names are on the tenancy, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is still owed by nobody and will be owed by nobody when the month ends.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and thirty-five days, one hundred and thirty-three weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail on that counter front is nine hundred and thirty-nine days, one hundred and thirty-four weeks and one day, four days on from the rooms, in a rail that holds two.

---

The second thing was put to that room by a woman of about forty-four who keeps a register of small claims for a court in this city, and she has kept it for about eleven years, and she is not on anything, and nobody sent her.

**She said in about nine seconds that she was going to say the whole of it in about four minutes and that she was going to say the parts of it that were true first, because there was a version of this evening in which everybody in that hall agreed with her for the wrong reasons and she would rather be agreed with for the right ones and be beaten.**

**The second thing, entire: that there be one place in this city where every answer anybody gives to anybody is entered, once, and kept, and that any person may ask whether an answer of theirs is in it and may be told, and that the place be kept by the standing committee and be a place a person can walk into without an appointment.**

**And then she said the true parts, and every one of them was true, and she said them in about ninety seconds and she did not enjoy saying them.**

**"A woman of about eighty-one was in a lift between two floors in the first of four towns on a Monday morning and a man of about fifty-two got her out in about four minutes with a bar and a lamp and could not afterwards tell anybody whether he was allowed to have done it, and the answer came back in fifty-six hours and fifty-one minutes and cost a woman of about thirty-one seventy-eight pounds and a week of a car with a clutch that is not right."**

**"A ramp on a bus would not go down in the second of those towns and a man of about thirty-eight held it with his knee and a stranger's hand and got a woman in a wheelchair off at the third stop and filled in the correct form in the correct box and marked the ramp out of service, and he was told at about four that he could not be told whether the bus could go out again, and the answer came back in ninety-six hours and twenty minutes."**

**"A woman of about fifty-eight with a shop had about nine hours of no cold behind her counter and rang about four people and one of them is not answering, and there is nowhere in this city to leave a message for her because there is nowhere in this city to leave a message for anybody."**

**"And a man of about thirty-seven came for a funeral in this city and was handed eleven printed paragraphs in about four minutes, and he had carried them four days, and that is the only one of the four where the answer arrived before anybody stopped waiting."**

The hall was very quiet for about nine seconds.

**"Every one of those four is a fact and I have read all four of them in a leaflet that a woman in this city printed because nobody printed them. And if there were one place in this city where every answer went, then all four of those people could have been found in about four seconds by a person with a grievance, and I have not got an argument against that and I have had a fortnight to make one."**

---

**The man who drains is about forty-four and he has drained this city for nineteen years and is on nothing and came because a man in a van told him on the Thursday that there would be a queue, and he said no in about four seconds, out loud, in a face, to about four hundred people, before the man holding the floor had finished reading.**

**"No."**

**"Sir—"**

**"I have said it and you can put a line in the book, and I will tell you why so that it is not just a man being difficult. I am the only man in this city who knows where the water goes down eleven streets. Not the best man. The only one. And I know it because for nineteen years the only person who has ever asked me where it goes is me, at about six in the morning, with a lamp."**

**"That is an argument for keeping it somewhere."**

**"It is an argument for keeping me. And in about a year there will be a book and I will be sixty-one and the book will say that the water goes down Ferrier Street and it will say it because a person in this hall decided to be helpful, and the book will be right, and the water will not."**

He let the man holding the floor get four words out and then took the floor back without asking anybody.

**"And there is the second thing and it is the real one and I want it written down that I said the first one to be polite. If every answer in this city goes in a book, then a person who is frightened of a book is a person who says yes to a book, and the book fills up with yeses that are not yeses, and that is worse than the mess we have, because the mess we have is at least honest about being a mess."**

The woman of about forty-four who keeps the register stood still for about four seconds and then said, in a face, to about four hundred people, that he was right, that she had been waiting a fortnight for that sentence and had not made it, and that she was going to vote no herself if anybody in that hall ever let her vote, and that asking anyway was the honest end of the evening and not the dishonest one.

**Nobody said his name. He gave it once, to a woman with a notebook, and it is Emrys Tallow, and she wrote it down and he asked her to spell it, and she spelled it, and that is the only record of it and it is in a notebook and not in a book.**

---

**No number was said out loud in that hall on the Friday, and the reason was given out loud by the man holding the floor in about nine seconds: a number spoken on a Friday by a man who does not take numbers is a number a person can carry home and use, and he had been in about nine rooms where a number like that was carried home and used, and the number was being taken on the Monday by a person whose whole work is taking numbers and writing them on a sheet.**

**What is true and was not said on the Friday: that when the man of about forty-four sat down, about four hundred people in that hall had refused a thing that was correct, and it is the largest number of people in this city who have ever said no to a thing that was correct, and the number of them is four hundred and thirty-six, and it will be said on the Monday by a woman of about fifty-nine with a sheet and not by anybody in this hall tonight.**

The doors stayed open all evening. About nine people were turned away at about nine o'clock because there was no room left with the doors open and nobody inside had been told to leave a gap, and the man of about thirty-six who lets the hall out wrote nine names on the back of her own hand in biro and did not know what to do with them and put the hand in her pocket.

---

**The man of about thirty-four who drains left at about nine and went out into a road with about four hundred people in it and stood about nine feet from the door and did not say one word to anybody, and about eleven people said the word register to him in about half an hour, and he said no to every one of the eleven in about four seconds each, and not one of the eleven had heard the two true things she had said at about ninety seconds, and about nine of the eleven had not been in that hall at all and had come because a leaflet was printed and somebody at a canteen had read out a date.**

**A woman of about thirty-one who has been in this city about nine years and who came for the second time in her life caught the tail of that in the road and said, to nobody, that the whole difficulty of a city is that about four hundred people will believe a thing that was said inside a room to about four hundred other people, and that the two hundred and sixty outside had heard a man with a brush repeat a printed sentence on a Saturday and had not heard a woman of about forty-four read out four disasters with dates in them, and that this is not anybody's fault and that it is simply what happens at the edge of a room.**

Nobody in that road thanked her either.

**The hall emptied in about forty minutes after that and it emptied the way a hall empties, which is all at once and then not at all, and the man of about thirty-four who drains was the first one out of the door and the last one into the road, and he stood about nine feet back against the wall of a shop that sells nothing anybody goes in for and let about four hundred people go past him, and about eleven of them said the word register to him on the way by and he said no to all eleven in about four seconds each, and the twelfth was a woman of about thirty-one who said nothing at all and walked on, and he has not told anybody that he noticed the twelfth and he will not.**

**The man of about twenty-two was about nine feet behind him and said nothing to him then or afterwards, and the reason is that a man who has just refused the largest thing anybody offered him in a year does not need a second man in a road telling him what it cost, and because he had been told that once already in this city and had not been the man who said it, and because there is a version of this where a man of twenty-two turns up in about nine hundred people's accounts of a Friday evening as the person who was standing behind the man who said no, and that is a different document from the one anybody wants written.**

---

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted, and did not say, standing at the back of that hall, was a sentence of his own in a woman's notebook, and he had wanted it since a Monday morning on a pavement across from this building, and about four hundred people in that hall could have watched him want it.**

**A woman of about thirty-one with a notebook came through the crowd at about nine o'clock and asked him, in a face, in about nine seconds, for one sentence, and he refused in about nine seconds, and the cost of the refusal is named exactly and it is not the refusal.**

**"Then I will write that a thing was refused at a counter in Lattice Ward."**

**"That is correct and that is a fair way to put it."**

**"And it will not say who refused or why, and there is no way in the world to add either afterwards, and if it is the only sentence of yours that ever appears on paper anywhere, then that is what you have bought with nine seconds."**

**"Then that is what I have bought."**

She wrote it, and the piece went in on the Thursday, and it is about a hall and a queue and a man who said the room would be full by eleven and he was outside it, and his four words are the second paragraph of it and the refusal is a line in the fourth and he has read it and has not told anybody in this city what the fourth line says.

**She asked him before she wrote it, once, whether the second of the two things would overrule anything a person had already said to the woman of about twenty-four, and he said nine words and they were these, and they are the same nine words he has now said four times this year: that he cannot say what it will overrule, and that she is not the person it will be put to, and that anybody who brings a form into a corridor should be told the person who may read it is not behind that counter.**

The board on two nails is nineteen lines and no twentieth. The newest is six hundred and thirty-one days, ninety weeks and one day, and it is the only line of the nineteen that has ever ended on a zero. The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and eighty-three days, sixty-nine weeks to the day. The hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and sixty-eight days, eighty-one weeks and one day. The man at the north wall has been there five hundred and forty-one days, seventy-seven weeks and two days, with his bag on the floor, and has said nothing this week. The ask is six hundred and twenty-five days, eighty-nine weeks and two days. Nine copied page fronts are five hundred and one days, seventy-one weeks and four days. The room off that service road is nine hundred and thirty-five days and the card in the rail is nine hundred and thirty-nine, and they are not one object and are not one figure. The gap between him and the woman of about twenty-four is three hundred and fifteen days, forty-five weeks to the day, not closed and not shorter.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
