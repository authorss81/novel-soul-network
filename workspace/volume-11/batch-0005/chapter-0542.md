# Chapter 542 — The First Of The Two Things Is Put To A Face In A Room With About Nine Chairs In It And Described By A Woman Who Keeps A Reading Room, And About Four People Find It Fair And About Four Find It Something Else

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a corridor outside a room with about nine chairs in it from about ten past seven, and its shape was one thing being explained once, by a person with an ordinary job, to about nine people who had each brought a different reason for being in that building on a Wednesday evening.**

---

Work booked for the Wednesday: eleven pieces, eleven dates, eleven through the counter, the last of them at about five past five. Bench until two, the counter two to six, the four rooms off the service road from about half past six, and about twenty minutes on a landing in another district. Nine and ten is the bench rate, wrong, and it reached nobody. **That flat's heating is on and dearer, four names share the tenancy, and a fortnight a cook put down out of her own money is owed to nobody and will be owed to nobody at the end of this month.** Those service-road rooms are nine hundred and thirty-three days, one hundred and thirty-three weeks and two days, four rooms and one at the back, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and thirty-seven days, one hundred and thirty-three weeks and six days, four days older than the rooms, and the rail holds two.

---

The room is a first floor over a shop in this city that sells nothing anybody goes in for, and a tenants' association has had it on a Wednesday for about nine years, and about nine people were in it, and one chair had nobody in it, and the door was propped with a folded piece of card.

The person who had the floor said in about nine seconds that there were two things going to be decided in this city on the Monday and that he had neither of them and would be talking out of a leaflet, and he gave the leaflet round and it was the fourth printing.

**A woman of about fifty-three named Hallie Strand came at about ten past seven, and she is the woman who unlocks a public reading room on a wide road six days a week and sweeps it out at about six, and the paper that pays her calls the job caretaker, and about two hundred people a week sit in that room for two hours and buy nothing at all.**

**"I am not on anything."**

**"I did not say you were."**

**"I say it because I have been in about nine rooms like this one since the spring and every one of them started by telling me what I am for."**

**"Then you already know what you are for and we can start later."**

---

**She put the first of the two things to the face of that room, and she put it in about eleven sentences, and every one of the eleven was true, and she said afterwards that she had checked them in the morning against a copy and that if a person in the room thought one of them was wrong she would like to be told.**

Here is the whole of it: that the Commons in this city keeps its own consent process, and that the process is that a person is asked and answers, and that the answer is left where the person put it, and that no firm may enter a person's name here by default and no firm may treat silence as agreement, and that anybody who has been entered may ask how and may be told.

**About four people in that room said, out loud, in about nine seconds each, that that was fair.**

And the four who said it were not on anything either, and one of them was a man of about forty-four who repairs boilers and one was a woman of about thirty-eight who works in a launderette in another district and one was a man of about twenty-six who is a bin man and one was a woman of about sixty-one who has never voted for anything in her life and said so.

**About four other people in that room said, out loud, in about four seconds each, that that was something else.**

And what the something else was, said by the four, was not that it was wrong, and this is the whole of what they said and none of it was a speech against it:

**"It is fair and it has a hole in it the shape of being frightened. If a person is asked and answers, and the answer is a no, then somebody comes back a week later and asks again with a different face, and the second asking is not this thing and the person who did it can say it was not this thing."**

**"It is fair and the person who has to say no has to say it to the person who wants the work, and this city has about four hundred competent people and every one of them can be sent to a door, and there is not one door in this room today."**

**"It is fair and I have been entered by default once, in about nine years, by a firm that did it in a font on the back of a piece of paper, and nobody has ever said sorry to me, and the second column being empty is not the same as the box being empty."**

**"It is fair and I would like to know who pays for the asking, because somebody pays for the asking, and if it is the people who are asked then it is not fair whatever the eleven sentences say."**

The man on the floor said that the fourth of those was the best question anybody had put in that room in about nine years, and that he did not have the answer to it, and that anybody who wanted to put it in front of about nine hundred people on the Monday could write it on the back of a leaflet and hand it in.

**He read both of the two things out again at about half past seven, in the same words, in about four minutes, and about nine people in that room heard the second reading and about four of them had heard the first, and when he had finished the woman of about fifty-three who had described the first of them put her hand up and asked him a question about the second of them, and the room turned round and looked at her, and she had not been in the room in about nine years.**

**"You have described that one four times in this city and you have not once put a person in front of it."**

**"No."**

**"Why not."**

**"Because there is no person in front of it to put. That is what it is and that is the objection to it and about four people in this room have said so in about nine words each and I am one of them."**

**"Then somebody should put a person in front of it."**

**"Somebody should, and I have not got one, and I am not going to invent one at half past seven on a Wednesday in a room where nine people have come for nine different reasons, and if you have got one then write it on the back of a leaflet and hand it in and I will read it out on the Monday whether I agree with it or not."**

Nobody thanked the woman of about fifty-three for describing it. She said at the door, to nobody in particular, that she had a reading room to open in the morning and that describing a thing accurately was not a favour to it and was not a favour against it.

---

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted, and did not say, on that landing at about ten past seven, was to be in that room.**

There was one chair in it with nobody in it and the door was propped with a folded piece of card and about nine people were inside and not one of them knew his name, and he stood on the landing for about eleven minutes and listened to two sentences of it through the gap and then went down the stairs.

The woman of about twenty-five who washes up was against the wall on that landing with her hands in her sleeves, waiting to be let in, and she had asked him for a thing twice in about four months and had not been given it, and he has never asked her what her name is and she has never offered it, and that is the arrangement between them, and it has been in place since the spring and neither of them has ever mentioned it.

**He did not say her name, because he does not know it. He did not ask, because the asking would be a thing he did so that she would know he had come up a stair. The cost of not saying it is that she was on that landing for about twenty minutes and went in last, and she has asked him for the same thing twice and will ask a third time, and there is a version of the next four months in which she does not, and it will be his doing and there will be no scene about it and no record of it anywhere.**

She went in at about half past seven and sat on the empty chair, and the man on the floor gave her the same eleven sentences he had given the other eight people out of a fourth printing of the same leaflet, and she read them off the back of it with one finger, and she did not say one word about them, and about four people in that room noticed that she had not said one word about them and about nine of the nine did not.

---

The hall the notice was painted on is a shuttered shop and a brick and about two hundred and sixty people, and the whole of it is printed on a leaflet that is the fourth printing, and the leaflet was on the counter on the Wednesday and the man of about twenty-two gave one to about four people without saying anything about where it came from.

**A man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts came in at about half past four and asked him, in a face, whether the second thing that is going to be decided would overrule anything a person had already said to the woman of about twenty-four.**

**"I cannot tell you what it will overrule and I can tell you that she is not the person it will be put to, and that if anybody comes to you with a form in their hand and asks you to agree to it in a corridor, the answer to that is that the person who may read it for you is not me."**

**"Then what is your answer to it."**

**"That is my answer and it is nine words and it is the same one I have given about four times this year and it is not going to get better with practice."**

Nobody thanked him for it and the man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts went out with the leaflet in his hand, and he did not put it in a drawer, and he has still not signed anything, and his name is still in a box at the foot of a form in a locked drawer in this city that has not been opened.

The board on two nails has nineteen lines and no twentieth. The newest of them is six hundred and twenty-nine days, eighty-nine weeks and six days, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever ended on a zero. The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and eighty-one days, sixty-eight weeks and five days. The hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and sixty-six days, eighty weeks and six days. The man at the north wall has been there five hundred and thirty-nine days, seventy-seven weeks to the day. The ask is six hundred and twenty-three days, eighty-nine weeks to the day. Nine copied page fronts are four hundred and ninety-nine days, seventy-one weeks and two days. The room off that service road is nine hundred and thirty-three days and the card in the rail is nine hundred and thirty-seven, and the two have never been the same figure on a single day of its life and are not the same object. The gap between him and the woman of about twenty-four is three hundred and thirteen days, forty-four weeks and five days, not closed and not shorter.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
