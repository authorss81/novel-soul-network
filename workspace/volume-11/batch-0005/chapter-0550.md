# Chapter 550 — The Fortieth Sitting Of A Room Above A Line, And The Book Opens, And One Ordinary Sentence Goes In From A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Mends Pumps And Who Is Not Thanked, And The Count Is Forty-Five Announced Of Which Forty Correspond

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a second floor above a line in another district from about ten until about eleven, and its shape was nine items at four minutes each and a tenth thing that was not an item, in which a man who had come four days on a bus said a sentence to a woman.**

---

Eleven jobs for the Wednesday, eleven dates against them, eleven people through that counter, and the last of them at about half past five. Bench in Lattice Ward to two, the counter two to six, a second-floor room above a line in another district from about ten to about eleven, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate and it is wrong and it went to nobody. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer than it was in the winter, four names on the tenancy are carrying the rise, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and will be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and fifty-four days, one hundred and thirty-six weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and fifty-eight days, one hundred and thirty-six weeks and six days, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

---

About eleven people came up that stair. Six had been in the same chair on the last Wednesday and five had not, and one of the five had not been in that room in nineteen years and came for a reason he gave.

Nine chairs went into a curve with the two ends turned in, and the ninth of them is the one hard against the wall with its back to the whole room, and it did not move in the hour, and nobody looked at it more than once, and whoever moves it is not named here and is not going to be.

There was a table, an unlidded tin with no lock on it and never one, and the book face up inside her right hand's reach from about ten until about eleven.

Nine items at four minutes each, and eight of them went to the chairs the way eight of them have gone for nineteen years.

**The eight were, in the order they went: a man of about thirty-one who had a question about a form and was told in about nine seconds what the question was actually about; a woman of about forty-four who wanted to say a thing about a person she works beside and was told, kindly, that it was not hers to bring; a man of about nineteen who had come for the third time and had not spoken on either of the first two and spoke on the third, in about eleven words, and about four people in that room looked at him; a woman of about thirty-six who asked whether a thing that had happened to her in a room two districts away counted, and was told in about four seconds that it counted and that it had counted for a long time, and did not cry and nobody expected her to; a man of about fifty-eight who said one sentence and would not say a second; a woman of about twenty-seven who wanted to know whether anybody had to believe her and was told, in about nine seconds, that nobody in that room had to believe anybody and that was the only thing in nineteen years anybody had got right about it; a man of about thirty-eight in a third chair who said nothing; and a person of about sixty-one who has been in that room in nineteen years and who said the one thing in the hour that anybody said twice, and both times it was the number seven.**

**The ninth thing went to a chair as well and it is not in the record of the eight because it came after a woman of about fifty-eight had said her one sentence and had been about to be thanked, and the man on the floor said, before anybody had started, that nobody was going to be thanked this morning because it had not been the case for nineteen years and he was not going to start on the fortieth, and about eleven people in that room said that was the correct decision and about four of them had wanted to do the opposite.**

**The tenth thing was not an item. It was a man of about thirty-four who came up that stair on the Sunday night with about four days of bus travel behind him, and who had been in that building before, and who did not come for a Wednesday, and who had been in a hall in this city on the Monday and had said nine words in about nine houses in a town four days away and had not been asked one question in any of them.**

---

**"I was in that hall on the Monday."**

**"I know. I was not in it and I have been told about it by about eleven people, and every one of the eleven has told me a different number, and the number I have heard most often is four hundred and seventeen."**

**"That is the number of the hands that went up for it and not against it. I was with the four hundred and thirty-six."**

**"Then that is a number too and I will not put it in."**

**"I am not asking you to put it in. I am telling you because nobody else in this city has asked me and I have not said it to anybody in about a fortnight and it has been in my head the whole time."**

**"Then say the thing you came to say and I will write down what you say and not what you have just said, and the reason for that is that in nineteen years this book has not got a vote in it and it is not going to start with one."**

**"Put down that I was in that hall on the Monday, because in about a year I am going to be a man who says he was in a room where nothing happened."**

She wrote it. It took her about nine seconds and she wrote the whole of it in one line and she did not ask him a single question about the Monday, and the line is in the book and it is the only thing in the book this year that is about a person saying he was in a room.

**Nobody in that room said thank you. The man of about thirty-four went down the stair at about eleven and did not speak on the landing and did not speak in the street, and about four people who were in that room have since described the moment and not one of the four accounts has a thank you in it, and he has not asked anybody whether it went in and he is not going to.**

---

**The book did what it does, and the reason it did it is not a rule and is not evidence of anything: a thing was said to her face. Nine things went to nine chairs. The tenth thing was said to her by a man about four days of bus travel away and she wrote one line of it and stopped, and nobody on that landing has ever been told in advance which mornings it will be in, and it is not written on the landing and it is not in the four columns, and a sequence of sittings is not a rule and is not going to be described by anybody as a change in her.**

**The count was announced at the end and it is announced at a sitting and on no other day: forty-five, of which forty correspond.** Forty of those correspond to the forty Wednesdays this book holds and five of the five predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody.

**On the nine days before this one the count was forty-four of which thirty-nine, and the two states are two states and the difference between them is one and one, and one and one is a count and is not a distance from anything.**

**No number went into the tin and the tin stands at seventy-three and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it has not moved in nineteen years. The book stood at fifty-nine lines before the morning and stands at sixty lines now, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and was not printed as one and is not said out loud in that room, and none of the four of them turns into any of the other three.**

Nobody asked her why it opened. Nobody asked her whether she was all right, and that has not been asked in that room in nineteen years.

---

**He came down that stair at about eleven and there were about four people on the landing and about nine of them had come up the same stair an hour before him, and he did not speak to any of them on the landing, and about a third of an hour later he was at a counter in this city and he had been in a room in another district and had not sat down in it, and about four people in that shop have worked out over about eleven years that he goes up that stair on a Wednesday and comes down it again and that neither half of that is a thing anybody has ever asked him about.**

**The man of about thirty-four who mends pumps went down the same stair about four minutes after him and stopped on the landing, and not to talk to him, and to put a hand flat on the rail for about nine seconds the way a man does when he has been in a warm room and is going out into a street, and then went on down.**

**"You were in it."**

**"I was in the hall on the Monday and I was in that room this morning and I have said one sentence out loud in about eleven years of Wednesdays and it went in a book and nobody thanked me and I am not going to talk about it in a stairwell."**

**"I was not asking about the book."**

**"Then what were you asking about."**

**"About whether the four hundred and thirty-six is the number of people in this city who agree with me."**

**"It is the number of hands that went up in a hall, and a hand is not a person, and about four hundred people in that room did not raise either, and about two hundred and sixty were outside in a doorway because the doors shut, and if you want the number of people in this city who agree with you then there is no number and there is not going to be one, and I have been the only man in four towns who could have told you a number and I have told it four times this year to people who could not use it."**

The man of about thirty-four went on down the stair. He has not said one word about that exchange to anybody, and neither has the man at the counter, and about four people in that stairwell heard about nine seconds of two sentences and not the rest.

**He had about four hours at a bench in Lattice Ward that night and about nine figures on two nails above it that he did not write up, and about four hundred and ninety-nine words he had said out loud in the last four days in rooms that were not his, and one sentence in a book that is not his and that he has not been told about and will not ask about, and a hall of about nine hundred people on the Monday, and a step on the Thursday, and about two hundred and sixty people in a doorway on the Thursday who heard four words and can still not say what they are.**

**At about half past six he was in a room off a service road with a bag of tools and there were about four people in it for the heat of it, and the man of about fifty-one was against the north wall with his bag down beside him at his feet, and had been there five hundred and sixty days, which is eighty weeks to the day, and had not said anything on any day of that week or any week since the spring, and the request he made in a warm room in the spring is still unmade, and the room is warm, and the card in the rail along a counter two districts away is nine hundred and fifty-eight days old and has not been moved and has not been turned over.**

---

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted, and did not say, on that Wednesday, was the second chair, and the reason is the same one it has been every Wednesday since he first came up that stair, and the reason is that a room with a second thing in it is a room that is waiting for him, and a room that is waiting for a man of twenty-two is a room that will hold for him whatever the woman in it says at about eleven, and the woman in it has said in a face that she will not allow that, and the only way to allow it is for him to be somewhere else, and he was at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two.**

A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a rota for a building of about nine flats came up that stair at about ten past ten and sat in the third chair and did not speak in the hour. He has a printed nine-line notice with a signature box at the foot of it in the inside pocket of a coat and it is not on a board anywhere, and he has an inspection at the end of this month, and he has been refused three times in about four months in about four seconds each time before his question was heard, and the woman of about thirty-one behind the counter did not ask him about it, and he did not raise it, and nobody in that room mentioned it, and it is not resolved and it is not resolved by being mentioned here, and it is four days from the end of the month.

The man of about fifty-one was at the north wall of a room in another district with his bag on the floor at his feet and had been there five hundred and sixty days, which is eighty weeks to the day, and he had said nothing on any day this month, and the request he made in the spring, that it be written down somewhere by somebody who is not the man at the counter, is still unmade and is still owed.

**The eighteenth of the nineteen lines on two nails above a bench in Lattice Ward is six hundred and seventy-two days old, and six hundred and seventy-two is an age, and it is also a day something happened, and the day something happened was the day the ask was put to somebody in this city. The ask itself is six hundred and forty-four days old, ninety-two weeks to the day, and it was put to a person and not to an institution, and six hundred and forty-four is an age and it is also a day something happened, and the day something happened was the day that eighteenth line was written on that board. Neither of those two figures is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating is a closed thing in this case with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it, and one of the two is an age and the other is a day and there is no third thing in the sentence.**

The newest of the nineteen is six hundred and fifty days, ninety-two weeks and six days, and it is the only line of the nineteen that has ever closed on a zero. The post at the end of the corridor is five hundred and two days, seventy-one weeks and five days, and it is the second of the twenty-five rows that every record of a working day in this city has carried since the spring and it has not been missed once. Nine copied page fronts are five hundred and twenty days, seventy-four weeks and two days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found. The man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors is five hundred and eighty-seven days into a hold, eighty-three weeks and six days, in another city, with no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority. The gap between him and the woman of about twenty-four is three hundred and thirty-four days, forty-seven weeks and five days, and it is not shorter and it is not ended, and about four days of bus travel each way this week did not shorten it by a minute.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
