# Chapter 546 — A Man Of About Forty-One With A Folder Is Told In A Forecourt In About Eleven Seconds What Four Hundred And Thirty-Six Hands Said Against Him, And He Does Not Bin The Folder And Says Out Loud That He Is Not A Villain And Is Owed Nothing Further, And Pays For A Room On A Friday He Is Not Going To Get

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a concrete forecourt outside a public hall from about ten past eleven, and its shape was a man of about forty-one being told a number in a face in the open air by a person with an ordinary job, and neither of them said anything that made the other smaller.**

---

Eleven on the Wednesday, eleven dates, eleven through the counter, the last of them at about ten past five. Bench in Lattice Ward to two, the counter two to six, the rooms off the service road from about half past six, and the front of a public hall off the market street from about ten past eleven. Nine and ten is the rate and it is wrong and it reached nobody. **The heating in that flat is on, dearer than in the winter, four names are on the tenancy, and a fortnight a cook put down out of her own wages is owed by nobody and will be owed by nobody when the month ends.** Those rooms are nine hundred and forty days, one hundred and thirty-four weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail on the front of that counter is nine hundred and forty-four days, one hundred and thirty-four weeks and six days, four days on from the rooms, in a rail that takes two.

**The forecourt is about nine feet deep and is where a hall's rubbish stands on a Wednesday, and on the Wednesday after a vote about nine people were in it, and a vote leaves a forecourt looking exactly like every other day, and that is most of what happened in it.**

Oren Vey is about forty-one and runs operations for a firm that maintains most of the relay hardware between this city and the coast, and he came out of the north door at about ten past eleven with a folder under his arm and a woman of about thirty-six about two paces behind him with a coat over her arm, and neither of them had been in the hall, and neither of them had been told anything.

**He was told, in a face, in about eleven seconds, by a woman of about fifty-three who unlocks a public reading room on a wide road and is called a caretaker on the paper that pays her, and she was in that forecourt because she had come to ask a question and had not got to it.**

**"Four hundred and thirty-six against. Four hundred and seventeen for. You were refused by nineteen hands."**

He did not look at the folder and he did not look at his own hands.

**"Say the second number again."**

**"No. I am not going to say it twice and I will tell you why, and it is not cleverness: a number said twice is a number that a person can be given by somebody else, and I am the only person in this forecourt who has said it to you and I would like that to stay true."**

**"You have made that a rule."**

**"I have made it a decision about a Wednesday morning, and it is mine and it is not about you."**

**"Then say the first number again."**

**"No."**

**"That is two noes in about nine seconds and you have not been in that hall."**

**"I was not in that hall. I have been in about nine rooms in this city and in about nine of them somebody was wrong on a fact and made the rest of it wrong with it, and I am not going to be a person who corrects a stranger's arithmetic in a forecourt for the sake of being the one who is careful."**

The man of about forty-one stood in that forecourt for about four seconds and then did the thing that is the whole of who he is, which is that he said the cost before anybody asked him for it.

**"I pay for the room. The address, the Friday morning, the door that opens at eight, the number anybody can be given, and the right to say no to a decision in about nine seconds. All of that still stands. Four hundred and thirty-six people in that hall have told you this week that they do not want it, and the offer stands, and I will go on paying for a room on a Friday morning that about eleven people in this city are not going to walk into for about four years, and that is the cost, and it is mine, and I am not asking anybody in this forecourt to feel it."**

**"I am not a villain."**

**"I know that and I have not said otherwise."**

**"I am not a villain and I am not owed anything further, and I have said that to about four people this week and not one of them wrote it down, so I am saying it to you in a forecourt where nobody is writing anything, and if you are the fourth person it does not cost you anything and I will not send you a bill."**

**The folder was not put in a bin. It went under his arm again, and he opened it once, in the open, and took out a printed sheet with about nine columns on it and looked at it and put it back and shut it, and nobody in that forecourt was shown a word of it, and he said why without being asked: that a sheet with nine columns is not a thing to be read out on a pavement to nine people, and that the woman of about twenty-three behind him has been right about it for about two years and that this morning proved her right about it and that he is going to say so to her in about four words before they get to the van.**

The company is not smaller. The company is not going to be smaller this month or the next, and about nine hundred competent people in this city work for it in one way or another, and four hundred and thirty-six hands in a hall did not put any of them out of a job on a Monday.

**The woman of about fifty-three who keeps a reading room said the second half of her argument in that forecourt, in a face, to the man of about forty-one, and it was a claim and not a plan, and nobody agreed with it and nobody wrote it down, and the sentence is whole.**

**"A public answer has to be agreed on before it can be given. That is not a fault in it. That is what makes it a public answer instead of a rumour. And it will not arrive in time. It will not arrive in time for the eighty-one-year-old in the lift, and it will not arrive in time for the ramp, and it will not arrive in time for the shop with no cold, and there is no arrangement of anybody in this city that changes that in the next four months, and I have been in about nine rooms where somebody said it would and I would like somebody to tell me which one."**

**"I am not going to tell you which one."**

**"No. And I notice you did not say it was not true, and about four people in this forecourt heard you not say it, and I am not going to be a person who says that is an admission, because it is not one and I have not earned it."**

A man in a lanyard came out of the north door with a ring of them in his hand and said the name of the woman of about fifty-two who chairs the standing committee to a man of about twenty-four who was standing by a wall, and said it in the ordinary way a person says a name they say every day, and did not look up, and went across the forecourt to a bin and put the ring in his pocket.

**The man at the wall did not know it. He has heard the word chair and the word committee about four hundred times in about eleven years in about nine rooms in this city, and he has never once heard the woman named, and he has not asked anybody, and it was said within nine feet of him on a Wednesday at about ten past eleven, and he is carrying it.**

**"I did not know her name."**

**"I know you did not. I have been the one everybody comes to for about eleven years and I have never once had to say a person's name in this city."**

---

**The woman of about thirty-six who lets the hall out came through that forecourt at about twenty past eleven with a bin bag over her shoulder and stopped, and she has a hand in her pocket and has had a hand in her pocket since Friday night, and the hand has nine names written on the back of it in biro and she has not decided what to do with them and she is not going to decide in a forecourt.**

**"You are the man who was in there."**

**"I was at the back of it."**

**"I do not need you to have been anywhere in particular. I need to say one sentence and I have said it four times since Friday and to about four people and two of them did not hear me, and I would like to say it to somebody who writes things down for a living, and if that is a bad reason then it is a bad reason and I am saying it anyway."**

**"Go on."**

**"There are nine people who came to that hall on Friday night and could not get into it because the doors were open and about four hundred people were already in it, and I have their names on my own hand, and I have not rung any of them, and I am not going to, and I have thought about why I am not going to for about four days and the reason is that if I ring them I am a woman who let a hall and I did not leave a gap, and if I do not ring them then nine people in this city spent an evening standing outside a door for nothing and they will remember that and they will not remember why, and I would like somebody to write down why, and I have not got anybody who will."**

**"I am not anybody who will."**

**"I know. I have worked that out in about four seconds and it is why I said it out loud here instead of going home."**

**A woman of about twenty-three who had been standing about nine paces back since about half past eleven and who works for the man of about forty-one came forward about half a step and said one sentence to the man of about twenty-two and then said three more, and the last one was addressed to her employer and not to the man at the wall.**

**"I have said for two years that a sheet with nine columns is not a thing to read out on a pavement, and I have been right, and he has never once said so, and I would like it noted that on Wednesday morning he said it himself and that he said it to about nine people in about four seconds."**

**"Then it is noted."**

**"By whom."**

**"By nobody. That is what I have just said and I did not mean to say it twice."**

---

**A man of about twenty-nine came in at about twenty-five to twelve, which is the hour he comes, and he was told by a man of about twenty-six with a barrow what had happened in about nine seconds while the barrow was being unloaded, and he asked the man at the wall one question about the second thing and whether it can be undone now that it is refused, and the answer he got was not the answer he wanted and it is the answer that is true.**

**"Yes. In about four days a company man will come round with a form that has about nine columns on it and a box at the foot, and you will be right about what it is, and there will be about four hundred competent people in this city who can each say a paragraph about it, and not one of them will have anywhere to put an objection to it."**

**"That is what the last one said."**

**"It is what about four hundred people have said since Monday and I am the last one and I am saying it at a counter, and I would like it noted that I have said it four times this year and every time it was to somebody who could not do anything about it."**

The man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts said right and took his part away and put it in a cardboard box under the counter, and his name is still in a box at the foot of a form in a drawer in this city that has not been opened.

---

**What he wanted, and did not say, in that forecourt, was to be the one who said any of it to any of them, and the woman of about fifty-three had said it before he got to about eleven feet of her, and he has been the one who is wanted in about four hundred rooms in this city and he was about eleven feet away and about four seconds late.**

**He did not go over to the man of about thirty-eight in a coat who was standing at the other end of the forecourt with a plastic sleeve in an inside pocket and a sheet with a box at the foot of it in the other, and that man has been refused three times in about four months, in about four seconds each time, before his question was heard, and he has an inspection at the end of this month, and the man of twenty-two stood about nine feet from him and decided not to cross the forecourt, and gave himself the reason out loud so that the reason would exist in a place where it could be checked.**

**"There is nothing I can give that man that does not require a woman of about twenty-four to be in a room, and I am not the person who may put her in a room, and if I go over there now then the only thing I will be is the twenty-fourth person to tell him no this year, and the twenty-fourth one will be the one he tells about."**

The man of about thirty-eight in the coat went out of the forecourt at about ten past twelve and the inspection at the end of the month is where it was, and it is not resolved, and it has not been resolved by being mentioned, and nobody in this forecourt mentioned it twice.

**A man of about twenty-six who is a bin man, who said the first of the two things was fair in a room of nine people on a Wednesday in another district, came through the forecourt with a barrow at about a quarter past twelve and said one sentence to nobody about a vote.**

**"That is the first time I have been in a room where somebody counted hands in front of me, and I have been in about nine of those rooms and they all did it with a show of hands, and about nine hundred people raised them, and I am telling you it is not the same as a count of heads."**

**He put the barrow down against the wall and wiped his hands on his thighs and went out through the north door into a forecourt that had been about nine feet of concrete two hours earlier and was now about eleven feet of concrete, and the extra two feet were a man of about forty-one getting into a van. At about half past five the shutter came down in that forecourt and it came down about two feet lower than it usually does, because a man of about twenty-one had been leaning on the guide rail since about half past four and had to be moved, and the man who moved him was the man of about thirty-four who opens that building every morning, and the man of about twenty-one said he was sorry and the man of about thirty-four said that is all right, and neither of them said one word about a sheet with two columns on it that the second of them had been holding on a Monday morning.**

**The board on two nails is nineteen lines and no twentieth. The newest is six hundred and thirty-six days, ninety weeks and six days, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever ended on a zero.
