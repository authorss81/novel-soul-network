# Chapter 521 — Nine Chairs Going Into A Shop Front, And A Limit Read Out Of A Sheet

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a warm room from about half past six, and its shape was chairs going into an empty shop front with the sign taken down, while a woman of twenty-four was asked whether her office could sit in a room nobody had named, and she answered out of a limit on a sheet and not out of an opinion.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven written into the date column and eleven of them attended, the last one at about six in the evening, and the day was a bench in Lattice Ward to about two, that district's counter on to about six, and the four rooms behind a service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, it has been wrong since before this shop opened, and it was said to nobody. **The heat in the flat above that road is on and dearer than it was, four names on one lease carry the difference, and a fortnight a cook put out of her own money is on nobody's account and will be asked for again in the spring.** The room behind the service road is eight hundred and eighty-nine days old, which is one hundred and twenty-seven weeks to the day, and is warm, and is four rooms and a back one behind them.

Along the counter front a card stands in a rail of slots at eight hundred and ninety-three days, which is one hundred and twenty-seven weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two and has held two for eight years. A shop front on a street off a wide one, in which nine chairs and a trestle table stood on a Monday morning with nobody in the building at work, is a fifth thing in this entry, and it is not that room, it is not in that rail, and nothing here joins them.

---

The shop had been a place where shoes were mended, and it had stopped being that in the winter, and the sign came off the wall on the Friday with a cold chisel and two men who did not charge for it because the man who owned it said they could have the brackets.

What was in it by nine on the Monday was a trestle table, nine chairs brought over four streets in a borrowed van, a kettle on an extension lead, a roll of brown paper, and a woman of about fifty-three sitting on the eleventh chair that had come from somewhere else and had not been counted.

The man with the tape was about forty-four and he owned the shop and he was not on anything and he said so himself before anybody asked him.

**"I have been asked to have a room in here and I do not know who to ask."**

**"Then do not ask me about the room. Ask me about the office and I will tell you what the office can sit in, and the room is the other half of the answer and the office is not mine to lend."**

He had the sheet with him. She took it off him without asking and read it standing up, in about nine seconds, in a room with no heating in it yet, and then she read it out loud, which she had not planned to do and which is the whole of what happened on that Monday.

**"Eleven weeks. Renewable once, and the second renewal is not automatic. A person may bring a thing they were asked to do and did not understand what they were agreeing to."**

**"It does not cover how a technique is performed. It does not cover a person's own care. It does not cover two people arguing about money."**

**"The standing committee can take it off me at any time and nobody else can. And nobody may speak for me, including the committee, and that last line is not mine and I would not have written it and I cannot take it back."**

He listened to all of it with the tape still in his hand.

**"So can the office be in my shop."**

**"One line of that sheet can be in your shop. A person may bring a thing they were asked to do and did not understand agreeing to. That is a room, and it is a small room, and it is nine chairs and a person at a table, and that is the whole of what I am offering anybody."**

**"And if I want the other two."**

**"Then you are asking me for a room about technique, or a room about money, and there is nobody in this city who can give you either of those in the next eleven weeks, and the reason there is nobody is not a shortage of people. It is that the two of those are the two a person is least able to bring into a room of nine chairs on a Monday evening."**

---

He put the tape down on the trestle table, which is the thing a man with a tape does when he has stopped measuring something.

**"I will take no rent for the eleven weeks. I have told you that before you asked and I am telling you again so it is in a mouth and not only in my head."**

**"All right."**

**"And if it goes on past the eleven weeks we will have to have a conversation about money, and that conversation is the one line of your sheet you have just told me you do not do, and I would rather you knew that today than in a month."**

**"That is the third limit and you have said it out loud before I had to, and I am not going to thank you for it because you did not do it for me."**

He looked at her for a second longer than a man with a shop needs to.

**"No. I did it for the twelve weeks after this one."**

**"That is a worse reason and it is a real one, and I would rather have it than the other."**

---

The woman of about fifty-three had said nothing for about nine minutes and had not moved, and when the man with the tape asked her whether she was waiting for somebody she said that she was not, and when he asked her whether she had come to help she said that she was not, and the man believed both.

She asked one question and it was not about the room and it was not about the chairs.

**"Is there a printed paragraph that people can quote about any of this?"**

**"There is a paragraph printed on the back of a sheet of paper and about four hundred competent people in this city can each say it out loud."**

**"And can any of the four hundred write an objection to it anywhere."**

**"No."**

**"Then your nine chairs are a very small thing and you should know that on the first day rather than the fourth."**

The man with the tape looked at her and then at the woman of twenty-four and did not ask who she was, and the woman of twenty-four did not say, and that is how a name stays off a page for a day longer.

---

A woman of about thirty-eight who runs the flat machines in a laundry came in at about half past two with her hands wet and asked whether she was meant to bring something with her or come empty handed, and she had brought nothing, and she stood in the doorway for about nine seconds deciding whether that made her late.

**"You have not brought anything and that is the correct answer, and nobody is going to look at you and think you came wrong, because there is nobody here to do the looking."**

**"Then what do I do."**

**"You sit down and you say the thing you were asked to do and did not understand, and if it turns out you understood it perfectly then you have wasted about four minutes, and that is the whole risk."**

**"Is it written anywhere what it is?"**

**"It is on the door in my hand and it is four lines and none of them is a rule for the room. The rule is a chair and about nine minutes and somebody who writes down what you said."**

The man with the tape came back in with the tape and without the shop keys and said that the kettle in that shop front belonged to the building and could be unplugged, and that if the room was still there in eleven weeks the kettle could stay plugged in, and that if it was not then the kettle would go back on a shelf with about forty other things that had not been asked for.

---

He was at the bench until about two and at the counter until about six.

At about five a man of about thirty-one came in who wanted a length of chain and who said, before the chain, that half the street was talking about a room with chairs in it, and asked whether the address on the half ticket in the plastic sleeve under the wall list was the one with the chairs in it.

**"I wrote that address. I have not been in that shop front and nobody has told me what is going to be in it."**

**"They are saying it is for people who signed something and did not know what it was."**

**"Then somebody has told them more than they told me."**

He sold the chain. The man went out with it and did not say anything else and did not have to.

**What he wanted at about five on the Monday was to walk up that street and stand in the doorway and look at nine chairs, and he did not, and the not going was the decision of the day and it cost him the four days between that Monday and that Thursday, and on the Thursday a man at that counter asked him what the room was for and he said that he did not know, in his own shop, at his own counter, which he has not had to do in ten years.**

---

At about half past six she came into the four rooms off the service road and sat down and told him the thing in one sentence, because she had worked out on the walk over that she was not going to be able to do it in nine words and there was no point in pretending otherwise.

**"The office is in a shop front for eleven weeks and the room is one line of what I was given to hold, and I am not on anything else, and I did not ask for it and the committee decided it and told me afterwards."**

He looked at her and asked the question he had been carrying since about five.

**"Can I say what the room is. To people. At the counter."**

**"You can say the address, and you can say the four lines off that sheet, and if you say one word past the four lines you are describing my office."**

**"That is not the same as speaking for it."**

**"It is the same to the person hearing it. There is a line on that paper that says nobody may speak for me and it is not mine, and a man at a counter who describes what an office is gets treated by about nine people a week as the man who speaks for it, and after about a month somebody will come in and ask you whether a thing is covered, and you will have said one word too many on a Wednesday and there will be no version of you afterwards that they believe."**

He did not say the fifth line. He has wanted, for about two years and in about nine rooms, to be the person who is asked, and the room that was opened on that Monday was opened by somebody else and the door of it is not his, and he sat in a warm room at about seven and did not ask her to describe it to him and she noticed that he had not and neither of them said anything about it.

The woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was asked nothing by anybody and thanked by nobody and nobody has said sorry to her. Her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf, and it was not read on the Monday and the binder was not lifted off the shelf.

The man of about fifty-one was at the north wall with his tool bag on the floor and had not spoken for four hundred and ninety-five days, which is seventy weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors was in another city, and his hold stood at five hundred and twenty-two days, which is seventy-four weeks and four days, and nothing had been asked of him and nothing had been given to him.

The ask itself is five hundred and seventy-nine days, which is eighty-two weeks and five days, counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since. The newest line on the hardboard on two nails is five hundred and eighty-five days old, which is eighty-three weeks and four days, and it is the only line ever put on that board which has carried a zero and it carries none now. The post at the end of that corridor stands at four hundred and thirty-seven days, which is sixty-two weeks and three days. The nine copies of the front of that page are four hundred and fifty-five days into it, which is sixty-five weeks to the day, and the space between them and him is two hundred and sixty-nine days, which is thirty-eight weeks and three days, and it did not get smaller and it did not end.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
