# Chapter 527 — A Sheet Of Paper On A Door In A Hand Only One Person Can Read, And Four Chairs Put Back By A Stranger

**The Thursday after the argument was a bench, a counter, a closed shop and a room seen from the outside for the first time, and the shape of the day was a sheet of paper on a door that four streets had stopped for, and a woman with no standing on it putting four chairs back on a stack in about nine minutes while nobody thanked her.**

---

Eleven for the day, eleven in the date column, eleven through the door, the last of them at about six. The bench until two, the counter two until six, the four rooms off the service road from half past six. The rate is nine and ten, and has never been right. **The heating on that side of the city is on and dearer than in the winter, four names on the tenancy are between them carrying the increase, and a fortnight a cook spent out of her own wages is still unaccounted for and will be still unaccounted for in a month.** The rooms off the service road number eight hundred and ninety-nine days, one hundred and twenty-eight weeks and three days, and are four rooms and a back one and are warm.

The card in the rail by the counter is nine hundred and three days, one hundred and twenty-nine weeks to the day, four days beyond the room, in a rail that takes two.

---

The sheet went up on the door on the Tuesday morning and it is the only thing on that door.

> There is a sheet of paper on the door of a shop front with nine chairs in it and it went up about a fortnight ago.
> It says what the room is for and it says the three things the room does not do, and it does not say where a person takes it when the room is shut.

It is in her handwriting. Her handwriting is small and it leans about four degrees to the right and it is, to anybody who is not her, difficult.

**He offered to do it again in a hand half the size of his own, on the Thursday morning, and she said no in about four seconds, and he did not ask why, and the reason is printed here because he worked it out on the walk and did not say it to her.** A sheet on a door can be read aloud by anybody standing in front of it. A sheet in a hand that nine people out of ten cannot get through is a sheet that is read by the person whose hand it is, and that was the whole of what the paper was for, and a better hand would have been a worse thing and he knew it and offered it anyway, which is a thing he does.

The cost of the offer being refused and not repeated is that for about eleven weeks a person standing on that pavement could not find out what the room would not do without knocking, and about four people knocked and were told by a woman with a small leaning hand, and that is now how it works and will stay how it works.

* * *

At about half past four on the Thursday the room was full and about seven people were on the pavement, and one of the seven was the woman of about fifty-three.

Nobody asked her in. She had been on the pavement from about two, and she had been asked in by nobody, and there was no list, and she was not on one, and she had not been in a room like that since about twenty years ago and had not said so.

At about twenty past five the room emptied and she went in, and she put four chairs back on a stack in about nine minutes, and she did it the way a person does a thing they have watched done a great many times, and she did not say anything to anybody while she did it, and the woman of twenty-four stood in the doorway and watched her do it and did not help and did not stop her.

Then she locked nothing, because there is nothing to lock, and she went out onto the pavement and stood on it for about four minutes and then went to a stop and got a bus, and she had said all this in a room on the Monday and the woman of fifty-three has not been in that room since and has not asked to be and nobody has written to ask her.

Nobody in that shop front thanked her for the nine minutes. She is not thanked now either. That is not a matter of the room; the room did not have a rule about it and neither of the two women in it has ever mentioned it to the other.

---

He came to the shop front at about ten past seven in the evening on the way to a bus and stood on the pavement for about nine minutes, which is longer than he has ever stood outside anything.

The sheet was on the door and the door was not locked and inside there were four chairs on a stack and a trestle and a kettle and a woman of twenty-four writing something on a sheet about half the size of the door.

A man of about twenty-seven who had been one of the seven on the pavement that afternoon came along the road, stopped, looked at the sheet on the door for about nine seconds, and said to nobody in particular that he had been one of the seven and that he had not gone in on the Monday either, and that this was the second time in a fortnight he had not gone in somewhere and had told nobody.

The woman of fifty-three was at a stop about forty feet away with her handbag on her knee and she had a ticket in her hand and she was not going to be on the bus she had, and she said, to the man, that the sheet on that door is a thing that takes four years of somebody's handwriting to be useless to nine people out of ten, and that he was right, and that he should not let her persuade him out of going back tomorrow.

He did go back on the Tuesday. He did not go on the Friday. Nobody at that door ever knew which and nobody asked.

He did not go in himself. He has gone in once, on the Tuesday, having gone in twice on the first Friday, and he is not on anything, and he stood on a pavement and looked at four lines written by somebody else and thought about a woman with a column of readings on her arm and a man at a weighbridge and a man who got on a bus on Friday and a woman of about thirty-four who put her head down, and he worked out that he could name all of them and that he was the only person in this city who could, and that this was not a qualification.

**What he wanted, at about ten past seven on that pavement, was for one of the nine people in that room on the Monday to have been wrong out loud and to have been told so by somebody in the room, and there was one, and she put her head down, and nobody opened a space, and he is the person who would have opened it and he did not, and that is the second thing in four days he has wanted and not said and the first one he was in the room for.**

---

A woman of about twenty-five came to the counter at about ten past one on the Thursday and put a job ticket on it, face up, and it has an address on it in his handwriting and has had one since before this month.

**"I have been past it."**

**"Have you."**

**"I have been past it and I have not been in and I am telling you the same thing I told you the last time, which is that an address is no use."**

**"I know it is no use. I have not found the thing that is use and I have had about four days to look for it and I have not found it."**

**"Then it is no use."**

**"It is no use, and I have not got anything better, and I am not going to invent one at this counter, because the last time somebody handed a woman of about thirty-one a sentence that was not hers it became a thing she could be asked about for a year."**

**"You have never once said to me what you want that room to be."**

**"No."**

**"Then that is what it is. You have never once said what you want, in about four months, and I have come about four times, and I have not asked you for it once, because I worked out about a month ago that if I asked you for it you would give me something and then it would be a thing you had said."**

**"That is nearly right and I would not have got there."**

**"It is entirely right. I have got four years of a column on my arm and about nine people a year who tell me things, and nobody in this city has ever handed me one sentence that was not mine, and that is not modesty, it is the only reason any of them tell me anything at all."**

She put the ticket back in her pocket and said that she would keep it, and that she was not angry with him, and that she would be back when there was something on the ticket, and that she had been saying that to about four people for about two years and one of the four was herself.

He watched her go and did not write anything down and did not ask her name, and that is the third time he has not asked somebody a name in about four days and the second of the three has been the hardest.

---

The man of about fifty-one had been at the north wall for five hundred and five days, seventy-two weeks and one day, and had said nothing, and his bag was on the floor. The man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors is five hundred and thirty-two days into that, seventy-six weeks to the day, in a city that is not this one, and nothing has been asked of him and nothing given to him and he is not going to be here on the Friday.

The ask is five hundred and eighty-nine days, eighty-four weeks and one day, from the day it was asked. The newest of the nineteen lines is five hundred and ninety-five days, eighty-five weeks to the day, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and forty-seven days, sixty-three weeks and six days. The nine copied fronts are four hundred and sixty-five days along, which is sixty-six weeks and three days, and what is between them is two hundred and seventy-nine days, which is thirty-nine weeks and six days. Four chairs on a stack and nine minutes on a pavement moved none of it.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
