# Chapter 529 — Four Days Of Bus Travel Away, A Question Asked In A Doorway By A Stranger, And An Answer That Changed Nothing

**The Monday of the third week was a bench, a counter, a shutter at ten, and about two hours on a landing in another city where a woman doing a check at forty-one doors asked a man who has held them for a year and a half the one question nobody had ever put to him, and the answer was true, and it moved nothing, and neither of them said thank you.**

---

Eleven for the Monday, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last at about five forty-five. Bench in Lattice Ward until two, that counter from two until six, the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, unaltered, and communicated to nobody. **The heat in the flat over the service road is on and dearer, and four names on the lease are carrying the increase between them, and the fortnight a cook put out of her own money in the winter is still not repaid and is not going to be repaid in this month.** The rooms behind that road are nine hundred and three days old, one hundred and twenty-nine weeks to the day, four rooms and a back one, and they are warm.

The card in the rail by the counter is nine hundred and seven days old, one hundred and twenty-nine weeks and four days, four days more than the room, and the rail holds two.

---

The woman of about thirty-four had a form and a case and about nine hours of work in front of her and she had been on the doors of that building since about ten in the morning.

She checks what has to be checked on about nine hundred doors a year and the building she was on held forty-one of them, and the form wants a landlord's name and a landlord's reference, and she had got as far as the eleventh door of the forty-one with two names written in a column and neither of them answering, and then a man came up the stairs behind her and asked her whether she was the gas and she said she was, and he said the last time anybody had been on that landing asking about the doors it was a different woman and it had been about a year.

**"You are the landlord."**

**"I hold the doors."**

**"That is a yes to a question I did not ask. I asked whether you were the landlord."**

**"Then it is a yes that I have thought about for about a year and a half, and I would like to give you the whole of it instead of the short version, because the short version gets written down and the whole of it will not."**

**"I do not have a pen."**

**"I know. I am going to talk anyway."**

---

**"There are about nine hundred doors in four buildings in this city and I have held all of them since a spring neither of us is going to name. There is no stamp on anything. There is no reference. There is no date anywhere on any of it, not on mine and not on the paper, and I did not come to the doors and I have not asked to, and I have been in four of the four buildings twice and I have never once been in a flat."**

**"Then who gave them to you."**

**"I do not know. I have never been told and I have not asked, and I have thought about asking about once a week for about a year and a half and I have not done it, because a man who asks is a man who has admitted he does not know, and after that he is a different sort of man to the person he is asking."**

She wrote two names in her column and looked at the form for about four seconds.

**"Then I have to go back to the office with two names that do not work, and I will do that, and I have got forty-one doors."**

**"I know you have."**

**"Right."**

**"Can I ask you one thing that is not on the form."**

**"You can ask."**

**"Have you ever had it explained to you why it is like this. Not who did it. Why there is a man with about nine hundred doors and no stamp and nobody to write to."**

He was quiet for long enough that she put her case down on the step.

**"No. I have a theory and it is that nobody wanted to be the first one whose name was on it, and I have had that theory for about a year and a half, and I have never said it out loud to anybody, and you are a stranger and I am about to say it out loud to you, and if that is a bad thing to have done then it is done and it was going to be done by about four o'clock this afternoon whatever happened."**

**"It is not a bad thing."**

**"I know it is not. That is the part I cannot get round."**

---

**"Is there anybody I can write to."**

He looked at the wall opposite for about nine seconds, which is a long time on a landing.

**"There is a man in a firm in this city. I have his card in a flat file in a flat in a street where the bins go out on a Wednesday, and I have not used it in about four months, and I have not used it because the last time I stood outside that office the man behind the glass told me he could not say whether he was dealing with these buildings, and I believed him, and I did not go back."**

**"Have you been asked anything."**

**"No. Not by anybody, not in a year and a half, not once, and I have been in this building twice and I have been in the other three never."**

**"Then there is nothing on this form I can honestly fill in and I am going to say that out loud so that you know it is me and not the office, and then I am going to go and do the other thirty doors."**

**"That would be better."**

**"It is not better for me either. That is not a complaint, that is just the same fact twice."**

She did the other thirty doors. She wrote nothing about the man of about thirty-three in her column and she told her office that the landlord's details were unavailable on that building, which is true, and her office wrote a letter to a firm in that city, which will be opened by somebody, and that letter will not name him.

**Nobody thanked anybody. She did not thank him and he did not thank her, and the reason she did not is that nothing came of it, and the reason that is a fair reason and not an unkind one, and he worked that out at about four o'clock in the afternoon on a landing and did not mind it and has thought about the not minding more than he has thought about the question.**

---

He was at the bench until two and at that counter until about six, and a man of about twenty-four came in at about half past four who rents a room above a shop and had a receipt in his hand.

**"I put four weeks of rent in a tin at the top of the stairs in a place I have never seen, and I have a receipt, and it has got a name on it and no address and no date, and the name is not a person, it is a word."**

**"What word."**

**"I am not going to say it here. It is four letters and it is not his name."**

**"Then you have given four weeks to a word."**

**"I have. And the man in the shop knows that is what I did, and he is about sixty, and he did not stop me, and he told me afterwards to come back if I wanted to and I did not go back, and I have been not going back for about a month."**

**"Why are you telling me."**

**"Because you are the only one in this city who has asked me a question about it and you did it about four months ago and I have never told anybody and I told you, and you have not offered me anything, and I want that to stay true for about another month."**

**"It will. I have nowhere to send you and I am not going to write your name down, because a name in a drawer is a thing a person with four minutes can find."**

**"Everybody says that."**

**"Everybody says it because it is the only true thing there is to say to a man with a receipt, and I have no idea whether it is any use to you and I have not got a better sentence and I am not going to invent one at four thirty on a Monday."**

**The man of about twenty-four said thank you and left, and he was the only person who said thank you to anybody in this entry, and it was for nothing, and the receipt went back in his pocket, and nothing was written down and nothing was done.**

---

**He did not ask a woman of about thirty-five with a bench off a court to find out anything about a man of about thirty-three in another city, and she would have done it, and she has told him in the past that she will ask nobody a question they have not asked her to ask, and it would have taken her about a fortnight.** The cost is that for about a year the only place in this whole case where a man of about thirty-three and about nine hundred doors exists is a figure that Marek Senn carries in his own head and has never written down, and if he dies the figure dies, and the four buildings do not, and about nine hundred people go on living behind doors that nobody has ever asked them about.

He did not decide that for the sake of the man. He decided it because a woman with a pick and a flat file has spent about two years being a person who is asked, and that is her arrangement and not his to spend, and he wanted to use it.

---

The man of about fifty-one had been at the north wall for five hundred and nine days, seventy-two weeks and five days, and had not said anything, and his bag was on the floor. The man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and thirty-six days into a hold that nobody authorised, which is seventy-six weeks and four days, and on the Monday of this week a stranger asked him a question in a doorway and he answered it and nothing came of it and he was not thanked and he is not going to be told that it happened, because nobody in this city knows it happened.

The ask is five hundred and ninety-three days, eighty-four weeks and five days, from the day it was asked. The newest of the nineteen lines on two nails is five hundred and ninety-nine days, eighty-five weeks and four days, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and fifty-one days, sixty-four weeks and three days. The nine copied page fronts are four hundred and sixty-nine days into the run, which is sixty-seven weeks to the day, and the two hundred and eighty-three days between him and her are forty weeks and three days, and a man on a landing in another city knew none of them and would not have cared.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
