# Chapter 526 — The Thirty-Eighth Wednesday In A Room Above A Line, And One Line That Went In

**The Wednesday was a bench, a counter, a shutter at ten, and one hour in a second-floor room in another district where nine things were said out loud and one of them was said to a woman instead of to a room, and that is the first time in nineteen years it has happened, and it is not a rule.**

---

Eleven entered on the docket, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening. The bench until about two, the counter from two to six, the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, standing. **Heat in the tenement over that road is on and dearer, four names on the lease carry the increase, and a fortnight a cook covered herself is on nobody's account and is not going to be on anybody's account before the weather turns.** The room behind the service road is eight hundred and ninety-eight days old, one hundred and twenty-eight weeks and two days, and is warm, and is four rooms with one behind them.

The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and two days, one hundred and twenty-eight weeks and six days, four days more than the room, and the rail holds two. A book of four columns on a table in another district is a different object from that card and from that room, and this entry does not put them together.

---

About eleven people came up the stair. Six had sat in the same chair on the last Wednesday and five had not, and one of the five had not been in that room in nineteen years and had not come for a reason he gave.

Nine chairs went into a curve with the two ends turned in, and the tenth chair was hard against the wall with its back to the whole room. It did not move in the hour. Nobody looked at it more than once. Whoever moves it is not named here and is not going to be.

There was a table, an unlidded tin with no lock on it and never one, and the book face up inside her right hand's reach from about ten until about eleven.

Nine items, four minutes each, and eight of them went to the chairs the way eight of them had gone for nineteen years.

**The ninth was said across a table, to her, in about nine seconds, and it was the first time in nineteen years that anything said in that room had been said to her instead of to the room, and she wrote one line and the book went from fifty-eight lines to fifty-nine.**

She has said twice in nineteen years what that would sound like, and on both of those mornings the book also stayed shut, and nobody in that room has ever been told which mornings it will be in advance, and it is not written on the landing, and it is not in the four columns, and it is not a rule and it is not evidence of anything.

---

The man who said it was about twenty-nine and he worked on lifts, and he had come into this city four days earlier on a bus and had gone back to his bench in a town four days away on Friday morning, and he had been in this city for four days and had been in that room for one of them.

On the Monday he had been at the back of nine chairs in a shop front off a wide road, and he had left at about ten to four, which was the only time that afternoon that anybody left before the woman of fifty-three sat down, and he had stood on the pavement opposite with his hands in his pockets until the door opened at half past five.

**"You were in that room on Monday."**

**"I was."**

**"You left before the end."**

**"I did."**

**"Were you agreeing with her."**

**"No,"** he said.
**"I was not. I have never in my life agreed with a woman about a parlour, and I have been wrong about a parlour once and I know what it cost me."**

She put her pen down, which she does not do.

**"Then say the other thing."**

**"She is wrong about the paragraph. All of that about four hundred people and no place to put a complaint. I signed a form once in a drawer in a room in this city and nobody asked me and I have not told anybody, and the paragraph was not on that form. There was a box at the bottom of it. The box is where the paragraph should have been, and it was not on it, and she has spent an hour telling a room that the paragraph is the wall, and the wall is a box."**

**"That is not a thing said to a room."**

**"No."**

**"Then say it to me."**

He looked at the tin for about four seconds and then at her, and he was not a man who was good at this and everybody in the room could see that he was not.

**"I am the second column. Nobody asked me, and I came to say it to you and not to the room."**

That is about nine seconds. She wrote one line. The line has no name in it and no employer in it and no district in it, and the rest of the room did not hear what he said because the four minutes of the ninth item are the four minutes of the ninth item and the others were talking over it the way they always do.

---

The count was announced at the end and it is announced at a sitting and on no other day, and it came out at forty-three, of which thirty-eight correspond, and thirty-eight of those correspond to the thirty-eight Wednesdays this book holds and five of them predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody.

**No number went into the tin, and the tin stands at seventy-three, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it has not moved.** The book stands at fifty-nine lines, which is one more than it stood four weeks ago and which is the second time in nineteen years it has moved twice in a calendar year, and the tin is a number and the book is a number and the count is a number and none of the three turns into either of the others, and the difference between the first two is not a number and was not said out loud in that room on that Wednesday and is not said here.

Nobody asked her why it opened. Nobody asked her whether she was all right, and that has not been asked in that room in nineteen years.

**The man of about twenty-nine went down that stair and out into a street in another district and did not speak to anybody on it, and he got a bus on the Friday, and the form is still on nine tables in this city with the second column empty on eight of them, and the master copy is still in a locked drawer in a room off a service road, and the drawer has not been opened and nobody has been told that anybody has said anything, and he is not going to tell anybody, and that is the reason the room is still a room.**

---

He was at the bench until about two and at that counter until about six, and about four people came in between two and two and a half and about four of them were told no.

The first of those four was a man of about thirty-one who wanted to know whether the room off a wide road would take his mother's complaint about a heating contractor and who had heard the address from two people and had it written on the back of a receipt.

**"I have not been inside it."**

**"You wrote the address."**

**"I wrote the address. I have not been inside it and I have not been told what happens to anything that anybody carries in there, and I am not going to tell you that I know."**

**"Then how am I meant to know."**

**"You are not, and I have thought about that for about four days, and I have not found an answer, and I would rather tell you that than invent one, because you have got a receipt and if I invent one you will write it down."**

**"I already have written your name down."**

**"Then take it off the receipt, because I have not said anything and I do not want to be a name on a piece of paper about a thing I have not seen."**

He took the receipt and tore it in half and gave her the half with the name on it, and the drawer under the counter took both halves because a drawer under a counter takes whatever is put in it and has no index.

Nobody in that room knew what had been written in the line and nobody would have been able to find it, because the book of four columns has no index and no second copy and the copy that would answer a question about it is the one in her hand, and she has never let a person read a page of it, and nobody has ever asked her to, in nineteen years. **That is the whole of what opening a book does to a room and it is not a small thing.**

A man of about thirty-four who has been in that room nineteen times came down that stair on the Wednesday, at about ten past eleven, and went straight out into a street in another district and did not tell anybody about a line he had not read. **He has been asked twice in twenty years whether anything is ever written down in that room and he has said no both times and both times he was believed, and the second time he said it he knew that it had been true about an hour before he said it.**

**He did not go up the stair in Saltmarket and he did not tell anybody in this city that the man of about twenty-nine was going, and the cost is the fourth of a count of five afternoons he has not spent in a room where somebody might have asked him something, and he is keeping the count to himself and it is the only number about himself that he has been certain of in about two years.**

---

At about half past six there were four people in the warm room for the temperature and one of them was the man of about fifty-one, who had not spoken for five hundred and four days, seventy-two weeks to the day, and who had his bag on the floor. The woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was thanked by nobody and nobody has said sorry to her, and her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder and was not read on the Wednesday.

The man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors is five hundred and thirty-one days into it, seventy-five weeks and six days, in another city, with no stamp, no reference, no date, nobody's authority, and nothing asked of him.

The ask is five hundred and eighty-eight days, eighty-four weeks to the day, counted from the day it was asked. The newest of the nineteen lines on two nails is five hundred and ninety-four days, eighty-four weeks and six days, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and forty-six days, sixty-three weeks and five days. Nine copied page fronts are four hundred and sixty-four days into the run, which is sixty-six weeks and two days, and the two hundred and seventy-eight days between them are thirty-nine weeks and five days. He knew both figures to the day and was in another district and did not go.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
