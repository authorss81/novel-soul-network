# Chapter 528 — Twenty-Two An Hour, Offered In A Face By Somebody Who Meant It, And A Woman With Forty-Four Figures On Her Arm

**The Friday was a bench, a counter, a shutter, and a room over a bakery where a woman of about forty-one offered twenty-two pounds an hour to the one person in this city who everybody had already been bringing things to for nothing, and was refused in her own mouth, and the cost of the refusal fell on about eleven people a week who were not in the room.**

---

Eleven on the docket for the Friday, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last at about five and a half. Bench in Lattice Ward until about two, that counter from two to six, the four rooms off the service road from half past six. Nine and ten is the price, is wrong, and was said to nobody. **The heat in the flat over the service road is on, up, and is carried by four names on one lease, and a fortnight that a cook laid out of her own money in the winter has still not been repaid to her and is not going to be repaid by anybody in this city this month.** The room behind that road is nine hundred days old, which is one hundred and twenty-eight weeks and four days, and a round figure is printed as a round figure, and it is four rooms and a back one and it is warm.

The card in the rail on the counter front is nine hundred and four days, one hundred and twenty-nine weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

---

The room over the bakery is a volunteer advice line that runs on a Tuesday and a Friday evening from six until nine and is staffed out of a pot that three people put into and nobody counts, and the woman of about forty-one who runs it has run it for about six years and has never once put a wage on anything and has thought about it, and has written nothing down about thinking about it.

She had seen the woman with the column on her forearm twice in about four years. Once in a stairwell, over a disputed reading. Once at three in the morning at a door, over a box that had been fitted by a polite man with a drill.

**"I am going to say a number and I am going to say why, and then you are going to say no, and I am not going to argue with you, and I want that agreed before I start."**

**"Go on then."**

**"Twenty-two an hour. Three hours on the Tuesday, three on the Friday, setting up and taking down not counted, which I know is the part that makes it not twenty-two an hour, and I am telling you that on purpose so that you cannot tell me afterwards that I dressed it up."**

**"I have not said no yet."**

**"I know you have not. That is why I am doing the small print first."**

---

**"Here is the job. You stand at the door of the shop front off the wide road, on the Tuesday and the Friday, from two until five, and you take what people bring you, and you take it the way you have taken it for about four years, which is without a form, in your own hand if you write anything at all, and about nine people in a week come past that door on their own and do not need anybody to invite them."**

**"And if I take it, what am I."**

**"You are paid."**

**"That is not what I asked and you know it is not what I asked."**

**"No. So: you are a service. And I am telling you that now rather than in three months, because a service is a good thing and a good thing is not what is happening here, and the reason it is not what is happening here is that you are the person people already do this to, and money does not arrive at a door that people already use, money arrives at a desk, and a desk is a different thing and everybody knows it is a different thing the minute they walk up to it."**

**"Who else is it."**

**"There is nobody else and that is not a compliment, it is the reason I am asking. There is a woman of about twenty-two who has done it twice unpaid and has not been asked to do it again, and a man of about thirty-eight who does it on a Tuesday only and is not good at it, and a lad of about nineteen who stood outside that door on the Monday and then went home and told his mother there was a queue."**

---

**"No."**

**"Right."**

**"I have not finished."**

**"I am not stopping you. I said I would not argue."**

**"For about four years people have brought me a meter at two in the morning, because I am the one they have already told. Nothing is written down, nothing is on a form, and that is the only reason any of them have said it to me, because I am not anything. The day there is a wage on it I am a service, and the first person who does not want a service is a person who has been difficult, and I am the one who has to decide whether they are difficult or frightened, and I have never in my life had to make that decision about anybody."**

**"That is a real reason."**

**"It is one of two. The other one is that you are the first person in about four years who has offered me money for the thing I already do for nothing, and if I say no then in about two years there will be somebody else at that door, and she will be doing it for twenty-two an hour, and in about a year the person on that door will be the person who decides whether the person in the room is difficult, and she will not have four years of about nine people telling her that they trust her, she will have a wage and a list and a poster."**

**"And you would like to be asked again."**

**"In about two years. By somebody who does not have twenty-two an hour. I am not saying no to money, I am saying that the money is on the wrong side of the door, and I have thought about that sentence for about four days and I have not found a better one and I am not going to stand here and improve it in front of you."**

---

The woman of about forty-one wrote one line in a book, and the line has a date on it and no name on it, and the book is a book of things she has been asked to do and has done, and this is the first thing in it that is somebody turning an offer down.

**"Then I will shut the Friday,"** she said.
**"and I am telling you here, in front of him, that it will not be reopened, because you should not be left thinking that a refusal is free for anybody. About eleven people a week come past that door on a Friday and none of them pay anything and none of them know my name. They are not the ones your no is going to cost. They are the ones who are going to find a shut door in about a month, and you have not met one of them and neither have I, and I have had the number on a door for about six years and I have never once had to use it."**

**"I know."**

**"Do you."**

**"I know because I have been the one at two in the morning and I know exactly what it is to arrive at a door that is shut and go home."**

**"Then you know it is not a small thing, and I am not going to pretend it is, and I would still rather shut it than have it done for a wage by somebody who has not been here at two in the morning."**

**"Can I say the thing I actually want to say."**

**"You can say whatever you want. I have said mine and I have used up about a year of patience that I did not know I had."**

**"Then I want to say that I have watched you turn down three things in about four years and I have never once seen you give a reason, and I have assumed for about two years that you were proud, and I would like to stop assuming that."**

**"It is not pride."**

**"Then what is it."**

**"It is that a person who is paid to be told what a thing is stops being told what a thing is and starts being given a version of it, and at two in the morning the version is what I say, and a person at two in the morning is exactly the person who should be getting the other one."**

**"Say that to the next person who offers it."**

**"I will, and they will not offer it, and in about two years somebody will, and I will be older and I will be harder, and that is not a plan and it is what happens."**

---

The man of about twenty-two was in that room for about nine minutes and said nothing in it.

At about eight the woman of about forty-one turned to him, in a face, and asked him whether that had been a stupid refusal from a woman with a good reason, and he answered in one sentence and it was the sentence he answers with and she has heard him answer with it twice in about three years and did not hear it differently the third time.

**"I am not the person who may say whether that was a good decision and I would rather tell you that than give you a thing I have no standing to give."**

**"You are the man who wrote the address on the ticket in her pocket."**

**"I am. And I have never been inside that room, and I do not know what happens to anything anybody carries in there, and if I tell you that was a good refusal then in about a month you will do it again on the strength of a man at a counter and neither of you will have thought about it."**

**"She was right, though."**

**"She was right about the money being on the wrong side of the door. I know that because it is what I would have said. That is not the same as knowing whether shutting a Friday is a small thing, and I do not know that, and I have been in this building for nine minutes."**

**"That is the most useful thing anybody has said in here in about a year and I am going to write it down and I am not going to pay you for it."**

**"Do not write it down."**

**"Then I will not."**

She did not write it down.

---

**Two figures in the same row are each other's age and each other's day, and they are the seventeen and the ask.** The seventeenth line on the hardboard on two nails is six hundred and seventy-two days old, and six hundred and seventy-two is the day the ask was put. The ask is five hundred and ninety days, which is eighty-four weeks and two days, and five hundred and ninety is the day the seventeenth line was written.

Neither figure is a day the heating failed in this city, and no figure of days off for the heating exists anywhere in this case. A Friday advice line in a room over a bakery and a form on nine tables are not the heating, and a person standing at a door for four years without a wage is not the heating.

The woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door in the warm room and was not asked anything and was not thanked and nobody has said sorry to her, and her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder and was not read on the Friday. The man of about fifty-one had been at the north wall for five hundred and six days, seventy-two weeks and two days, with his bag on the floor at his feet.

The man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors is five hundred and thirty-three days into a hold with no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority, and is in another city, and was not asked anything this week and will not be.

The newest of the nineteen lines is five hundred and ninety-six days, eighty-five weeks and one day, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and forty-eight days, sixty-four weeks to the day. The nine hand copies of a page front are four hundred and sixty-six days, sixty-six weeks and four days, eight of nine of them unfinished and the first disagreement in the fourth line still not found. The hold is five hundred and thirty-three days, seventy-six weeks and one day, and the two hundred and eighty days between him and her are forty weeks to the day, and a shut Friday does not shorten a figure either.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
