# Chapter 530 — A Title Given In A Street To A Woman Who Did Not Ask For It, And A Bench, And Two Things Nobody Signed

**The Thursday of the third week closed a run of ten days with a woman of fifty-three on a pavement being given a name for something she did in a room, and a man of twenty-two at a bench who had been on her side of that room for eleven days and had not said so to one person, and nothing at all was written down by anybody.**

---

Eleven on the docket, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last at about six. The bench in Lattice Ward until about two, that counter from two to six, the four rooms off the service road from half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, fixed, and not said to anybody. **The heat in the tenement above the service road is on, dearer than in the winter, four names on the tenancy carry the increase, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own money is still owed to nobody and is not going to be paid this month.** The rooms off that road are nine hundred and six days, one hundred and twenty-nine weeks and three days, four rooms and a back one, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the counter front is nine hundred and ten days, which is one hundred and thirty weeks to the day, four days more than the room, and the rail holds two.

---

She did not go on the Friday. The bus was at four and she was not on it, and she has not been on a bus since, and she has been in this city for eight days longer than she said she would be, and nobody in this city has asked her why, and she has not said.

On the Thursday she was at the counter of a place that does not repair anything, at about four in the afternoon, drinking something she had not asked for, and a woman of about thirty-four came in who had been in nine chairs on the Monday and had put her head down in them, and who stood at the counter for about a minute before she said anything at all.

**"I have said a thing about you to two people and I have come to say it to your face so that you have it from a person."**

**"Say it."**

**"They have started calling you the one who was right. On my street it is the one who was right. I have said it twice this week and I have not corrected myself either time, and I have come to tell you that I did not correct myself, and to give you the chance to tell me it is wrong."**

**"It is not wrong."**

**"I know it is not wrong. That is the difficulty and I have had it for about four days and I have not slept properly on two of them."**

**"Then say the difficulty."**

**"The difficulty is that a person who is the one who was right cannot be in a room where somebody is wrong. And about nine people in that room on Monday had something to say to you and did not say it, and I was one of them, and I put my head down, and I have thought about why I put my head down every day since and it is not because you were too much. It is because I was afraid of being the only one who disagreed with the one who was right, and there is a person in this city who has been exactly that before and it cost her a career and a flat and a fortnight's notice, and I am not brave and I am not stupid."**

The woman of fifty-three looked at her for about four seconds.

**"Then that is the whole of what your title is and you did not need me to tell you."**

**"No. But I would have gone on saying it in a street."**

---

**"I want to say what it costs and then I am going to get a bus."**

**"Say it."**

**"It cost me eight days and a ticket I could not change and a week of a thing I cannot name, and it cost me a woman in a city she has not been invited to who has not written to me once, and I sat on a pavement for three hours last Tuesday with about seven people and nobody asked me in, and that was the correct arrangement and I have not resented it once and I noticed that I had not."**

**"You are not going to be able to fix any of that by telling a stranger on a pavement."**

**"No. I have found that the reliable way to be in rooms is to be the one who was right, and I did not set out to find it and I am not going to be able to go back to being a person who is in rooms to argue. There is a woman of about twenty-four in this city who can be in a room where somebody is wrong for eleven weeks and then not be, and I can be in a room for about a year if I am agreed with and not a day if I am not, and I do not know which of those two things I would have chosen and I am not going to find out on a bus."**

**"Then get the bus."**

**"I am getting it at six."**

Nobody in that queue said thank you. The woman of thirty-four stayed for about four minutes and then went, and she did not say the name again, and it went on being said in her street for about another month and then, like most street things, it stopped.

---

Nobody thanked anybody in the queue and nobody in it said the phrase a second time.

A man of about twenty-six asked her at about ten to six, at the edge of it, whether the woman in the street was still in this city, and Marek said that she was not on the pavement in front of that shop front and that he did not know where she was and that he was not going to find out, and the man said that he had heard she was in a market somewhere, and Marek said that if she is, that is not something either of them can check, and that the man of twenty-six said right, and went and caught a bus.

**He did not say that he had stood on that pavement for nine minutes on the Thursday of the week before with four chairs on a stack behind the glass, and he did not say that he could have told the man of twenty-six where a woman of fifty-three was in about nine seconds on a Monday, and that on the Monday he had not asked and on the Thursday he would not have told.**

The woman of twenty-four read nothing out at a sitting in that week, and at the Friday of that week she sat at a table in a second-floor room in another district with her hands flat either side of a sheet and did not turn it over, and about four people knew that there was a sheet and none of them asked about it, and that is what nineteen years of a room above a line looks like when nothing has been said to the woman holding it.

---

He was at the bench in Lattice Ward until about two and then at the counter, and at about half past six he went down to the four rooms off the service road with about nine words arranged in a way that he had been carrying since the Monday of the week before.

She was at the table with the sheet in front of her, and there were four other people in for the temperature, and one of them was the man of about fifty-one, who had been at the north wall for five hundred and twelve days, which is seventy-three weeks and one day, and whose bag was on the floor at his feet.

**"Are you going to ask me."**

**"I am not."**

**"That was quick."**

**"It has been about eleven days."**

**"I want to say why, and then I am going to work, and then we are not going to do this again."**

**"All right."**

**"I have read out, at a sitting, in a room in another district, a thing that somebody else wrote. I have done that three times in about four months. It is the only piece of work this office has. And I did not agree with two of the three and I signed nothing and my name is not on them and that is the arrangement, and it works, and it works because of the sheet and because eleven weeks is a short time."**

**"And if I tell you what I thought of her on Monday it is a fourth thing you have to carry to a room and decide about on your own, and I am not going to be the reason that happens."**

**"That is a very good reason and it is about half of mine."**

**"What is the other half."**

**"That in about a fortnight a woman of about thirty-four is going to read out that somebody has been calling a stranger the one who was right on a street in this city, and she will not know that you were in this building about ten minutes ago, and she will say it as an example of something, and it will be about nine seconds, and there is nothing anybody can do about it."**

**"No."**

**"And you are the only person in this city who would have been allowed to tell me what you thought of her on the Monday, and you are the one person who could not, and I have worked that out over about four days and I am not going to do anything about it and I am telling you that I have worked it out."**

**"Thank you for working it out."**

**"Do not do that. I have not done anything for you. I have told you that a thing I could have done, I did not do, and that I have understood why I did not do it, and that is a different thing and you know it is a different thing, and I would rather you did not thank me for it."**

**"Then I am not going to."**

**"Good."**

He did not say it. He has wanted for about two years, in about nine rooms, to be the person who is asked, and in eleven days he has been the second person in this city who could have agreed out loud with a woman about a room and has not been it, and the reason is the reason on the sheet, and it is in her mouth and not his, and it is not going to soften.

---

Nothing was signed at the end of that week. Nothing was adopted, no list was created, no sheet went to a firm, no minute was kept, and the chair of the committee read out nothing at a sitting, and the fourth line of the front of that page is still not found.

The man of about thirty-three in another city is five hundred and thirty-nine days into a hold of four buildings and about nine hundred doors, seventy-seven weeks to the day, with no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority, and he was asked something in a doorway four days ago by a stranger and answered it and it changed nothing and he was not thanked and nobody in this city knows it happened.

The ask is five hundred and ninety-six days, eighty-five weeks and one day, from the day it was asked. The newest of the nineteen lines on two nails is six hundred and two days, eighty-six weeks to the day, and it is the only one of the nineteen that has ever carried a zero. The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and fifty-four days, sixty-four weeks and six days. The nine hand copies of a page front are four hundred and seventy-two days, sixty-seven weeks and three days, eight of nine unfinished. Nothing was shortened by the week in which a woman of fifty-three was given a name for what she had said, and nothing was ended by a man of twenty-two not saying nine words in a room.

The woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and nobody has said sorry to her, and her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder and was not read on the Thursday. The separation is two hundred and eighty-six days, forty weeks and six days, not shorter and not ended, and nothing that has happened in these eighteen days has been counted into it, and nothing in it has been counted from anything but the day it began.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
