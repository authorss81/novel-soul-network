# Chapter 524 — Fourteen People Through A Door That Holds Nine, And A Man In A Cellar Who Is Not Coming

**The Friday was the day the room was used rather than prepared, and its shape was fourteen people going through a door that takes nine at a time, a weighbridge man who had not gone home, a plumber four days of bus travel away saying no in a cellar for a reason that is not fear, and a number on a board that is also somebody's birthday.**

---

Eleven on the day's sheet and eleven against the date, and eleven attended, the last at about five and a half. The bench in Lattice Ward until two, the counter on to six, the four rooms behind the service road from about half past six. The rate is nine and ten, has been wrong for years, and went nowhere. **In the flat over the service road the heat is on, dearer, shared by four names on one lease, and there is a fortnight of a cook's own money that has not been repaid to her and will not be repaid this month.** The room behind that road is eight hundred and ninety-three days, which is one hundred and twenty-seven weeks and four days, and it is warm, and it is four rooms and one more at the back.

In the rail along the counter front a card is eight hundred and ninety-seven days old, one hundred and twenty-eight weeks and one day, four days older than the room, and the rail holds two. A shop front with nine chairs in it and fourteen people through the door of it is not that room and is not in that rail, and no sentence here joins them.

---

Fourteen came between about two and about five, and the room takes nine, and the other five waited on the pavement, and the door was not open earlier than two, and nobody had told any of the fourteen that two was when it opened, because nobody knew.

That is the first thing the room did and it is a small thing and it will not appear anywhere. A man of about twenty-seven who came at ten past two and found five people already sitting said to the woman of twenty-four that he had stood on that pavement for about twenty minutes not knowing whether to go home, and she said that she was sorry and meant it, and he said that he was not complaining and that a pavement is at least outside.

A woman of about forty-four came at about half past two with a folded sheet of her own and put it on the trestle and did not open it, and said that she had brought it in case anybody wanted to look at it, and that if nobody did she would take it away again, and about nine people in the room said that they would like to look at it and nobody did, and she put it back in her bag and said that this was what she had expected and that she had come two districts to find out.

The woman of fifty-three read the four lines on that door at about ten past three and stood there for the length of it, and then she said one thing to nobody, out loud, in a voice that carried about four feet.

**"None of the three things it does not do is the thing that is going to come through that door."**

Nobody took her up on it and nobody asked which of the three she meant and she did not say, and the room carried on, and about four people heard her and about nine did not.

---

**The man of about thirty-three came on foot from a hotel bed four streets away, and he had been in this city since the Monday before last, and he was not going back until the Sunday, and every hour of that was coming out of a weighbridge in the third of the four towns that he had asked for and could not get for.**

He had come on the Friday of the week before with a folder lying face up on a table beside him that nobody turned over, and he had said three things in a canteen off a market and got one word out of a woman of about thirty-five, and he had gone back into the shop front on the Thursday of that week and had not said a word in it, and on this Friday he came in and read the four lines off the door before he came through it, which four of the other fourteen did not do.

**"That is the only door in this city that has the nots on the outside of it."**

**"Do you want to sit down."**

**"I want to say the thing I have been saying for eleven years and I have never said it in a room before."**

**"Then say it and I will not stop you, and there are five people outside on a pavement and I would rather you were quick."**

**"A form says yes. It has said yes every time for eleven years. I have never once gone and taken myself off it, and I have never once been asked whether I wanted to, and every one of those two facts is somebody's decision and not mine."**

**"That is true."**

**"And I have been calling that a gap and I would like somebody in a room to have heard me call it a gap, because I have been calling it a gap to about nine people a year in a hut and it does not add up to anything in a hut."**

She did not write it down. He noticed that she did not write it down and he did not say anything about that, and afterwards he told a man of about twenty-one on the pavement that he had said a true thing in a room and that it had not been written down, and the man of about twenty-one said that this city runs on that, and both of them left and the door shut behind them.

---

At about ten past four on that Friday, four days of bus travel from here and about as far from this counter as a person can be in this case, a woman of about twenty-nine who sells seeds in a market went down eleven steps into a cellar behind a shop and asked a man of about fifty-three who fits plumbing whether he had heard about the room.

He had the plate out of its sleeve and laid on the bench in front of him and had not lifted it, and it is a plate of iron with four slots in it, all the same width, and it is wedged and not bolted, and it was under a floor in a building on a road that goes nowhere anybody goes on purpose.

**"I have heard."**

**"Are you coming then."**

**"No."**

**"You have come four days to ask me and you are not coming."**

**"I have come four days to sell seeds and I am asking you for nothing and I will be gone in the morning."**

He put his hand flat on the plate and left it there.

**"I am not coming because the only thing I have that anybody in your city would want is under this floor, and I have told one person in one cellar where it is, and that was nine words about two winters ago and I have not given the address to the person I told, and if I get on a bus for four days to stand in a room in a city and say a sentence out loud, then in about a year there will be two of us who know where it is, and in about two years there will be a room somewhere that asks both of us, and I will have made that room. That is the whole of my reason and it is not fear."**

**"I do not think anybody there is going to ask you for it."**

**"I know. That is why it would work."**

**"Then say no and go home."**

**"No, I will say the rest of it, because you have come four days and you are owed the whole of it."**

**"The whole of it is that I will not come, and that I have thought about it for about two days, and that the reason I have thought about it for two days is that if I do not come then in about a year this is a thing I heard about from a stranger in a cellar, and I will have been the kind of man who heard about a room from a stranger in a cellar, and I have spent about two winters deciding that I am not that man, and it turns out that the way to be that man is to stay where you are and be certain about it, and I am not certain."**

**"Say that last part again and say it more slowly."**

**"The way to be a man who heard about a room from a stranger is to stay exactly where you are and be completely certain about staying, and I am not certain, and so I am going to have to decide something, and I have decided it, and I have decided it is no, and you can tell them that in a market and they will believe you because you have come four days and you are not selling anything."**

She went up the eleven steps and did not write any of it down either, and on the bus she said the sentence about the plate to about four people who had not asked, and one of them was a man of about thirty-three who had a folder face up on his knees.

---

He had the address in his hand and a piece of paper and he did not give it to her and did not give it to the man of about fifty-three, and he did not ask anybody to carry it four days in a pocket, and that was the Friday's decision.** The cost is a man of fifty-three in a cellar who was asked by a stranger instead of by the one person in this city who would have been allowed to ask him, and who therefore got a stranger's version of the room and a stranger's account of the cost, and the man has never been told any of that and never will be.**

**Two figures on that board are each other's age and each other's day.** The fourteenth line on the hardboard on two nails is seven hundred and twenty-nine days old, and seven hundred and twenty-nine is the day a man of about thirty-three, in another city, took on four buildings and about nine hundred doors that nobody had referred, stamped or dated. That hold is five hundred and twenty-six days old, which is seventy-five weeks and one day, and five hundred and twenty-six is the day the fourteenth line was written.

Neither of those is a day anything happened to the heating in this city. The heating in this case is a closed thing with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it, and a man in a cellar and a man in another city are not the heating.

The woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door in the warm room that evening and was not asked anything and was thanked by nobody, and her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder and was not read on the Friday. The man of about fifty-one had been at the north wall for four hundred and ninety-nine days, seventy-one weeks and two days, with his bag on the floor.

The ask is five hundred and eighty-three days, eighty-three weeks and two days, from the day it was asked. The newest of the nineteen lines is five hundred and eighty-nine days, eighty-four weeks and one day, and carries no zero. The post at the end of the corridor is four hundred and forty-one days, sixty-three weeks to the day. The nine copied fronts stand at four hundred and fifty-nine days, which is sixty-five weeks and four days, and the two hundred and seventy-three days between them are thirty-nine weeks to the day. A man in a cellar four days of bus travel away did not move any of them and was not told that he could have.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
