# Chapter 508 — A Notice With A Box On It, And The Word That Is In Nobody's Mouth

**The Wednesday was a bench, a counter, and a warm room that people came into for the temperature, and the shape of the day was a man with a printed notice standing up out of a chair, and a woman of twenty-four reading her own limits out loud to him, and a man of twenty-two being asked to settle it and not settling it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and the counter until about six and the room from about half past six, and the appointment in it is eleven weeks long and this was the fourth day of it. The rate was at nine and ten, and the sheet it is on is the one that lives on the bench, and the figure on it is the figure that is wrong. **The heat in that building is on and has gone up twice since the autumn, four names on one lease are carrying it, and a fortnight of one cook's own spending has been owed to nobody for longer than the heat has been on.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and fifty-six days old and is warm, and on the Wednesday evening five people came in for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in that rail is eight hundred and sixty days old, four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two. A printed notice with a box at the foot of it and nine lines of print above the box is a fourth object in this entry, and the nine lines he wrote on the back of a job ticket and did not use are a fifth, and neither of them is joined to any other.

---

He was about thirty-eight and he kept the rota for a building of about nine flats, and he came at about half past six on the Wednesday because the room is warm and the man in it is the one people go to.

He had the notice in his hand. It was a printed sheet with a company name at the top and nine lines of print in the middle and a box at the foot with a printed heading over it, and the heading was the same heading that is on the box at the foot of a form in a locked drawer in the room off the service road, and Marek saw it and did not say anything about it.

**"I want you to tell her it's fine."**

**"I can't tell her anything. I've never met her."**

**"You've been in the room."**

**"I've been in a room off a service road for two years,"** Marek said.

**"She was in this room on the Thursday before last and she has a place with a green stair and a Friday slot and I have a half ticket with the address on it in my own handwriting. That's the entire extent of what I know and I've been waiting for somebody to need more than that so I'd be embarrassed."**

**"Well, she might be here."**

**"She might."**

**"Then I'll ask her."**

**"Ask her."**

The man did not sit down for about eleven minutes. Marek watched him hold the notice the way a person holds a thing they have already decided to bring and have not yet decided to use, and at about seven the woman of twenty-four came in with a bag and sat down at the far end and did not look at either of them, and the man got up out of the chair and put the notice on the table in front of her, face down, and then turned it back over.

**"This is for the board in the entrance. It says what the relay is and what the setting is and it says it applies from Monday, and it needs somebody to sign the bottom, and I've signed the top."**

**"You've signed the top."**

**"I've signed the top as the person who put it up. The bottom wants somebody who understands it."**

She read it. She read all nine lines and then she read the box at the foot and then she read the printed heading over the box twice, which was longer than the notice had taken him to sign the top of.

**"This is a decision about how a technique is performed in your building,"** she said.

**"It is not a decision about anybody's care, and it is not two people arguing about money, and it is not a person being asked to do something and not understanding what they were agreeing to, and so it is not in the eleven weeks I have been given."**

**"Right."**

**"I am going to say the rest of it anyway, because you have stood up out of a chair to ask me and I would rather give you the whole answer than the fast one."** She put two fingers on the notice.

**"I cannot tell you it is fine. I have not read a relay schedule, I do not keep a register of buildings, and the person in this city who would be able to tell you whether that setting is safe is not me and does not work in this room. If I said it was fine, then the next time somebody comes to a room off a service road and asks the same question, they will be told that this room says yes to things, and that will be true, and it will be a lie told in advance."**

**"So what do I do."**

**"You take it to whoever set the relay, and you ask them to write down what the setting is for and what it changes, and if they will not do that, you do not put it up."**

The man picked the notice up. He did not fold it.

**"There's an inspection at the end of the month,"** he said,

**"and if that notice is not on the board with a signature under it, it is my name that goes on the report and not hers and not yours, and I have a flat on the fourth floor that I found last year and I would like to keep."**

**"I know,"** she said,

**"and I am not going to soften that either."**

Then he turned round, in the room, with the notice in his hand, and looked at the man at the bench, and said the thing that the next four months of this story are made out of.

**"You'll tell her it's all right."**

Marek put the tool down.

**"No."**

**"It is a relay. It's a fire panel setting. It is nine lines."**

**"It is a decision about how a technique is performed in a building with about nine flats in it,"** Marek said,

**"and she is the only person in this room who has been given the job of saying what happens when a person is asked to do that, and I am not the person who speaks for her, and you are not going to be able to use me to get round the fact that she has just told you no in a room with five other people in it."**

**"That's a great answer."**

**"It is not mine. It is hers. I am only carrying it from one end of a room to the other, and I would like it noted that I am doing that badly."**

Nobody said anything for about four seconds. The parts cage man had his back to the room and had had it since a quarter past seven. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in the room the whole time and was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and was not read on the Wednesday.

**What Marek did not say, in the four seconds after he had said no, was that he could have written the notice out in nine lines in his own words in about six minutes, and that the nine lines would have been better than the nine printed ones, and that a notice in his handwriting with a rota man's name under it would have gone up on Monday and the man's inspection would have been fine and the flat on the fourth floor would have been kept. He did not say it because a notice in his words in that building is him deciding what happens in a building, and once there is one of those the office has a second mouth, and the second mouth would be his, and it would be his by accident and inside a week.**

He has never worked out whether that was restraint or pride, and he has had a long time to work on it, and he has not got there.

---

He walked the man with the notice to the door, which took eleven seconds, and the man stopped in the doorway and asked the second question, which was the one he should have asked in the first place and had not.

**"Is there anybody else?"**

**"There's nobody else who can tell you whether a relay setting is safe,"** Marek said.

**"There's about four hundred people in this city who could each decide to look at it and tell you, and none of them is doing that today, and one of them could not tell you the difference between a person being asked and a person being enrolled, and she has been in a room for four days and has a sheet of paper with the limits written on it, and the limits are the honest part."**

**"So the answer is no."**

**"The answer is that you have to ask whoever set it, in writing, and if they won't, then you don't put it up, and that is not a punishment, that is the only thing anybody has got."**

The man put the notice inside his coat rather than back in his bag, which is not the same as agreeing to anything and is not the same as not agreeing to anything, and Marek watched him go and did not follow and did not offer to write the nine lines and did not want to.

**The man with the notice was gone by about half past seven and the room went back to being a room about four minutes after that, and Marek stood at the bench and did the thing he always does after a thing has gone wrong in a room, which is to go and find the part of it he can do.**Tonight that was a kettle, and it took eleven minutes, and it needed a washer he did not have and one that he did, and he put the wrong one in first on purpose because his hands wanted to be busy for a quarter of an hour, and he was right to.

**What he wanted at about seven, and could not have said out loud to anybody in the room, was to be asked to do it. Not to fix the relay. To be asked. He has been the person in this room for two years and there is not one thing he is asked to do that a person could not ask a competent adult to do instead, and the appointment has made that visible for the first time, and the visibility is the cost, and he is going to pay it on a Friday in about two weeks by standing in a warm room saying nothing, and he already knows that he will find it unbearable, and he has already agreed to it.**

The woman of twenty-four stayed until about eight and did not discuss it. There are four people in this city who now know the terms of that office in their own words and three of them are in this building, and the fourth is a woman of about thirty-eight driving a van with a sheet of paper in a drawer that nobody in that town has been told about.

**Nobody in that room knew that a man of fifty-three in a town had told a woman of thirty-five six days ago that he had not moved a plate of iron and was not going to, and nobody in either of those places knew that a rota man had just been told no in a warm room, and the four of these people have never been in one building and no arrangement of them exists.**

The man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall on the Wednesday and had said nothing for four hundred and sixty-two days.

A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date was not in this city and his hold ran on for sixty-nine weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*Two refusals, as a record of a woman saying what her office does not cover and a man refusing to be her second mouth.* **Where: the warm room off the service road, on the Wednesday evening, from about half past six, with five people in it and one of them there for the temperature. Who asked: a man of about thirty-eight who keeps the rota for a building of about nine flats, holding a printed notice with a company name at the top, nine lines in the middle, and a box with a printed heading at the foot, having signed the top of it himself and being asked for a signature at the bottom from somebody who understands it. What he called himself, afterwards, in his own head: a man carrying a sentence from one end of a room to the other and doing it badly. What he could have done and did not: rewritten the nine lines in his own words in about six minutes, which would have gone up on Monday. Owner: a notice, and a second mouth that does not exist.**

*The nine lines, as a record of what could have been written in six minutes and was not.* **Where: the warm room off a service road, on a Wednesday evening, in the four seconds after a man with a notice had been refused twice by two people. What the notice was: a printed sheet with a company name at the top, nine lines in the middle, and a box with a printed heading at the foot, the top of it signed by the man who put it up and the foot of it wanting somebody who understands.
