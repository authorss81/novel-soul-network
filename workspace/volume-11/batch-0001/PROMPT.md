## 2. The form, carried from Volume 10's close and not re-derived

**The house form is `workspace/volume-10/batch-0005/` as it actually ran and not as it described itself.** A chapter opens on a declarative paragraph stating the day, the thing that happens and the thing that is true of it, set entirely in bold from the first word to the last. Then a docket paragraph carrying the day's work, the rate, the heating, the room's age and the card's age. Then the scene, broken with `---`, in about two to six sentences a paragraph. Then the shutter line. Then the load book: one italic entry line, then a conditions paragraph, then between two and four records, each one an italic label and one unbroken bold span, then the standing record of what the day did not touch under its standard heading. **Every speech in this manuscript is in a double-asterisk span. No single-asterisk span is nested inside a double-asterisk span anywhere, no line carries more than one bold span, and no span crosses a paragraph break.** The shutter line and the sign-off sentence are the motif and stay.

**AND THE FIVE THINGS `logs/batch-0005.review.log` FOUND WRONG WITH THE FORM, WHICH ARE THE FIVE THINGS THIS MOVEMENT MUST NOT REPEAT. THE REVIEWER IS RIGHT ON ALL FIVE AND THE ARITHMETIC UNDERNEATH THEM IS EXCELLENT AND IS NOT THE PROBLEM.**

1. **The opening bold paragraph is not a summary of the chapter.** Volume 10's ten ran one hundred and forty-eight to two hundred and twenty-seven words, mean about one hundred and eighty-eight, and each one gave away every figure, every cost and every consequence before a scene had started. **Write forty to seventy-five words. State the day and the shape of the day. Do not print a figure in it, do not print the outcome, and do not name what a person in another building said.**
2. **The lead has an inner life or the file is not a chapter.** Across Volume 10's last ten chapters there were two instances of a thought verb in thirty-seven thousand words, and he was a schedule and not a person. He is twenty-two. He is a third-year student who needs a seal and a scholarship, a night-shift repairer, a man who splits a thumbnail and counts exits before he enters a room and wears his work gloves under his jacket. **He wants a small respectable life badly enough that he has made himself useful to the point of not being asked. Give him, in every chapter, one thing he notices that is not on any form, one thing he wants that he does not say, and one decision he makes that costs him something small. 3. **Nobody important in this manuscript is reported instead of heard.** Four of Volume 10's last ten chapters carried no dialogue at all, and both halves of its climax were entirely reported. **Every refusal, every offer, every cost named out loud, and every milestone in this movement is said in a double-asterisk span, in a face.** A milestone is still not a scene and a kiss is still not a scene, and a nine-word operational arrangement is still nine words; what is forbidden is *summarising a person's only line instead of giving it to the reader in their mouth*.
4. **No sentence of twelve words or more may appear in two of the ten files.** Volume 10's last ten files shared fifty-three such sentences, at least eight of them in all ten, and the overlap benchmark was rewarding the copying. **The conditions row and the standing record are the two places this happens and both are repairable without cutting a single mandated row: carry every mandated item, and write each one in that file's own words.** Measure it before you stop.
5. **The hedge is not the prose.** `about four people have said since that X and about nine have said nothing` is a sentence generator and it is self-defeating, because the hedge exists so that the sentence never has to say what happened. **Keep every hedged count that is a published figure.
