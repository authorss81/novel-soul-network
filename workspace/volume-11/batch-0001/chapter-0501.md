# Chapter 501 — A Room With Chairs Pushed Back, And The Word Nobody

**The Thursday was a bench, then a counter, and then a warm room with the heating already on, and the shape of the day was a morning in a room with chairs pushed back from a long table, an afternoon at a counter that never closes for long, and a conversation at the end of it that he had not seen coming and was not ready for.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter in the same district until about six, and a room off a service road from about half past six, and he has never been in the room the appointment was made in and was not in it on the Thursday either. The rate was at nine and ten and it was the last thing he read on the list. **The heating was on and had gone up again, and so had four names on one lease, and the fortnight a cook laid out was still on nobody's account and still was not going to be.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and forty-three days old and is warm, and six people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is eight hundred and forty-seven days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two and has held two for eight years. A folder on a table on a second floor in another district is a third object and a paper with a name at the top of it is a fourth, and nothing in this entry joins any two of them.

---

The room was on a second floor above a street of small offices in Crown Terrace and the chairs had been pushed back from the table by whoever set it up and not pushed in again by whoever came in. There were nine of the chairs and eight of the people, and the ninth was the chair that had a stack of paper on it and no owner, and nobody moved it in either direction all morning.

A woman of about fifty-two who chairs the standing committee put a single sheet on the table in front of the woman in the second chair from the end. She had a clinic bag beside her knee and she had not opened it, and she came in eleven minutes late and said so before she sat down, and nobody said that it did not matter.

**"We have four minutes and then two people have to go to a bus, so I am going to read the whole thing including the parts that make it smaller."**

Nobody asked to see it before she read it, and nobody asked for a copy, and the man at the end nearest the door wrote one thing on the back of his hand and did not read his own handwriting afterwards.

**"Eleven weeks. Renewable once, and the second one is not automatic. A person may bring to this office a thing they were asked to do and did not understand what they were agreeing to. It does not cover a decision about how a technique is performed. It does not cover a person's own care. It does not cover a quarrel about money between two people, and I am telling you that last one because two people will bring it to this door inside a fortnight. The committee may remove her at any time. Nobody but the committee may remove her. And nobody may speak for her, including the committee."**

The woman of about fifty-two stopped there and put her finger on the line and left it there, and the man at the end nearest the door looked up for the first time in eleven minutes.

**"You wrote that last line,"** the nurse said.

**"I did."**

**"It is not in the form."**

**"It is not in the form and it is on the paper in front of you, and if you take the paper away with you then it is on the paper, and if you do not then it is on the paper in this room and I will read it out again at the next sitting whether or not anybody asks me to."**

The nurse read the sheet from the top. She read it twice and the second time she read it aloud to the two people who had a bus, and one of them wrote a number on a card and the other one wrote nothing, and neither of them asked her whether she had read the number.

**"Who hears it,"** she said.

**"If the thing I am told about is a decision somebody in this room made?"**

The woman of about fifty-two did not answer straight away, and the pause was the most useful thing that happened in the room all morning.

**"You write it down and you hand it to me and I read it out at the next sitting. That is the whole of the answer and I want to be honest with you about it, which is that it is not a good answer and I have not found a better one and there is a stack of paper on that chair that nobody has read since spring."**

**"Then it is the answer."**

**"I will take it as written and I will not soften it for anybody, including the people who are fond of me, and there are three of those in this room and one of them is you."**

The woman of about fifty-two laughed once, which nobody in that room had heard her do before, and said that the room with a door that shuts was on the second floor of a building with a green stair and that there was a second address for written things and a slot on a Friday afternoon for anybody who could not come in person.

Nobody signed anything. Nobody photographed the sheet. When the two people left for a bus the nurse stayed in her chair with the paper in front of her for another four minutes and did not say anything to anybody, and then she folded it once, and put it in the side pocket of the clinic bag, and stood up.

---

At that counter in Lattice Ward the machine was running and the parts cage man had his back to the room and had had it there since a quarter past seven, and a man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall waiting for a part that was not finished.

She came in at about six and put a bracket on the counter and did not take the bag off, and he found the bracket in about four minutes the way he always found it, and when he put it down she said the thing.

**"I have a job. Eleven weeks. And you do not have anything to do with it, and that is written down, and I want you to hear the written-down part before you decide how you feel about the rest."**

**"What do you need?"** he said, and heard it leave him, and knew about four seconds later that it was the wrong sentence, and it was too late to be a better one.

**"Nothing you can carry."**

**"That is not an answer."**

**"It is the whole of one. Eleven weeks, renewable once, and nine people can end it in an afternoon and one person who is not on the committee cannot end it at all, and you are the one person in this city I have told, and you asked me what you could hold instead of what happened."**

He put the bracket in a paper bag. The parts cage man turned a radio down to a murmur and did not turn round.

**"What does it not cover?"** he said.

**"Decisions about technique, and anybody's own care, and two people arguing about money."**

**"And the last line."**

**"Nobody may speak for me. Including the committee, and including the man who fixed the wall list in this building and did it for nothing."**

He wanted to say that he would not do that, and he did not say it, and the not-saying took about four seconds and was the first time in a year and a half that he had let something go unsaid on purpose. What he wanted to say instead, and did not, was that he would like there to be nothing in it that needed him at all, and he had a whole evening in which to say it and did not, and that was the thing he was going to have to look at later.

**"There is a slot on a Friday afternoon for anybody who cannot come in person."**

**"That is in the letter and it is the only part of the letter I am going to read twice tonight."**

**"Write it on the back of the ticket,"** he said.

**"In case you lose the paper."**

She wrote it on the back of his own job ticket, which is not a form and is not on anything, and he kept the ticket, and it is on a hook under the wall list in a plastic sleeve, and it is not a record of anything and it is the only copy.

Then a delivery note came in on the bench with a relay unit on it, and he turned it over because the second column had a line ruled in it in pencil and the first column was printed, and somebody had ruled the second one by hand at some point and had not printed a single word above it, and there was nothing in it.

Nobody had ever written in it. The sheet had been on that bench going on five weeks, and the second column had been empty every day including this one, and he understood while he was looking at it that a person had ruled it expecting to be able to write in it later, and that the expecting was the only thing in the document.

He picked up his pen, then put it down, then picked it up again, and put it in his pocket instead, and that was the whole of the decision he made on the Thursday about anything at all.

---

He was in the room from about half past six and the radio went on at seven and the temperature settled and four people came in and none of them wanted anything, which is the part of the evening he has never been able to explain to anybody who has not stood in it.

A man of about twenty-eight came in at about eight with a wall plate that had been pulled out of a flat and wanted to know whether it was the same as the ones in the corridor. It was not, and Marek told him so in about nine seconds, and told him why, and told him which of the two he should buy and what it would cost him if he bought the other one, and the man wrote it on the back of his hand in biro the way people do in that building.

Then the man thanked him, and Marek stood there afterwards with the feeling of having been exactly the right person for something, and it lasted about four seconds, and he understood during those four seconds that it was the same feeling he had every single evening and that he had been arranging his whole week around manufacturing it.

**Nobody had asked him anything about the appointment in that room. Nobody in that room knew there was an appointment, and the arrangement he had spent six days building — a green stair, a Friday slot, a half ticket under a wall list — was an arrangement about somebody else's authority, and the man with the wall plate had just been helped, in four seconds, by a person with no authority over anything at all, and had gone away glad.**

He did not enjoy the noticing. He has never enjoyed it and he has never once been able to put it down, and the closest he gets is a job he has done for nothing on a bench on a Monday, which is why he does them.

**At about a quarter to nine the parts cage man put the radio on again and a man of about thirty-three came in with a kettle and asked whether he would look at a switch on a stair that nobody had looked at in a year, and Marek said yes, and did it, and charged him four pounds, and the man paid without arguing, and Marek thought, for about a second and a half, that there is no version of this city in which a person is asked less, and then he thought that a man of thirty-three in a kettle shop on a Thursday evening is not the person who has been under-informed, and that he is a man who has always been able to get a kettle fixed, and that the two of those are the same man and the difference is somebody else's drawer.**

Nothing in that room on the second floor knew that a man of twenty-three walked into that room on the Thursday before with nine lines copied out by hand and no name on any of them, and that the standing in that room includes people who have never been introduced to him and have never heard of each other, and that one of the nine people on the list of about nine is a man of thirty-three who came in tonight for a kettle and has never once been asked anything by anybody, and would not know to complain.

**Nobody in that room on the second floor knew that a man of twenty-two stood at a counter in another district four hours later holding a job ticket with four words written on the back of it in another person's hand, and nobody in that shop knew what was on the second sheet of paper in a folder in Crown Terrace, and the two facts were never in the same sentence in front of either of the two people they belonged to.**

The man of about fifty-one at the wall was still at the wall at about six and his part was still not finished and he did not ask and was not asked.

The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door came in at about half past six, sat down, and was not asked a question, and was not thanked, and was not apologised to. Her page is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf in that room and was not read on the Thursday and was not taken down.

A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and some nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for sixty-eight weeks to the day without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
