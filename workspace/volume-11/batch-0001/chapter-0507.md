# Chapter 507 — A Boy Of Twenty-One, A Stair With A Green Door, And A Rule He Misheard

**The Monday was a bench, a counter, and a room, and the shape of the day was one young man standing at the end of a counter explaining, correctly and at some length, why he was not going to do the sensible thing, and one man of twenty-two letting a rule do an argument's work for him.**

---

The docket said eleven and the date column said eleven and eleven came, the last of them at about six in the evening, and the hours were bench in Lattice Ward until about two, counter until about six, and the rooms off the service road from about half past six. The rate was at nine and ten, a line he has not corrected in two years and is not going to correct this year. **The heating was on and dearer than in the winter, the four names on one lease paying most of the difference, and a fortnight laid out by a cook and owed to nobody is still owed to nobody.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and fifty-four days old and is warm, and on the Monday evening five people came in for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in that rail is eight hundred and fifty-eight days old, four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two. A green stair on a second floor in another district, a Friday afternoon slot, and a strip of biro on the back of a hand twenty-one years old are three more objects and none of them is a record of anything.

---

He came in at about four and did not have anything with him, which is the first thing Marek noticed, because everybody who comes to that counter has something with them, even if it is only a complaint.

He was about twenty-one and he had a school bag on one shoulder and the other strap was hanging, and he stood about two feet off the end of the counter rather than at it.

**"Is the woman in Crown Terrace the one about not understanding what you agreed to?"**

**"She's the one about not understanding what you agreed to."**

**"Right. Good. I've got the address."** He turned his left hand over. The address was on it in biro, four lines of it, and it was spelled correctly, including the street, which is spelled on the building differently from the way anybody says it.

**"I've been to a room like that once. Not that room. A different one, in the institute, on a Wednesday, and I was nineteen and I asked to sit in and I was told no, and I went away, and about nine weeks later something happened to somebody who had been in that room and I thought, well, I have been told no once."**

**"And you have decided that means you don't go."**

**"I've decided it means I go on my own or not at all,"** the boy said.

**"Because if I turn up with somebody, I'm not a person with a thing, I'm a person with a thing plus a person, and the second one makes me into a story about the second one."**

**"That is a very careful thing to have worked out on your own."**

**"I've had two years."**

Marek put down the cable he had been stripping and looked at the hand with the address on it, and what he noticed was not the address. It was that the boy had copied it off something printed rather than off a person, because the street is spelled on the building the way the boy had it and not the way it is said, and everybody who has ever said that street out loud spells it the other way.

**"What happened to the somebody,"** he said.

**"A relay went and a woman of about fifty tried a technique on her own to fix it and it did not work the way it works when there is somebody in it."** The boy stopped.

**"I'm not saying it was the room's fault. I'm saying I was in the room and I wasn't in the corridor, and if I'd been in the corridor I'd have been in the corridor."**

**"That's a fair thing to want."**

**"It isn't a thing I can have. I keep going back to that."**

**"You can go."**

**"I can go on my own."**

**"You can go on your own and you can go with somebody, and nobody has said otherwise, and nobody in that room has ever heard of you and does not have a rule about you."**

The boy looked at him for a second and then said the thing that Marek has thought about since, on and off, for longer than one evening.

**"So you won't come, because you're not allowed to."**

**"That's not—"**

**"It's in the thing you read out. Nobody may speak for her. You going with me is speaking for her."** He had it about four words out of six and Marek let him keep it.

**"That's fine. That's how it should be. I'd rather be told by a rule than be the reason somebody had to break one."**

Nobody said anything for about four seconds. The parts cage man had his back to the room and had had it since a quarter past seven and would have it until he went, and Marek understood that he was about to use a true sentence as a lid, and that the sentence was true, and that it was not the reason.

The real reason was that if he walked up a green stair on a Friday afternoon beside a young man holding a piece of paper, then every person in that room for the next two years would be a person who was brought, and a person who is brought does not come on their own later, and he would have built the office's whole method out of his own convenience without ever once asking anyone to approve it.

**He told the boy the true reason instead, because the boy had earned a true reason, and the true reason took about nine seconds and made him sound exactly as good as the rule would have.**

**"It isn't that I'm not allowed. It's that you'd be a person who was brought, and in about two years that is the only way anybody in that room will get there, and it will be because of me, and nobody will have decided that."**

The boy took the strip of biro back off his own hand with his thumb and looked at it and put it on the counter.

**"Then I'll take the address on paper,"** he said.

**"My hand's going to be wet on Thursday."**

Marek wrote the four lines on the back of a job ticket, and the boy folded it into the school bag, and neither of them mentioned that there were now two of those in the district with the same four lines on them and neither of them knew the other.

**What the boy took, and did not bring back, and was not asked for, was a screwdriver out of the fourth pocket of a tool roll that had not been opened in two years. It is a good one and it cost about half of what Marek takes for a morning's work, and he has not replaced it, and he is not going to replace it this month, and the fourth pocket of that roll is not empty and not full and he knows exactly what is in it.**

---

The fourth pocket of the tool roll had been shut for two years and it was not shut any more, and the roll travels to every call he makes, and there is a company in this city that will sell a man a good screwdriver and it is not the one that is missing.

He worked out what replacing it costs at about half past four and worked out that replacing it this month means not taking a Saturday, and he has taken every Saturday since the spring, and the Saturday is the one he puts the rent on, and the rent is not a thing anybody in this city has ever discussed.

**The boy's hand had the address on it in biro and it would be gone by Thursday, and the boy had known that before he asked for paper, and the fact that a young man of twenty-one has learned to expect that whatever is written on a person's hand is temporary is not a thing this city taught him and is a thing that is happening anyway.**

At about six he was at the wall and the man of about fifty-one put his hand out flat on the bench and did not say anything and did not take anything, and Marek gave him a five-eighths because you give a man a tool when he puts his hand out, and the man put it in the bag and said nothing, and it is the two hundred and seventeenth day since he has said something of any length in that room.

**At about a quarter past five a woman of about thirty-one came in with a pram wheel and asked whether a place in another district had ever heard of a place in her own street, and Marek said he did not know, and she said that is the answer, and went away satisfied, and was right, and he charged her nothing and told her that was because the answer had been free and not the work.** He has been told by two people in two years that he makes everything free and makes it sound like a favour, and both of them were wrong, and one of them was in the room on the Monday, and he is not going to correct the record with either of them.

The young man did not come back on the Friday. He was not expected to and was not asked to. The address on the job ticket is in a school bag somewhere about four days of bus travel is not away, in a town that has a school corridor and a timetable with nine lines on the back of it, and the school bag belongs to a young man who did not know that when he wrote the four lines on his hand.

Nobody in that shop knew that a boy of twenty-one had read the whole of an appointment out loud in a repair shop and got the middle of it slightly wrong in a way that made the man telling it sound better than he was, and nobody in that room on a second floor in Crown Terrace knew that the man who had read it out was the reason the sentence was repeated, and both of those facts are true and neither of them is on a piece of paper.

**Two figures in the row below are the same figure and are not the same thing. The sixteenth line on that board is six hundred and forty-four days old, and six hundred and forty-four is also the day the eighteenth line was written, and the eighteenth line is five hundred and seventy-two days old. They are each other's ages and each other's days and neither of them is a day the heating went off anywhere, and the heating in this case has no figure of days off for it in any of it.**

The man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for four hundred and sixty days.

The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in that room and was not asked a question, and her page is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and was not read.

A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date was not in this city and his hold ran on for sixty-nine weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
