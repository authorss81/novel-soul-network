# Chapter 505 — One Line In A Column That Has Been Empty For Four Months

**The Thursday was a bench and a counter and a room, and the shape of the day was a man of twenty-nine putting his name in a box that a company printed for a signature, and then not telling the one person who holds the key to the drawer it went into.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six and a room from about half past six, and none of those three places is where the form was. The rate was at nine and ten, uncorrected, and it was the last line on that sheet he did not read twice. **The heating was on and dearer, four names on one lease were still carrying it, and the fortnight a cook laid out was still on nobody's account and still had no paper against it.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and fifty days old and is warm, and on the Thursday evening six people came in for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in that rail is eight hundred and fifty-four days old, four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two. A form with two printed columns is a third object, a locked drawer is a fourth, and a key on a hook inside a door is a fifth, and this entry does not join any of them to any other.

---

He came at about half past four with a lift shaft part in a bag and the part was not why he had come.

He was about twenty-nine and worked on lifts, and he had been in that shop four times in two years and had never once been asked to sit down, and he stood at the end of the counter with his hand flat on it.

**"You've got a form in a drawer somewhere."**

**"There is a form in a drawer."**

**"Right."** He did not ask where.

**"I'm not going to sign it. I want to say that first, before anything, because if I'm here and then I'm not signing, that's a different thing and I don't want it to be the second one."**

**"Nobody has answered it. There have been nine copies of that form on nine tables in this city since it was delivered and the second column is empty on all nine of them, and the master one is in a drawer and the key is on a hook inside the door and I have not had the drawer open since the Friday it went in."**

**"You know the second column's a signature box."**

**"I know what it is printed as."**

**"It's a signature box."** The man took his hand off the counter.

**"It's got a printed heading on it and the heading is a person's name, and it doesn't say name, it says signature, and there's a difference and I've spent four months thinking about the difference and I want to put something in it."**

**"You can put anything in it. It isn't a locked form."**

**"That's what I'm afraid of."**

Marek said nothing for a moment. Behind him the cage man had his back to the room and had had it there since a quarter past seven, and at the north end a man of about fifty-one had a tool bag at his feet and had not said anything since before Marek was hired.

**"It isn't a signature if you don't sign it,"** Marek said.

**"If you write your name in it, that's a name, and a name in a box with a heading that says signature is a small lie that you would have to keep for as long as the paper exists."**

**"Yes."**

**"So what's the small true thing?"**

**"My name,"** the man said.

**"In that box. On that paper. In that drawer. Because the paper is the only record in this city that anybody made about us, and it is blank, and a blank record about a person is not a neutral thing. It is a thing that says nothing happened. Something happened. I happened. It happened to me and I am the only proof of it and I am going to put my name in the box."**

**"Who is it for."**

**"That is the part I cannot answer."**

**"It is for somebody,"** Marek said.

**"Somebody opens that drawer at some point, or somebody has a copy of that form on a table, and whoever it is reads a name and does not know what the name is about, and the name sits on a piece of paper doing no work at all."**

**"Then it isn't for anybody."**

**"Then it isn't for anybody, and it isn't for you, and it isn't for me, and the only person it is for is a version of you that has to prove in about two years that this happened. And that version is going to be somebody else, and she is going to have nothing, and your name on a line in a box is not going to help her, and I have not worked out what would."**

The man of about twenty-nine looked at the counter for a while and the man in the cage turned the radio up and then turned it down again as though he had been caught at something.

**"Is it for the company?"**

**"I have no idea and I have not asked,"** Marek said, and then heard himself say it, and went on rather than stopping.

**"I could ask. There is a man who comes round on Fridays and I could ask him on Friday. I have not asked, and I am telling you that I have not asked instead of letting you find out later that I had."**

**"Why have you not asked."**

**"Because the answer will be yes, and then I will be a man who knows, and then the next person who comes in here with something will be looking at the man who knows."**

**"And does somebody come in here with something?"**

**"Somebody comes in here with something on nearly every day of the week,"** Marek said,

**"and until this week I was the only route out of this room to anywhere at all, and I liked it, and that is the version I would rather hand you than the other one."**

Nobody thanked either of them. The man left the lift part and went at about five, and Marek put the part on the bench and then stood in the middle of the shop for about a minute and did not go and open the drawer, and it was not because of the rule he had made up on the Friday.

The cage man turned the radio up at about five and left it on for eleven minutes, which he has never done, and Marek said nothing about that either, and the man of about fifty-one at the wall looked at the cage man's back for the whole eleven minutes and then looked at the wall again, and Marek looked at both of them and thought about the fact that he is the only person in that building who cannot ask either of them a question and has assumed for two years that this is a fact about them.

**The decision he made at about ten past five, standing at the bench with a lift part in his hand, was to say nothing to the man who had just left, and the reason was not integrity. The reason was that the man had said in words that if Marek asked him on the Friday he would find out the answer and become the person who knew, and Marek believed him, and believed him because it is true, and had a bad half second when he noticed that he was relieved rather than sorry, and a worse half second after that, when he noticed what the relief was for.**

At about half past five he went into the room. The drawer is under the counter along the far wall and the key is on a hook inside the door and the drawer was not open and the key did not move, and he stood there for about forty seconds and then went and sat down.

There are now two people in this city who know there is a name in that drawer. One of them wrote it and one of them did not open it, and the rest of the city has not been told, and the difference between those two people and everybody else is that they happened to be in a shop.

**What he noticed, standing there, was the hook.** The key was on a hook inside the door and the paint around the hook had gone pale in a ring, a bright ring where a thing had been lifted off and put back a great many times, and the ring was not the shape of the key. It was round. It was the shape of a thumb and a finger and a great many months, and the key itself was a flat square-headed thing that fitted nothing you would expect it to fit, and the wear on the wall was the wear of a person and not of the object.

**What he wanted to say, and did not, was that the man should come back on the Friday and sign nothing and say the four words out loud to whoever is in the room, because a name in a box that a company printed is still a name in a box, and there is a woman on a second floor whose entire job is the difference. He did not say it. He said the true thing instead, which is that there is nobody in this city who has answered that form and there has not been one, and that part of the sentence was true and the other part was true and he left out the fact that a name went into the drawer at about ten past five on the Thursday and that the man had not told him he was going to do it, and Marek knew anyway, and said nothing, and went to the bench.**

He is the holder of a key to a drawer that now has a man's name in it. There is no line anywhere, in any book, on any wall, or on any piece of paper, that says whose drawer it is or what he is answerable for, and there has not been one since the drawer was put in that room.

---

**At about half past six, with the shop empty, a woman of about forty-three came in for a plug and stood at the counter while he found it, and said, without any preamble at all, that the new woman is a fool.** Marek said nothing. She said that a woman who cannot ask a company anything is a woman who will be here in a fortnight and gone in a year, and that the only people who ever change anything are the people who make the company do a thing in writing on a Tuesday.

Then she said the thing that Marek has thought about on and off for a long time, and she said it while he was holding a plug.

**"I have been saying a true sentence to a stranger in a room for about nine years and I have not once made anybody write it down, and I would go back and do it differently if I could do it again, and I could not, so it is not advice, it is a complaint."**

He gave her the plug. She paid. She said she was sorry and he said there was nothing to be sorry for, and both of those sentences were true, and she was not thanked and he was not thanked, and the second column of the form in the drawer now has one name in it and one name in a room above a line in another district and none of the three people involved has ever been introduced to the other two.

Nobody in that shop knew that the man who had just left worked on lifts in a building whose relay was on the company list, and nobody knew that he had put his name in a box in a room forty minutes after he said he would not sign anything.

The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in the room that evening and was not asked a question, and her page is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and was not read.

A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date was not in this city on the Thursday and his hold ran on for sixty-nine weeks to the day without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The second column, as a record of the first line ever entered and of what it is not.* **Where: a counter in one district of this city, at about half past four, with a lift part on the bench and a radio on low behind it. Who filled it in: a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts, who had been in that shop four times in two years and had never been asked to sit down. What he said before anything else: that he was not going to sign it, and that he wanted that established first so that his being there and not signing would not be mistaken for a second thing. What the box is printed as: a signature box, with a printed heading, and the difference between a name and a signature having been what he had spent four months thinking about. What was said against that: that writing a name in a box headed signature is a small lie the man would have to keep for as long as the paper exists. What he said his reason was: that the form is the only record anybody in this city has made about people like him, that it is blank, that a blank record about a person says nothing happened, and that something happened and he is the only proof of it. What was written: his name. When: about ten past five, in the room off the service road, in the second column of the master copy, after he had gone from the counter. What was said about it afterwards: nothing, to anybody, by either man. Whether the drawer was opened: no, and the key stayed on its hook and the wear round the hook is round and is not the key's shape. Whether the holder told him: no, and he already knew, and he chose the not-telling. Owner: a name, and a drawer, and the space between them.**

*The wear, as a record of a mark on a wall that is the shape of a person and not of a key.* **Where: inside the door of a converted shop unit off a service road, standing up, on the Thursday afternoon, about a minute after a man of about twenty-nine had left. What is on the wall: a hook with a key on it, and a pale ring in the paint round the hook where something has been lifted off and put back a great many times. What the ring is: round. What the key is: a flat square-headed thing that fits nothing a person would expect it to fit. What the difference means, and it took about a minute to see: the wear on that wall is the wear of a thumb and a finger and a great many months, and it is the wear of a person and not of the object, and every object in that room has been here for a fraction of the time. What was not done with that knowledge: nothing. Which drawer the key opens: one, under the counter along the far wall, holding one form with two printed columns in which a man had put his name about forty minutes earlier. Whether the drawer was opened: no, and the key did not move, and the man stood there for about forty seconds. What he wanted to say to the man who had just gone and did not: come back on the Friday, sign nothing, and say the four words out loud to whoever is in the room, because a name in a box a company printed is still a name in a box. Whether the man was told any of it: no. Owner: a ring, a key, and a drawer with a name in it.**

*What the day did not touch.* **The binder on the shelf in the warm room was not opened, its eighth of eight leaves is still unread, and the folded sheet beneath it did not move. A drawer in a building in another district stayed shut, a pencil case in a street in another district was not lifted, and the folder face up on a weighbridge table in the working Ashfields lay where it lies. The four rooms in this city, one in each of its four districts, have still never met and have never been introduced. The page of about nine lines in the second of the four districts remains unsigned, unadopted, and on nothing. Four copies of nine lines sit in four towns on four different objects and no two of the four has yet been laid beside another. Nine copies of the front of a page with about eleven lines on it are four hundred and sixteen days into the run, eight of the nine unfinished, and the first disagreement in the fourth line remains untraced. Nine doors on the far road are still on one hinge each, no wage mends them, and about eleven people have still not got their week back. Four plates of iron with four bolts each remain under four floors in four towns and have not been carried anywhere by anybody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 508.**
