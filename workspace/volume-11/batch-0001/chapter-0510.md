# Chapter 510 — Sunday, A Bag On A Chair, And A Friday Asked For Out Loud

**The Sunday was a counter in the morning and a warm room in the evening and nothing else at all, and the shape of the day was a tool bag that had been on the floor for sixty-six weeks put on a chair instead, and one man of twenty-two asking a woman of twenty-four for a Friday, out loud, with a condition attached.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; nine attended, the last of them about seven in the evening, and his day was a counter from about ten until about one and a room from about half past six, and there is no bench on a Sunday because the bench belongs to a department that does not open on one. The rate was at nine and ten and he did not read it. **The heating was on and dearer, four names on one lease were carrying it, and the fortnight a cook laid out was still on nobody's account and was still going to be on nobody's account in the spring.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and sixty days old and is warm, and on the Sunday evening four people came in and one of the four was there for a chair.

The card in that rail is eight hundred and sixty-four days old, four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two. A Friday is not a figure in this entry and the word is used four times, and the four times mean three different things, and only one of them is a slot.

---

He had been in that room on every working day for four hundred and sixty-six days and he had put the tool bag on the floor at his feet every one of them, and on the Sunday he put it on the chair.

Marek saw him do it and did not understand it for about a minute. There is a difference between a man sitting in a room and a man sitting in a room with his bag beside him, and the difference is not large, and it took him a minute to work out that the difference was that a bag on the floor is a man who is leaving and a bag on a chair is a man who has decided something, and that the something was that he was going to be asked a question and had decided to be in the room for the answer.

Nobody asked him anything until about half past seven.

**"I am going to say a thing and then I am going to stop talking,"** he said.

**"I have had it since the spring and I have been waiting for a day when there were not four people in here for the temperature."**

**"There are three,"** Marek said.

**"There are three,"** the man said,

**"and one of them is a woman who has never been asked anything, and I am aware of that, and I am saying it anyway, and if that is the wrong day then say so and I will take it back."**

**"It is not the wrong day. Say it."**

**"Four hundred and sixty-six days,"** he said.

**"That is how long I have been in this room. I have not been asked where I am from, what I do, what my bag holds, why I come at the hours I come, or whether I would go home if somebody asked me to. I have worked out what is in that bag and it is not a secret, and I have worked out that nobody is hiding anything from me, because hiding takes energy and nobody has spent any."**

He stopped for about four seconds, and Marek understood that this was going to be a short speech and that he had prepared it in the way people prepare speeches, standing up.

**"I want to say that I have noticed. That it is not a complaint and I am not asking anybody to do anything about it. And that I am going to keep coming, and that the reason I am saying it out loud to you is that if I say it only inside my own head then in about a year it will be a resentment, and in about two years I will be a man in a room who is owed something, and I would rather be a man in a room who is owed nothing and has said so."**

Nobody thanked him. Nobody said anything at all for about four seconds, and then the radio went off in the cage because it was a quarter past seven on a Sunday, and the man at the wall put his bag back on the floor.

**Marek had three things available to say and only one of them was true.** He could have said that the room was glad, which would have been a transaction, because a room cannot be glad and the sentence would have converted a man sitting down into a man who had been given something. He could have said that he should have asked, which would have been true and would have been about him. And he could have said nothing, which was what he did.

**What he wanted, standing at that bench with his hands in the pockets of a jacket with gloves under it, was to be told that he was doing this right. Nobody in this room was ever going to tell him that, and the man who had just spoken had understood the arrangement well enough to remove the possibility on purpose, and Marek understood it about a second and a half later, and by then the conversation had ended, and the not-being-told was the thing he was going to be carrying when he shut the shutter at about ten.**

---

She came at about half past eight, on foot, from a clinic across two districts, and she had a bag and she was tired in a way that was visible from the door.

**"Are you working?"**

**"No."**

**"Then I am going to sit down and I am going to say a thing and you are going to be allowed to say no to it."**

**"Go on."**

**"I want you to be in this room on a Friday."** She said it without stopping, the way she said things.

**"Not to run anything. Not to be asked for anything. I want you to be a person standing in a room so that a person who comes in here and has never been asked anything by anybody can see one man who has been asked something and has not been asked it twice."**

**"That is a good reason."**

**"It is not the reason."**

**"No,"** Marek said.

**"It is not. The reason is that I would like to be useful in a room where nobody can ask me for anything, and that is not a reason I can say out loud in the sentence you have just used, and I am going to say it out loud anyway, because if I do not say it now I will spend the next eleven weeks arranging myself to be asked."**

She looked at him for a while and then she gave him the condition, and she gave it in the voice she uses for a patient's medication.

**"Not on a Friday when there is a man in a lanyard in the other room. And not on a Friday when you are at that counter."**

**"Why not at the counter."**

**"Because if you are behind that counter, a person who has never been asked anything will look at you and see the man who does things for people, and they will not be able to see anything else, and you will have made it harder for them and you will have made it easier for you."**

**"That is fair."**

**"It is not fair. It is the minimum, and I am giving you the minimum on a Sunday because I am tired."** She put her bag down.

**"One Friday. Then we will see whether it was a good idea or whether I have built a thing out of a man who happens to be standing up."**

**"One Friday."**

**"One Friday, and you will not speak unless somebody speaks to you, and if somebody speaks to you, you will not tell them what I do or what I do not do, and if they ask, you will say that you are not the person who answers that, which is true, and which will be more use to them than anything you could have said."**

**"I can do that."**

**"You said that about a bracket on a Thursday and you did it in about four seconds and you were right. Say it again and mean it this time."**

**"I can do that."**

She stayed for about an hour and the two of them did not discuss it again, and nine people came into the room over the evening in all, five of them for the temperature, and one of the five was the man of about fifty-one, who put his bag on the floor again at about nine because it was Sunday and Sunday was different and he had decided that much himself.

---

After she had gone he stayed in the room with the radio off, which he does not do on any other day of the week, and four people came in for the temperature and went out again, and the man of about fifty-one stayed until about nine, and neither of them said anything for about an hour and a half, and it was the longest conversation either of them has had.

At about nine the man put his bag on the floor and said one more thing, not to Marek, to the middle of the room.

**"I am not going to be a person who is owed something,"** he said.

**"I would like that written down somewhere and I would like it written down by somebody who is not you."**

Marek said nothing at all, which was correct, and the man went out at about ten past nine and stood in the lane for a bit and then went home.

**What Marek wanted to say, and did not, was that there is nowhere he can write it down, and that there is a hook inside a door and a wall with nineteen lines on it and a book of four columns in another district and not one of the three is his, and that a man of fifty-one has just asked the wrong room to hold something and picked the right one anyway, and that he is going to keep picking it, and that if Marek ever builds a room where a thing like that can be put down then the man will find it in about four days and will not bring it up.**

The shutter came down at about ten and the room was warm and the Friday is not until next week and there is nothing on the wall about it.

**At about a quarter to ten a woman of about twenty-five came in with a coat over her arm and asked whether the room was open on a Friday, and Marek said it was, and she said she had heard it was not, and he said it is not an office, and she said that is what she had heard too, and then she asked him, in a way that was not unfriendly, whether he was the one who had sent it.** He said no. She said that the person who had told her about it had said his name, and he stood there for about four seconds, and then he said that he had given an address to about four people this week and that if she had a name and a time he would find out who said it and why, and she said she would come back on Friday and gave him neither.

**He sat down afterwards and understood, for the first time all week, what the appointment is actually for, and it is not for the man of twenty-seven in the canteen and it is not for the rota man with the notice and it is not for the boy of twenty-one who is on a bus. It is for the people who have been told his name by somebody who got it from him, and there is no office in this city that those people can go to, and she came to a warm room on a Sunday because it is the only room she knows of, and he cannot tell her that he cannot be that.**

He did not sleep much on the Sunday night and did not tell anybody why, and on the Monday he is at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at that counter until about six and the Friday is five days away and is not going to arrive any sooner for being wanted.

**Nobody in that room knew that a rota man in a building of about nine flats had a notice in his hand that is still not on a board, and nobody in the room with the shuttered window knew that a man of about forty-one had offered that room on a Friday to somebody else, and neither of those people has ever spoken to the man of about fifty-three about a Sunday, and all three of them are in this city.**

The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in that room on the Sunday and was not asked a question, and her page is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and was not read, and the folded page at the bottom of that binder did not move.

A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date was not in this city and his hold ran on for seventy weeks and three days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
