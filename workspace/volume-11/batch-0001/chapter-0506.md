# Chapter 506 — A Man Of Fifty-Three Who Says Where A Plate Is, And Does Not Move It

**The Friday was a bench, a counter, and a room with a man in a lanyard at the front of it, and the shape of the day was two people standing at the end of a cold pipe in a cellar in one town, saying where the same thing is under two other floors, and neither of them saying the word for it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about five and a shuttered room from about half past five, and no cellar in any town was on that list. The rate was at nine and ten, and he did not get to that line on the Friday at all, because a man was in the shop who had come to be refused. **The heating had been on all winter and dearer than it was, with four names on one lease paying for the increase, and the fortnight a cook laid out is still on nobody's account.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and fifty-one days old and is warm, and four people came in on the Friday night for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in that rail is eight hundred and fifty-five days old, four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two. A plate of iron with four bolts in it at the end of a cold pipe under a floor is a sixth object in this entry and is named as itself and is not joined to any other.

---

The woman came in at about one with a meter that had been read by a company man on the first and that she wanted checked before the company man came back on the first again, which is a month away and which she said four times in about ninety seconds.

Marek did it for nothing because the shop does that, and while the cover was off he asked her how she kept her readings, and she turned her left forearm over without being asked, which is what people do when you ask a question whose answer is on their body.

She kept them in a column down the inside of her forearm in biro, no headings, no date against any of them, forty-one figures in a column that ran from the wrist to just below the elbow, and the last four digits of the thirtieth, the thirty-fourth and the thirty-eighth were identical.

**"That's the meter."**

**"That's the meter."**

**"Or somebody's copied it off the one above."**

**"Or somebody's copied it off the one above,"** she agreed,

**"and I have known that since about the twelfth and I have not done anything about it, because what I would do about it is a letter, and a letter goes to a company, and then a man comes out here and stands where you're standing and does not have a conversation."**

**"You could keep it and not send it."**

**"I have been keeping it,"** she said.

**"Keeping it is what I have been doing since the twelfth. Keeping it and saying nothing to anybody is the same as not keeping it, only it takes longer and it is worse, because now I have a column with a lie in it and my handwriting on the lie."**

He wanted to write it down. He wanted it more than he wanted most of the things he wanted in that week, because he had a piece of paper under the counter that nobody would ever read and a person in front of him whose lie was written on her own arm, and the two of those facts fitted together so neatly that the fitting felt like an instruction.

**He did not do it. He put the cover back and told her the meter was fine and the reading was true and that he could not tell her which, and that the only way to find out was for the next reader to be somebody who had never met the man who read it on the first, and that she could ask the office on a second floor in Crown Terrace for a Friday slot, and she said she would think about it, and he knew what thinking about it looked like because he does it.**

The cost of not writing it down is that in about a month the column on her arm will have forty-three figures on it and one of them is a copy of another, and nobody will ever know which, and he knew on the Friday and did nothing, and the not doing was correct and it still cost.

---

The cellar had a low ceiling and a floor that had been lifted and put back more than once by people who did not care that it had been.

**"That's a plate."**

**"That's a plate."**

**"Four bolts."**

**"Four slots, and they're all the same width, and it isn't bolted, it's wedged, and there's about an inch of mortar round three sides and none on the fourth, and whoever put it in did it fast and did it alone."**

He was about fifty-three and he fitted plumbing and he had been in that cellar for about four minutes when he said the thing that made the woman on the step look at him instead of at the floor.

**"It's under this floor and it's been under it longer than the building's been here, and I have not moved it and I am not going to."**

**"You know that?"**

**"I know I won't."**

She was about thirty-five and she read meters and she had been in two cellars that week and neither of them had anything in it, and she came down the steps with a lamp in her hand and stayed about six feet away, which is a distance she uses.

**"Mine's in a school in a town about a bus day from here,"** she said.

**"End of a cold run under a corridor floor, four bolts, same plate. I found it reading a boiler that wasn't drawing. I put the floor back and I wrote nothing down."**

**"I found mine reading a stack that was doing nothing."**

**"Do you want to know where yours is?"**

**"No."**

That was the whole of it. She nodded at the ceiling and went up the steps, and he stayed in the cellar for another eleven minutes doing the job he had come for, and neither of them used the word for the thing more than once between them, and neither of them asked the other for an address.

**"Why not?"** he said, to nobody, with his back to a wall.

**"Because if you lift it, it stops being a thing that was always there and it becomes a thing somebody put somewhere, and then it is a thing that can be found by anybody with a torch and a weekend, and the only person who has any interest in it being found is a person who would rather it were not under a floor."**

**"That is not the reason either,"** she said, from the step.

**"No."**

**"Then give me the reason, because I have just told you where mine is to a stranger and I would like it to have been worth something."**

He thought about it for longer than the cellar deserved and then he said the true thing, which was not the thing he had said first.

**"Because the day somebody says where it is, it stops being a curiosity and starts being a job. And a job has a man assigned to it. And the man who gets assigned to it will be paid, and the paying is how it stops being a curiosity for good."**

She came down four steps and looked at the plate for about four seconds and then went back up.

**"Then I have made it worse by telling you,"** she said.

**"You have made it worse by telling me and better by telling me, and both of those are true, and I am not going to pick one for you."**

**"You are doing that on purpose."**

**"I am doing that because I have watched about nine people do it in about nine rooms and it is the only technique in this business I have never once got wrong."**

He came up out of that cellar and did the two hours of the rest of his Friday in a school corridor in the same town, and the school corridor is the fourth of the four objects and it is not a cellar and nobody in that school has ever been told.

**He said where his was to a woman of about thirty-five on the Thursday and he said it in about nine words and he did not write it down and he did not tell a man of twenty-two in a shop about two districts away, and by the time anybody from a company stands on that road — and somebody will, inside a month, because that is what a company does with a road that goes nowhere — the two of them will have known each other's existence for about three weeks and will still not have compared a single thing.**

---

He came out of the shuttered room at about seven and a man of about twenty-eight was waiting in the corridor with a question about a release that had not held, and it is a question Marek can answer in about nine seconds and does.

He answered it. The man wrote nothing down and went away satisfied, and it was correct, and nobody thanked Marek, and he stood in the corridor afterwards for about a minute feeling the thing he cannot switch off, which is that he was the person in this city who knew, and that knowing is a thing that happens to him rather than a thing he has done.

**On the same evening, in a town about a bus day away, a man of about fifty-three said a woman's school out loud to a stranger and did not write it down, and in this city a man of about thirty-one was told the answer to a question by a man he did not ask, and in a canteen a woman with forty-one figures on her arm decided to go on keeping them. Three rooms, three towns, one evening, and the only one of the three that is on a page is this one, and the only reason is that the room is warm.**

He went back down the corridor and did not tell the man in the lanyard that a man of about forty-one had offered that room on a Friday to somebody in another district four days ago, and the not-telling was the same shape as the not-telling on the Thursday in the other shop with the drawer, and he is the only person in the city who can see the shape, and seeing it has never once made him stop.

**The corridor outside that room has a bench on it and a notice about a fire door that has been on the bench for about nine months, and Marek sat on it for four minutes at about a quarter to eight and did not read the notice and thought about the four plates of iron instead.** He has no reason to think about them. Nobody has told him about any of them, no plate has been described to him, and the word has never been used. He thought about them anyway, in the sense that he thought about the fact that a thing under a floor that nobody has written down is the only kind of thing in this city that has not yet become somebody's job.

That is all he had, and it was not much, and it did not have a shape, and he wrote nothing down about it, and the not-writing was not restraint. It was that he could not think of a place to put it that was his.

**Two figures on the row below are the same figure and they are not the same thing, and a person who gets this far without saying so has printed a coincidence instead of an arithmetic.** The fifteenth line on the hardboard is six hundred and sixty-six days old, and six hundred and sixty-six is also the day the nineteenth line was written, and the nineteenth line is five hundred and forty-seven days old, and the two numbers are one another's ages and days and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, which is a closed thing in this case with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it.

**Nobody in that shop on the Friday night knew that two people had stood six feet apart in a cellar in a town and each said the name of a building, and the woman reading meters has never given the address of the school to anybody, and the man who fits plumbing has never given the address of the cellar to anybody, and the two of them have not exchanged a card and are not going to.**

The man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall on the Friday and his part was ready and he took it and said nothing, and it is the four hundred and fifty-seventh day of his saying nothing and he has been in that building on every working day since the room was two rooms.

In the chair nearest the door on the Friday night there was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything and nobody thanked, and the page that belongs to her is the eighth of eight in a binder on a shelf and was not opened.

A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date was not in this city and his hold ran on for sixty-nine weeks and one day without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The cellar, as a record of where a thing is and why nobody lifted it.* **Where: beneath the floor of a building on a road that goes nowhere anybody goes on purpose, on the Thursday, with a low ceiling and a lamp, about four minutes after the man arrived. Who was there: a man of about fifty-three who fits plumbing, and a woman of about thirty-five who reads meters, who stood six feet away on the steps, which is a distance she uses. What was under the floor: a plate of iron with four slots rather than four holes, all four slots the same width, an inch of mortar on three sides and none on the fourth, and wedged rather than bolted. What he said about it: that it has been under that floor longer than the building, that he has not moved it, and that he is not going to. What she said: that the same plate is at the end of a cold run under a corridor floor in a school in a town about a bus day away, found while reading a boiler that was not drawing, with the floor put back and nothing written down. Whether the addresses were exchanged: no. Whether anything was written down: no. Whether the two have met since: no. Owner: four slots, and two floors, and no list.**
