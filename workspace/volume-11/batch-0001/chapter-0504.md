# Chapter 504 — A Name She Was Not Given, And A Card She Was

**The Wednesday was a bench, a counter, and a warm room that people came into for the temperature, and the shape of the day was one woman standing in a doorway doing three things in a particular order that nobody had taught her, and one question at the end of it that a man of twenty-two did not answer the way he wanted to answer it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and the counter until about six and the room off that service road from about half past six. The rate was at nine and ten, and no customer, employee or committee has ever asked him what the rate is for. **The heat is on, up again, and the four names on one lease are carrying the difference, and a fortnight of a cook's own money is still owed to nobody and is not going to be asked for.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and forty-nine days old and is warm, and on the Wednesday evening five people came in for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in that rail is eight hundred and fifty-three days old, four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two. Half a ticket on a hook under a wall list is a different object again and is not a record of anything, and a canteen on a corner three streets from a company depot is a sixth thing in this entry that joins none of the others.

---

At about five a woman of about twenty-eight brought in a bracket for a mobile clinic in the Lower Wards and waited for it and did not hurry him, and while he was in the cage getting it she read the wall list, which anybody can do, and then she read the job ticket hooked underneath it, which is a different thing that anybody cannot really do.

She did not say anything about it. She paid, she left, and the bracket is in a bag on the counter with a name on it, and the four words in another person's handwriting are in a plastic sleeve nine inches from where she was standing.

Marek looked at the sleeve for about a minute afterwards and worked out that the office's address is now in three places he knows about, which is two more than a thing with eleven weeks on it should be in, and that every one of those places is a place he can get to, and that there is no version of this in which that is an accident.

**He wanted to take the ticket down. He wanted to take it down because a person stood nine inches from a woman's handwriting without being invited to, and because in two months there will be somebody standing nine inches from it, and because he can see the shape of the thing that happens next and he does not want it to happen and cannot stop it and has no standing to stop it.**

He left the ticket on the hook. The cost of leaving it was two months of watching, and the cost of taking it down would have been a woman of twenty-four finding out on a Friday that he had gone through her pockets, and he chose the watching, and he did not tell her that he was choosing it, and it took about eleven seconds to make.

---

The woman at the doorway was about twenty-five and she washed up at a canteen on a corner where nine people eat at one and nine at seven, and she had a coat on for an indoor room because she had come straight from a shift.

She stood in the doorway of the warm room for about four seconds before she came the rest of the way in, and in those four seconds she looked at the people, and then at the door, and then at the wall.

Marek noticed the order of it from the counter with his back half turned, and noticed it about two hours later, standing at the bench, and by then he had also noticed that the man in the cage does the same three things and does them in a different order, wall first, and that neither of them learned it from the other.

She was about twenty-five and she washed up at a canteen on a corner where nine people eat at one and nine at seven, and she had a coat on for an indoor room because she had come straight from a shift.

**"You're the one who does the evenings here."**

**"I'm the one who's in the evenings."**

**"Right."** She sat down, then stood up again, then sat down.

**"Sorry. I've come to ask you something and I'd rather not sit for it."**

**"Ask it standing."**

**"There's a woman who washes up with me,"** she said.

**"No — sorry. There's a man who works the counter in that canteen and he's twenty-seven and he's been there nine years, and on the Monday a man from a company came in with a form about a relay in the back and my colleague put his name on it because she told him it was a stock check."**

**"And?"**

**"And he has not slept. And he has not said anything at work, and I have washed up beside him four nights and I have said nothing either, and I would like to stop carrying it and I do not want him to lose the canteen over it."**

Marek looked at the wall for a while. The cage man had his back to the room and had had it since a quarter past seven and would have it until he left.

**"There is a new office,"** he said.

**"Second floor, green stair, Friday afternoon slot for anybody who cannot come in person. It is for a person who was asked to do a thing and did not understand what they were agreeing to. That is his."**

**"That's a room."**

**"It is a room and a slot and a woman who can be spoken to directly."**

**"That's a room and a Friday,"** she said.

**"He works Fridays at a depot. He has done for two years. If he loses a Friday he loses about a week's money and he will not lose a Friday to go and stand in a room in Crown Terrace and say that a woman from his own canteen told him to."**

**"Then send it in."**

**"Send it in what?"** She almost laughed.

**"There's no box. There's no address that means anything to him. He's twenty-seven and he signed a form about a stock check and he thinks he's in trouble for it, and if somebody from a company finds a sheet with his name on it in a building in Crown Terrace he is finished and it will be my hand that put it there."**

**"Then do not send it."**

**"Then I am what I am."**

She stood up. Marek stood up as well, which was the correct thing to do and which he would think about later, because he had not decided to.

**"What I am asking,"** she said,

**"is whether there is a person. Because a room with a door that shuts is not a person, and I have been carrying a thing for three weeks and I would like to put it down in front of somebody who has to be counted."**

**"I know."**

**"You don't."**

**"No,"** Marek said.

**"I don't."**

**"Then why did you say it."**

**"Because it is true and because it is what I would have said if you had not asked me, and I would rather have said it to you than had you find out on a Friday in a corridor."**

She took the address on the back of the half ticket when he read it out, and he wrote it on the back of a job ticket with his own hand while she watched him do it, and gave that one to her as well, and there were two of it now where there had been one, and that was the cost and he had chosen it in about a second and a half and he knew the price while he was paying it.

**He wanted to say that he would go with her. He wanted it so much that his mouth had started on the word. He did not say it, because she had said that the whole of her difficulty was that the hand that put the paper there would be found out, and a man standing next to her when she walked in would be the same hand in a different position.**

---

She had not been in the room four minutes and Marek had already failed her once, and he spent the rest of the evening being unable to put that down, which is not a thing he is used to, because the things he is used to failing at are the ones where somebody wants a bracket.

He went to the canteen on the corner at about a quarter to six on the Thursday, on the way to a call, and stood across the road from it for about two minutes and did not go in. There were nine people at the counter and one of them was a man of twenty-seven who had not slept since Monday and who would, in about three weeks, find out that a woman who washed up had been in a room off a service road asking about him.

Marek had the address in his own handwriting in his pocket and about eleven seconds to decide whether a man who repairs lifts for a living was the right person to walk into a canteen and introduce himself to that.

**He decided he was not, and the deciding was correct, and the reason it was correct is that she had told him the reason and he had listened to it, and the reason he remembers the eleven seconds afterwards is that the correct decision and the useful decision came apart about nine seconds apart and he took the correct one, and there is no version of this man in which taking the useful one would have been the same choice.** 

He went to the call, it was a kettle, it took twenty minutes, and he charged her.

**At about a quarter past seven a man of about forty-six came in to ask whether a company could be told to stop coming to his mother's building on a Wednesday, and Marek said no, in about four seconds, and was right, and then said the true thing afterwards, which was that a company is a company and a Wednesday is a Wednesday and a person with a key to a building has more standing in that building than any office in Crown Terrace, and that the question had been answered before it was asked by the fact of the key.** The man wrote nothing down and went away unconvinced and came back on the Friday, and Marek refused him again in about four seconds, and the second refusal took him about two seconds longer than the first, and he could not work out why, and has not since.

**What he wanted, in the second refusal, was to be the person who could say it was fine. He has wanted that every day for two years. On the Wednesday a woman of twenty-four was made into a person whose entire job is to say exactly that it is not fine, with a written limit on it, and he is not jealous of her office and is jealous of the shape of it, and the shape is the only thing he has ever wanted for himself.**

She went. He was at that counter until about six and then in the room until the shutter.

**What he wanted to say, and had not said, was that he would like there to be nothing in it that needed him at all, and that he had wanted to say that since a Thursday, and that saying it now would have been a way of taking something back out of a room he was not even in. He said a different thing, out loud, to a woman of twenty-four in a corridor of a clinic building at about half past six, and it was the question he had not asked since that Thursday.**

**"Is there anything in it that needs me?"**

She was carrying a bag and she stopped walking for about a second and then kept walking, which is a thing she does.

**"Not yet."**

**"Right."**

**"And if there is, I will not ask you, and you will have to wait, and I am telling you that now so that you do not spend a month arranging yourself around being useful to me."**

**"That is fair."**

**"It is not fair. It is just true, and I have not had a minute to make it fair, and you are going to have to hold the difference."**

**"I can hold the difference."**

**"I know you can. That is the whole problem and it has been the whole problem since the first week you met me."**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
