# Chapter 509 — A Man With Two Cards Who Gave Away The One Without The Second Column

**The Thursday was a bench, a counter, and a room, and the shape of the day was a company operations director offering a room on a Friday and asking for one number, and a man of twenty-two refusing him in about nine seconds, and both of them naming a cost out loud, and neither of them being thanked.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and the counter until about six and the room from about half past six. The rate was at nine and ten, and on a day when somebody is offering a building for nothing that is not the number worth having accurate. **The heating is on and dearer and the four names on that lease are paying the difference, and a fortnight of a cook's own laying-out has been owed to nobody since the winter.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and fifty-seven days old and is warm, and on the Thursday evening six people came in for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in that rail is eight hundred and sixty-one days old, four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two. A business card with a company name and no second column, and the second card that was not handed over, and a figure of about four hundred competent people in this city who can each invoke a printed paragraph and not one of whom has anywhere to put an objection, are three more things in this entry and none of them is joined to any other.

---

He came at about half past three and stood at the counter in a coat for a warm day and did not sit on the stool by the till, which is the stool people sit on.

He was about forty-one and dressed better than that counter has ever been dressed for, and he had two cards, and Marek watched him hold both of them and then watch him put one away before he said anything.

The card that stayed out had a company name on it, a title, and a postal district. There was no second column. There was no box at the foot of it. It was a card with one piece of information on it and a great deal of white space where information usually goes, and the person holding it was entirely comfortable with that, which Marek noticed and did not enjoy noticing.

**"I am not here to sell you anything,"** the man said,

**"and I am not here about the form, whatever your man at the door thinks when I say a company name out loud."**

**"Nothing was said about the form."**

**"Good."** He put the card down on the counter, at the edge, in the place where people put things they do not want touched.

**"I run operations for a company that maintains most of the relay hardware between this city and the coast. I have read what four people copied onto a chopping board. I have not copied any of it and I have not asked anybody to copy any of it, and I would like to give you a room."**

**"What room."**

**"Our Friday room. It is warm, it has nine chairs, and it is empty on a Friday from about ten because the training it was built for was cancelled in the spring and nobody upstairs will re-let it this year."** He said all of that in about eleven seconds and he said it as though he had said it before and not to this person.

**"You would have it on a Friday. You would have the door. Nobody from this company would sit in it, and I would put that in writing on the same sheet you are already imagining."**

**"In exchange for what."**

**"For a number."**

**"No."**

**"You have not heard the number."**

**"I have heard what a number is for."**

The man of about forty-one did not raise his voice and did not put his hands in his pockets. He turned the card on the counter a quarter turn, which is a thing men do with objects when they are buying two seconds.

**"I am not asking for a name,"** he said.

**"I am not asking for a list. I am asking for one figure, and I am telling you what I would do with it, which is that I would put it in a document that goes to a room of people who decide where relay work goes. That document has said the words the inter-city arrangement for four months and nothing has moved, because nobody can be given money for a thing that does not have a size."**

Marek was aware that he had stopped stripping the cable. He was also aware, about two seconds later, of the thing underneath the answer, which was that he knew the figure. He had given a number to a room of about nine people in a town once, and it had been the only honest number he had, and it had become four hundred inside a fortnight, and it could not be taken back, and he had never been able to work out whether the disaster was the number or the nine words in front of it.

**He wanted, for about a second and a half, to say the number. He wanted it the way a man wants to put a hand on a sore tooth. The wanting was so specific and so quick that he understood, while it was happening, that this was the whole of what a person like this one was for.**

**"No,"** he said, and the nine seconds were up.

**"May I ask what that no is."**

**"You may ask."**

**"Is it a rule, or is it you?"**

**"It is a figure about four hundred people who have each said a different thing to a different person in a different room, and about nine of them said it to me, and I do not know which of the four hundred is the true one, and I gave one of them a number once and it turned into a different number without anybody lying."** He put the cable down.

**"If I give you a figure, it is the ninth one, and in a fortnight it will be four hundred and one, and then a person in a room in another district will be asked to account for a number they have never seen, and they will be asked it by you."**

The man of about forty-one looked at him for a second longer than was comfortable and then said the thing that made Marek think about him afterwards.

**"That is a good answer and it is also the reason the work does not get done, and I have been in this for nineteen years and I have watched about nine people say something like that to about nine rooms."**

**"What is the cost, though."** Marek said it before he could decide not to.

**"Say the cost out loud, because I would rather be told a price than be told a principle."**

**"The cost to me is a Thursday and a hotel room and a conversation on Friday with a woman in a building who will not have enjoyed the answer."** He picked the card up off the counter.

**"The cost to the arrangement is that when this does eventually get funded, the first question anybody asks is who said no to free infrastructure on a Thursday, and it will be a man who repairs lifts for a living, and that is the sentence that will be in a room I will not be in."**

**"Then write that sentence down."**

**"No,"** said the man of about forty-one.

**"I don't think I will."**

He went out at about ten to four and did not hurry. Marek watched him through the window turn left towards the room with the shuttered window and the man in the lanyard, and watched him go in, and stayed at the counter, and did not say one word to the man in the lanyard about it at the end of the evening, which was the cost of the nine seconds and the only one he could name.

---

The counter was empty for about eleven minutes after he went and Marek stood at it and looked at the stool by the till, which the man of about forty-one had not sat on, and thought about the fact that a person in this city can be handed a warm room with nine chairs in it for nothing, and that the correct answer was no, and that the reason the correct answer was no was a number that a man of twenty-two gave to a room of about nine people in a town once and watched become four hundred.

That is the whole of the defence and it took nine seconds and it is good, and the nine seconds that were not part of the defence were the four seconds in the middle of them where his mouth was actually open and the figure was actually in it.

**He did not say the figure. He has never said the figure to a second person in his life and there is no rule anywhere in this city that says he cannot, and the reason he does not is that he watched it move once, and a rule is a much easier thing to carry than a memory.**

At about ten past four a man of about thirty-three came in with a lamp and paid and left and Marek said nothing to the man in the lanyard at six, and the not-saying is the only part of the Thursday he would not do again and the only part he does every week.

**He stood at the counter for about eleven minutes after the man of about forty-one had gone and did not do anything, which is not a thing he does.** The radio was on. The cage man had his back to the room. The man of about fifty-one was at the wall with a bag at his feet, four hundred and sixty-three days into it, and he has never once looked at Marek in a way that could be called waiting, and Marek has never once looked at him in a way that could be called not.

What Marek did in the eleven minutes was reconstruct the Thursday. A card in one hand and a card in the other, and the one that stayed out had nowhere to sign, and the one that went away did, and a man of forty-one in a warm room deciding in a doorway which of two pieces of card to hand to a man who repairs lifts. Somebody in a company has worked out that the card with a signature box is a document and the card without one is a greeting, and has decided to hand out the greeting, and has done it for years, and there is no rule anywhere that says it is wrong.

**And the whole of that is in the difference between two cards and in about four seconds of a man's hand, and it took the counter eleven minutes to understand it and it will not be written down, and that is the third time this week that something true has been available in this building and has had nowhere to go.**

At about ten to five a man of about nineteen came in for a lamp and Marek fixed it in nine minutes and did not charge him and the young man left without saying anything, and Marek charged the next one.

**Nobody in that shop knew that the second card in that man's pocket had a box printed at the foot of it, and that he had decided which of the two to hand over before he came through the door, and that the one he put away is the one with somewhere for a signature.**

Nobody in the room with the shuttered window knew that the man who had just gone in had offered that room on a Friday to a man in another district and had been refused, and the man in the lanyard has not been asked and would not have answered.

The man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall on the Thursday and had said nothing for four hundred and sixty-three days.

The chair nearest the door was taken at about half past six by a woman of about thirty, who was not asked a question, was not thanked and was not apologised to, and whose page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf and went unread.

A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date was not in this city and his hold ran on for seventy weeks to the day without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
