# Chapter 519 — A Bench Woman In A Warm Room, And A Man Of Thirty-Three Naming What She Is Not Saying

**The Monday went from a bench to a counter to a warm room, and the shape of the day was a woman with a bag standing in a doorway too long, and a refusal to ask her anything, and a man who had come on a bus from a town on the far side of the road saying the thing she had never said about herself.**

---

Eleven on the docket, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening, and the day was a bench in Lattice Ward to about two, that district's counter to about six, and the four rooms off a service road from about half past six. The rate stands at nine and ten, wrong, uncorrected, and not said to anybody. **Heat in the building above the road: on, dearer, four names on one lease up with it, and a fortnight that one cook laid out is on nobody's account.** The service-road room is eight hundred and eighty-two days old, which is one hundred and twenty-six weeks to the day, four rooms and one behind, warm.

The card in the rail is eight hundred and eighty-six days, four days beyond the room, and the rail takes two. A bag with a vice key and a pick in it, put down on a chair by somebody who then did not sit down, is a fifth object in this entry and is not that card and is not in that rail.

---

At about three in the afternoon a man of about thirty-eight came in with a key and a job card and asked whether a thing that was not in his name could still be in his name in about a fortnight, and Marek said that it could, and that the only thing he needed from him was the name of the unit and the name of the person who put it there, and the man gave him both and then stood there for about eleven seconds and asked the second question.

**"And you will not write it down."**

**"I will write the unit and the date and nothing else, and the date goes on the ticket, and that is a job card and a job card goes in a drawer on a Friday."**

**"That is what I am asking you to do. That is exactly it."**

**"I know it is. I have been asked it three times in about a week by three different men and all three of them asked it in the same words, and I would like you to know that I noticed the three words and not the three men."**

The man laughed at that and went, and Marek wrote the unit and the date on a job card and filed it, and the second question did not go on the card and the name of the person who put the thing there did not go on the card, and the card is a card.

**What he wanted to write and did not, and this is the second time in about a fortnight and it is a habit now and habits are the things that get into a room before anybody is ready for them, was the man's face.** Not the name, not the question, the face. A man of about thirty-eight who had been somewhere else in the city that morning and had seen something about the same shape as this, and who had come to a counter and asked, and who had been told the truth, and who had accepted it, and who had gone.

---

She came in at about seven on the Monday evening with a bag, and she had come on foot from across two districts, and she stood in the doorway for about four seconds longer than a person stands in a doorway, which is a thing Marek has learned to read in about two years and has never once been wrong about.

She is about thirty-five. She reads meters and she opens locks. She has a bench off a court with a vice on it and two tools lying on the top of the bench and a ring on and a door that is left open on purpose.

**"I have come to ask about a boy."**

Marek put a kettle on, which he does when somebody comes in with a bag and stands in a doorway, and then he stopped with his hand on the switch, because he understood about a second and a half later what he was about to do and what it would mean if he did it.

**"I am not the person who asks."**

**"You are the person in the room."**

**"I am the person in the room. There is a woman on a second floor with a green stair who is the person who asks, and she has eleven weeks, and I am not allowed to speak for her, and she is not mine to send you to. If I asked you what you came for and then took it up those stairs, then those stairs would have a second mouth and the second mouth would be me, and that is the one thing she stood in a room with nine chairs in and made somebody write down."**

**"You are refusing me."**

**"I am telling you the truth, which is slower and which you are about to find irritating."**

---

**"I am not one of anything,"** she said, before he had asked, and she said it to the middle of the room, and the man of about thirty-three by the door turned round.

**"I did not ask you that."**

**"Everybody asks it inside about four minutes. You had a face on and I have seen that face on a rota man and on a woman who types hours and on about four hundred other people, and the face is the same face."**

**"Then you can put the face down and I will not ask it."**

**"Put it down yourself."**

**"All right. It is down."**

She sat down, and then she stood up again about four seconds later, and said the thing she had come for.

**"There is a boy of about nineteen. He came to me on the Wednesday and he did not sign a card. On the Thursday he brought it back and he did not sign it again, and he came to tell me he had not, and he looked like somebody who has not slept, and I did not ask him anything and he went."**

**"And you have come here."**

**"I have come here to find out whether he is all right, and I am not going to go and ask him, and I know that those are the same sentence in this city."**

**"They are not the same sentence, and I will tell you the difference, and then you will not like it."**

**"Go on."**

**"Going to ask him is a thing you can do in eleven minutes and it puts him in a room where a man knows his name. Not going to ask him leaves him where he is, and where he is, on the evidence of a Wednesday and a Thursday, is a boy who said no twice to a man who is not a monster."**

---

**"I want to tell you what I am protecting,"** the man of about thirty-three said, and he had been standing by the door since about a quarter past seven and he had not said a word in that time, and he said it now because about four other people had come in for the temperature and he had decided it was now or never.

**"You are not protecting a list. You are not protecting a building, and you are not protecting a set of people, and anybody in this room who has told themselves that in the last week has got you wrong and has got themselves wrong first."**

**"Then what am I protecting."**

**"How long a person has before they have to answer."**

He said it the way a man says a figure he has checked.

**"There is a woman in a town four days of bus travel from here who put a folded sheet on a table in front of me about eleven days ago and would not tell me what was on it, and she was right to, and I have not stopped thinking about it. You are not keeping anybody from being asked things. You are keeping the space between the question and the answer wide enough for a person to be slow in, and everybody else in this city, including the office on the second floor, is trying to make that space narrower, and it is not because any of them is a villain. It is because a narrow space is easier to put a form in."**

**"Nobody has ever said that to me."**

**"I have known you for about six minutes."**

**"That is not a reason."**

**"It is the only reason I have, and I have just used it, and I am not going to pretend I did not."**

---

The woman of about thirty-five sat down on that one and put her hands flat on her knees, and did not say anything for about eleven seconds, and then said the thing the man of about thirty-three had crossed four days of bus travel to hear somebody else say, and she said it in one word, and nobody had asked her a question first.

**"Yes."**

**"I am not going to say it again and I am not going to say it to anybody who comes here about it, and if you tell anyone in this room what I just said I will take my bag and go and you can have the chair back."**

Nobody in that room said a word for about four seconds, and the man of about thirty-three put his hand flat on the table the way she had, and did not say thank you, and did not say she was right, and said the only thing he had.

**"I came four days of bus travel on a bus to hear a person say one word, and I have got it, and I am going to go back to a hut on a Monday and I am not going to write it down."**

**"Then you are as bad as me."**

**"I am a man who has been asked questions by about nine hundred people and I have never once written down what any of them said, and you are the first of the nine hundred who has noticed."**

---

**What Marek wanted, at about half past seven, and did not ask for, was to be the one who went and found out whether a boy of about nineteen is all right.** He has the shop, the counter, the room, and a pair of hands, and the boy is somewhere in this city and probably at work, and finding out would take him about ninety minutes, and the whole of the room's argument tonight is that nobody should do that.

He did not go. The not going was the fifth of a count he is keeping, and the count is the only thing about this arrangement he has told anybody about, and he told a woman of about twenty-five who washes up one sentence of it in a canteen on a corner about a week ago and did not explain the rest of it and has not explained the rest of it.

**The cost of the fifth was a Monday evening he will not get back, and the thing he was carrying instead of it was this: a man of about thirty-three came on a bus about four days' travel to say one sentence to a stranger, and a woman of about thirty-five said yes, and neither of them thanked anybody, and the yes is the only thing that happened in that room that belongs to either of them.**

**Two figures on that board are each other's age and each other's day, and the pair of them is the sixteen and the ask, and nobody in that room said one word about it.**

The sixteenth line on the hardboard on two nails is six hundred and seventy-two days old, and six hundred and seventy-two is also the day the ask was asked. The ask is five hundred and seventy-two days old, and five hundred and seventy-two is also the day the sixteenth line was written.

**Not one of the two is a day the heating in this city did anything, and the heating is a closed thing in this case and carries no figure of days off for it anywhere, and the man at the bench read both numbers twice and wrote neither of them down, and about four people in that room could have told him what they were and did not.**

The room got to about eleven people over the evening and six of them came in for the temperature and none of the six was asked anything. A woman of about thirty sat in the chair nearest the door for the better part of an hour and nobody asked her a question and nobody thanked her and no one has said sorry to her, and the page that belongs to her is the eighth of the eight in a ring binder on a shelf, and it went unread.

The man of about fifty-one was at the wall with his bag on the floor and had said nothing for four hundred and eighty-eight days, which is sixty-nine weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors was not in this city, and his hold ran on for seventy-three weeks and four days with nothing asked of him and nothing given to him, and he is not the man who came on the bus and neither of the two has ever heard of the other.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
