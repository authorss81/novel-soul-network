# Chapter 513 — A Man Asked To Sign, And The True Reason He Gave Out Loud

**The Thursday was a bench, then a counter, then four warm rooms, and the shape of the day was four flights of stairs in a block of flats where a man was asked to sign a thing for the second time and told the truth about why, and the truth was worth a Friday to him.**

---

The docket carried eleven, the date column carried eleven, and eleven came through the door, the last of them about six in the evening. Where the day went: bench in Lattice Ward to about two, that district's counter to about six, the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate and it is not right and it is not spoken. **The tenement over the road has its heat on and dearer, four names on a lease are bearing the rise, and a fortnight one cook laid out of her own is owed to nobody.** The service-road room is eight hundred and seventy-one days, which is one hundred and twenty-four weeks and three days, four rooms and one behind, warm.

The card in the rail is eight hundred and seventy-five days, four days older than the room, in a rail that holds two. A printed sheet with a box at the foot of it, carried up four flights by a man who is nobody's courier, is a fifth object in this entry and is not that card and is not in that rail.

---

He came to the counter at about eleven in the morning with a question written on the back of his own hand in grease pencil, which is a thing Marek had seen on that hand before and had not seen on any other hand in this city.

The question was four lines long and it was about whether a notice could go up in a landing if nobody had signed the box, and he asked it standing up, the way people ask a question they have rehearsed in a corridor and are ashamed of having rehearsed.

**"You are going to say four seconds."**

**"I am going to say it however long it takes me, and I have got about nine minutes before my next thing."**

**"Say it."**

**"Yes, I can put up a notice with nobody's name on it, and no, I am not the person who decides whether it goes up, and those are two different sentences and I have thought about them in the wrong order for a week."**

**"Then you have not asked me a question. You have told me a fact and asked me to confirm it."**

**"I have asked you whether the fact is true."**

**"It is true and I did not need to be here and you did not need me and the grease pencil is going to come off your hand in about two days and it will be gone and neither of us will know it is gone."**

The man looked at the back of his own hand for about four seconds and then at the grease pencil and then he laughed, once, which Marek has heard about four hundred times from men who are not laughing.

**"Then why am I here."**

**"You are here because you have a person in this city who is good at things and you have been carrying a question to him for a week instead of to the woman who does the hours, and the woman who does the hours is upstairs in your own building, and I have known that since the Monday and I have not said it and I am saying it now because a lamp costs a man nothing and a week costs him a Friday."**

The man did not write that down either, and he went up the stairs at about eleven, and he was back at the counter at about half past two with the true reason in his mouth, and that is the whole of what happened to him in a day.

---

The block is four storeys and the stairs smell of other people's cooking and the light on the second landing is a bulb in a shade with the fuse gone. Four of his nine flats are on that landing and he keeps the key to the meter cupboard, which is a job that nobody has ever described to him in a sentence with a verb in it.

He is about forty-four and he keeps the stair, the bins, the bulbs and the notice board, and a woman of about thirty-one who types and does the hours has been asked twice this month to sign for a Friday.

**"It is the same nine lines,"** she said.

**"It says what the relay is and what the setting is and it applies from the first. You have seen it."**

**"I signed the top of one in the other block and I put it on that board and nothing has happened and nothing has not happened."**

**"That one was a different setting and a different board. This one wants somebody under the box."**

**"Then it wants somebody."**

The man of about thirty-one who had been on the board in the other block came up the stairs at that moment, which is a thing that happens in a building, and stood in the doorway at the second landing and did not come in, and heard the last of it, and went back down. **Nobody in that stairwell has ever told that man he was not the only one, and nobody is going to, and he went back down two flights and carried his own bin out and nobody saw him do it.**

**"I am not signing it."**

He had been asked this in a corridor once already, and the answer he got was four seconds long, and he had written the question on the back of his own hand in grease pencil so that he would not lose it, and then he lost it, and that is the whole of what happened and he has not told anybody that he lost it.

**"I am not signing it, and that is the first sentence out of my mouth about it."**

**"You are being asked by me and not in a corridor."**

**"I know. That is why I am telling you the real reason instead of the one I have been saving."**

---

**"The real reason is that my hours were cut in the spring and they came back on a Friday, and one of the Fridays has me on it, and if I am not on the board with a name under the box then the Friday goes to a man who has not been asked anything and he does not want it, and it will be gone by the first."**

She did not write it down. She had a pen out and she put it back in her coat.

**"You are aware that you have just told me something I have to hold and cannot use."**

**"I am aware that you have just said that to me, and that is the first time anybody has said that to me while I was still standing here."**

**"It is true and it is not a favour and it is not a promise, because I write the hours and I do not decide them and the person who decides them is a man I have met twice and who has never once been in this building."**

**"Write the second column,"** he said.

**"There is no second column on this. There is a signature box and it wants a signature."**

**"Then write on the back. Anything. I do not care what it is. I want there to be a piece of paper in this building that says a man was asked and said a true thing and was listened to, because I have been in this building four years and I have never once seen that, and you are the first person who has stood on this landing and not told me it was fine."**

She stood on the landing with the sheet in her hand for about eleven seconds and did not write on the back of it, and he watched her do the arithmetic that he had watched about four hundred people do, and what she said when she had finished it was this.

**"No."**

**"That is fair."**

**"It is not fair and I am telling you why, and then I am going down two flights and you are going to stand there and be annoyed, and I would rather you be annoyed at the right thing."** She folded the sheet once, which is not what a person does to a document they intend to sign.

**"If I put a true sentence on the back of that, then in about a year somebody will find it in a folder and read it and see a man who asked to be on a board in a stairwell and wanted a witness. And then this building has a man who asked to be on a board, and that is a different building, and I have put it there, and I am not the person who is allowed to decide which building this is."**

**"You are the only person in this building anybody listens to."**

**"For eleven weeks."**

**"That is not the same as being listened to."**

**"No. It is the same as being consulted and it is not the same as being listened to, and the difference is about nine months long, and I have been in this job for a fortnight."**

---

He took the sheet back and did not fold it and did not put it on the board and did not put it in the bin either, and it went into the inside pocket of a coat that has a name on the second lining stitch that he has had since before this city started counting anything, and the sheet is in that pocket now, and the inspection at the end of the month is in about three weeks and is not on anybody's calendar in this building.

**What he named out loud, to her and then to the empty landing, was the cost, and he named it before she left rather than after, and that is the whole of what is different about him.** A Saturday in which he had already promised to do his own mother's shopping. The half hour in which he could have gone and done it. A man of thirty-one on the same landing who would have had the Friday and did not want it and does not know any of this is happening.

**And a name of his own going on a piece of paper in a building where a woman types the hours, and that being the cheapest thing he could buy and the one he did not buy.**

**"You have cost me a Friday."**

He said it to her and then to the landing.

**"I want that written down somewhere. Not on that sheet. Somewhere that is not that sheet."**

**"I will not write it down anywhere, and I am telling you that so that you can stop asking me for it in about a week, and I am sorry, and I am not going to soften the Friday."**

Nobody thanked anybody and the stairwell has never contained a thank you in the four years he has been on it.

---

He came in at about half past six and there were five people in the room for the temperature and one of the four of them was the man of about fifty-one with his bag on the floor, and it is the four hundred and seventy-seventh day of that man's silence, which is sixty-eight weeks and one day.

**What Marek wanted, and did not ask for, was to have been the person who wrote the true reason on the back of that sheet.** He has wanted it since about ten past two in the afternoon, when a man of about forty-four came into the counter and told him the whole of it without being asked, and he stood there and gave three pieces of advice that were all correct and none of which was what the man came for, and the man took all three of them and went up the stairs and the last thing the man said at the counter was that somebody in this city has to be the one who hears it.

**The decision he took on the Thursday cost him a Thursday.** He did not go up to that block and stand on the second landing. He had the address, because the man had written it on a job ticket in his own handwriting in the way people write an address they have never had written down, and the ticket was in his hand, and the afternoon's work could have gone up those four flights and back.

**He did not go because the woman typing the hours had said out loud that a second mouth in a building is how a building gets a second mouth, and because he agreed with her, and because agreeing with her and then going up the stairs anyway would have made the agreement worth nothing and would have cost him nothing that he could name, which is the exact shape of a decision that a man makes in order to be able to tell himself he made a decision.**

He went back to the counter and did about an hour and a half of work he could have done well, and he was slower than usual at it, and nobody could tell why, and he has not told anybody.

The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in the room for about an hour and was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and was not read.

Nobody in that room knew that a man had put a printed sheet into the inside pocket of a coat on a second landing and had named a cost to a woman who types hours and had not written it down, and nobody in that block knew that the man who goes into the man of about fifty-one's room every working day has a name in this city that about four people have been given by somebody else.

A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date was not in this city, and his hold ran on for seventy-two weeks to the day without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
