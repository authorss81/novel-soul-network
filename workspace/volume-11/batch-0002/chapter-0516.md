# Chapter 516 — Nine Words On A Bench With Two Tools On It, And A Man Who Believed Them

**The Wednesday went from a bench to a counter to a warm room, and the shape of it was a front room off a court with a vice on the bench and a door left open on purpose, and a boy of about nineteen was told something true at a door and then was told what it had cost a man in a doorway two winters before.**

---

Eleven on the docket, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and the day was bench in Lattice Ward to about two, that district's counter to about six, and the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. The rate is nine and ten, wrong, left alone, and told to nobody. **Heat in the tenement over the road: on, dearer, four names on a lease carrying the increase, and a fortnight of a cook's own spending still owed to nobody.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and seventy-seven days old, which is one hundred and twenty-five weeks and two days, four rooms and one behind, warm.

The card in the rail along the counter front is eight hundred and eighty-one days, four days beyond the room, in a rail that holds two. A bench in a front room off a court, with a vice at one end and two tools lying on the top of it, is a fifth object in this entry and is not that card and is not in that rail.

---

The front room is nine feet wide and has a bench along one wall and a vice at the end of it and a stool and a kettle on a ring, and it is warm because the ring is on, and the door is open because the room is warm, and she leaves it open on purpose.

She is about thirty-five. She reads meters and she opens locks, and the two jobs are not connected by anybody except her, and she has never said out loud that they are connected and has been asked about four times.

The two tools on the bench are a pick and a flat file, and there is a third thing on the bench which is a mug, and the mug is not a tool, and about nine people have made that mistake.

He came in at about four in the afternoon because he is nineteen and it was his first week and he was told to come and see somebody about a card, and he came to a room in a street of small workshops rather than to a building with a name on it, because somebody had told him that this room is where you go when you have not been asked anything yet.

**"Have I been asked something?"**

**"You have been handed something."**

She did not look up from the bench.

**"Which is different and I will come to it."**

He put a printed card on the bench. It was the size of a postcard and it had a company name at the top and a box at the bottom with a printed heading over it, and he put it down the way a person puts down something they have been holding in a bag for two days without reading it.

**"I have not signed it. My mate signed his on the first and he says it is nothing."**

**"It is not nothing and your mate was not asked."**

**"So what do I do."**

**"You can decline, and the declining is the protection."**

He stood in the doorway with that and did not come further in, and she let him stand there, because a man of about nineteen who has been told something true in a doorway is a different animal from a man of about nineteen who has been told it at a bench.

---

**"Say that again."**

**"You can decline, and the declining is the protection."**

**"That's it? That's the whole thing?"**

**"That is the whole thing and it is nine words and I have been saying it for about four years and about nine hundred people have heard it and about four of them have believed it at the first time."**

**"Why not more."**

**"Because the believing is not the hard part. The hard part is that a no costs somebody something on the same day, and it is almost never the person saying the no, and the person saying the no is nearly always the person who can least afford it."**

**"Me."**

**"You, on Thursday, when the man who has your hours finds out."**

**"He can't do anything. He doesn't even know I'm here."**

**"Then he will find out and then he will do one of three things and I can tell you which three and I have watched all three."**

---

**"One: he asks you again, in a corridor, and this time he has your name against something."**

**"Two?"**

**"He says nothing and puts you on the ones nobody wants, and you will not know it has happened until a Thursday comes and you are not on it."**

**"Three."**

**"He says it was nothing and he was only asking, and he says it kindly, and about a year later you will be in a room where somebody is being asked something and you will say no without a single person having done anything to you, and that is the one that works and it works on me."**

The boy sat down on the stool, which she had not offered him, and put the card face up on the bench, and did not pick it up again for about four minutes.

**"Are you the person who decides things here?"**

**"No."**

**"Are you anybody?"**

**"I am a woman with a bench and two tools and I am about as far from anybody as a person can be who opens locks for a living."**

**"Do you want me to sign it?"**

**"I want you to go back and not sign it and not tell anybody you were told not to. That is the only version of this that is any use, and I am not going to pretend it is a good version."**

**"That sounds like it's nothing."**

**"It is not nothing. It is the only thing anybody can hand you that does not have somebody else's name on it."**

---

She said the rest of it at about five and it took about a minute and it is the part she tells people and the part that makes her difficult to sit in a room with.

**"There is a man I watched decline one in the entrance of a block about a bus day from here, about two winters ago, and he did it politely, in a doorway, with his hat in his hand. He was on the Friday rota and he was on it for a reason, and the reason was that a woman of about sixty had a hospital appointment and somebody had to take her. And he lost the Friday, and the woman lost the lift, and the hospital moved the appointment by nine days, and none of those three things was connected to the doorway in any way anybody could put on a form."**

**"What happened to him."**

**"He is still on the rota. He is still the one who does the Fridays. He has never been asked about it again and he has never said anything about it and I have never said anything to him, and I was standing about eleven feet away and I said nothing, and I have thought about the eleven feet more than I have thought about most things."**

**"Why didn't you say something."**

**"Because he did not ask me, and because a person who declines in a doorway and is then rescued by a stranger is not a person who declined. He is a person who was allowed to, and the allowance is a debt, and in about a year he would have been the man in the room telling somebody that they can say no, and he would have been believed, and it would have been me that put it there."**

The boy did not say anything for a while and the kettle went off boiling and neither of them noticed it, which is a thing that happens in that room most evenings.

**"So what do I actually do with the card."**

**"You put it in your bag and you bring it back on Thursday and you let the man find out that you have had it for a week, and you do not use it as a weapon, and you do not put it on a table, and if he asks you why you kept it you say that you did not understand it and that you wanted somebody to explain it and nobody did."**

**"That is a lie."**

**"It is the least useful lie anybody will tell you this year and it is not a lie about anything that matters."**

**"And if he asks again."**

**"Then you decline again, and that is the second time, and the second time is the one that costs."**

---

He left at about ten past five with the card in his bag and no signature on it and no address, and she did not write anything down, and she has never written anything down in four years, and that is not modesty with her, it is a working position, and she gave it out loud to a man of about thirty-three in a hut in another town about eleven days ago without explaining it.

**What she wanted, and said to nobody, was for one person in this city to be told that a boy of about nineteen left her room on a Wednesday with a card unsigned.** She wanted it said out loud to somebody who could do something with it and there is nobody, and the not doing anything with it is the only thing she does, and she has got very good at it, and on the Wednesday evening she sat on the stool in a warm room off a court with the door open and the ring on and found that being good at it had started to feel like a decision she was making rather than a thing that was happening to her.

---

He came into the room at about half past six and there were five people in it for the temperature and one of them was the man of about fifty-one with his bag on the floor on the four hundred and eighty-third day of his saying nothing, which is sixty-nine weeks to the day.

**What Marek wanted and did not ask for was a front room off a court with a bench and a vice and a door that is left open on purpose.** He has been in about four hundred warm rooms in this city in ten years and every one of them has a reason for the door, and he has worked out over about two years that a door left open on purpose is the only kind of door that is actually an offer, and he has never once walked into one, and he did not walk into this one on the Wednesday, and the not walking was the fourth of a count he is keeping and has told nobody about.

He did the two hours of the evening and the four people who came in for the temperature and did not ask any of them anything, and at about half past nine a woman of about thirty-one asked him whether the room was open at the weekend, and he said it was, and she said that is not what she meant, and he said he understood that it was not, and she said goodnight and went, and that is the whole of that.

Nobody in that room knew that a woman with a pick and a file off a court had told a boy of about nineteen that he was allowed to say no, and had then told him what it had cost a man in a doorway two winters ago, and that the man in the doorway is still on the Friday rota, and that she is the same woman who stood six feet away in a cellar in a town and was told where a plate of iron is and said no to hearing where hers was.

The man of about fifty-one was at the wall and had said nothing for four hundred and eighty-three days. A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date was not in this city, and his hold ran on for seventy-two weeks and six days with nothing asked of him and nothing given to him. The ask is at five hundred and sixty-seven days, which is eighty-one weeks to the day, from the day it was asked.

The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in the room and was not asked a question and was not thanked and nobody said sorry to her, and her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf and was not read on the Wednesday.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The bench, as a record of nine words and what a person is told after them.* **Where: a front room nine feet wide off a court, in this city, on the Wednesday afternoon, with a bench along one wall, a vice at the end of it, a stool, a kettle on a ring that is on because the room is warm, and a door left open on purpose. Who was in it: a woman of about thirty-five who reads meters and opens locks and has never said out loud that the two are connected, and a boy of about nineteen in his first week who had been handed a printed card the size of a postcard with a company name and a signature box and had not signed it. What he said on the way in: that he had not signed it and that his mate had signed his on the first and said it was nothing. What she said about the difference: that he had been handed something rather than asked something. The nine words, said twice at his request: you can decline, and the declining is the protection. *The eleven feet, as a record of a decline that cost a man a Friday and a woman a hospital appointment.* **Where: the entrance of a block about a bus day from here, about two winters ago, in a doorway, with the declarer holding his hat. What he was declining: a shift. What the shift was for: taking a woman of about sixty to a hospital appointment that could not be moved. What it cost: he lost the Friday, she lost the lift, the appointment moved by about nine days, and nothing that happened in that entrance could be entered on any form in this city. Who saw it: the woman with the bench, standing about eleven feet away. What she did: said nothing, and has said nothing to him since, and he has never asked her and has never said anything about it. What he is now: still on the Friday rota, and still the one who does the Fridays, and the rota has not been re-cut in his name and no form in this city has his name on it. What has been said about any of it: nothing, to anybody, including the man at the bench in a warm room about a bus day away, who does not know that she exists.
