# Chapter 511 — A Question Asked At A Weighbridge On A Monday Night, And Ten Words In Answer

**The Monday ran from a bench in the middle of the day to a counter in the early evening and then to a warm room, and the shape of it was a question asked at night in a weighbridge hut in the third of the four towns, and a man who has worked that weighbridge for longer than some of the buildings around it being asked something nobody has ever asked him.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in the date column and eleven came, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, that district's counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six. The rate is nine and ten and is wrong and he did not correct it and told nobody. **The heating in the building above that road is on and dearer than it was before the autumn, four names on one lease are carrying the increase, and a fortnight that one cook laid out of her own money is on nobody's account and is expected in the spring to be on nobody's account still.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and sixty-eight days old, which is one hundred and twenty-four weeks to the day, and is warm, and is four rooms with a back one behind them.

The card in the rail along the counter front is eight hundred and seventy-two days old, four days the more than the room, and the rail holds two and has held two since somebody wedged it there. A sheet of paper with a printed first column and a second column ruled in pencil with nothing printed over the line is a fifth object in this entry and is not that card and is not in that rail.

---

The hut is eight feet by eleven and it has a plate outside it, a lamp on a bracket above the plate, a kettle, a chair with a split in the seat, and a window that faces the back of a mill.

The man in it is about thirty-three. He has been in that hut for eleven years. He writes down what comes over and he does not write down whose it is, and in eleven years nobody has asked him to write down whose it is, and he would not have known what to do if they had.

**"You have got about forty minutes before I have to be at the mill for the second weigh,"** he said.

**"I have got a meter that reads nothing and a torch and a question I have been carrying about eleven days, and the meter can wait."**

She was about thirty-one. She reads meters, she works nights because shops and workshops are shut when she is in them, and she had come in out of the dark with her sleeves pushed up and the inside of her left forearm turned away from him on purpose, which is a thing people do with a thing they have not decided to show.

He noticed it and did not ask about it and that was the correct thing to do and it was also the only reason she said anything.

**"I want to ask you how you get asked things."**

**"By being in a hut."**

**"That is not an answer and you have given it too fast, and I have asked about eleven people that this month and eight of them did that."**

**"Ask me the question you came with."**

**"I am going to have to say three things first and they will not make sense."**

**"Say them."**

She put the torch on the desk with the beam up so that it was lighting the ceiling and not either of them, which is what she does when she is going to say something she has not said.

**"Somebody asks you, and you answer, and what you said counts."**

**"Right."**

**"Or you are put on a list, and you are correct as of the day you went on it, and nothing that happens to you afterwards counts unless you go and take yourself off."**

**"Right."**

**"Or nobody asks you anything at all, and the not asking is the answer, and the not asking is the whole of what there is."**

The kettle had gone off boiling about a minute before and neither of them had noticed, and the hut was the kind of cold that only happens when a person has been in it long enough to stop feeling it.

**"Right,"** he said.

**"Which of those three."**

**"I'm asked. The form says yes. I go at four."**

He did not say anything for a few seconds, and she thought she had got it wrong, and then he said the thing that made her put both hands flat on the desk.

**"That is ten words and it is all three of them and it is not wrong."**

**"It is not a bad answer?"**

**"It is not a good answer. There is no version of this where one of those three is good. You are asked and it counts for about as long as the person asking is in the room. You are on a list and you have never once in eleven years gone and taken yourself off, and that is not a decision, that is a gap. And nobody has left you alone in a way that meant anything, because being left alone is what happens to a man nobody wants an answer from."**

**"Which of those is the one you would change."**

**"None of them. I would change the amount of time I have spent believing the second one was a decision."**

---

She took the folded sheet out of her inside pocket and put it on the desk between them, squared, with the printed column facing up and the pencil-ruled column showing nothing at all.

**"I am not going to tell you where I got it and I am not going to tell you what is on it, and I want you to notice that I have made a decision about that in about a second and a half and that a decision made that fast is usually a bad one."**

**"It is not a bad one,"** he said.

**"It is the correct one and it is not good, and the difference between those is the whole of what I have learned in a hut."**

**"Then say the correct one out loud so that I have heard you say it."**

**"If a piece of paper gets a name on it in a hut at eleven at night, then it stops being a piece of paper and it becomes a job, and a job has a man assigned to it, and the man who is assigned to it will be paid, and the paying is how it stops being a piece of paper for good."**

She looked at the sheet for a while.

**"That is the second reason."**

**"Go on."**

**"No. That is the only reason, and you have been talking about it for a week."**

He did not ask where the sheet came from. That was a thing he could have done in one word and it would have been a free move, and he understood, sitting in a hut at eleven at night with the lamp on a bracket outside, that he was not going to get the chance again and that this was the correct arrangement and not a failure of nerve.

**"You are the second person this month to put a piece of paper on a table in front of me and not tell me what is on it."**

**"I did not know that was a thing people do."**

**"It is not a thing people do. It is a thing two people do, and we have both done it, and neither of us has said so, and that is the only reason I am going to be able to sleep tonight."**

A woman of about twenty-six came to the counter at about one in the afternoon with a lamp that had been converted twice and did not work on either conversion, and she had it in a bag and she had carried it four stops on a bus.

He opened it at the bench, which is not the work, and he did it because the fourth pocket had a screwdriver in it that was the wrong size and the work was going to take nine minutes either way.

**"This is not my lamp."**

**"No."**

**"Then what is it."**

**"It is a lamp that two people have fitted things to and neither of them wrote down what they did, and I have been to two shops and one of them told me it was new and one of them told me it was not worth repairing."**

**"Which of those two was right."**

**"The second one. It is worth repairing. It is also not a lamp any more, and if I take it back to the woman who fitted the first thing then she will fit a third and I will own something I did not choose."**

He fixed it in about forty minutes and told her the true price, which was the price of the forty minutes and not the price of a conversion, and she asked him whether that was the right way round and he said that it was the only way round he had ever found, and she said that the second shop had said something almost the same in nearly the same words, and he said that the second shop was probably right about most things, and she left with the lamp and said thank you, and it was the only thank you of his Monday and it was for a lamp and not for anything else, and he stood at the bench afterwards for about a minute working out that a person who has been told the true price four times in a month stops being a person who has been refused and starts being a person who has been answered, and that the difference between those two is about eleven minutes and a lamp.

---

She put the sheet back in her pocket and the pocket was an inside one, and the inside one is the reason the paper is in the third of the four towns instead of on a bench in Lattice Ward, and neither of them said so and Marek Senn, who owns the bench it left three weeks ago and does not know who took it, was not told and did not ask and will not ask.

---

He came in at about half past six and there were four people in the room for the temperature and one of them was the man of about fifty-one, who put his bag on the floor at his feet and said nothing, and it is the four hundred and seventy-fourth day of his saying nothing and he chose the floor again on the Monday without being asked.

**What Marek wanted, at about seven, and said to nobody, was to be told which of the three he was in.** He has a bench in the middle of the day, a counter in the middle of the evening and a warm room at the end of it, and there is a woman of twenty-four with eleven weeks and a room with a green stair who cannot be removed because of him, and the arrangement he is inside of has an answer to that question and he is not the person it is for, and he has not asked, and he is not going to.

He wanted to be asked which of the three he was in, and the honest answer is that he is in the first one about four days a week and in the second one about three and that on the other three he is in a version of the third one that nobody designed and that he has been standing in for about two years without knowing it had a name.

**The thing he decided on the Monday, and it cost him, was not to ring the number on the back of a job ticket in a plastic sleeve under a wall list.**

The sleeve has four words on it and the four words are the address of an office with a green stair, and a woman of about twenty-five who washes up has been to a canteen on a corner and been told about it, and a woman of about twenty-two came to a warm room on a Sunday and said that somebody had given her his name.

**He did not ring it because a person who has been handed his name by somebody she cannot name has been handed a piece of information and not a service, and if he rang it he would find out who said his name and why, and he would then owe that person a conversation, and the conversation would be about him.**

The cost is that the woman comes back on a Friday, which is four days away, and he will not be able to tell her anything, and he will have to say that to her face in a warm room.

**He would not have made this decision two winters ago, because two winters ago the cost of doing the right thing was always somebody else's, and this one is his, and it is four days of carrying a thing he cannot fix.**

The room got to nine people over the evening and four of them came in for the temperature and none of the four was asked anything. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in it for about an hour and was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and was not read on the Monday.

**A woman of about thirty-five with two tools on a bench had not been in that room in her life and was not going to be, and did not know that a room existed where a person gets asked things, and would find out in about a week from a man of about thirty-three who has not yet decided to tell her.** The man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date was not in this city, and his hold ran on for seventy-one weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him, and he is a different man of about thirty-three from the one in the hut and neither of the two has ever heard of the other.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The weighbridge hut, as a record of a question and an answer that was not a good one.* **Where: the third of the four towns, a hut eight feet by eleven beside a weighbridge plate, at about eleven at night on the Monday, with a kettle, a lamp on a bracket over the plate and a chair with a split in the seat. Who was in it: a man of about thirty-three who has been in that hut for eleven years and writes down what comes over without whose it is, and a woman of about thirty-one who reads meters and works nights, who came with a meter that reads nothing, a torch laid on the desk with the beam up at the ceiling, and a question she had been carrying about eleven days. The question as she put it: how a person gets asked things, which needed three sentences first. The three sentences, in her mouth and in this order: that somebody asks you and you answer and what you said counts; or that you are put on a list and you are correct as of the day you went on it and nothing afterwards counts unless you take yourself off; or that nobody asks you anything at all and the not asking is the answer and is the whole of what there is. The answer she was given, in ten words: I'm asked. The form says yes. I go at four. What he then said about it: that ten words is all three of them and that it is not wrong; that it is not good, because there is no version of any of the three that is good; that the first counts for about as long as the person asking is in the room; that the second had never once been taken back in eleven years, which is a gap and not a decision; and that the third is what happens to a man nobody wants an answer from. What he was asked he would change, and his answer: none of them, and the amount of time he had spent believing the second one was a decision. What was put on the desk: a folded sheet with a printed first column and a second ruled in pencil carrying nothing, taken out of an inside pocket and put back into it. What was said about it: that she was not going to say where it came from or what was on it, and his account of why that was correct and not good, being that a piece of paper with a name on it in a hut at eleven at night becomes a job. What he did not do: ask her where it came from. What she noticed about that: that it was the second time this month a piece of paper had been put in front of him and not explained, and that neither of them has said that it was the second time, and that this was the only reason she would sleep. Owner: ten words, a kettle, and no address.**
