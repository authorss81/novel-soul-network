# Chapter 517 — A Corridor, And The Same Question Asked Twice And Made Smaller Both Times

**The Thursday was a bench, a counter and a warm room, and the shape of the day was a first-floor corridor in which a question was made smaller four times before lunch and then made smaller a fifth time, to a man's face, at five, and the fifth one cost him.**

---

Eleven on the docket, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward to about two, that district's counter to about six, and the four rooms off a service road from about half past six. Charges: nine and ten, uncorrected, unannounced. **The heating in the flat above the road is on and dearer, four names on one lease are carrying it, and a fortnight that one cook laid out is still on nobody's account.** The service-road room: eight hundred and seventy-eight days old, which is one hundred and twenty-five weeks and three days, four rooms and one behind, warm.

The card in the rail: eight hundred and eighty-two days, four days older than the room, in a rail that holds two. A steel door with a small glass panel and a list of rooms behind it, none of which is on the list of the door, is a fifth object in this entry and is not that card and is not in that rail.

---

The corridor is on a first floor and it runs the length of the building and it has a window at each end and eleven chairs against one wall, of which four are occupied, and a counter at the far end with a man behind it who is not the man people come for.

The man people come for is about fifty-eight and he wears a coat that has been brushed that morning and he speaks at about the level of somebody who is not asking you to change what you are doing, and he is the head of an office that holds records, and that is the only description of him anybody in that corridor has ever given anybody.

Marek was in that corridor for about nine minutes at about four in the afternoon and he was not on an appointment and he did not have a card and he went there because a man of about thirty-eight had said a name in a stairwell and the name had a door on it.

**"Is there a record of who put a notice up on a board in a block of flats in this city."**

**"Which block."**

**"One in the fourth district, four storeys, nine flats, a second landing with a bulb out."**

**"Which notice."**

**"A printed one, nine lines, a company name at the top and a box with a printed heading at the foot, and it says it applies from the first."**

**"Who is asking."**

**"I am asking."**

**"Then you are the fourth person today and I have answered none of you, and you may draw whatever comfort there is in that."**

---

**"I have not refused you."**

Iven Sore said it without turning round, and he was not talking to a wall.

**"I have made the question smaller four times and each of the four was smaller than the one before it, and you will notice that I have not once said the word no and that nobody in this corridor can say I have."**

**"You have made it so small that it is about nothing."**

**"It is about a block, a landing and a sheet of paper, and those three are the whole of it, and you brought me the address of a building and the description of a document and no reason, and the only reason a person gives is a person."**

**"I have a reason."**

**"Then you had it when you came and you have not given it, and the reason is the part I would have had to do something with, and I do not do anything with the part of a question that is not a question."**

Marek stood in that corridor for another two minutes and understood that he had been made to say the address of a building out loud in a place where four people were sitting, and that he had said it, and that the man in the immaculate coat had not asked him anything and had taken a building.

**"You did that on purpose."**

**"I did it because you would have told me about the block and about the notice and about the man, and the three of them together are a person, and I do not know what I would do with a person in a corridor on a Thursday."**

He turned round.

**"I know exactly what I would do with a block. I would look at it. That is the whole of the difference between us and it is why you are the one who is frightened and I am not, and I would rather you noticed that than had it explained to you."**

---

The man of about thirty-eight came up that corridor at about five and he had been in it before, on the Monday of the week before, and he was holding a printed sheet in an inside pocket and he had a key to a meter cupboard in his other hand and he knew the building.

**"You are the one whose name is on a door."**

**"I have a name on a door. That is not the same as being the one whose name is on a door, and the difference is the whole of my job, and you have just used it wrongly in front of a corridor."**

**"Then tell me who signed the top of the sheet that is in my pocket."**

**"Have you signed anything."**

The man of about thirty-eight did not say anything for about four seconds, and in those four seconds he understood, and Marek watched him understand it, and it was the first thing in that corridor that was given away for free.

**"That is a smaller question."**

**"It is a smaller question, and you have noticed, and I am going to tell you why I am allowed to do it and I am going to do it once and I am not going to do it kindly."**

Iven Sore put his hands behind his back.

**"Somebody asked me the identical question at about four o'clock and I made it smaller four times and he walked away. You are the same question, in a worse mood, and I am tired, and the tired is my own business and not yours."**

**"I am not here for you to be tired at."**

**"No. You are here because you think there is a name attached to a sheet and you want it removed, or you want it confirmed, and both of those are the same request, and neither of them is one I can serve."**

**"Why not."**

**"Because I do not know what the name is for. That is not modesty. If the name is on the sheet because a man signed the top of a sheet he was asked to sign, then the name is a record of a man doing his job and removing it makes this city worse. If the name is on the sheet because a man was entered onto something he did not know about, then the name is the only evidence that exists and removing it is worse still. I do not know which of those two is true, and until I do, the sheet stays as it is, and I would rather be wrong in one direction than the other."**

**"That is the most honest thing anybody has said to me in a month."**

**"It is a thing I said and I will thank you not to repeat it as though it were a favour, because I did not say it for you."**

---

**The cost of the second answer was not his and he knew the exact moment he spent it, and he did not try to give it back.** The man of about thirty-eight walked out of that corridor at about ten past five with the same sheet in the same pocket, and he had learned one thing, and the one thing was that his building is inside a system with an index in it, and that from today there is a record that a man from that block asked a question in this corridor, and an inspection at the end of the month in about three weeks, and a name on a board that is not on a board, are now three things and not two.

**He named that out loud, to Marek, on the stairs, and he named it as a cost to himself and not as a complaint, which is a thing he has been practising for about eleven years and has not yet got good at.** About a hundred and forty pounds of a person's Friday. The name of a woman of about sixty who was going to a hospital. And now an index.

Marek heard all three and said nothing, and the nothing was not politeness, and the rota man looked at him for a second and then said the thing that decided the rest of his evening.

**"You are the one who goes to the green stair."**

**"No."**

**"They said a woman there could say whether a thing was fine."**

**"She can say whether it is inside her eleven weeks. She cannot say whether your relay is safe, and I have told you that in a warm room and I am telling you it again here for nothing."**

**"Will you tell her I asked you."**

**"No. And that is not a favour either, and you should not want it to be. She cannot speak for herself through me and I cannot speak for her through anybody, including you, and if I carried your question up those stairs it would be the four words of somebody else's mouth in a room where the limits of an office are read out loud, and the whole of the point of those limits is that nobody does that."**

The rota man went down the stairs and the stairwell door closed and Marek stood in the corridor for about four minutes and then went back down to the counter and did about an hour and a half of work.

---

**What he wanted, in that corridor and afterwards, was for the man of about thirty-eight to have been given a straight no in about four seconds by somebody who was allowed to give one.** He has watched about nine people try to say a straight no to a man in a system and turn it into a question instead, and he wanted to be in a room with somebody competent enough to do the other thing, and the only man in that corridor was competent and chose not to, and the choice was defensible and Marek did not think it was right, and he is not going to say so to anybody, and not saying it is a decision he made on the stairs and it is the fourth of those in about a fortnight.

The room got to about nine people over the evening and four of them came in for the temperature and none of the four was asked anything. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in it and was not asked a question and was not thanked and nobody said sorry to her, and her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf and was not read on the Thursday.

The man of about fifty-one was at the wall with his bag on the floor and had said nothing for four hundred and eighty-four days, which is sixty-nine weeks and one day. A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors was not in this city, and his hold ran on for seventy-three weeks to the day with nothing asked of him and nothing given to him.

Nobody in that corridor knew that a man of about thirty-five with a bench and a vice and two tools on it had told a boy of about nineteen in a front room off a court that the second time is the one that costs, and nobody in that building knew that the man of about thirty-eight had come out of a stairwell on the Monday having named the cost of a decline he had watched, and the two of those men have never been in the same room and there is no arrangement of them.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
