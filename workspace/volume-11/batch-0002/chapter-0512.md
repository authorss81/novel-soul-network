# Chapter 512 — A Wednesday In A Room Above A Line, And A Book That Stayed Shut

**A Wednesday, and the shape of it was a morning in a room above a line in another district where nine ordinary things were said to a room, and one woman who has opened the same morning for nineteen years did not open anything, and nobody in that room noticed that she had been asked.**

---

Eleven on the docket, eleven in the date column, eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. The hours ran a bench in Lattice Ward to about two, that district's counter on to about six, and then the four rooms behind a service road from about half past six. The rate stands at nine and ten, and it is wrong, and it went nowhere near anybody's mouth. **Heat in the flat over that road is on and costlier than in the autumn, four names on one lease are up with it, and a fortnight a cook spent out of her own pocket is on nobody's books.** The room behind the service road stands at eight hundred and seventy days, which is one hundred and twenty-four weeks and two days, four rooms and a back one, warm.

In the rail across the counter front there is a card at eight hundred and seventy-four days, four days past the room, in a rail that takes two. On a table in Saltmarket there is a book of four columns inside a woman's reach, and it is a different object from that card and no sentence in this entry joins them.

---

The room is above a line and up a stair with the shutters on the landing rather than on the door, and it opens at about ten and closes at about eleven, and it has done that on a Wednesday for nineteen years without a break that anybody has ever mentioned.

Nine chairs go into a curve with the two ends turned in. There is one chair set hard against the wall with its back to the whole room, and it did not move in about an hour, and nobody looked at it more than once, and whoever moves it is not named here and is not going to be.

There is a table, and a tin on the table with no lock on it and never one, and the book. The book was face up inside her right hand's reach from about ten until about eleven and it was shut for the whole of that hour, which is not unusual and which nobody in the room has ever remarked on, because remarking on it would be a question and she does not answer questions that are about the table.

About eleven people came up the stair. Six of them had sat in the same chair on the last Wednesday and five had not. One of the eleven had not been in that room before and was not asked which town he came from and did not say.

---

Nine items, four minutes apiece, and the whole of the morning is the kind of thing that has been said in that room every four weeks since a year nobody in it can now name.

**The reason the book did not open is that not one of the nine said a thing about themselves to the woman who holds the room.** Nine people said nine true things to a room, at a table, out loud, in front of other people, and every one of them was addressed to the chairs and not to her.

That is not a rule. She has never written it down, it is not in the four columns, and there is nothing on that landing that says a book opens on one kind of morning and not another. It is also not evidence of anything. It is what happened, on this Wednesday, and it will be a different morning in four weeks, and nobody in that room is going to be told which it will be in advance.

**She has said twice in nineteen years what it would sound like if she had to say it, and she did not have to say it this morning, and that is the whole of the reason.** She said it the first time in about the fourth year and the second time in about the eleventh, and on both of those mornings the book stayed shut as well, and on both of those mornings the person she said it to was the same person who is in the chair nearest the door this week.

Nobody asked her about it. Nobody asked her why the book was shut, and nobody asked her what she would have needed, and nobody asked her the thing that a room of eleven people is one question away from asking, which is whether the woman who holds the room is all right, and which has not been asked in that room in nineteen years and is not going to be asked on this Wednesday either.

Nobody said anything to her about the count. The count is announced at a sitting and on no other day, and it came out at forty-two, of which thirty-seven correspond, and thirty-seven of the forty-two correspond to the thirty-seven Wednesdays this book holds and five of them predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody.

**No number was given into the tin, and the tin stands at seventy-three, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it has not moved.** The book stands at fifty-eight lines, which is where it stood four weeks ago, and which is where it stood a year ago, and which is a number and the tin is a number and the count is a number and none of the three turns into either of the other two, and the difference between the first two is not a number and was not printed as one this morning and is not printed as one here.

---

The man who had not been in the room before was about thirty and he was a printer's runner, and his item was about a door on a stair.

**"There is a door on a stair in a building I use that has been opening out onto the fire escape instead of into the building, and the landlord's man has been told twice and has been to it once."**

That went to the room. She wrote nothing. A woman of about thirty-eight said that a door like that is a landlord's question and not a room's, and the man said he knew that, and it was still nine seconds that he had wanted to put somewhere, and the room was the somewhere, and he was not wrong about that and nothing happened.

The fifth item was a woman of about forty-four who has come to that room nine times, and she said a thing about a form with two columns on it, and said it to the room, and the woman of about thirty-eight said that a second column is a question and that a question is not a complaint, and the woman of about forty-four said that back to her in one sentence, and then the woman of about thirty-eight said the second sentence, and the two of them were both correct and the item took the full four minutes and nothing was written down.

**Nobody in that room asked the woman of about thirty-eight about the company, and nobody asked her where the form came from, and nobody has ever asked her, and she has never offered.**

**The ninth item was the one that came closest, and it came closest because the man who brought it did not know which of the nine it was.**

He was about fifty-five and he drove and he had a form in a plastic wallet behind the sun visor and he had driven here instead of to a shop.

**"I signed something in the spring and I have thought about it every working day since and I cannot get anybody to tell me what the second column was for."**

Nobody in the room said a word for about four seconds.

**"That is a question for a person and not a room,"** the woman of about thirty-eight said.

**"I know it is. I have driven the length of this city twice to find a person."**

**"And you have not been told whether the second column is a question or a permission."**

**"I have not."**

**"Then you have a thing that is worse than a bad column, and the thing is that you do not know which, and I am not going to guess for you in front of nine people, because if I guess wrong you will do the wrong thing for the rest of the year."**

He said that he understood, and he did not understand, and everybody in that room could tell the difference between the two and nobody said which was which. He took the wallet out of the door pocket and put it back in the door pocket and the four minutes went, and the book stayed shut.

**What went in the book on this Wednesday was nothing, and what did not go in it was not a refusal of anything.** Nobody in that room decided that the man of about fifty-five had been refused. He asked a question in the wrong shape and the room is not a shape, and a room that had tried to be a shape would have been a different room and would not have been nineteen years old.

---

He was at the bench until about two and at that counter until about six, and about four people came in the half hour between about two and about two and a half and about four of them were told no to something.

The first of those four was a woman of about thirty-three who wanted a part by Friday and who had brought the part number written on the back of a receipt, and he looked at the number for about nine seconds and then said no, and she said that she had been told there was a person in this city who could find anything, and he said that there was and that he was not going to be the one who found it for her on a Wednesday when the same part was already on a bench in a town she had never heard of.

**"Then what are you for,"** she said, and it was not unkind, and he answered it in one sentence and the sentence was not clever.

**"I am for the things in this district that are here, and you have come to the wrong counter for the thing you actually want, and the wrong counter is not a refusal of you."**

**A bus ride from that counter a book on a table stood shut for an hour, and at that counter the shutter came down at about ten, and neither person has ever heard of the other one, and there is a bench in Lattice Ward and a table in Saltmarket and the two went quiet at about the same hour and neither of them was told about the other.**

He went down to the rooms at about half past six and there were four people in there for the temperature and one of them said the word warm to the room and not to him.

**He wanted, at about seven, to be in the room in Saltmarket, and he has not been in it and is not on anything that would put him in it, and there is no version of the office with a green stair that sends a man of twenty-two to a Wednesday, and he did not ask, and the not asking is the third time in about a month and he is keeping count of them in a way he has not told anybody about.**

The man of about fifty-one was at the wall with his bag on the floor and had said nothing for four hundred and seventy-six days, which is sixty-eight weeks to the day. A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors was not in this city, and his hold stood at five hundred and three days, which is seventy-one weeks and six days, and nothing was asked of him and nothing was given to him.

The ask itself is at five hundred and sixty days, which is eighty weeks to the day, counted from the day it was asked and never from a sitting since. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in the room for about an hour, and the things that did not happen to her were a question, a thank you and an apology, and her page is the eighth of the eight in a ring binder on a shelf and it did not get read on the Wednesday.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

What was asked of her: nothing. What was said to her: nothing. What was said about her in front of anybody: nothing. What she is owed by anybody in that room: nothing, and she is owed nothing by anybody outside it, and there is no office in this city that has a form on which a person in a chair can be recorded as having been in a room. Whether anybody has put it to her: nobody has, in the whole of the time she has been in that room.
