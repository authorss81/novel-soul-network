# Chapter 514 — Nine Words About A Switch, And A Woman With A Column On Her Arm

**A Friday went from a bench to a counter to a warm room, and the shape of it was a cupboard under a staircase at ten at night where a man who installs things explained a switch in nine words, and a woman with a column of numbers on her own arm went out into a street knowing one of them was not hers.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in the date column and eleven attended over the day, the last of them about six in the evening. His Friday was a bench in Lattice Ward to about two, that district's counter to about six, and the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. The rate is nine and ten, uncorrected, and known to nobody but him. **The heating in the building above the road is on, dearer, carried by four names on one lease, and a fortnight that one cook laid out is still owed to nobody.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and seventy-two days old, which is one hundred and twenty-four weeks and four days, and is warm, and is four rooms and a back one.

The card in the rail along the counter front is eight hundred and seventy-six days, four days beyond the room, and the rail takes two. A grey box the size of a paperback screwed over a meter in a cupboard is a fifth object in this entry, and it is not that card and is not in that rail.

---

The cupboard is under the stairs of a building with four workshops in it and a lock on the door that is a padlock with a number on it and a number written in grease pencil over the number, which is a different system and has been there longer.

The man is about thirty-six and he installs. He has been doing it for six years and he is not a salesman and he is not a manager and he has never in his life been asked to sell anybody anything, and he came at about ten at night because the workshops are shut at ten at night.

He was about eleven minutes with the cover off before he said the thing, and he said it without putting the cover back on and without looking at her, and she has thought since that he said it to the meter.

**"Nothing happens unless you turn it off."**

**"Say that again slower."**

**"Nothing happens unless you go and turn it off."**

He put the cover back.

**"The meter reads the same either way. The meter has read the same for about eighty years. What is new is that the box sends. It sends on its own schedule and it sends to us and it sends whether you are in the building or not, and it sends as a thing that is installed, and installed is the default, and nobody chose it and everybody is in it."**

**"Say the first part again."**

**"Nothing happens unless you go and turn it off. That is nine words and I have said them to about four hundred people and about four of them have heard them."**

**"That is nine words and the first one you said was seven."**

**"You have been listening. I said seven the first time and nine the second and the second is the true one, because the first one had no going in it and the going is the whole of what I have to say to you about that box."**

---

She has forty-four figures on the inside of her left forearm in biro, running from the wrist to just below the elbow, in one column, with no headings and no dates against any of them.

**"Read me the thirtieth,"** she said.

**"I cannot read your arm."**

**"Four, four, nine, one."**

**"That is a number."**

**"That is a number that is on your arm and not on mine, and I wrote it, and I know what I wrote and I know when I wrote it, and I want you to tell me whether your box would have known it before I did."**

**"Yes."**

**"That is one word."**

**"That is one word and it is the correct one, and I am not going to dress it up, and I would rather you had it from me than work it out on your own in about six weeks and be angry at the wrong person."**

**"The thirty-fourth is the same number."**

**"I know it is. I looked at it in your file before I came. That is not allowed and I looked at it."**

**"Why did you look at it."**

**"Because the installer who put it in left a note about a meter that was doing nothing and I wanted to see if the meter was doing nothing now."**

**"And it is not doing nothing."**

**"It is doing what it did before the box went in. The box does not change what the meter does. The box changes who hears it."**

---

That was the whole of what he said about it and he said it in about nine minutes and then he asked her whether the padlock number and the grease pencil number matched, because they do not, and the grease pencil one is a man's handwriting from about two years ago, and he wrote the padlock number on the inside of the door in his own handwriting and did not put a number on the wall, and he did not take the old number off, and he said that taking it off would have been a bigger thing than the box.

**"I am not going to write anything on a wall in a building I have been in for eleven minutes."**

**"If somebody has a piece of paper from me they will have it in their hand, and if they lose it they will lose it, and I would rather that than a number on a wall that says a company was here."**

**"You have just put a new number on a padlock that is inside a door and not on a wall."**

**"Yes, and I have not written my name on it, and the number on a padlock is a lock and the number on a wall is a claim, and there is a difference between the two and almost nobody knows it."**

**"I know it."**

**"Then you are one of about four."**

He left at about ten past ten. He did not ask her to sign anything, he did not leave a card, and there was no second column on anything he touched, and the two of them stood in that cupboard afterwards for about six minutes without saying anything, and then she went up the stairs and out into a street where about four of the workshops have their own lights off because their owners are asleep.

---

**What she now knew, and where she put it: nowhere.** That is the sentence and it is the whole of the Friday, and it is the reason she has not taken the column off her arm and is not going to for a while.

She knows that the thirty-fourth figure on her own body was not a reading she took. She knows that the number was already somewhere before she wrote it down. She knows that the man who installed the box was accurate about all of it and that being accurate was not a favour and cost him about nine minutes of unpaid honesty and the ability to say he had followed the script.

She has a column and no place to put a correction, because a correction is a second column, and the second column is a printed box on nine tables in this city, and she is not going to fill one in for a meter. She has a forearm. She has a man at a counter in another district who was honest with her a month ago and told her that the only way to find out who read what was for the next reader to be somebody who had never met the man who read it on the first, and she did that, and it worked, and it has produced a fact she cannot put anywhere.

**She named the cost out loud, in a cupboard, to a man who was leaving, and it was the cost of about six weeks of being sure and it was hers and she said it exactly once and did not say it twice.** A fortnight's pay on one week where she had done two nights instead of one because she could not tell whether the figure was hers. And a column of forty-four numbers on her own arm, of which she now believes one is not hers, and which she is going to leave on her arm, and which she cannot explain to the next person who sees it.

---

He came into the room at about half past six and there were four people in it for the temperature and one of them said the word warm to the room and not to him.

**What he wanted, at about seven, and did not say, was to be the person she could put it in front of.** She has not been to this room. He has not seen her since the counter. There is a woman of twenty-four on a second floor with a green stair and eleven weeks, and the limits on that office are written on a sheet of paper and read out loud in a room with nine chairs in it, and one of the limits is that it does not cover anybody's own care, and a meter reading in a cupboard in another district is not a technique and is not anybody's own care and is not two people arguing about money, and it is in the eleven weeks, and he is not the person who takes it there, and he cannot ask to be.

**The decision he made on the Friday evening cost him an evening.** A woman of about thirty-one with a column on her arm came to that counter a month ago and he did not write her reading down, and that was correct, and the cost of it has now arrived in a different building from a different man, and the only thing he could have done to stop it was to have written it down, and the reason he did not is the reason he is not the person she should put it in front of.

**He did not go to the second floor on the Friday, and the not going was the third time in about a fortnight, and he is keeping a count of them in his head, and he has told nobody, and the count is the only thing about this arrangement he is proud of.**

**Two figures on that board are each other's age and each other's day, and the pair of them is the seventeen and the eighteen, and the man at the bench has worked that out and has not written it anywhere.**

The seventeenth line on the hardboard on two nails is six hundred and forty-four days old, and six hundred and forty-four is also the day the eighteenth line was written. The eighteenth line is five hundred and ninety days old, and five hundred and ninety is also the day the seventeenth line was written.

**Not one of the two is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating is a closed thing in this case, with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it, and the two numbers are each other's ages and each other's birthdays, and the man at the bench looked at the pair of them for a long time and wrote neither of them down.**

The man of about fifty-one was at the wall on the Friday with his bag on the floor and had said nothing for four hundred and seventy-eight days, which is sixty-eight weeks and two days. A man of about thirty-three keeps four buildings and about nine hundred doors on the other side of the road, and there is no stamp on any of it and no reference and no date, and he was not in this city, and on the Friday his hold stood at seventy-two weeks and one day, and nobody asked him anything and he was given nothing.

The ask is at five hundred and sixty-two days, which is eighty weeks and two days, counted from the day it was asked. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in the room and was asked nothing and was thanked by nobody and has not had sorry said to her, and the sheet of hers on that shelf is the eighth of the eight inside a ring binder, and on the Friday it was not opened.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The meter cupboard, as a record of a default explained by a man who was not selling anything.* **Where: under the stairs of a building with four workshops in it, at about ten at night on the Friday, with a padlock on the door carrying a stamped number and a grease-pencil number written over it in a man's handwriting from about two years ago.
