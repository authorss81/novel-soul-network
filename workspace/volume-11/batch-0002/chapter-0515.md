# Chapter 515 — A Number Said Out Loud In A Yard, And The Number Being Right

**The Monday was a bench in the middle, a counter in the evening and a warm room at the end of it, and the shape of the day was a loading yard where two men who had never met agreed with each other about a number and then put the card back in a pocket, and the number was right.**

---

The list for the day held eleven, the date column held eleven, and eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening; the hours were a bench in Lattice Ward to about two, a counter in that district until ten past six, and the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, it is wrong, and it is not said. **The flat above the road is on, dearer, four names on one lease up with the increase, and a fortnight of a cook's own money owed to nobody.** The service-road room is eight hundred and seventy-five days old, which is one hundred and twenty-five weeks to the day, four rooms and one behind, warm.

The card in its rail is eight hundred and seventy-nine days, four days past the room, and a rail of which two is the whole count. A stiff trade card with a figure in pencil and a line ruled in pencil beneath it is a fifth object in this entry and is not that card and has never been near it.

---

The yard is behind a row of trade units and it holds about nine vehicles at a time and about four pallets nobody has moved since the spring. It is not in this city and it is not in a town, and the person who owns the ground does not know what happens in it on any day.

The crate is on a trolley against the wall. It is about four feet long and it has a manufacturer's board on it with a model number and a quantity and nothing else, and the quantity on the board does not agree with what is in the crate, and both men agree about that within about a minute of meeting each other.

He is about thirty-eight and he has driven for a firm for nine years and he is not a manager and has never once been asked to decide a price, and he came to the yard in his own van on his own hour, and neither of those two facts is going to be said out loud in that yard by anybody, least of all by him.

**"The card went in my inside pocket on the Friday at the crate price."**

**"And it came back in the same pocket this morning with the same number on it and nothing else on it at all, not a line, not a note, not a name."**

**"Who has it now."**

**"A man in a first-floor office over a trade unit on the other side of this yard, and I have not been in that office and I am not going to be."**

**"Then he has read it."**

**"He has had it for three days and it has come back exactly as it went, and I want to say something about that before I say the number."**

---

He said the thing about the number first and he said it carefully and in the order he had prepared it, and he had prepared it in a van.

**"The crate price on the card is what you get when the goods go out on a Friday and the yard is closed and the driver is told to sign for the wait. The wait is not a price of the goods. The wait is about four an hour and it is real and it is somebody's Friday, and it is not in the price of a box of what is in the box."**

**"Say the number."**

**"Nine and four a unit is what is in that crate. Nineteen and six is the number on the card. The difference is the wait and the difference belongs to a person and not to a box, and if you want it in the price then put it in the price and write whose it is, and if you do not want it in the price then take it off, and either one of those is fine and what is not fine is the third thing, which is having it in there quietly."**

**"That is the right number."** It was the man from the trade unit and he said it immediately and did not argue and did not look at the board on the crate.

**"I know it is the right number. That is the part I have been not saying for three days."**

---

**He named the cost out loud, and it was his own, and it was the largest thing he had said in the scene.** Eleven weeks of a Friday wait that his own firm had charged and had not paid, which came to about a hundred and forty pounds over a quarter and which nobody had ever said out loud to him and which he had worked out on the fourth of the month with a pen and a piece of cardboard.

**"And I am not owed it by anybody as far as I can tell, and I have spent three days trying to work out who I could say that to, and the answer is that there is a man in an office on the other side of this yard who has a system and that the system has no column for it, and that I am not going to be a man who has gone round a system."**

**"You want the wait on the card."**

**"I do not. I have never wanted the wait on the card. I want the wait to be a person's time, and I have been driving for nine years and I know exactly what a Friday wait is worth and I know exactly that nobody in this yard has ever been asked to say it out loud, and I have said it out loud, and that is the whole of what I have done today."**

The man from the trade unit took the card out of his own pocket, which had also been in an inside pocket, and looked at the back of it where there was nothing, and then said the thing that turned the morning into what it was.

**"I can agree with you and I cannot change it. That is not a company thing and it is not a refusal. The price is issued from an office in another city and it is issued on a day and it comes back the same, and if I put a different number on this card it does not go anywhere, it just becomes a card in a yard that is wrong in a different way."**

**"Then say the number is right and leave the card."**

The quantity on the board was the thing that nobody had argued about, and it turned out to be the only part of the morning that anybody had been right about before anybody arrived.

Marek counted them off the trolley in about four minutes because he was the only person in that yard who had a reason to, and the crate holds eleven and the board says four, and the difference is a shelf at the back of a unit on the other side of the yard that somebody had put them in about a week ago for a job that has not been invoiced.

**"Then the crate is right and the board is wrong,"** the man of about thirty-eight said.

**"Then the crate is right and the board is wrong and neither of those is anybody's fault, and I am going to say the thing that is the actual difficulty and then I am going to go and load my van."**

**"Say it."**

**"The eleven are correct and the four is correct and the arithmetic between them is not anybody's job. In nine years I have never once been asked to be the man who is the arithmetic between two correct numbers, and if I had been asked, I would have said the number out loud and gone back to the van, and the shelf would still be in the unit and the board would still say four."**

**"That is the second time today."**

**"It is."**

**"It is going to be the last time today, and it is going to be the last time in a yard, and I would like it written down that I said it twice and was right twice, because nobody is going to know that but me."**

**"It is not written down."**

**"No. It is not."**

The seven went back onto the shelf in the unit on the other side of the yard at about half past eleven, and what was left of the crate went onto a trolley against a wall in about the same time, and the man of thirty-eight said that was correct and went to count something else.

He came back across the yard at about half past twelve, which he did not have to do, and stood at the trolley and said the thing he had clearly been holding since the Friday.

**"You are the one everybody says to take it to."**

**"I am."**

**"Then take it to him for me. Not as a favour to me. As the job. Tell him a man drove here and said a number and a card came back clean."**

**"I can't."**

**"You can. You have done it for about nine hundred people."**

**"I have, and I am not going to do it for you, and the reason is not that I do not believe you. There is a woman on a second floor with a green stair and eleven weeks, and the limits of what she covers are read out loud in a room with about nine chairs in it, and one of the limits is that nobody speaks for her, and I am on the wrong side of that limit in a way I have been for about a fortnight."**

**"Then who do I take it to."**

**"Nobody, today. You have said the number out loud and a man has agreed with you, and that is a complete afternoon, and I am not going to be the one who teaches you that the second half is the part that matters."**

The man of thirty-eight looked at the trolley for a while and then said that he had not expected to be told no by a man holding four parts he had counted himself out of eleven, and Marek said that this was a reasonable expectation and a bad one, and the man laughed and went, and the card went into the inside pocket for the second time in three days with a clean back on it.

**"The number is right. I am leaving the card."**

He said the number was right, and he left the card, and nobody wrote anything on the back of it, and the crate is on a trolley against a wall behind a row of trade units and it is the same crate on the same trolley, and the man of about thirty-eight went back to his van and started it and then sat in it for about four minutes with the engine running.

---

Marek had been in the yard for about two hours of the middle of the day because a unit on the far side had bought four of a part he makes and could not account for two of them, and the two turned up on a trolley in a yard behind a row of trade units on the same bill.

**He could have done the whole of it in about nine seconds and he did, and then he stood there and let two men who had never met argue about a number, and he did not leave, and the not-leaving was the thing he did on the Monday that cost him something.**

The cost was an afternoon. He had four jobs booked for the middle of the day and he did two of them in a van in a yard with the doors open, and the other two were put off to the evening, and the evening is when the room is warm and the room is the part of the day he has organised his whole working life around, and so the cost of standing in a yard being unnecessary was an evening at a counter with his own warmth taken out of it.

**What he wanted and did not say: that he wanted the man of about thirty-eight to be thanked.** He wanted it in a way that was visible from about nine feet, and nobody looked at him, and the man of about thirty-eight did not want thanking, and the wanting was the wrong shape and he knew it inside about four seconds and stayed in the yard for another hour anyway.

He came back to the counter at about ten past six instead of at six and did the two jobs he had put off and neither of them took longer than it should have.

---

Nine or so people came through that room over the evening and four of them came in for the temperature, and not one of the four was asked anything. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in it for about an hour and got no question out of anybody and no thanks and no apology, and the eighth of the eight pages in the binder on that shelf is hers, and it was not read on the Monday.

**Nobody in that yard knew that the card in the inside pocket had a second column ruled in pencil on the back of it with nothing in it, and nobody in that room knew that a man had said a correct number out loud in a yard and had been told he was correct and had gone back to his van, and the two facts are not joined by anybody and the day did not join them.** The delivery note with a printed first column and a pencil-ruled second one is on about four days of bus travel away in the third of the four towns in an inside pocket, and a man of about thirty-three in a weighbridge hut has not asked about it, and a man of about twenty-two in Lattice Ward has not been told it exists in four towns.

The man of about fifty-one was at the wall with his bag on the floor and had said nothing for four hundred and eighty-one days, which is sixty-eight weeks and five days. On the far side of the road a man of about thirty-three holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors, and not one of the four has a stamp on it, or a reference, or a date, and he was not in this city, and his hold ran on for seventy-two weeks and four days with nothing asked of him and nothing given to him, and he is a different man of about thirty-three from the one in the hut.

The ask is at five hundred and sixty-five days, which is eighty weeks and five days, and it is counted from the day it was asked and not from a sitting since. Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
