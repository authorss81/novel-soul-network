# Chapter 520 — A Sentence Nine Years Old, Written Down For The First Time, On A Job Ticket

**The Thursday was a bench, a counter, a warm room, and then a canteen off a market at about ten past midnight, and the shape of the day was nine words a woman had been saying at a door for nine years being asked to be written down for the first time, and a drawer.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in the date column and eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and the hours were a bench in Lattice Ward to about two, that district's counter to about six, and the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. Nine and ten is the rate, wrong, unchanged, and known to one man. **The heating in the flat above that road is on and dearer, four names on one lease are carrying the increase, and a fortnight that one cook laid out of her own money is on nobody's account and will be in the spring as well.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and eighty-five days old, which is one hundred and twenty-six weeks and three days, and is warm, and is four rooms with a back one behind them.

The card in the rail along the counter front is eight hundred and eighty-nine days old, four days the more than the room, and the rail holds two. A job ticket with nine words on the back of it in a repairman's handwriting, going into a drawer in about four minutes, is a fifth object in this entry and is not that card and is not in that rail.

---

The canteen is off a market and it is open from about eleven at night until about three, because the people who use it come off work when everybody else has finished, and it has four tables and a counter and a hatch and about nine people in it at two in the morning.

She is about forty-one and she runs it and has run it for nine years, and in nine years nobody has ever heard her say anything about what it is for, and the reason for that is a sentence she says at the door.

**"Eating here does not mean you agreed to anything."**

She has said it to about four people an hour at two in the morning for nine years and she has never once asked anybody to write it down, and she has never once made a person repeat it, and she does not say it to anybody who works in the building.

**"The ones who work in here have agreed to a wage and a rota and a fire drill, and that is a different sentence, and I am not interested in their having opinions about the other one."**

A man of about twenty-two came in at about ten past midnight because he had been in the market and had not eaten since the afternoon and had been told by a woman at a fruit stall that the canteen did not ask you anything, which is a sentence that has been going round this city for about a year attached to about four different rooms and none of them is a room that anybody has named.

She said it to him at the door. He sat down. She gave him a plate without asking which of the four things he wanted, because there are four things and two of them are the same and the choice is a formality that people are too tired to make and resent being asked to make.

He ate, and then he said the thing that made her put both hands on the counter.

**"You should write that down."**

**"I have thought about it."**

**"How long."**

**"Nine years. About four times a week, and I have never asked anybody, and I want to tell you exactly why I have never asked, and it is not modesty and I would like that on the record before anything else."**

**"There is no record. I have a counter."**

**"Then it is on the counter."**

She put her hands flat on it.

**"Because the day I ask somebody to write it down, it stops being a thing I say and it starts being a thing that is true, and a thing that is true has to be checked, and the first person who checks it is going to be a man with a form who wants to know whether a canteen off a market counts as a room, and if it does count then it goes on a list, and if it goes on a list then the day somebody wants to know what happens in here there is a piece of paper saying what I said, and that piece of paper is a door into this room with my handwriting on the hinge."**

**"So you have protected it by never saying it out loud to anybody who writes."**

**"I have protected it by never saying it to anybody at all except about four hundred people a week who are too tired to do anything with a sentence."**

---

**"Then I am not anybody who writes."**

**"And that is a lie and you know it is a lie, and I would rather say the true thing, which is that I write about eleven things a day and that everything I write goes in a drawer inside four minutes, and if you want your sentence on a piece of paper I will put it on the back of a ticket in about nine seconds and that ticket will be in a drawer in this city at about ten past midnight, and nothing will have been done, and you will have said it in ink, and that is the whole of what you would be getting."**

She looked at him for a long time.

**"Nothing will have been done."**

**"Nothing."**

**"Say that to me twice and mean it both times, and then I will ask you for it, and if you say it a third time I will not ask you at all."**

**"Nothing will have been done."**

**"Nothing will have been done."**

He wrote it on the back of a job ticket in about nine seconds, in the handwriting he uses on the wall list, and turned it round, and pushed it across the counter without taking his hand off it.

She read it. It said what she had said and it said it in his hand and not in hers, and the difference between the two was the entire content of the evening.

**"You have written it in your own hand and not asked me to check it."**

**"You are the only person who could check it and you are the only person who would be harmed by it being wrong, and if I had asked you to check it I would have put you in the position of approving it, and an approval is a door with your name on it instead of mine."**

**"That is the cleverest thing anybody has said to me in nine years and I am not going to tell you that."**

**"I know. It is in the drawer."**

---

She put the ticket face down on the counter, and he picked it up, and it went into the drawer under the counter in about four minutes, behind about forty other tickets, and the drawer is a drawer and has no index and no key and is emptied on a Friday.

**"There is a man who has been in this room twice."**

**"Then he is a man who has been in this room twice."**

**"He has been in here twice with a form. Two columns on it. The first one is what this room already does and the second one is whether this room still wants to be doing it, and I have had eleven days to decide what I want in the first one, and he was not here tonight, and the form was not on your counter, and nobody has offered me anything."**

**"And what do you want in the first one."**

**"Nine words in my own hand and not a shorter version of them, and that is what is in the drawer now, and nothing about any of it is going to be different in the morning."**

She fed about four people between about half past midnight and about two, and one of them was a man of about thirty-three who had come in off a bus and who had been in the room off a service road four days earlier and did not say so, and she said the nine words to him and he said thank you and she said **"Do not thank me, I am reading a number off a door"**, and he did not take it back, and neither did she.

**She has been said a true sentence to about four hundred people a week for nine years and has never once been thanked for it and has never once wanted to be, and the only person who asked her to write it down did it in about nine seconds and put it in a drawer, and she watched him do that and understood exactly what he had done and told a woman of about twenty-one at the hatch the whole of it about an hour later, and the woman of about twenty-one said that the drawer is the most honest room in this city, and the woman of forty-one said that a drawer is a drawer, and the two of them agreed and nothing else came of it.**

---

He came into the room off the service road at about half past six and there were four people in it for the temperature and one of them was the man of about fifty-one, who had his bag on the floor at his feet and had said nothing for four hundred and ninety-one days, which is seventy weeks and one day.

**What he wanted, at about seven, and did not say to anybody, was for one of the four hundred people a week to have been a person who writes and to have written it in a way that lasted.** He has wanted that for about two years, in about nine rooms, and every version of it he has ever been offered required a room, a chair, a form or a woman with a green stair, and none of those was on offer at about twenty past midnight off a market, and a ticket went in a drawer instead, and it is the smallest thing he has ever been part of and he is going to be thinking about it for a year.

**The decision he took on the Thursday cost him a Thursday.** He could have asked her the name of the canteen and written it at the top of the ticket, and a name at the top of a ticket is a thing that can be found by a person with four minutes, and a drawer with a name in it is a different object from a drawer without one. He did not ask. The cost is that in about a year he will not be able to remember which of about four canteens it was, except that he will, because he goes past it, and the cost is that the sentence in his own hand exists once in a drawer and in no other copy anywhere, and if the drawer is emptied on a Friday then it is gone, and he chose that, and he chose it for her and not for himself.

**Two figures on that board are each other's age and each other's day, and the pair of them is the thirteen and the man at the north wall, and they are about four feet apart and neither of them has moved.**

The thirteenth line on the hardboard on two nails is seven hundred and fifty-six days old, and seven hundred and fifty-six is also the day the man of about fifty-one first sat down at the north wall of that room with a tool bag and said nothing. The man of about fifty-one has been at that north wall for four hundred and ninety-one days, and four hundred and ninety-one is also the day the thirteenth line was written.

**Neither number is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating is a closed thing in this case, with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it, and the man at the north wall is standing about four feet from the board with his bag on the floor and has not looked at either of them since the twelfth.**

The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in the room and was asked nothing by anybody and thanked by nobody and nobody has said sorry to her, and her page is the eighth of eight in a ring binder on a shelf and was not read on the Thursday. A man of about thirty-three who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors was not in this city, and his hold ran on for seventy-four weeks to the day with nothing asked of him and nothing given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
