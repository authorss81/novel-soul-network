# Chapter 368 — Eleven Hours Of Dark

The Monday morning of week one hundred and thirty-four was the morning a room in the Quiet Quarter was dark on purpose for eleven hours, and **the dark was made by a person pulling a light switch at about seven in the morning and pushing it up again at about six in the evening, with nothing issued to anybody and no card and no key and nobody's authority behind it, and the person is not a villain and is owed nothing, and she is right about the boundary and wrong about the room, and both of those took about nine seconds each and nobody improved on either.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward from about half past eight until about six and was not in the Quiet Quarter and knows nothing about the dark and was not asked.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and sixty-two days old and is warm, and four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The room is four feet of shopfront in the Quiet Quarter with a counter across about three-quarters of the width, a kettle on the counter, a window, and a light on a pull switch by the door. There is a flat above it with two windows and a landing. There is no name over the door and nothing on it and no key on the door, and about four people in that building have keys and one of the four is a woman of thirty-five.

It is the third of the four rooms that agreed four sentences about a night on the Monday of week one hundred and thirty-two, and the night it had was the Wednesday, and the man of about forty-one who is in it sat in it from about ten at night until about six in the morning with no telephone in it and no call in it and nobody has found that out.

**Nobody changed the rota because of the light. Nobody asked whether the Wednesday night would be sat in the dark and nobody has asked since, and about four people in four rooms know the room went dark and about four do not, and the four who know have not said a word to each other about it.**

---

**The light was off when the first person came past at about ten past seven.** It went off at about seven, and it came on at about six in the evening, and that is eleven hours, and a person standing in that four feet of shop in the middle of a Monday morning in the middle of a working day could not see the counter.

The kettle is on the counter and the kettle was cold. The window is nine foot by about four and the window showed the street and the street showed the window and anybody standing in the street could look straight in. **A shut shop in a quiet street at ten in the morning on a Monday is an ordinary thing and about four people walked past it and about four did not and the difference between those two groups of about four is eleven hours.**

**It takes one person, one hand, and about a second. There is nothing in this city that can be done to that switch by anybody with a card or a form or a reference, and about four hundred competent people in this city could each write a different document about a room with no light in it, and not one of the four hundred has a door to write it at, and the person who did it was in the room.**

Nobody went into that shop between about seven and about six except the person who had made it dark.

She sat on the floor. **There is one chair in that four feet of shop and she was not sitting in it, and the chair is a wooden chair with a straight back and it has been in that room for about four years, and about four people in that street have noticed the chair and about four have not, and the chair is not the reason the light is off and nobody has ever said it was.**

A woman of about twenty-nine who answers a telephone four hours a day for a company in the second of the four districts came past at about one and looked in and could see nothing and went on. She is not in that room and was not let in and was not asked anything and has not said anything since, and she has not been thanked and is not owed anything.

At about four in the afternoon the light was still off and the window still showed the street and the street still showed the window, and about four people went past more slowly than they had at about ten.

---

She was in it. **She is thirty-five, and she is answerable for a place with no door-name which is not a company and not a guild and not a body, and nineteen days ago she asked a table of about nine people for a chair and was refused in about nine seconds by somebody who is not a villain and is owed nothing, and she did not leave, and she has not forgiven, and nobody has thanked her for either.**

She said the first thing at about ten in the morning, standing at the counter, to about four people who had come to the shopfront in four minutes, and it took her about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it.

"**A quiet room is not a place things get handed to. In about two weeks about four people a day have been handing things to this four feet of shop because it is one of the four rooms, and the only thing a quiet room has got to do about that is stop being lit.**"

**Nobody said anything.** Nobody agreed with her and nobody disagreed with her and about four people in that street have said since that it is the first sensible thing anybody in those four rooms has said and about four have said nothing.

Then she said the second thing, in about nine seconds, to the same about four people, and it is the reason the first one is on the page.

"**I have made it dark in a building I do not own, over a flat I do not know the name of. I was right about the first of those and I was wrong about the second. I am not going to be told which of the two mattered and I am not going to be told I was wrong for being right, and I am not a villain and nobody owes me anything for this.**"

Nobody improved on that either. Nobody said she was right and nobody said she was wrong, and nobody in that street has asked her which of the two she thinks mattered, and she has not been asked and is not going to be.

**Nobody has established what the light going off was about. It is her account and it is hers and there is no document, and this is the whole of what anybody knows about the eleven hours.**

---

**Four buildings in this city went dark on a Friday night one hundred and one days ago, and that is a different thing, and the person in the flat above that one had a light on at four in the morning and a stair light working and a lift running.**

Nobody in this case has put those two events next to each other. **The two of them are two of the many nights a city has gone dark in, and the eleven hours this week cost somebody something and the night one hundred and one days ago cost somebody something else, and nobody is going to make them the same object.**

About nine people were in a warm room off a service road at half past six on the Monday, and one of them was a first-year of nineteen who had walked past a shopfront in the Quiet Quarter four hours earlier and had not gone in, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink saying nothing. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door. Nobody asked her anything, nobody thanked her, nobody apologised to her, and the reason she comes is the heat, and the heat costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing and has not been asked about anything for about nine weeks and five days. A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds about a shopfront in the Quiet Quarter being dark at about ten in the morning with the kettle cold behind the window, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her and nobody in that room went and looked.

A man of about thirty-three has held four buildings and about nine hundred doors for ninety-five days with no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority behind him, and in that time he has needed nothing from anybody and been given nothing, and nobody has told him about the eleven hours and he was not in any of the four rooms.

That board on two nails has nineteen lines on it and has had nineteen since the nineteenth of them went up, which is one hundred and fifty-eight days ago, and the nineteenth is a lift mechanic's and the only promise on it. The heating is on and costs more and the four names on a lease cost more, and a fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The other dark, as a record of a night that is not this one.* **What happened: four buildings in this city went dark on a Friday night one hundred and one days before this Monday, and it was a different thing with different people in it. What was true in the flat above that one at about four in the morning: the light was on, the stair light worked, and the lift was running. *What the day did not touch.* **A room was dark on purpose for eleven hours in a working day and nobody in that street was harmed by it and nobody above it has yet said a word about it, and two people said about eighteen seconds at a counter and nobody improved on either. A night rota of four sentences was not changed and nobody asked about it. A switch by a shop door went back up at about six and nothing was recorded about it, the card in a rail in Lattice Ward did not move, the master's fifth line is still blank, and a drawer stayed shut with the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder unopened in it. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 371.**
