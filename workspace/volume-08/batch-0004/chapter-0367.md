# Chapter 367 — The Fourth Friday

The Friday evening of week one hundred and thirty-three was the fourth Friday on which a company training room in Crown Terrace ran a floor, and **the run went, and nothing in that room was a rehearsal of anything and nothing in it was a resolution of anything, and the one thing that happened in it was that the person who calls the run said out loud, in about nine seconds, that she has been calling it for four years and does not know who it is for, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her and she is owed nothing.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening, and there are no jobs of his after about six on a Friday. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward until about half past four and was not in Crown Terrace and was not told any of this.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and fifty-nine days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The floor is the second floor of a building in Crown Terrace. There is a corridor, a fire door at each end, a stair, and about nine doors that are not opened during the run. There are nine folding chairs and about eleven people.

The route is the same route it has been for four years. Down the corridor, past four of the nine doors, through the fire door at the far end, down a flight, along a second corridor with a window at the end of it, and back. It is about nine minutes at the speed of nine people who know it, and it is longer than that with anybody new on it, and about four of the about eleven are new each time and about four are not.

The fire door at the far end has a bar on it and the bar is never pushed and the door is never opened during the run by anybody, and about four people in that room have never noticed that and about four have, and the four who have have not mentioned it to the four who have not.

Nobody in that room has ever been told what the run is for by anybody in that company, and the woman of thirty-six who calls it has a job and is not asked about it by anybody, and she has not said.

The post is by the corridor end and there is nothing on it. It has been on that post for four years and it came down two weeks ago and nobody has put anything in its place and nobody has asked for it to be put back and nobody in that building has said a word about the post since, and **the post is not a thing anybody in that room has an opinion about, and about four of them looked at it once and about four did not.**

Seven of the about eleven are on the company's list and four are on no list of any kind, and the four are not missing on any particular Friday and are not chased, and nobody in that building has ever been asked to say.

Two of the eleven did not come on this evening. Nobody chased them. Nobody in that room has ever chased anybody on any Friday, and about four people in that room have noticed that nobody chases anybody and four have not.

**Nobody knows which two they were, in the sense that nobody has asked anybody, and about four of the about eleven could name the two who were not there on this evening and about four could not, and neither of those groups has said so and neither is going to be asked.**

There is a woman of about twenty-six who stacks shelves in a place that opens at eight in the second of the four districts, and who has been in that room twice and has not said a word on either occasion, and nobody has asked her to and nobody has asked her why she comes, and she is one of about four who are on no list and she is not going to be the subject of anything.

---

The run went at about half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by the woman of thirty-six, and everybody did the thing they did, and nothing was handed to anybody and nobody counted for anybody and nobody prompted anybody.

**Nobody in that room referred to any other Friday. Not one of the about eleven, and not the woman of thirty-six, and there is no way of knowing what anybody was thinking about and nobody has said, and the four who noticed that nobody had and the four who did not have not spoken to each other and are not going to.**

Nobody said how long it took. Nobody said how long it did not take. The man of about twenty-nine who comes on a Friday sat down at the right moment without being told, and the woman of about thirty-eight who has not spoken in that room for seven Fridays spoke in it on this one, once, in four seconds, at the back, and what she said was about a bus.

"**The nine-forty has been coming at nine thirty-five and I have got four minutes at the stop now instead of none, and I have not told anybody and I am telling eleven people I have met twice.**"

Nobody answered her. Nobody asked her which stop and nobody said that was a good thing and about four people in that room looked at the floor, and about four looked at the door.

Then the chairs went back on the stack, four of the about eleven put them back and about four did not, and nobody has ever said anything about the chairs.

---

Then the woman of thirty-six said it, from the front, to about nine people, in about nine seconds, and it was not asked for.

"**I have been calling this run for four years. I do not know who it is for. I have never known and I have never asked and I am not going to start asking anybody tonight.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her what she meant and nobody asked her whether she wanted to stop calling it and nobody said that somebody should be found who knew. A man of about forty-four who has been in that room for four years and who does not speak in it looked at the floor, and about four people in that room noticed that he looked at the floor and about four did not, and neither group has said anything since.

The man of fifty-eight said nothing at all on this Friday. He was in the room from about half past six until about nine and he said nine words to nobody about a bus route, and about four people in that room noticed that he had not said the thing he said in an earlier week and about four did not, and **nobody asked him about it and nobody is going to.**

"**The eleven goes at ten past and the eleven twenty has gone. That is all I have got.**"

**She is a person with a job. She is owed nothing, she was thanked by nobody, she is not a villain, and she has not been asked what she meant and she has not been asked to say it again. About nine people in a room in Crown Terrace now know that the person who calls the run does not know who it is for, and none of them is going to do anything about it, and there is nothing on any surface in this city that says so.**

They go out through a lobby and up a ramp and onto a forecourt with about nine cars on it and a man in a booth who has never once been inside that building and who does not know any of the eleven and who has never been asked to and would not be told. He watched about nine of them go past and did not raise the barrier and nobody waved and nobody has ever waved.

The ramp is about four metres and it takes about nine seconds, and about four of the about eleven go up it more slowly than they need to, and nobody has ever said so to anybody.

**He is the only person in that building with a post, a stamp and a piece of paper, and none of the three has ever touched anything that happens on the second floor, and nobody has ever put those two facts next to each other and this entry does not put them next to each other either.**

---

About half past six the room off a service road was warm and about nine people were in it. A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door, and she was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she comes is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds, which was that a woman had come into the room off a service road at about four that afternoon and had asked whether a man in Lattice Ward was in on Fridays, and that she had been told he was not on Fridays and had not said anything else. Nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her and nobody in that room said whether that was true.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing and has not been asked about anything for about nine weeks and two days, and the standing arrangement that nobody renews is that he is a man with a bag in a warm room and it is not going to be changed this month either.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was in it for about half an hour and told about four people that a catch has come off a door in the first of the four districts for the second time in about two years, and nobody in that room asked her who had fitted it, and she did not say.

Nineteen lines on two nails, the newest of them the only promise on that wall, written by a man of twenty-nine about the middle, and it is one hundred and fifty-five days old and it has not been bettered or worse by the week. The heating is on and dearer, the four names on that lease are dearer, and a fortnight a cook put in is put in and not paid back. **Nobody in that room said a word on that Friday about anything that happened in a different building that week, and about four of them have since said to about four other people that they were not in any of it, and the other four have not said anything.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*370. Friday of week 133, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is nine folding chairs, a bare post, a run of about nine minutes, nine seconds said from the front, two people who did not come, and nobody who chased them.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening, and there are no jobs of his after about six on a Friday. One company training room in Crown Terrace, second floor, from about half past six until about nine; a corridor, a fire door at each end, a stair, about nine doors not opened during the run. Nine folding chairs, about eleven people, seven of them on the company's list and four on none. One post by the corridor end with nothing on it, and nothing has been put in its place and nobody has asked for anything to be put there. Two of the eleven did not come and nobody chased them, and nobody in that room has ever chased anybody. One run of nine people over one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front at about half past seven by a woman of thirty-six with a lanyard on who works there. One statement of about nine seconds from the front, unasked, by that same woman. One man of fifty-eight in the room from about half past six until about nine who said nothing but nine words about a bus route. One woman of about thirty-eight who spoke in that room for the first time in seven Fridays, in four seconds, about a bus. One room off a service road from about half past six, about nine people. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on, dearer, four names on the lease dearer still, a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid. Room off a service road: four hundred and fifty-nine days. Card in a rail in Lattice Ward: four hundred and sixty-three days, a third object in a third building, and the two have never been in one room. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines three hundred and seventy-nine days, the thirteenth three hundred and thirty, the fourteenth two hundred and ninety-five, the fifteenth two hundred and seventy-four, the sixteenth two hundred and forty-nine, the seventeenth two hundred and thirty-one, the eighteenth one hundred and seventy-seven, the nineteenth one hundred and fifty-five. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, fourteen names, nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off. Line one is a cook's and has never moved. Line nineteen is a lift mechanic's and is the only promise on that board. Eight things in a ring binder, unopened, unread, shut. The woman's page is unread and is shut with them. The master's fifth line empty, its drawer unopened. The Exchange did not sit on this day and does not sit on a Friday. Its last sitting was a Wednesday nine days ago and its next one is a Wednesday nineteen days from today, and the book is at fifty-one lines, the tin at seventy-three, the count announced twenty-seven times, and the business nineteen years old. All four printed, none converted.**

*The post, as a record of a thing with nothing on it.* **Post: one, by the corridor end of a second floor in Crown Terrace. On it, until two weeks ago, for four years: one laminated sheet saying what a run is and who calls it. What is on it now: nothing. Times anything has been put in its place: none. Times anybody has asked for anything to be put in its place: none. Times anybody in that room has said a word about the post since: none. People who looked at it once on this evening: about four. People who did not: about four. Whether the post has an opinion attached to it by anybody: no, and it is not a notice board and it has never held a notice and there is no notice anywhere in that building about that floor. Owner: nobody, and the room.**

*The run, as a record of nine minutes.* **Called from the front at about half past seven by a woman of thirty-six with a lanyard on who works there. People on the floor: nine. Length: about nine minutes. Whether anything was handed to anybody: no. Whether anybody counted for anybody: nobody. Whether anybody prompted anybody: nobody. Whether anybody said how long it took: no, and whether anybody said how long it did not take: also no. Whether this run was compared with any other by anybody in that room: no, and four Fridays have now been run in four months in a room with a post in it and none of the four has ever been compared with any of the other three, and the comparison is nobody's job. Whether anybody referred to any other Friday: no. Owner: the eleven, and the room, and the company.**

*The nine seconds, as a record of what the person who calls it said.* **Said by: a woman of thirty-six with a lanyard on who works there and who calls the run, from the front, to about nine people, unasked, at about ten to nine. Her points, in order: that she has been calling this run for four years; that she does not know who it is for; that she has never known; that she has never asked; and that she is not going to start asking anybody tonight. Whether she explained it: no. Whether anybody asked her what she meant: no. Whether anybody asked whether she wanted to stop calling it: no. Whether anybody said somebody should be found who knew: no, and nobody in that room has said since that somebody should be found who knows, and about four people in that company have discussed it and about four have not and neither group has done anything. Improved on: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody, no. Owed: nothing. Whether she is a villain: no. People who now know that the person who calls the run does not know who it is for: about nine, in that room only. Owner: her, and the room, and nobody.**

*The two, as a record of people who did not come.* **Number: two, of about eleven. Chased: by nobody. Times anybody in that room has ever chased anybody on any Friday: none, in four years. Whether the two have said they would not come: no, and nobody has asked them. Whether the two have been asked why: no. Whether the number of people in that room varies from Friday to Friday: yes, and it is not a register and nobody keeps one, and about four people in that room could give a rough count of any given Friday from memory and about four could not, and neither group has said so. Owner: the two, and the room, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **A floor was run and nothing about it was measured, named, or put on a surface, and the four of these Fridays have never been compared with each other by anybody. About nine people in that room now know a thing about the person who calls the run and not one of them is going to act on it. A hardboard on two nails was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, a drawer stayed shut, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder was not read. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 370.**
