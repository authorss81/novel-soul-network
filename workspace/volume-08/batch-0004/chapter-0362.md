# Chapter 362 — Nobody Said It To Her Face

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and thirty-two was the twenty-second sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and **the book did not open, and that is the first of two sittings in a row in this case that have not opened, and the reason was given in a mouth at about eleven and it is hers and it is not a rule and it was not decided by her this morning.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward for the whole of the day and knows nothing about the room above a line in Saltmarket and was not asked about it and would not have been told.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and fifty days old and is warm, and four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The shutters were up in a room above a line in a building in Saltmarket and the holder of the room said so at the start, in the way she says it at the start of every sitting.

Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in. The ninth was against the wall with its back to the room, and it stayed where it was, and nobody looked at it, and nobody has to. There is a table, a tin that has never had a lock on it, and a book with four columns and fifty-one lines in it, and the book was on the table all morning and was not opened.

Nine people. A man of about forty-four, who asked her for a rule twenty weeks ago and was given nine words that were not it and has not asked since. A young man of twenty-one who has been in that room since he was seventeen and who keeps a count he has never given her and did not give her this morning. A young woman of twenty-one who has been in that room since she was fifteen and who has never been asked anything and was not asked anything this morning. And about five others not described here.

**Nobody wrote down that she was there. Nobody writes it down.**

The light comes in over about nine feet of a landing and it is on at about a quarter to ten and off again by about half past eleven, and about four of the nine sat with their backs to it and about four did not, and nobody in that room has ever suggested moving a chair.

The landing itself is about nine feet wide and there are four doors off it and none of the four is the door of that room, and the shutters are on the landing and not on the room, and a person coming up those stairs in the dark goes past four doors before the light reaches them. **About four of the nine have walked those stairs in the dark and about four have not, and the young woman of twenty-one had been walking them since she was fifteen.**

There is a kettle in that room that is boiled at about ten and is not boiled again. There is a counter with nothing on it. There is no list, no agenda, no order of items, and the order of the six was the order people telephoned in and the holder of the room did not choose it and has never chosen it.

---

She did not open the book, and she said so herself, and it took her about four seconds.

"**No. Not this morning.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A person who came for that item had come for it on the Tuesday, in about nine years, and had not been told in advance and was not told now, and the not being told is not a decision and has not been a decision and nobody in that room has ever been told that there is a way to be told.

Then the items, four minutes each, and there were six, and two of the six were about rooms and neither of the two was written down.

The first room was a room in a building in the second of the four districts in which about nine people had arranged to be at about nine o'clock on the Friday night, and the person on the telephone said it and the person on the telephone stopped, and she wrote nothing.

The second item was a man of about thirty-one who said a thing he had said at a previous sitting and asked whether it had been written, and she said that it had not, and he said that he had not expected it to have been, and neither of them said anything else and it took four minutes.

The third item was a number, and it is the only number anybody gave into that tin on this morning. The person on the telephone had spent a fortnight counting, and the count was all he had, and he gave it in one sentence and then stopped talking.

"**Two hundred and nineteen doors in four buildings.**"

"**Two hundred and nineteen.**"

She said it once, wrote it once, and it went into the tin, and the figure the tin has reached is seventy-three, and it moved from seventy-two because a number was given into it on this morning and for no other reason and at no other point of it.

**Nobody said anything about two hundred and nineteen. Nobody asked her what the number was a number of, and she did not say, and the number is not in the book, is not in any column of the book, and is not written on the sheet the five are on. He had spent a fortnight on it and had been told it was noted, and it was noted, and a fortnight is not a thing that goes into a tin.**

The fourth item was four minutes long and was about a door that does not latch on a stair in one of four buildings, and the person who rang it did not know which of the four and had not been told and did not ask again, and she wrote nothing and told him nothing, and he rang off at about eleven minutes past ten.

The fifth item was a room again, and the person on the telephone was a person who has been in that room before and who could not get from it to anything else in about nine seconds and then stopped, and she wrote nothing, and there is no rule that says she has to and there has not been one in nineteen years.

The sixth item was the youngest person in that room saying one sentence, and the sentence was that the shutters on the landing were down when she came up and that they had been up the week before, and the holder of the room said that they were up at about a quarter to ten every week and had been for nineteen years, and the young woman said that she knew and had been looking for the reason, and about four people in that room noticed that she had said she had been looking and about four did not. **Nobody in that room has ever asked her what she is looking for and nobody in this case is going to.**

At about half past ten the light went off the landing and came back on to about half of the room, and about four of the nine moved their chairs and about four did not, and the ninth chair against the wall was in the dark half and stayed there.

---

The man of about forty-four sat down at about a quarter to ten and looked at the ninth chair at about half past ten, and about four people in that room noticed him looking at it, and he did not ask about it, and he was not asked about that either.

**He has not asked her for anything since the seventeenth sitting. It is twenty weeks since he asked. Nobody in that room has asked her for the rule she has not given in nineteen years, and she has said for the last time that she will say it, and she has kept that six times, and nothing was given in its place this morning, and there was no substitute, and nobody asked her for one.**

She said one more thing at about eleven, unasked, in about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**I write it down when a person says a thing to my face. In the two months before this morning nobody said a thing to me. That is the whole of it. It is not a rule, nobody agreed it, and I have not changed anything this morning.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

One of the nine started to say something and did not, and about four people noticed that she had started and did not say what it was, and she was not asked about it, and nobody in that room has asked her since what she would have said.

Then the young man of twenty-one, who has been in that room since he was seventeen and who keeps a count he has never given her, said one thing in about four seconds and it was to the table and not to her.

"**She has opened it four times in the six years I have been in this room and I have never once known in advance which of them it would be, and I gave up trying to work it out at about four years.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked him what the count is, and he did not give it, and he has not given it and was not asked for it and is not going to be. The holder of the room said nothing about it and wrote nothing about it.

And the man of about forty-four said one thing, in about four seconds, to nobody.

"**I have asked her once in nineteen years and I am not going to ask her twice, and the reason I am not is that I did not understand the nine words and I have had twenty weeks to think about them and I have not.**"

Nobody improved on that either, and nobody told him that he was right not to ask, and nobody told him he was wrong, and he sat down and looked at the ninth chair again at about five to eleven and then stopped looking at it.

**The count was announced and the announcement is made at every sitting whether the book opens or not. It is twenty-seven. Twenty-two of them are the Wednesdays this book has sat on, and five are older than the book, and the five are on a sheet she has never shown anybody and has not shown this morning.**

The book is at fifty-one lines. It is at fifty-one lines and it does not grow. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced twenty-seven times. The whole business is nineteen years old. **Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and every one of them is the whole of itself.**

**None of those four was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and was not given as one, and she did not say it and nobody asked her for it and she said nothing in its place.**

---

Nine people were in a warm room off a service road at half past six and none of them knew that anything had happened four miles away that morning. At a sink was a cook of twenty-seven who said nothing at all. Nearest the door, in the chair, was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to; the reason she comes is the heat; the heat costs more than it did; and both of those are correct.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing and has not been asked about anything for about eight weeks, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it. A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds about a lift that had stopped between two floors on the Tuesday and had not been reported, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was in it for about twenty minutes on her way out and told about four people that she had said a thing to a man in a corridor on the Monday and that he had said no, and she gave it back exactly as he said it and not one word more, and nobody in that room asked her why she had said it to him.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, the last of them a lift mechanic's, the only promise on that board, and it has been up one hundred and forty-six days without anybody touching it. The heating stays on and costs more than it did, the four names on that lease cost more than they did, and what a cook paid for a fortnight has not been paid back by a soul. **Nothing that happened in a room above a line in Saltmarket on this Wednesday was said in this room, and nobody brought any of it here, and about four people in this room have no idea that a book stood shut four miles away this morning.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*365. Wednesday of week 132, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one sitting of four minutes an item, a book that did not open, one number into a tin, a chair against a wall, an ask twenty weeks old, and a reason given in a mouth that is not a rule.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening, all of them at a bench in Lattice Ward. One room above a line in a building in Saltmarket, shutters up, about ten past ten to about eleven, nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, a table, a tin with no lock on it, and a four-column book. Present: nine. Ninth chair: against the wall, back to the room, not moved, not looked at, and nobody suggested moving any chair in that room in nineteen years. Items: six, four minutes each, two of them about rooms and neither of the two written down. Numbers given into the tin: one, at the third item, two hundred and nineteen doors in four buildings, counted over a fortnight by the person who gave it, said once and written once. Times anybody has asked her for the rule she has not given: none this morning, and the last time was twenty weeks ago, at the seventeenth sitting. Times she has said for the last time that she will say it: six, and nothing was given in its place and no substitute was offered. One room off a service road from about half past six, about nine people. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on, dearer, four names on the lease dearer still, a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid. Room off a service road: four hundred and fifty days. Card in a rail in Lattice Ward: four hundred and fifty-four days, a third object, in a third building, and the two have never been in one room. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines three hundred and seventy days, the thirteenth three hundred and twenty-one, the fourteenth two hundred and eighty-six, the fifteenth two hundred and sixty-five, the sixteenth two hundred and forty, the seventeenth two hundred and twenty-two, the eighteenth one hundred and sixty-eight, the nineteenth one hundred and forty-six. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. Line one is a cook's. Line nineteen is a lift mechanic's and is the only promise on that board. Eight things in a ring binder, unopened, unread, shut. The woman's page is unread and is shut with them. The master's fifth line empty, its drawer unopened. The Exchange did not sit on this day. Its last sitting was a Wednesday twenty-eight days ago and its next sitting is a Wednesday twenty-eight days from today, and the book is at fifty-one lines, the tin at seventy-three, the count announced twenty-seven times, and the business is nineteen years old. All four printed, none converted, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number.**

*The book, as a record of an object that did not open.* **Lines in it at the start of the morning: fifty-one. Lines in it at the end: fifty-one, and it did not grow by one and it did not shrink. Whether it was opened: no, and she said so herself in about four seconds, and she did not say why at the time. Times anything has been written into it in nineteen years: about nine people, and each time a caller said the thing to her face. Times anything was written into it on this morning: none. Whether anybody in that room was told in advance that it would not open: nobody, and there is no way to be told, and that is not a decision and has not been a decision. Times a caller has asked her why it is shut: about four, over nineteen years, and she has answered about four times and no answer has improved on any other. Whether she is right about any of that: it is not written down and nobody in that room said. Whether the book being shut is a judgement about the callers: it is not and she did not say it was and nobody said it was. Owner: the book, and the tin, and the room, and nineteen years.**

*The reason, as a record of nine seconds said at about eleven.* **Said by: the holder of the room, unasked, at about eleven. What she said, in her order: that she writes it down when a person says a thing to her face; that in the two months before this morning nobody said a thing to her; that that is the whole of it; that it is not a rule; that nobody agreed it; and that she has not changed anything this morning. Whether it is a rule: no, and there is no rule and nobody agreed one and none is going to be made. Whether it was decided by her this morning: no. Whether it may be described as a change in her: no, and nothing written here describes it as one, and it is a fact about two months in a city and not about a person. Improved on: by nobody, in that room or since. Asked about afterwards: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody, no. Owed: nothing. Owner: hers, and two months, and nobody.**

*The ask, as a record of a column that has not moved.* **Asked by: a man of about forty-four, of the seventeenth sitting, twenty weeks ago. Asked for: a rule. What he was given: nine words that were not it. Times he has asked since: none. Times he has sat since: every one of them, and he was in the room this morning and looked at the ninth chair at about half past ten. Whether anybody in that room asked her for that rule on his behalf: no, and nobody has, and there was no substitute offered and none taken. Whether the interval was carried from a chair: no, and it is twenty weeks and it is the number of days between the day he asked and this morning, and it is not a number of sittings. Owner: him, and the ask, and nobody.**

*The announcement, as a record of a count.* **Announced: whether or not the book opened, and it was announced. Figure: twenty-seven. What the twenty-seven are, as a sheet describes them: twenty-two of them are the Wednesdays this book has sat on, and five are older than the book. Where the five are: on a sheet. Whether the sheet has been shown to anybody: no, and she has not shown it this morning. Times the count has been announced: twenty-seven. Whether the count is a rate: no, and it is not on a form and it is not a figure anybody in this case may quote. Whether the count was turned into the lines in the book or into the number in the tin: no, and the difference between the book and the tin was not given as a number. Owner: the sheet, and the tin, and nineteen years.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody thanked anybody, and the holder of the room was not thanked and was not asked for anything and gave nothing and nothing was given in its place. The ninth chair was not moved and nobody in that room suggested moving any chair. The book in a room above a line in Saltmarket was not written in and nobody counted the tin after the morning. A hardboard on two nails in a different building was not written on either, a card in a rail did not move, and the master's fifth line is still blank. A night rota of four sentences was not looked at by anybody in that room and was not mentioned. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 365.**
