# Chapter 361 — Four People And A Night

The Monday morning of week one hundred and thirty-two was the morning four people in four rooms in four districts each said one sentence about a night, and **there is no agreement anywhere in this case, because an agreement is a document and there is none, and by the evening there is a rota with four names in it and no owner and no way of telling which of the four rooms is on tonight.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward from about half past eight until about six, was in none of the four rooms, and was asked about the night once in a corridor and said no in about nine seconds.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and forty-eight days old and is warm, and four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The four rooms are four rooms. One is off a service road in the Lower Wards, with a hardboard on two nails in it and a board over the door carrying four words in chalk. One is on the first floor above a row of shops in Saltmarket, with about nine chairs and a table and a sheet of paper with nine words on it face up in the middle. One is four feet of shopfront in the Quiet Quarter, with a counter across three-quarters of the width and a kettle on the counter. One is a weighbridge office in the working Ashfields, with a weighbridge outside it and a window that looks at the scale and a telephone on the wall that has a pencil tied to it by a piece of string.

They have the furniture every other room in this case has, which is a table where there is a table and chairs where there are chairs and a counter where there is a counter. **No name on any of the four doors, nothing written on any of them, and not one key between the four, and there is no key to a room in this case that anybody can produce.**

**About four of the nine rooms where a page of paper with about eleven lines on it is being copied out by hand are these four, and nobody organised that either, and nobody has connected the two things in a room.**

The weighbridge office is the one that is hardest to be in at night, and nobody said so when the rota was being made. The office shuts at about six, the yard is floodlit until about ten, and after ten the building is dark on three sides and the door is unlocked and the telephone is in a room with a hard chair in it.

The one thing is a telephone at night. Between about ten at night and about six in the morning, if anybody rings one of those four rooms about something that cannot wait for the morning, one of the four will pick it up and go.

What people ring about is smaller than anybody expected. A door that has locked somebody in on the other side. A stair light gone between two landings. A person on the floor who does not want an ambulance. A company that has shut a floor for the night and locked the stair to it. **About four people in this case have wanted that in about four months and about four have not asked for it, and the asking is the whole of what a night is for.**

The rota is four nights out of seven. Monday the room off the service road. Tuesday the first floor above the row of shops. Wednesday the four feet of shop. Thursday the weighbridge office. Friday, Saturday and Sunday are in nobody's mouth.

**Four rooms, seven nights, four names, three empty ones, and nobody in any of the four rooms has said that out loud, and nobody has been asked.**

---

They said the sentences across the Monday, on telephones and on stairwells and in a weighbridge office at about half past three in the afternoon, and each of the four took about four seconds and none of the four was said twice. **Nobody in this case has the four sentences in the same order twice, and there is no order, and the order they were said in is the order they happened in and that is the whole of the record.**

The room off a service road had it from a woman of about thirty-four whose job is to check about nine hundred doors in four buildings, and she offered it before anybody asked her, and she said it standing at a counter with a coat still on.

The first floor above the row of shops had it from a courier of twenty-one who works in a cooperative of about eleven people and who owns his own hours, and he said it in nine words and rang two of the other rooms himself to say it had been said.

The four feet of shop had it from a man of about forty-one who has been in that room for four years and who does not speak in it, and he said it in eleven words to one person and to nobody else, and the one person told about nine others.

And the weighbridge office had it from a man of about twenty-one who works on a counter in a shop in the working Ashfields, and **he said it at about half past three on the Monday afternoon because the man who runs the weighbridge office asked him whether he would, and the asking and the saying are one sentence and not two, and nobody in that office asked him what his Thursdays are, and his Thursdays are the counter, which shuts at nine.**

Nobody wrote down which of the four had offered and which of the four had been asked. That is the whole of what there is on the page about the rota, and **it is not on a page.**

---

At about ten past four on the Monday he was at the counter of the repair shop in Lattice Ward with a lamp in his hand and the woman of about thirty-four came past on her way out and stopped, and she had been in the room off a service road that morning and had told about four people about it, and he is one of the four.

She asked him in about four seconds. **Not to be the one anybody rings, which he has already refused, and not to be asked anything twice.** She asked him to be a fifth night.

"**No.**"

"**You do not know what the nights are.**"

"**I am not one of your four, and if I was, you would be careful with me, and I am not going to be careful with a thing I do not own.**"

That is about nine seconds. She said "**All right**" and went out into the street and did not ask him again and has not asked him since, and about four people in the repair shop that afternoon were within about nine feet of it, and about four noticed that he said no to a thing that had nothing wrong with it, and about four did not, and the two groups have not met and are not going to.

**Nobody improved on the refusal and nobody said it was the right answer and nobody said it was the wrong answer, and he is not on the rota, and the rota is four, and this is the second time he has been asked for something that was not a job and the second answer was not the first answer.**

He went back to the lamp. He had a job in front of him that he had already done once and had to do again because the first one had come out wrong, and he did that one twice, and it took about an hour, and nobody in that shop helped him with it and nobody was asked to.

---

**By about seven on the Monday evening four rooms had a night each and about nine people knew it, and about four knew which night their own room had, and about nine could not find out who was on tonight without telephoning a person who had not been asked to remember.**

There is no list. There is no sheet. There is no card in a rail in any of the four rooms, and a card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a different object in a different building. **Nobody's job is the night, nobody is paid for the night, and nobody would know on any given night whether the person who had said the sentence was going to be there, because saying it is not the same as being there and the difference is four words long and nobody has said them.**

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter of the room off a service road at about half past six and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds, which was that a lorry had come into the yard behind the shops at about four and had gone again by about five, and that it had not come on any of the other days that week. Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. Nobody went to look at the yard.

It was warm in that room off a service road by half past six and there were nine people in it. Nothing in it had been decided yet. A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty, and nobody asked her anything, nobody thanked her and nobody apologised to her, and the reason she is in that room is the heat, and the heat costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

**Nobody in that room could say which of the four rooms had the Wednesday, and nobody asked, and the board over the door in this one still carries four words in chalk and has never said which of them is a night. A lorry came into the yard behind the shops at about four that afternoon and went again by about five, and nobody went out to it.**

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing, and nobody in that room has asked him anything for about seven weeks and five days. A hardboard on two nails in that room holds nineteen lines, and the newest of the nineteen is a lift mechanic's and is the only thing on it that anybody is relying on, and it has been there one hundred and forty-four days. The heating is on, it costs more, four names on the lease cost more still, and a fortnight that a cook of twenty-seven laid out of her own money is owed to her by nobody and is not going to be.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*364. Monday of week 132, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is four rooms, seven nights, four names, three empty ones, one second refusal of nine seconds, and nobody's job.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening, and the four rooms are not rooms this docket covers. He worked at the bench in Lattice Ward from about half past eight to about six and was in none of the four rooms. Four rooms in four districts: off a service road in the Lower Wards; the first floor above a row of shops in Saltmarket; four feet of shopfront in the Quiet Quarter; a weighbridge office in the working Ashfields. Names over the doors, signs on the doors, keys: none of the three in any of the four. Nights taken: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday. Nights not taken: Friday, Saturday, Sunday. Four sentences, about four seconds each, one each, said on telephones and stairwells and in a weighbridge office at about half past three in the afternoon. One of the four said his sentence in answer to a question. Whether anybody recorded which of the four had been asked: nobody, and it is on nothing. One ask of about four seconds in a corridor at about ten past four, and one no of about nine seconds, and a reason that was not a rule. One room off a service road from about half past six, about nine people. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on, dearer than it was, four names on the lease dearer still, and a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid. Room off a service road: four hundred and forty-eight days. Card in a rail in Lattice Ward: four hundred and fifty-two days, a third object in a third building, never in one room with the other. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines three hundred and sixty-eight days, the thirteenth three hundred and nineteen, the fourteenth two hundred and eighty-four, the fifteenth two hundred and sixty-three, the sixteenth two hundred and thirty-eight, the seventeenth two hundred and twenty, the eighteenth one hundred and sixty-six, the nineteenth one hundred and forty-four. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. Line one is a cook's and is still the first line. Line nineteen is a lift mechanic's, the newest, and the only promise on that board. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread, and shut. The woman's page is unread and is shut with them. The master's fifth line empty, its drawer unopened. The Exchange did not sit on this day and does not sit on a Monday. Its last sitting was a Wednesday twenty-six days ago and its next sitting is a Wednesday two days from today, and the book stands at fifty-one lines, its telephone at seventy-two, the announcement made twenty-six times, and the whole business nineteen years old. All four printed, none of them turned into another.**

*The rota, as a record of something that is not a document.* **What it is: one night each, four rooms, out of seven nights in a week, agreed by four people each saying one sentence. What it is not: a rota in any sense a body could act on, an agreement, an undertaking, a schedule, a list, a register, a minute, a form, a notice, or a resolution. Written on: nothing. Signed by: nobody. Countersigned: nobody, and no copy of it exists in this city and none is going to. Agreed by discussion: no. Agreed by vote: no, and no vote exists in this case and none has been called. Nights covered: four. Nights not covered: three, and nobody in any of the four rooms has said so. Time of night: from about ten at night to about six in the morning. What a night consists of: answering a telephone and going to whatever is on the other end of it. Person whose job it is: nobody, and nobody is paid for it, and nobody has been given a key, a card, a reference, a date or a anybody's authority. Owner: nobody, and the four rooms, and the three empty nights. Thanked: by anybody, no. Improved on: by nobody. Whether anybody can find out who is on tonight without telephoning a person who was not asked to remember: no, and about nine people can do it and about four cannot.**

*The four sentences, as a record of four mouths.* **One, in about four seconds, offered unasked at a counter with a coat on, by a woman of about thirty-four whose job is to check about nine hundred doors in four buildings, for the room off a service road, and Monday. Two, in nine words, by a courier of twenty-one who works in a cooperative of about eleven people who own their own hours, for the first floor above a row of shops in Saltmarket, and Tuesday, and he telephoned two of the other rooms himself to say that it had been said. Three, in eleven words, to one person only, by a man of about forty-one who has been in a room for four years and who does not speak in it, for four feet of shopfront in the Quiet Quarter, and Wednesday, and the one person told about nine others. Four, at about half past three in the afternoon, for the weighbridge office in the working Ashfields, and Thursday, and the sentence was an answer to a question. Whether any of the four has been asked since: no. Whether any of the four has been asked whether the sentence was an offer or an answer: no, and not one of the four can remember which it was without stopping to think about it. Improved on: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody, no. Owed: nothing. Owner: the four of them, and the four rooms, and nobody.**

*The asked one, as a record of a sentence with a question in front of it.* **Person: a man of about twenty-one who works on a counter in a shop in the working Ashfields. Room: the weighbridge office in the working Ashfields. Night: Thursday. Time of the asking: about half past three on the Monday afternoon, by the man who runs that office. Time of the saying: inside about four seconds of the asking. What was asked: whether he would take the Thursday night. What was said: that he would. Whether anybody asked him what his Thursdays are: no. What his Thursdays are: a counter, which shuts at about nine. Whether he has said the second half of that anywhere: no, and he has not been asked for it and has not offered it. Whether anybody blamed him: no, and nobody in this case has put his name to anything. Whether anybody thanked him: no. Whether he is owed anything: nothing, and he is not a villain, and he is not going to be made the answer to anything. Owner: him, and the sentence, and nobody.**

*The fifth name, as a record of nine seconds.* **Asked by: a woman of about thirty-four, in a corridor of the repair shop in Lattice Ward, at about ten past four on the Monday, in about four seconds. Asked for: a fifth night, and not a role and not a job and not a telephone anybody would ring him about, and the difference from the first refusal of this case is in the asking and not in the answer. Said: no, in one word. Said after that, in about nine seconds: that he is not one of their four, and that if he was, they would be careful with him, and that he is not going to be careful with a thing he does not own. Whether the reason was a rule: no, and there is no rule and there is not going to be one, and nobody in that corridor quoted anything at anybody. Whether he offered a version of it that would have worked: no. Whether he asked what she would do instead: no. Whether he said he would think about it: no. Whether he said he was sorry: no. Whether he said he understood: no, and she had not given a reason to understand. People within about nine feet: about four. People who noticed that he said no to a thing with nothing wrong with it: about four. People who did not: about four. Whether the two groups have met: no. Improved on: by anybody, no. Thanked: by anybody, no. Owner: him, and the four rooms, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **No night was worked and nobody was on one, and the rota that came out of the day is four sentences long and is not on anything. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in a warm room and said nothing and has now gone seven weeks and five days without being asked about anything. A page of paper with about eleven lines on it was not copied again and about eight of the nine copies are not finished. A hardboard on two nails was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, and the master's fifth line is still blank. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 364.**
