# Chapter 369 — The Flat Above It

The Wednesday evening of week one hundred and thirty-four was the evening the cost of the eleven hours of dark was said out loud, in a warm room off a service road, by a woman of about thirty-three who lives in the flat above the shopfront, **and she said it in about four seconds in her own words, and nobody improved on it, and nobody asked her a question about it, and nobody repaid her anything, and the child she brought with her is not a device and is owed nothing by this and is owed nothing at all.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward for the whole of the day and knows nothing about the dark and was not told and would not have been asked.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and sixty-four days old and is warm, and four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The flat has two windows and a landing. The light switch by the shop door is on the same board as the flat's light, and that has been true for as long as anybody has been in that building, and about four people who live in it have known it and about four have not, and nobody has ever had a reason to find out.

**So the flat was dark for the same eleven hours as the shop. Nobody in that building was cut off from anything, and nobody in that building was hurt, and the one thing the dark did was make eleven hours of an ordinary Monday into a different Monday, and the different Monday belonged to two people and one of them is six.**

She is about thirty-three and she has an ordinary job in the second of the four districts and her hours are not her own. She does a thing on Mondays in that flat for about four hours that she is paid for by nobody and that is not anybody's business, and on the Monday of week one hundred and thirty-four she could not do it, and the reason she could not do it is that she could not see the work.

**Nobody in this case knows what the work is and nobody has asked and nobody is going to.**

The child was in the flat. The child is at a place two days a week and was not at that place on the Monday. The two windows of that flat face the street and the back of another building and about four feet of the front one look at the shopfront window, and from inside, in the dark, a person can see the shape of the window and nothing through it.

She said nothing to anybody in that building about the supply being off. **Nobody in that building knew there was a supply off, and nobody has found out, and about four people in that street have said since that the shopfront was dark on the Monday and about four have said they did not notice, and neither group has connected the two to a flat, and the person who made it dark has not been told by anybody that there was a flat and she did not know there was a flat and she has not asked.**

---

She came in at about half past six with a child of about six, and the child came because there was nowhere else to be at half past six and not because anybody arranged it, and the child sat on the floor by the counter for about an hour and a half and did not ask for anything.

Nobody in that room asked her anything. Nobody asked her why she was there and nobody asked her about the Monday and nobody asked her about the child and nobody asked the child anything at all.

A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all and carried a chair over to the counter about ten minutes in and put it down next to the child and went back to the sink, and said nothing, and has not said anything since, and nobody thanked her for it.

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door, and she was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she comes is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct. **The woman of about thirty-one with a key in her pocket is not in that room and has not been in any room this fortnight and was not asked about any of this and is not going to be.**

---

A woman of thirty-five was in it, and she had been in that room off a service road twice since the Monday, and she has not left.

Nobody in that room said anything to her about the Monday. Nobody asked her to explain it and nobody asked her whether she was right and nobody said she was sorry about the Monday. **She did not apologise in a room. She has not and she is not going to, and nobody in that room has forgiven her and nobody in that room has said that they have not, and both of those are the same silence and it lasted about an hour and a half.**

At about seven the woman of about thirty-three said it, to the room, in about four seconds, in her own words, and nobody improved on it.

"**I could not see to do my four hours on the Monday and I did not do them and I am not going to be paid for them and I am not saying that to anybody. My mother's sister had a stair light out for a fortnight in the winter and she did not say one word about it to anybody and she is seventy-one, and I have thought about that for two days and I cannot get past it, and that is all I have got.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her what the four hours were. Nobody said she was owed the money. Nobody said somebody should pay her and nobody offered and nobody wrote anything down.

**And nobody in that room is going to repay her for eleven hours, and no chapter is going to, and the reason is not that she is owed it and it is not that she is not, and it is that there is no line in this city that a person can be paid on for having been in the dark on a Monday.**

---

The child of about six was on the floor by the counter for about an hour and a half. She looked at the hardboard on two nails for four minutes and did not read any of it and nobody read any of it to her. She asked about the kettle once and was told by the cook of twenty-seven, in about four seconds, that it was not for her, and the child said that was all right.

At about half past seven a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts came in for about ten minutes and looked at the child on the floor and did not say anything to her and did not say anything to the woman, and he has not spoken about either of them since and he is not going to be asked, and the nineteenth line on that board is his and it is the only promise on it.

At about eight the child fell asleep on the floor against the leg of a chair and a woman of about thirty-one who is not in this case and who works in the first of the four districts put a coat over her and sat back down and said nothing, and nobody thanked her and she has not been thanked and is not owed anything.

Then she said one thing to the mother, in about four seconds, and it was not about the Monday.

"**I have got a daughter of five and I would have had that coat over her. I am not saying that to make it better. I am saying it because I have not said anything for an hour and a half and I have been sitting on it.**"

The mother said "**All right**" and nothing else, and the woman of about thirty-one did not say anything else, and about four people in that room were within about nine feet and about four of them heard it and about four did not, and the two groups have not met.

The mother did not wake her. **The mother has not been asked since whether that was the right thing and she has not said and nobody is going to, and the child is a person of about six and not a thing that happened to anybody.**

---

Four buildings in this city went dark on a Friday night one hundred and one days before the Monday, and that is not this and this is not the heating, and the heating is on and dearer and the four names on the lease are dearer, and what a cook paid for a fortnight nobody has paid and nobody is going to. **Three separate things in this city have gone dark or been cold in four months and not one of them is the other one, and nobody in this case has put them together and nobody is going to.**

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing and has not been asked about anything for about ten weeks. A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds about a light on a board and a switch by a door, and that it was the first time she had heard of that building and she was not going to go and look at it.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines and none of them is about a Monday, and the newest of them is the only promise on that surface, and it is one hundred and sixty days old. Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*372. Wednesday of week 134, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is a board with two things on it, a flat with two windows, four seconds said at about seven by a woman of about thirty-three, a child of about six asleep on a floor, and a woman of thirty-five who did not apologise.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening, and he was at a bench in Lattice Ward and is in none of this. One building in the Quiet Quarter: four feet of shopfront at the bottom, a flat above it with two windows and one landing, and a light switch by the shop door which is on the same board as the flat's light. Hours the supply was off at both: eleven, the same eleven. People living in that flat: a woman of about thirty-three with an ordinary job in the second of the four districts, and a child of about six. Four hours of unpaid work not done on the Monday, in that flat, for want of light, and nobody knows what the work is. One room off a service road from about half past six, about eleven people. A chair carried from a sink and put down and not said anything about. One statement of about four seconds at about seven by the woman of about thirty-three, to the room. Times anybody asked her what the work was: none. Times anybody said she was owed money: none. Times anybody offered: none. A woman of thirty-five in that room from about half past six, who has been in it twice since the Monday and has not left. Times anybody said anything to her about the Monday: none. Times she apologised in a room: none. Times anybody has said they have forgiven her: none. A child on the floor for about an hour and a half, one question about a kettle, a coat put over her by a woman of about thirty-one who is not in this case, and no sleep until about eight. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on, dearer, four names on the lease dearer still, a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid, and no figure of any kind is carried for it. Room off a service road: four hundred and sixty-four days. Card in a rail in Lattice Ward: four hundred and sixty-eight days, a third object in a third building, and the two have never been in one room. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines three hundred and eighty-four days, the thirteenth three hundred and thirty-five, the fourteenth three hundred, the fifteenth two hundred and seventy-nine, the sixteenth two hundred and fifty-four, the seventeenth two hundred and thirty-six, the eighteenth one hundred and eighty-two, the nineteenth one hundred and sixty. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, fourteen names, nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off. Line one is a cook's and has never moved. Line nineteen is a lift mechanic's and is the only promise on that board. Eight things in a ring binder, unopened, unread, shut. The woman's page is unread and is shut with them. The master's fifth line empty, its drawer unopened. The Exchange did not sit on this day. Its last sitting was a Wednesday fourteen days ago and its next one is a Wednesday fourteen days from today, and the book is at fifty-one lines, the tin at seventy-three, the count announced twenty-seven times, and the business nineteen years old. All four printed, none converted.**

*The board, as a record of a fact about a building.* **What is on one board in that building in the Quiet Quarter: the light of four feet of shopfront and the light of the flat above it, and the switch for both of them is by the shop door. How long that has been so: for as long as anybody in that building has been there. People in the building who knew it: about four. People who did not: about four. Times anybody has had a reason to find out: none. What it cost to know it: one switch, at about seven in the morning on the Monday, and the same eleven hours at both ends of it. What it did not cost: nobody was cut off from anything and nobody was hurt and nothing in that building was damaged. Whether this is a fact about electricity or about a person: it is a fact about a building and it happened because of a person, and both of those are true and neither of them is a cause of anything else. Owner: the building, and the board, and nobody.**

*The four seconds, as a record of a cost named out loud.* **Said by: a woman of about thirty-three who has an ordinary job in the second of the four districts and whose hours are not her own. Where: a warm room off a service road, at about seven on the Wednesday evening, to the room. Length: about four seconds. Her points, in order: that she could not see to do her four hours on the Monday; that she did not do them; that she is not going to be paid for them; that she is not saying that to anybody; that her mother's sister had a stair light out for a fortnight one winter and said nothing about it to anybody and is seventy-one; that she has thought about that for two days and cannot get past it; and that that is all she has got. Replies: none. Additions: by nobody. Questions asked of her: none, and specifically nobody asked what the four hours were. Whether anybody said she was owed the money: no. Whether anybody offered to make it right: no. Whether anything was written down: no. Improved on: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody, no. Whether she is owed anything by this case: nothing, and this entry does not say she is owed nothing in the world, and it says only that nobody in this case is going to do anything about it. Owner: her, and four hours, and nobody.**

*The child, as a record of a person of about six.* **Age: about six. How the evening came about: the mother came to a warm room off a service road at about half past six and the child came with her, and the child was not arranged and not sent and not left. Where the child was for about an hour and a half: the floor by the counter. Questions the child asked of anybody: one, about the kettle, answered in about four seconds by a cook of twenty-seven who said it was not for her. Things the child did: looked at a hardboard on two nails for four minutes without reading any of it, and fell asleep at about eight against the leg of a chair. Coat put over her by: a woman of about thirty-one who is not in this case and who works in the first of the four districts. Whether the child was repaid anything: no, and nothing in this city can repay a child of about six for a Monday morning. Whether the child was asked anything: no, and nobody asked the child one question all evening. Owner: the child, and nobody.**

*The woman of thirty-five, as a record of a person in a room saying nothing.* **In that warm room: from about half past six to about eight. Times she has been in it since the Monday: twice. Whether she has left: no, and she has not said she is going to. Whether she apologised in a room: no, and she was not asked to and nobody asked her to. Whether anybody said anything to her about the Monday: no, for about an hour and a half, and nobody said they had forgiven her and nobody said they had not, and those are the same silence. Whether she is right about the boundary: that is not a thing anybody in that room decided. Whether she is wrong about the room: same. Whether she is a villain: no. Whether she is owed anything: nothing, and nobody in that room thinks she is, and nobody in that room has said so. Owner: her, and the silence, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **A cost was named out loud in about four seconds by the person who paid it and nobody improved on it and nobody repaid it and nobody wrote it down. Eleven hours of dark on a Monday in the Quiet Quarter is not the heating, and the heating is not a Friday afternoon, and a night one hundred and one days ago in four other buildings is a third thing, and the three have not been joined. A hardboard on two nails was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, a drawer stayed shut, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder was not read. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 372.**
