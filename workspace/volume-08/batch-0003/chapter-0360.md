# Chapter 360 — What It Changed

The Thursday morning of week one hundred and thirty-one was the last morning of this movement, and **a man of fifty-one with a tool bag did a correct thing in four buildings in about nine minutes that changed nothing, said so out loud in nine words, is owed nothing and was thanked by nobody, and it is the first of at least four people in this volume who will do that.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Eleven attended, and the last of them finished at about six in the evening. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward all day and was told about the four buildings at about half past four and did not go to any of them.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and forty-four days old and is warm, and four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

He can say how many buildings in this city he can get into that a person cannot get out of. It is about four hundred. It is one of at least three numbers that carry the same figure and nobody in this case can separate them and nobody has ever put them in an order, and it is not the number of competent people who can write down a rule about somebody, and it is not the number of lines one cook's four words have travelled down in four years in about nine buildings.

There are four of the buildings in that count that he has been inside in the last year, and he has been inside the same four most weeks, and nobody has asked him to and nobody has given him a key and he has not asked for one.

**He is fifty-one and he has been doing this since he was about thirty-four and he has never once been asked to describe it and has never once offered to, and the four buildings are not his and the doors are not his and the bag is his and that is the only thing in it that belongs to anybody.**

He does not carry a list. He carries a meter he does not use and a set of keys that are not a key to a door, and about four doors a week open for him that would not open for a person with a card, and about four doors a week that would not open for him either and that he has not tried again since, and he has never told anybody which four and nobody has asked.

**A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors met him once on a stairwell in an earlier week and has not told anybody about it and has not been thanked for saying nothing, and a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts has never met him and has never been asked whether he has.**

**He did the correct thing on the Thursday morning of week one hundred and thirty-one, in about nine minutes, in four buildings, and there is no form for it and no register and no list and it is not a rate and nobody is going to ask him about it.**

What he did, in each of the four, to a person behind a desk, in the right words and in about two minutes each: that there is a page of paper in this city being copied out by hand in about nine rooms, that anybody arriving at that building saying they are from a room or from a practice is to be asked to say the fourth line of it out loud before they are let in, and that if they cannot, they are to be asked to wait in the lobby.

All four were correct. All four were said in the right words. About two of the four people behind those desks wrote it on something and about two did not, and the two who did have not written anything by the following week and neither has been asked.

**Nobody came to any of those four buildings in about four weeks on the strength of it, and nobody was going to, and four people inside the four buildings have said since that they would not have thought of it, and about four have said nothing, and the two groups have not met.**

He came into the room off a service road at about half past five on the Thursday and put the bag down and said the thing, in nine words, to about nine people, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him for it.

**Nobody in that room asked him why he had done it, and nobody asked him whether it had worked, and four people in that room understood that he had already answered the second question out loud and four did not, and neither group has said one word about it since.**

"**I did it right and nothing came of it.**"

Nobody said anything. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

**He is owed nothing. He is not thanked. Nobody in that room has thanked him for anything in about nine weeks and the asking about him has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it, and the standing arrangement is that he is a man with a tool bag in a warm room and it is not going to be changed this month either.**

---

Here is the change and it is small and it is the whole of what this movement has produced.

About nine people in that room know that the page of paper with about eleven lines on it is being copied out by hand in nine rooms in about four districts. They know it because they were told, and four of them saw the first copy, and about four did not.

Nobody in that room knows whether that is a good thing.

**A clerk of forty-four, who has been in a room with nine people in it twice, said that the copying is the first time in about eleven years that she has seen anything come out of one of those rooms that anybody outside it wanted, and then said that she does not know what it is for.**

**A woman of about thirty-eight who made the first copy said that it is not the page, on the day she made it and not since.** A man of about twenty-seven who moved a chair said nothing about either. A woman of thirty-five who was refused a chair said nothing about either, and was in that room on the Thursday, and has not mentioned it to anybody, and has not left either, and both of those are hers to decide and nobody has asked her about either.

Nobody improved on the clerk's sentence. Nobody answered it. **It is the only open question anybody in that room is holding, and about nine people are holding it, and it has no owner, and it is not on anything, and nobody has written it down, and it is not going to be written down by anybody in this case.** It is still the case, on the page, that about nine people know the page is being copied nine times and that not one of them can say whether it should be.

---

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines. Nobody wrote on it on the Thursday and nobody has written on it in four movements and the plan of record does not open a twentieth. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line. **The newest is a man of twenty-nine's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that wall, and it is one hundred and forty days old, and it is still the only promise on it, and it was not made better or worse by anything that happened in this movement.**

**The heating is on and costs more and four names on a lease cost more and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved and is not going to be resolved in this volume.**

**The Monday of this week had a courier of twenty-one ask that table for a chair and a woman of thirty-five be refused one two days later, and the Friday before that had eight words said at the back of a room in Crown Terrace that nobody made an item of, and the Wednesday before that had a man of about thirty-three at a door on a fourth-floor landing for about four hours, and the Monday before that had a page of paper copied out by hand for the first time, and the Friday of the week before had four seconds come off the end of a floor that nobody counted, and about four of those five things are still true and four people in that room could name all five.**

A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip has not been in any room this week and was not asked about and did not come. A man of about thirty-three has held four buildings for seventy-seven days and has needed nothing from anybody and been given nothing. A first-year of nineteen has a half hour of silence from an earlier month that is still not in her mouth and is not going to be asked for.

**The room was full from about half past five and there were nine people in it and a counter with nothing on it and a kettle and a bell-push that does not ring by itself and a plate on the outside with a telephone number and no name, and the counter had nothing on it on the Thursday for the fourth week running, and about four people noticed that and about four did not.** A man of about forty-four asked a holder of a book for a rule she has not given since an earlier volume, and that was a hundred and thirty-four days ago, which is nineteen weeks and one day, and he is not at that table and was not discussed and is not going to be.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
