# Chapter 359 — Nine Seconds

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and thirty-one was the morning a woman of thirty-five asked the same table for the same chair that a man of twenty-one had been given two days before, and **she was refused in about nine seconds by a woman of sixty whose job is to ask questions, and the person refusing was not a villain and is owed nothing, and the sentence she refused her with is the truest sentence anybody has said at that table, and it is also the reason the chair is not hers.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Eleven attended, and the last of them finished at about six in the evening. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward for the whole day. Nobody at the table with the chair knows that he is a person anybody can find and nobody in that room has asked.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and forty-three days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The chair is still against the wall of a first-floor room above a row of shops in the second of the four districts, and the man who moved it has moved it back twice since, and neither time was asked of him and neither time has been mentioned.

The courier of twenty-one was at that table. He is at every meeting. Nobody has asked him to be and he has never said that he comes because of a chair, and about four people in that room have wondered about it and about four have not.

There were eight of the nine at it. The ninth was not there and was not rung.

---

She came at about ten past eleven and she was thirty-five and she ran a place called the Quiet House, which is not a company, is not a guild, has no name over a door, and is not a faction, and about four hundred people in this city have heard of it and about nine people in this case have been inside it.

It is a quiet room in every sense the word has. It does not hold a meeting, it does not take a name, it does not put anybody on a list, and it has refused twice in about two years to be anything that could be quoted, and both refusals were said out loud in about four seconds by a person who is not a villain.

Nobody in that room had asked her for anything before. About four of the nine had met her and about four had not.

She asked in about four seconds, from the doorway, in the same words the courier used two days before, and about four people heard that they were the same words and about four did not.

"**Can I sit at this table.**"

---

The woman of sixty whose job is to ask questions had not asked a single question in any of these rooms in about a month, and she had produced a finding in about nine words in one of them and had not been thanked, and she said the no in about nine seconds.

"**No. I am going to say why in one sentence and then I am not going to say it again, and I want you to know that the no itself took nine seconds and the sentence is free.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**A room a person can be in because they asked is a public room, and a public room cannot be a quiet one, and this is a quiet room, and I have sat in a quiet room that became a public room and it took about four minutes and I am not doing it again.**"

She said one more thing and it was about nine seconds and it was the reason she said the sentence at all.

"**And the only thing in this city that can make a quiet room into a public room is a piece of paper printed by somebody who has never been in the room, and the Assembly is the reason I know that, and I have never been inside it and I am not going to be, and I am a person with a job and my job is to ask questions and I am not going to ask you one today.**"

**Nobody improved on it. The word went into a room in a mouth and not into a document and not onto any surface, and it is not in this record in the same words, and nobody in that room asked her a question about it, and about four people in that room had heard her say that word before and about four had not.**

---

Then the woman of thirty-five said the thing she is right about, and it took her about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and it is the reason this is the most expensive nine seconds anybody has spent at that table.

"**You are right. That is exactly right and I have been saying some version of it for four years. And you have just used my own sentence to refuse me a chair. And I have never once used it to refuse anybody anything, and I am not going to start today, and I am not going to argue with you in a room where you have just been fair to me.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**And I am not going to say that this is unfair. It is not unfair. You have about nine people in this room and a man of twenty-one at your table and you have never once asked either of them whether they were allowed to be here, and neither have I, and the reason I am being refused is that I asked, and that is the correct reason and I would give the same answer.**"

The woman of sixty said: "**Yes. I would.**"

"**I know you would. That is why I am not leaving.**"

**She did not leave. She stayed for about fifty minutes after that, and she said about nine more words in the whole of it, and none of them were about the chair, and about four people in that room have told about four other people what happened and not one of them has said anything about it since.**

---

Here is the change and it is the whole of what this chapter is about.

There is now a rule at that table. Nobody wrote it and nobody said the word rule and nobody took a vote and there is no minute. The rule is that a person from outside the table is refused, and it arrived inside a true sentence, and the true sentence cannot be argued with, and so the rule cannot be either.

**The rule is worse than the chair would have been. A chair is furniture, and it can be moved, and four people can be asked to move, and nobody has to be right about anything. A rule that is true and unwritten cannot be argued with by the person it refuses, and it applies to the next person, and it applies to the person already sitting, and nobody in that room said one word about that last part.**

A man of about twenty-seven who moved the chair looked at the courier of twenty-one, who was at that table, and did not say anything, and the courier of twenty-one did not look up, and neither of them has said one word about it since and neither of them is going to be asked.

**A woman of thirty-five was right about the one thing she is right about, and a woman of sixty was right about the one thing she is right about, and both of them were right, and the cost fell on the person who was refused, and that is not a failure of anybody in that room and it is a room working exactly the way a room works.**

---

At about half past six the room off a service road was warm and about nine people were in it. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds a bus timetable that had come down, and it was not about any of it and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing and has been asked about nothing for about eight weeks and a half, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines and the newest of them is the only promise on it and it is one hundred and thirty-nine days old. The heating is on and costs more and four names on a lease cost more and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*362. Wednesday of week 131, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is a woman of thirty-five, a woman of sixty, one ask of four seconds, one no of nine seconds, one true sentence, one word said in a mouth and nowhere else, and a rule that nobody wrote.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening. He spent the whole day at the bench in Lattice Ward and knows nothing of this and was not asked. One room over a shopfront in the second of the four districts: about nine chairs, a table, nothing on the door, nothing on the walls, no post of any kind in it. Eight present of nine; the ninth absent, unringed, and no post exists that would make the call his duty. One chair standing against the wall, moved back twice by the man who moved it, on neither occasion asked. The man of twenty-one present, as he is at every meeting, never asked to be and never asked why. One woman of thirty-five at the door at about ten past eleven, for about an hour, in the words the courier had used two days before. One no, about nine seconds, by a woman of sixty whose job is to ask questions and who has asked none in a month. One sentence of explanation following it, and one further statement of about nine seconds in which the name of a public body was spoken once, by a person with a job, in a room, and was not written down and is not reproduced here. One statement by the refused person of nine seconds, and about nine words more in the fifty minutes after it. One room off a service road from about half past six, about nine people. Rate: nine and ten, uncorrected, and not the subject of anything. Heating: on, dearer, four names on the lease dearer, a fortnight laid out by a cook unrepaid and not to be repaid. Age of the room off the service road: four hundred and forty-three days. Age of the card in its rail in Lattice Ward: four hundred and forty-seven days; a separate object, in a separate building, never in one room with the other. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines at three hundred and sixty-three days, the thirteenth at three hundred and fourteen, the fourteenth at two hundred and seventy-nine, the fifteenth at two hundred and fifty-eight, the sixteenth at two hundred and thirty-three, the seventeenth at two hundred and fifteen, the eighteenth at one hundred and sixty-one, the nineteenth at one hundred and thirty-nine. Nineteen lines, thirteen hands, fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. Line one is a cook's and is still line one; line nineteen is a lift mechanic's, is the newest, and is the only promise on that board. Eight things in a ring binder, shut, the folded page among them shut with it. The master's fifth line empty, its drawer unopened. The Exchange did not sit and no Friday in this volume is a day it sits: last time a Wednesday twenty-one days back, next time a Wednesday seven days on. Book at fifty-one lines, telephone at seventy-two, the announcement made twenty-six times, the business nineteen years old. All four printed, none converted.**

*The no, as a record of nine seconds.* **Refused: a woman of thirty-five, who runs a place with no name over its door, which is not a company, not a guild, not a body, and has no list. Time: about ten past eleven on the Wednesday. Words of the refusal: nine seconds, and she said the count herself and said the reason would be a separate sentence. What the no was given for: no reason at the moment of the asking. What the person refusing owed the person refused: nothing, and is owed nothing, and is not a villain and has not been defeated and is not going to be turned. Whether the person refusing had asked a question that morning: no, and she has not asked one in about a month. Whether the person refused said anything raised in her voice: no. Whether the person refusing explained the no twice: no, and said once that it would not be explained again. Improved on: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody: no. Owner: the case, and the two of them.**

*The sentence, as a record.* **Speaker: the woman of sixty. Length: one sentence. Its four claims, in her order: that availability and membership are the same property of a room; that a room holding the first cannot keep the second; that the room she is sitting in holds the second and not the first; and that she has been in a room where that stopped being true, that it took about four minutes, and that she is not doing it again. Whether the sentence is true: yes, and four people in that room agreed with every word of it and about four said nothing, and the two groups have not met. Second statement, nine seconds, and in it she named the public body once: the only thing in this city that can make a quiet room into a public room is a piece of paper printed by somebody who has never been in the room; she named the body; she said she has never been inside it and is not going to be; she said she is a person with a job and that her job is to ask questions and that she was not going to ask one today. Where the word went: into a room, in a mouth, once. In a document: nowhere. On a surface: nowhere. In this record: not in the same words. Asked about afterwards by anybody: no. People in that room who had heard her use that word before: about four. People who had not: about four. Improved on: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody: no. Owner: her, and the room.**

*The reply, as a record.* **Said by: the woman of thirty-five, in about nine seconds, at the same table, to the same person. Points, in her order, and nobody improved on any of them: that the sentence is right and she has been saying some version of it for four years; that the sentence has just been used to refuse her a chair; that she has never used it to refuse anybody anything and is not going to start; that she is not going to argue in a room where she has just been treated fairly. Second statement, about nine seconds: that she is not going to call this unfair, because it is not unfair; that the room has nine people and a man of twenty-one at it and has never once asked either of them whether they were allowed to be there, and neither has she; and that the reason she is being refused is that she asked, which is the correct reason, and she would give the same answer. The woman's reply, in two words: yes, I would. Her last words at that table: that she knows the other woman would, and that this is why she is not leaving. Whether she left: no. Time she stayed afterwards: about fifty minutes. Further words spoken: about nine, none of them about the chair. Forgiven: no, and she has not asked for it and has not been offered it and is not going to be. Thanked: by anybody: no. Owner: the two of them.**

*The rule, as a record of an object that is not an object.* **What exists: a standing instruction at a table, that a person from outside the table is refused. Written down: no, on nothing. Agreed by vote: no, and no vote exists at that table and none has been called. Agreed by discussion: no, and it was not discussed. Source: one true sentence and one true answer to it. Whether it can be argued with by the person it refuses: no, and the reason is that the sentence is true. Whether it can be moved: no, and nothing about it is furniture. Whether it applies to the next person: yes. Whether anybody said it applies to the man of twenty-one who was at that table: no. Whether the man of twenty-seven who moved the chair looked at him: yes. Whether the man of twenty-one looked up: no. Whether either said anything: no. Whether either has said anything since: no, and neither is going to be asked. Improved on: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody: no. Owner: the room, and nobody in it, and it is worse than a chair would have been.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody was thanked and nobody was blamed, and the woman of thirty-five stayed and the woman of sixty stayed and the man of twenty-one stayed and all three of them have not spoken about it since. A page of paper with about eleven lines on it was not copied again and about eight of the nine copies are unfinished. A hardboard on two nails was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, and the master's fifth line is still blank. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 362.**
