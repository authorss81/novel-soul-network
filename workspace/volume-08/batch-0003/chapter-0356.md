# Chapter 356 — Nine Hundred Doors

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and thirty was a working Wednesday, and a man of about thirty-three was in four buildings for the sixty-ninth day of holding them, and **he has no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority behind him, and in sixty-nine days he has needed nothing from anybody and been given nothing, and nobody has ever asked him why he is doing it, and he has never given a reason.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Eleven attended, and the last of them finished at about six in the evening. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward for the whole day. The word was at nine and ten.

The room off a service road is four hundred and thirty-six days old and is warm, and four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

Four buildings. About nine hundred doors between them, in four districts, and the count is about nine hundred and not exactly nine hundred and the difference is four doors on a landing that are counted twice.

He has had them since the Thursday of week one hundred and twenty. There is no instrument. **There is no stamp on it and no reference and no date and no office behind it and no authority, and the four buildings belong to four different landlords, and none of the four has asked him to hold anything, and four people inside those buildings have said to him that he does not need to be there.**

He comes in at about half past seven. He has a bag. He has a bag because he has always had a bag, and the bag has a meter in it and a set of keys that are not a key to a door, and he does not use the meter in about four buildings out of four.

He does the ordinary work of a person who is in a building. A door that does not latch. A catch that has come off a fire door. A kettle in an office that has been left on. A window that has been painted shut by somebody in four years. He carries a chair up four flights for a man who cannot manage a bag and a staircase at once, and he carries it back down again, and the man does not pay him and did not ask him to and would not know what to do if he offered.

**Nobody has ever asked him to do any of it. He has never been given a list, a rota, an instruction, a key that is his, or a piece of paper of any kind, and about four hundred competent people in this city can write down the fact that he is doing it and about four hundred competent people in this city cannot make anybody do anything about it.**

He is not named in any document in this city. He is not thanked. About four people in four buildings have said thank you to him in sixty-nine days and he has not repeated any of them to anybody and has not been asked to.

---

The woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors came up the stairwell of the second building at about ten past four on the Wednesday with a list. Her job is to check about nine hundred doors, and about nine hundred is not a figure anybody in this case has ever put beside any other, and the two numbers this case does keep beside each other are written down in the record of the man with the tool bag and are not on this stair and were not mentioned.

She had a stair with nine of her doors on it. She had a pen and a list and about nine minutes before the lift she was not going to wait for.

He was at the fourth-floor landing with a door open and a floor of four people in offices on either side of it, and he had been there since about two, and he had the bag on the floor and his hands were under the top of the door and he was doing something with a screw that takes longer than it should.

She stopped. She looked at him for about four seconds and four people came out of the offices on that landing and looked at him too, and none of them said anything, and the four of them have not said anything since and do not know each other.

**She could not say anything useful. She has thought about it since, on a bus, for about four miles, and there is nothing, and the reason there is nothing is that she does not know what he is doing, whether he is doing it for anybody, whether anybody knows, and whether it is his, and there is no version of a sentence that gets a person out of a conversation by being pleased with him.**

So she said the small thing, and it took her about four seconds.

"**I have got nine of them on this stair and none of them is yours.**"

He said: "**No.**"

"**Right.**"

Then she went down one flight and checked two doors and came back up and the door was shut and latched and she wrote nothing about it on her list, because it was not one of hers and there is no column on her list for a door that is not hers.

He said: "**That catch has been on for four years and it is not the catch that is wrong.**"

"**All right.**"

"**I am not saying that to be interesting. I am saying it because you are going to write down that it shut and it shut for a reason and in about two months somebody is going to move that door and it will not shut.**"

She said: "**Then I will not write it down.**"

"**You should write it down. Not on yours.**"

**Nobody improved on it. She is not thanked and he is not thanked and neither of them said the other's name and neither of them asked the other a question about the other, and they have not spoken since and are not going to, and about four people on that landing have told about four other people that a man was at that door for about four hours and have not said anything else about it.**

---

Here is the change and it is small and it is the whole of what this chapter does.

For sixty-nine days a man of about thirty-three has been doing a thing that four hundred people in this city could write a rule about, and nobody has written one, and he has needed nothing and been given nothing, and the reason is not that nobody has noticed him.

**A woman of thirty-four with an ordinary job noticed him, on an ordinary Wednesday, in about nine seconds, at a landing, and there was nothing she could say to him that would have cost him anything, and so she said the smallest thing she had, and the smallest thing she had was a fact about her own list.**

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody named him. Nobody in this case knows what he would do if four buildings went at once on a Thursday, and he has not been asked, and he would have to be asked by somebody, and there is nobody whose job it is.

---

From about half past six the room off a service road was warm and nine people were in it, a cook of twenty-seven at the sink saying nothing at all and a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door. She was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds about a number on a door that had been repainted around, and it was not about any of it and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing and has been asked about nothing for about six weeks, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it.

A door on a fourth-floor landing in the second of the four districts was shut and latched at about twenty past four this afternoon by a man with a bag, and nobody wrote it on anybody's list, and there is no column on anybody's list for a door that is not theirs, and in about two months somebody is going to move that door.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines, the newest of them the only promise on it, and it is one hundred and thirty-two days old. The heating is on and costs more, four names on a lease cost more, and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*359. Wednesday of week 130, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is a man of about thirty-three, four buildings, about nine hundred doors, sixty-nine days with no stamp and no authority, one stairwell, one woman of thirty-four with a list, and the smallest thing she had.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward for the whole day and did not see the man of about thirty-three and was not asked about him. Four buildings, in four districts, about nine hundred doors between them, the figure being about nine hundred and not exactly nine hundred, and the difference is four doors on one landing that are counted twice. Held since the Thursday of week one hundred and twenty, being the sixty-ninth day on this Wednesday. Instrument: none. Stamp: none. Reference: none. Date: none. Authority behind it: none, and no office, and four landlords none of whom has asked him for anything. Times he has been given something by anybody in that time: none. Times he has needed something from anybody: none. Times he has been thanked by a person in those four buildings: about four. One stairwell in the second building, from about two in the afternoon to about twenty past four, a fourth-floor landing, a door and a screw, four people in offices on that landing, and about nine doors on the same stair on a list that is not his. One woman of about thirty-four on that stair at about ten past four, for about nine minutes, with a list and a pen. One room off a service road from about half past six, about nine people. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on and costs more and no figure of days off is carried. The room off a service road is four hundred and thirty-six days old. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward is four hundred and forty days in the rail, a third object, and is not in that room and is not in a stairwell. The hardboard: twelve lines three hundred and fifty-six, thirteen three hundred and seven, fourteen two hundred and seventy-two, fifteen two hundred and fifty-one, sixteen two hundred and twenty-six, seventeen two hundred and eight, eighteen one hundred and fifty-four, nineteen one hundred and thirty-two. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines in thirteen hands and fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and is still the first line and the newest is eleven words and is the only promise on it. Eight items in a ring binder, unopened, unread. The woman's page is unread. The master's fifth line is blank and the drawer was not opened. The Exchange did not sit on this day. Its last sitting was a Wednesday fourteen days ago and its next sitting is a Wednesday fourteen days from today, and the book is at fifty-one lines, the telephone at seventy-two, the count has been announced twenty-six times, and nineteen years is nineteen years. The four figures, printed and not converted: fifty-one, seventy-two, twenty-six, nineteen years.**

*The hold, as a record of a fact.* **Buildings: four, in four districts, four landlords, none of whom has asked him for anything and none of whom knows he is there beyond the days he is there. Doors: about nine hundred, the count being about nine hundred and not exactly nine hundred, and four doors on one landing are counted twice. Held since: the Thursday of week one hundred and twenty. Days held on this Wednesday: sixty-nine. Instrument: none. Stamp: none. Reference: none. Date: none. Authority: none, and no office exists that has given him any. List, rota, instruction, key that is his, piece of paper of any kind: none, and none has been given to him in sixty-nine days. Times he has needed anything from anybody: none. Times anything has been given to him by anybody: none. Times he has been asked to do any of it: none. Times he has given a reason: none. Times he has been asked for a reason: none. People in this city who could write down the fact that he is doing it: about four hundred. People who could make anybody do anything about it: none of them, in this case, and none of them has written anything. Named in a document: nowhere. Thanked: about four times in sixty-nine days, by people in four buildings, and not repeated by him to anybody. Owner: the case, and four buildings, and nobody.**

*The stairwell, as a record of hands and seconds.* **Place: a stairwell in the second building, fourth-floor landing, about two in the afternoon to about twenty past four. Door: open for about four hours, a screw at the top of it, and a catch that has been on it for four years. Person at the door: one, a man of about thirty-three, with a bag on the floor, hands under the top of the door. Bag contents used: a meter, in none of the four buildings. People who came out of the offices on that landing and looked: about four, and none of them said anything, and the four of them do not know each other. Person who came up the stair: one, a woman of about thirty-four, at about ten past four, with a list of about nine hundred doors and a pen and about nine minutes before a lift she did not wait for. Time she looked at him before she said anything: about four seconds. What she said, in about four seconds: that she has got nine of them on this stair and none of them is his. His reply, in one word: no. Her reply, in one word: right. Second exchange, in about nine seconds each: that the catch has been on for four years and is not the catch that is wrong; that she is not saying that to be interesting, and is saying it because she is going to write down that it shut and in about two months somebody is going to move that door and it will not shut; that then she will not write it down; and that she should write it down, and not on hers. Whether she wrote it down anywhere: no. Whether he is named on her list: no, and there is no column for a door that is not hers. Whether either asked the other a question: no. Whether either said the other's name: no. Improved on: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody: no. Owner: the two of them, and the landing, and they have not spoken since.**

*The small thing, as a record.* **Why nothing useful was available to her: that she does not know what he is doing, whether he is doing it for anybody, whether anybody knows, and whether it is his, and there is no version of a sentence that ends a conversation by being pleased with a person. What she said instead: the smallest fact she had, which was a fact about her own list and not about him. Whether it helped him: no. Whether it helped the door: yes, and the door latched, and the latch will come off again because the catch is not the catch that is wrong. Whether either of them thanked the other: no, and neither was thanked. Whether anybody else knows this happened: about four people on that landing know that a man was at that door for about four hours and do not know anything else about it, and the four of them have told about four other people and it stopped there. Improved on: by anybody: no. Owner: the case, and nobody in particular, and the door on the fourth-floor landing of the second building.**

*The Wednesday, as a record of an order.* **Arrival: about half past seven. In the first building: a catch off a fire door, and a kettle in an office that has been left on, and a window painted shut by somebody in about four years. In the second building: a fourth-floor landing, a door, and a screw, from about two in the afternoon until about twenty past four, and about four people from the offices on that landing came out and looked and said nothing. In the third building: a chair carried up four flights for a man who cannot manage a bag and a staircase at once, and carried down again, unpaid and unasked for. In the fourth building: nothing that anybody has recorded, and he was there for about an hour. Breaks: he has not taken one anybody saw. Meals on that day: he has said nothing to anybody about any. Times he was asked to do any of it: none. Times he was given anything: none. Whether he has a list: no. Whether he has a rota: no. Whether he has an instrument, a reference, a date, a stamp or a person behind him: none of the five, and the four buildings have four landlords and not one of them knows he is there beyond the days he is there. Owner: him, and the four buildings, and the bag.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody asked the man of about thirty-three to do anything and nobody gave him anything, and about four hundred people in this city could have written a rule about the sixty-ninth day and none of them has. Nobody has asked him what he would do if four buildings went at once on a Thursday and he has not been asked, and there is nobody whose job it is to ask him. A page of paper with about eleven lines on it was not copied again in any of those four buildings and about eight of the nine copies are not finished. A table of about nine people with a list did not sit and the sheet with nine words on it was not raised. A hardboard on two nails was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, and the master's fifth line is still blank. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 359.**
