# Chapter 358 — The Chair

The Monday morning of week one hundred and thirty-one was the morning a courier of twenty-one asked a table of about nine people for a chair, and **he was given one in about nine seconds, and nobody in that room can say who gave it, and by the end of the week four other people had asked for one and had been told there is one, which was true, and which is the whole of what it cost him.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Eleven attended, and the last of them finished at about six in the evening. He was at the repair shop in Lattice Ward for the whole day and was not in the room with the table and was not asked about the chair and does not know about it.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and forty-one days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The table is in a first-floor room above a row of shops in the second of the four districts, and it is the same room as every other room in this case: about nine chairs, a table, the same furniture, no name on the door, nothing on the wall, not a meeting, and no minute of it by anybody.

There were eight of the nine at it on the Monday morning. The ninth was not there and was not rung, and there is nobody whose job it is to ring anybody. The sheet of paper with nine words on it in a clerk of forty-four's hand was in the middle of the table and had been in the middle of that table at every meeting since, and nobody has put it anywhere else and nobody has taken it away.

**There is no procedure at that table for anything. There is no procedure for arriving late, and none for bringing something, and none for leaving early, and there is no form for any of those things and no person whose job it is to notice them happening.**

---

He came at about ten past eleven. He is twenty-one and he is a courier and he works in a cooperative of about eleven people who own their own hours, and he came on his own time and told nobody he was coming and nobody asked him to come.

He was in that room once before, in an earlier volume, and gave nothing to it and was not thanked and has not been asked about it since. He cut an arrangement of four years standing in the middle of a working night, with the agreement of the man on the other end of it, and nobody took a vote about him and nobody has forgiven him a word that is not in this case and nobody is going to and he has not asked.

Nobody in that room knew he was coming. About four of the nine had met him and four had not, and the four who had not were not told who he was and he did not offer, and a man of about twenty-seven who had not met him nodded.

---

He asked in about four seconds, from the doorway, to four people, and it was not built up to and it was not apologised for.

"**Can I sit at this table.**"

Nobody said no. About four people said something and what they said was not a yes either. A woman of about thirty-eight who has not spoken in a company training room for four Fridays said "**I suppose so**" in about two seconds and then said that she was sorry and that she should not have said it like that.

He said: "**That is fine. I asked.**"

Then a man of about twenty-seven, who has read and replaced the numbers on the doors in a building in the first of the four districts for four years, got up, went to the wall, and pulled a chair out from against it, and set it at the table, and went back to his own place, and sat down, and **did not say that anybody could sit there, and did not say that it was his, and has not been asked about it since and is not going to be asked about it, and he does not remember deciding to do it and has said so twice.**

The courier of twenty-one sat down. He was at that table for about forty minutes and he said about nine words in it and nobody improved on any of the nine and nobody asked him anything.

**Nobody in that room can say who gave him the chair. Four people in that room have tried to describe it since and the four descriptions do not agree about who moved, and the man who moved it is the only one who has not been asked, and he says he does not know.**

---

Nobody thanked him for asking. Nobody said that he had earned it. Nobody in that room mentioned the arrangement of four years that he cut in the middle of a night, and four of the nine know about it and about four do not, and the four who know did not raise it, and he did not raise it, and he has not asked anybody to raise it.

**He was a courier before he was anything else and he still is, and the nine words he said at that table were about a set of stairs and about a Sunday and about a company that has changed the hours on a route, and four people in that room did not understand one word of it and about four understood all of it and neither group said so.**

There is a thing about him that four people in that room have noticed and four have not, and it is not that he is owed anything. It is that he arrives in a room having already decided to leave, and it is visible, and four people have watched him do it and four have not, and the two groups have not met and are not going to. **He decided to leave at about ten past twelve and he was not asked to stay and he was not asked to go and he was not thanked on the way out, and the door is nine feet from the chair.**

A man of about forty-four who has been in that room for four years and who does not speak in it said one thing after he had gone, in about four seconds, to nobody.

"**I have been in this room for four years and I did not know until about ten past eleven this morning that a chair was a thing you could ask for.**"

A clerk of forty-four said one thing at about half past eleven, to nobody in particular, in four seconds, and nobody improved on it.

"**I have been in rooms for about eleven years and I have never once seen a chair arrive.**"

He said it to a wall. About four people were within about nine feet of him and not one of them has said anything about it since.

---

Here is the cost and it took the rest of the week and it is not a large thing.

By the Wednesday four other people had asked that table for a chair. A woman of about twenty-nine who answers a telephone four hours a day. A man of about fifty-eight who has not been in a room like this since an earlier volume. A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors. And a man of about twenty-one who works on a counter in a shop in the working Ashfields.

Each of them asked in about four seconds. Each of them was told there is one. **Each of them was told it by a person who had been in the room on the Monday, and each of them was told it in four seconds, and it was true, and the chair was against the wall where a chair goes, and the courier of twenty-one was at that table on the Wednesday as well and will be at it on the next Monday.**

Nobody refused any of the four anything. Nobody said no. Nobody said they were sorry and nobody said they were sorry, and four people in that room noticed the difference between being told there is one and being refused and four did not, and the two groups have not met.

**A chair is furniture. A post is not a chair. Nobody at that table has the standing of anybody, and there is no rule that anybody has written, and the reason four people were told there is one is that four people in that room saw four other people sit down.**

That is the cost and he has not been told about it and has not asked and is not going to be told by anybody in this case, and it is not a punishment and it is not a reward and it is the price of a thing that took about nine seconds.

---

At about half past six the room off a service road was warm and nine people were in it. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds a delivery that had gone to the wrong street, and it was not about any of it and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing and has been asked about nothing for about eight weeks, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it.

The hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines and the newest of them is the only promise on it and it is one hundred and thirty-seven days old. The heating is on and costs more and four names on a lease cost more and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*361. Monday of week 131, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is a table of about nine people, a courier of twenty-one, one ask of about four seconds, one chair pulled off a wall in nine seconds, and four people told there is one.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Work of the day: eleven jobs, all attended, the last finished about six, and no job of his after that. Two rooms. The first is a first-floor office over a shopfront in the second of the four districts, with the same furniture as every other room in this case, nothing on the door, nothing on the walls, no officer of anything in it, and eight of a possible nine present; the one who was missing was not telephoned because no post exists whose business it would be. Nine words on a sheet, in a clerk's hand, sat in the middle of the table and have sat in the middle of it every time since. The second is a room off a service road from about half past six with nine people in it. A man of twenty-one in the first room for about forty minutes, on his own time, unannounced. Nine seconds spent moving one chair. Four seconds spent saying one sentence to a wall, with about four people close enough to hear it. By the middle of the week, four other requests for the same chair, answered in four seconds each, all four answers accurate and none of them a refusal. The rate: nine and ten, uncorrected. Heating: on, dearer than it was, four names on the lease dearer still, and a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid and nobody is going to. Age of the room off the service road: four hundred and forty-one days. Age of the card in its rail in Lattice Ward: four hundred and forty-five days; a different object, in a different building, and the two have never shared a room. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines at three hundred and sixty-one days, the thirteenth at three hundred and twelve, the fourteenth at two hundred and seventy-seven, the fifteenth at two hundred and fifty-six, the sixteenth at two hundred and thirty-one, the seventeenth at two hundred and thirteen, the eighteenth at one hundred and fifty-nine, the nineteenth at one hundred and thirty-seven. Nineteen lines, thirteen hands, fourteen names; nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off. Line one belongs to a cook and has never moved. Line nineteen is the newest, is a lift mechanic's, and is the only promise on that board. Eight things in a ring binder, shut. The folded page at the bottom of it is shut. The fifth line of the master's own hand is empty and its drawer stayed shut. The Exchange did not sit, and no Friday in this volume is a day it sits: last time was a Wednesday nineteen days back, next time is a Wednesday nine days on. Its book stands at fifty-one lines, its telephone at seventy-two, the announcement has been made twenty-six times, and the whole business is nineteen years old. All four of those printed here, none of them turned into another.**

*The ask, as a record.* **Asker: a man of twenty-one, a courier, a worker in a cooperative of about eleven people who own their own hours. Where: in the doorway of a first-floor room above a row of shops in the second of the four districts. Time: about ten past eleven on the Monday morning. Words used: five, in about four seconds: can I sit at this table. Preparation: none, and he told nobody he was coming and nobody asked him to come. Answer from a woman of about thirty-eight, in about two seconds: I suppose so, followed by an apology for how she had said it. His answer to that, in five words: that this is fine and that he asked. Whether anybody said yes: no. Whether anybody said no: no. Whether anybody made a decision: nobody, and four people have tried to describe what happened and the four descriptions do not agree about who moved. What happened instead: a man of about twenty-seven got up, went to the wall, pulled a chair out, set it at the table, went back to his place and sat down, and said nothing. Length of the whole thing: about nine seconds. Improved on: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody: no. Owner: the case, and the room, and a chair.**

*The chair, as a record of an object.* **Object: one chair, from against the wall of a first-floor room. Moved by: a man of about twenty-seven who has read and replaced the numbers on the doors in a building in the first of the four districts for four years. Time: about ten past eleven. Duration of the moving: about nine seconds. Words said while moving it: none. Decision recorded before moving it: none, and he has said twice since that he does not remember deciding to. Procedure at that table for a chair: none, and a chair is furniture and a post is not a chair and no post exists at that table. Standing of anybody at that table: none. Times anybody in that room has been told who gave the chair: nobody can say, and the four accounts differ, and the man who moved it is the only one who has not been asked and has said he does not know. Times the courier of twenty-one has been at that table since: every meeting, and nobody has asked him to come to any of them. Times he has said anything about how he came to be there: none. Owner: nobody, and the room, and that is the whole of the finding.**

*The four, as a record of what a yes costs.* **Number: four. Who: a woman of about twenty-nine who answers a telephone four hours a day; a man of about fifty-eight who has not been in a room like that since an earlier volume; a woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors; a man of about twenty-one who works on a counter in the working Ashfields. When: by the Wednesday of the same week. Each ask: about four seconds, in a room with no procedure for one. Each answer: that there is one, in about four seconds, given by a person who had been in the room on the Monday. Whether each answer was true: yes. Whether any of the four was refused anything: no. Whether any of the four was told they were sorry: no. Whether the chair was free on the Wednesday: no, and it was where a chair goes and the courier of twenty-one was at that table. People in the room who noticed the difference between being told there is one and being refused: about four. People who did not: about four. Improved on: by nobody. Owner: the four, and the room, and nobody in particular.**

*The table, as a record of a room.* **Kind: a first-floor room above a row of shops in the second of the four districts. Chairs: about nine. Table: one. Name on the door: none. Anything on the walls: none. Chair at the top: none. Terms of reference: none. Quorum: none. Minute: none, by anybody, now or in prospect. Procedures for arriving late, for bringing something, or for leaving early: none, and no form for any of them and no post whose business it would be to notice. Sheet in the middle of it: one, nine words in a clerk's hand, no heading, no date, no reference and no signature, and it has been at every meeting since it was written. Present on the Monday: eight of about nine. Absent: one, unringed. Key: none of the nine keys this case has ever talked about is in that room, and eight of the nine have never existed. Person whose job it is to notice a chair: nobody. Owner: the eight, and the table, and the chair, and nobody has been in charge of any of the three at any point.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody was thanked and nobody was blamed. An arrangement of four years standing that a man of twenty-one cut in the middle of a night with the agreement of the man on the other end of it was not raised, not referred to, and not forgiven, and no vote was taken about him then or now. A page of paper with about eleven lines on it was not copied again in any room and about eight of the nine copies are not finished. A man of about thirty-three who has held four buildings for seventy-four days was not mentioned by anybody at that table. A hardboard on two nails was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, and the master's fifth line is still blank. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 361.**
