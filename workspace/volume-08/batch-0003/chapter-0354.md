# Chapter 354 — The First Friday

The Friday evening of week one hundred and twenty-nine was the first Friday in about a month on which a company training room in Crown Terrace ran a floor, and eleven people were in it, and a woman of thirty-six with a lanyard on, who works there, called the run from the front of the floor, and **it finished about four seconds early, and nobody in that room said the word, and nobody counted the four seconds, and about four people noticed and four did not, and the four seconds are settled for this Friday and are not going to be opened.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Eleven attended, and the last of them finished at about six in the evening, and there are no jobs of his after about six on a Friday.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and thirty-one days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The room has nine folding chairs in it and eleven people, and a laminated sheet on a post, and the sheet has been on that post for four years and nobody has ever taken it down and nobody has ever written on it and it is the company's copy of a run sheet and it is about eleven lines long and it says what a run is and who calls it and not one of those things changed.

Seven of the eleven are on the company's list, and the list is the company's, and it has seven names on it, and the other four come, and nobody has ever asked any of them why they come, and nobody in that room has ever wondered out loud, and that has been the arrangement for four years.

The room holds about eleven people in nine chairs on a Friday and has held them on every Friday it has been used, and the arithmetic has never once been right and nobody has ever mentioned it. Two of them come late and have come late on every one of them, and the two of them have never sat together.

The floor is the second floor of a building in Crown Terrace and has a corridor, a fire door at each end, a stair, and about nine doors that are not opened during the run. It is not a place anybody works in. The training is bought by a company that does not have anybody in that room on its own staff list other than the woman who calls it, and nobody in that room has ever been asked what the training is for.

---

The run is a walk-through. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by one person, and everybody does the thing they did in the last one, and it is a run and not a drill and every person in that room would tell you it is a run and is not a drill.

Nobody read a number off the post. Nobody was handed anything. Nobody counted for anybody and nobody counted with anybody, and the sheet on the post is about eleven lines long and none of the eleven is a number that anybody in that room could use this evening.

It began at about half past seven. **A woman of thirty-four who has been in that room for two years and who has not spoken in it for a long time did the second half of it properly, without being told, and has not said that she did it and is not going to be asked.** A man of about twenty-nine who comes on a Friday and who has not spoken in that room in four years sat down at the right moment without being told either, and about four people noticed that and four did not, and the two groups have not met and are not going to.

**Nobody in that room has ever been told why the run is nine minutes long. Nobody has ever asked.** The sheet on the post does not say and the woman who calls it has never been asked to say and did not say on this evening, and four people in that room have assumed the number was chosen on purpose and about four have assumed it was not, and the two assumptions have never met in that room.

It finished at about forty past. **It finished about four seconds early, and the four seconds were not counted by anybody, and no clock in that room is the company's, and the four seconds have not been counted since and are not going to be.**

Nobody said the word. Nobody said any word. About nine people stood where they had stopped for about nine seconds and four of them knew why the floor was finished and four of them did not, and neither group has spoken to the other about it, then or since.

About four minutes after the floor was finished somebody asked the woman of thirty-six a practical question about a form that had gone missing in a building on the other side of the city, and she answered it practically, in nine seconds, and the room became a room again. **That exchange is the only other thing that happened in that room on that Friday, and it is recorded here because four people have since described the four seconds as the whole of the evening and it was not.** The four seconds went into the same place the missing form went, which is nowhere that anybody wrote down.

A man of about thirty-one who comes on a Friday and who works in a depot at the far end of the city said one other thing, to four people, in nine seconds, and it was about how long a person can stand in a stairwell before somebody comes, and four people in that room understood every word of it and four did not, and he has not said one word about any of it since and nobody has asked him.

---

A man of fifty-eight who has been in that room for four years and who comes for the sake of coming said the standing sentence, at the back, in the way a person says a thing they have said before.

"**About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard.**"

Nobody improved on it and nobody asked him anything about it and four people in that room had heard him say it in about four other weeks in four years.

---

Then the woman of thirty-six said the thing she said, and it took her about four seconds, and it is the whole of what she said in the second half hour of that Friday.

"**I am not going to call that a finding.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I have said that before, in a room in this building, to four people, and nothing came of it then either, and I am not going to say it again after this, and I would like everybody in here to know that I said it once and that it was enough.**"

**Nobody improved on it. She did not improve on it and four people in that room did not improve on it and four were not in the room.**

**She has said that before and nobody in that room has asked her where, and she was not asked, and she did not say what the reason was for saying it a second time, and four people in that room wanted to ask her and four did not and the two groups did not meet.**

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody read a number off the post. **Nothing was written on the sheet, and the sheet was not taken off the post, and the four seconds are not on any surface in this city, and there is no form and no register and no line on any hardboard, and nothing in this city is going to say what the four seconds were.**

They were in the room for about ninety minutes afterwards and four of the eleven stayed and the rest went at about nine. Half of the eleven have been in that room on every Friday for four years and the other half for about two, and neither half knows it is a half, and four people have noticed and four have not, and the two groups have not met.

Nobody in that room improved on anything that happened in it. Nobody said one word about the four seconds to anybody outside it, and the two people who were closest to them have not said one word to each other about them, and they sit next to each other on a Friday and have for four years.

---

At about half past six the room off a service road was warm and nine people were in it. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door and was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds about a bin on the corner that had been moved to the other side of the road, and it was not about any of it and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing and has been asked about nothing for about five weeks and two days, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it.

A hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines, the newest of them the only promise on it, and it is one hundred and twenty-seven days old. The heating is on and costs more, four names on a lease cost more, and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

Questions asked of her about the four seconds: none. Replies given to anybody: none, and she gave none. Is it settled: for this Friday, yes, and settled is not the same as understood and the two words have not been used about it in the same breath by anybody. Improved on: by nobody. Times he has said it in this case: about five, in four years, in four different weeks. Whether it is a rate: it is not a rate and it is not on a form and it is not a figure anybody may quote, and the number on the back of one person's own card is not this number and is not on any sheet. Times said on the two of these Fridays: one. Improved on: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody: no. Owner: the case, and the man, and the room, and about eleven people a week in this city who are not in this case.**

Floor: a corridor, a fire door at each end, a stair, and about nine doors that stay shut during a run. Who calls the run: one woman of thirty-six with a lanyard on who works there. Register of attendance: none. Form: none. Correction made to that room in about four years: the substitution of a chair. Owner: the company, and the sheet, and the nine chairs, and none of the three is anybody's in the sense the case uses the word.**

*What the day did not touch.* **That room is a floor, nine folding chairs, a sheet on a post, about two and a half hours, eleven people, a woman of thirty-six who called the run from the front, and a man of fifty-eight who came for the sake of coming. The four seconds are on no surface, are on nobody's sheet, and are in nobody's mouth, and the woman of thirty-six said once that she was not going to call them a finding and has not said it again. Nine folding chairs held about eleven people for about two and a half hours and the difference is not a fault and is not on a form. A page of paper with about eleven lines on it in four hands was not in that room and was not discussed in it. A man of about thirty-three who has held four buildings since the Thursday of week one hundred and twenty was not in that room and nobody in it mentioned him.
