# Chapter 352 — A Document-Shaped Nothing

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and twenty-nine was the first Wednesday in about a month on which eight of about nine people in about nine rooms in about four districts were in one room with a list, and **what the first month of it produced was not a recommendation but nine words said out loud by a woman whose job is to ask questions and who asked none of them, and nobody improved on the nine words, and then a clerk of forty-four wrote them on a sheet of paper, and a sheet of paper with nothing in it turns out to be harder to argue with than an opinion.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date. Eleven attended, and the last of them finished at about six in the evening. He was in one building in the second of the four districts for about an hour and ten minutes in the middle of the morning and was at his own counter for the rest of the day.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and twenty-nine days old and is warm, and four people came in on the Friday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The room is in a first-floor office above a row of shops and it is the same room as every other room in this case: about nine chairs, a table, the same furniture, no name on the door, nothing on the wall, not a meeting, and no minute of it by anybody.

Eight came. The ninth did not come and nobody rang him, and there is nobody in that room whose job it is to ring anybody.

Four of the eight have been in this case for four years and four have been in it for two. One of the eight, a man of about forty-one in an ordinary coat with an ordinary job, did not know until the Friday before this that he was one of nine, because a woman of about thirty-eight told him on a stair and gave him a building and a morning and did not say anything else and has not been asked why she did it.

Nobody at the top. No terms of reference. No quorum. No minute. **A list exists, and four people in that room have seen it, and nobody in that room knows who keeps it, and it was on the table from about ten until about half past eleven and went away with the woman who brought it and no copy of it exists.**

---

Here is what the eight brought, and it took about forty minutes and it is worth having in one place because it is the whole of what a month of this produced before the nine words.

The woman of sixty whose job is to ask questions brought nothing and had been asked to bring nothing.

A lead repairer of forty-five brought a list of about eleven sites where a thing that worked in one building had never been tried in another. The list is correct. Four of the eleven have been tried, one of those four did not work, and she has written down why it did not work and nobody has read that part.

A woman of about forty-one on the first floor of an office building, who has typed about four hundred of a kind of line in eleven years, brought a sentence she had said out loud to a person on a telephone and had watched the telephone not help.

A man of about forty-one brought a piece of paper of his own with a form on it and a line on the form that says to use the tenant of the premises below. He said, in nine seconds, that he wrote the answer on that line four years ago, that the answer was correct, and that it has cost somebody an evening. He did not say whose. Four people in that room looked at him and did not ask.

A woman of about thirty-eight who has been in a company training room for two years and has not spoken in it for a long time brought nothing, said nothing, and was not asked.

A man of about twenty-seven who lives in a building in the first of the four districts, and who has read and replaced the numbers on the doors in it for four years, brought a piece of card with nine door numbers on it in a row. The card is the reason he came and not the reason he stayed.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings in four districts brought a count of about nine hundred doors a person cannot get out of. The count is hers. It is not a rate and it is not on a form.

A clerk of forty-four brought the list and nothing else, and put the list face down on the table at ten, and it stayed face down for an hour and a half.

---

At about half past ten the woman of sixty read the eight sheets, seven of them paper and one of them a card and two of the seven blank, and she did it in about nine minutes, and she did not ask a single question, and four people in that room noticed that she was not asking any and four did not, and the two groups did not meet.

Then she said the finding. It took her about nine seconds and it is nine words long and it is not a recommendation and it is not a plan and it is not a thing anybody can do on Monday.

"**Nine of us are doing the same work alone.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

A man of about twenty-seven said: "**That is not a recommendation.**"

"**No.**"

"**What do we do with it.**"

"**I have not got that and I am not the person who has got that.**"

Nobody improved on it. Not one of the eight improved on it, and four of them have been asked since by people who were not in that room, and four of them have given the nine words exactly as she said them and not one word more, and the two of them who have given it with a sentence in front of it have been corrected by the other two and have dropped the sentence.

Nobody thanked her. She is owed nothing and holds no post and did not ask for anything and has not been thanked, and her job on the Thursday is unchanged.

---

Then a clerk of forty-four took a sheet of paper out of a folder and wrote the nine words on it in her own hand.

She wrote no heading. She wrote no date and no reference and no name and no signature, and she did not number it, and she wrote it in the middle of the sheet with about four inches of paper above it and about four inches below, and she put it in the middle of the table face up and sat down again.

A man of about twenty-seven read it standing up, and put it back down, and did not sit.

"**This is not a recommendation.**"

"**No.**"

"**It is a sheet of paper.**"

"**It is.**"

"**Then what is it.**"

The clerk of forty-four said: "**It is the only thing anybody has written down. I have been in rooms for about eleven years and there is nothing else in this one, and I am not going to leave it off the table because it does not say anything.**"

Nobody turned it over. Nobody put it in a drawer. Nobody threw it out, and nobody suggested throwing it out, and the sheet was in the middle of that table from about twenty to eleven twenty with four people looking at it and four not.

---

Here is the change and it is small and it is the whole of what the chapter is about.

An opinion can be argued with. Nine people in a room can take nine words apart over about forty minutes and go home with nothing, and every one of them can go home having done the work. **A sheet of paper with nine words on it in a clerk's hand cannot be argued with. The only two things anybody can do with it are agree with it and disagree with it, and disagreeing with it requires somebody to say the nine words first, and four people in that room would rather not say the nine words first, and so the sheet stayed in the middle of the table and the meeting ended around it and nobody had decided anything and everybody had been in the room.**

That is a document-shaped nothing. It is shaped exactly like a finding and it is not one, and the difference between the two is that a finding has somebody at the end of it who is going to do something about it, and there is nobody at the end of this one, and the reason there is nobody is that nobody in that room could name a person who was not already in the room.

Nobody improved on that either, and four people in that room have said since that it was a wasted morning, and about four have said that it was the first morning anybody had put anything down at all, and the two are both correct and neither of them is in a record.

---

At about half past six the room off a service road was warm and nine people were in it. A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door, and she was not asked anything and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds about a shop that had changed its hours on a Sunday, and it was not about any of it and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing, and has been asked about nothing for about five weeks, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it.

A hardboard on two nails carries nineteen lines, the newest of them the only promise on it, and it is one hundred and twenty-five days old. The heating is on and costs more, four names on a lease cost more, and the fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
