# Chapter 393 — A Man In A Yard, And A Woman Who Was Not Asked

On the Thursday of week one hundred and forty-two a man of about thirty-three stood in the yard of a converted shop unit off a service road in the Lower Wards for about twenty minutes and was not asked for anything, and **he said nothing at all, nobody spoke to him, he was not let in and he was not turned away, and the woman whose Thursday it is was four miles away in a workroom and nothing about the Thursday was spent.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past eight until about four and then walked to the service road and back, and he passed a man in a yard at about half past two and neither of them said anything.

His word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is five hundred and twenty-one days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature, and nobody asked them anything.

---

**The yard is about nine paces deep and about four wide and there is a door on the left of it with four words in chalk above it and a plate outside with a letting agent's telephone number and no name. About four people work in and around that building and about four more pass through the yard on a working day, and none of the four who pass through it has ever been asked what they are doing in it.**

**Nothing happened in that yard on the Thursday. That is the whole of the yard.**

---

**He was there from about half past two until about ten to three. He had no bag and nothing in his hands. He stood at the wall opposite the door with his weight on one leg and the other one not doing what the other one was doing.**

Nobody came out of the door while he was there. Two people came in through the yard from the far end at about ten to three, went into the door, and came out again at about a quarter past three, and neither of them looked at him and neither of them said anything to him, and about four people in that building have since said that there was a man standing in that yard on the Thursday afternoon, and about four have not.

**He has held four buildings and about nine hundred doors for one hundred and fifty-four days, which is twenty-two weeks, with no stamp and no reference and no date and nobody's authority behind him, and in that time he has needed nothing from anybody and been given nothing by anybody. Nobody in this case has asked him to be in that yard. Nobody has said he was doing it for the room and he has not said so.**

---

**A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was not asked about anything on the Thursday, and has not been asked about anything since she said one half of a sentence to him at a counter in a shop in Lattice Ward in an earlier week, and about four people in this case have noticed that she has said nothing since and about four have not noticed, and nobody has asked her and nobody is going to.**

A first-year of nineteen was at a counter in Lattice Ward at about four in the afternoon and said one thing, and it was shorter than about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**I have been in nine rooms in four districts since the summer and in two of them this week somebody said no out loud and in one of them somebody would not take a line, and there is no list anywhere of any of that, and I have decided to go on being the one who is not asked about it.**"

Nobody asked her. Nobody has said since that she is careful or that she is being left out, and she has not said which of the two she thinks it is, and about four people in that shop have said that a person who says no on purpose is making a decision and about four have said that deciding is not the same as doing.

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields was in that room at about five and said one thing, and nobody said it back to her.

"**There is a man standing in a yard in the Lower Wards on a Thursday afternoon and he has been there twenty minutes and nobody has asked him one question, and I have been in nine rooms in four months and nobody has asked me one either, and I would like it recorded that those two facts have been in this city at the same time.**"

Nobody recorded it. Nobody has said since that the two facts belong together, and she has not said it again, and about four people in that room have said that being unasked is a thing that happens to about four people at once and about four have said nothing.

---

**Nobody in that yard spoke to him and he spoke to nobody, and nobody thanked him for standing in it, and nobody has since said that he was watching the door, and he has not said that is what he was doing.**

**Nobody in that building has asked whether the man in the yard knows what is on the back of a sheet of paper, and nobody in this case has asked him, and he has not mentioned it, and it has been in that building's reach for about a week and has not been in his mouth.**

**The four rooms in four districts were four rooms on that Thursday. One of them said no on Monday in about nine seconds and one of them wrote that down on Wednesday in a different district and neither of those two knows about the other, and about four hundred competent people in this city can each put the printed paragraph in front of anybody and not one of them has anywhere to put an objection.**

---

Half past six, and about nine of them in a warm room off a service road, and a cook of twenty-seven at a sink with her hands in the water and not a word out of her. The chair nearest the door had a woman of about thirty in it, and nobody in that room put a question to her or said thank you to her or said sorry to her. She comes for the temperature. The temperature costs more than it did. Both of those are right.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that warm room and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for one hundred and twenty-seven days, which is eighteen weeks and a day, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.

A woman of about twenty-four who works as a resonance nurse at a mobile clinic was not in that room on the Thursday and was not in any other room, and nobody in this case has asked her about any of it and nobody is going to.

Nineteen lines are on a hardboard on two nails in that room, and the newest is eleven words about the middle written by a man of twenty-nine, and it is the one promise on that board, and it is two hundred and seventeen days old, and twenty minutes in a yard has not made it more or less true.

The heating is on. It costs more. Four names on a lease cost more than they did. A fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody, is not resolved, and is not going to be.

**A man stood in a yard for twenty minutes on the Thursday of the last Thursday anybody in this case has written down and said nothing and was asked nothing and was let in by nobody and turned away by nobody, and a woman who checks about nine hundred doors said nothing and was asked nothing, and the last Thursday anybody in this case has written down is not going to be a day either of them said anything on.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The yard, as a record of twenty minutes with nothing in them.* **Yard: one, about nine paces by about four, against the wall opposite a door that has four words in chalk above it. Man in it: one, of about thirty-three, present from about half past two to about ten to three, with no bag and nothing in his hands. He had been in that yard before: about four times, and this case has no record of when and nobody in this case has asked. What he was doing: standing, and there is nothing else, and nobody in this case has established a second reading of it and he has not offered one. Whether anybody in that building knows he was there: two people, and both were inside, and neither saw him. Whether anybody has since said he was watching the door: no, and he has not said it either. Whether he has ever been asked why he stands in yards: no. Whether he was thanked: no. Whether he was asked for anything on this day or on any day since he began holding four buildings: no, and he has needed nothing and been given nothing in one hundred and fifty-four days of doing it. Whether the two facts fit on a page: no page exists and nobody has proposed one. Owner: him, and the yard, and nobody.**

*The two rooms, as a record of two refusals that have not met.* **First: a converted shop unit off a service road in the Lower Wards, Monday morning, about nine seconds, by word, no reason given, about five present, about four of that room's nine not yet in it. Second: the same sentence, written down on the Wednesday on a sheet of about nine lines in a room in a building in the second of the four districts, in about four seconds, by one person at the request of four others, at the end of three people who were in the first room on the Monday. Whether the two know about each other: the first room does not know it was written down, and the second room does not know it was said in a room four hundred yards away, and the man who said it has not been told either of the two. Whether the printed paragraph on the back of the page affects either: no, and one of its printed sentences says that an arrangement formed by the members of a room is not a bar to that room being merged with another one. Times this refusal has crossed a building boundary: twice, both on one afternoon, both about a man and a woman who were in the first room. Times it has crossed a district: none. Owner: two rooms, and a sheet, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **A man who has held four buildings and about nine hundred doors for twenty-two weeks stood in a yard for twenty minutes and was not asked one question, and nobody in this case has said that he was waiting for anything. A woman who checks about nine hundred doors said nothing and was asked nothing and the half of a sentence she said in an earlier week has not been finished and has not been asked for. Nothing was merged, nothing was adopted, nothing was signed, and the nine people copying the front of a page of paper with about eleven lines in it are twelve weeks and three days into it with about eight of the nine unfinished and the first disagreement in its fourth line still not found. A hardboard on two nails carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, the card in a rail did not move, a drawer stayed shut, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Thursday. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 396.**
