# Chapter 400 — A Line In A Book, A Rota Handed Over, And Four Rooms Still Four

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and forty-eight was the twenty-sixth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and **the book opened, and it grew by one line because a person said a thing to her face in about four seconds, and it stood at fifty-three lines at the end of the morning, the tin stood at seventy-three, the count was thirty-one of which twenty-six correspond, nobody thanked her, nobody stood up, the ninth chair was against the wall with its back to the room at the start of the morning and against the wall at the end of it, and the four rooms in four districts are four rooms.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, then four miles away for about three hours on a rota he did not write, and then at a counter until about six. He has never been in the room above a line and knows none of what happened there.

His word was at nine and ten, uncorrected, told to nobody, and it has been said out loud in this case once in eight volumes and once is what it is going to stay at.

The room off a service road is five hundred and sixty-two days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature, and nobody asked them anything.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

**There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it, and it did not move in about four hours and nobody in that room looked at it more than once. There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, shut for about forty minutes of it and open for about four.**

There were about eleven people, and there was a young man of twenty-one who has climbed those stairs since he was seventeen and a young woman of twenty-one who has climbed them since she was fifteen and about five others who this morning are not being described.

**She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.**

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece.

The first was a person who wanted to know whether a book that has not grown for two sittings is a thing anybody can be worried about, and she said that it is a book, and that it grows when somebody speaks to her, and that she is not going to say anything else about it, and she wrote nothing.

The second was a man of about forty-seven who said a thing twice in a different order and she wrote nothing, and he said thank you and she said yes.

The third was a person who asked her what a word meant and she told him in about nine seconds and wrote nothing.

The fourth was the man of about forty-four who put the ask a long time back and has not put it since. He was in that room and he did not put it and she did not offer it and nobody in that room wanted anything offered, and about four people in that room noticed that and about four did not.

The fifth was nothing anybody has reported.

**The sixth was the one that is in this case. A man of about fifty-four who works nights at a depot came up those stairs that morning, and he had been in a room in the working Ashfields in the week before last, and he had said something in it that was wrong, and he had worked out afterwards that he had said it wrong, and he said the second part to her face, in about four seconds, and it was not a request.**

"**I said a thing out loud in a room and I worked out afterwards that I had said it wrong, and I have told two people that I got it wrong, and nobody has said to me that getting it wrong counts for anything, and I have come up here to say it to a third person, and that is the whole of why I am here.**"

She wrote it. It took her about four seconds and she wrote it in the fourth column and she did not ask him a question and he did not say anything else, and about four people in that room did not hear it and about four said nothing, and neither group has said anything about it since.

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked him which room, and nobody asked him what he had said that was wrong, and he has not said, and there is no name against his line, and nothing in that book has ever carried a name.

**The book was at fifty-two lines when the morning began. It is at fifty-three now. It grew by one, and it grew because somebody said a thing to her face, which is the only way it has ever grown and the only way anybody in this case can say that it grows.**

---

**No number was given into the tin this morning. The tin stands at seventy-three, and it stood at seventy-three at the last sitting four weeks ago, and it stood at seventy-three at the one before that, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved, and nobody in that room wanted a number.**

The count came out as it comes out at every sitting. It is thirty-one. Twenty-six of them are the Wednesdays this book has sat on, and five are older than the book, and the five are on a sheet she has never shown anybody and did not show this morning either.

The book is at fifty-three lines. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced thirty-one times. The whole business is nineteen years old. **Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and every one of the four is the whole of itself, and none was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and was not given as one.**

Nobody thanked her. Nobody said anything about the two sittings before this one that did not open, and she has not said anything about them, and nothing about that has ever been said in that room.

---

**The ask is two hundred and fifty-two days old, which is thirty-six weeks, counted from the day it was asked at the seventeenth sitting and not from any sitting since.** Nobody in that room asked her for that rule. She has said six times that the last time she says it is the last time, and she has kept that six times, and this morning nothing was put in the space it left and nothing was offered in its place and nobody in that room wanted anything offered.

---

**Four miles from that landing, in a building of nine flats over a canteen in the Lower Wards, a maintenance rota for that building came down off a nail at about four in the afternoon and changed hands.**

The rota is a sheet of paper with five names on it in five hands, and it covers a week that has already started, and about four of the five names on it are people who do not know each other. He has been one of the five for about four months and did not write it and was not asked to write it, and the name the rota gives as the one who takes it from him is a man of about fifty-six who works for a contractor and has been inside four buildings in the last month that he was not sent into, and nobody has asked him what he was doing in any of them.

He put the sheet down on a table in that building and said, and it took him about nine seconds:

"**I am handing this over and I am not going to say why I do not want it, because the reason is that I have watched him be in four rooms he was not sent into and I have not worked out a reason to say out loud, and a reason I cannot say out loud is not a reason, and I would rather hand it over than keep saying nothing about it. That is all. Take it.**"

**The man of about fifty-six took it. Nobody thanked either of them, and nobody asked him to explain the four rooms, and he has not explained them, and nobody in that building has since said that the man of twenty-two was right about him or that he was wrong about him, and about four people in that building have said that handing something over is not the same as saying a thing and about four have said it is.**

**Everything he has made in seven volumes was a refusal, a list on nothing, a door, a stroke, a signature, a knot, a number of words announced in advance, an appointment with no date on it, or a line somebody else wrote. This is the first of them that is a decision, and the cost of it is a building looked after from tomorrow by a man he does not trust, and he cannot write that down in a load book because a load book is about loads, and he did not improve on it and nobody did and nobody was thanked.**

---

The first floor above a row of shops in Saltmarket has about nine chairs in it, a table, and a sheet of paper with nine words on it face up in the middle of the table, and on the Wednesday of week one hundred and forty-eight at about half past ten a man of about thirty who delivers groceries in that district said the fourth of the four things.

"**We are not going to be joined to anything. I have got a round to do and I am not going to be late for it for a room. That is the whole of it.**"

Nobody asked him why. Nobody thanked him, and nobody said it was right and nobody said it was wrong, and he went down the stairs and out and was back on his round at about a quarter past ten.

**Four rooms, and four buildings, and four districts, and four weeks, and two of the four said nothing to the other three. The other two said one thing to one person each, and neither of those two people is in a position to act, and neither has been asked. The first was said on a Monday in week one hundred and forty-two, the second on a Monday in week one hundred and forty-three, the third on a Monday in week one hundred and forty-four, and the fourth on a Wednesday in week one hundred and forty-eight, and none of the four has been written down and none of the four has been in the same room as any other of the four.**

**The four rooms survive because they are four rooms and not one. There is no list of them, no office, no name on a door, and no key beyond the two that exist. Nine are wanted and there are two, and the first of the nine still cannot be asked back by anybody.**

---

About nine people came in at half past six into a warm room off a service road, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in a ring binder and is unread and was not read this evening.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that warm room and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for one hundred and forty-eight days, which is twenty-one weeks and a day, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.

A man of about thirty-three, holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for one hundred and ninety-five days, which is twenty-seven weeks and six days, with no stamp and no reference and no date and nobody's authority behind him, was in that warm room for about ten minutes on the last evening anybody in this case has written down and said nothing at all, and nobody asked him for anything, and he has needed nothing and been given nothing in twenty-seven weeks and six days of doing it, and nobody has thanked him.

Two nails and a hardboard and the same nineteen lines, and the newest of the nineteen is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board, and it is two hundred and fifty-eight days old, and a line went into a book in a room four miles away on the morning of this Wednesday and has not made it more or less true.

The heating is on and costs more. Four names on a lease cost more than they did. A fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved and is not going to be resolved by anything that happened that day or by anybody in it.

**Four rooms said no in four buildings in four weeks and two of them never told the other two, a book opened and grew by one line because a man who had been wrong said so to a woman's face, a maintenance rota he did not write went to a man he does not trust, a line was held on the last day of it and nobody in this case can say by what, and nobody thanked anybody.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
