# Chapter 399 — Four Minutes In A Workroom, And A Book That Stayed Shut

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and forty-four was the twenty-fifth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and **the book did not open, it stood at fifty-two lines where it stood four weeks ago, the count was announced at thirty of which twenty-five correspond, the tin was at seventy-three, the ninth chair was against the wall with its back to the room at the start of the morning and against the wall at the end of it, and four miles away in about four minutes in a workroom a man of twenty-two and a woman of twenty-four said two sentences about where a load goes, and nobody thanked either of them.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about two in the afternoon, and the last two hours of that day were the four miles. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward until about half past ten and has never been in that room and knows none of this and did not ask to be told.

His word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is five hundred and thirty-four days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature, and nobody asked them anything.

---

**There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket, four miles from a workroom with four desks in it. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years, and nobody in that room has ever told her not to.**

There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, shut for about forty minutes of it and not opened for about four.

**There were about eleven people. Nobody read anything out. Nobody was handed anything.**

**She was not on any sheet of it, and nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.**

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece.

The first was a person who wanted to know whether about four rooms in four districts were allowed to have refused to be joined, and she said that she does not know, that she has not been asked, and that she was not going to find out in four minutes, and she wrote nothing.

The second was a man of about forty-seven who asked her a question and then asked it again in a different order, and she wrote nothing, and at the end of the item he said thank you and she said yes, and neither of them said anything else.

The third was a person who wanted to know what a word meant, and she told him what it meant in about nine seconds and wrote nothing, and he thanked her for telling him and she said yes.

The fourth was a person who asked her for a rule she has not given in nineteen years, and it was the fourth time anybody has asked her for it, and she gave four seconds of something else.

"**There are four things on this table and none of them is a rule, and if you want to know what to do then you are asking the wrong room, and I have been the wrong room for nineteen years and I am not going to stop being it on a Wednesday.**"

**Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her for it again and nobody in that room said she had refused, and about four people in that room noticed that she had given four seconds to a thing that was not the thing, and about four did not notice, and nobody has said since that she should have given the thing itself.**

The fifth was the youngest person in that room, who had not been in front of her before an item, and what he said was about a building.

"**I have been in a room in the working Ashfields four times and there is a piece of paper lying on a table in it that nobody has turned over, and I have decided that I am not going to turn it over, and I would like to know whether there is anybody in this city that a person like me is supposed to tell.**"

She said four seconds of something and it was not an answer and it was not the no-conversion rule, and he wrote nothing down and she wrote nothing down, and about four people in that room said afterwards that the honest answer is no and about four said that the question was not hers, and both of those are things she has said at other sittings and neither of them is new.

The sixth was a man of about forty-four who put a question to her a long time back and got nine words back that were not the answer, and who has not put it since, and who was in that room and looked at the floor.

**No number was given into the tin this morning. The tin stands at seventy-three and it stood at seventy-three at the last sitting four weeks ago and at the one before that. The count came out as it comes out at every sitting: thirty, of which twenty-five are the Wednesdays this book has sat on, and five are older than the book, and the five are on a sheet she has never shown to anybody and did not show this morning either.**

The book is at fifty-two lines. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced thirty times. The whole business is nineteen years old. **Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and none of the four was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and was not given as one.**

**Nobody thanked her. Nobody said the book had been shut too long. Nothing about two sittings in a row was said in that room, and she has said nothing about it, and nobody in this case has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**

---

Four miles away, at about ten past eleven, he came up to a workroom with about four desks in it and a consent clipboard on the wall that no institution has asked for, and a woman of about twenty-four was on the second desk and was waiting for a thing that is not medical.

He had a sheet in his hand. It is a page of his own with about seven lines on it in his own hand, and it is what he did that week and where he was and how long it took, and it has no heading and no date and no reference and no signature, and it is not a report and it is not a return and it is not a minute and it is not a form.

He put it on the second desk and said, in about four seconds:

"**That is the ninth of those and it is the last one and it is going into that drawer and it is not coming out again unless one of us opens it in this room, and I am not going to ask you to open it.**"

She did not open it. She put it in the drawer and shut the drawer, and the drawer is in a building four miles from the room off a service road, and the page of paper with about eleven lines in it that nine people are copying by hand is not in that building and has never been in it.

Then he said the other one, and it took about four seconds, and it was the whole of the four minutes.

"**If a load comes to this workroom it comes to the bay door and not to the office, and if you are not here it does not come in at all, and if it comes in with you not here it goes back out to the bay door the same day and nobody has to have an opinion about it. That is where a load goes. And if you want two workrooms four miles apart again, we go back on a Wednesday, and neither of us has to give a reason, and neither of us has to be in a good mood about it.**"

She said, in about four seconds, and it was about the room and not about either of them:

"**Then I am putting the second desk back by the window and not by the door, because by the door is where the people who are not working come, and I am not having a conversation at that desk.**"

**Nobody said anything else. There was no scene and there was no kiss and there was nothing said about the four months and nothing said about the four years before them, and nobody in either building has been told that it happened, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him and she did not thank him and he did not thank her.**

**That is the whole of it. It is a sentence about where a load goes and a sentence about where a desk goes, and two return paths that are both easy to walk, and neither of them is a reconciliation and neither of them is a promise and the four months is not shorter on the Wednesday afternoon than it was on the Monday.**

---

By half past six there were about nine people in the warm room off the service road, a cook of twenty-seven at a sink saying nothing, and a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door who was not asked a question and not thanked and not apologised to by anybody in that room. She comes for the temperature and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that warm room and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for one hundred and forty days, which is twenty weeks, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.

A man of about thirty-three, holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for one hundred and sixty-seven days with no stamp and no reference and no date and nobody behind him, was not in that warm room and has not been in it for a fortnight, and nobody has asked him for anything and nothing has been given to him.

Nineteen lines are still on two nails and the newest of them is a lift mechanic's — eleven words, signed, about the middle — and it is the only promise on that board, and it is two hundred and thirty days old, and about four minutes in a workroom has not made it more or less true.

On, and dearer, and four names on a lease are dearer again, and a fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody and is not resolved and is not going to be.

**A book did not open for about four minutes on the Wednesday of week one hundred and forty-four and stood at fifty-two lines, and in about four minutes in a workroom four miles away two people said two sentences about where a load goes and where a desk goes and put a sheet with about seven lines in a drawer and shut it, and nobody in either building was told anything and nobody thanked anybody.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
