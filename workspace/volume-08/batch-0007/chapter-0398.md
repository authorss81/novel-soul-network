# Chapter 398 — A Hut With A Window That Looks At A Scale

On the Monday morning of week one hundred and forty-four the man of about thirty-four who runs a weighbridge office in the working Ashfields said out loud that the office is not going to be joined to another room, and **it took about four seconds, no reason was given, a man of about forty-five in that hut was right about something and was refused twice, and neither of the two rooms that have said the same thing in two other districts has been told about this one.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past eight until about five and then at a counter until about six, and the working Ashfields are twenty-two miles away and he was not in them and knows nothing about them.

His word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is five hundred and thirty-two days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature, and nobody asked them anything.

---

**There is a weighbridge outside that office and four yards and a hut with a window, and the window looks at the scale. There is a telephone on the wall with a pencil tied to it by a piece of string. There are about nine people who work in and around that weighbridge, and about four of them have been in that hut on any given morning, and about four of the nine are among the nine people copying the front of a page of paper with about eleven lines in it.**

**There is a folder on a table in that hut. It has been on that table since the Monday of a fortnight ago, face up, and the copy of the front of that page is in it, and nobody has turned it over and nobody has taken it out and nobody has put it beside another copy.**

---

He said it at about ten past ten, standing, with his back to the window and the scale behind him in the glass.

"**This office is not going to be part of another room. That is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to explain it, and you can all carry on working and nobody is losing anything.**"

**Nobody asked him to explain it. Nobody thanked him and nobody said it was right and nobody said it was wrong, and about nine people in that hut were in it and about four of them were not, and about four of the four who were in it heard him and about four were on the telephone.**

A man of about forty-five who reads a scale for a living was in that hut and was right, twice, and was refused twice.

"**I have worked out that this is a workplace and not a room, and that a room is a place a person is not working in, and that if this office is a workplace then nothing anybody does in it can be joined to anything without somebody being at work when it is joined. I think that is true and I think it is the only thing anybody in this building has got.**"

Nobody said it was true. The man of about thirty-four who runs that office said, and it took him about four seconds:

"**That is a good thing to have worked out and I am not going to take my hands off this because of it, and you can keep saying it and I will keep saying my sentence, and neither of us is going to move.**"

And then he said it again, at about ten past ten, the same four seconds, to about four people, because the man of about forty-five had not accepted the first one.

**Nobody thanked either of them. Nobody improved on the sentence about the workplace, and nobody has since said that it was right or that it was wrong, and the man of about forty-five has said it once more, on a Wednesday, to a person who is not in that hut, and that person has not repeated it.**

---

**The folder is still on that table. The copy of the front of that page is in it, face up, and the back of it is where about fifty words of print are, and nobody in that hut has turned it over in about four weeks, and about four people have been in that building since the Monday it went down and about four have not.**

A man of about fifty-two who drives for a firm was in that hut at about eleven and said, to the folder, and it was not improved on.

"**There has been a piece of paper lying face up on that table for about four weeks and about four of us have walked round it, and I have worked out this week that not turning it over is worse than turning it over, and I am not going to turn it over, and I would like somebody to write down that I have worked that out and did not do it.**"

Nobody wrote it down. Nobody turned it over, and nobody has said he should, and he has not mentioned it since, and about four people in that yard have said that a man who works out a thing and does not do it is a man with a job, and about four have said nothing.

A woman of about thirty-nine who works at a counter in a building in the second of the four districts said, at about a quarter past eleven, and nobody took it up.

"**Three rooms in three districts have now said no to being joined to anything and I have been in all three, and two of the three have never heard of each other and I have decided that is correct and I would like it left that way for as long as it stays that way.**"

Nobody asked her why. Nobody suggested that the three should be put in a room together, and nobody in this case has proposed it, and the three refusals are three buildings in three districts on three Mondays and two of them have not been told about the third.

---

**Nobody merged anything. Nobody in that hut, or in that yard, or in the four other yards, has said the word merge out loud since the Monday, and about four hundred competent people in this city can each put the printed paragraph on the back of that page in front of anybody at all, and not one of them has anywhere to put an objection.**

The nine people copying the front of that page are fourteen weeks into it with about eight of the nine still unfinished, and the first disagreement, a figure in its fourth line, right in six copies and wrong in three, has still not been found, and nobody has looked for it in a fortnight, and nobody has put a copy beside another copy in four districts in about fourteen weeks.

**A man of about thirty-three, holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for one hundred and sixty-five days with no stamp and no reference and no date and nobody behind him, has not been in that hut and has not been told about it.**

---

About nine people were in that warm room off a service road by half past six, a cook of twenty-seven was at the sink and said nothing at all, and in the chair nearest the door a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody said sorry to. She is there for the temperature, the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that warm room and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for one hundred and thirty-eight days, which is nineteen weeks and five days, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.

A first-year of nineteen was at a counter in Lattice Ward at about four in the afternoon and said one thing, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**Three rooms have said no and about nine people in this city are still copying the front of a page that has something on the back of it, and neither of those numbers is on the same piece of paper, and I have decided that the reason neither of them is going to be on the same piece of paper is that nobody in this case has anywhere to put it.**"

Nobody put it anywhere. Nobody has anywhere to put it, and about four people in that shop have said that is a shortage of paper and about four have said it is not a shortage of paper, and she has not been asked which she thinks it is.

The nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails are where they were, and the newest of the nineteen is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board, and it is two hundred and twenty-eight days old, and three rooms saying no has not made it more or less true.

The heating is on and dearer, four names on a lease dearer still, and the fortnight a cook laid out is owed to her by nobody and is not going to be settled.

**A man who runs a weighbridge office said four seconds of no with a scale behind him in the glass, and a man who was right about a workplace and a room was refused twice and has said it once more to somebody who has not repeated it, and a folder is still on that table face up with the back of a page on it, and three rooms in three districts have said no and two of them have never been told about the others.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
