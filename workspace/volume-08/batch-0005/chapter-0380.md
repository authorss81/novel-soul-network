# Chapter 380 — The Back Of It

Three flood districts were saved by four rooms and about nine people over four days and nobody died and about eleven people lost a working week, and **on the Thursday of week one hundred and thirty-seven, in a room in a building in the third of the four districts, the page of paper with about eleven lines on it that nine people have been copying out by hand for fifty-two days was lifted off a wet table and turned over, and there is a printed paragraph on the back of it, and it grants the office that still has emergency authority the power to merge four rooms into one during a declared emergency.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and then carried four boxes of paper up two flights in a building in the third of the four districts because somebody asked a room and not a person, and he did not ask what for and nobody told him.

His word was at nine and ten, the room off a service road was four hundred and eighty-six days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Thursday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The paper got wet. That is all of it. Nine people in nine rooms in about four districts have been copying one page out by hand since the Monday of week one hundred and thirty, which is fifty-two days, and about eight of the nine copies are still not finished, and the flood put water through about four of the nine rooms, and about four people brought their copies to a room in a building in the third of the four districts on the Friday and the Saturday and the Sunday because it is dry and has a table and about nine chairs in it and no name on the door.

The original is on that table. It has been on that table since a Monday in an earlier week and nobody put it there on purpose and nobody has ever put it in a drawer.

---

About eleven people in the three flooded districts were out of their work in the week that began on the Monday, and none of them was told at the time, and the week is over now, and on the Thursday afternoon seven of the eleven were in that room and eleven of them were in the building.

They named it out loud. Not in turn. It took about four minutes and nobody had asked them to, and four of the eleven were not in that room and were not asked and are not going to be.

A man of about forty-two who types in an office of nine people in a building in Saltmarket said his first, in about four seconds, to a table.

"**My office is on the ground floor and I was on the ground floor and I did not get wet, and I still lost the week, and I have not worked out how to say that in a way that anybody can pay me for.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody said that was not fair and nobody said that it was.

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields said hers, in about nine seconds, to nobody.

"**I shut on the Monday and I opened on the Thursday and the three days in between are gone and nobody has told me they are going to come back and I have stopped asking, and I want that said once.**"

Nobody said it twice. Nobody said she had done the right thing and about four people in that room wanted to say it and about four did not, and the two groups have not met.

A man of about fifty-three who drives a minibus said his, in four seconds, to about nine people, and it was the one that made the room go quiet.

"**I brought nine people to a room three days running and I never wrote any of it down and I have been thinking about that for four days and I have decided that I do not want to do it again, and I cannot say why, and I am not going to pretend to.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked him why not. Nobody has asked him since, and he has not said, and the four people he does not bring any more are not the four he brought on the Monday and nobody has worked that out.

---

Then the page came off the table.

The table by the window had about an inch of water standing on it, off nine copies of that sheet that had been carried through a flood in a plastic bag and had not been dried out, and the page was lying on the wet table, and a woman of about thirty-eight who made the first of the nine copies picked it up with both hands to move it, and she turned it over, and she read what was on the other side.

There is a small printed number at the top of the front of that sheet. Nobody in nine rooms in fifty-two days has ever asked what it is and it is not the number of anything anybody in this case can use.

**On the back, at the foot, below where eleven lines in a woman's hand with four other hands in it were written, there is a printed paragraph. She read it out, once, in a flat voice, and it is these words.**

"**Where a declared emergency is in force, the Continuity Office may merge any two or more local rooms operating under an arrangement that is not a company into a single room. The merged room answers to the office for the duration of the emergency. An arrangement formed by its members is not a bar to this.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

**Nine people agreed to the front of that sheet. None of them read the back of it, because it was face up on a table and there was nothing to turn it over for, and reading a thing out is work, and nobody in nine rooms in fifty-two days has ever made anybody do that.**

A man of about twenty-nine who was standing by the door said one thing, in nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.

"**I have been in nine rooms in this city and there is no minute in any of them, and there is no list, and about four of the people in this room are going to be frightened now, and I would like somebody to say what is frightened, because nobody has.**"

Nobody said what is frightened. About four people in that room have said since that the finding of it is a thing, and about four have said that the finding of it is a relief, and the two groups have not met.

---

Four rooms in four districts have been four rooms since the first of them was a room. Nobody in this case has ever proposed a merger. Nobody has ever been asked to agree to one, and a room that has not been asked has not said no, and about four hundred competent people in this city can each write down that a merger may be imposed and not one of them has ever been asked to fill one in.

**And that is the whole of the four months of refusing: the four rooms never said no to anything. They have simply never said yes either, and there is no difference between those two things on a printed sheet.**

A man of about fifty-eight with a bad hip, who runs the office that has kept emergency authority through every one of those years, was in that room and sat down in a chair when he came in. He said one thing, in nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.

"**My office did not write that paragraph. Nobody in it has seen it. Nobody in it asked for it. I cannot tell you who printed it, and I have been trying since about four this afternoon, and I am going to stop now because I am not getting anywhere.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked him who he thought had, and he did not say, and nobody has said since that he should have, and nobody has said since that he should not have.

A woman of about forty-nine with an ordinary job and a stamp in a room in Crown Terrace, who has signed about eleven of a kind a year for nineteen years, said one thing, in nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**The only thing in this city that can put a paragraph into nine people's hands without asking any of them is the Assembly, and I have never been inside it, and I am not going to be, and I am a person with a job and my job is to sign things and I did not sign that.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her to say the word again and she did not say it again, and four people in that room heard her and four did not, and the four who did not have not asked either of them since.

---

**Nobody merged anything. Nobody proposed it and nobody refused it, because refusing requires the question, and nobody has asked anybody in four districts anything, and a question is a thing a room turns into an item, and this case has learned that the only way it can afford to hold a line is to have nobody put anything in front of it.**

The page went back on the table face up, on the patch that was wet, and nobody moved it to another table.

A woman of about twenty-four who works as a resonance nurse at a mobile clinic was in that room from about half past three and she saw it happen and she said one thing, and it was about the paragraph and it was not about any man in the room, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**Nine of us agreed to the front of that sheet for fifty-two days and not one of us turned it over, and that is not a trick and nobody in this room did it on purpose, and I would like somebody to write down that we did not do it on purpose, because in about four days somebody is going to say that we did.**"

A man of about thirty-two who carried two of those four boxes up the stairs said one thing at about half past four, in nine seconds, to nobody, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.

"**I carried them up because a room asked and not a person, and I have been in that room for about four hours and nobody has told me what is in them, and I am not asking, because a man asked me not to ask things in an earlier week and I have not forgotten it.**"

Nobody said anything about that. Nobody asked him what is in them, and the man of twenty-two was in that room and heard him and did not say so, and the woman of twenty-four heard him and did not say so.

Nobody wrote it down. Nobody asked her to write it down, and she has not written it down for anybody, and four people in that room heard her and four did not.

---

At half past four she sat down on a chair by the wall with a notebook and wrote for four minutes, in her own hand, in about nine words a line.

Then she put the notebook in her coat pocket. Then she telephoned none of it. There is a telephone in that building on the ground floor and she went past it and she did not use it, and four of the people in that room saw her go past it and the other four did not.

**She has not told anybody. Not the nine, not the four rooms, not the man of fifty-eight, and not the man of twenty-two who carried four boxes up two flights that afternoon and has not spoken in that room at all, and he did not ask her what she was writing and she did not say. The not telling is hers and it is a decision and it was made at half past four and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**

---

A man of about forty-three who runs a laundrette in Saltmarket said one thing in that warm room at about half past seven, in nine seconds, to nobody, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.

"**Nine people copied a sheet by hand for about seven weeks and eight of them have not finished, and the first one of them told me it was not a copy and she was right and nobody argued with her, and I have been in that room once and I have not copied anything.**"

Nobody argued with him. Nobody asked him to copy anything, and he has not been asked since, and about four people in that room have said that the room is not for copying and about four have said nothing.

A first-year of nineteen was at a counter in a repair shop in Lattice Ward and said one small unasked thing at six in the evening, in four seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**Nine people found out about a paragraph this afternoon and about four of them have already said the same sentence about it, and that sentence is not the sentence the man of fifty-eight said, and I do not know which of the two is going to be the one people remember.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her which sentence.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in a warm room off a service road at about half past six and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for about thirteen weeks and one day, and has not been asked about a gate, and has not been asked about a paragraph.

At a sink was a cook of twenty-seven who said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, and the newest of them is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board, and it is one hundred and eighty-two days old, and nothing on the back of a page of paper has made it more or less true.

The heating is on and costs more, four names on the lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved and is not going to be.

**Four rooms and about nine people kept about nine people out of a night of it over four days and nobody died, and about eleven people lost a week and said so in four minutes, and a paragraph nobody read out has been read out once, and nothing has been merged, and the page is face up on a wet table with the back of it against the wood.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*383. Thursday of week 137, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is four days of water, seven weeks of copying, seven people naming a week in about four minutes, one sheet turned over, one printed paragraph read out once in a flat voice, four statements in about nine seconds each, about four minutes of handwriting, no telephone call, and nobody's name on the paragraph.*

*The paragraph, as a record of about fifty words of print.* **Position: underneath, at the foot, below where the eleven lines were written. Printer: unknown, and the man of fifty-eight with a bad hip has been trying since about four in the afternoon and has said he cannot find out. Author: nobody knows.
