# Chapter 378 — A Card That Is Nearly Right

A printed card went round four buildings on the Monday morning of week one hundred and thirty-seven and it was nearly right, and **a woman of about fifty who types in an office in the second of the four districts had about nine words of standing to refuse it with and used all nine, and nobody improved on them and nobody thanked her, and it went up on a board in a canteen in the first of the four districts anyway, put there by a man of about thirty-two who was not asked to stop him and could not have been.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward and knows none of this.

Nine and ten. That room, off a service road, is four hundred and eighty-three days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Monday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them a question.

---

The card is the size of a postcard and it is printed, and it carries a heading and a date in the heading and a small number at the foot, and it is not the size of a playing card and it has nothing to do with any rail in any building in this city.

It lists four rooms and calls them four practices. Three of the four are rooms where something runs. The fourth is the top floor of a building in the working Ashfields, with a door that is not locked and a light that works, and it has been empty for about two years and nothing has ever run in it.

**That is the whole of what is wrong with it. The other three are right, and the card has a heading and a date and a number, and about eleven people in four buildings read it on the Monday and about nine of them believed all four lines of it.**

Nobody in this case wrote it. Nobody printed it. Nobody has admitted to it and the printer is not a person anybody in this case has.

---

The woman of about fifty who types in that office had it on her desk on the Monday morning with about nine words on it from somebody who wanted it on a board, and she had to decide whether to sign it off, and the only standing she had was the nine words and the fact that she types.

She said the nine words, in about four seconds, to a man of about forty-two who types in an office of nine people in a building in Saltmarket, and nobody improved on them and nobody thanked her.

"**I don't sign for rooms I've never been in.**"

He said all right. He asked her what she would like on the board and she said nothing, or did not say, and about four people in that office have since said that she was right and about four have said that three lines is most of a poster, and the two groups have not met.

**Nobody asked her to reconsider. Nobody went over her head, because there is no one to go over her head to, and the decision she made is not enforceable against anybody who did not write it down, and nobody wrote it down, and she did not write it down either, and about four hundred competent people in this city could each write down what was done here and not one of them has a line a person can be held to.**

She is not a villain. She is owed nothing. She has said nine words once and she has not been asked to say them again and she is not going to be.

---

It went up on the Monday at about eleven, on a board in a canteen in a building in the first of the four districts, on a piece of blu-tack, at the height of about five foot six.

A man of about thirty-two who works in that canteen put it there. Nobody asked him to and nobody told him not to and nobody has standing over him that anybody in this case can name, and about nine people saw him do it and about nine people read it and about four of the nine asked him where it came from.

He said it came on a tray, in a tray, off a load. Nobody asked him to say that twice and nobody has since, and about four people have said since that they were not on the load and four have said nothing.

A woman of about fifty who types in an office in the second of the four districts was not in that warm room on the Monday and has not been in it since, and about four people in that office have asked her whether she minds and about four have not, and she has not said to any of them whether she minds.

A man of about twenty who cleans offices in the second of the four districts said one thing at about five in the afternoon, in four seconds, to nobody, in that building.

"**I read that card at about eleven this morning and I have been thinking about the fourth line since about eleven this morning and I am not going to say what I think about it, because I have not been in that room and about nine people are going to go on the same evening anyway.**"

Nobody asked him what he thought. Nobody said he was right not to say it, and about four people in that building have said they are going anyway and about four have said nothing.

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields saw the card on the Monday and said one thing at about one in the afternoon to about nine people, in four seconds.

"**It is my floor on the second line and I have had that floor for about nine years, and I have never run anything in it, and nobody ever asked me whether I would, and I am not blaming anybody, I am saying that nobody asked.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her whether she would run something in it, and about four people in that canteen have since asked her and about four have not, and the two groups have not met, and she has not said yes to anybody.

A man of about forty-three who runs a laundrette in Saltmarket said one thing at about eleven, in four seconds, to about nine people in that canteen, and nobody improved on it.

"**I have been on that card and I have not been in that room and I have not been asked to be in that room, and about four people have told me to go, and nobody has told me what happens there, and I have not asked, and I am saying that I have not asked.**"

Nobody told him what happens there. Nobody improved on it, nobody thanked him, and about four people in that canteen have said that going is not the same as knowing and about four have said nothing.

A man of about fifty who owns a garage in the Lower Wards read it at about half past one and said one thing to nobody, in four seconds.

"**Four rooms, and I have been in three of them, and I have never heard of the fourth, and that is not the same as it not being there, and I do not know what to do with that, and nobody else does either.**"

Nobody told him. Nobody has since. He has not taken it down and nobody has asked him to and the card is on that board and has been since the Monday.

---

A woman of about thirty-nine who works in a pharmacy in Saltmarket saw it on the Tuesday and said one thing at about nine in the morning, in four seconds, to a customer.

"**That is three rooms and one address. I have been to the three. I have driven past the one. I am not saying it is a lie, I am saying it is a card and it is the shape of a card and nobody has to have been in a room to print it.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her whether she thought it was a lie, and she was not asked, and she has not said whether it is one, and about four people in that pharmacy have since said the same thing to about four other people and the two groups have not met.

---

A man of about twenty-eight who works nights and who was in a room in Saltmarket on the Monday said one thing at six in the evening to about nine people, in about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.

"**I have been in nine rooms in this city over about four years and I have never seen a card with a number at the foot, and a number at the foot means somebody can be asked to produce it, and about four people in this room have now looked at that number and about four have not.**"

Nobody asked him who could produce it. Nobody has looked that number up, and about four people in that room have said since that a number at the foot is a threat and four have said that it is a number, and the two groups have not met.

A first-year of nineteen was at a counter in Lattice Ward and said one small unasked thing at about four in the afternoon, in four seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**Four buildings have had that card and about four people have said that three of the lines are right, and none of the four has said the fourth line is wrong, and that is not the same as saying it is right.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her which four.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in a warm room off a service road at half past six and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for about twelve weeks and five days, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it.

At a sink was a cook of twenty-seven who said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, and the newest of them is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is one hundred and seventy-nine days old, and a nearly right card on a canteen board has not made it more or less true.

The heating is on and costs more, four names on the lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved and is not going to be.

**Nobody has taken that card down. Nobody has a line in this city on which a person can be held to having put one up, and about eleven people are going to go somewhere on the Monday evening because of a card with a heading and a date and a number on it and three right lines in it.**

A woman of about thirty-two who runs a food shop in a building in the first of the four districts was in a warm room off a service road at about seven and said one thing, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**I have been shut since Monday and I am opening on the Thursday and I have a card on a wall somewhere that has my floor on it and I have not been asked to run anything, and I would like somebody to tell me whether the card is the reason I am opening or whether the water is.**"

Nobody answered her. Nobody said the water was the reason, and nobody has told her what the card is, and she is opening on the Thursday.

A man of about fifty-five who drives for a firm that has nothing to do with the company said one thing in a warm room off a service road at about seven, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**I have been in about nine buildings in this city over four years and four of them are on that card and five of them are not on anything, and I am not saying one of those groups is better, I am saying that I have been in both and nobody has ever asked me which.**"

Nobody asked him which. Nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him, and he is owed nothing.

A woman of about thirty-five who runs a laundrette in the working Ashfields was in that warm room on the Monday for about half an hour and said one thing in four seconds, to nobody.

"**About four people have told me this week that a card is not a promise, and I have said yes to all four, and about four people have told me it is not a promise and I have said yes to those four as well, and I have not said yes to anybody who has said it is.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her, and nobody has told her anything since.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that room and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for about twelve weeks and five days, and nobody in that room has mentioned a card to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
