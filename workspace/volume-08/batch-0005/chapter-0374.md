# Chapter 374 — The Fifth Friday, And A Man Who Was Asked Something

The water was down on the Friday of this week, which was the fourth day of it, and about eleven people in three districts did not open again that week, and **a floor in a building in Crown Terrace was run for the fifth time in four months, and the post beside the corridor was still bare, and for the first time in four years a man of about forty-four in that room was asked a question and answered it, and nobody improved on the answer and nobody thanked him for it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a repair shop in Lattice Ward until about half past four and was not in Crown Terrace and knows nothing about a post.

The word was nine and ten that day. The room off a service road, four hundred and seventy-three days old, was warm, and about four people came in on the Friday for the temperature, and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

There were nine folding chairs and about eleven people on the second floor of a building in Crown Terrace. Seven of the eleven are on the company's list and four are on none, and the four have not been chased and have not been.

The post is by the corridor end and there is nothing on it. It was bare for about three weeks by that Friday and four people in that room looked at it every time they walked past and about four did not, and the two groups have not spoken about it and are not going to.

**Nobody in that room referred to any other Friday. Not one of the about eleven, and not the woman of thirty-six who called the run, and there is no way of knowing which of them know that this is the fifth and which of them do not, and nobody has asked.**

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by the woman of thirty-six with a lanyard on who works there. Nobody read anything off anything. Nobody handed anything to anybody and nobody counted for anybody and nobody prompted anybody.

A woman of about thirty-four did the whole of it properly without being told. A man of about twenty-nine who comes on a Friday sat down at the right moment without being told and has still not said a word in that room in four years and a bit.

Nobody said how long it took. Nobody said anything at all for about nine seconds afterwards, the way they have not said anything for about four years, and about nine people stood still and about four of them thought about the three weeks the post has been bare and four did not.

---

A woman of about thirty who is in that room about twice a month said one thing before the run, in four seconds, to a chair.

"**I have been in this room nine months and I have not worked out what the nine of us on a Friday are for, and I have not asked, and I am saying that I have not asked so that if anybody in here thinks I have not noticed, they know that I have noticed.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody said she had noticed, and four people in that room heard her and four did not.

Then a woman of about twenty-three who stacks shelves in a place that opens at eight in the second of the four districts asked the man of about forty-four a question.

She has been in that room for about a year and has not asked anything in it before. She asked it at about ten to nine, in about four seconds, and it was not about the run and it was not about the post, and he answered it in four seconds.

"**Is it always this quiet on a Friday, or is it quiet today?**"

"**It is always this quiet. It is quieter this year.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody said it back better. Nobody asked him which year it got quieter, and nobody has since, and four people in that room heard the answer and four did not, and nobody said that a man who has not spoken in a room for four years had spoken in it twice.

He has not spoken in that room since. Nobody has asked him anything else. Nobody thanked him and he is owed nothing and he did the whole of a run on the floor that evening like the other eight, and he put a chair back on the stack afterwards with about four other people, and nobody said anything about the chairs.

A woman of about thirty-eight, who has been in that room for two years and who said nothing in it on six Fridays and then broke that silence on the seventh, said nothing in it on this Friday, and four people in that room noticed and the other four did not.

**Nobody in that room has ever been told what the run is for. Nobody has asked. The company that buys the training has never sent anybody to look at it, in about four years, and the only person from that company who comes into that room is the woman with the lanyard on who calls the run.**

---

About four of the eleven in that room are on no list of any kind. The four are a man of about twenty-seven who comes when he can get the hours, and who has no jobs of his own after about six on a Friday, and about whom nobody in that room knows anything except that he comes; a woman of about thirty who is in it about twice a month; a man of about forty-three who drives for a firm that has nothing to do with the company; and a man of about thirty-six who works in that building and who comes up for the run and goes down again. A man of about twenty-five is also in that room and he is on the company's list, and he is there because a man of twenty-seven asked him once in a car park, and the woman of thirty-six with the lanyard on has never said to anybody which of them she put on it.

**None of the four has ever been asked why they come, and four of the people in that room have wondered, and about four have not, and neither group has said anything to any of the four and neither group is going to.**

A man of about twenty-nine who comes on a Friday said nothing at all on this Friday, in about four years and a bit, and four people in that room noticed that he had said nothing and four did not, and he has been doing the run properly for four years and a bit without being told once.

A woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard on who calls the run said one thing at ten to nine to about nine people, in four seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**The post is going to stay bare, and I am not telling you that as a threat, I am telling you because one of you is going to ask me whether it was meant, and I would rather say it now than be asked.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody said she had told them so, and four people in it have not asked her about it since and about four have, and the four who have were told the same four seconds and nothing else.

They went out through a lobby and up a ramp and onto a forecourt with about nine cars on it and a man in a booth who has never been inside that building and does not know any of the eleven and has never been asked to and would not be told.

A woman of about thirty who is in that room about twice a month said one thing at the top of the stairs, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**Four years I have been in that room and I have never been told what a run is, and I have asked exactly nobody, and I am saying that I have asked nobody so that nobody in here has to be the one who tells me.**"

Nobody told her. Nobody took it up and nobody thanked her, and about four people on that stairwell heard her and about four did not.

A man of about thirty-six who works in that building said one thing at the bottom of the ramp, in four seconds, to nobody, and it was not improved on and nobody thanked him.

"**I have four offices on that floor and I go up for the run every Friday and I have never once asked anybody up there why, and I have not been asked why I go, and I would like both of those to stay as they are.**"

Nobody took it up. Nobody said they would stay as they are, and about four of the eleven have since said that they are as they are and about four have said nothing, and the two groups have not met.

A man of about twenty-seven who comes when he can get the hours said one thing at the bottom of the ramp, in four seconds, to nobody, and nothing was added to it and nobody answered him.

"**It is better with a bare post and I have said that before in a room like this one and I am not going to say it twice, so that is me not saying it twice.**"

Nobody said anything. About four people heard him and about four did not and the two have not spoken.

---

About nine people came in at half past six into a warm room off a service road, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to. The reason she comes is the heat. The heat costs more than it did. Both of those are correct.

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in four seconds, which was that a man had answered a question in a building four miles away and that about four people in that building had heard him and about four had not, and nobody said it back better and nobody thanked her and nobody asked her how she knew.

A woman of about thirty-nine who works in a pharmacy in Saltmarket was in that warm room on the Friday for half an hour and said one thing in four seconds, to nobody.

"**I have spent three days telling people that a card is a shape and not a lie, and four of the people I told have now been to a floor that was not there, and I am going to stop saying it, and I do not have anything to put in place of it.**"

Nobody gave her something to put in place of it. Nobody asked her what she would say instead, and she has not said anything about it since.

A woman of about thirty-five who runs a laundrette in the working Ashfields said one thing in four seconds at about half past seven, to nobody.

"**About nine people have been coming into this room all week for a kettle and about four of them have never spoken to anybody in it and I have made tea for all nine and I have not asked one of them a question, and I am not going to start on a Friday.**"

Nobody went further with it. Nobody said she had made the right choice, and about four people in that room have said that nobody ever asks and about four have said nothing.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for about eleven weeks and two days, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it.

**There was a post in a building in Crown Terrace with nothing on it on the fourth Friday and about four of the people in that room had looked at it and about four had not, and the two groups have not met, and nobody in that room has ever referred to any other Friday and nobody is going to.**

Nineteen lines and two nails, and the newest of the nineteen is eleven words a man of twenty-nine wrote about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and is one hundred and sixty-nine days old. The heating is on and costs more, the lease costs more for four names than it did, and the fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.

A man of about forty-three who drives for a firm that has nothing to do with the company said one thing at the top of the stairs, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**I drive four nights and I come here on a Friday because it is warm and there is a kettle in it, and that is the whole of why, and I have never once been asked to be useful in this room and I would like that recorded.**"

Nobody recorded it. Nobody said that a man who comes for the kettle and a man who comes because he was asked are the same, and four people in that corridor heard him and four did not.

A man of about twenty-five who is in that room because a man of twenty-seven asked him once in a car park said one thing at about a quarter to nine, in four seconds, to nobody, and it was not improved on and nobody thanked him.

"**I have been in this room about nine times and I do not know what it is for and I have never asked, and I have started to think that not asking is the only reason I am still in it, and I am saying that out loud in a corridor because I would like somebody to contradict me.**"

Nobody contradicted him. About four people in that corridor heard him and four did not, and the man of about twenty-seven who asked him once in a car park heard him and said nothing to him then and has said nothing to him since.

---

A woman of about fifty-three who runs a hardware shop in the working Ashfields was not in that warm room on the Friday and has not been in it since the Monday, and nobody rang her about the water and nobody rang her about anything else, and four people in that room have noticed the empty chair and four have not, and neither group has said anything.

The water was down on that Friday, which was the fourth day of it, and about eleven people in three districts did not open again that week and did not open on the Monday either, and about four of them were told by somebody else that they would not be opening and about four were not told by anybody.

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields said one thing at about seven in the evening in a warm room off a service road, in about nine seconds, and nobody went further with it and nobody thanked her.

"**My floor was under a foot of it on Monday and under two inches on Wednesday and it is dry today, and all three of those are the truth about the same forty feet, and nobody has ever asked me which of the three I believed while it was happening.**"

Nobody asked her. She has not been asked since and is not going to be, and the laundrette is open and her rent is the same as it was.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that room and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for about eleven weeks and two days, and he was not asked about a gate on the following Monday and he has not been asked about it since.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at ten.

*377. Friday of week 135, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is about eleven people, nine folding chairs, a bare post, a run of about nine minutes, one question asked in about four seconds and one answer of about four seconds, and nobody's name on either.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening. One company training room in Crown Terrace, the second floor, from about half past six until about nine; a corridor, a fire door at each end, a stair, and about nine doors that are not opened during the run. Nine folding chairs, about eleven people, seven of them on the company's list and four on none, and the four have not been chased. One post by the corridor end with nothing on it, bare for about three weeks, that is twenty-one days. One run of nine people over one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front at about half past seven by a woman of thirty-six with a lanyard on who works there. One question asked in that room for the first time in about four years, at about ten to nine, by a woman of about twenty-three who stacks shelves in a place that opens at eight in the second of the four districts, and one answer of about four seconds from a man of about forty-four who has been in that room for four years and had not spoken in it. Times anybody asked him anything else afterwards: none. Times anybody asked him which year it got quieter: none. Times he spoke in that room again: none. Times he was thanked: none. Times the run was called a rehearsal or a resolution of anything: none. People who did the whole of it without being told: about four. One statement of about four seconds at the bottom of a ramp, made on purpose not to be made twice. One room off a service road from about half past six, about nine people. The rate did not move: nine and ten. On the heating, briefly: it is on, it is dearer, the four names on the lease are dearer still, and the fortnight a cook put down is still not back. Room, off a service road, at four hundred and seventy-three days. The card in a rail in Lattice Ward, four hundred and seventy-seven days, and it is a different object in a different building from the room, and the two have never shared a room. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines three hundred and ninety-three days, the thirteen three hundred and forty-four, the fourteen three hundred and nine, the fifteen two hundred and eighty-eight, the sixteen two hundred and sixty-three, the seventeen two hundred and forty-five, the eighteen one hundred and ninety-one, the nineteen one hundred and sixty-nine. That board carries nineteen lines and thirteen hands and fourteen names and has not been touched since the last of them went on it. A cook wrote the first of those nineteen and it has not moved since. A man of twenty-nine wrote the last of them and it is the only promise on the board. Eight things are in a ring binder and none of the eight has been read, and one of the eight is the woman's page. The master's fifth line is empty and the drawer is shut. The Exchange did not sit on this day and does not sit on any Friday. Its last sitting was a Wednesday twenty-three days ago and its next one is a Wednesday five days from today. The figures are the same four they have been: a book at fifty-one lines, a tin at seventy-three, a count announced twenty-seven times, and nineteen years of it. The standing ask is at twenty-three weeks and two days and was made at the seventeenth sitting. The four figures are printed and none of them is a version of any of the others.**

*The question and the answer, as a record of about four seconds each.* **Asked by: a woman of about twenty-three who stacks shelves in a place that opens at eight in the second of the four districts, in that room for about a year, at about ten to nine on this Friday evening, in about four seconds, to a man of about forty-four. Asked: whether it is always that quiet on a Friday, or whether it is quiet today. Answered: that it is always that quiet, and that it is quieter this year. Times anything about it was said: none. Times she said anything else in that room before: none, in about a year. Times he had spoken in that room in four years: none, and then two on this evening. Times anybody asked which year: none, then or since. Times he spoke in that room again: none, and he has put a chair back on the stack since. Times anybody thanked him: none, then or since. Times anybody said it was good of him to answer: none. Whether the answer has been given to anybody: no, and there is no way to give it to anybody. Owner: the two of them, and four seconds, and nobody.**

*The post, as a record of an object that has been bare for about three weeks.* **Object: one post by the corridor end of a company training room in Crown Terrace. On it for about four years: one laminated sheet, written on never, read from twice, and taken off at about ten to nine on a Friday three weeks before this one. On it now: nothing. Put back: no, and nobody has asked for it to be put back and nobody has asked anybody for one. People in that room who look at the post when they walk past: about four. People in that room who do not: about four. Whether the two have spoken about it: no. Times a run has been referred to any other run by anybody in that room: none. Times a run has been called a rehearsal: none. Times anybody has asked what the training is for: none, in about four years. People sent by the company that buys the training to watch a run: none, in about four years. Owner: the post, and the company, and the about eleven.**

*What the day did not touch.* **A floor was run and nothing about it was measured, named, or put on a surface, and none of the five Fridays that room has had has ever been compared with any other by anybody in it. Four of about eleven in that room are on no list and nobody in that building has been asked to say so and nobody said so tonight. The post in Crown Terrace is bare and nothing has been put on it, and a page of paper with about eleven lines on it in a woman's hand with four other hands in it was not in that room and was not discussed in it, and eight of its nine copies are unfinished. A hardboard on two nails in a room off a service road was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, a drawer stayed shut, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Friday. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 377.**
