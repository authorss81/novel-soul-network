# Chapter 375 — About Nine Lorries At A Gate

About nine lorries stood in a yard behind a company's own gate on the Monday of this week and did not go through it, and **a man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was asked at about eleven in the morning to open that gate and said no in nine words and was not thanked, and nobody said why the lorries were there and nobody has since, and the gate was shut until about six.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past eight until about six and was in no yard and knows none of this.

His word was at nine and ten, the room off a service road was four hundred and seventy-six days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Monday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The gate is a company's own and it is at the end of an approach road in the working Ashfields, and there is a fence either side of it, and a small office inside the gate with a window in it and a man in it.

About nine lorries came in at about seven in the morning to deliver, and stopped about thirty feet short of the gate, and stayed there. They are still there, in the photograph a driver took at about eleven, and there is no photograph anybody else has.

Nobody has said why. Not the drivers and not the company and not the man in the office, and about four people have asked and about four have been told that there is nothing to tell, and one of the four who was told that has since said that there was something to tell.

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields said one thing at about two in the afternoon to about nine people, in about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**I have been past that gate for about four years and I have never once seen nine lorries in a row doing nothing, and I am not saying I know what it is, I am saying that I drive past it and I can see it and nobody has told me anything.**"

Nobody told her anything. Nobody has told anybody anything.

---

The man in the small office came out at about eleven and asked the man of about fifty-one with a tool bag to open the gate.

He was on the footpath at the time with a bag and he was not going that way, and about four people saw the request being made and about four did not, and nobody has described what the man in the office said before he asked and nobody has asked him.

The answer took nine words.

"**I don't work here and it isn't my gate.**"

He said it once and he did not say it again and he did not explain it, and the man in the office said something back that about four people heard and four did not, and the man with the tool bag did not answer that either and went on with what he was doing.

**Nobody thanked him. Nobody said he had been right. Nobody said anything about it in that yard for about nine seconds and then a driver started an engine and that was the end of it, and about four people in this case have since said that he should have opened it and about four have said that he should not have been asked, and the two groups have not met.**

He has not been asked about anything since. He has not been asked about the gate, or the nine lorries, or the morning, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it, and he has not said anything about any of it and is not going to.

---

Nobody asked him because there is no minute and there are no minutes of anything here. A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag can say how many buildings in this city he can get into that a person cannot get out of, and that is the second of the three things anybody in this case can point at, and neither of the three has ever been resolved and none of them is going to be.

A man of about thirty-one who drives one of the nine lorries said the cost out loud at half past two, standing in the cab with the door open, in nine seconds, and took it up and nobody asked him a question about it.

"**I am paid by the hour and I have been here since about seven and it is now half past two, and none of that is mine, and I am not saying somebody should be fired, and I am saying I would like somebody to say the hours out loud.**"

Nobody said the hours out loud. Nobody offered to pay them and nobody offered to split them, and he did not ask for that and did not say he had expected it, and about four hundred competent people in this city can each write down what is owed to a man for seven hours on a Monday and not one of them has a line that a person can be paid on.

He is going to be paid for the hours his company pays for. Nobody has said that to him and he has not asked and it is not going to be said to him.

---

A man of about forty-eight who drives one of the other lorries came back from a cafe at about one and stood at the front of his cab and said one thing to the man in the office, in nine seconds, and the man in the office wrote nothing down.

"**I have been at this gate about forty times a month for about four years and I have never been told to wait before, and I am not saying I mind, and I would like it written somewhere that I did not mind, because in about a week somebody is going to ask me whether I minded.**"

Nobody wrote it down. Nobody said they would remember it, and nobody has, and about four of the other drivers have since said that they did not mind either and four have said nothing, and the two groups have not met.

A man of about twenty-seven who is a checker in that yard said one thing at about half past three, to about nine people, in about four seconds, and it was not improved on and nobody thanked him.

"**I have checked lorries in and out of that gate for about two years and the only thing that has ever stopped a lorry is a form, and there is no form today, and I have been standing at this window since about seven with nothing to check.**"

Nobody improved on that either. Nobody said he should go home. He was still at that window at about ten past six when the gate opened, and about four people in that yard have said that he stood there for about eleven hours for nothing and four have said that he stood there for something.

A man of about forty-seven who drives a refuse lorry came past that gate at about half past three with a full load and could not get out and turned round, and said one thing to his mate, in four seconds, and his mate has repeated it to four people since and four have not.

"**I have turned round in that road for about nine years and I have never once done it because of nine lorries, and I am not saying I am against them and I am not saying I am for them, I am saying it was a Wednesday that was not a Wednesday.**"

Nothing was added to it. Nobody asked which Wednesday, and there is no Wednesday in it.

A woman of about thirty-nine who works in a pharmacy in Saltmarket drove past that gate at about four on the Monday and said one thing to nobody, in four seconds.

"**I have been in this city for about nine years and I have never seen a thing happen at a gate that nobody could explain, and I am not saying this one is the first, because I have not been watching every gate in this city and I am not going to start.**"

Nobody said it back better. Nobody asked her which gate she meant and she would not have said.

The gate was opened at ten past six in the evening by the man in the office, with his own key, and about nine lorries went out through it over about forty minutes, and nobody has asked whether that was the first time it had been opened that day.

---

A woman of about fifty-three who runs a hardware shop in the working Ashfields drove past that gate at about five and stopped and looked and said one thing to the driver of the first lorry, in four seconds.

"**I am shut because of the water and I have been shut since Monday, and I have come here because it is the only thing going on this road, and that is not a reason and I know it is not a reason.**"

The driver said all right. Nobody said it back better. Nobody asked her what she wanted, and she did not want anything, and she drove on, and about four people in that yard have said since that a shopkeeper stood at a gate for about five minutes and about four have said nothing.

A woman of about forty-two who is the office manager in that building and who has the key to the gate said one thing at about half past four, standing in the yard, in nine seconds, to nine people, and nobody took it up.

"**I have been asked about that gate eleven times this afternoon and I have told all eleven of them that the key is in the office and that I am not the person who decides, and I have said that eleven times and not one of the eleven has asked me who is.**"

Nobody asked her who is. Nobody went further with it, nobody thanked her, and four of the nine drivers have since said that somebody is and four have said nothing.

A first-year of nineteen was at a counter in Lattice Ward and said one small unasked thing at about five in the afternoon, in four seconds, and nothing was added to it and nobody thanked her.

"**Nine lorries were stood outside a gate for about eleven hours and about nine people drove past them and about four of those nine said the same sentence about it, and none of the four has ever met the other three.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her which four.

A woman of about thirty-one who does building maintenance was in that warm room on the Monday and said one thing in four seconds, to nobody.

"**I have been standing outside that gate since about eleven and four people have come up to me and asked me whether I knew anything, and I have said no four times, and I have not said no to anybody who did not ask.**"

It was not improved on. Nobody asked her whether she knew anything, and she is owed nothing, and four people in that yard have said she should have been told more and four have said nothing.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in a warm room off a service road at half past six and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for about eleven weeks and five days.

At a sink was a cook of twenty-seven who said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did.

A man of about thirty-one who drives one of the nine lorries said one thing at about seven in that warm room, in four seconds, to nobody, and nobody went further with it and nobody thanked him.

"**I have been in a yard in this city every working day for about nine years and I have never once been asked why we stop, and today I have stopped and about four people have asked me why and about four have not, and I have not told either of them and I am telling nine strangers instead.**"

Nobody asked him why in that room. Nobody said it back better, and nobody has rung him since, and he has not told anybody else.

A woman of about forty-two who is the office manager in that building said one thing in that warm room at about half past seven, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**Eleven people have asked me about that gate since about five and I have told all eleven of them that the key is in the office, and not one of the eleven has asked me who decides, and about four of them are still standing in my yard at eight o'clock.**"

Nobody asked her who decides. Nothing was added to it and nobody thanked her, and about nine lorries went out of that gate over about forty minutes and about four of the drivers have said since that the office manager should have been told more and about four have said nothing.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, and the newest of them is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board, and it is one hundred and seventy-two days old, and nine lorries at a gate have not made it more or less true.

The heating is on and costs more, four names on the lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved and is not going to be.

**Nobody in this case has a line that a person can be paid on, and about nine lorries were shut out of a company's own yard for about eleven hours on a Monday, and the man of fifty-one with a tool bag has said nothing about any of it and does not intend to.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at ten.

Fence: either side, about nine feet. Office inside the gate: one room, one window, one man in it. Time the lorries arrived: about seven in the morning. Time the gate was opened: about ten past six in the evening. Time the lorries left: between about ten past six and about seven. Number of lorries: about nine. *The nine words, as a record of something that was not a rule.* **Asked by: the man in the office inside that gate, at about eleven in the morning, on a Monday, to a man of about fifty-one with a tool bag who was on a footpath. *What the day did not touch.* **About nine lorries stood at a gate for about eleven hours and nobody wrote down why, and there is no list of them and no minute and no reference and nobody in this city has a line that a person can be paid on for seven hours of a Monday.
