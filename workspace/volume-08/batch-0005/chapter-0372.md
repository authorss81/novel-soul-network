# Chapter 372 — Second Time In, From Another Direction

The water came back into three of the four districts on the Wednesday morning from a direction it had not come from, and **nobody in any of the three could tell anybody which of the four rooms was doing what, because the night rota of four sentences failed on the Thursday of week one hundred and thirty-two, which is twenty days ago, and there is nothing written down anywhere about any of it, and about four of the nine practices do not get there in time.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward and knows none of this and was not asked.

His word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and seventy-one days old, and it is warm, and four people came in on the Wednesday for the temperature and nobody asked them anything.

---

It came in at about six in the morning and it came up a street that had been dry since Monday, and the first person to see it was a man of about forty-three who runs a laundrette in Saltmarket, who had opened at about six because he always opens at about six, and who has said since that he would rather have opened at ten.

**Nobody in the three districts had a way of knowing that a street that was dry on Tuesday might not be dry on Wednesday, and nobody has one now, and nobody has asked for one, and the three rooms that were open when it came in were not the three that were open on Monday and not the three that were open at about nine o'clock on Tuesday.**

The room above a line in Saltmarket had about eleven people in it by eight. The four feet of shop in the Quiet Quarter had about nine in it by nine and stayed dry again. The weighbridge office in the working Ashfields had about four in it by ten and the weighbridge itself stood in six inches of water by eleven.

That is three of the four. **The first of the four, off a service road in the Lower Wards, had nine people in it at about ten and a kettle going and a hardboard on two nails on the wall, and it was doing what the other three were doing, which was standing open, and it had no idea what the other three were doing and nobody told it and nobody has since.**

---

A man of about thirty-four who had been in a room in Saltmarket on the Monday telephoned the weighbridge office in the working Ashfields at ten past nine and asked one question, which was which of the four rooms was taking people that morning, and the answer took about four seconds.

"**I do not know. We do not know. Nobody has ever told us and we have never asked and there is a sheet of paper on a wall in this building that four of us wrote in a week in the dark and it has got four sentences on it and none of them is that.**"

Nobody said anything to that. Nobody said the man should have found out beforehand, and nobody has, and the man did not ring a second time and has not since.

The man of about thirty-four put the telephone down at ten past nine and did not try the other three, and about four people in the room above a line in Saltmarket were standing near enough to hear him not try them.

Nobody improved on what the weighbridge office said. About four people in that room said afterwards that the four sentences should have said which room was on, and about four said that the four of them who wrote the sentences are the only four who could have said, and the two groups have not met.

---

A man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts was in a building in the first of the four districts at about eleven on the Wednesday, and he was not sent for again, and the lift in that building was still between the ground floor and the first where it was on Monday.

There were about eleven people above the ground floor and about four of them could not use a stair. He got nine of the eleven down over the course of the day, in about four goes, and the eleventh person is over eighty and was not got down and was not got down on Thursday either and nobody has said why not and the answer is that he would not come.

He said one thing, at about half past two, in a lobby, to about nine people, in about nine seconds, and took it up and nobody thanked him.

"**I have been in this building three times since Monday and neither of the other two times was because anybody asked me, and about four of you have already decided that I am the answer to something, and I am not the answer, I am the person who came.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked him what the answer would have been. Nobody told him that it did not matter what he called himself.

**There is a man of about twenty-nine in this city who comes up to a second floor in Crown Terrace on a Friday, and that one is not this one, and the two of them have never met and nobody has ever put them in a room together.**

---

A man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts said one more thing at about half past four, in a lobby, to about nine people, and nobody went further with it.

"**I have been in four buildings this week on my own account and not one of the four sent for me, and about four people have said thank you to me and about four have not, and neither of those is the thing I would want.**"

Nobody said what he would want. Nobody asked him, and he has not said, and the two of those groups have not spoken.

A woman of about thirty-one who does building maintenance went to the weighbridge office in the working Ashfields at half past one on foot, because a shop of hers in Saltmarket was under six inches and she wanted to know which of the four rooms was dry.

She stood in that office for about nine minutes. The man there, who is about thirty-seven and has been in that building for six years, showed her the card of four sentences on the wall without being asked, and did not read it aloud.

"**I am not going to read that out. Two of the four of us wrote it in the dark in a week and it is the only thing any of us has ever written down, and you can look at it as long as you like, and I am not going to stand here and be the man who reads it.**"

She looked at it. She said that it did not say what she had come for. He said that he knew.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody said he had been helpful and nobody has rung that office since about anybody, and about four of the eleven people in that Saltmarket shop do not know that the card exists.

A woman of about thirty-nine who works in a pharmacy in Saltmarket said one thing at about four in the afternoon to a man of about forty-two who types in an office of nine people in a building in that district, and it took her nine seconds, and it was not about the pharmacy.

"**You stood in that doorway on Monday and let eleven people past you and you have not mentioned it since, and about four of those eleven have told me that you did, and I am telling you that they told me, and I am not asking you for anything.**"

He said all right. Nobody said it back better. Nobody asked her which four, and she has not said, and he has not asked since and is not going to.

---

The woman of thirty-five who chose that room's dark in the Quiet Quarter said one thing at about eleven on the Wednesday, to nobody, in nine seconds, and nothing was added to it and nobody thanked her.

"**I was wrong about this room on the day I put the light out and I have not been wrong about it since, and I am not going to be asked to say that again, and about nine people came in here today and none of them was asked anything by me.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her what she was wrong about, and nobody has, and she has not repeated it and is not going to.

The eighty-year-old in the first of the four districts was not got down on the Wednesday and was not got down on the day after, and the reason he gave both times was the same and it took him about four seconds, and nobody improved on it.

"**I have come down those stairs twice in my life and I did it badly both times and I am not doing it in front of nine strangers, and I have a chair by the window and I have got food, and you can all come and look at me.**"

About four people went and looked at him over two days. Nobody said he was being awkward. Nobody said he should come down and nobody has asked him again, and he is still in that room on the Friday, and about four people in that building have decided between them that somebody ought to go on going and nobody has been chosen.

A woman of about thirty-two who runs a food shop in a building in the first of the four districts said one thing at about nine in the morning, in four seconds, to a customer, and it was not improved on.

"**I was shut on Monday and I was shut on Tuesday and I am open now, and I have not worked out whether the water has stopped or whether I have, and I am not going to be the one who decides that today.**"

Nobody took it up. Nobody told her which of the two it was.

A woman of about fifty-seven who works in a laundrette in Saltmarket said one thing at about five, standing in a doorway, in four seconds, and it was to nobody.

"**There is a line on the floor of my shop that is nine years old and it was at the skirting on Tuesday and it is four inches up the door today, and about four people have told me that is normal, and not one of them has a shop.**"

Nobody noted it. Nothing was added to it. She went home and reopened on the Thursday and has not said any of it again.

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter of a repair shop in Lattice Ward from half past eight and said one small unasked thing at about three in the afternoon, in four seconds, and nobody said it back better and nobody thanked her.

"**Four people telephoned that weighbridge office this morning and about four of them were asking which room was open, and none of them were asking for anything for themselves, and that is four people who did not have anywhere to put a person.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her what she meant by a person. Nobody said that the four rooms were not for putting people in.

A man of about forty-three who runs a laundrette in Saltmarket said one thing at six in the evening to four people who had come into the room off a service road out of the Ashfields, and nobody went further with it and nobody thanked him.

"**I have been shut for two days and I am opening on the Thursday whatever anybody says, and I am telling four strangers in a warm room about it, and I would like somebody to write it down and nobody is going to.**"

Nobody wrote it down. Nobody said they would remember it and nobody has, and the laundrette opened on the Thursday.

A man of about thirty-two who works in a canteen in a building in the first of the four districts said one thing at about seven, in four seconds, to nobody, and it was not improved on.

"**Nine people came up my stairs on Tuesday who were not on my stairs on Monday, and I have not asked any of them why, and about four of them have come back and about four have not, and I have not asked about that either.**"

Nobody asked him about either. Nobody went further with it and nobody thanked him, and he is owed nothing.

A man of about thirty-eight who drives a van said one thing in that warm room at about half past six, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**Nine people have been up my stairs in a van this week and four of them tipped me and four did not, and four of the four that did not told me they could not, and I have not asked the other four at all.**"

Nobody asked the other four. Nobody took it up and nobody thanked him, and he is owed nothing and nobody is going to pay him for the two he was not tipped for.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in a warm room off a service road at about half past six and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for about eleven weeks, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it.

At a sink was a cook of twenty-seven who said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything and nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did.

A man of about fifty-three who drives a minibus said one thing in that warm room at about seven, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**I have driven nine people to a room in Saltmarket three days running and I have never been asked to, and I have never been asked to stop, and about four of the nine have stopped coming and I have noticed and I have not asked, and I am saying that here because I said it in a room in Saltmarket and nobody there said anything.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked him which four, and he has not been asked since, and he is owed nothing.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, and the newest of them is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is one hundred and sixty-seven days old, and three days of a lift in a building have not made it more or less true.

The heating is on and costs more, four names on the lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved and is not going to be.

**About four of the nine ordinary practices did not get to the water in time on the Wednesday, and two of them were not open at all until about two in the afternoon, and the man who runs one of them has said since that he did not know he was the sort of person who got there in time, and nobody improved on that either.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*375. Wednesday of week 135, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is a second time in from another direction, four sentences on a sheet of corrugated card with no owner, nine people out of eleven floors, two claims about a doorway, and nobody's name on any of it.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening, all of them at a bench in Lattice Ward. Second time the water came into the three districts: the Wednesday of week one hundred and thirty-five, from a direction it had not come from, at about six in the morning. Streets dry on the Tuesday and under water on the Wednesday: about four. Rooms of the four open by ten: three, and the fourth did not know what the other three were doing and was not told. People in the room above a line in Saltmarket by eight: about eleven. People in the four feet of shop in the Quiet Quarter by nine: about nine, and dry. Depth over the weighbridge in the working Ashfields by eleven: six inches. One question telephoned to a weighbridge office at about ten past nine, and the four seconds of the answer, and the two further numbers that were not rung. The night rota: four sentences on a sheet of corrugated card, written in the dark in a week, and it failed on the Thursday of week one hundred and thirty-two, twenty days ago, and it says which of the four rooms is on tonight in none of its four sentences. One building in the first of the four districts with a car between the ground floor and the first. People above the ground floor: about eleven. Got down on the day: nine, in about four goes. Not got down: one, who is over eighty and would not come, and the same answer on the day after. Person sent for on the day: nobody. Statements made about that: one, of about nine seconds, by a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts. Times anybody thanked him: none. One doorway claim, twice told, to two people, and it was not asked for. One room off a service road from about half past six, about nine people. The rate was at nine and ten, and that is the same figure it has been all week. Heating: on, and dearer than it was, and the four names on that lease are dearer still, and what a cook laid out for a fortnight is owed to her by nobody. The room off a service road is four hundred and seventy-one days old. The card in a rail in Lattice Ward: four hundred and seventy-five days, which is a third object in a third building, and the two objects have never been in one room together. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines three hundred and ninety-one days, the thirteen three hundred and forty-two, the fourteen three hundred and seven, the fifteen two hundred and eighty-six, the sixteen two hundred and sixty-one, the seventeen two hundred and forty-three, the eighteen one hundred and eighty-nine, the nineteen one hundred and sixty-seven. The wall is as it was: nineteen lines, in thirteen hands, fourteen names, nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off. The first line is a cook's and has never moved. The nineteenth is a lift mechanic's, the newest of them, and the only promise on that board. The ring binder holds eight things and has not been opened. The woman's page is in it and has not been read. The master's fifth line is empty and its drawer has not been opened. The Exchange did not sit on this day. Its last sitting was a Wednesday twenty-one days ago and its next one is a Wednesday seven days from today. The book is at fifty-one lines and the tin at seventy-three and the count has been announced twenty-seven times and the business is nineteen years old. The ask is at twenty-three weeks and was made at the seventeenth sitting. None of the four was turned into another one of them.**

*The answer, as a record of four seconds on a telephone in the working Ashfields.* **Asked by: a man of about thirty-four, at about ten past nine, one question, which was which of the four rooms was taking people that morning. Answered by: a person in a weighbridge office, in about four seconds. The answer, in their order: that they do not know; that we do not know; that nobody has ever told us; and that we have never asked; and that there is a sheet of paper on a wall in that building that four of them wrote in a week in the dark with four sentences on it and none of them is that. What the sheet is: corrugated card, about the size of two hands, and it is not a rota in any sense a body may rely on and it is not on a form and it has no owner. Times the four sentences have said which room was on: none. Numbers that were not rung after the answer: two. Times anybody has said the four of them who wrote it should have written that in it: about four, and about four have said that the four of them who wrote it are the only four who could have said. Whether the two groups have spoken: no. Owner: the four, and the card, and nobody.**

*The eleven floors, as a record of a machine and a man who came.* **Building: one in the first of the four districts, about nine floors. Fault: the car between the ground floor and the first, present on the Monday and still present on the Wednesday. Trips made by a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts: about four, over one day. People above the ground floor: about eleven. People who could not use a stair: about four. People got down: nine. Person not got down: one, who is over eighty and would not come, and the reason was not asked for and the answer on the day after is the same and will be the same. Times anybody in this case was asked to send him: none, and there is no procedure for it. Times he was thanked: none. Whether anybody said that the four people who could not use a stair had somewhere to go last night: no, and nobody has since, and one of them slept on a landing. Owner: him, and the car, and the eleven.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Water came into a second time from a second direction and nobody wrote down which direction the first one came from, and four of the nine ordinary businesses did not get to it in time and two of them were not open until two in the afternoon. The page of paper with about eleven lines on it in a woman's hand with four other hands in it was not copied that day, and eight of its nine copies are unfinished, and the first disagreement in its fourth line has not been found. Four rooms in four districts were not merged and no merger was proposed, and nobody in any of the four rooms said the word out loud this week, and the three empty nights in the card of four sentences are still three. A hardboard on two nails in a different building was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, a drawer stayed shut, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Wednesday. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 375.**
