# Chapter 379 — Eleven People Went To The Top Floor

About eleven people went to the top floor of a building in the working Ashfields on the Monday evening because of a card with a heading and a date on it, and **the door was not locked and the light worked and there was nobody there, and on the Wednesday two of those eleven said what it had cost them in about nine seconds each, in their own words, and nobody improved on either and nobody was thanked and nobody was repaid anything by anybody.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward and knows none of this.

The word was nine and ten that day. The room off a service road, four hundred and eighty-five days old, was warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday for the temperature, and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The top floor is one room with about nine windows and a door that is not locked and a light on a pull switch that works, and it has been empty for about two years, and there is a stair to it and the stair is not lit.

About eleven people went up it on the Monday evening between about seven and about nine. They went because of the card, and about four of the eleven had seen the card in a canteen in the first of the four districts and about four had been told about it by somebody who had and about four cannot say where they saw it.

**Nobody sent them. Nobody arranged it. Nobody told them what would be there, and four of the nine had looked the address up first and about four had not, and the four that had did not go.**

There is a light. There is a floor. There is a smell of cold plaster. And there is nobody, and there was nobody from about seven until about nine, and the light was on the whole time, and four of the eleven said afterwards that the light being on was the worst of it.

---

A woman of about twenty-two who cleans offices in the second of the four districts was one of the eleven, and she said what it had cost her on the Wednesday, in nine seconds, at a counter in a building in that district, and took it up and nobody thanked her.

"**I got the bus for it. That is the whole of it. It is a bus each way and I do not get paid for being somewhere and I am not saying I am owed anything, I am saying I got two buses for a room with a light on in it.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody offered to pay her the buses. Nobody asked her what she had expected, and she has not said, and about four people in that building have since said that they would not have gone and about four have said that they would, and the two groups have not met.

She is not owed anything. Nobody in this city has a line a person can be paid on for two buses, and she did not ask, and about four hundred competent people in this city can each write down what happened to her and not one of them has anywhere to put it.

---

A man of about thirty-two who works in a canteen in the first of the four districts put the card up, and he was one of the eleven, and he said what it had cost him on the Wednesday in nine seconds, to nobody, in a warm room off a service road, and nobody went further with it and nobody thanked him and nobody said he had caused it.

"**I put it up. I am not going to stand here and say I did not put it up, and I am also not going to stand here and say I put up a lie, because I did not know it was one and I still do not know whether it is one, and about four people have told me since that it is not my fault and about four have told me nothing at all.**"

Nobody said either. Nobody said he had done the right thing and nobody said he had done the wrong thing, and he has not taken the card down and nobody has asked him to, and it is still on that board.

**He is not a villain. He is owed nothing. He is a man who works in a canteen and put a piece of paper on a board and about eleven people went up a stair because of it, and he has said the one true thing about it out loud and nobody has improved on it.**

---

A man of about thirty-five who rents the upper floor of that canteen's building said one thing at about one in the afternoon on the Wednesday, in four seconds, to nine people in that canteen, and it was not improved on and nobody thanked him.

"**I have eaten in that canteen every day for about four years and four people read that card and four of them asked the man who put it up where it came from, and I have eaten there every day and I have not asked, and I would like somebody to decide whether that makes me part of it.**"

Nobody decided. Nobody told him he was or he was not, and four people in that canteen have since said that everybody who ate there is part of it and four have said that nobody is, and the two groups have not met.

A woman of about fifty who types in an office in the second of the four districts, who said nine words on the Monday morning and was not thanked, was told about the stair on the Wednesday by about four separate people and has not said anything about it to anybody.

She has not been asked for an explanation. She has not offered one. Nobody in this case has rung her about the top floor and nobody is going to, and about four people in that office have said she was right on Monday and four have said nothing, and the two groups have not met.

A man of about fifty who owns a garage in the Lower Wards, who said on the Monday that three lines being right is not the same as the fourth being wrong, drove past that building on the Wednesday at about eight in the morning and stopped, and did not go up, and has not said why to anybody.

---

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields said one thing in that warm room on the Wednesday, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**About nine people went up a stair on Monday night to a floor with a light on and I have been thinking about the light for two days, and I am not saying it was a lie and I am not saying it was not, I am saying the light was on before anybody got there and that is the whole of what I have.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody went further with it and nobody thanked her, and nobody has rung the man who put the card up, and the light is still on.

A man of about fifty who owns a garage in the Lower Wards stopped outside that building on the Wednesday at about eight and looked up at the top floor and said one thing to nobody, in about four seconds.

"**I have driven past that door four hundred times and I have never once looked up at it and I looked up at it on Wednesday because of a card, and I am not saying that is a good thing and I am not saying it is a bad thing.**"

Nobody said either. Nobody asked him what he saw, and he has not said what he saw, and about four people in that district have since said that a garage man standing in the road is not a thing anybody should have to see and four have said nothing.

A woman of about thirty-nine who works in a pharmacy in Saltmarket was in a warm room off a service road on the Wednesday and said one thing at about half past six, in about nine seconds, to nobody.

"**I have been telling about four people since Tuesday that a card is a shape and not a lie, and about four of those four have now been to a floor that was not there, so I would like to say one more thing about cards and then stop.**"

Nobody asked her what the other thing was. She did not say the other thing. She has not said it since and nobody has asked her again.

A first-year of nineteen was at a counter in Lattice Ward and said one small unasked thing at about eleven in the morning on the Wednesday, in four seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**About eleven people went to a room that was not there on Monday night and about four of them have said the cost out loud and about seven have not, and nobody is going to ask the seven, and I would like it recorded that nobody is going to ask the seven.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her how she knew the seven had not said anything.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in a warm room off a service road at half past six and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for about thirteen weeks, and has not been asked about a gate either.

At a sink was a cook of twenty-seven who said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A woman of about thirty-nine who works in a pharmacy in Saltmarket said one thing in that warm room at about seven, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**I have been in a pharmacy for nine years and in about nine buildings in this city, and about four of the nine have a card on a wall and about five do not, and I have been in both kinds and nobody has ever asked me which one I would send a person to.**"

Nobody asked her. It was not improved on and nobody thanked her, and about four people in that room have since said that neither kind is better and about four have said nothing.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, and the newest of them is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is one hundred and eighty-one days old, and a light on a pull switch on an empty floor has not made it more or less true.

The heating is on and costs more, four names on the lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is unpaid by anybody and is not resolved and is not going to be.

**About eleven people lost two buses and an evening between them, and the eleven are not the four who lost a day of pay twenty days ago and are not the woman and the child in a flat above a dark shopfront, and nobody has put the two sets of people together, and two of the eleven have said so out loud and nine have not been asked.**

A man of about twenty-eight who works nights said one thing at about half past six in that warm room, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**There is a rule in this city somewhere that says you do not send a person to a room you have not been in, and I have found out this week that nobody enforces it, and I am not asking anybody to enforce it, I am saying that it is not being enforced and four people in this room now know that.**"

Nobody said it was or it was not. Nobody has been asked to enforce it, and four people in that room have said they would if somebody asked them and four have said nothing, and nobody has asked.

The eleven hours a man of about fifty-one with a tool bag refused to open a gate on the Monday came up on Wednesday in that warm room from a man of about thirty-two who works in a canteen in the first of the four districts, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him for saying it.

"**He is a person with a bag who is owed nothing and he is the only person anybody has asked to do anything in three days, and about four people said no to him and about four did not get asked, and those are not the same as agreeing with him.**"

Nobody said whether they agreed with him. Nobody told him whether about four people had agreed with him, and the man with the tool bag was in that room and heard none of it and has said nothing about any of it.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*382. Wednesday of week 137, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one stair, one light, eleven people, two costs named in about nine seconds each, one card still on a board, and nobody's name on the card.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening, all of them at a bench in Lattice Ward. One stair, not lit, to one top floor in a building in the working Ashfields. That floor: one room, about nine windows, a door that is not locked, a light on a pull switch that works. Empty: about two years. People who went up it on the Monday evening between about seven and about nine: about eleven. How they knew: about four saw a card in a canteen in the first of the four districts, about four were told by somebody who had, and about four cannot say. People who looked the address up first: about four, and those four did not go. Whether anybody sent them: nobody. Whether anybody arranged it: nobody. Whether the light was on the whole time: yes, and four of the nine have said that the light being on was the worst of it. Cost named on the Wednesday, in about nine seconds, in their own words: two, by a woman of about twenty-two who cleans offices in the second of the four districts, and by a man of about thirty-two who works in a canteen in the first of the four districts and who put the card up. Offers of money, of the buses, or of a split: none. Times anybody asked the woman of about fifty who typed it why she had said no on the Monday: none. Times she has said anything about the stair: none. One room off a service road from about half past six, about nine people. The rate did not move: nine and ten. On the heating, briefly: it is on, it is dearer, the four names on the lease are dearer still, and the fortnight a cook put down is still not back. Room, off a service road, at four hundred and eighty-five days. The card in a rail in Lattice Ward, four hundred and eighty-nine days, and it is a different object in a different building from the room, and the two have never shared a room. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines four hundred and five days, the thirteen three hundred and fifty-six, the fourteen three hundred and twenty-one, the fifteen three hundred, the sixteen two hundred and seventy-five, the seventeen two hundred and fifty-seven, the eighteen two hundred and three, the nineteen one hundred and eighty-one. That board carries nineteen lines and thirteen hands and fourteen names and has not been touched since the last of them went on it. A cook wrote the first of those nineteen and it has not moved since. A man of twenty-nine wrote the last of them and it is the only promise on the board. Eight things are in a ring binder and none of the eight has been read, and one of the eight is the woman's page. The master's fifth line is empty and the drawer is shut. The Exchange did not sit on this day. Its last sitting was a Wednesday seven days ago and its next one is a Wednesday twenty-one days from today. The figures are the same four they have been: a book at fifty-one lines, a tin at seventy-three, a count announced twenty-eight times, and nineteen years of it. The standing ask is at twenty-five weeks and was made at the seventeenth sitting. The four figures are printed and none of them is a version of any of the others.**

*The eleven, as a record of a stair and a light.* **Stair: one, to a top floor, not lit. Floor: one room, about nine windows, a door that is not locked, a light on a pull switch. Empty for about two years. People who went up: about eleven, on the Monday evening, between about seven and about nine. How each of the eleven knew: about four from a canteen board, about four from somebody who had seen the board, about four cannot say. People who checked the address before going: about four, and they did not go. What was there when they arrived: a light on, a floor, a smell of cold plaster, and nobody. Whether the light was switched on by any of the eleven: no, and about four of them say it was on before they got to the door. Whether the light is on now: it is, and nobody in this case knows who put it on. Whether anybody went up that stair between the Monday and this Wednesday: about nine people, and none of them was sent. Whether anybody has asked the seven who have not said anything: nobody, and nobody is going to. Owner: the eleven, and the stair, and nobody.**

*The two costs, as a record of about nine seconds each, said on the Wednesday.* **First: a woman of about twenty-two who cleans offices in the second of the four districts, at a counter in a building in that district, in about nine seconds: that she got the bus for it; that that is the whole of it; that it is a bus each way; that she is not paid for being somewhere; that she is not saying she is owed anything; and that she is saying she got two buses for a room with a light on in it. Second: a man of about thirty-two who works in a canteen in the first of the four districts, who put the card up, in a warm room off a service road, in about nine seconds, to nobody: that he put it up; that he is not going to stand there and say he did not; that he is also not going to stand there and say he put up a lie; that he did not know it was one and still does not know whether it is one; and that about four people have told him since that it is not his fault and about four have told him nothing. Whether the card has been taken down: no, and nobody has asked him to. Whether anybody has said he was a villain: no, and he is owed nothing and is a person with an ordinary job. Offers made: none. Times the buses were offered back: none. Times either was thanked: none. Owner: the two of them, and the stair, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Eleven people went to a floor that was not there and about two of them have said what it cost and about nine have not been asked, and the card that sent them is still on a board and nobody can say who printed it, and no name is attached to it. A page of paper with about eleven lines on it in a woman's hand with four other hands in it was not compared with that card and is not on it, and eight of its nine copies are unfinished. Four rooms in four districts were not merged and no merger was proposed, and the three rooms on that card are the same three rooms and are not joined to each other by anything anybody has said. A hardboard on two nails in a different building was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, a drawer stayed shut, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Wednesday. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 382.**
