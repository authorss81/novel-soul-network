# Chapter 376 — The Book Did Not Open, And The Tin Did Not Move

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and thirty-six was the twenty-third sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and **the book did not open, and that is the second of two sittings in a row in this case that have not, and the reason was the same shape of reason as last time and it is not the same sentence and it is not a rule, and no number was given into the tin and the tin is where it was four weeks ago, and nobody thanked her and nobody asked her for anything.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a repair shop in Lattice Ward for the whole of the day and knows nothing about a room above a line in Saltmarket and was not asked about it and would not have been told.

The word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and seventy-eight days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday for the temperature and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The shutters were up in a room above a line in a building in Saltmarket and the holder of the room said so at the start, in the way she says it at the start of every sitting.

Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in. The ninth was against the wall with its back to the room, and it stayed where it was, and nobody looked at it, and nobody has to. There is a table, a tin that has never had a lock on it, and a book with four columns and fifty-one lines in it, and the book was on the table all morning and was not opened.

Nine people. A man of about forty-four, who asked her for a rule twenty-four weeks ago and was given nine words that were not it and has not asked since. A young man of twenty-one who has been in that room since he was seventeen and who keeps a count he has never given her. A young woman of twenty-one who has been in that room since she was fifteen and who has never been asked anything and was not asked anything this morning. And about five others not described here.

**Nobody wrote down that she was there. Nobody writes it down.**

She said it herself at about ten past ten, and it took her about four seconds.

"**No. Not this morning, and not the morning after.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A person who had come for that item had come for it on a Tuesday in about nine years and had not been told in advance and was not told now, and the not being told is not a decision and has not been a decision and nobody in that room has ever been told that there is a way of being told.

---

Then the items, four minutes each, and there were six, and two of the six were about rooms and neither of the two was written down.

The first was a woman who wanted to know whether a room in the Quiet Quarter was open on a Wednesday, and she would not say which room, and she was not asked, and she telephoned off in about four minutes with nothing said twice.

A man of about thirty-one who had been in that room four times said one thing before the items, in four seconds, to nobody, and nobody improved on it.

"**I have been in this room four times in about four years and I have never once been the second person to say anything, and I have noticed that, and I am saying it out loud now so that somebody knows that somebody has noticed.**"

Nobody answered. Nobody said he had been the second person to say anything, and about four people in that room looked at him and about four did not.

The second was a man of about thirty-one who said a thing he had said at an earlier sitting and asked whether it had been written, and she said that it had not, and he said that he had not expected it to have been, and neither of them said anything else.

The third was a person who said that about nine people had come into four rooms in three districts over four days and that the four rooms had told three different stories about where to go, and he stopped in the middle of the sentence and did not finish it, and she wrote nothing, and there is no rule that says she has to and there has not been one in nineteen years.

The fourth was about a door on a stair in a building in the first of the four districts that does not latch, and the person who rang it said which building it was in, and she wrote nothing and told him nothing, and he rang off at about eleven minutes past ten.

A woman of about thirty-two who was in that room on her third visit said one thing at about twenty past ten, in four seconds, to the table, and nobody improved on it.

"**I asked her last month for a number and she did not give me one and I did not mind, and I have come four weeks to see whether I minded, and I did not, and I would like that written down and I know it will not be.**"

Nobody wrote it down. Nobody said it would not be. She has not been back.

The fifth was a person who asked her to write down a thing that had been said to her by somebody else, and she said that she writes down what a person says to her face, and he rang off without saying anything else, and about four people in that room noticed that he had rung off and about four did not.

The sixth was the youngest person in that room, and what she said was not about the shutters at all, which is what the other eight had braced for. She said that she has been counting the people who come up the other stairwell in that building without a light, and that the number is different every week, and that nobody in this room has ever asked her to stop counting it. **The holder of the room said that nobody had ever asked her to stop counting anything, and that this was the first time in nineteen years that a person had told her in a room that they were counting, and she did not say whether that was welcome.** Nobody said whether it was welcome. Nobody asked her what the number was. About four people in that room looked at the ninth chair while she said it and about four did not.

---

**No number was given into the tin this morning. The tin stands at seventy-three and it stood at seventy-three at the last sitting four weeks ago and it moved to that figure because a person spent a fortnight counting doors and gave the count in, and nothing has been given into it since, and nobody counted it afterwards and nobody asked why it had not moved.**

The count was announced and the announcement is made at every sitting whether the book opens or not. It is twenty-eight. Twenty-three of them are the Wednesdays this book has sat on, and five are older than the book, and the five are on a sheet she has never shown anybody and has not shown this morning.

The book is at fifty-one lines. It is at fifty-one lines and it does not grow. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced twenty-eight times. The whole business is nineteen years old. **Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and every one of them is the whole of itself, and none of those four was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and was not given as one.**

---

At about eleven she said one more thing, unasked, in about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

"**The last two months have been four rooms telling each other where they are, and nobody has come to this room wanting anything of me; that I do not know what that is; that I have not asked anybody about it; and that nothing in this morning was decided by anybody, least of all by me.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

The man of about forty-four looked at the ninth chair at about a quarter to eleven and then looked at the floor. **He has not asked her for anything since the seventeenth sitting. It is twenty-four weeks since he asked. Nobody in that room has asked her for the rule she has not given in nineteen years, and she has said for the last time that she will say it, and she has kept that six times, and nothing was given in its place this morning, and there was no substitute and nobody asked for one.**

A man of about fifty-two who has come to that room for about six years said one thing, in four seconds, to the ninth chair, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.

"**I have not said anything in the six years I have been coming here and I have sat next to that chair every one of them, and I am not asking anybody to move it and I am not asking anybody why it is there, and I am saying that I have sat next to it.**"

Nobody asked him why. Nobody said anything about the chair, and four people in that room looked at it at about a quarter to eleven and four did not.

The young man of twenty-one said one thing, in about four seconds, to the table and not to her.

"**I have been coming up these stairs since I was seventeen and I have never once been in front of her before an item, and I used to think that was luck, and about four years ago I worked out that it is not luck and it is the order people ring in, and I have not worked out anything else since.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked him what the count is, and he did not give it, and he has not given it and was not asked for it and is not going to be. The holder of the room said nothing about it and wrote nothing about it.

At about twenty to eleven the landing went dark and stayed dark, and the light that came back through the door lit the second row of chairs and not the first, and nobody in that room got up to open the door because in nineteen years nobody in that room has got up to open the door, and the ninth chair was against the wall in the part of the room with no light in it.

---

A man of about thirty-two who works in a canteen in a building in the first of the four districts was in that warm room on the Wednesday evening and said one thing in nine seconds, to nobody, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.

"**There is a piece of paper on a board where I work that has got a number at the foot of it, and I have looked at the number four times, and it has not done anything, and I keep looking at it.**"

Nobody told him to stop looking at it. Nobody told him what the number is, and nobody in this case can either.

Nine people were in a warm room off a service road at half past six and none of them knew that anything had happened four miles away that morning. At a sink was a cook of twenty-seven who said nothing at all. Nearest the door, in the chair, was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to; the reason she comes is the heat; the heat costs more than it did; and both of those are correct.

A man of about thirty-seven who has been in that Saltmarket room four times said one thing in the warm room at about seven, in four seconds, to nobody.

"**There is a book four miles from here with fifty-one lines in it and it has not grown in about nine years, and four people in this city can tell you that and four cannot, and I have never been in that room and I have worked that out from this kitchen.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody told him which of the four he was, and he has not been back to that room since and nobody has asked him to.

A first-year of nineteen was at the counter and said one small unasked thing in four seconds, about a tin in a room four miles away that has not moved since a Wednesday four weeks ago, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her, and four people in that room had not known and about four had.

A woman of about thirty-one who does building maintenance was in that warm room on the Wednesday and said one thing in four seconds, to nobody.

"**A number went into a tin four weeks ago and nobody has told me what it was a number of, and about four people in this room know and about four do not, and I have not asked and I am not going to, because I have decided that not asking is the same as being owed nothing.**"

Nobody said whether that was the same. Nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing and has not been asked about anything for twelve weeks, and the asking has not been renewed and nobody has stopped it.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was in it for about twenty minutes on her way out and said nothing at all, and nobody asked her why, and she has not said.

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields was in that warm room and said one thing in four seconds, to nobody.

"**A number went into that tin four weeks ago and this morning it did not, and both of those are the same morning to the people who were not in that room, and I have been in two laundrettes today and nobody in either has heard about a tin.**"

Nobody has. Nobody told her about the tin and nobody has told anybody since, and the laundrettes are in Saltmarket and in the working Ashfields and the room is in Saltmarket and about four miles away.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, the last of them a lift mechanic's, the only promise on that board, and it has been up one hundred and seventy-four days without anybody touching it. The heating stays on and costs more than it did, the four names on that lease cost more than they did, and what a cook paid for a fortnight has not been paid back by a soul. **Nothing that happened in a room above a line in Saltmarket on this Wednesday was said in this room, and nobody brought any of it here.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at ten.

*379. Wednesday of week 136, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one sitting of four minutes an item, a book that did not open, no number into a tin, a chair against a wall, an ask twenty-four weeks old, and a reason given in a mouth that is not a rule.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening, all of them at a bench in Lattice Ward. One room above a line in a building in Saltmarket, shutters up, about ten past ten to about eleven, nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, a table, a tin with no lock on it, and a four-column book. Present: nine. Ninth chair: against the wall, back to the room, not moved, not looked at by anybody except one man of about forty-four, and nobody suggested moving any chair in that room in nineteen years. Items: six, four minutes each, two of them about rooms and neither of the two written down. Numbers given into the tin: none, and the tin therefore stands where it stood at the last sitting, four weeks ago, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why. Whether the book was opened: no, and she said so herself in about four seconds, and she said not this morning and not the morning after. Times anything has been written into it in nineteen years: about nine people, and each time a caller said the thing to her face. Times a caller asked her to write down a thing said to her by somebody else: one, this morning, at the fifth item, and she refused and he rang off. Times anybody has asked her for the rule she has not given: none this morning, and the last time was twenty-four weeks ago, at the seventeenth sitting. Times she has said for the last time that she will say it: six, and nothing was given in its place and no substitute was offered. One room off a service road from about half past six, about nine people. The rate held at nine and ten. The heating is on, dearer, four names on the lease dearer still, a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid. Room off a service road: four hundred and seventy-eight days. Card in a rail in Lattice Ward: four hundred and eighty-two days, a third object, in a third building, and the two have never been in one room. Hardboard, on two nails: twelve lines three hundred and ninety-eight days, the thirteen three hundred and forty-nine, the fourteen three hundred and fourteen, the fifteen two hundred and ninety-three, the sixteen two hundred and sixty-eight, the seventeen two hundred and fifty, the eighteen one hundred and ninety-six, the nineteen one hundred and seventy-four. Wall unchanged: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, fourteen names, none added, none removed, none rubbed off. Line one is a cook's and has never moved. Line nineteen is a lift mechanic's, the newest, and the only promise on that board. Eight things in a ring binder, unopened, unread, shut. The woman's page is unread and is shut with them. The master's fifth line empty, its drawer unopened. The Exchange sat on this day. Its last sitting before this one was a Wednesday twenty-eight days ago and its next one is a Wednesday twenty-eight days from today. The book stands at fifty-one lines, the tin at seventy-three, the count announced twenty-eight times, and the business nineteen years old. The ask stands at twenty-four weeks and was made at the seventeenth sitting. All four printed, none converted.**

*The book and the tin, as a record of two objects that stayed where they were.* **Lines in the book at the start of the morning: fifty-one. Lines in the book at the end: fifty-one, and it did not grow by one and it did not shrink. Whether it was opened: no, said by the holder of the room in about four seconds, at about ten past ten. Whether anybody in that room was told in advance that it would not open: nobody, and there is no way to be told, and that is not a decision and has not been a decision. Times a caller has asked her why it is shut: about four, over nineteen years, and no answer has improved on any other. Whether the book being shut is a judgement about the callers: it is not and she did not say it was and nobody said it was. The tin: one, no lock, and a figure it stands at, which is seventy-three, and which it has stood at since a Wednesday four weeks ago. What moved it then: one number, given in on the third of six items, being a count of doors in four buildings that a person had spent a fortnight on, and it was said once and written once. What moved it this morning: nothing. Times it has been counted by anybody since: none. The count announced: twenty-eight, of which twenty-three are the Wednesdays this book has sat on and five are older than the book, and the five are on a sheet she has never shown anybody. Owner: the book, and the tin, and the room, and nineteen years.**

*The reason, as a record of nine seconds said at about eleven.* **Said by: the holder of the room, unasked, at about eleven. What she said, in her order: that the last two months have been four rooms telling each other where they are; that nobody has come to this room wanting anything of her; that she does not know what that is; that she has not asked anybody about it; and that nothing in this morning was decided by anybody, least of all by her. Whether it is a rule: no, and there is no rule and nobody agreed one and none is going to be. Whether it was decided by her this morning: no. Whether it may be described as a change in her: no, and nothing written here describes it as one, and it is a fact about two months in a city and not about a person. Whether it is the same reason as four weeks ago: the same shape and not the same sentence, and the two have not been compared in that room and were not compared this morning. Improved on: by nobody, in that room or since. Asked about afterwards: by nobody. Thanked: by anybody, no. Owed: nothing. Owner: hers, and two months, and nobody.**

*The ask, as a record of a column that has not moved.* **Asked by: a man of about forty-four, of the seventeenth sitting, twenty-four weeks ago, being the number of days between the day he asked and this morning, and not a number of sittings. Asked for: a rule. What he was given: nine words that were not it. Times he has asked since: none. Times he has sat since: every one of them, and he was in the room this morning and looked at the ninth chair at about a quarter to eleven. Whether anybody in that room asked her for that rule on his behalf: no, and nobody has, and there was no substitute offered and none taken. Owner: him, and the ask, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody thanked anybody, and the holder of the room was not thanked and was not asked for anything and gave nothing and nothing was given in its place. The ninth chair was not moved and nobody in that room suggested moving any chair. The book in a room above a line in Saltmarket was not written in and the tin was not counted. A page of paper with about eleven lines on it in a woman's hand with four other hands in it was not in that room and was not discussed in it, and eight of its nine copies are unfinished. A hardboard on two nails in a different building was not written on, a card in a rail did not move, and the master's fifth line is still blank. A night rota of four sentences was not looked at by anybody in that room and was not mentioned. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 379.**
