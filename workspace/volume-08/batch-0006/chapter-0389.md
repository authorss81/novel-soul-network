# Chapter 389 — The Book Opened, And Nobody Stood Up

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and forty was the twenty-fourth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and **the book opened, and two of the sittings before it did not, and it grew by one line, because a person said a thing to her face in about four seconds, and she wrote it, and at the end of the morning the book was at fifty-two lines and the tin was at seventy-three and the count was twenty-nine of which twenty-four correspond, and nobody thanked her and nobody stood up and the ninth chair was against the wall with its back to the room when the morning began and it was against the wall with its back to the room when the morning ended.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward all day and has never been in that room and knows none of this and has not asked to be told.

His word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is five hundred and six days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature, and nobody asked them anything.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and the woman who holds that room said so out loud, as she has said at the start of every sitting for nineteen years, and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in. There was one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it, and it was there at about ten and it was there at about eleven and nobody looked at it more than once. There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, shut for about forty minutes of it and open for about four.

There were about eleven people. There was a young man of twenty-one who has climbed those stairs since he was seventeen. There was a young woman of twenty-one who has climbed them since she was fifteen and who has never been asked for anything and was not asked for anything this morning. And about five others who this morning are not being described.

She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece.

Six items, at four minutes apiece.

A man of about thirty-one who has been in that room four times was not there this morning and has not been since, and nobody has telephoned him, and nobody in that room has said that he was missed.

A woman of about thirty-eight who walks about nine miles to get to a building in a third of the four districts was in that warm room four miles away on the Wednesday evening and said nothing at all, and she comes for the temperature, and the temperature costs more, and both of those are correct.

A man of about fifty-three who runs a hardware shop in the working Ashfields was in that room and said, about a book, and nobody said it back to him.

**Somebody four miles from me put a line in a book this morning because he said a true thing out loud, and I have been in nine rooms in four districts since the summer and nobody in any of them has ever asked me a question, and I am not saying those are connected. I am saying I have noticed them next to each other and I do not like the shape.**

Nobody connected them. Nobody has, and he has not said anything else, and about four people in that room have said that a man who does not like the shape is a man who has noticed the shape, and four have said nothing, and the two have not met and he has not been asked which of them he was. Two of them turned out to be about rooms and not one of the two was taken down in any way by anybody, and no rule anywhere obliges her to and none has been made in nineteen years of mornings.

The first was a man of about thirty-one who wanted to know whether a room in the first of the four districts opens on a Monday, and he would not say which room, and he was not asked, and he telephoned off in about four minutes with nothing said twice.

The second was a person who wanted to know whether about four rooms in four districts were allowed to have gone their own way for five weeks after the water, and she said that she does not know, and that she has not been asked, and that she was not going to find out in four minutes, and she wrote nothing.

The third was a person who said a thing that took about nine seconds and then said the same thing again in a different order, and she wrote nothing, and at the end of the item he said *thank you* and she said *yes*, and neither of them said anything else.

The fourth was a person who asked her what a word meant. She told him what it meant, in about nine seconds, and wrote nothing, and he thanked her for telling him and she said yes, and about four people in that room noticed that the word is not a word anybody in this case has asked about and four did not notice did not.

The sixth was the youngest person in that room, who had not been in front of her before an item, and what he said was about a chair. **He had brought a chair up those stairs, which has happened about four times in nineteen years, and he said that the one he usually sits in has a leg gone and that he did not want to say anything about it in case somebody fixed it.**

Nobody fixed it. Nobody said that somebody should fix it, and nobody has fixed it, and the chair he brought is still in that room and is not anybody's chair and is not in any record, and he has brought it every week since.

---

The fifth item is the one that is in this case, and about nine people have put a line in that book in nineteen years and each of them said the thing to her face, and it took about four seconds, and it was not a request.

A man of about thirty-four came up those stairs that morning and had not been in that room before and said, to her face, in about four seconds:

"**I am the only person who knows where the stop is in four buildings, and nobody has ever asked me and I have never told anybody, and I have just told you, and I do not want anything.**"

She wrote it. It took her about four seconds and she wrote it in the fourth column and she did not ask him a question and he did not say anything else, and about four people in that room did not hear it and four said nothing did, and neither group has said anything about it since.

Nobody improved on it. Nobody said what a good thing it was, and nobody asked him what four buildings, and he has not been back, and she has not written anything else about him and there is no second line and no name against his.

**The book was at fifty-one lines when the morning began. It is at fifty-two now. It grew by one, and it grew because somebody said a thing to her face, which is the only way it has ever grown and the only way anybody in this case can say it grows.**

Nobody thanked her. Nobody said the book had been shut too long. Nobody said anything about two sittings, and she has not said anything about two sittings, and nothing about that has been said in that room and nobody in this case has asked her about it and nobody is going to.

---

**No number was given into the tin this morning. The tin stands at seventy-three and it stood at seventy-three at the last sitting four weeks ago, and it moved to that figure because a person spent a fortnight counting doors in four buildings and gave the count in, and nothing has been given into it since, and nobody counted it afterwards and nobody asked why it had not moved.**

The count came out as it comes out at every sitting, open book or shut. It is twenty-nine. Twenty-four of them are the Wednesdays this book has sat on, and five are older than the book, and the five are on a sheet she has never shown anybody and did not show this morning either.

The book is at fifty-two lines. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced twenty-nine times. The whole business is nineteen years old. **Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and every one of them is the whole of itself, and none of those four was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and was not given as one.**

---

The man of about forty-four, who put a question to her a long time back and got nine words back that were not the answer, and who has not put it since, was in that room this morning and looked at the chair against the wall at about half past ten and then looked at the floor.

**The ask is one hundred and ninety-six days old, which is twenty-eight weeks, counted from the day it was asked and not from the sitting before this one. Nobody in that room asked her for that rule. She has said six times that the last time she says it is the last time and she has kept that six times, and this morning nothing was put in the space it left and nothing was offered instead and nobody in that room wanted anything offered.**

A man of about thirty-one who has been in that room four times said, in about nine seconds, to the table, and it was not improved on, and it was not about the book.

"**I have noticed that a person can come up here and be written down, and I have noticed that nobody has ever come up here and been told what it did, and I have decided to stop noticing that and I would like it on the record that I have not stopped noticing it.**"

Nobody wrote it on the record. Nobody said there was a record, and he has not been back since, and about four people in that room noticed the saying of it and four have said nothing did not.

At about twenty to eleven somebody in that corridor asked her whether it was always a Wednesday, and she said four seconds of something and it was not the no-conversion rule and nobody heard it as one.

"**It is a Wednesday and it has been a Wednesday for nineteen years, and on the one day it is not a Wednesday this room does not open, and that is not a rule, it is a bus.**"

Nobody said anything about the bus. Nobody has said since that a bus is a good reason, and nobody has asked her what happens on the days the bus does not come, and nobody in this case knows.

---

A man of about twenty-three who works at a counter in a building in the second of the four districts was in that room and said one thing, and it was about a book, and nobody said it back to him.

**I have been in nine rooms in four districts over four months and there is a thing that happens in one of them that I have never been in, and I have decided that not knowing is cheaper than asking, and I have also decided that I would tell somebody if I were ever in it, and I have not worked out how those two are the same decision.**

A woman of about thirty-two who runs a food shop in a building in the first of the four districts was in that room and said one thing, and it was about a woman of about thirty-four and about nine hundred doors, and nobody improved on it.

**She has checked those doors for four years and she sat in a chair in here and said nothing, and I have been shut in my own shop twice this month by women ringing me to check things, and neither of those facts has ever been in the same room and I have just put them there and I would like somebody to know that I did it on purpose.**

Nobody said anything about that. Nobody has said it was right and nobody has said it was a mistake, and she has not said anything else about it, and about four people in that room have said that two things in one room is worth more than nine things in nine rooms and about four said nothing, and the two have not met and neither of them has been told.

The book four miles from that room is at fifty-two lines and the tin in the same room is at seventy-three, and she does not know either of those figures, and nobody in this case has offered them to her.

About nine people came in at half past six into a warm room off a service road, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to. The reason she is in that room is the heat. The heat costs more than it did. Both of those are correct.

By about half past six there were about nine people in that room and not one of them knew that four miles away, in that same morning, a book had been shut for about forty minutes and then opened for about four.

A man of about thirty-two who works in a canteen in the first of the four districts was in it and said one thing about a paragraph in about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.

"**There is a page in this city with a paragraph printed on the back of it that can join four rooms into one, and I have known about it for about a week, and I have said the word join out loud exactly twice and both times was to myself, and I have not said it to the woman in the food shop and I am not going to, and I would like somebody to know that I have decided that and not done it.**"

Nobody said anything about that. Nobody said it was the right decision and nobody said it was the wrong one, and about four people in that room have since said that keeping a thing to yourself is the only power anybody has in this city and four have not have said that it is not power, and the two have not met.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was in that room and said nothing at all, and she has said nothing there five days running, and nobody has asked her why and she has not said and nobody is going to.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that warm room and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for one hundred and twelve days, which is sixteen weeks, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, and the newest of them is a man of about twenty-nine's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board, and it is two hundred and two days old, and a book four miles away did not make it more or less true.

The heating is on and costs more, four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not going to be.

**A book opened for about four minutes this morning and gained one line, and the man who caused it has not been back, and the chair against the wall was where it was at the start of it and where it was at the end of it, and nobody stood up and nobody thanked her and the paragraph on the back of a page of paper was not discussed in that room by anybody.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
