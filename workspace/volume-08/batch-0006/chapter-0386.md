# Chapter 386 — A Man Of Fifty-Eight Says The Arithmetic Out Loud

A man of about fifty-eight with a bad hip went into a room of about nine people in a building in the second of the four districts on the Wednesday morning of this week and said, out loud, for about four minutes, that local independence is why the Hush happened, and **he is right about the arithmetic and wrong about a number and wrong about which district, and nobody in that room can prove him wrong about the arithmetic because the history is not in this case, and nobody thanked him, and he was not turned and he was not defeated and he did not get a new wing.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward all day and was not in that room and was not told about it until the Friday.

His word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and ninety-nine days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday for the temperature, and nobody asked any of them anything.

---

The room has about nine chairs in it and the same furniture as every other room in this city. Nine people were in it at about ten past ten on the Wednesday. They had asked to go through the printed paragraph word by word, and nobody said they could not, and then a man of about fifty-eight with a bad hip was let in, and nobody had invited him, and nobody asked him to leave.

Nobody asks him to leave anything. He has never been asked and he has never asked, and his office has kept emergency authority through every year of this case and has never once used it in a room, and everybody in that room knew both of those things before he opened his mouth.

---

He stood up to say it, which took him a while, and then he said it, and it took about four minutes, and it was not interrupted.

"**Here is the arithmetic and I have done it four times. In the weeks the Hush was in force this city had about two hundred and eleven rooms with their own supply. About nine hundred buildings lost something for a period. Every one of those two hundred and eleven was in a district that had never been joined to anything, because nothing had been joined to anything, and that is the only reason two hundred and eleven rooms kept working while nine hundred buildings did not. Had those two hundred and eleven been joined into nine, then when the supply went, nine would have gone and not two hundred and eleven, and nine is a number a city can find in a morning and two hundred and eleven is not.**"

He sat down. Nobody said anything. Nobody asked him a question for about nine seconds, and then a woman of about thirty-eight who made the first of the nine hand copies asked him one, and it was the only question anybody asked him all day, and it took her about four seconds.

"**What is the number?**"

He said the number. He said two hundred and eleven, and he said the outer district, and both of those are the things he was wrong about and neither of them was available to anybody in that room.

---

Nobody in that room can prove him wrong about the arithmetic. The history is not in this case. There is no ledger in it, no register, no sheet, no wall binder with the weeks in it, and there has never been one in any room any of these people has been in, and about four people in that room said out loud on the way out that they believed him and four have said nothing said that believing a man is not the same as being able to check him, and the two groups have not met.

He is wrong about a number and it is one too many, and it is one too many because a building in the district he named was counted twice in one week by two offices and both counts were added, and the double is in a place that is not in this case and that nobody in that room has seen and that he has not seen either.

He is wrong about which district. The district that kept the most was not the one he said, and the reason it was not the one he said is a boundary that was redrawn in a week in the middle of that period, and nobody in this case knows which way and nobody in that room asked.

**Nobody has corrected him. Nobody has said the number is one too many, and nobody has said which district, and about four people in that room have gone to somebody else in this city to ask and four did not have not, and nobody has come back with anything.**

A man of about forty-two who types in an office of nine people in a building in Saltmarket said, at about twelve, and it was to a chair, and nobody said it back to him.

**I have been keeping a count of my own in a notebook for about four years and it is wrong and I know it is wrong and I have never thrown it out. He has said a number out loud to a room full of people and it is wrong by one. Mine is wrong by more than I can put a figure on and I have stopped counting how wrong. I do not know what the difference is and I have been thinking about it since he sat down and I have not got it.**

Nobody got it. Nobody asked him where his notebook is and nobody has asked, and it is not in this case and is not on anything, and about four people in that room have since said that a wrong number said out loud is better than a right number kept, and four have said the opposite, and the two have not met and neither of them has said it to him.

A woman of about thirty-eight who made the first of the nine hand copies, who asked him the only question anybody asked him that day, said nothing more, and she has not said anything about the arithmetic since, and about four people have asked her why not and the rest have said nothing.

---

Nobody thanked him. Nobody said he had done the right thing and nobody said he had done the wrong thing. Nobody has said since that he was right, and nobody has said since that he was wrong, and a man of about fifty-three who has been in that room since it was two rooms has said out loud that he would rather be wrong by a good margin than right about nothing, and about four people heard that and the others did not did not.

He is not defeated. He is not turned. He has not been told to leave and he was not asked to and he will not be, and his office did not write the printed paragraph on the back of a page of paper, has never seen it, did not ask for it, and cannot say who printed it, and he said that out loud in another room in a fortnight and said it again in that room on the Wednesday, in the same words, because a man saying a thing twice about a thing nobody can explain is not a defect.

---

A man of about thirty-three was in that room and had been in it since about eleven, and he is the man who has held four buildings and about nine hundred doors since the Thursday of a week in the early part of this year, with no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority behind him.

**He has needed nothing from anybody in one hundred and thirty-two days and he has been given nothing in one hundred and thirty-two days. He is not a villain. He is owed nothing. He is not thanked on that Wednesday and he is not thanked in this case.**

He said one thing, at about a quarter to twelve, and it was the only thing he said in that room all morning, and it took longer than about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it.

"**I have put a light on in four buildings for one hundred and thirty-two days and nobody has ever asked me what it costs me and nobody would thank me if I turned it off, and I have worked out over that time that I am not being paid to do it and that nobody is waiting for me to do it, and I would like that written down somewhere so that it stops being a surprise every time somebody in this city asks me why.**"

Nobody wrote it down. Nobody asked him what the cost is, and he has not said, and about four people in that room have since said that a man who is not paid and is not thanked is a man doing it for something and about four have said he is doing it because nobody else is, and the two have not met.

**A boundary that moved once in a fortnight is a boundary that can move again, and I have four vans and eleven men and a lease that says an address, and I have worked out that the address is the only part of that business I am sure of, and I have not been sure of an address since I was twenty-four.**

Nobody said which address he means. Nobody has asked him and nobody is going to, and about four people in that room have said that a lease is a strong thing and four have said that a lease is a piece of paper in a drawer in a building in the third of the four districts, and the two have not met and he has not been asked which one he believes.

"**He has been in four of the buildings I have been in and I have never seen him there at the same time as anybody, and I have always assumed that meant he was careful. I am no longer assuming that. I am now assuming he was the only one there, which is a different thing and a worse one.**"

Nobody said it. Nobody asked him what he is assuming now instead, and he has not said, and nobody in this case has checked whether he is right, and he may not be.

---

A man of about twenty-one who works in a counter in a shop in the working Ashfields was in that room and said one thing, and it was about the man of fifty-eight, and nobody said it back to him.

**I have listened to about four of those arguments in my life and I have never once heard the person they were aimed at answer, and I have started to think that is what an argument is for, and I do not think that is a thing I would want if I were the one being aimed at.**

A woman of about thirty-nine who works in a pharmacy in Saltmarket was in that room and said one thing, and it was in answer to nothing anybody had asked, and nobody improved on it.

**I have sold nine things across that counter in nineteen years and I could not tell you what any of them is for, and on Wednesday a man said a number out loud to nine people and I wrote it on the back of my hand, and it is still on my hand, and I have not washed it, and I am not going to.**

Nobody asked what the number was. Nobody in this case can tell her, and she has not washed her hand and nobody has asked her to, and about four people in that room have said that a number on a hand is the only copy of that arithmetic in this city and four have not said anything have said nothing, and the two have not met and she has not heard either of them.

The number is on a hand in a warm room in the second of the four districts and it is not on anything else in this city, and the man who said it is a man of about fifty-eight with a bad hip and he does not know it is on a hand and nobody has told him and nobody is going to.

About nine people came in at half past six into a warm room off a service road. A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the reason she comes is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was in it for half an hour and said, in about four seconds, and it was about a man who does a job at night in four buildings and was not in any way a reply to anybody.

"**There is a man who does a night round in four buildings and has done it every night since about the turn of the year, and none of the nine of us knows what he is paid, and I have assumed for about a month that it is nothing, and I have worked out this week that assuming that is a thing I have been doing rather than a thing I know.**"

Nobody said what he is paid. Nobody has asked him and nobody is going to, and about four people in that room have said that the nine hundred doors have a number on a company's list and four did not notice have said that a number is not a wage, and the two have not met.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that room and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for one hundred and five days, which is fifteen weeks, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields was in it and said, about a Wednesday, and it was about a man who had shut for a morning and had not been paid for it, and nobody improved on it.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, and the newest of them is a man of about twenty-nine's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board, and it is one hundred and ninety-five days old, and a man of fifty-eight has not made it more or less true.

The heating is on and costs more, four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not going to be.

**A man of fifty-eight said four minutes of arithmetic into a room that did not want it and nobody could check him and nobody thanked him, and a man of about thirty-three said one thing about a light and nobody wrote it down, and the two of them are the only two things anybody said in that room that anybody has repeated since.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
