# Chapter 384 — The Seventh Friday, And A Post That Stayed Bare

A floor on the second floor of a building in Crown Terrace was run for the seventh time in five months on the Friday evening, and **the post by the corridor end has now been bare for six weeks, which is forty-two days, and the run went, and about eleven people were on it, seven of them on the company's list and four on none, and one of the seven said at the bottom of a ramp that he would not be on the next one and gave a reason about a shift, and nobody asked him a question and nobody thanked anybody.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last at about six in the evening. He was at a repair shop in Lattice Ward until about half past four and was four miles away from all of it and knows nothing about a post.

His word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and ninety-four days old, it is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday for the temperature, and nobody asked them anything.

---

There were nine folding chairs on that floor. There were about eleven people on it, and seven of the eleven were on the company's list, and four were on none of anything. There was a corridor with a fire door at each end of it and about nine doors along it that are not opened during the run. There was a post at the corridor end with nothing on it. There was a desk by the fire door with four drawers, and the second drawer on the left of that desk has a laminated sheet in it that has been in that drawer for about two weeks.

There was nobody from the company in that building. There has not been anybody sent from the company that buys the training to watch a run in about four years, and the only person from that company who comes into that room is the woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard on who calls the run, and she has said out loud that she does not know who it is for, and nobody has asked her since and nobody is going to.

---

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front.

A woman of about thirty-one who does building maintenance was in that room and said, before the run, and nobody took it up, and she was not asked anything before she said it or after.

**I am in here because it is warm and because I am not wanted in a corridor, and I have worked out that those are the same reason, and I have not told anybody which of the two I would rather it was, and I am not going to, and I would like that to be the end of it.**

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her which she would rather, and about four people in that corridor have noticed that she said it and four have not, and she has not been in that room since and nobody has asked her why. Nothing was read off anything. Nothing was handed to anybody. Nobody counted for anybody and nobody prompted anybody, and about four of the nine did the whole of it without being told, which is the same number as the last four Fridays, and nobody has compared this Friday with any other Friday and there is no way in that building of knowing which of the eleven knows it is the seventh.

**Nobody in that room referred to any other Friday. Not one of the about eleven, and not the woman of about thirty-six, and about four people in that room could tell you how many Fridays it has been and about four could not.**

The run finished and nobody said how long it took. Nobody said anything at all for about nine seconds afterwards, the way nobody has said anything for about four years, and about nine people stood still and about four of them thought about the sixth week of a bare post and four did not.

---

A man of about twenty-five, who is on the company's list and is in that room because a man of about twenty-seven asked him once in a car park, said, at the bottom of the ramp at about ten to nine, and he was not stopped, and nobody improved on it.

"**I am not going to be on this one next Friday. I have got a shift and I took the shift, and I want it on the record that I took the shift rather than that I was let go from the room, and there is no record and I know there is no record.**"

A woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard on who calls the run said it back, and it was the only thing anybody said about it all evening, and she did not make it better.

"**Good. That is two of the seven. I am not going to say anything else about it and I am not going to try to get you back in the spring.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked him what the shift was, and nobody asked her what the other one was, and nobody in that corridor understood that she had said a number and about four people noticed and the others did not did not.

---

Nobody asked the four who are on no list why they come. That was settled in an earlier fortnight and it has not moved, and none of the other three has been asked since and none is going to be, and that is not a favour and it is not an arrangement and it is on nothing.

The man of about twenty-seven who comes when he can get the hours, and who has no work of his own after about six on a Friday, was on the floor that night and did the whole of the run and said nothing at all, and three Fridays ago he said in that corridor that it was better with a bare post and that he was not going to say it twice.

A man of about forty-three who drives for a firm that has nothing to do with the company was on the floor and said, to a chair at about ten to nine, and nobody answered it, and it was not about the post and not about the shift.

"**I drove a van for four nights last week and I came up here on the Friday of the same week, and both of those are true, and about four people in this room do one of them and about four do the other, and about four of us do both.**"

Nobody said which four. Nobody asked him what the firm was, and he has never been asked and is not going to be, and about four people in that room have said since that doing both is not a state of mind and about four have said nothing.

A man of about thirty-six who works in that building came up for the run and went down again and said, in the lobby at about a quarter to nine, and nobody took it up, and he has now been asked once in about four years whether he is on the list and has said no in about four seconds.

"**There is a sheet in a drawer in this building that came back in a bag about two weeks ago and it is the fourth thing I know about that this room does not put anywhere anybody can see it, and I am not asking anybody to do anything about the drawer.**"

Nobody asked him what the other three things were. He has not said and nobody has asked since, and the drawer does not lock and there is nothing in that building that keeps a register of what is in a drawer.

---

A woman of about thirty who is in that room about twice a month said, at the top of the stairs, in about four seconds, and it was the fourth time she has said something in that room and nobody in it knows how many times.

"**Four years I have been in this room and I still do not know what a run is for, and I have said that before in this corridor, and I am saying it again because somebody has just said a number out loud and I would like the number to have company.**"

Nobody told her what a run is for. Nobody said the number was a good one, and she has not said anything else in that room, and about four people on that stairwell heard her and four have not said anything did not.

A woman of about thirty-eight who was in that room for two years and who said nothing in it on six Fridays and then said, on the seventh was in it again and said nothing, and about four people in that room noticed that she had said nothing and four did not notice did not, and that is twice now.

A man of about twenty-nine who comes on a Friday and who has been doing the run properly for about four years without being told once said nothing, as he has said nothing in that room for the whole of it, and nobody has asked him why and nobody is going to.

A man of about forty-four who is in that room and who was asked a question there three weeks ago and answered it in about four seconds said nothing on this Friday, and he has not spoken in that room since, and nobody has asked him anything else and nobody has said that it would be a shame if he stopped.

---

They went out through a lobby, up a ramp, and onto a forecourt with about nine cars on it and a man in a booth who has never once been inside that building and does not know any of the eleven.

A woman of about twenty-six who stacks shelves in a place that opens at eight in the second of the four districts was in that room and said, to the man in the booth, in about four seconds, and he has never been inside and would not be told.

"**You have never been up there. I have been up there about nine times. Neither of us has been told what goes on up there, and I am not asking you to come, I am saying that you and I are the same about it and I have never said that to anybody and you are a stranger and that is why.**"

He said nothing. Nobody took it up, and nobody thanked her, and about four of the eleven have said since that it was four seconds well spent and four have said nothing, and the man in that booth has not been asked about it and would not be told.

A man of about fifty-eight who has been in that room for four years and who came for the sake of being in it said, at the bottom of the ramp and it was about a season, and nobody took it up.

"**I am not going to be in this room after the turn of the year and I have not told anybody that, and I am telling nine strangers on a forecourt because there is nobody else in this city I could tell, and I said this same shape of thing to this same forecourt a while back and nobody has said anything about having said it before.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked him what changed between the two times, and he has not been asked, and nobody in this case has said that a man who says the same shape of thing twice is a man with something to say, and about four of the eleven have said he looked tired and about four have said nothing.

A man of about thirty-eight who sells plumbing supplies from a unit in a yard in the working Ashfields was in that room and said one thing, and it was about a Friday, and nobody said it back to him.

**I have been in nine of those rooms over four years and I have never once been asked why I come, and I have worked out this week that nobody is going to, and I have also worked out that I would not tell them if they did, and I do not know which of those two facts is the one I mind.**

A woman of about thirty-two who runs a food shop in a building in the first of the four districts was in that room and said one thing, and it was to the woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors, and neither of them said anything else.

**I have had a woman ring me about a card on a wall and I have had a woman ring me about a paragraph on the back of a page, and both of them said right, and I have shut in both cases for the same reason, and I have not told my staff and my staff do not need to know.**

Nobody said the two were the same thing. Nobody said they were different, and she has not mentioned either again, and the woman of about thirty-four has not asked her about either, and about four people in that room have said that a shopkeeper who shuts is a shopkeeper who is alone and about four said nothing.

The woman of about thirty-four has not said one word about the doors in that room for six days, and nobody has asked her, and she has not left.

About nine people came in at half past six into a warm room off a service road, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to. The reason she is in that room is the heat. The heat costs more than it did. Both of those are correct.

A first-year of nineteen was at a counter in Lattice Ward and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds about a man who did not come next time, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her and nobody asked her how she knew.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was in that warm room for about twenty minutes and said, in about nine seconds about a building of nine flats over a canteen, and nobody said anything back.

"**A man came into our building on Tuesday and asked the fitters which of about nine rooms they were working for, and one of them said, and the other eight did not, and that man went back down and told somebody and I do not know who, and nobody has rung us about it.**"

Nobody said whether that mattered. Nobody told her that it did not, and about four people in that room have said that eight fitters not knowing is a Tuesday and four have not have said that somebody went back down.

Nineteen lines and two nails, and the newest of the nineteen is eleven words a man of twenty-nine wrote about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and is one hundred and ninety days old.

The heating is on and costs more, the lease costs more for four names than it did, and the fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved and is not going to be.

**A floor was run for the seventh time in five months, the post has been bare for forty-two days, one of the seven on the list is not coming next time because of a shift he chose, the other six of the seven are coming, and the four who are on no list are coming, and there is no way in that building of telling those two groups apart from the outside.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
