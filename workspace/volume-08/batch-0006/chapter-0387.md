# Chapter 387 — The Eighth Friday, And Two Of The Seven

A floor on the second floor of a building in Crown Terrace was run for the eighth time in five months on the Friday evening of this week, and **the post by the corridor end has now been bare for seven weeks, which is forty-nine days, and two of the seven people who were on that company's list two weeks ago were not on the floor, and nobody in that building has asked either of them whether they are coming back, and the run went, and about nine people were on it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a repair shop in Lattice Ward until about half past four and was four miles from all of it and knows nothing about a corridor.

His word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is five hundred and one days old and it is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday for the temperature, and nobody asked them anything.

---

There were nine folding chairs on that floor and about nine people on it. Five of the nine were on the company's list and four were on none, and the two who were on that list seven days ago and are not on it now have not been spoken to by anybody in that building, and neither of them has been spoken to by anybody in this case.

One of them took a shift. One of them did not say why, and nobody in that building has asked either, and the woman who calls the run said out loud at the bottom of a ramp that she is not going to try to get either of them back, and nobody argued with her and nobody thanked her.

---

The post has been bare for forty-nine days.

The post has been bare for forty-nine days.

A man of about twenty-one who works in a counter in a shop in the working Ashfields was in that room and said one thing, and it was about nine chairs, and nobody said it back to him.

**There were nine of us on a Friday in the summer and there are four of us on a Friday now and I have worked out that the difference is not anybody leaving, it is that two of them were always only coming for the heat, and I have not told either of them that I have worked it out.**

Nobody told them. Nobody has, and he has not said which two, and about four people in that corridor have said that a man who works that out about a room is a man who has been counting and four have said nothing, and the two have not met and he has not been asked. There is a laminated sheet in the second drawer on the left of a desk by the fire door that has been in that drawer for about three weeks, and the drawer does not lock, and nothing in that building keeps a register of what is in a drawer, and nobody has asked to see it and nobody has asked for it to go back up.

About four people in that room look at the post every time they walk past and about four do not, and that has not changed in seven weeks and the two groups have never spoken about it and are not going to.

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard on. Nothing was read off anything and nothing was handed to anybody and nobody counted for anybody. About four of the nine did the whole of it without being told, and nobody said how long it took, and nobody said anything for about nine seconds afterwards, the way nobody has for about four years.

**Nobody in that room referred to any other Friday, and about four of the nine know this is the eighth and about four do not, and there is no way in that building of working out which four.**

---

A woman of about thirty who is in that room about twice a month was in it and said, before the run, in about nine seconds, and nobody said it back to her.

"**I was here two weeks ago and I am not going to be here in a fortnight, and I have told nobody except you nine people and a ramp, and I am telling you that so that you can decide whether to change the chairs, and I am not asking you to.**"

Nobody changed the chairs. Nobody asked her what she meant by a fortnight, and about four people in that corridor have worked out that the room has lost about four of its regulars in about four months and the other four have not noticed, and neither group has said anything to anybody.

A man of about twenty-seven who comes when he can get the hours, and who has no work of his own after about six on a Friday, and who said in an earlier week that it was better with a bare post and that he was not going to say it twice, was on the floor and said nothing at all.

A man of about forty-three who drives four nights for a firm that has nothing to do with the company said, at about ten to nine, and it was not about the two who were not there.

"**Four of us do the four nights and this and about four of us do something else, and I have never asked the other three what the something else is and I am not going to, and I would like that to be a thing that is normal rather than a thing that is generous.**"

Nobody said it was generous. Nobody said it was normal, and he has not asked them and they have not said, and about four people in that room have noticed that he is the one who mentions it and the rest have not.

A man of about thirty-six who works in that building came up for the run and said, in the lobby, and it was about the same drawer and about a different thing, and nobody improved on it.

"**There is a thing in a drawer in this building that came back in a bag and there is a post with nothing on it, and about four of us in this building have now quietly decided that things go in drawers in this city, and none of us decided it and I would like somebody to notice that we decided it.**"

Nobody noticed. Nobody said anything about it, and nobody has said anything about it since, and about four people in that room have since said that he is right about that and about four have said that a man who does the whole of a run every Friday has no business reading the building.

---

A man of about forty-four who is in that room and who answered a question there four weeks ago in about four seconds has not spoken in it since, and nobody has asked him anything, and about four people in that room notice his silence and about four do not, and the noticing has not once been mentioned to him.

A man of about twenty-nine who comes on that Friday and who has been doing the run properly for about four years without being told once said nothing again. A woman of about thirty-eight who broke a silence of six Fridays in an earlier week said nothing on the Friday after that and has said nothing since, and about four people have noticed the pattern and about four have not.

A man of about fifty-eight who had been in that room for four years and who said the same shape of thing twice on that forecourt was not on that floor on this Friday, and nobody in that room said he had said anything on a forecourt, and one person looked at the door.

They went out through a lobby, up a ramp, and onto a forecourt with about nine cars on it and a man in a booth who has never been inside that building and does not know any of the nine and has never been asked to and would not be told.

A woman of about twenty-six who stacks shelves in a place that opens at eight in the second of the four districts said, to that man in about four seconds, and she had said a similar shape of thing to him a fortnight before and he had not been told and has not said anything since.

"**You were not here last time either, and I have been twice, and I have worked out that we are the two kinds of person who come to a building like this and I do not know what the difference is and I have stopped looking.**"

He said nothing. Nobody took it up, and about four of the nine have said since that it was four seconds well spent and four have said nothing, and he has not been asked about it and would not be told.

A man of about twenty-three who works at a counter in a building in the second of the four districts was in that room and said one thing, and it was about a list, and nobody said it back to him.

**I have been in nine rooms in four districts over four months and I could not tell you one thing that any of them is for, and I have stopped minding, and I have also noticed that the stopping is the only thing any of them has ever produced.**

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields was in that room and said one thing, and it was about a morning she lost, and nobody said it back to her.

**I lost a morning in an earlier week and I have just been told by a woman of about thirty-two that if the four of us who lost that morning stood up in a room nothing would happen, and I have thought about that for two days and I have decided she is right and that I am not going to be one of the four, and I would like both of those to be on the same piece of paper.**

Nobody put them on a piece of paper. Nobody said she was right and nobody said she was wrong, and about four people in that room have said that a woman who has decided not to be one of the four has still not been paid and four did not notice have said nothing, and the two have not met and she has not heard either.

The four who lost that morning are not the four who are in that room and have not been asked, and nobody has put the two groups together, and the woman of about thirty-two who said the thing is not one of the four and has not been asked to be.

About nine people came in at half past six into a warm room off a service road. A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to. The reason she comes is the heat. The heat costs more than it did. Both of those are correct.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that room and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for one hundred and seven days, which is fifteen weeks and two days, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.

A first-year of nineteen was at a counter in Lattice Ward and said one small unasked thing in about four seconds about a room where nobody has asked anybody anything for about four years, and it was not improved on and nobody thanked her and nobody asked her how she knew.

A woman of about thirty-nine who works in a pharmacy in Saltmarket was in that warm room for half an hour and said, about a bus in about four seconds, and it was not improved on and nobody took it up.

A man of about fifty who owns a garage in the Lower Wards was in it and said nothing at all for the first time since the water went down, and nobody has asked him why and he has not said.

Nineteen lines and two nails, and the newest of the nineteen is eleven words a man of twenty-nine wrote about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and is one hundred and ninety-seven days old.

The heating is on and costs more, the lease costs more for four names than it did, and the fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved and is not going to be.

**A floor was run for the eighth time in five months, with about nine people on it instead of about eleven, and the post has been bare for forty-nine days, and the two who are not coming back have not been asked, and about four of the nine know which Friday number it was and about four do not.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
