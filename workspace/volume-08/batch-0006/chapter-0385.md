# Chapter 385 — A Printed Rule, Correctly, About A Man Who Was Not There

On the Monday morning of week one hundred and thirty-nine a printed standard in a wall binder in the third of the four districts was invoked, correctly, about a man who is in a room of about nine people in the second of those districts and who was not there, and **it cost about four people a morning, and the man found out at about eleven from somebody else, and he did not say he would have come, and nobody has said he would have, and nobody has said he would not.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward and was not in either of those rooms and was not told about any of it until the Wednesday.

His word was at nine and ten. The room off a service road is four hundred and ninety-seven days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Monday for the temperature, and nobody asked any of them what they had been doing.

---

The day's work, in the order it happened: a room of about nine people in a building in the second of the four districts set a morning aside to go through a printed paragraph word by word, and a person of sixty whose job it is to ask questions came to that room at about ten, and a printed standard was read out from a binder on a wall, and the morning did not take place, and about four people had come for it.

Then: about four people went home. Then: a telephone rang in a different building in the same district at about eleven, and a man was told.

---

The standard is in a binder on a wall of an office in the third of the four districts, on a shelf with about nine other binders on it, and it has a number and a date on the front and the front of the volume it is in has never been read by anybody in this case. It is one of about nine on that shelf and the office has about nine rooms' worth of paper in it and a counter and a person at the counter whose job is to ask questions.

The person at the counter is sixty. She asked one question, in about nine seconds, and she read the standard out afterwards, in about four seconds, and nobody in that room said she was wrong.

"**You have a name in that book and you have not given him notice in person, and the rule says a room that cannot give notice in person before the hour it has set may not meet at that hour to do it, and I am not asking whether you want to do it. I am telling you that you may not.**"

Nobody argued with her. Nobody asked her which year of the standard she was reading, and about four people in that room had not known it was in a binder and about four had, and nobody has said since which of the two she was right about.

---

The standard says, in the words on that wall, that a room which has entered a person's name in its book as the person it will act for is not to do anything in that person's name without first giving that person notice in person; that notice given in a room is not notice; and that a room which cannot give that notice in person before the hour it has set is not to meet at that hour to do the thing.

It is printed, and it has a number, and anybody competent can point at it. There are about four hundred competent people in this city and this case has no way of counting them and does not try, and the person of sixty is one of them and is a person with a job and holds no post of any kind and is owed nothing and is not a category.

**She was right. The man is in the book, he was entered before that room had anybody else in the book, he was not there that morning, and nobody had given him notice in person, and there is no version of the morning that is lawful without it. Nobody in that room has said that the standard is a good standard and nobody in that room has said that it is a bad one.**

And a woman of about fifty who types in an office of nine people in the second of the four districts was one, and she had brought a sheet of paper with nothing on it.

Nobody said the four of them had wasted their morning. Nobody offered anybody a different morning, and there is no line in this city that a person can be paid on for a morning they did not get, and about four people have since said that the rule is the rule and the rest have said that whoever put that name in that book put it there for a reason, and the two have not met.

A man of about twenty-one who works in a counter in a shop in the working Ashfields was in that office and said one thing, and it was about the binder, and nobody said it back to him.

**There are nine binders on that shelf and every one of them has a number on it, and I have worked out this week that the number is the only thing anybody in that building can give you, and that is not a small thing to have worked out about a room where your own name is a promise somebody else made.**

Nobody said it was sensible. Nobody said it was not, and about four people in that office have said she was right and about four have said that a rule you do not know about is the only kind worth having, and the two have not met.
