# Chapter 381 — Four Rooms Know It Is There

About eleven people in three of the four districts are still carrying the week that water took off them, and a printed paragraph on the back of a page of paper was read out once, in one room, in the last week, and **it has not been read out in any room in any of those three districts since, and about four people have heard of it and about four have not, and there is no procedure in this city for telling a room a thing that nine people already agreed to.**

Nobody said the first half out loud. A man of about thirty-one who has come to that room about four times said, at about one o'clock, and it was about the copying, and nobody said it back to him.

**Eight of nine people are copying one page and not one of them will read it out, and I have worked out that this is not a secret because a secret has a person keeping it, and I would like somebody who is in one of those nine rooms to tell me tonight whether I am wrong, and I am not going to telephone you.**

Nobody telephoned anybody that afternoon. Nobody has telephoned anybody in those nine rooms since and nobody is going to, and about four people in that room have said that the offer was generous and four have said it was a trick, and the two have not met and he has not been told which of them said what. The week the eleven lost is the week that began on the Monday of the week before that, and it has not been paid by anybody, and it is not going to be paid by anybody, and about four of the eleven have never been asked to say what it was.

Nobody has asked them and nobody is going to. Seven of the eleven said their own account out loud on the Thursday of last week, in a room in the third of the four districts, in about four minutes and not in turn, and the other four were not in that room and were not asked and have not been spoken to since.

A woman of about twenty-nine who answers a telephone four hours a day in an office of nine people in the second of the four districts has the ringing sheet for the week of the water on her desk and did not copy it out and has never been asked to.

She said, about it to about four people in an office on the Monday afternoon, and it took her longer than four seconds, and nobody said it back to her.

"**I have got about eleven names on a sheet of paper in a drawer in this room and I did not write them down, I wrote them on the sheet, and every one of those eleven rang this room at some point in that week and about four of them rang about money and about four rang about a bus, and there is nobody in this city I could ring who could do anything for any of the eleven, and I have thought about it for four days and I have not rung one of them.**"

Nobody said she should. Nobody asked her which four rang about money, and about four have since said she was right not to ring and four have said nothing, and the two groups have not met, and she has not rung any of them.

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields answered, at half past two on the Monday to a man of about fifty who owns a garage in the Lower Wards, who had come in about a coat, and it was not improved on.

"**My floor is dry and my rent is the same and I have been shut four days this year, three of them for water, and I am not asking anybody for the four days. I am telling you because you are the only person I have seen this week who was also shut, and that is the whole of why I am saying it.**"

He said his garage was shut for two of them. Then neither of them said anything else, and about four other people in that garage have since said that the two of them are owed something and about four have said that nobody in this city has a line to pay it on, and the two have not met.

Then the paragraph.

It is about fifty words of print at the foot of the back of a page of paper with about eleven lines in a woman's hand in it, four of the lines in four other hands. It was read out once, on the Thursday of last week, by the woman of about thirty-eight who made the first of the nine hand copies, in a flat voice, in a room in the third of the four districts, to about nine people.

Since then it has not been read out anywhere. Not in a second room and not in the same room. There is no copy of the back of it, and nobody has asked for one, and the nine people who agreed to the front of that page are in nine rooms in four districts and about four of them have spoken to another person about the front of it and none of them has spoken to another person about the back.

A man of about twenty-three who works at a counter in a building in the second of the four districts said the thing that is true about it, and he said it to a wall, and nobody took it up.

"**Anybody competent in this city can read a printed paragraph and act on it. There are about four hundred of us. Not one of us has a place to put a thing that says no.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

Nobody said that was a discovery and nobody said it was obvious, and about four people in the building have since said that the paragraph is only a problem when somebody needs a room, and four have not have said that a paragraph nobody can object to is not a paragraph anybody has to worry about, and the two have not spoken.

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her which room had the finished one, and about four people in that room noticed that she had not asked and the rest have said nothing did not, and she has not asked since and is not going to.

A first-year of nineteen was at a counter in a repair shop in Lattice Ward and said one small unasked thing at four in the afternoon, in about four seconds, and nothing was added to it and she was not thanked.

"**Nobody has said it out loud. I have been in three rooms since Thursday and not one of them has said the word merge, and I have been waiting to hear somebody say it and I have started to think that saying it is the whole of what it takes and that nobody is going to.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody asked her what she would do if somebody said it, and she has not said since and nobody has asked her, and she is owed nothing.

"**I have got a piece of paper on that wall and I do not know who printed it, and now there is a thing in a room somewhere that says four rooms can be joined up, and I have not put those two things next to each other in my head and I am not going to, and I would like somebody to notice that I have decided that.**"

A woman of about thirty-five who runs a food shop in a building in the first of the four districts, who was in that canteen, said one thing back, and it was the first time two people in that canteen had said, to each other in about four years.

"**I have noticed. I have also decided the same thing and about four days before you, and neither of us is going to say it, and if we ever do it is going to be in a room with nobody in it.**"

Nobody laughed. Nobody said that was the sensible thing, and the two of them have not mentioned it since, and about four people in that canteen have said they heard and the others said nothing.

A man of about fifty-four who came in out of the working Ashfields said, to the canteen man after that, and nobody took it up.

**I have a shop of my own and I have been in nine of those rooms since the summer and I have never once said the word merge out loud, and I have decided that is going to be my position, and I have not told my wife that I have decided it.**

Nobody asked his wife. Nobody said it was a good position and nobody said it was a bad one, and he has not said the word out loud since and is not going to, and nobody in this case has told anybody what his position is, and he has not told anybody either.

**You said you had not rung any of the eleven.**

**No. I have thought about it every night since and I have not rung one of them, and I would like it recorded that the reason is not that I do not have the number.**
