# Chapter 383 — The Question He Was Not Asked

It was a Thursday, and the woman whose Thursday it is was four miles away in a workroom doing work that gets paid for, and **at ten past nine on the Thursday morning a man of about thirty-five who rents the upper floor of a building in the first of the four districts stood at a counter in Lattice Ward for about nine seconds with a question in his mouth and did not ask it, and nobody in that shop noticed for about half an hour, and nothing about the Thursday was spent.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about six in the evening. He was at a counter in a repair shop in Lattice Ward from about nine until about six, and about nine people came and went.

Nine and ten, the whole day, the same two words the same number of times. That room off a service road is four hundred and ninety-three days old and warm, and about four people came in on the Thursday for the temperature, and nobody asked any of them a question.

---

About half past nine on the Wednesday there had been a man at that counter. On the Thursday there was one again, and he was the same man, and he came in at ten past nine and stood about a foot from the counter and put his hands flat on it and said nothing.

He is a man of about thirty-five and he rents the upper floor of a building in the first of the four districts, over a canteen, and about three weeks ago he ate in that canteen every day for about four years and read a card on a board and did not ask who put it up, and he has not asked since and nobody has asked him why not.

The question took about nine seconds. He has not said what it was and nobody in that shop has guessed and nobody has asked him, and he has been back once since, on the following Monday, and did not say it then either.

---

What he said instead, at about half past nine, was about a bin, and it took about four seconds, and nobody answered it.

"**There is a bin at the top of that lane that four of the people who live over my shop do not use and I have worked out in the last month that it is not because of the bin.**"

A man of about twenty-six who works at that counter said "**Why not?**" and he said back, straight away, and this is the second of the two sentences.

"**Because a man came round in the summer telling a woman to report him and she reported the man.**"

Nobody went any further with it. Nobody said the man was right, and nobody said the man was not right, and nobody has said since that a man who goes round telling a woman to report him is a man doing a job, and about four people in that shop have said that is a fact about a man and four did not notice have said nothing.

---

Then he said the other thing, and it came at about ten past ten, and there was nobody else in the shop, and it is the truest thing anybody said in Lattice Ward that day and nobody has written it down anywhere.

"**I have a building of nine flats over a canteen and about four of the nine live in this city and I know that a room in a building can be asked a question and made to answer it, and I have been thinking about my building since Wednesday, and I have decided that I do not want my building to be one of the four anybody goes to. That is a decision I made about my own building and I want you to know I made it, and I am not asking you for anything.**"

He said the last part before he got to the rest of it, and the rest of it did not come.

Nobody said anything. Nobody said he had done the right thing, and nobody said he had done the wrong thing, and nobody has said since that it was a cowardly sentence, and about four people in that shop have said he was right to make it and about four have said nothing at all, and the two groups have not met.

He put his hands in his pockets and said good day and left, and he was gone for about four seconds.

---

Nobody asked him what the other thing was. Nobody asked him who the four rooms were,

Nobody asked him what the other thing was. Nobody asked him who the four rooms were, and nobody asked him what he thought a merge would do to nine flats over a canteen, and nobody asked him what he wanted instead, and he has not been asked any of it and is not going to be.

A man of about twenty-eight who works nights said, at about half past two to nobody, and it was about nine flats, and nobody improved on it.

**I have been in that building for two years and I have a key and I have never once asked what happens to it if the people who own it are joined to three other rooms in three other districts, and I have asked nobody because I could not have phrased the question, and that is the first time in my life a sentence has been too long for me.**

Nobody helped him phrase it. Nobody has tried and nobody is going to, and he has not been asked to say it again, and about four people in that shop have said that a man who cannot phrase a question does not get an answer and four have said that he asked it perfectly well in the only words there were. Nobody asked him what he thought a merge would do to nine flats over a canteen, and nobody asked him what he wanted instead, and he has not been asked any of it and is not going to be.

**He had every reason to ask. Four rooms in four districts now know there is a printed paragraph, and a building is not a room, and there is no line between the two in anything anybody in this case has read, and a man who has a building of nine flats is exactly the sort of person a paragraph like that would be aimed at by whoever wrote it, and nobody in this case has said that out loud and he did not say it either.**

---

It was a Thursday. She was four miles away in a workroom, on a shift that gets paid for, from about nine until about half past four, and she was in none of the three districts and she was not told about any of the eleven people and she was not asked about the paragraph.

He had not spoken to her since a Thursday in an earlier week. The four months between that Thursday and this one is the same length it was on the Monday and the same length it was when the water came, and nothing in three weeks has moved it and nothing since has moved it.

The sheet with about seven lines in his hand is still stopped. It is in a drawer with clips in it about four miles from here and it is not a page and it is not the piece of paper with about eleven lines on it, and the two have never been in the same drawer and nobody in this case has put them together.

Nobody telephoned a workroom four miles away on the Thursday. Nobody has done that in a fortnight, and there is a telephone in the building she works in and there is one in his and neither of them rang.

**Nothing about the Thursday was spent. A man did not ask him for anything at about ten past ten and that is the whole of what happened in it, and it is the fourth Thursday since the first of them and no more of them has been said about than any of the other three.**

---

A man of about fifty who owns a garage in the Lower Wards came in at about eleven and said one thing about a Friday, and it was not about a paragraph.

"**A woman rang me on Wednesday and asked me whether my garage was a room, and I said no, and she said right, and rang off, and I have not been able to stop thinking about the right, and I have decided that I am not going to ring her back.**"

Nobody told him to ring her back. Nobody told him not to, and nobody knows the number, and he has not rung it and nobody has asked him whether he will, and about four people in that shop have said that a garage is not a room and about four have said that a person who rings and says right has already decided the answer.

A woman of about twenty-nine who answers a telephone four hours a day was in that shop at about one o'clock on her day off and said, to a bench and it was about her own job and nobody took it up.

"**I answer a telephone four hours a day for a company and about four people ring me every week and ask me whether their room is a room, and I have been saying no for about a year, and last week I wrote the word no on a piece of paper and put it in my drawer beside a sheet with about eleven names on it.**"

Nobody said she had done the wrong thing. Nobody said she should put it on the wall, and nobody asked her what the paper said after the word no, and she has not said, and the paper is in a drawer in an office of nine people in the second of the four districts and it is on nobody's system.

A first-year of nineteen was at that counter and said one small unasked thing at about four in the afternoon, and nobody said anything back to her, and she was not thanked.

"**A man stood at that counter for about nine seconds this morning and did not say a thing, and I was the only one who saw him do it, and I have been thinking about it all day and I have worked out that the not saying was the expensive part, and I do not think I would have known that before this week.**"

A man of about thirty-eight who sells plumbing supplies from a unit in a yard in the working Ashfields was in that shop on the Thursday afternoon and said one thing, and it was about his name in a book, and nobody improved on it.

"**A man rang me at eleven on Monday and told me a thing about a rule and then let me go, and I have been in this city for about nine years and that is the first time anybody has rung me to tell me a thing about myself that was true, and I did not know what to do with it and I have had four days.**"

Nobody told him what to do with it. Nobody has asked him what he did with the four days, and he has not said, and about four people in that shop have said that a man who is told a true thing about himself is a man who is owed something, and about four said nothing, and the two have not met and neither of them has said a word to him.

Nobody said what she had worked out. Nobody said it was right, and about four people in that shop have since said that she is right and four have not have said nothing.

---

A man of about twenty-three who works at a counter in a building in the second of the four districts was in that room and said one thing about the man who did not ask, and nobody said it back to him.

**I have worked out that he did not ask because he has decided something about himself, and I know he has decided something about himself, and I have not asked him what, and I have not asked him what, and I would like somebody to notice that the not asking is the part I keep coming back to.**

A woman of about fifty who types in an office of nine people in the second of the four districts was in that room and said one thing, and it was about a sentence, and nobody improved on it.

**A man of fifty-eight said nine words in a room in the middle of the week and about four of us have been saying a different nine words ever since, and neither of the two sets of nine words is better and I have stopped expecting it to be, and I have also stopped being able to hear the other one without hearing mine.**

Nobody said which set of nine words was hers. Nobody has asked her, and about four people in that room have said the same and the rest have said nothing, and the two groups have not met and she has not asked either.

Nobody improved on either set. Nobody has said which set is the one people will remember, and nobody has asked the man of about fifty-eight, and he is four miles away in another building and has not been asked and is not going to be.

About nine people came in at half past six into a warm room off a service road. A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing at all. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the reason she is in that room is the temperature, and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was in it for about half an hour and said nothing at all, which is the second Wednesday and Thursday in a row she has said nothing in that room, and nobody has asked her why and she has not said.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in it and said nothing, and has not been asked about anything for ninety-nine days, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.

A woman of about thirty-five who works in a laundrette in the working Ashfields was in it and said, about a Wednesday and about a car park and about a woman who was in a chair for four and a half hours, and it was not improved on and nobody thanked her.

Nineteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, and the newest of them is a man of about twenty-nine's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board, and it is one hundred and eighty-nine days old, and a man not asking a question has not made it more or less true.

The heating is on and costs more, four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not going to be.

**A man of about thirty-five made a decision about his own building and said it out loud to a stranger and asked for nothing, and the stranger gave him nothing, and nobody in that shop worked out for about half an hour that a question had nearly happened there.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
