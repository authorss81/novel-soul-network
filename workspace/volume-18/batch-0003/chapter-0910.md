# Chapter 910 — A Wednesday Of The Eighth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In That First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket With About Nine People In It, The Sixty-Sixth Sitting, The Count Said Once Into The Face Of That Room And The Book With A Green Cover Shut And Standing Where It Stood, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Asked Nothing And Signing Nothing And Saying Afterwards One Thing About A Piece Of Paper With Two Sides To It And What Is On Both Of Them, And Nobody Saying Anything Back, And Forty-Four Pounds For Four Jobs

**The Wednesday of that week was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and the whole of the evening after that went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were, and the shutter came down at about ten.**

---

Nine names and nine dates went onto that Wednesday, the ninth of the dates at about twenty to five.

**The last line ruled on that board is the nineteenth and the eighteenth above it had been ruled for one thousand four hundred days, which is two hundred weeks to the day. The open page lying open in front of the man of about fifty-one is owed one thousand three hundred and seventy-two days, which is one hundred and ninety-six weeks to the day, and that man had been against that north wall one thousand two hundred and eighty-eight days, which is one hundred and eighty-four weeks to the day.**

---

**The count was said once that evening at about half past six, into the face of that room, and it took about nine seconds, and it has been said that way every fourth Wednesday for about nineteen years.**

She said: seventy-one, of which sixty-six correspond.

She said it once and she did not say it twice and she did not explain it, and a man of about thirty-four said nothing at all, which is what a man in that room has done every fourth Wednesday for about nineteen years.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds afterwards.**

---

**And at about half past seven the woman who holds that room put her hand flat on the book in the green binding, and did not open it, and took her hand off it again, and that is the whole of what happened.**

That book stood at sixty-nine lines when that room opened and it was on sixty-nine lines when the shutter came down.

The tin standing beside it stood at seventy-three with its lid down and was not opened at any part of that evening.

**Nobody in that room has asked her why she did not open it, and nobody in that room has said anything about her not opening it since, and about four people in that room have said since that they had it ready and did not use it.**

---

**Nobody in that room said that the nine rows on that sheet had been dealt with on that Wednesday. Nobody in that room said that they had not been dealt with either. This page does not say which of those two is a true sentence.**

A book that shuts on a Wednesday evening because the person who holds that room has nothing to enter is a book that shuts.

**About four people in that room have said since that it was the quietest evening they have been in, and about four of them have said since that they are not sure that quiet is the word, and neither of those two is a thing anybody in that room said out loud.**

---

**Behind that woman's chair there is a space in that first floor that has had nothing standing in it since a week in the spring, and nobody has asked her about it and nobody in this city has said a word about it, and it is five hundred and sixty days old, and that is eighty weeks to the day.**

That ninth chair is against that wall with its back to the whole of that room and it did not move when the count was said and it did not move when the book shut, and no page of this stretch of days names whoever would move it.

**The woman of about forty-three was in that room and put the sheet with four columns on it back on its table at about nine the way she does, and the fifth heading stood over nothing on all nine of its rows, and she did not say one word about the two sentences of that evening.**

---

**He was in that room on that Wednesday, a man of twenty-two, asked nothing at all and signing nothing, and he is not the person who signs anything in this stretch of days.**

At about half past seven he was standing at the back by the rail with the card in it and nobody was facing him.

He said afterwards, at about ten past eight, four things.

He said: that paper has two sides and there are two kinds on them.

He said: one side of it is a choice and one side is an injury.

He said: and a person can only ever be on one of them.

He said: and nobody has written down which, and I am not the one who is going to.

**Not one person in that room answered him, and about four of the people in it have said since that they heard all four of those sentences, and about four of them have said since that they have not been able to put any of them down.**

---

**What was left of that Wednesday went into about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A sub-main switch in a workshop had no isolating way above it on the board, so that nothing above that board could be isolated without pulling the whole supply.

He put an isolator in above it and proved it off and on with everything downstream stopped.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A sub-main with no isolator above it is a supply that has to be pulled to be worked on. About four of those switches in that workshop have nothing above them, and one of them has been a board nobody could dead for about four years.**"

A socket in a print bay of an office had a rewireable fuse in it that had been filled with a piece of bare copper off the roll instead of the fuse the plate was stamped for, so that the fuse had been carrying the fault it was there to stop.

He pulled the copper out, put in one of the rating stamped on that plate, and proved the whole circuit with a load on it.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A fuse made of copper is no fuse at all. About four of those rewireables in that bay have a piece of wire where the fuse goes, and one of them has been an office with a fault nobody could account for in about four years.**"

A light over a fire point in a corridor had its own battery on a shelf above it that nobody had ever looked at, and it was eleven weeks past the date on the label.

He took the battery out, put one in that is in date, and proved the whole fitting on its own supply.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A battery nobody has looked at is a light that is off when it is wanted. About four of those fittings in that corridor have never been opened, and one of them has been a point on a wall that has been there in case for about four years and would not have lit.**"

A hose reel outlet in a passage had been put on the lighting board, and it had been tripping a stair light whenever the reel was run out.

He put that outlet on a spare way out of the board and proved it with the reel run out.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A hose reel on a lighting circuit is a stair light that fails when the fire is. About four of those outlets in that building are on lighting circuits, and one of them has been an argument about testing that has gone nowhere for about four years.**"

**Forty-four pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

**The fourth of those four rooms was shut with a woman of about thirty behind its door from about half past six on that Wednesday, the binder on the shelf at the back of it has not come out on any of the ten days of this stretch, and this page prints no figure for it.**
