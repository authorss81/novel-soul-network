# Chapter 903 — A Monday Of The Seventh Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then An Hour Of The Evening In That Same First Floor Above The Line, A Woman Of About Thirty-Eight Who Asked About Five On A Thursday And Was Refused Once Coming Back A Fortnight Later And Being Refused Again, And Nobody In That Room Saying Whether The Second Of Those Two Was Worse Than The First Or The Same, And Thirty-Nine Pounds For Four Jobs

**Monday came round to a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about an hour of the evening after that went into the same first floor above the line, where about nine people were and about four of them had been in that room on a Monday before.**

---

Ten names and ten dates went onto that Monday, the tenth of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road had stood one thousand six hundred and seventy-three days by that Monday, which is two hundred and thirty-nine weeks to the day. The sixteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on that board was one thousand four hundred and sixty-three days old, which is two hundred and nine weeks to the day, and the nine hands' copies of the front of one page were one thousand two hundred and thirty-nine days old, which is one hundred and seventy-seven weeks to the day, and no two of those nine have ever been laid beside another.**

---

**A woman of about thirty-eight came into that room at about a quarter to seven on that Monday, and she is a return to it and not a new person in it, and she has been in about four rooms in this city in about four weeks.**

She is the one who asked about the five on a Thursday about a fortnight ago and was told no, and she did not ask again on that Thursday and she did not write to anybody about it, and about four people in this city have said since that they expected her to write to somebody about it.

**She came in on that Monday and nobody in that room asked her what for, and she has said since that she has stopped coming in expecting to be asked what for, and that this is the second thing that has changed about her and that she has not told anybody which of the two changed first.**

---

**And at about ten past seven she asked the second question, and it was the same question a man of about thirty-three put on a Wednesday, and she asked it whole.**

She asked: who was in the room when those nine were written down.

The woman who holds that room did not stop what she was doing.

She said: no, and that one is nobody's to answer.

**Nobody in that room said whether that no was worse than the one she was given on that Thursday, and nobody in that room said that it was the same, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted one of those two sentences said out loud and did not say either of them themselves.**

She has said since that she noticed the not-saying, and that it took her about nine seconds to notice it, and that she has thought about it on and off since and has not worked out whether the two of them were even being weighed.

**Nobody in that room has asked her which of the two she would rather had been said, and she has not said.**

---

A man of about thirty-three was in that room and said nothing at all on that Monday, and he has said since that he heard the question go and knew the answer to it and has not offered it, and that he does not know whether that was loyalty or something worse.

A man of twenty-two was in that room, asked nothing, signed nothing, and said one thing afterwards about a drawer.

He said that the drawer under that till has four years of receipts in it and that he still has never thrown one of them out.

**Nobody in that room said anything about the drawer, and about four people in that room have said since that they have stopped being surprised by it.**

---

**The sheet went back onto that table at about nine the way it goes on it, and the fifth heading stood over nothing on all nine of its rows, and nobody in that room has ever asked after that piece of paper with the two kinds on it.**

---

**What was left of that Monday was about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A pilaster light on a shopfront had its transformer inside a metal box that was earthed to its own frame and to nothing else, so that the whole earth path ended at the fixing screws.

He earthed the box to the building, made off the earth at the fitting, and proved it with the circuit off and on.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A transformer earthed to its own frame is an earth that stops at two screws. About four of those boxes on that shopfront are earthed to themselves, and one of them has been a light that has gone out in wet weather for about four years.**"

A stove outlet in a canteen had a heavy ring on a spur that had never been sized for it, so that the spur would have carried the load and the ring would have done nothing at all.

He put the ring on a spur of its own and proved it at full load.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A heavy ring on a light spur is a ring that is decoration. About four of those outlets in that canteen are on undersized spurs, and one of them has been a kitchen that has been told not to use two things at once for about four years.**"

A downlight in a stairwell had no earth conductor arriving at the fitting at all, and was holding its earth back through a length of conduit with a couple of saddles on it.

He brought an earth in to the fitting, took it off the conduit, and proved it on a tester.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp with no earth of its own is a lamp on somebody else's plumbing. About four of those lights in that stairwell have no earth at the fitting, and one of them has been a stairwell kept dark on account of a fault for about four years.**"

A roller door control in a yard had its own supply taken off a lighting circuit, and the door had been stopping halfway because the lighting circuit was already carrying what the lights carried.

He gave the control its own way out of the board and proved it from shut to open and back.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A control on a lighting circuit is a door that stops when somebody switches a light on. About four of those controls in that yard are on lighting circuits, and one of them has been a door nobody could get in at night for about four years.**"

**Thirty-nine pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms had a woman of about thirty behind its shut door from about half past six on that Monday, and the binder on the shelf at the back of it has not come out on any day of this stretch, and this page prints no figure for it.**

Monday of week 307, at ten. That is the two hundred and eighth day of this stretch of days. Ten names and ten dates went onto that Monday, the tenth of the dates at about twenty to five.
**No sitting was held on that Monday and nothing was counted out loud in any room in this city. A woman of about thirty-eight came into that first floor above the line and asked who was in the room when those nine were written down, and the woman who holds that room did not stop what she was doing and said that it was nobody's to answer. *Conditions of the close.* **With sixty-nine lines ruled in that green binding and seventy-three on the tin standing beside it, that Monday carried neither of those figures out of the other and printed the difference of neither.
With its back to that room and its seat against that wall, the ninth chair stayed where the last person to shift it left it, and no name for that person stands anywhere in this stretch of days.
With the door of the fourth of those four rooms shut and a woman of about thirty behind it, nothing came off the binder on the shelf at the back of it, and no figure for anything on that shelf is printed here.
With the hour at about eleven and the building being in a first district, the room under it had nothing burning in it on that Monday and has nothing burning in it now.
With a figure in a sentence and not a count that anybody keeps, that register stood at four when that room opened and stands at four at the shutter, and nothing that happened on any of these ten days is on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are listed in the lines under this one and nothing below them brings a pair of them together. A book with a green cover. A card creased once, standing in a doorway. Nine copies of the front of one printed page. A tin with the lid down. A strip of paper, cut narrow, with one word on it. Nineteen ruled lines on a board on two nails. A shutter at about ten. A rail carrying two places. A ring binder on a shelf at the back of a room with its door shut. A page ruled in four and a fifth heading over it with no entry on any of its nine rows.
Three of these are outside the other ten altogether, and they are what came back on a bus, what stands down one side of that counter, and what was put up on a wall by two drawing pins. Those four columns and that empty heading are counted in the ten above and are not one more than the ten, and that is so whether anybody looked at that heading on that Monday or not.
A woman of about thirty is behind the shut door of the fourth of those four rooms, and this page does not count her and does not describe her and prints no figure for her page.

A dated rule stands over something quite apart from all of this, and the papers behind it are for anybody who comes and asks to see them.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and two questions were put into that room and both were turned away, and nobody in this city has said a word to either woman about it since.**
