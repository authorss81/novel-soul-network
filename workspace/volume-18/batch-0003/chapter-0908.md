# Chapter 908 — A Monday Of The Eighth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In That First Floor Above The Line With About Nine People In It And Nobody In That Room Having Told Anybody That Anything Falls On The Wednesday, A Man Of Twenty-Two Saying A Date Out Loud And Writing It On A Piece Of Paper Of His Own And Then Crossing It Out About Three Quarters Of An Hour Later, And Nobody Asking Him Why, And Forty-One Pounds For Four Jobs

**The Monday of that week put a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then that evening, after about two hours of the counter, went into that first floor above the line, where about nine people were, and nothing was counted aloud in this city that day.**

---

Eleven names and eleven dates went onto that Monday, the eleventh of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood one thousand six hundred and eighty days on that Monday, which is two hundred and forty weeks to the day. Of those nineteen ruled lines the sixteenth was one thousand four hundred and seventy days old, which is two hundred and ten weeks to the day. And the nine hands' copies of the front of one page were one thousand two hundred and forty-six days old, which is one hundred and seventy-eight weeks to the day, and no two of those nine have been put beside each other on any day of this stretch.**

---

**A man of twenty-two was in that room from about half past six on that Monday and was asked nothing and signed nothing and sat with his coat on, and about four people in that room have said since that he had a folded piece of paper in that coat and that all of them noticed it and none of them said anything about it.**

At about half past seven he said a date out loud.

He said it flatly, the way a person reads a number off a list, and there was no year in it and there was no month in it and there was nothing on it but the day itself.

**Nobody in that room said anything when he said it, and about four people in that room have said since that they did not know until afterwards that what he had said was a date and not an amount.**

---

**Then he took a folded piece of paper out of his coat and put it flat on his knee and wrote the date on it, once, in the middle, and folded it back along the same two folds.**

He did not write it on the sheet on that table. The sheet was about two feet from his hand and nobody in that room saw him consider it and about four people in that room have said since that they thought about it afterwards and not then.

**Nobody in that room has asked him why he wrote it on a piece of paper of his own, and nobody has asked him since, and he has said since that he does not know and has thought about it for about nine days.**

---

**And at about ten past eight, about three quarters of an hour after he had said it and about as long after he had written it, he took a pen out and drew one line through the middle of it, once, from one edge of the paper to the other.**

The paper went back into his coat in the same two folds.

Nobody in that room said anything when he did it. Nobody in that room has said anything about it since, and nobody in that room asked him before or after whether it was going anywhere.

**Nobody in that room has said whether he meant to write it in that fifth column, and about four people in that room have said since that each of them has turned that question over and that none of them has worked out whether it was a question worth turning over.**

---

**The man of about thirty-three was in that room on that Monday and said nothing at all about the pen, and has said since that he understood the whole of it in about nine seconds and did not look at it, and that looking at it would have made it into something.**

The man of about fifty-seven was in that room that evening and nobody put a single question to him about himself, which is what happens on every page of this manuscript that he stands on.

The sheet went back onto that table at about nine with the fifth heading standing over nothing on all nine of its rows.

**Not one person in that room has asked the woman of about forty-three about the piece of paper with the two kinds on it, and nobody asked the man of twenty-two what he would do if a person did ask him.**

---

**The rest of that Monday was about two hours in that yard and that building, and four jobs went into it.**

A gate intercom in a yard had its supply taken off the office lighting board, so that the office lights going off at six took the gate with them.

He gave the intercom a supply of its own and proved it on and off.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**An intercom on the office lighting is a gate that opens in daylight. About four of those intercoms in that yard are on lighting circuits, and one of them has been a locked yard after six for about four years.**"

A fan in a plant room was earthed off its own casing with two screws and a paint run, so that the whole earth path ended at the mounting bolts.

He bonded the casing to the frame and proved it at full speed.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A fan earthed to its own casing is a fan on two bolts. About four of those fans in that plant room are earthed to themselves, and one of them has been a nuisance that came and went with the weather for about four years.**"

A heat pump in that plant room was on the lighting board, and it had been tripping a stair light every time a compressor started.

He gave it a way of its own and proved it through three starts.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A heat pump on a lighting board is a heat pump that puts the lights out. About four of those units in that building are on lighting circuits, and one of them has been reported as a bad starter in about four offices for about four years.**"

A bench socket in a workshop had an earth conductor going back to the board about a third of the size it should have been, put in by somebody in a hurry.

He pulled it back, made it off again at the size it should be, and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A bench socket with a thin earth is a socket that is only earthed on paper. About four of those sockets in that workshop are undersized on the earth, and one of them has been a fault that would not repeat itself for about four years.**"

**Forty-one pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**In the fourth of those four rooms a woman of about thirty stood behind a shut door from about half past six on that Monday, nothing has come off the binder standing on the shelf at the back of that room on any day of this stretch, and no figure for it appears on this page.**

Nobody in that room has said whether he was going to write it in the fifth column. Nobody has put to the woman of about forty-three the question of that piece of paper with the two kinds on it.
