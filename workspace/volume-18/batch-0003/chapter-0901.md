# Chapter 901 — A Wednesday Of The Sixth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In That First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket With About Nine People In It, And No Figure Said Out Loud In Any Room In This City, And About Four Of The Nine In That Room Who Have Read The One Piece Of Paper With The Two Kinds On It And About Five Who Have Not, And Nobody Asking The Woman Of About Forty-Three Where That Paper Went, And Forty Pounds For Four Jobs

**The first Wednesday of that stretch was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about two hours of that Wednesday evening went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were. It was not a sitting and no figure was said out loud in any room in this city.**

---

Nine names went onto that Wednesday's sheet with nine dates beside them, the ninth of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The eighteenth of those nineteen ruled lines had been ruled for one thousand three hundred and eighty-six days on that Wednesday, which is one hundred and ninety-eight weeks to the day. What the open page lying in front of the man of about fifty-one is owed came to one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight days, which is one hundred and ninety-four weeks to the day, and that man had been against that north wall one thousand two hundred and seventy-four days, which is one hundred and eighty-two weeks to the day.**

---

**The sheet with the four columns on it went onto that table at about seven the way it goes on it every week, and the fifth heading stood over nothing on all nine of its rows, and nobody in that room had put anything in it on any day that any of them could remember.**

About nine people were in that room. About four of them had been in it before and about five had not, and the woman of about forty-three was one of the four.

She has said since that she did not know that half of that room had never seen the paper, and that she had gone on talking for about nine minutes on that Wednesday without once looking round, and that this is the sort of thing she has been not noticing for about four years.

---

**And there is one piece of paper in this city that has the two kinds of that word on it, on two sides, in about four minutes, and about four people in that room have read it and about five have not.**

Nobody in that room has asked the woman of about forty-three where that piece of paper went.

**Four of the people in that room have said since that they wanted to ask her and did not, and one of them has said since that she decided about four times in nine minutes not to, and that each of those four times had a different reason and none of the four reasons was the true one.**

One of the about five who has not read it said one thing that evening about not having read it, and it was said into the middle of the room and not to anybody.

He said: nobody ever brings the paper to the room.

He said that as though it were a thing about the paper and not about the room, and about four people in that room have said since that they heard the second of those two things in it and did not hear the first.

---

**A man of about thirty-three was in that room and asked one question at about half past seven, and it was the first time in about four years that anybody in this city had asked it out loud in a room.**

He asked: who was in the room when this was written down.

**Nobody in that room answered it. About nine people were standing in it and about four of them have said since that they wanted to say something and that there was nothing to say, and about four of them have said since the other thing, which is that they understood the question and the understanding was the whole of what they had.**

The sheet on that table has been on a table in this city for about four years and the four columns on it are in the order somebody wrote them in, and there is no line on it anywhere saying who was standing in the room while it was being written.

He has said since that he did not ask it as a trick and that he has not been able to work out why he asked it, and that he has thought about it for the about nine minutes it took him to walk to the bus stop after.

---

A man of twenty-two was in that room on that Wednesday and was asked nothing and signed nothing, and he has said since that this is the fifth time in about two months that it has happened to him and that he has stopped taking it as a thing to work out.

**Nobody in that room has said anything about the piece of paper with the two kinds on it in his hearing, and he has said since that he has never seen it and did not know there was one.**

---

**What was left of that Wednesday went into the same yard and the same building for about two hours, and four jobs went into it.**

A distribution board in a workshop there had two circuits arriving on one way out with a spare way out standing unused beside them, so that one of the two circuits was only ever as good as the other one's terminal.

He split the two ways, gave each circuit one of its own, and proved both.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**Two circuits sharing one way out is one circuit with a spare. About four of those boards in that workshop have an unused way standing beside a used one, and one of them has been a trip nobody could account for in about four years.**"

A light in a store there had no earth of its own at the fitting and was holding its earth back through a length of conduit that was itself held on two clips.

He took the earth off the conduit, put it on the metal box, and proved it with a tester.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A light with no earth of its own is a light held together by its own conduit. About four of those fittings in that store are earthed that way, and one of them has been a complaint about a light that stayed on for about four years.**"

A socket on a landing had been changed for one of a narrower make into a box that was a shallow pattern, so that the new face stood proud of the wall by about a quarter of an inch.

He took the shallow box out, put a deeper one in the same hole, and refitted the face flush.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A face standing proud of its own wall is a face somebody will catch a sleeve on. About four of those sockets on that landing are proud of the plaster, and one of them has been a scuffed sleeve in a corridor for about four years.**"

A bracket in a corridor had been fixed into a hollow block with four fixings and no solid anywhere behind any of them.

He filled the holes, found the solid, and put the bracket back on four screws into it.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A bracket into a hollow block is four screws into air. About four of those brackets in that corridor are in hollow block, and one of them has been an extinguisher that came off the wall in about four years.**"

**Forty pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms had its door shut by about half past six on that Wednesday with a woman of about thirty behind it, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come out, and this page prints no figure for it.**
