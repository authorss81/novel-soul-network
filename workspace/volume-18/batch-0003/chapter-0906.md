# Chapter 906 — A Thursday Of The Seventh Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In That First Floor Above The Line With A Woman Of About Sixty Writing Out A Heading For The Fourth Time In About Four Years And Three Other People Watching Her Do It, And Nobody Knowing Where Two Of The Earlier Ones Went, And Forty-Two Pounds For Four Jobs

**Thursday at that shop is a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about two hours of the evening after that went into that first floor above the line, where about nine people were and about four of them had a heading to write out.**

**A woman of about sixty came into that room at about ten past seven on that Thursday and put a blank piece of paper on that table, and about four people in that room watched her write on it and none of them said anything while she did.**

She wrote one word at the top of it and nothing under the word.

**Nobody in that room has asked her why she wrote one word and nothing under it, and about four people in that room have said since that they asked themselves and did not ask her.**

She is the woman who wrote the tick-box that the forms have been built on since, and she wrote it after a week of looking at it, and she chose the word because it was the word on the other form, and she has said that she did not know there were two forms.

**Nobody in this city has asked her to write anything out for about four years until that Thursday, and about four people in that room have said since that they had assumed somebody had asked her and that they were the first to find out that nobody had.**

---

**And it was the fourth time. Three of those four rooms had asked her what the heading over that empty column says, and she had written it out for three of them, and she wrote it out for the fourth on that Thursday, and that is four times in about four years.**

Nobody in that room knows where two of the earlier three went.

One of them has said since that the first went home in a coat pocket and that the woman who wrote it had gone to live somewhere else by then.

One of them has said since that the second was never collected and that nobody ever asked after it and that whoever asked for it has stopped asking.

**Nobody in that room asked where any of them went and nobody has asked her since, and she has said since that she has not been asked where they went by anybody at all and that she did not think of them again after she had handed them over.**

---

**A woman of about forty-three was in that room on that Thursday and watched all of it, and the piece of paper with the two kinds on it was in her coat, and nobody in that room has asked her where it went, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted to on that Thursday and did not.**

She put the sheet with four columns on it back onto that table at about nine the way she does, and the fifth heading stood over nothing on all nine of its rows, and she did not say anything about the word the woman of about sixty had just written out for the fourth time.

---

He stood in that room, a man of twenty-two, and was asked nothing and signed nothing, and he has said since that four rooms had asked a woman of about sixty to write a word on a piece of paper four times in four years and that nobody had ever asked her to write the two kinds.

**Nobody in that room said anything about that either.**

**What was left of that Thursday was about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A ticket machine at the entrance to a car park had been put on the same way out of the kiosk board as the barrier arm, and it had been resetting itself every time the arm went up.

He gave the machine a supply of its own and proved it through a full cycle.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A ticket machine sharing a supply with a barrier is a machine that loses its count every time the barrier moves. About four of those machines in that yard are on the arm's way out, and one of them has been losing about nine counts an hour for about four years.**"

A chest freezer in a store had been put on the same spur as the shop's copier, so that the copier and the freezer could not be on together.

He took the freezer onto a spur of its own and proved it through a full pull-down cycle.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A freezer and a copier on one spur is a business that has to choose. About four of those freezers in that district are on a shared spur, and one of them has been a stock of about four hundred people running out every Monday for about four years.**"

A fire alarm isolator in a corridor had been mounted on a plywood backing with the whole of it standing on a piece of timber about nine millimetres thick.

He took the isolator off the timber, made up proper fixings into the wall, and proved it dead and live.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**An isolator on a plywood back is an isolator standing on its own case. About four of those isolators in that corridor are on timber, and one of them has worked loose for about four years.**"

A roller shutter motor in a yard had its own supply flex pinched flat where it went through the guide, and it had been running on a quarter of what it should have been.

He re-routed the flex, took it out of the guide, and proved the motor from shut to open.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A flex pinched in a guide is a motor on a quarter of its supply. About four of those runs in that yard are pinched in a guide, and one of them has been a shutter that has been reported as slow for about four years.**"

**Forty-two pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**
