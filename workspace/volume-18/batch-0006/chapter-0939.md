# Chapter 939 — The Monday Of The Sixteenth Week And The Last Full Week Of This Movement, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six Before It, And Then A Room In Which Nobody Asked, A Date Was Published, And Money Was Counted Out Of A Box That Cannot Cover It, And Nobody In That Room Saying That Any Of That Is A Defence, And The Fifth Heading Still Standing Over Nothing, And Forty Pounds For Four Jobs

**Money and no agreement were both on the table on that Monday, and the day went as a bench until two and a counter until about six the way it has gone every day of that year, and about nine people came in at about half past seven.**

---

Nine callers went through that shop on that Monday between about seven and about half past nine, and at about ten that shutter came down.

**Those four converted shop units had stood there off that service road since one thousand seven hundred and thirty-six days by that Monday, and that is two hundred and forty-eight weeks to the day. That sixteenth ruled line on the board up on two nails was still blank after one thousand five hundred and twenty-six days, and that is two hundred and eighteen weeks to the day. Nine written copies of the front of one page, every one of them with a corner torn off, had been written out one thousand three hundred and two days before that Monday, and that is one hundred and eighty-six weeks to the day.**

---

**What that room can do was said out loud on that Monday at about half past seven, in three parts, by the man of about fifty-two who keeps a register, and nobody in that room took any of the three parts up.**

"It does not ask. It prints a day. It pays for the printing."

**Nobody in that room has said since that any of those three things is a defence, and nobody in that room has said since that any of those three things is not one either.**

It took about four months to get to that Monday. It took three of them to get a form printed that carries nine rows and no names on it. It took two of them to find a person willing to carry it between two districts without being asked. Nobody in that room has counted the months aloud and about four people in that room have counted them to themselves.

---

**The money was counted out on that Monday at about half past eight, in that room, out of a box on a shelf, and the amount left in it after that was said out loud once and nobody in that room said anything back about it.**

Nobody in that room asked where the money for that printing had come from. Nobody in that room has asked whether there is any more of it, and nobody in this city has said who decides what there is more of.

**And the room is one room, and it holds about nine people, and it has been one room for about four years, and about four people in that room have said since that it has been the same room the whole time.**

Nobody in that room said that any of that — the money, the months, the one room — makes the fifth column any less empty. Nobody in that room has said that it does not.

---

**The woman of twenty-four was in that room on that Monday for the second time, and she did not approve anything in it and she said so to nobody in it.**

She has said since that she can look into that room and into him and that she looked at both on that Monday and that the file she opened on him in the corridor is still open.

He is not the person who signs anything in this city. She is not the person who approves anything here. Neither of those two things was said out loud in that room and neither of them needed to be.

---

**About nine people were in that room on that Monday and the fifth heading on that sheet stood over nothing on all nine of its rows, and nobody in that room has filled it in yet, and it stood over nothing when that shutter came down at about ten.**

Nobody in that room has put it that way, that the nine rows have been dealt with. Nobody in that room has said since that they have not been dealt with either, and this page sets down no gap between those two claims.

---

**What remained of that Monday went into the same yard and the same building for about two hours, and four jobs went in there.**

A socket in a school had been put in on the lighting, so that the sockets by the tables only worked while the lights were on, which was always, and could not be reached to be tested.

He gave them a supply of their own and proved them with the lights off.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**Classroom sockets on the lighting cannot be proved without the lights on. About four of those rooms in that school are on a lighting way, and one of them has been a room where nothing on that wall has ever been tested for about four years.**"

An exterior light in a yard had been put in with a gland that had been cut too long, so that water sat in the base of it and had been getting into the fitting.

He made a proper gland at it and proved the fitting from outside with a meter.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A gland cut too long is a fitting that fills with water. About four of those fittings in that yard were done that way, and one of them has been a light standing in a puddle for about four years.**"

A distribution board in a workshop had a spare way used for a light and a heater both, so that the heater could not be run without the light coming on and the light could not be isolated for a test.

He gave the heater its own way and proved it on its own at full load.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A heater and a light sharing a spare is a heater that cannot be proved and a light that cannot be worked on. About four such pairs in that workshop are joined in just that way, and one of them has been a workshop that cannot test a circuit in the evening for about four years.**"

A socket in an office had been put in on the lighting circuit behind a desk that had been moved about four times without anybody disconnecting it.

He took it onto a spare and proved the desk's supply with the desk in place.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A socket behind a moved desk on the lighting is a socket that has been unplugged by furniture for about four years. About four of those desks in that office have been moved since they were fitted, and one of them has never been checked since it was put in for about four years.**"

**Those four came to forty pounds on that Monday, and that is exact.**

---

**On that Monday the fourth of those four rooms was shut by about half past six with a woman of about thirty behind it, and the binder stood on the shelf at the back and did not move, and no figure for the page in it is printed here.**

*942.
Monday of week 316, at ten. That is the two hundred and forty-fourth day of this stretch of days. Nine callers came into that shop on that Monday, the last of them at about half past nine.
**There was no sitting on that Monday and nothing was said aloud as a figure in any room in this city. A date was published on a form about nine people, money was counted out for it, and the fifth heading was still standing over nothing. Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody. The charge on that Monday was forty pounds, exact.**

*Conditions of the close.* **Sixty-nine lines stood on that green binding at the end of that Monday and nothing came off it. Seventy-three was on the tin beside it and its lid did not move at any hour of that Monday. That ninth chair stood against that wall with its back to that room and its seat to the paint and it was not shifted. From about half past six the fourth of those four rooms was shut and a woman of about thirty was behind that door, and a ring binder on the shelf at the back did not come down, and this page carries no figure for the page inside it. The room under that building in a first district stood dark at about eleven and is shut. The register of correct acts that changed nothing stands at four and a printed day is not on it, and no fifth of it is printed in this volume. The shutter came down at about ten on that Monday and nobody in that room has filled in the fifth heading.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named below and this page never puts two of them into one sentence.
The book in its green binding, shut.
A narrow strip of paper cut to a width, with one word on it.
A board carrying nineteen ruled lines, up on two nails.
A single binder with rings in it, standing on a back shelf.
A card creased once through the middle, holding a doorway open.
The tin beside that book, lid down.
Nine hand copies of the front of a page, every one torn at a corner.
A rail with two places cut in it and one card in one of them.
One sheet of four columns with a fifth heading printed over the fourth, empty on all nine rows.
That shutter, which came down at about ten.
Three objects lie outside all ten of those and are named here only to place them: a printed sheet up by two drawing pins, a cage down one side of a counter, and a form that came back on a bus. Those four columns and that empty fifth heading belong to the ten above and are not one further.
On the far side of the shut door of the fourth of those four rooms there is a woman of about thirty, and this block neither counts her nor describes her, and prints no figure for the page in the binder.
Some other question sits under a dated rule, and what is written behind that one can be seen by anybody.
On that Monday nobody in this city thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people in that room have said since that it is the same room it has always been and one room, and about four have said since that nobody in it said that anything about that makes the fifth column any less empty.**
