# Chapter 933 — The Friday That Ended That Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six Before It, And Then A Man Of About Thirty-Three Who Was Not Entitled To Measure Anything Putting A Thumb On A Space In A Form And Nobody In That Room Contradicted Him, And An Empty Place Behind A Chair Named Once And Counted By Nobody, And Forty-Three Pounds For Four Jobs

**Nothing was entered on that Friday, and that Friday was a bench until two and a counter until about six and then the ordinary end of a week in one room, where about nine people stood about a table and a man who had no standing to measure anything was allowed to put a thumb on a space in a form.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Friday between about seven and about half past nine, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**That seventeenth ruled line had been ruled one thousand four hundred and ninety-eight days before that Friday, and that is two hundred and fourteen weeks to the day. The last post at the end of that corridor had been standing at that end of it for one thousand two hundred and seventy-four days, and that is one hundred and eighty-two weeks to the day. Inside that box off that road, one written line had been filled in one thousand one hundred and six days before that Friday, and that is one hundred and fifty-eight weeks to the day.**

---

**The man of about thirty-three measured the space on that form where a date would go, on that Friday, at about half past six, and nobody had asked him to, and he was not the person whose job it is to measure anything.**

He put his thumb flat on that space and held it there while about nine people watched him do it.

"Nobody can be asked to put a date in that box."

Nobody in that room told him he had it wrong. Nobody in that room backed him either, and nobody in that room asked him how he knew.

**And what he said is a fact about a form and not about anybody's opinion, and there are two ways of putting anything in that fifth column and both of them have failed for about four years.** One of them is to ask somebody whether a date may go there, and about nine people in this city cannot be asked that and never have been. The other of them is to write the date in without asking, and then nobody agreed to it and nobody said they did not.

He has said since that he did not do it to be listened to and that he has not been able to put either of those two things back together in his own head since he said it.

---

**Nobody in that room has ever said out loud what this body exists to do, and this page does not say it either, and there is a notice on a wall of that room that has been read by about four people and has not been taken down and has not been attributed to anybody.**

It reads *that sheet is for people who cannot answer*.

Nobody in this city has said who wrote it or when. About four people in that room have read it and about two of them have read it more than once, and not one of them has ever mentioned it to anybody else in that room.

**Nobody in that room has asked whether anybody who can answer is on that sheet as well.**

---

**Behind the chair the woman of about forty-three sits in there is a space in the floor and a stretch of wall that has had nothing standing against it since a week in the spring, and this page names that space once and sets no figure on it.**

Nobody in that room has said why it is empty. Nobody in that room has said how long it has been empty, and nobody in that room has said what was standing in it.

---

**The fifth heading on that sheet stood over nothing on all nine of its rows for the whole of that Friday and stood over nothing on all nine of its rows when the shutter came down at about ten.**

No one in that room has said since that those nine rows were dealt with. Nobody in that room has said since that they have not been dealt with either, and this page sets down no gap between them.

Nobody in that room asked the man of about thirty-three one question about the thumb on the box after he took it away.

---

**What was left of that Friday went into about two hours of the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A socket in a garage had been put in on a spur that came off the lighting in that garage, so that neither could ever be worked on at night without the other going down with it.

He gave that socket a supply of its own and proved the garage with the lights off and the door shut.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A socket on the lighting is a socket that cannot be used in the evening at all. About four of those sockets in that garage are on the lighting, and one of them has been a garage that cannot have a machine run in it after dark for about four years.**"

A hand dryer in a washroom had been put in over a circuit that also supplied the boiler, so that switching the boiler off at the end of a day left no hot water anywhere in that building.

He put the dryer on a way of its own and proved the boiler circuit on its own at the same time.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A dryer on the boiler circuit is a building with no hot water after six. About four of those dryers in that building are on that circuit, and one of them has been a washroom that could not be used at closing time for about four years.**"

A floodlight over a yard had been mounted on a bracket that had been cut and then welded back, and the weld had never been painted, so that the joint had gone at the edge and the bracket was working loose.

He took that bracket down and put a new one up and proved it hanging off a ladder.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A bracket welded back and left bare goes at the edge. About four of those brackets in that yard were done that way, and one of them has been a light that has been loose on its mount for about four years.**"

A socket in a corridor had been put in on a ring that two other sockets were also on, so that the whole of that end of the corridor went out whenever anything on it did.

She took it off that ring and gave it a spur and proved the corridor end with the ring loaded.

"**Nine pounds,**" she said. "**Three sockets on one ring is three sockets that go out together. About four of those ends in that corridor are wired like that, and one of them has been a corridor where a cleaner could not tell which end had gone for about four years.**"

**Forty-three pounds is what those four came to on that Friday, exact.**

---

**That Friday evening the fourth of those four rooms was shut by about half past six with a woman of about thirty behind it, and the binder standing on the shelf at the back of that room did not come off the shelf, and nothing in this paragraph is a figure for it.**
