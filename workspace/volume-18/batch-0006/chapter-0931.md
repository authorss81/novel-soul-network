# Chapter 931 — A Wednesday Of The Fourteenth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six Before It, Then About Two Hours Of That Wednesday Evening In A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where A Thing Was Said Out Loud For The First Time By A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Is Not The Person Who Signs Anything In This City, And Thirty-Nine Pounds For Four Jobs

**That Wednesday was shop and a bench until two and a counter until about six, and the rest of it went into about two hours of one evening on a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were and a sheet of paper lay on a table of its own with one heading on it that nothing had ever been written under.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Wednesday between about seven and about half past nine, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**That eighteenth ruled line near the foot of that board had been ruled one thousand four hundred and forty-two days before that Wednesday, and that is two hundred and six weeks to the day. The space on that sheet for a date had been standing there one thousand four hundred and fourteen days, and that is two hundred and two weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one had not moved off that north wall for one thousand three hundred and thirty days, and that is one hundred and ninety weeks to the day.**

---

**The thing this has been about was said out loud in that room on that Wednesday at about half past seven, and it was said by the man of twenty-two, and he said it into the face of the woman of about forty-three, and he had not said it before in four years.**

"I want one date," he said. "**One date for nine rows. Nobody has to be asked.**"

Nobody in that room took it up. About four people in that room looked at the sheet on the table while he was saying it and did not say anything about it afterwards, and not one of them has said since what they made of it.

**And the woman of about forty-three has said twice this week, out loud, into two different faces, that a day to turn up tells nobody anything, and she has not said it a third time and nobody in that room has asked her to.**

"A day to turn up tells nobody anything."

**And nobody in that room has ever asked her what the fifth heading on that sheet is for.** She put it there herself. She copied four columns off a form belonging to somebody else and added a fifth heading over the fourth and she has never once said out loud what it is to be used for, and about four people in that room have wanted to ask her for about four years.

---

**About nine people were in that room on that Wednesday and the sheet with four columns was on its own table, and the fifth heading stood over nothing on all nine of its rows, and it was on that table at about nine.**

Nobody in that room has said since that the nine rows on that sheet have been dealt with. Nobody in that room has said since that they have not been dealt with either, and this page does not print any difference between them.

**Nobody in that room has said who the nine are.** There are nine rows and no name on any of them and about four people in that room can each name somebody they think is on it, and no two of those four names the same one, and nobody has ever compared the four.

---

**The man of twenty-two stood at the back with the card in the rail beside him and signed nothing on that Wednesday and he is not the person who signs anything in this city.**

He has said since that he did not know, when he said it, whether he was saying it because it was the only thing anybody could do or because he wanted it to be true, and he has not been able to settle that since, and nobody in that room has asked him about it.

Nobody in that room has said anything back to him about any of it.

---

**What was left of that Wednesday went into about two hours of the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A distribution board in a workshop had been taken off a spur that came out of another board in a different room, so that switching one off in the week took the other off with it and nobody could work out which had done which.

He took that spur off and gave it a supply of its own and proved both boards with the workshop empty.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**Two boards on one spur is two boards that cannot be worked on separately. About four of those boards in those workshops come off a neighbour, and one of them has been a workshop that cannot have a circuit proved on it for about four years.**"

A heater in a drying room had been put in on a way that came off a light circuit further along the same corridor, so that the light at the far end came on whenever the heater did.

He put the heater on a way of its own and proved the corridor with the heater running.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A heater on a lighting way is a heater that brings the light up with it. About four of those heaters in that corridor are on a lighting way, and one of them has been a corridor where nobody could see anything after about four in the afternoon for about four years.**"

A handrail in a stair had been put back up with two of its brackets into hollow block, which holds nothing, so that the whole rail moved about an inch at the top.

He took those two brackets out and put them into the solid at the sides and proved the rail with a person standing on it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A bracket into hollow block is a rail that moves when somebody leans on it. About four of those rails in that stair have brackets like that, and one of them has been a stair that nobody would put a hand on for about four years.**"

A socket in a workshop had been put in on a spur that came off a lighting board at the far end, so that a piece of machinery near it dropped out every time a light was switched at the other end of the building.

He took it onto a way of its own and proved it with the lights off and on.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A machine socket on a lighting spur is a machine that stops when a light is switched. About four of those sockets in that building are on a lighting spur, and one of them has been a machine that could not be run with the lights on for about four years.**"

**Thirty-nine pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**At about half past six on that Wednesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut, and a woman of about thirty was behind that door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of it came off no shelf, and no figure for the page inside it is printed here.**
