**The rest of that Friday went into the same yard and the same building for about two hours, and four jobs went into it there.**

A socket in a garage had been put in off the lighting of a neighbouring workshop, so that two businesses shared one way and one of them could not be worked on alone.

He put that socket on its own supply and proved both businesses separately.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**Two businesses on one way means neither can be proved on its own. About four of those pairs in that row are joined that way, and one of them has been a garage where a fault could only be found by shutting the next door as well for about four years.**"

A machine in a canteen had been put in on a supply that came off the lighting through the same conduit, so that the machine's earth went back the way it came.

He took it onto its own supply and proved the case with a meter on it.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A machine earthed back through the lighting is a machine with no earth of its own. About four of those machines in that canteen are supplied that way, and one of them has never had a proper test on it for about four years.**"

A light in a car park had been put in on a spur that came off a lamp column at a point where the column's own earth had been cut out for a sign and never made good.

She re-made that earth at the column and proved the light with a meter.

"**Ten pounds,**" she said. "**An earth cut out of a column and not made good puts every light on it on nothing. About four of those columns in that car park have been cut into, and one of them has been a light with no earth behind it for about four years.**"

A socket in a passage had been put in on a spur that already carried four outside lights and had been at its limit with them.

He gave it a way of its own and proved the passage with all four lights running.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A socket on the end of the outside lights is a socket that stops working when it is needed. About four of those passages in that yard are wired that way, and one of them has been a doorway that goes dark at exactly the wrong time for about four years.**"
