# Chapter 899 — A Monday Of The Fifth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then That Same First Floor Above The Line With A Printed Sheet Of Four Lines Gone Up On A Wall By Two Drawing Pins That Nobody Asked For And Nobody Has Taken Down, A Man Of About Fifty-Two Who Keeps A Register Saying That A Form With A Fifth Column And Nothing In It Is A Form For Waiting, And A Word Written At The Foot Of That Sheet Which Appears On No Other Page Of These Ten, And Thirty-Six Pounds For Four Jobs

**That Monday was a bench until two and afterwards a counter until about six, and then the evening went into that same first floor above the line, where a printed sheet of four lines had gone up on the wall that week with about nine people standing in front of it. Nobody in that room had asked for it to be printed and nobody has taken it down.**

---

Ten names went onto that Monday and ten dates with them, the tenth of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Four converted units behind that service road had been standing one thousand six hundred and fifty-nine days by that Monday, and that comes to two hundred and thirty-seven weeks to the day. Of the nineteen lines ruled on that board the sixteenth was one thousand four hundred and forty-nine days old, which is two hundred and seven weeks to the day; and the nine hand copies of the front of one page were one thousand two hundred and twenty-five days old, which is one hundred and seventy-five weeks to the day, and nobody has ever laid those nine beside each other.**

---

**The sheet went up on the wall of that first floor on a Monday of that week by two drawing pins and it is four lines long, and there is nothing above the four lines and nothing under them and nothing beside them, and it is printed and it is not a list anybody made about anybody.**

It says:

> the word in the fourth column is *load*
> it means two things
> the fifth column is headed *review*
> it is empty

**About nine people in that room have read all four lines of it and about four of them have read it more than once, and nobody in that room has added anything to it and nobody has taken a copy of it and nobody has asked who printed it.**

At the foot of that sheet, under the four lines, there is a single word and nothing under the word:

> `unasked`

**Nobody in that room has said anything about that word, and about four people in that room have said since that they had looked at it for about nine minutes without knowing what it was the word for, and one of them has said since that she has not stopped looking at it.**

---

**And at about ten past seven a man of about fifty-two who keeps a register said three things about that sheet, and about a register he keeps of work that has been reported and not dealt with, and nobody in that room asked him what the register was for.**

He said: a form with a fifth column and nothing in it is a form for waiting.

He said: that has been a form for waiting for about four years.

He said: and I have been the one waiting.

**Nobody in that room said he was the person anybody had been waiting for, and nobody in that room asked him what he had been waiting on, and about four people in that room have said since that they had thought about the third of those three sentences on the bus home.**

He has said since that he keeps a book of things that have been reported to him and have not been finished, and that his own name is in it about nine times, and that none of the nine is this.

**Nobody in that room asked him which nine, and he has not said, and about four people in that room have said since that they would rather he did not.**

---

A man of about thirty-three was in that room and said one thing about the four lines on that sheet.

He said that it was the first thing anybody had printed in this city about that column that was not a form.

**Nobody in that room asked him whether it should have been printed, and nobody has asked him since, and about four people in that room have said since that they have wanted to ask him and have not.**

He was in that room as well, was asked nothing that was not on the list, signed nothing, and what he said at about half past eight

He said that a card creased once goes into that rail and holds a doorway open, and that a sheet of paper with four lines on it goes up on a wall and holds nothing up at all, and that both of those were put there by somebody.

**Nobody in that room asked him who, and about four people in that room have said since that they have asked themselves.**

---

**What was left of that Monday was about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A fitting in a store there had two starters in it, because it had been changed with the one that came out of the case left in the box beside it.

He took the spare out, proved the remaining starter, and proved the fitting.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A fitting with two starters in it is a fitting somebody has already fixed twice. About four of those fittings in that store have a spare still in the box, and one of them has been a light that flickers whatever anybody does to it for about four years.**"

A bell push in a corridor there had been wired to a light in that same corridor instead of to a bell anywhere at all.

He took it off the light, put it on a bell, and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A bell push on a light is a light somebody pressed and forgot. About four of those pushes in that building are on lights, and one of them has been reported as a bell for about four years.**"

A socket in a canteen there had a face plate painted round the edge, so that it could not be lifted without taking about four layers of paint with it.

He cut it back, made the screw slots new, and proved the circuit.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A face painted round is a face nobody can take off to look behind. About four of those faces in that canteen are painted round, and one of them has been somebody's fault that could not be found for about four years.**"

A time clock in a workshop there had its control box on a magnetic base, and about half of the base had lifted off the wall in about nine years.

He took the box down, made up proper fixings, and proved the clock.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A control box on a magnet is a control box waiting for a wall to move. About four of those boxes in that workshop are on magnets, and one of them has been a clock that has run slow for about four years.**"

**Thirty-six pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

**The fourth of those four rooms had its door shut by about half past six on that Monday with a woman of about thirty behind it, and the binder on the shelf at the back of it has not been opened on any of the ten days of this stretch, and this page prints no figure for it.**
