# Chapter 892 — A Tuesday Of The Third Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then About Four Minutes In Which A Woman Of About Forty-Three Put Nine Rows Onto Two Sides Of One Piece Of Paper And Took The Two Kinds Of That Word Apart For The First Time, A Woman Who Reads It Saying Out Loud That The Form Has Been Lying For About Four Years, And A Man Of About Thirty-Three Refusing In About Nine Seconds To Have His Own Name Put To That Sentence, And Fifty-Five Pounds For Four Jobs

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and from about half past six a first floor above a line in Saltmarket had about nine people in it. One kind of that word went on one side of a sheet of paper and the other kind went on the other, and nobody in that room had seen the two written apart before.**

---

That Tuesday took eleven names and eleven dates, the eleventh of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four converted units behind that service road stand at one thousand six hundred and forty-six days, which is two hundred and thirty-five weeks and one day. The thirteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on that board stands at one thousand five hundred and seventeen days, two hundred and sixteen weeks and five days, and the fourteenth at one thousand four hundred and eighty-two days, two hundred and eleven weeks and five days. The hold over nine crates in that room off the road has been sitting there one thousand two hundred and seventy-nine days, which is one hundred and eighty-two weeks and five days.**

**And one line inside one box in that room was written about sixteen months before that Tuesday and is one thousand and twenty-six days old, one hundred and forty-six weeks and four days, and it describes nothing at all and has described nothing for longer than anybody in this city has been counting.**

---

**It took about four minutes and it was done by the woman of about forty-three, at the table, with a pen and a sheet of paper the same size as the one with the four columns on it, and about nine people in that room watched all of it.**

She ruled one line under the other about halfway down the sheet. Then she read the fourth column off the sheet with the four columns on it, nine words, and wrote each one on one side or the other, and about four of the nine went one way and about five went the other.

**The nine words are the same word on all nine rows. They had been in one box for about four years and they were in two boxes on that sheet for about four minutes, and nobody in that room had asked for that and she had not offered it.**

The side with about four of the nine on it was the side where the thing was something a body had been asked to hold. The side with about five of the nine on it was the side where the thing was something a person could no longer do.

**Nobody in that room said which of those two was worse, and about four people in that room have said since that they would rather not know which of the two is in front of them.**

---

**And then she said the thing about herself, after, standing at the same table with the pen still in her hand, and nobody had asked her for any of it.**

She said: nobody told me it came in two kinds.

She said: I would have written two columns.

She said: nobody asked me for two columns, then or since.

She said that she had written those four columns out longhand in her own kitchen twice and that the second time she had copied them off the first time, and that she had gone along the fourth column both times without stopping on a single row.

She said that and then she put the pen down on the table.

**Nobody in that room asked her why, and about four people there have said since that they would have asked her and did not, and one of them has said since that she had decided in about nine seconds not to and that she was not going to explain the nine seconds either.**

---

**And a woman read that sheet at about twenty past seven and said two things about it, and the second of them is why nobody in that room has anybody to be angry with.**

She said: that form has been lying for about four years.

She said: you will not be told who it lied to.

**Nobody in that room asked her the second question and nobody in that room asked it afterwards, and about four people in that room have said since that the whole of that evening turned on somebody being angry with a person who is not in the room and cannot be.**

Nobody in that room has worked out who wrote anything down in those four years. Nobody has needed to in order to know what the column says.

---

**And at about half past seven a man of about thirty-three was asked whether he would put his name to the second of those two sentences, and he said no in about nine seconds into the face of the woman who had said it, and it held.**

He said: I will not have my name on that sentence.

Nobody asked him a second time. Nobody in that room has asked him about it since and one person has said in that room that she had intended to and stopped herself at about nine seconds.

**He has said since, at a table in his own kitchen, that he said it because a sentence with his name on it is a sentence somebody can hand to somebody else, and that he has spent about four years trying to stop handing sentences to people, and that neither of those two things is a reason anybody in that room was entitled to accept.**

---

**The balance of that Tuesday was the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A motor in that yard had its star point made up on the terminal block with the links left in place, so that the machine could be run either way or neither way and nobody could say which without stopping it and looking.

He took the links out, marked them, put them where they belong, and proved the changeover both ways.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A star point with the links left in is a machine nobody can change over. About four of those motors in that yard have been left that way, and one of them has been a start that nobody could explain in about four years.**"

A junction box in the same building had a lid that would not sit flat because the box it was on had been screwed to a face that was not true, and the lid had been left off ever since.

He packed the box back until the lid ran true, and proved it.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A box lid that will not close is a junction with nothing on it. About four of those boxes in that building have lids that do not shut, and one of them has been an open box in a washroom for about four years.**"

A batten in a workshop there had its holder earthed through a length of flexible conduit that stopped at the wall, so that the earth was whatever the conduit was leaning on.

He took the earth onto the holder itself and proved the path end to end.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A batten earthed to a piece of conduit is a batten earthed to the wall. About four of those battens in that workshop are done that way, and one of them has been a complaint about touching that nobody ever found in about four years.**"

A box in a corridor there had been plastered over after the cable had gone into it, and the plaster had gone hard round the edges of the lid.

He cut the plaster out, made the box up properly, and proved it.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A box filled with plaster is a box with no lid and a lid drawn on it. About four of those boxes in that corridor are buried, and one of them has been somebody's idea of a shut box for about four years.**"

**Fifty-five pounds is what those four came to on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**From about half past six on that Tuesday there was a woman of about thirty behind the shut door of the fourth of those four rooms, and nothing has come off the binder on the shelf at the back of that room on any of the ten days of this stretch, and this page puts no figure on it.**
