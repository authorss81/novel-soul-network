# Chapter 897 — A Thursday Of The Fourth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then About An Hour In That First Floor Again With A Book Lying Open On A Table Nobody Had Shut, About Four People Looking At The One Line That Had Been Added To It And Nobody Reading It Out, A Woman Of About Thirty-Eight Asking Who The Five Are And Being Told No In Nine Words, And Nobody In That Room Saying Whether That Was Unkind Or Kind, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Saying One Thing About A Drawer, And Twenty-Nine Pounds For Four Jobs

**That Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and about an hour of the evening after that went into that first floor again, where a book was lying open on a table that nobody had shut. About four people in that room looked at the one line that had been added to it and about four did not, and nobody in that room read it out.**

---

That Thursday's sheet took eleven names with as many dates beside them, the eleventh of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The card creased once in that two-place rail had been standing there one thousand six hundred and fifty-nine days on that Thursday, which is two hundred and thirty-seven weeks to the day. Seven of the figures standing in that stretch of days carried a whole number of weeks on that day and every one of them was correct: the twelfth of those nineteen ruled lines at one thousand five hundred and seventy-five days, two hundred and twenty-five weeks to the day; the thirteenth at one thousand five hundred and twenty-six, two hundred and eighteen weeks to the day; the fourteenth at one thousand four hundred and ninety-one, two hundred and thirteen weeks to the day; the fifteenth at one thousand four hundred and seventy, two hundred and ten weeks to the day; the nineteenth and last of them at one thousand three hundred and fifty-one days, one hundred and ninety-three weeks to the day; and the hold over nine crates and the boards under them at one thousand two hundred and eighty-eight days, one hundred and eighty-four weeks to the day.**

---

**The book was on that table open at the line it had gained the night before, and it stayed open for about an hour, and about four people looked at that line and about four of them did not look at it at all.**

Nobody read it out. Nobody has read it out since and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted somebody to read it out and that it was not theirs to ask.

**Nobody in that room copied it and nobody in that room has been asked for a copy and about four people in that room have said since that they could have copied it and did not and do not know why they did not.**

---

**And a woman of about thirty-eight asked about the other figure anyway, at about ten past seven, standing at the front of that table, in about nine seconds.**

She asked: who are the five and where are they now.

She asked it the way somebody asks a thing they have already decided to be the only thing they are going to hear.

The woman who holds that room did not stop what she was doing.

She said: no, and I said that once and that was it.

**Nobody in that room said that the no was unkind, and nobody in that room said that it was kind, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have said something and are glad they did not.**

---

**And here is the whole of what was said about the five, and it was said once, at the sixty-third sitting, in one sentence, and it has not been said since by anybody including the person who said it.**

The second figure is the count less those five, and the five are older than that book, and they are on a piece of paper of her own that she has never shown anybody and has never been asked to show.

Nobody asked her again on that Thursday. About four people in that room have said since that they wanted to and did not and one of them has said since that she has wanted to since the Wednesday about four months ago and has stopped wanting to, because the answer would not make the other question easier.

---

He was in that room for about an hour and said one thing, and it was about a drawer in his own shop and not about any of it.

He said that the drawer under that till has four years of receipts in it and he has never thrown one of them out and nobody in that shop has ever asked him why he keeps them.

He said that it is the only column in this city that has ever had anything put in it without anybody being asked to.

**Nobody in that room said anything about the drawer, and about four people in that room have said since that they understood it in about nine seconds and about four of them have said since that they have gone home and thought about it for a fortnight.**

He has said since that he does not know whether keeping a drawer full of receipts is the same thing as filling in a review, and that he has decided it is not the same thing and that he is not sure what he decided that for.

---

**The rest of that Thursday was about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A lamp in a store there had its control switch mounted on the wall about a foot past the fitting, so that the switch could only be worked by somebody who put a shoulder into it.

He moved the switch under the fitting and proved both ways.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A switch a foot past its own lamp is a switch somebody falls into. About four of those switches in that store are out past the fitting, and one of them has been a broken switch on the end of somebody's walk for about four years.**"

A floor box in a workshop there had a lid that had been painted shut at some point and had never been opened again, and the box underneath it was the only thing holding about four metres of cable at that point.

He got the lid off with a blade, refitted a proper one, and proved the cable.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A lid painted shut is a box holding itself on paint. About four of those floor boxes in that workshop have been painted shut, and one of them has been a cable that nobody could find for about four years.**"

A socket in a corridor there had been fitted over a second cable coming out of the same box, so that the face plate was holding about four of somebody's work on top of it.

He took the second cable out to its own box and refitted the face.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A face plate over two cables is two jobs for one box. About four of those boxes in that corridor have two cables in them, and one of them has been somebody's rewire that vanished under a face for about four years.**"

A hand dryer in a washroom there had its duct off behind the cover and lying on the top of the motor, so that about half of what it moved went out into the wall cavity.

He put the duct back, sealed it, and proved it at the outlet.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A dryer with its duct off is a dryer blowing into a wall. About four of those dryers in that building have duct off, and one of them has been a washroom that never dries for about four years.**"

**Twenty-nine pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**

**The fourth of those four rooms had a woman of about thirty behind its shut door from about half past six on that Thursday, the binder on the shelf at the back of it has not come out on any of the ten days of this stretch, and this page does not print anything about how long it has stood there.**
