# Chapter 893 — A Wednesday Of The Third Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then A Room Above A Line In Saltmarket Open From About Half Past Six With About Nine People In It And No Number Said In Any Room In This City, And A Man Of About Thirty-Eight Who Keeps A Clinic In A Fourth District Saying That A Review Being Due Is Not A Thing A Clinic Can Be Told About By A Person At A Counter And That He Will Do It Anyway, And Nobody There Asking Him What He Was Going To Do It With, And Twenty-Four Pounds For Four Jobs

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and afterwards a counter until about six, and the room above that line was open from about half past six with about nine people in it. About four of the nine had stood in that room on a Wednesday before and about five had not, and one of the five had come in on a bus that takes about half an hour.**

---

Nine names went onto that Wednesday and nine dates beside them, the ninth of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The eighteen of those nineteen ruled lines has been ruled for one thousand three hundred and sixty-five days on that board, and that is one hundred and ninety-five weeks to the day. The page lying open in front of the man of about fifty-one in that first floor is owed one thousand three hundred and thirty-seven days, which is one hundred and ninety-one weeks to the day, and that man has been against that north wall one thousand two hundred and fifty-three days, which is one hundred and seventy-nine weeks to the day.**

**And the one line inside one box in that room off the road was put there about sixteen months before that Wednesday and stands at one thousand and twenty-seven days, one hundred and forty-six weeks and five days, and it is not about any of this.**

---

**It was not a sitting and nothing was said out loud in any room in this city that carried a figure, because a sitting is a Wednesday of every fourth week and that Wednesday was not one of them.**

About nine people came up between about half past six and about seven. About four of them had been in that room on a Wednesday before and about five had not.

**The sheet with the four columns on it was on its table with the fifth heading standing over nothing on all nine rows, and about four people in that room had not looked at the fifth column since the Wednesday before and about five had not looked at it since the first week it was on that table.**

---

**And at about ten past seven a man of about thirty-eight who keeps a clinic in a fourth district stood up at the end of the table and said two things about why he was standing there.**

He said he had come because a woman at a counter had told him a review was due.

He said: a clinic is not told that at a counter.

He said, in about nine seconds, that a review being due was not a thing anybody could be told about by a person behind a counter, and that whatever else was true about it, it was not how anybody had ever handed him a piece of work.

And then he said the second of the two things, which was the one he had come to say.

He said: I will do it anyway, and I will do it properly.

**Nobody in that room asked him what he was going to do it with. Nobody in that room has asked him since, and about four people in that room have said since that they had it ready and did not use it.**

---

He has said since that he has a clinic and eleven people on the books and about nine of them who have been coming long enough that he knows what they cannot do, and that the whole of what he said that evening came down to the fact that a column with a heading on it and nothing under it had been in front of him for four years as a thing somebody might do something about.

He said that he did not know whether a clinic could be the thing that asked.

**About four people in that room have said since that the fifth of them went home and thought about that sentence for a fortnight, and one of them has said since that she has not got to the bottom of whether that had been the answer.**

---

A man of about thirty-three was in that room that evening and said one thing about what a clinic is.

He said: a clinic has never once asked me anything either.

**Nobody in that room asked him whether that made the two of them the same and nobody in that room has asked him since, and about four people there have said since that they thought about it for about nine minutes afterwards.**

He was not asked anything else and he signed nothing. He has said since that he went to that room to find out whether anybody was going to do anything about that column and that he found out that there was about nine people in it who were all waiting for each other.

**The woman of about forty-three was in that room and put the sheet with four columns back on its table at about nine, the way she does every Monday, and she did not say anything about the man of about thirty-eight and he did not say anything to her.**

---

**What was left of that Wednesday went into a fourth district and about two hours, and four jobs were done in it.**

A fire door in that building had its closer set to the fastest the arm would bear, so that it came to on its own and went on its own and had been reported for doing it.

He set the closer back to where it shut without a bang and proved it four times.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A door on its fastest closer is a door that argues with itself. About four of those closers in that building are wound as far as they will go, and one of them has been a noise in a stairwell at six in the morning for about four years.**"

A switch in that yard had been painted over twice, and there was a pale mark on the brick beside it at the height of somebody's hand where somebody had gone on using it after the first coat.

He cut a box out, brought the cable back into it, and fitted a face on the mark.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A switch painted over twice is a switch that somebody still wants. About four of those switches in that yard are under two coats of paint, and one of them has been a hand going to a wall for about four years.**"

A floor socket in a workshop there had been set into a steel cover plate that had never been bonded to anything, so that the whole of the earth path depended on the plate touching the floor.

He bonded the plate, took the socket up to working height, and proved it.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A socket earthed to a plate touching a floor is a socket earthed to a floor. About four of those floor sockets in that workshop are on unbonded plates, and one of them has been a complaint that went nowhere for about four years.**"

A cooker outlet in a kitchen of that building was on a spur with a freezer standing on it, so that the freezer and the cooker could not ever both be on.

He took the freezer off the spur, put it on its own, and proved the ring.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A freezer and a cooker on one spur is a choice nobody in that kitchen gets to make. About four of those spurs in that building put two heavy things on them together, and one of them has been tripped every Sunday for about four years.**"

**Twenty-four pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**By about half past six on that Wednesday that fourth room had been shut and a woman of about thirty was behind its door, and the ring binder on its back shelf has not been out on any day of this stretch, and no page of them prints how long it has stood there.**
