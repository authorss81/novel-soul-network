# Chapter 895 — A Monday Of The Fourth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then That Same First Floor Above The Line With About Nine People In It And Nobody In It Having Told Anybody That Anything Is Going To Happen On The Wednesday, A Man Of About Thirty-Three Saying In About Nine Seconds That The Figure Said In That Room Goes Up By One Whatever Happens And That The Nine Rows Are Not A Count And Have Never Been One, And Four People In That Room Saying Since That They Had Been Treating Them As One And Stopped, And Thirty-Three Pounds For Four Jobs

**That Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and the evening after that went into that same first floor above the line with about nine people in it. Nobody in that room had told anybody that anything was going to happen on the Wednesday, and about four of the nine had not known that a Wednesday of that week carried anything at all.**

---

That Monday's sheet took ten names and as many dates, the tenth of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four units off that service road have stood one thousand six hundred and fifty-two days, which is two hundred and thirty-six weeks to the day. The sixteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on that board is one thousand four hundred and forty-two days, which is two hundred and six weeks to the day. And nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand two hundred and eighteen days old, which is one hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day, and eight of the nine have never been brought through.**

---

**That Monday was the first ordinary weekday of a week that carries something in it, and the room above that line has been open on a Wednesday for about four years and about four people in it had worked out what that meant and about five had not.**

Nobody in that room had told anybody that a sitting was on the Wednesday. Nobody in that room had written it on anything and nobody had read it out and nobody had come to that room on that Monday and said the word.

**Four of the people in that room have said since that each of them worked it out alone off the calendar on that wall, that nobody announced anything, and that this is the nearest this sheet of paper has ever come to having a rule about it.**

---

**And at about ten past seven a man of about thirty-three said three things in about nine seconds and the second of them is the whole of what this movement found.**

He said: the number said in that room goes up by one whatever happens.

He said: those nine rows are not a count and have never been one.

He said: somebody has been treating them as if they were.

**He did not say when the number is said and nobody in that room asked him, and he has said since that he was not going to be the one who said when, and that he has not changed his mind about that in the fortnight since.**

---

**Nobody in that room said he was wrong. Nobody in that room agreed with him either, and about four people there have said since that they had been treating those nine rows as a count, and had stopped, and had not noticed themselves stopping.**

He has said since that a figure that goes up whether anybody opens anything or not is not a measure of anything, and that a column of nine rows that nobody has ever counted is not a count either, and that the two have been sitting about nine feet apart in the same city for about four years and nobody ever put them side by side.

**About four people in that room have said since that they went home and looked at their own paperwork for about nine days afterwards.**

---

A woman of about thirty-eight was in that room and said one thing about the second of those three sentences.

She said: I have quoted those nine as a number before.

**Nobody in that room asked her what for, and she did not say what for, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the fourth time in about four weeks that somebody had wanted to answer that question and had not.**

A man of about fifty-seven was in that room for about an hour and did not say anything at all, and about four people in that room have said since that they watched him not saying anything and that it took him longer than usual.

**Nobody in that room asked him one question about that, and nobody in this city has asked him one question about anything since the Wednesday about four months ago.**

---

He was in that room too, asked nothing that was not on the list and signed nothing, and what he said at about half past eight was about the word standing in the fourth column**

He said that he had gone back along that column about nine times that week and that he could now tell which of the two things a row was about by looking at the length of a name on it, and that he could not have told you how he did it.

**Nobody in that room asked him how he did it and about four people in that room have said since that they have tried it themselves and cannot do it either.**

---

**What was left of that Monday was the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into about two hours of it.**

A bell transformer in a corridor there had been screwed to the back of a metal door frame, and the frame had been a door frame and not a structural one.

He took it off the door, put it on the wall with its own fixings, and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A transformer on a door frame is a transformer on a door. About four of those bells in that building are on door frames, and one of them has been a bell that would ring from the yard for about four years.**"

A twin fitting in a store there had been switched on the neutral at one end only, so that the other end of it could not be turned off at all.

He rewired both ends of it and proved each one separately.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**Half a fitting switched is half a fitting that stays on. About four of those twins in that store are half switched, and one of them has been a light nobody could put out for about four years.**"

A handrail bracket on a stair in that building had been fixed with two screws and a resin plug in a hollow block, and the whole of it had been reported as loose for years.

He filled the holes, found the solid, and put four fixings into it.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A handrail on two screws and a plug is a handrail on a promise. About four of those brackets on those stairs are in hollow block, and one of them has been loose since before anybody started writing it down.**"

A socket in a workshop there was fed from a lighting circuit that had a dimmer on it, so that anything plugged into it came up with the room lights.

He moved the socket onto its own circuit and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A socket on a dimmed circuit is a machine that speeds up when somebody dims the lights. About four of those sockets in that workshop are on lighting circuits, and one of them has been a machine that has never held a speed in about four years.**"

**Thirty-three pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**
