# Chapter 894 — A Friday Of The Third Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And Then A Records Room In A Second District Where A Landlord And A Filing System Are The Only Two Things In That Building, About Nine People Go To Look At One File, About Four Of Them Are Let In And About Five Are Not, Nine Dates On Nine Rows With Every One Of Them In The Past, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Nine Who Says One Thing About The Word On The Sheet And Not About Her Own Relative, And Forty-Seven Pounds For Four Jobs

**That Friday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then the afternoon went into a records room in a second district, where a landlord lets four people stand at a time in front of a shelf, and about nine had come to look at one file.**

---

Ten names went onto that Friday and ten dates with them, the tenth of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The seventeenth of those nineteen ruled lines on the board has been ruled for one thousand four hundred and twenty-one days, and that is two hundred and three weeks to the day.One fitting has been standing at the end of that corridor for one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven days, which is one hundred and seventy-one weeks to the day.Inside one box in that room off the road there is a single line, it is the only one in that box, and it was filled in about sixteen months before that Friday at one thousand and twenty-nine days, which is one hundred and forty-seven weeks to the day.**

---

**The building is a landlord and a filing system and there is nothing else in it, and about nine people went there on that Friday afternoon to look at one file, and the landlord let four of them stand in front of the shelf at a time and did not say why and did not say no to anybody.**

The other five stood in a corridor with a window and about four of them have said since that they did not go in afterwards and one of them has said since that he would not have gone in if he had been offered.

**Nobody in that building took a note of anybody's name and nobody in that building has been asked since who was let in.**

---

**And the file has a column the sheet on that desk in Saltmarket does not have, which is that the fifth column on it has nine dates in it, and the sheet's fifth column has never had anything in it on any of its nine rows.**

The oldest of those nine dates is about four years past. The newest of them is about two months past. Every one of the other seven is somewhere between those two and not one of them is in the future.

**About four people who stood in front of that shelf have said since that the thing that took them about nine seconds was not that the dates were old but that they had all arrived.**

Nobody in that room asked who put them in. Nobody in that room worked out who had put them in either, and the file carries no name against any of the nine, and there is nothing in it about who, and there is no minute, and there is no second copy of it anywhere anybody has ever been told about.

---

**And at about half past four a woman of about twenty-nine who is one of the nine's relatives said one thing, in about nine seconds, into a room that had been arguing about the file for about an hour, and then she did not say anything else all the way back to the road.**

She said: that word is a verdict and not a description.

**Nobody in that room asked her which of the nine she was a relative of, and nobody in that room asked her anything at all about that, and she did not offer it and she has not offered it since and she was not asked again.**

She is not on the sheet with the four columns on it. She has not asked to be on it and nobody has put her on it and nobody in that room said that she ought to be on it.

**Four of the people standing in front of that shelf have said since that each of them had expected whatever got said there to turn out to be about her relative, and that it did not, and one of them has said since that she had decided on the bus which of the two words standing on that sheet she was going to use.**

She has said since that she came because a woman at a counter told her there was a file, and that she has still not asked what the woman told her and why.

---

**The four who were let in came out of that building at about twenty to five and about four of the five who had waited in the corridor were still in the corridor when they came out, and nobody in that corridor said anything about the file and about four of them have said since that they did not want to be the first one to say anything about it.**

A man of about thirty-three was one of the four who went in. He has said since that he did not write those dates and that he has never seen the file before that Friday and that he has not been able to get past the fact that somebody had to have written nine of them.

**Nobody has asked him whether he would sign anything about that and nobody in this city has asked him.**

---

**What was left of that Friday was the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into about two hours of it.**

A meter cabinet in a passage there had its door strap earthed to the frame, so that the earth went through a hinge as the door was moved.

He took the strap off the earth and put the earth on the frame itself, and proved it.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A strap earthed to a frame is an earth through a hinge. About four of those cabinets in that building have their straps earthed, and one of them has been an intermittent fault nobody could pin down in about four years.**"

A batten in a store there had been replaced with one of a different length, so that the new tube was held at one end and was free at the other.

He took that batten out, fitted one of a proper length, and proved both ends.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A tube held at one end is a tube that moves when anybody walks past it. About four of those battens in that store are the wrong length, and one of them has been a flickering tube for about four years.**"

A socket in a corridor there had been wired with the earth tail under the same terminal as the neutral, which meant the earth only earthed something while the circuit was on.

He took the earth onto its own terminal and proved the path with the circuit off.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**An earth on the same terminal as a neutral is an earth that only works switched on. About four of those sockets in that corridor are wired that way, and one of them has been an earth reading of zero on a tester that works for about four years.**"

An isolator in a yard there was mounted on a back plate that was not earthed, so that the whole of it was held up by the conduit above it.

He earthed the back plate, refitted the unit, and proved it dead and live.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**An isolator on an unearthed plate is an isolator hanging off a pipe. About four of those isolators in that yard are on unearthed plates, and one of them has been a nuisance in wet weather for about four years.**"

**Forty-seven pounds is what those four came to on that Friday, exact.**

---

**That fourth of those four rooms was shut by about half past six on that Friday with a woman of about thirty behind it, and nothing has been taken off the binder on the back shelf in twenty years, and this page does not put a figure on it.**
