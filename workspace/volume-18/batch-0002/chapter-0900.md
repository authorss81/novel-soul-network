# Chapter 900 — A Tuesday Of The Fifth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then That First Floor Above The Line Open From About Half Past Six With About Nine People In It And About Nine People Who Have Been In That Room On About Nine Days In About Two Weeks, A Woman Of About Forty-Three Saying That Two Things Could Go In That Column And That Only Two And That A Date Is The Only One Of The Two Which Is Not A Name And That She Has Not Written One, And Nobody In That Room Saying What Would Go In It, And Nothing Decided On Any Of The Ten Days, And Forty-Four Pounds For Four Jobs

**The last Tuesday of that stretch was a bench until two and afterwards a counter until about six, and from about half past six that first floor above the line stood open with about nine people in it, and about nine people had been in that room on about nine days in about two weeks. Nothing had been written in that column on any of them.**

---

On that Tuesday nine names were written on that sheet and nine dates went with them, the ninth of the dates at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four converted units off that road stood one thousand six hundred and sixty days on that Tuesday, which is two hundred and thirty-seven weeks and one day. The card creased once in the two-place rail stood one thousand six hundred and sixty-four days, two hundred and thirty-seven weeks and five days. The thirteenth of those nineteen ruled lines was one thousand five hundred and thirty-one days, two hundred and eighteen weeks and five days, and the twentieth of them has never been ruled at all.**

---

**Nobody has put anything in that fifth column on any of those nine days and nobody has said a date out loud in any room in this city on any of them, and the heading has been read out loud once, in about nine seconds, by a man of about thirty-three, in a room in a fourth district, and about four people in that room have said since that they had all known it was there for about four years.**

About nine people have been in that first floor on about nine days in about two weeks and about four of them had been in it on all nine of those days and about five had been in it once or twice.

**Nobody in that room had put a date in anything in the whole of that time and nobody had put a name in it either, and about four people in that room have said since that the two of them had a long conversation about a heading and nothing else.**

---

**And at about twenty past seven a woman of about forty-three said four things about that column, in about nine seconds, standing at the same table she puts the sheet on every Monday, and then she put it back on the table the way she does.**

She said: two things could go in that column and only two.

She said: one of them is a name and one of them is a date.

She said: a date is the only one of the two that is not a name.

She said: I have not written one.

**She said that last one into the face of a man of about thirty-three and he took it and did not answer it, and about four people in that room have said since that they had expected him to say something and had not been ready for him not to.**

She has said since that she has been the person who asks nine questions about nine things for about four years, and that she has asked all nine, and that not one of the nine is a question she is allowed to put.

---

**Nobody in that room said what would go in that column, and nobody has said it since, and about four people in that room have each said since, separately, and to nobody else, that they know exactly what would go in it, and that they have not said it in a room.**

One of them has said since that she has written the word down once at the back of a notebook and has not looked at it since and would not know anybody who would believe her about it.

**Nobody in that room asked any of them, and about four people in that room have said since that they were glad, and one of them has said since that being glad was the part that told her it was the wrong thing to be glad about.**

---

**Nothing was decided on any of the ten days of this stretch and nothing was signed on any of them and nothing was changed on any of them, and about four people who were in that room on that Tuesday have said since that they did not know that when they walked in and that they are not sure it is a thing they would have wanted to know.**

A man of about fifty-two was in that room and said one thing.

He said: I will wait, and there is a shelf for it.

**Nobody in that room said how long, and nobody in that room has said how long since, and about four people in that room have said since that they have started to notice how long it is, and that nobody in that room is going to say it out loud first.**

---

**What was left of that Tuesday was about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A cable tray in a workshop there had been cut for a corner and left with the cut end open, so that about nine metres of cable were hanging out of it over a walkway.

He closed the end with a plate, dressed the cable back in, and proved the tray.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A tray with its end open is a tray with loose cable over a walkway. About four of those trays in that workshop have open ends, and one of them has been a trip hazard nobody would own for about four years.**"

A socket in an office there was on a spur with an office machine standing on it, so that the machine and a kettle could not both be used.

He moved the machine to its own supply and proved the ring.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A machine and a kettle on one spur is a stand-off every afternoon in that office.About four of those spurs in that building carry two heavy things at once, and one of them has gone off every afternoon for about four years.**"

A switch in a corridor there had a plastic plate painted the same colour as the wall, so that about nine people a week took hold of it and got nothing.

He took the plate off, cleaned it, and refitted it so that it reads as a switch.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A switch painted like a wall is a switch nobody trusts. About four of those plates in that corridor are painted to match, and one of them has been called useless for about four years.**"

A motor starter in a yard there had an enclosure door that closed on two bolts out of four, and the other two were missing and had been for long enough that nobody had looked.

He made up the two missing bolts and proved the door shut on all four.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**An enclosure shut on two of four bolts is an enclosure somebody has opened. About four of those doors in that yard are down to two bolts, and one of them has been a starter with a live part behind it for about four years.**"

**Forty-four pounds is what those four came to on that Tuesday, exact.**
