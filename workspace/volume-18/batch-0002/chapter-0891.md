# Chapter 891 — A Monday Of The Third Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Half An Hour Of A Landing In A Second District, And Then A Woman Who Can Take Him Out Of Any Room Standing At That Counter From About Half Past Four And Saying Two Things Into His Face, And Nobody In That Shop Asking Her The One Thing She Came In To Be Asked, And Thirty-Eight Pounds For Four Jobs

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and about half an hour of it went into a landing in a second district where a light and its switch are on two different circuits. Nine names and nine dates went onto that sheet, and a woman came into that shop at about half past four and stood at the counter for about nine minutes.**

---

Nine names were written onto that Monday and nine dates went with them, the last of the nine at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The four units off that service road have stood one thousand six hundred and forty-five days, and that is two hundred and thirty-five weeks to the day. The card creased once in the rail by that first door is four days further on than the door it holds. The sixteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on that board stands at one thousand four hundred and thirty-five days, which is two hundred and five weeks to the day, and the nine hand copies of the front of one page are one thousand two hundred and eleven days old, which is one hundred and seventy-three weeks to the day.**

**And the separation in its one-line box is one thousand and twenty-five days, which is one hundred and forty-six weeks and three days, and describes nothing and has described nothing for longer than anybody in this city has been counting.**

---

The landing was off a stair that came up between two shops and went on to four rooms nobody used the stairs for. The light over it had been reported as being on in the day as well as the night, and the switch beside the door was the landlord's, and the light was on the meter that fed the whole of the stair.

A man of twenty-two proved both of them with a tester and wrote it on a card and put the card on the desk of the shop at the bottom and said he would take the card back in about nine days.

**Nobody at that desk read the card to him and nobody at that desk has asked him about it, and about four people in that building have said since that the man who owns those stairs has never once come up them.**

---

**And then at about half past four a woman came in and stood at that counter for about nine minutes, and she is Sera Quill, and she can take him out of any room in this city in about four seconds, and she did not do it.**

She said: a review means asking the person, not a counter.

He said: I can put your name in that column today.

She said: no, nobody has ever put my name in anything.

She said: nine years in this city, and not once asked.

He said nothing at all to that, and she waited about nine seconds and then said the rest of it.

She said that she had said it to him and not to anybody else because he was the only person in the room, and that she had not come in to be asked anything, and that she came in because a woman of about forty-three had told her there was a column with nothing in it.

**Nobody in that shop asked her whether she had been reviewed, and nobody in that shop has asked her since, and she has not said anything about it to him either.**

He has said since that she did not take him out of anything on that Monday and that he did not know whether that was because there was nothing on him or because she had not got round to it, and that he has not put the second of those two to her.

---

**About nine people have been in that shop on a Monday and about four of them were in it on the Monday before, and the cage down one side of that counter took four tickets out of nine on that day and did not take the rest.**

**The four jobs were in the same yard and the same building and they were four, and they came to thirty-eight pounds between them.**

A distribution board in that yard had two circuits arriving on one way out and a spare way out sitting unused beside them, so that one of the two circuits was only as good as the other one's terminal.

He split the two ways, put each circuit on its own, and proved both.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**Two circuits sharing one exit is one circuit with a spare. About four of those boards in that yard have unused ways beside used ones, and one of them has been a trip nobody could account for in about four years.**"

A light on the landing of that same building had no earth of its own at the fitting and was holding its earth back through a length of conduit that was itself on two clips.

He took the earth off the conduit, put it on the metal box, and proved it with a tester.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A light with no earth of its own is a light held together by its own conduit. About four of those fittings in that building are earthed that way, and one of them has been a complaint about a light that stayed on for about four years.**"

A socket on that landing had been changed for one of a narrower make, and the box behind it was a shallow pattern, so that the new face stood proud of the wall by about a quarter of an inch.

He took the shallow box out, put a deeper one in the same hole, and refitted the face flush.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A face standing proud of its own wall is a face somebody will catch a sleeve on. About four of those sockets in that building are proud of the plaster, and one of them has been a scuffed sleeve in a corridor for about four years.**"

A bracket in that same corridor was holding a fire extinguisher against a hollow block, with four fixings into nothing at all.

He filled the holes, found the solid, and put the bracket back on four screws into it.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A bracket into a hollow block is four screws into air. About four of those brackets in that building are in hollow block, and one of them has been an extinguisher that came off the wall in about four years.**"

**Thirty-eight pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**
