# Chapter 898 — A Friday Of The Fourth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And Then A Man Of About Fifty-Seven Standing At That Counter At About Ten Past Four With Nothing Broken And Nothing Written Down On Paper, Saying That A Woman Wrote Something About Him Down Once And That He Has Thought About It For About Four Years, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Saying He Does Not Know What She Wrote And Not Asking Her, And Nobody In That Shop Asking Him Why, And Fifty-One Pounds For Four Jobs

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and a man came into that shop at about ten past four and stood at the counter for about nine minutes without a piece of paper and without anything broken. Nobody in that shop had told him there was a form and nobody in that shop had told him there was a Wednesday.**

---

Nine names went onto that Friday's sheet with nine dates set beside them, the ninth of those dates taken at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The seventeenth of those nineteen ruled lines had been ruled for one thousand four hundred and twenty-eight days on that Friday, and that is two hundred and four weeks to the day. One fitting stands at the end of that corridor and has stood there one thousand two hundred and four days, which is one hundred and seventy-two weeks to the day. One box in that room off that road holds a single line and has held it about sixteen months, standing at one thousand and thirty-six days, which is one hundred and forty-eight weeks to the day, and the line is not about any of this.**

---

He had walked to that shop. He had been in that city for about nine years and he had been in that room off a road about four times, and he came in that Friday afternoon with no job behind him and nothing in his hands.

He said: a woman wrote something about me down once.

He said: I have thought about that for about four years.

He said that he had come because of it, and that he was not going to say what was written because he could not remember which of about four things she had written and he did not want to get one of them wrong in front of anybody.

**Nobody in that shop had told him that there was a form with an empty column on it, and nobody in that shop had told him that there was a Wednesday of that week, and nobody has told him since.**

---

He said that he could talk for about four minutes about anything at all and that he had found that out about himself and had not expected to.

He said that the one thing he could not do was answer a question about himself, and that he had been told about four months ago by somebody in a room that she was not going to ask him again, and that she had kept to it.

He said that what he wanted that Friday was to know whether there was any piece of paper in this city with his name on it and nothing in one of its columns, and that he had walked into three shops and two offices before this one.

**He asked that at the counter of a shop. Nobody has asked him anything about why.**

---

**The man behind that counter said two things and did not say a third thing, and the third thing is the one he has not said.**

He said: I do not know what she wrote.

He said: and I am not going to ask her.

**He has said since that he could have asked, on that Friday or any Friday since, and that there is a version of that conversation he could have had in about nine seconds, and that he has not had it, and that he has not been asked by anybody why he has not had it.**

He has said since that a man of about fifty-seven who cannot answer a question about himself is the last person in this city who ought to be asked one by somebody who does not have to.

---

**Nobody in that shop asked him why he had been thinking about it for about four years. He was not asked, and about four people who have been in that shop since have said since that they would have asked him and did not, and one of them has said since that she decided in about nine seconds not to and has not been able to say what the nine seconds were.**

He stood at that counter for about nine minutes and then went out into the rain and went home on a bus that takes about half an hour.

**Nobody in that shop has seen him since and nobody has come to that shop to ask after him, and about four people in that shop have said since that they would have done it and did not.**

---

**The rest of that Friday was about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A main bar in a panel in that yard had been insulated with two wraps of tape put on by hand and the tape had gone brittle and let about nine millimetres of copper stand out into the panel.

He cut the tape out, made the bar up properly with its own shroud, and proved the panel dead.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A bar held in by tape is a bar waiting. About four of those bars in that panel are wrapped instead of shrouded, and one of them has been a smell in a plant room for about four years.**"

A lamp holder in a yard there had no ring at all, so that the lamp was being held in by the thread at the end of its base.

He fitted a ring, proved the lamp dropped out when the circuit went, and proved it held when the circuit was on.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp with no ring is a lamp hanging on a thread. About four of those holders in that yard have no rings, and one of them has been a lamp that has come down in about four years.**"

A socket in an office there had a face plate fitted over a ventilation hole in the box behind it, so that the box could not draw anything at all.

He took the face off, opened the hole out, and refitted the plate clear of it.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A face over a vent hole is a box that cannot cool. About four of those faces in that office are over the vents, and one of them has been a socket that tripped and could not be reset for about four years.**"

A stair light in that building had its switch at the bottom of the flight, so that somebody wanting to go up had to put their hand on a wall behind them and then walk into the dark.

He moved the switch to the top landing and proved the light at both ends.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A stair switch at the wrong end is a switch somebody learns to live without. About four of those switches on those stairs are at the wrong end, and one of them has been a complaint about that flight for about four years.**"

**Fifty-one pounds is what those four came to on that Friday, exact.**

---

**By about half past six on that Friday the fourth of those four converted units had been shut with a woman of about thirty behind its door, nothing has come off the binder on the shelf at the back of that room, and this page prints no figure for it.**
