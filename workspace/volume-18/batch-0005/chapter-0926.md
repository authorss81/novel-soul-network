# Chapter 926 — A Monday Of The Next Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six Before It, Then About Two Hours Of That Monday Evening In That First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where One Thing Was Said Into A Face By A Person Who Was Not In That Room On The Sunday And Who Does Not Know What Happened In It, And Thirty-Seven Pounds For Four Jobs

**A room is not the same room on the morning after a Sunday as it was on the Friday before it, and this Monday went a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about two hours of that Monday evening went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were and one of them said one thing at somebody and was not answered.**

---

Between about seven and about half past nine on that Monday nine callers came into that shop, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**By that Monday those four converted units off that service road had stood one thousand seven hundred and fifteen days, which is two hundred and forty-five weeks to the day. The sixteenth of those nineteen ruled lines had been ruled one thousand five hundred and five days before that Monday, which is two hundred and fifteen weeks to the day, and nine hands' copies of the front of one page had been taken one thousand two hundred and eighty-one days before that, which is one hundred and eighty-three weeks to the day.**

---

**That sheet with four columns was on its own table at about seven on that Monday with the fifth heading standing over nothing on all nine of its rows, and it was on that table at about nine, and nobody in that room said one word about it.**

Nobody in that room asked about the Sunday. Nobody in that room had been in the room off that road on that Sunday except about four of them who had been in it and had not said so.

**Nobody in that room has said whether those about four have told anybody what happened in that other room, and nobody in that city has asked them.**

---

**At about half past seven on that Monday the man of about thirty-three said one thing into the face of the woman of about forty-three about what that Sunday had done to a room, and nobody answered him and nobody took it up.**

He said: it takes a room a week to come back.

He said it to her and she heard it and did not reply to it, and about four people in that room were close enough to hear it and about four of them have said since that they have not been able to give it a meaning that holds.

**He was not in that room off that road on that Sunday. He does not know what happened in it. He has said since that he was not asked and that he would have gone if he had been asked and that nobody asked him.**

---

**Nobody in that room asked the man of about thirty-three where he had got it from and nobody has asked him since.**

He has said since that it came to him on a bus and that he did not write it down and that he has thought about it about nine times since and has not been able to improve it.

**About four people in that room have said since that each of them sat in that other room on that Sunday and that nobody has asked them what was said in it, and about four others have said since that they were not in it and would not have gone.**

---

**The nine chairs were in that room in that row in that Monday in the same gaps they have stood in for about four weeks now, and nobody in that room has said which one is not used.**

The man of twenty-two was in that room on that Monday, asked nothing and signing nothing, and he stood at the back by the rail.

He has said since that a room takes a week to come back and that it took him most of that Monday and about half of the evening before it, and that he has no idea whether that is a thing he knows or a thing he has been told.

---

**The rest of that Monday went into about two hours of the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A fridge in a café had been put in on the lighting board, so that it came on with the lights and went off when the lights were switched off at the front.

He gave that cabinet its own way off the kitchen board and proved it with the front lights off.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A fridge on the front lights is a fridge that is off when the café is shut. About four of those cabinets in that street are on a lighting way, and one of them has been a café that cannot keep anything cold after six for about four years.**"

A hold-open magnet on a fire door had been put in off the lighting board, so that the door let itself go every time somebody switched the lights off at that hour.

He gave that magnet a way of its own and proved it with the lights off and the door on its closer.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A door held open by the lighting is a door that lets go when the lights go. About four of those magnets in that building are on a lighting way, and one of them has been a fire door that has not stayed open all evening for about four years.**"

A pump in that yard had been put on a way out with nothing spare left on it, so that anything else fitted in that yard could not be given a way of its own.

It took the pump onto a spare and proved the yard's other socket at the same time.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A pump on the last spare is a pump that has used up everybody else's way out. About four of those yards have a pump on the last spare, and one of them has been an argument about new sockets for about four years.**"

A lamp in a workshop had been put into a holder with its earth tag left loose at the ring, so that the earth went nowhere at all when that lamp was in it.

He put that tag home and proved the lamp holder with a meter on it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp holder with a loose earth tag is a lamp with no earth and a fitting that looks earthed. About four of those holders in that workshop have loose tags, and one of them has been a bench lamp nobody would touch for about four years.**"

**Thirty-seven pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**At about half past six on that Monday the door of the fourth of those four rooms was shut and a woman of about thirty was behind it, and the binder on the shelf at the back did not come out, and this page prints no figure for it.**
