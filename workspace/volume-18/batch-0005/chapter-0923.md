# Chapter 923 — A Wednesday Of That Same Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six Before It, And Then The Whole Of That Evening In That First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket, The Sixty-Seventh Sitting, The Count Said Once Into The Face Of That Room And The Book With A Green Binding Put Flat By A Hand And Not Opened And Standing Where It Stood, And Nobody In That Room Told Anything About The Oldest Of Nine Dates And Nobody Told Anything About It Not Being, And Forty-Three Pounds For Four Jobs

**The sixty-seventh sitting was held on that Wednesday, and before it there had been a bench until two and a counter until about six, and the whole of the evening after that went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were and a man of twenty-two stood at the back. It is the only sitting in this movement, and nothing else was counted aloud in this city.**

---

Ten callers came into that shop on that Wednesday, the tenth of them at about half past four, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The eighteenth of those nineteen ruled lines had been ruled one thousand four hundred and twenty-eight days before that Wednesday, and that is two hundred and four weeks to the day. What the open page lying open in front of the man of about fifty-one is owed came to one thousand four hundred days, and that is two hundred weeks to the day, and that man himself had been against that north wall one thousand three hundred and sixteen days, and that is one hundred and eighty-eight weeks to the day.**

---

Ten names went onto that Wednesday and ten dates went with them, the tenth of the dates at about half past four.

**The count was said once that evening at about half past six, into the face of that room, and it took about nine seconds, and it has been said that way every fourth Wednesday for about nineteen years.**

She said: seventy-two, of which sixty-seven correspond.

She said it once and she did not say it twice and she did not explain it, and a man of about thirty-four said nothing at all, which is what a man in that room has done at every one of those for about nineteen years.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds afterwards.**

---

**And at about half past seven the woman who holds that room put her hand flat on the book in the green binding, and did not open it, and took her hand off it again, and that is the whole of what happened.**

That book stood at sixty-nine lines when that room opened and it stood at sixty-nine lines when the shutter came down.

**Nobody in that room was given any reason. Nobody in that room asked her for one and nobody in that room has said one word about it since, and about four people in that room have said since that they had it ready and did not use it.**

---

**Nobody in that room said one word about the oldest of the nine dates on that sheet, which stands about four years behind the evening it was said on.**

Nobody in that room said that it stands about four years behind. Nobody in that room said that it does not stand about four years behind either.

About four people in that room looked at that row of that sheet on that Wednesday and looked away from it again, and about four of them have said since that they did not look at it deliberately and that they have not been able to account for the looking.

**Nobody in that room said that the nine rows on that sheet had been dealt with on that Wednesday. Nobody in that room said that they had not been dealt with either. This page does not say which of those two is a true sentence.**

---

**The woman of about forty-three put that sheet back on its own table at about nine the way she does, and the fifth heading stood over nothing on all nine of its rows, and she did not say one word about the evening.**

Nobody in that room on that Wednesday referred to the Tuesday evening before it. About four people in that room noticed that, and about four of them have said since that they have not been able to work out whether that was a decision or whether there was nothing anybody wanted to bring.

---

**He was in that room on that Wednesday, a man of twenty-two, asked nothing at all and signing nothing, and he is not the person who signs anything in this city.**

At about eight o'clock he said one thing into the face of the man of about thirty-three, and nobody answered him.

He said: nobody has ever put anything in that fifth column, and I am not going to be the first one, and whoever is will do it on a day that is not this one.

**Nobody in that room took that up, and about four people in that room have said since that they heard all of it and have not been able to put any of it down, and nothing whatever came of it.**

---

**What was left of that Wednesday went into about two hours of the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A basin in a corridor had its hot water taken off the same pipe as a radiator two rooms along, so that running the tap at that basin put the heat off in that other room.

He took the basin onto a pipe of its own and proved it with the radiator in the other room at full heat.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A basin sharing its hot water with a radiator is a basin that turns a room off. About four of those basins in that corridor are piped that way, and one of them has been a complaint about two rooms at once for about four years.**"

A floodlight over a yard door had its earth taken off the clip where its conduit was fixed to a brick pier, so that the earth was going back through a piece of metal and a fixing.

He moved the earth to the building and proved the case against the pier.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A floodlight earthed to its own clip is a floodlight earthed to a fixing. About four of those fittings on that yard are clipped that way, and one of them has been a door nobody could light for about four years.**"

A light in a machine room had been put in on the machine's own supply, so that it went out every time the machine stopped at the end of a day and came on by itself in the morning.

He gave it a way of its own off the board in that room and proved it with the machine stopped.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A room light on the machine's supply is a light that belongs to the machine. About four of those lights in those machine rooms are on the machine, and one of them has been a room that has been dark every evening for about four years.**"

A time clock on an all-plastic base in a workshop had no earth at all anywhere in it, because there was no metal on that base to take one.

He put an earth on the case from the conduit that came into the room and proved the case with a meter on it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A clock on a plastic base is a clock with no earth and nothing to put one on. About four of those clocks in that workshop sit on plastic, and one of them has been a clock that could not be tested for about four years.**"

**Forty-three pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**Nobody opened the fourth of those four rooms on that Wednesday; its door was shut by about half past six with a woman of about thirty behind it, the binder on the back shelf did not come out, and this page prints no figure for it.**
