# Chapter 924 — A Thursday Of That Same Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six Before It, And Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In That First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where About Nine People Were And Where Four Of The People Who Had Been In That Room On The Wednesday Were Not In It And Nobody In That Room Has Said Why Any One Of Those Four Is Not, And Thirty-Nine Pounds For Four Jobs

**Thursday of that week began with a bench until two and a counter until about six, and the whole of the evening after that went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were. That Thursday carried no sitting, and nothing was counted aloud in any room in this city.**

---

Nine callers were into that shop on that Thursday and the ninth of them at about ten past nine, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The creased card in that rail had stood in that rail one thousand seven hundred and fifteen days by that Thursday, and that is two hundred and forty-five weeks to the day. The twelfth of those nineteen ruled lines had been ruled one thousand six hundred and thirty-one days before it, and that is two hundred and thirty-three weeks to the day, and its thirteenth one thousand five hundred and eighty-two days, and that is two hundred and twenty-six weeks to the day.**

**Its fourteenth line had been ruled one thousand five hundred and forty-seven days before that, and that is two hundred and twenty-one weeks to the day, and the fifteenth one thousand five hundred and twenty-six days, and that is two hundred and eighteen weeks to the day. The last of those nineteen had been ruled one thousand four hundred and seven days before that Thursday, and that is two hundred and one weeks to the day, and the hold over nine crates and the ground under them had been standing there one thousand three hundred and forty-four days, and that is one hundred and ninety-two weeks to the day.**

---

**About nine people were in that room on that Thursday and about nine people had been in it on the Wednesday, and about four of the ones who had been there on the Wednesday were not in it on the Thursday.**

Nobody in that room said why any one of those four was not there. Nobody in that room asked any one of the four who was there why any of the others had not come.

One of those four has said since that she does not know whether she is expected, and that nobody has said to her that she is, and that she has not asked.

**Nobody in that room has said why. Nobody in that city has said why. About four people in that room have said since that they have each assumed for about four years that they knew, and that on that Thursday it turned out that not one of them knew.**

---

**And nobody in that room can say when the next one is, because there is nothing in that room that says.**

There is no calendar on that wall and there is nothing on that table, and the woman of about forty-three asked the man of about thirty-three at about eight o'clock whether there would be another one before the month turned over, and he said that he did not know either.

Nobody in that room picked the question up after that. About four people in that room have said since that they had been certain they knew when the next one was, and that they have not been able to say how long they had been certain.

---

**The nine chairs were still in that room in that row in that Thursday and about eight people sat down in them, and nobody said to anybody which one was theirs.**

The woman of about forty-three put that sheet on its own table at about seven with the fifth heading standing over nothing on all nine of its rows, and nobody in that room said one word about it, and nobody in that room has said one word about it on any of the ten days of this movement.

**The man of twenty-two was in that room on that Thursday, asked nothing and signing nothing, and he stood at the back by the rail because there were nine chairs in that room and he did not sit in one of them.**

He has said since that he counted the chairs twice that evening and did not count the people, and that he has not been able to give a reason for doing it that he would put in writing.

---

**What was left of that Thursday went into about two hours of the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A socket in a stair in a block of flats had a heater and a hair dryer on the same way out of the board, so that one of them going on took the whole stair circuit off.

He took the dryer onto a way of its own and proved both of them running together.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A socket in a stair with two things on one way is a stair circuit waiting for somebody's morning. About four of those sockets in those stairs carry two things at once, and one of them has been a stair that goes dark every morning for about four years.**"

A lamp in that same stair had its switch on the wall on the far side of a fire door, so that the switch could only be reached by standing in a doorway.

He moved the switch to the near side and proved it from both sides of that door.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A light switched from behind a fire door is a light somebody stands in a doorway to turn off. About four of those switches in that stair are on the far side of the door, and one of them has been a stair nobody could light without standing in a fire door for about four years.**"

A machine in a workshop had its supply run through a length of conduit that was supported on the machine's own frame, so that the frame was carrying the weight of the cable and its earth at the same time.

He supported that conduit on the building and put the machine's earth on the building as well.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A machine that carries its own cable is a machine carrying its own earth. About four of those machines in that workshop are piped that way, and one of them has been a machine that could not be tested properly for about four years.**"

An alley light had been put in off the alley way out of the meter board, so that it came on with the alley lighting and no switch in any building could turn it off.

He gave it a way of its own with a switch inside and proved it with the alley board off.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**An alley light on the alley board is a light nobody in those houses can switch. About four of those lights in those alleys are wired off that board, and one of them has been an alley that stays light all night for about four years.**"

**Thirty-nine pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**
