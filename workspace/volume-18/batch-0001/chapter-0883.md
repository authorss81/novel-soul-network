# Chapter 883 — A Wednesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, About Nine People In A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket From Half Past Six, A Woman Of About Sixty Who Says In About Nine Seconds That She Wrote The Tick-Box After A Week Of About Seven Evenings And Took The Word Off Another Form And Did Not Know There Were Two Kinds Of Thing To Put In A Box, And Nineteen Pounds For Four Jobs

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and the room above that line was open from about half past six with about nine people in it. It was not a sitting and nothing was counted in any room in this city that day. A woman of about sixty said one thing at the back of that room and about four people there had not known it was hers.**

---

Nine names went on that Wednesday's sheet and nine dates with them, the last taken at about twenty to five. The shutter came down at about ten.

**That board on two nails carries nineteen ruled lines and the twentieth has never been opened. The twelfth of the nineteen has been ruled for one thousand five hundred and fifty-three days, two hundred and twenty-one weeks and six days, and the thirteenth for one thousand five hundred and four, two hundred and fourteen weeks and six days.**

**The nineteenth is the last one that anybody has ruled and it stands at one thousand three hundred and twenty-nine days, a hundred and eighty-nine weeks and six days. The hold on those nine crates has gone on for one thousand two hundred and sixty-six days, a hundred and eighty weeks and six days. The fitting at the end of that corridor is one thousand one hundred and eighty-one days old, a hundred and sixty-eight weeks and five days.**

**And behind the woman of about sixty there is a space that has had nothing standing in it since a week in the spring, and it is five hundred and eleven days, and five hundred and eleven days is seventy-three weeks to the day.**

**Nobody has asked her about that space and nobody is going to, and this page is the only page of this stretch of days that puts a figure on it.**

---

**The room was open and it was not a sitting, and no number was said in it or anywhere in this city that day, because a number is said at a sitting and a sitting is a Wednesday of every fourth week and this was not one of them.**

About nine people came up between about half past six and about seven. About four of them had not been in that room since the Wednesday four weeks back. The sheet with the four columns was on its table with the fifth column empty on all nine rows and nobody looked at it, because about half of that room had read it about four times and the other half had not walked past it.

**A man of about thirty-three who writes the wording on this city's forms came in at about ten past seven and put his hand flat on the fourth column for about nine seconds and did not say anything at all, and about four people in that room watched him do it.**

---

**And at about twenty past seven a woman of about sixty said the one thing only she could say, from the back of that room, standing, in about nine seconds.**

She said that she wrote the tick-box.

She said that she wrote it after a week of about seven evenings, at a table in her own kitchen, with a pen that kept skipping.

She said in about nine seconds that she took the word out of the fourth column off another form, because it was the word already standing in that box on that form, and because it fit, and because she did not know that it came in two kinds.

She said: I did not know it came in two kinds.

Those are nine words and she said them and then she stopped, and about nine seconds passed, and nobody in that room said anything in those nine seconds.

**Nobody in that room said she had to apologise for it and nobody in that room said she had not, and about four people in that room have said since that somebody should have said something and did not.**

She is not on any body in this city. She has no post and no vote and no minute and her name is on the sheet of nobody. She has kept the form in a drawer in a kitchen in a second district since before the spring and about four people in this city knew that before this Wednesday and nobody had asked her.

**Nobody thanked her. She has said since that she was not expecting it and that she had put the word in about four years ago and had not expected to still be in the room when somebody found out.**

---

**The ninth chair in that first floor is hard against that wall with its back to the whole of the room, and it is about nine inches from the space behind her chair that has had nothing in it since a week in the spring.**

It did not move on that Wednesday, and it did not move when she said it, and about four people in that room have said since that they looked at it while she was talking and neither of them saw anybody near it.

**No page of this stretch of days names whoever moves it. Nobody in that room has ever been in the position to know.**

---

**And the sheet went back onto its table at about nine with the fifth column empty on all nine rows, and a man of about twenty-two was in that room that evening and was asked nothing at all and signed nothing.**

He said afterwards, at about ten to eight, that he had looked at the fourth column for about nine seconds and that he had understood both of the things it meant and that he had not known which was which, and that is the whole of what he took out of the evening.

**Nobody in that room asked him how he could tell and nobody has asked him since.**

---

**What was left of that Wednesday went into a fourth district for about two hours, and there were four jobs inside those two hours.**

A fuse holder on a machine in the same yard had its lid off, and the lid is what stops a hand getting to the two terminals behind it.

He put the lid back, clipped it, and proved it.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A fuse holder with no lid is a holder with a hole in it. About four of those holders in that yard have no lid, and one of them has been a machine that somebody has had to hold for about four years.**"

A saddle clip on a cold feed in the same yard had been screwed to a run of pipe with a screw either side of the joint, so that the clip itself was what held the two halves together.

He took the clip off, fitted one either side of the joint, and proved it.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A clip across a joint is a joint held by two screws and a piece of steel. About four of those clips in that yard are fixed that way, and one of them has been a split that somebody has blamed on the pipe for about four years.**"

A meter tail in the same building had two conductors going into one way out, so that the second of them could not be taken out without taking the first one out as well.

He split them, gave each its own way out, and proved the panel.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**Two conductors in one way out is a conductor you cannot change on its own. About four of those tails in that panel are doubled up that way, and one of them has been a meter reading that could not be believed for about four years.**"

A light on a machine in the same place had been earthed to a length of conduit that was itself hung on the wall on two clips.

He took the earth off the conduit and put it on the frame, and proved the fitting.

"**Four pounds,**" he said. "**An earth on conduit is an earth on whatever the conduit is touching. About four of those lights in that building are earthed that way, and one of them has been reported as a light that will not switch off for about four years.**"

**Nineteen pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**By half past six the fourth of those four rooms had been shut with a woman of about thirty behind it, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room was where it had been since a week in the spring and nobody in this city prints how many days that is.**
