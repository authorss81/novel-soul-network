# Chapter 884 — A Thursday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, About Two Hours Of A Room Off A Service Road Where Somebody Asked Where A Form Came From And Was Told That Two Places Had Copied Ours And That Nobody Had Decided Anything, And Fifty-Two Pounds For Four Jobs

**A Thursday was a bench until two, a counter until about six, and then two hours of the evening in a room off that service road with nine people sitting in it. Somebody asked where a form came from and got an answer that took about nine seconds and that nobody in that room had expected.**

---

Eleven names went onto that Thursday's sheet with eleven dates, the last of the eleven at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten after that.

**The card creased once standing on end in the two-place rail by that first door is one thousand six hundred and thirty-eight days old, and that is two hundred and thirty-four weeks to the day.**

**On the board on two nails, four lines carry a whole number of weeks this week and all four of them are correct: the twelfth at one thousand five hundred and fifty-four days, two hundred and twenty-two weeks to the day; the thirteenth at one thousand five hundred and five, two hundred and fifteen weeks to the day; the fourteenth at one thousand four hundred and seventy, two hundred and ten weeks to the day; the fifteenth at one thousand four hundred and forty-nine, two hundred and seven weeks to the day. The nineteenth and the last is one thousand three hundred and thirty days, one hundred and ninety weeks to the day. The hold over nine crates has gone on for one thousand two hundred and sixty-seven days, one hundred and eighty-one weeks to the day.**

---

**The question came out of a woman of about thirty-eight at about half past seven and it was not a hard question, and it went into a face in about nine seconds.**

She asked the man of about thirty-three where the four columns came from, and he said they came off another form, and she asked which form, and he said the one for a room.

She said, in about nine seconds, that two places she has dealt with this year have the same four columns and the same fifth heading and the same word, and that the paper on theirs is a different weight.

**Nobody in that room said the word *copied* out loud, and about four people in it have said since that they would have said it out loud and did not.**

---

**And the man of about thirty-three said the whole of it in about nine seconds and what he said was that a form gets taken when it works, and that nobody had sat down and decided anything.**

He said that this one was the plainest thing anybody in this city had ever handed him and that two places had looked at it and kept it, and that they had changed the paper and left the words, and that he could not tell them to change the words back because they had not asked him and he was not going to go and ask.

He said: nobody decided anything about it.

He said nothing at all for about nine seconds after that.

**Nobody in that room said that was a defence and nobody said it was not one, and about four people in that room have said since that it was both and that they had each picked one.**

---

**Asha Reed was in that room and had been up twice before anybody in it knew she had been, and she said one thing at about ten past eight, and it was not a hard thing either.**

She said that a form being copied is not a thing anybody can un-copy, and that the fifth column is now on four versions of a sheet in four cities and is empty on all thirty-six rows of the three that are not this one, and that this is not anybody's fault and is not anybody's doing.

**Nobody in that room told her she was wrong and about four people in that room have said since that Asha Reed has said it better than the man who wrote it.**

The man of about thirty-three said, about nine seconds later, that she had got it, and that he had not thought of it in those words, and that he had been carrying it for about four years as a thing somebody might one day decide to stop.

**Nobody in that room said the word *decide* again and nobody has asked him since whether it was ever going to.**

---

**He was in that room and was asked nothing at all and signed nothing, and what he said at about half past eight was about the paper.**

He said that the sheet on that table has been on that table since before the spring and that it was on that table when he came in on the first Monday of this stretch, and that it has not moved, and that the only thing that has moved in that room in about four months is him.

**Nobody in that room asked him how he could tell it had not moved and he said that he could not, and that it had not been dusted, and that nobody in that building dusts anything.**

---

**The rest of that Thursday was about two hours in a fourth district and four jobs in it.**

A rail bracket in the same yard had been bolted with the bolt passing through a cable behind it, so that every time the bracket was tightened it nicked the cable.

He took the bracket off, re-drove the bolt clear of the cable, and made good the insulation.

"**Twenty-one pounds,**" he said. "**A bracket through a cable is a bracket that cuts a cable every time it is tightened. About four of those brackets in that yard are through something, and one of them has been a report of a light that goes off when a gate moves for about four years.**"

A door closer in the same building had its arm set past the backcheck so that the door came back and hit the frame every time.

He backed the arm off and proved it four times running.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**An arm set past the backcheck is a door that hits itself four times a day. About four of those closers in that building are set like that, and one of them has been a door people stopped using for about four years.**"

An isolator in the same place had its enclosure lid leaning against the wall beside it and its four screws in a tin.

He put the lid on, put the four screws through, and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**An isolator with its lid off is an isolator anybody can put a finger in. About four of those enclosures in that building have their lids off, and one of them has been an accident that took a man's hands for about four years.**"

A joint on a feed in the same yard had a washer under it that had gone soft and been squeezed out, so that the joint was weeping onto the floor of that bay.

He cut the joint back, made it up with a new washer, and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A washer squeezed out is a joint that is holding on nothing. About four of those joints in that bay are done that way, and one of them has been a puddle somebody keeps putting a bucket under for about four years.**"

**Fifty-two pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**That fourth room was shut with a woman of about thirty behind it a little before half past six, and the ring binder on the shelf at the back of it stood in the place it stands in, and no figure for how long it has been there is printed on this page.**
