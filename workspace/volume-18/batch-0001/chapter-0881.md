# Chapter 881 — A Monday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Half An Hour Of A Stair In A Second District With A Door Closer Screwed To The Top Of A Step, A Woman Of About Forty-Three Who Put A Sheet Of About Nine Rows On A Table About Nine Feet Away And Said Nothing To The Man On The Floor, And Thirty-One Pounds For Four Jobs

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and about half an hour of it in a stair in a second district with a door closer in his hand. Nine jobs were dated for the day on a board by the till. A sheet went onto a table in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket at about eleven while he was on the step.**

---

Nine names went on that Monday's sheet and nine dates went with them, the last one at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down. The cage in the round-the-side had been going about four times in the fortnight and the van was not needed.

**The four converted units off that service road have stood one thousand six hundred and thirty-one days, and that is two hundred and thirty-three weeks to the day. The card creased once that holds a doorway open in the rail by that door is four days further on, at one thousand six hundred and thirty-five, or two hundred and thirty-three weeks and four days, and the rail takes two.**

**On the board of two nails the eighteenth of the nineteen ruled lines has been ruled for one thousand three hundred and forty-nine days, one hundred and ninety-two weeks and five days. What is owed on the open page lying in front of the man of about fifty-one is one thousand three hundred and twenty-one days, and that man himself is one thousand two hundred and thirty-seven days old in that spot, one hundred and seventy-six weeks and five days.**

The separation stands at one thousand and eleven days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and three days, in a one-line box about sixteen months old.

---

**The stair is the top of a stair in a building that lets out four rooms on a first floor, and the door that slams is the one at the bottom of it, and it has been slamming since before the spring because the closer on top of it is set to shut it as fast as the door will bear.**

He had it off before he went and he had the new one in a bag, and the adjustment screw on the old one was down as far as it will go and somebody had put a piece of tape round it to keep it there.

**About nine people came up that stair between ten and half past eleven and about four of them were going to the rooms and about five of the other five were not going anywhere in particular, which is what a stair in that building is at that hour.**

He had the old unit off and the back plate out and the dust in his hands and his knees on the step when the door at the bottom went and did not slam, because the closer was on the bench beside him in pieces.

---

**And the sheet was already on the table when he got back, and it had been there for about four minutes, and about four people had stood at the other side of it and read it and gone back down the stair without saying anything to anybody.**

It is about the size of a sheet of writing paper with a fold down the middle. There are four columns with a heading over each of them and nothing above the headings, and under the fourth column there is a fifth heading with nothing under it, and the fifth heading is *review*.

**There is one word in the fourth column and it is the same word on every row of it, and the word is *load*.**

Nobody in this city has written that word in a column on a form and meant two different things by it. **A woman of about forty-three put the sheet on that table on a Monday morning at about eleven, and she has been the person who puts it there about once a week since before the spring, and she is not the author of it and she did not choose the word.**

He read it three times standing at the counter when he got in.

He wanted to ask her what the fifth column was for. **He had a sentence ready in about two seconds and he did not say it, and the reason he did not say it, which he worked out on the Thursday and not at the time, was that she had not asked him what he was doing, and a person who is not asked does not ask.** That is the third time in about four years and he has noticed it is a habit and not a virtue.

---

**The building had paid him before she came up, in an envelope, at the door, by a man of about fifty-eight who said nothing beyond the amount and did not ask him to sign anything.**

He was on the step again by about half past eleven with the new unit bolted to the top of the door and the tape off the old screw and the old unit in the bag with the tape still round it, because he does not throw away a piece of tape that somebody had put there on purpose.

**Nobody in that building said one word to him about the sheet, and nobody in that building read it out loud to him, and about four people read it and about five did not.**

He was back at the counter at about half past one and the shop had gone on without him and had not stopped.

---

**About nine jobs went out on that Monday and about nine came in and nothing escalated and nothing was handed back, and the till came up about two pounds out and he counted the tray twice.**

A woman of about thirty-eight brought in a bracket for a gate and wanted it the same afternoon because the gate was open on a lane and the lane is a bus route.

"**That is a bracket,**" he said. "**It'll be Thursday.**"

She said Thursday and it was Thursday and she came back on Thursday and it went on.

**He charged about nine pounds for it and the bracket cost him about two and he did not tell her that and did not charge her for the ten minutes it took him to explain that a gate bracket that has been open on a bus route for a fortnight is a bracket somebody has already been told to replace and has not.**

---

**A terminal block in the same yard had two conductors under one clamp, so that the second of them had no clamp of its own and had been carrying about half its load through the first.**

He split them and gave the second its own clamp.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**Two conductors under one clamp is one conductor with no connection. About four of those blocks in that panel are doubled up that way, and one of them has been an intermittent fault for about four years.**"

---

**A foot switch on a machine in the same building had a cam worn on the inside of the face so that the switch had been staying in for the last month of its life.**

He took the switch off, put a new cam in it, and proved it drops out when the foot comes off.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A cam worn on the inside is a switch that never comes back. About four of those switches in that building are worn on the inside, and one of them has been a machine that runs on for about four years.**"

---

**A lamp holder in a corridor of the same yard had its centre contact blackened round, because the ring that holds the lamp had been loose for long enough to let the arc across it.**

He changed the ring, cleaned the contact, and proved it.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A black ring is a lamp that has been arcing to a loose contact. About four of those holders in that corridor have loose rings, and one of them has been a lamp that flickers for about four years.**"

---

**A gate in the same place had a closing stop fitted with a screw in a slotted hole, so that anybody leaning on it could push it past its own stop.**

He packed the hole and put a bolt in the middle of it.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A stop in a slotted hole is a suggestion to anybody in a hurry. About four of those gates have stops like that, and one of them has been a gate that will not stay shut for about four years.**"

**Thirty-one pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**At about ten he went up a stair near Civic Spine, about nine doors along from the tram stop, and about four minutes after him a woman of about twenty-four came up the same stair and went to a door about nine metres from his and shut it.**

They had rice and a stew that was three days old because that was in the pot, and bread, and there was no second thing.

She asked him what he had done on the day and he told her about the stair and the closer and the tape and the man of about fifty-eight with the envelope, and he did not tell her about the sheet, **because he did not want to say the word *review* at a table on a Monday night and have it be the thing they talked about.**

She said a gate in that yard had been reported for four years and was still open, and he said it was, and he said the bracket was nine pounds, and she said nine pounds for a bracket.

**Nobody said anything else about the sheet that night and neither of them has mentioned it since and it has been on that table for about four months.**

---

**Behind the shut door of the fourth of those four rooms there was a woman of about thirty at about half past six, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room has not been out since a week in the spring of a year nobody in this city counts any more.**

**Nobody has put a question to her about that page and nobody ever is going to, and this page does not print how long it has been on that shelf, because the last time this manuscript printed that figure it printed it for the last time.**

---

**He wanted, all of that afternoon, for one person to come in and ask him what was in the fourth column.**

Nobody came in and asked him what was in the fourth column. Nobody came in and asked him about anything that was not on the ticket.
