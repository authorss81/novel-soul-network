## 2. THE MOVEMENT IN ONE PARAGRAPH, AND WHAT IT IS ABOUT

**A sheet with about nine rows on it has been on a table in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket since before the spring. It has four columns and a fifth heading, and nothing has ever been in the fifth column on any of the nine rows. Movement I finds out three things and resolves none of them. **First**, that the fourth column came off another form and that a woman of about sixty put it there after a week of about seven evenings at her own kitchen table, taking the word off a form she already had, not knowing that it came in two kinds. **Second**, that the same four columns and the same fifth heading are on a form in two other cities, because two other cities copied this one, and that a form gets copied when it works, and that nobody decided anything. **Third**, that a man of about fifty-seven is one of the nine and can talk for about four minutes about anything at all and cannot answer a question about himself, and that the two facts are not connected by anything he can say. **The movement closes on a Friday with the reason for the empty column said out loud once, in a face, in about nine seconds, by the woman of about forty-three, and with about nine people in a room then doing nothing on purpose, and with nothing decided and nothing changed.**
