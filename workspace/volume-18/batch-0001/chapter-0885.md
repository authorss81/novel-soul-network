# Chapter 885 — A Friday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Hall In A Fourth District At About Seven Where A Man Of About Fifty-Seven Talked For About Four Minutes About Anything At All And Then Could Not Answer A Question About Himself, And Thirty-Seven Pounds For Four Jobs

He talked for about four minutes and then a woman asked him a question about himself and he could not answer it.**

**The man of about fifty-seven came at about seven and he had walked to it, and a woman of about forty-three came with him because he had asked her to come with him and because it is far.**

He talked from about seven until about nine minutes past seven and he was the fastest talker in that hall. He talked about the doors on that stretch of a road and about two men he worked with about nine years ago and about a bus and about a price somebody paid in about four months in a year he could remember the shape of and not the number of.

**Nobody in that hall interrupted him once. About four people in that hall have said since that they wanted to ask him four things in those four minutes and did not.**

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-one asked him a question about himself, standing in front of him, and he could not answer it.**

She said: do you still know how to do the thing you came in here to talk about.

**He did not stop talking because of the question. That is the part about that about four people in that hall have described since and none of them described it the same way.** He carried on for about nine seconds and then he stopped, and the room was the quietest that room had been in about four months.

She asked it a second time, more slowly, with the same words.

**And he said no, in about nine seconds, into her face, and he did not say it unkindly and he did not apologise for it, and nobody in that hall has said since that he should have.**

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-eight asked him a question that was not about the thing, to see whether it was only that one.**

She said: what is your name.

He said it, and he said the street he lives on, and he said how long he had lived there, and he answered all three in about four seconds each.

**Nobody in that hall asked him a third question and about four people in that hall have said since that they wanted to and did not, and one of them has said since that she decided in the nine seconds not to.**

He has said since that the second of those two was the one that frightened him and that he could not say why the first one was not the same shape.

---

**And the woman of about forty-three did not say one word for the whole of those eleven minutes, and said one thing afterwards at about ten past eight, and the thing was about her.**

She said that she had written the four columns out by hand in her own kitchen twice and that the second time she had copied them off the first time and that she had gone over the fourth column both times without stopping on it.

**Nobody in that hall asked her why and nobody has asked her since, and about four people in that hall have said since that they would have asked and did not.**

The man of about fifty-seven said afterwards, at about half past eight, that she was not to blame for it, and that nobody in that hall had said she was, and that he had wanted to say it before she did.

**Nobody in that hall said what he wanted to say it for and nobody has asked him.**

---

**He was in that hall that evening and was asked nothing that was not on the list and signed nothing.**

He said afterwards, at about a quarter to nine, that the man of about thirty-three had put his hand on the fourth column on the Wednesday and had not said anything, and that he had been in this city for about nine years and had not known anybody who could do that.

**Nobody in that hall asked him how he knew that and he said that he could not, and that it was only that he had watched.**

**The rest of that Friday was about two hours in a fourth district and four jobs in it.**

A swing mirror in a washroom in the same building had been hung on one screw through a plastic boss, so that the whole of it swung when anybody shut the door.

He found the stud, took the single screw out, and fixed the mirror to the frame on two screws.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A mirror on one screw is a mirror on a hinge. About four of those mirrors in that building are fixed that way, and one of them has been a mirror nobody can use for about four years.**"

A fluorescent fitting in the same building had an earth continuity broken somewhere between the batten and the tube, so that the fitting had no earth path at all.

He proved it dead, found the break in the starter holder, and made it good.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A light with no earth path is a light that is only held together. About four of those fittings in that building have a broken path, and one of them has been a complaint about touching for about four years.**"

A window restrictor in the same place had been fixed to nothing at all, to a piece of frame that had come away from the wall behind it.

He took it off, filled the hole, and fixed the restrictor into the masonry.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A restrictor fixed to a frame that has come off is a restrictor on two pieces of wood. About four of those restrictors in that building are fixed that way, and one of them has been an open window that has been called a fire risk for about four years.**"

A fire door in the same yard had a wedge under it holding it a finger's width clear of the floor, on the latch side.

He took the wedge away and adjusted the closer so the door sits on its seal.

"**Four pounds,**" he said. "**A wedge under a fire door is a room that cannot be a room. About four of those doors in that yard have something under them, and one of them has been propped for about four years.**"

**Thirty-seven pounds is what those four came to on that Friday, exact.**
