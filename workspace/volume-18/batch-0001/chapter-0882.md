# Chapter 882 — A Tuesday, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, A Hall Where Somebody Reads In On Fridays And Nobody Was There On A Tuesday Until About Seven, A Woman Of About Nineteen Who Said The Thing That Nine People In A Room Already Knew And Had Never Said In One Sentence, And Forty-Four Pounds For Four Jobs

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and after that about an hour and a half of it was in a hall where about nine people stood and listened to a sheet of paper being read out, and the word in the fourth column of that sheet is one word on every row and it does not mean one thing.**

---

Ten names and ten dates went onto that Tuesday's sheet, and the tenth of them at about twenty to five, and then the shutter came down.

**Those four converted units behind that service road stand at one thousand six hundred and thirty-two days, and the seventeenth of the nineteen lines ruled on the board on two nails has been ruled for one thousand four hundred and four days, which is two hundred weeks and four days.**

**The hold over those nine crates has been on for one thousand two hundred and sixty-five days, one hundred and eighty weeks and five days. The nine hands' copies of the front of one page have been in those nine hands for one thousand one hundred and ninety-eight days, one hundred and seventy-one weeks and one day, and no two of the nine has ever been set one against another.**

The separation is at one thousand and twelve days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and four days.

---

**The hall is on a road in a fourth district and it is used on Fridays by a woman of about nineteen who reads to about nine people who turn up, and on no other day of the week anybody has ever been inside it, and on this Tuesday it had about nine people in it who had not come for that.**

They had come because a woman of about nineteen had walked into the middle of that floor at about ten past seven and said, in about nine seconds, that the word in the fourth column was doing two jobs and that nine people in this city knew that and had never said it in the same sentence.

She said: load means too much, and it also means gone.

She said that into the face of a woman of about forty-three, and she did not say it twice, and she said it standing, and she is nineteen and she reads on Fridays.

**Nobody in that hall said whether she had got it and about four people in that hall have said since that she had got it, and about four have said since that she had said it in the wrong building.**

---

**And the woman of about forty-three was asked what the word meant, in a face, in about nine seconds, and she answered in about nine seconds and it was true.**

She said: it means exactly what it says on the row.

**Nobody in that hall said that was not an answer and about four people in that hall have said since that it was the answer to a different question, and that she had answered it correctly on purpose.**

The man of about thirty-three, who writes the wording on this city's forms and who was in that hall standing at the back, said afterwards that she had answered it correctly on purpose and that he had watched her decide to.

**Nobody has asked him since how he could tell and he has said since that he could not and that he has thought about it perhaps four times.**

---

**The sheet went back onto its table at about a quarter to nine with the fifth column still empty on all nine rows, and nobody put anything in it, and nobody had been asked to.**

**On the same Tuesday, at about half past two, a man of twenty-two was at a counter and a woman of about thirty-eight asked him whether he had heard about a sheet.**

He said no, and she said it was not important, and he said it was not important either, and she said it was the only thing anybody had said in about four years.

**That is a refusal, and it took about four seconds, and it was given to himself and to nobody else, and he has said since that the four seconds went before he had decided anything and that he does not know whether they were the reason.**

---

**The rest of that Tuesday was a counter and ten jobs, and he did all ten and charged for all ten.**

A man of about sixty came in with a lamp he had brought back because he had brought it back before.

"**That is a lamp,**" Marek said. "**You have had that lamp before.**"

"**Twice,**" the man said. "**It is not the lamp, it is the flex.**"

"**Then it is the flex,**" Marek said, and it was the flex, and it cost him about eleven pounds to make, and the man had paid twenty-six for it at a shop on a wide road, and Marek did not ask which shop and did not say anything about it and has thought about that shop perhaps four times since.

---

**A gland plate on a motor in a yard in a fourth district had been fitted over a hole bigger than itself, so that about four millimetres of the cable had been outside the plate and inside the machine.**

He took the plate off, cut the hole to the size the plate is for, and refitted it and proved it.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**A plate that does not cover its hole is a plate fitted by eye, and about four of those glands in that yard were fitted that way, and one of them has been a motor that trips for about four years.**"

---

**A handrail on a gangway in the same building had been made up from two lengths of different diameter with a sleeve over the join, and the sleeve had not been fixed at the top.**

He took the sleeve off, made one length out of the two, and left the gangway with one rail on it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A sleeve over a join is a place for a rail to come apart. About four gangways in that yard have rails like that, and one of them has been a rail that moves for about four years.**"

---

**A bearing in the same place had its grease nipple turned into a corner, where a thumb could not reach it without taking something else off first.**

He moved the nipple to where a thumb goes and greased it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A nipple in a corner is a nipple nobody oils. About four bearings in that building are nippleed into corners, and one of them has been a bearing that runs hot for about four years.**"

---

**A stair light in the same yard had its earth taken off its own metal plate and onto a length of flex that somebody could pull.**

He put the earth on the frame where it belongs and proved it.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**An earth on flex is an earth a person can pull out. About four of those lights in that building are earthed that way, and one of them has been a light that hums when it is on for about four years.**"

**Forty-four pounds is what those four came to on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**At about half past six the fourth of those four rooms was shut with a woman of about thirty behind the door, and the binder on the back shelf of that room has not been out.**

**Nobody has asked her one question about that page, nobody is going to, and how long it has been on that shelf is a figure this page does not print.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody in that hall on that Tuesday and nobody has thanked the woman of about nineteen since, and she has said since that she did not expect it and that she had said it out loud because otherwise it would have stayed a thing she thought.**

She said that thinking a thing is not the same as saying it in a room with nine people in it.

**Nobody in that hall said she had been brave and nobody said she had made a fool of herself, and about four people there have said since that they wanted to say one of those and did not.**
