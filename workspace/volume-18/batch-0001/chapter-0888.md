# Chapter 888 — A Wednesday Of The Second Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, About Two Hours In A Room Off A Service Road Where The Fifth Column Was Found To Have A Heading On All Nine Rows And Nothing In It On Any Of Them, And Forty-Eight Pounds For Four Jobs

**A Wednesday was a bench until two, then a counter until about six, and about two hours of the rest of it were in a room off that service road with about nine people in it. The fifth column turned out to have a heading over all nine rows and nothing at all under it on any of them, and a man of about thirty-three put that in one sentence in about nine seconds.**

---

That Wednesday brought eleven names onto the sheet and eleven dates with them, the last taken at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten that evening.

**The four units off that service road stand at one thousand six hundred and forty days, two hundred and thirty-four weeks and two days. The card creased once in the two-place rail at that first door is one thousand six hundred and forty-four days, two hundred and thirty-four weeks and six days.**

**On that board and in that first floor, three of the standing figures carry a whole number of weeks on this day and all three are correct: the eighteenth of the nineteen ruled lines at one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight days, one hundred and ninety-four weeks to the day; what that open page is owed, lying in front of the man of about fifty-one, at one thousand three hundred and thirty days, one hundred and ninety weeks to the day; and that man himself at one thousand two hundred and forty-six days in that spot, one hundred and seventy-eight weeks to the day. The nineteenth line on that board is one thousand three hundred and thirty-six days, one hundred and ninety weeks and six days, and the thirteenth is one thousand five hundred and eleven days, two hundred and fifteen weeks and six days.**

---

**The fifth column was found at about half past seven on that Wednesday by a woman of about thirty-one who had come to that room on her own account and had told nobody she was coming.**

She said, standing at the head of the table, in about nine seconds, that there were nine rows and every row had the heading on it and nothing under the heading on any of the nine, and that she had been looking at that sheet for about four days.

**Nobody in that room had looked at the fifth column. Nobody in that room had looked at the fifth column in the four months it has been on that table, and about four people in that room have said since that they had read the sheet from the top down nine times and had gone past it every time.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-three said it in one sentence and it took him about nine seconds, and he was not asked to take it back and he was not asked for a second one.**

He said: that sheet has been due for about four years.

He said that the oldest of the nine rows is about four years old and that every row of them has the same heading and nothing in it, and that a heading with nothing under it on every row is not a thing anybody has forgotten to fill in, because somebody would have filled one in.

**Nobody in that room said what would go in it and about four people in that room have said since that they each had an answer ready and kept it to themselves and have not been asked for it since.**

---

**And then she asked him what a review is for, and he answered in about nine seconds, and what he said is the whole of what this volume is about and it did not settle anything.**

He said a review is there so you ask the person.

He said that it is there so that a person cannot be held on somebody else's say-so for ever, and that the answer can be yes or it can be no, and that it is the only column on that sheet that anybody cannot fill in for them.

**Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds and about four people in that room have said since that they had not known that was what the column was.**

He said, in about nine seconds, that he wrote that heading in about four years ago as the last thing on that sheet on purpose, and that he has been putting it on about once a week ever since, and that he did not know until that Wednesday what it was like to read it nine times.

---

**A woman of about forty-three was in that room and said two things, and the first was about the sheet and the second was not.**

She said that the sheet goes back onto that table on the Monday and it does not go into a drawer, and that it is the only thing in this building that does not go into a drawer, and that she had been asked about that once by a man in about four years.

She said, in about nine seconds, that the fourth column is the reason the fifth one is empty and that she was not going to say anything else about the fourth column in that room that evening.

**Nobody in that room asked her for anything else about the fourth column, and about four people there have said since that they would have asked her and did not, and one of them has said since that she made up her mind in about nine seconds not to.**

---

**He was in that room, asked nothing at all, signed nothing, and what he said at about twenty past eight was about the empty space.**

He said that there are about nine empty squares on that sheet and that the sheet has been on that table about four months, and that he had looked at them on the Monday of this stretch and on every Monday since and had counted them each time.

**Nobody in that room asked him why he counted and he said that he could not, and that he did it anyway.**

---

**The balance of that Wednesday was a fourth district and about two hours of it, and four jobs were got through in those two hours.**

A machine guard in the same yard had been left hinged with two screws and none of them into the frame, so that the whole of it came away if it was pushed.

He took it off, made up the two holes, and fixed it to the frame everywhere it was meant to be fixed.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A guard with no screws into the frame is a card. About four of those guards in that yard are fixed that way, and one of them has been a report of a hand that went in for about four years.**"

The supply hose of a washing machine in the same building had been routed behind a drawer that came out, so that every time the drawer came out it pulled at the joint.

He re-routed the hose with a clip out of the swing and proved it.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A hose behind a drawer is a hose the drawer has hold of. About four of those machines in that building are plumbed that way, and one of them has been a machine that leaks every time anybody looks in it for about four years.**"

A carbon monoxide alarm in the same place had a date on the face of it about nine years ago and had been on a shelf ever since, above a gas fire.

He took it down, threw it out, and fitted a new one on the same bracket.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**An alarm older than the room goes on a shelf and stays on the shelf. About four of those alarms in that building are past their date, and one of them has been on a shelf above a fire for about four years.**"

A socket in that same room had been put under a doormat, so that anything plugged into it was under about half an inch of rubber matting.

He lifted the mat, moved the socket up the wall, and proved the circuit.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A socket under a mat is a socket that cannot be seen and cannot be felt. About four of those sockets in that building are under something, and one of them has been a complaint that nobody could explain for about four years.**"

**Forty-eight pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**
