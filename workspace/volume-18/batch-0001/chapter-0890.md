**About nine people came up into that first floor between about half past six and about seven and about four of them had been in that room on the Wednesday of the same week and the other five had not.**

The sheet was on its table. The fifth column had a heading on all nine rows and nothing under the heading on any of them. Nobody looked at the fifth column for about the first four minutes.

**About four people in that room have said since that they stood in that room deliberately not looking at a column, and that this is a thing nobody has ever said out loud in a room in this city.**

---

**And at about ten past seven a woman of about forty-three said it, once, into a face, in about nine seconds, and it was the only time it was said that evening.**

She said there are two ways and both of them fail.

She said, in about nine seconds, that the first way is to ask the person, and that she has been the person who asks nine questions about nine things for about four years and has never once been able to finish one, and that the second way is to carry on putting their names in the fourth column until somebody who is not her decides to stop.

She said: I have known that for about four years now.

She said that and stopped.

**Nobody in that room asked her a second question about it, nobody has asked her since, and about four people in that room have said since that they wanted to ask and did not.**

---

**And what the room did next was nothing, and it did it deliberately, and about four people in that room have said since that they noticed it happening.**

A man of about thirty-three said that the sheet goes back on the table on Monday.

He said, in about nine seconds, that he was not going to put anything in that column on that Friday and that he was not going to say why, and that it was not because anybody had asked him to.

Nobody argued with him. Nobody agreed with him. A woman of about thirty-one said that she had wanted somebody to say something on that Friday for about nine days and that nobody had.

**Nobody in that room said what anybody would do about it after that, and about four people in that room have said since that each of them knows what they are going to do about it and not one of them has said it in a room.**

---

**He was in that room that evening, was asked nothing at all and signed nothing, and he is not the person who signs anything in this stretch of days.**

He said afterwards, at about half past eight, that he had counted the empty squares again, and that there were nine of them, and that it was nine on the Monday and nine on the Friday and that nobody in that room had ever once suggested that the count might change.

**Nobody in that room asked him what he would do if the count ever changed, and nobody in this city has asked him.**

---

**In that first floor the ninth chair has its back to that wall and to everything done in the room, and it did not move on that Friday, and its mover is not named on any page of this stretch of days.**

**And the woman of about sixty was not in that first floor on that Friday, and nobody sent for her, and nobody has told her that anything was said in that room about the column.**

**What was left of that Friday was a fourth district and about two hours of the evening, and four jobs were in it.**

An extract fan in the same yard had been running with the isolator on the wall turned off, because somebody had put a jumper across the switch.

He took the jumper out, proved the fan, and proved the isolator.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A fan that runs with the isolator off is a fan nobody can find. About four of those isolators in that yard have jumpers across them, and one of them has been a fan that ran all night four times a month for about four years.**"

A door in the same building closed on a tile overhang that had been laid about four millimetres proud.

He took the hinge off, packed it back about a quarter of an inch, and refitted it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A door that closes on its own tile is a door that gets kicked. About four of those doors in that building are hung on a proud tile, and one of them has been reported as a door that will not shut quietly for about four years.**"

A lamp in the same yard had a missing ring, so that the lamp was being held in the holder by the thread of its base.

He fitted a ring and proved it.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp with no ring is a lamp hanging on a thread. About four of those holders in that yard have no rings, and one of them has been a lamp that has fallen for about four years.**"

A stair rail in the same building had a bracket at the bottom end with two of its four fixings in nothing at all.

He took the rail off, filled the holes, and put it back with four fixings.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A rail bracket with two screws in air is a rail on two screws. About four of those brackets in that building are fixed into nothing, and one of them has been a handrail that nobody could shut for about four years.**"

**Thirty-four pounds is what those four came to on that Friday, exact.**
