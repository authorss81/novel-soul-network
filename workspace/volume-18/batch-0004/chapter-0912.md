# Chapter 912 — A Tuesday Of The Ninth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six Before It, And Then About Two Hours Of That Evening Gone Into A First Floor In A Second District Above A Shop Where Nine Folding Chairs Stood In A Row In Front Of A Table And Nobody Knew Why, A Woman Of About Sixty-Two Sitting In One Of Them With A Key On A Loop Of Tape, And Nobody Asked Her What It Was, And Thirty-Eight Pounds For Four Jobs

**That Tuesday evening had a bench until two and a counter until about six behind it, and then about two hours of it went into a first floor in a second district, above a shop, where about nine people were. It was not a sitting, and nothing in this city was counted out loud in any room.**

---

Eight callers came into that shop on that Tuesday and the last of them at about ten to nine, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four rooms off that service road had stood one thousand six hundred and eighty-eight days by that Tuesday, and that is two hundred and forty-one weeks and one day. That Tuesday the sixteenth of those nineteen lines had been ruled for one thousand four hundred and seventy-eight days, and that is two hundred and eleven weeks and one day, and nine hands' copies of the front of one page had been taken one thousand two hundred and fifty-four days before that, and that is one hundred and seventy-nine weeks and one day.**

---

**The four converted units off that service road were four rooms in another district, and they were shut by the time that Tuesday evening got to a second district, and about nine people had gone up a steel stair above a shop there instead.**

The stair turned once and the landing had four folding chairs on it that were not the chairs.

At the top of it there was a room with a table in it and nine more folding chairs with metal frames set out in a row in front of that table, and the chairs had been set out in an even row with the gaps the same width, and there was nothing on the table at all.

**Nobody in that room knew why the chairs were there and nobody in that room asked anybody else in that room why.**

The room is not the records room in that district, which is a landlord and a filing system and nothing else, and it is not the room off the road where a service group meets. It is a room over a shop that lets it out on a Tuesday because the group that has it on a Wednesday does not come on a Tuesday.

---

**Asha Reed was in that room and had not seen that row of chairs before she went up that stair, and she said one thing about them, and it was said into the middle of the room and not to anybody.**

She said: somebody set this room up for a meeting and never came back for it.

**Nobody in that room told her she was wrong, and about four people in that room have said since that they had walked past those chairs on their way to the table without counting them, and that they have not been able to say since when.**

---

**And a woman of about sixty-two was sitting in one of those nine chairs when about nine people came into that room, and she had a thing in her hand that she had kept for about four years, and nobody in that room knew what it was and nobody in that room asked her what it was.**

It is a key on a loop of brown tape, and the tape had writing on it, and the writing on that tape has gone so far that nobody who has looked at it has been able to read it.

There were five of them. She kept them in a purse and she brought one.

**Nobody in that room asked her about the tape and nobody in that room asked her about the tin and nobody in that room has asked her since, on any of the ten days of this movement.**

She has said since that she had not known she was going to bring it, and that she had put it in her coat at about half past five for no reason she could put into words afterwards.

---

**A man of about thirty-three was in that room and asked nobody what the chairs were for and answered nothing, and he has said since that he wanted to ask and did not, and that he has been thinking about the row of them since.**

He has said since that the gaps between the chairs were all the same and that whoever set them out had measured, and that he has not been able to think of a reason to measure the gaps on a set of chairs for a meeting that has not happened.

---

**Nothing in that room had a space behind it. The chairs stood in an even row with their backs to a wall that had nothing against it, and the table stood clear of that wall with a foot of space all round it, and about four people in that room have said since that they noticed the foot of space and have not been able to say what they expected to find in it.**

---

**A man of twenty-two was in that room on that Tuesday and he was the one who carried a chair up that stair with the others, and nobody in that room asked him anything or gave him anything to sign.**

He has said since that the chair was heavier than it looked and that he had thought about saying so and had not, and that he has carried other things up stairs for that room before and never thought about any of them again.

---

**About two hours of that Tuesday went into the same yard and the same building afterwards, and four jobs went into them.**

A machine in a workshop had been given a supply that came off a spare way out on a board where the same spare way was doing something else, so that one machine starting was one other machine stopping.

He gave the machine a supply of its own out of a way that was not in use and proved both circuits with everything running.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A machine sharing a spare way is a machine that stops something else. About four of those machines in that workshop are on ways they share, and one of them has been a workshop that blamed itself for about four years.**"

A light in a stairwell had its switch on a landing circuit that also carried the light in the next stairwell, so that the two had been put on and off together for as long as anybody could remember.

He gave that light a switch of its own and proved the two stairwells separately.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**Two lights on one switch is a person who cannot light one stair without lighting another. About four of those switches in that block are shared, and one of them has been an argument about a dark stair for about four years.**"

A socket in a workshop had been fitted into a metal box that was itself resting on a length of wooden batten, so that the earth went back through timber and the batten had two screws into nothing behind it.

He fixed the box to the wall, took the earth onto it, and proved the socket with a load on it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A socket in a box on a batten is a socket on a length of timber. About four of those fittings in that workshop are on battens, and one of them has been a machine that would not hold its earth for about four years.**"

A dehumidifier in a store had been put on the lighting board for the store because there was nothing else free on it, and it came on with the lights and went off with them.

He gave it a way of its own out of the board and proved it running on its own.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A dehumidifier on the lights is a dehumidifier that runs when somebody walks past. About four of those machines in that store are on a lighting board, and one of them has been a damp problem nobody could account for in about four years.**"

**Thirty-eight pounds is what those four came to on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**By about half past six on that Tuesday the door of the fourth of those four rooms was shut, with a woman of about thirty behind it, and nothing came off the binder standing on the shelf at the back of that room, and this page prints no figure for it.**
