# Chapter 920 — A Monday Of The Eleventh Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In That First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket With About Nine People In It, A Thing Held For About Four Years Gone Out Of That Room And Nobody Saying Where It Went, A Woman Of About Sixty-Two Not Thanked And Not Asked To Stay, And Forty-One Pounds For Four Jobs

**One more Monday came round with a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about two hours of that Monday evening went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were. There was no sitting in it, and nothing was said aloud in this city as a figure.**

---

Ten callers came into that shop on that Monday and the tenth of them at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road had stood one thousand seven hundred and one days by that Monday, and that is two hundred and forty-three weeks to the day. That Monday the sixteenth of those nineteen ruled lines had been ruled one thousand four hundred and ninety-one days before it, and that is two hundred and thirteen weeks to the day, and nine hands' copies of the front of one page had been taken one thousand two hundred and sixty-seven days before that, and that is one hundred and eighty-one weeks to the day.**

---

**The nine chairs were still in that room on that Monday, in the row in front of that table, in the same gaps, and nobody in that room has said since why they were not taken back down.**

Nobody was told which of them was theirs on any of the days they were in that room, and nobody asked to be told, and nobody has said since how many of the nine people who came into that room sat down in one of them on any given evening.

**Nobody in that room said which chair in that row was not used on that Monday, and nobody in that room said it on any of the ten days of this movement, and nobody in that room knows which one it is.**

---

**And the thing a woman of about sixty-two had held for about four years was not in that room on that Monday, and nobody in that room said where it had gone.**

Nobody in that room saw her take it and nobody in that room has said since that anybody else took it, and nobody in that room has asked her about it, and she has said nothing about it to anybody.

She has said since that she was not in that room on that Monday and that she has not been in that room since the Friday before, and that she was told about that Monday by somebody who has not said who.

---

**And nobody in that room thanked her and nobody asked her to stay, and about four people in that room have said since that they would have asked her to stay.**

That was said after the shutter came down, in about nine minutes, in a yard in another district, by four people who were not all in that room on that Monday and have not said which of them was.

One of those four has said since that asking her to stay would have meant asking her to explain, and that she had watched her put a thing on a table and not explain it, and that she could not see how those two were kept apart.

A second of those four has said since that she would have asked her to stay and would have said nothing else at all, and that this is not the same thing as the first of those four said and that nobody in this city has said it is.

---

**That sheet with four columns was on its own table at about seven on that Monday with the fifth heading standing over nothing on all nine of its rows, and it was on that table at about nine, and nobody in that room said one word about it on that Monday or on any of the ten days of this movement.**

Nobody in that room said on that Monday that the nine rows on that sheet had been dealt with. Nobody in that room said that they had not been dealt with either.

**Nobody in that room has said since which of the two is a true one, and this page does not say either, and there is nobody in this city who could.**

---

**In that room on that Monday was a man of twenty-two, asked nothing and signing nothing, and he stood at the back by the rail with the card in it because there were nine chairs in that room and he did not sit in one of them.**

He has said since that he has been asked about that row of chairs by nobody in this city, and that he is the only person he knows of who has stood behind them every evening, and that he has not told anybody why.

---

**The rest of that Monday went into about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A distribution board in a laundry had two circuits on one way out because the second had been added with a spare that was not there, so that one of them was always at the end of what the board could give.

He took the second onto a way of its own and proved both at their loads together.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**Two circuits on one way out is a board that has run out. About four of those boards in that laundry were added to without a way being spare, and one of them has been a laundry that cannot dry two loads at once for about four years.**"

A washer in that laundry had its supply taken off the lighting board because there was nothing else free, so that it came on with the lights and went off at the switch by the door.

He gave it a way of its own and proved it through a full cycle with the lights off.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A washer on the lights is a washer that only runs when somebody is standing in the room. About four of those machines in that laundry are on a lighting way, and one of them has been a laundry nobody could explain the running costs of for about four years.**"

A light in a corridor had been put in over a door that was opened every day, so that the fitting was in a draught and its switch was on the wrong side of the door.

He moved the switch, closed the gap, and proved it from both sides of the door.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A light over a door that opens all day is a light in a draught with its switch in the way. About four of those fittings in that corridor are over doors, and one of them has been a corridor with a light everybody switched off and on for about four years.**"

An immersion heater in a flat had been put in on a way that was shared with the hot water cylinder, so that running one of them pulled the other.

He gave the immersion its own way and proved the cylinder separately.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**An immersion sharing a way with the cylinder is a bath that ends the hot water. About four of those immersions in that block share a way, and one of them has been a complaint nobody could account for in about four years.**"

**Those four came to forty-one pounds on that Monday, exact.**

---

**By about half past six on that Monday the fourth of those four rooms was shut, with a woman of about thirty behind the door and a binder on the shelf at the back that did not come out, and this page prints no figure for it.**
