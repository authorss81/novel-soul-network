# Chapter 913 — A Wednesday Of The Ninth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket, A Man Of About Thirty-Three Asking What A Sheet Is For And A Woman Of About Sixty Answering Him Into His Face In One Sentence, The Same Sentence That Is Already On The Wall Of That Room And Nobody In That Room Saying So, And Forty-Two Pounds For Four Jobs

**On the Wednesday after that there was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about two hours of that Wednesday evening went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were. There was no sitting in it, and no number was said aloud in any room in this city.**

---

Ten callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and the last of them at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The eighteenth of the nineteen ruled lines had been ruled one thousand four hundred and seven days before that Wednesday, and that is two hundred and one weeks to the day. The sum owed on the open page lying in front of the man of about fifty-one was one thousand three hundred and seventy-nine days, and that is one hundred and ninety-seven weeks to the day, and that man had been against that north wall one thousand two hundred and ninety-five days, and that is one hundred and eighty-five weeks to the day.**

---

**The sheet with four columns on it was on its table at about seven, and the fifth heading stood over nothing on all nine of its rows, and there is writing on the plaster of that room above the rail which nobody asked for and which nobody has taken down.**

About four people in that room had read that writing and about five had not.

**And nobody in that room had said to anybody else in that room that they had read it, on that Wednesday or on any day of the four years before it.**

---

**A man of about thirty-three asked the woman of about forty-three, at about half past seven on that Wednesday, what that sheet on that table was for, and he asked it into her face and he asked it once.**

She looked at him for a moment and then she said that she was going to have to find out, and he waited, and about nine people in that room waited with him.

A woman of about sixty was sitting at that table. She wrote the tick-box in the fourth column of that sheet, about four years ago, after a week, and she chose the word she put in it because it was the word on another form.

She had been listening for about nine minutes.

**She said: that sheet is for people who cannot answer.**

**She said it into the face of the man of about thirty-three and she said it once and she did not say it again and she did not explain it.**

Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds afterwards.

---

**And about four of the people in that room had read that sentence on that wall on the Monday before and about two of them had read it more than once, and not one of them said a word.**

The man of about thirty-three has said since that he had read it and that he did not know it was hers until she said it, and that the wall and her mouth had put it in the same words and that he has not been able to get the two apart since.

**Nobody in that room has said since whether she had put it on that wall.**

A woman of about forty-three, who had read it on that wall before anybody said anything about it, has said since that she was watching the man of about thirty-three and that she was watching the woman of about sixty at the same time, and that she was watching them for the whole of it and that she did not take her eyes off either of them.

She has said since that if she had said something in those nine seconds the room would have been a different room afterwards, and that she has not been able to work out whether she wanted it to be.

---

**Nobody in that room on that Wednesday asked the woman of about sixty what she meant by that, and nobody in this city has asked her since, and she has said nothing further about it to anybody.**

She has said, to one person, not in a room, that she wrote it about four years ago in her own kitchen and put it on the wall because there was nowhere else in this city to put it.

**Nobody in this city has said who took it off anything and put it on that wall, and nobody in this city has said when.**

---

**A man of twenty-two was in that room on that Wednesday, was asked nothing and signed nothing, and he had read the writing on that wall on the Monday before.**

He has said since that on the Monday he had looked at that writing and thought it was somebody's list of shopping, and that he had thought so because of the way the lines were broken up, and that he had not read it a second time after he read what the woman of about sixty said out loud on that Wednesday.

He has said since that he does not know why that is true and that he has thought about it since and has not got any further than the Tuesday.

---

**The rest of that Wednesday went into about two hours of the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A heater on a wall in a workshop had been put on the lighting circuit of that workshop, so that it came on with the lights and went off at the switch by the door that everybody used for the lights.

He gave it a way of its own and proved the workshop with the door switch off and on.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A heater on the lights is a heater nobody can leave running. About four of those wall heaters in that workshop are on a lighting way, and one of them has been a workshop that was cold every morning for about four years.**"

An outside socket in a yard had its earth taken back through the conduit gland and no further, so that the earth path went through a thread and an oiled washer.

He took the earth onto the enclosure, made it off at the gland, and proved the socket from the machine to the yard gate.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**An outside socket earthed at its own gland is an outside socket earthed through a washer. About four of those sockets in that yard are earthed that way, and one of them has been a machine that would not start in the wet for about four years.**"

A hand dryer in a washroom had been put in behind a light on a fitting that was rated for one of them and not the other.

He took it off that fitting, gave it one of its own rating, and proved it with the lights on the same board.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**Two different machines behind one fitting is whichever of them happens to be switched first. About four of those dryers in that washroom are behind a light, and one of them has been a washroom complaint nobody could reproduce for about four years.**"

A handrail in a stair had been earthed to the conduit that ran beside it rather than to the stair itself, which meant the rail's earth was as good as the clip that held the conduit.

He earthed it to the stair, took it off the conduit, and proved it against the steelwork.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A handrail earthed to the conduit beside it is a handrail earthed to a clip. About four of those rails in that stair are done that way, and one of them has been a rail a man was afraid of in the wet for about four years.**"

**Forty-two pounds is what those four came to on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**That fourth room's door was shut by about half past six on that Wednesday and a woman of about thirty was behind it; the binder on the back shelf did not come out, and no figure for it is printed here.**
