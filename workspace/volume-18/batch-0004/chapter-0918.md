# Chapter 918 — A Thursday Of The Tenth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six Before It, Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In That First Floor Above The Line Where Nine Chairs Were Set Out In A Row In Front Of The Table Before Anybody Came Up, About Nine People In That Room And One Of Those Nine Chairs Not Used, Nobody In That Room Saying Which One And Nobody In That Room Knowing Which One, And Forty-Two Pounds For Four Jobs

**By the Thursday of that week there had been a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about two hours of that Thursday evening went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were. That Thursday carried no sitting, and nothing was counted aloud in any room in this city.**

---

Eleven callers came into that shop on that Thursday and the last of them at about ten to nine, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The creased card standing in the two-place rail by that first door had stood in that rail one thousand seven hundred and one days by that Thursday, and that is two hundred and forty-three weeks to the day. The twelfth of the nineteen ruled lines had been ruled one thousand six hundred and seventeen days before it, and that is two hundred and thirty-one weeks to the day, and its thirteenth one thousand five hundred and sixty-eight days, and that is two hundred and twenty-four weeks to the day.**

**Its fourteenth line had been ruled one thousand five hundred and thirty-three days before that, and that is two hundred and nineteen weeks to the day, and the fifteenth one thousand five hundred and twelve days, and that is two hundred and sixteen weeks to the day. And the nineteenth, which is the last of the nineteen, had been ruled one thousand three hundred and ninety-three days before it, and that is one hundred and ninety-nine weeks to the day, and the hold over nine crates and the ground under them had been sitting there one thousand three hundred and thirty days, and that is one hundred and ninety weeks to the day.**

---

**Nine chairs were set out in a row in front of that table before anybody went up that stair on that Thursday, and about two people carried them up out of a cupboard at the bottom of it, and nobody in that room has said who sent for them.**

They are wooden, and they are not the folding chairs from the room in a second district, and they are all the same, and they were set out with the gaps the same width and their backs to the room and their seats to the table.

**Nobody in that room had been told which of those nine chairs was theirs, and nobody in that room said afterwards that they had been.**

---

**About nine people were in that room from about half past six on that Thursday, and about eight of them sat down in one of those nine chairs, and one of them did not.**

He was a man of twenty-two and he stood at the back by the rail with the card in it for the whole of that Thursday, the way he stands, and nobody in that room said one word to him about the row of chairs in front of him and nobody asked him why he was standing.

People shifted along that row during the evening. Nobody had been told which chair was which, and every chair in that row was the same as every other chair in it.

**At about ten on that Thursday there was one chair standing empty in that row, and nobody in that room could have said which of the nine it was, and nobody in that room said.**

About four people in that room have said since that they counted the chairs before they counted the people, and each of the four has said that it was the other order of counting that would have told them something.

---

**And the space on that form had something in it on that Thursday, and nobody in that room saw it put there and nobody in that room has said when it came to be full.**

Nobody in that room has said who put anything in it. Nobody in this city has said either.

The sheet with four columns was on its own table at about seven with the fifth heading over nothing on all nine rows, and it is on that table now in the same state, and nobody in that room has said one word about either of those two objects being in the same room.

---

**He stood at the back of that room by the rail with the card in it for the whole of that Thursday evening, a man of twenty-two, asked nothing and signing nothing.**

He has said since that there were nine chairs in front of that table and that he could have sat in any one of them, and that that is why he did not sit in any of them.

He has said since that this is the first reason he has given for any of it and that he has not been able to give it to anybody else.

---

**What was left of that Thursday went into about two hours of the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A distribution board in a block of flats had a two-way switch on a stair light that was wired so that both ways of it drew on the lamp from one terminal, and the lamp could be put off from either floor and neither floor could tell which had done it.

He rewired the two-way so that each floor had a lamp of its own on it and proved it from both floors at once.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A two-way switch drawing on one terminal is a switch that fights itself. About four of those switches in that block are wired that way, and one of them has been two neighbours blaming each other for about four years.**"

A lift machine room had its earth taken off the machine frame onto a piece of scaffold tube that had been left across the floor for another job, so that the earth depended on a tube that somebody could kick.

He earthed it to the structure and proved the machine against that structure with it moving.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A machine earthed to a length of tube is a machine earthed to whatever is holding the tube. About four of those machine rooms in that block are earthed that way, and one of them has been a lift that trips in one direction only for about four years.**"

A light in a stair had been put in on a way that carried the whole of that landing's sockets, so that it went out whenever anything was plugged in anywhere along it.

He gave the light on that landing a way of its own and proved it with a heater running there.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A light on the socket way is a light that goes out when the kettle goes on. About four of those lights on that landing are on it, and one of them has been a stair that has been argued about for about four years.**"

An electric gate on a workshop had its stop switch put in the earth and not in the control, so that it stopped by cutting the machine's own supply back to itself.

He moved it into the control circuit and proved the gate stopping on the stop.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A stop switch in the earth is a stop that stops by accident. About four of those gates in that yard have their stop on the earth, and one of them has been a gate nobody would leave running for about four years.**"

**Forty-two pounds is what those four came to on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**Nobody went near the fourth of those four rooms on that Thursday; its door stood shut from about half past six with a woman of about thirty behind it, the binder on the back shelf stayed where it was, and nothing is printed here for anything on it.**
