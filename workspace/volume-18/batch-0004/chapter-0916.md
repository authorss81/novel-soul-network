# Chapter 916 — A Tuesday Of The Tenth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, And Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In That First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket Where About Nine People Were And Where The Same Question Was Asked A Second Time In The Same Words By A Different Person And Answered Once And Nobody Saying Anything Back, And Forty Pounds For Four Jobs

**The second Tuesday of that week was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about two hours of that Tuesday evening went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were. There was no sitting on that Tuesday, and nothing in this city was said aloud as a figure.**

---

Ten callers came into that shop on that Tuesday and the tenth of them at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**By that Tuesday those four rooms off that service road had stood one thousand six hundred and ninety-five days, and that is two hundred and forty-two weeks and one day. That week the sixteenth of the nineteen ruled lines had been ruled one thousand four hundred and eighty-five days before it, and that is two hundred and twelve weeks and one day, and the ask had been standing in front of the man of about fifty-one for one thousand three hundred and eighty-five days, and that is one hundred and ninety-seven weeks and six days.**

---

**The form was on that table again on that Tuesday with a space on it for a date, and this page does not say what was in that space and does not say when it came to be in it.**

The sheet with four columns was on its own table with the fifth heading over nothing on all nine rows.

About nine people were in that room. The woman of about forty-three was at that table. The woman of about sixty-two was not in that room on that Tuesday and nobody in that room has said since whether she was anywhere else in the city.

---

**And a man of about thirty-three asked the woman of about forty-three, at about half past seven on that Tuesday, the question she had put to nine people on the Thursday before, in the same words he had heard her put it, and he asked it once and into her face.**

She did not say she was going to have to find out this time.

She answered him.

She said: a date is never asked of anybody, it is only ever written down.

**She said that once and did not say it again and she said nothing else, and nobody in that room said anything back to her about any part of it.**

---

**And that is a thing asked for twice in that room inside four days and answered once, and nobody in that room put the two halves of it side by side.**

The man of about thirty-three has said since that he asked it because he had wanted to hear whether she would say the same thing twice, and that he had not known until after she had answered that he had wanted to hear it.

He has said since that she said the true thing and that he has not been able to work out what a true thing does in a room where nobody can do anything with it, and that this is the fourth time in about four years that he has come up with nothing.

---

**Nobody in that room asked her on that Tuesday whether the four people who said it could not be put in on the Thursday before had understood what she was asking, and nobody in that room has asked her since.**

About four people in that room have said since that they do not know what to do with what she said, and each of the four has given a different reason for not knowing, and not one of the four reasons is that she was wrong.

A woman of about thirty-eight has said since that she had gone home on that Tuesday and written the sentence down, and that she had looked at it and had not been able to see what it was for.

**Nobody in this city has said since whether the sentence she gave on that Tuesday had anything to do with the space on that form, and nobody in that room asked her whether it had.**

---

**He was in that room that Tuesday, a man of twenty-two, asked nothing and signing nothing, and he heard a question asked twice in four days and heard it answered once.**

He has said since that he noticed the man of about thirty-three say the words in the same order the woman of about forty-three had said them on the Thursday, and that he has been trying to work out since how a man can do that from memory in four days without it being a thing he meant to do.

---

**What was left of that Tuesday went into about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A freezer in a workshop had its supply taken off a way out of a board that also carried a freezer in another room, so that one compressor starting pulled the other one down with it.

He gave each freezer a way of its own and proved both with the other one running.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**Two freezers on one way out is one freezer deciding when the other starts. About four of those freezers in that workshop are paired, and one of them has been a cold room nobody could account for in about four years.**"

A distribution board in a store had two circuits arriving on the same way out through a terminal that had been rewired twice, so that the second rewire had taken the first one's earth with it.

He took both circuits to separate ways and re-terminated the earth, and proved both of them.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**Two circuits arriving on one way is one circuit wearing two names. About four of those boards in that store have a terminal that has been at it more than once, and one of them has been a trip nobody could put to anything for about four years.**"

A light in a car park had its photocell on the same way out as the sign above it, so that the sign came on when the lights thought it was dark and the lights came on when the sign was on.

He gave the photocell its own way and proved it against a covered cell.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A photocell sharing a way with a sign is a car park that lights up for its own sign. About four of those photocells in that car park are shared, and one of them has been a lamp that came on at about four in the afternoon for about four years.**"

A handrail in a stair had been painted over two or three times over about a decade and the paint had built up into a layer that came off in sheets when it was touched.

He took that layer back off, proved the rail against the stair, and left it bare.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**Four coats on a handrail is a handrail that comes off in your hand. About four of those rails in that stair have been painted over again and again, and one of them has been a person who did not trust it for about four years.**"

**Forty pounds is what those four came to on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**From about half past six on that Tuesday the fourth of those four rooms had a shut door with a woman of about thirty behind it and a binder on the shelf at the back that stayed on that shelf, and no figure for it is printed on this page.**
