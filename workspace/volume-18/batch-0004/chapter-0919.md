# Chapter 919 — A Friday Of The Tenth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six Before It, Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In That First Floor Above The Line Where The Sentence A Woman Of About Sixty Said Into A Face On The Wednesday Of That Week Was Found To Be On The Plaster Above The Rail, And Nobody In That Room Wrote It There And Nobody In That Room Has Said Who Did, And Forty-Four Pounds For Four Jobs

**That Friday the writing was already on the wall when the day began, and there had been a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about two hours of that Friday evening went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were. No sitting was held on that Friday, and no figure was said out loud in any room in this city.**

---

Eight callers came into that shop on that Friday and the last of them at about twenty to five, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The seventeenth of those nineteen ruled lines had been ruled one thousand four hundred and seventy days before that Friday, and that is two hundred and ten weeks to the day. The post at the far end of that corridor had been standing there one thousand two hundred and forty-six days, and that is one hundred and seventy-eight weeks to the day, and one line inside one box off that road had been filled in about sixteen months back, which is one thousand and seventy-eight days, and that is one hundred and fifty-four weeks to the day.**

---

**The sentence is on the plaster of that room above the rail, and it is the same sentence the woman of about sixty said into the face of the man of about thirty-three on the Wednesday of that week, and it has been on that wall since before that Monday.**

It is in pencil. It has gone most of the way to the colour of the plaster behind it. It stands in lines that each end well before the one beneath it ends, so that the ends of it do not sit anywhere near the ends of each other.

**Nobody in that room wrote it there. Nobody in this city has said who did, and nobody has said when, and nobody has taken it down on any of the ten days of this movement.**

---

**Four people in that room have said since that nobody had asked them to put it on that wall and that they had not known it was going there.**

One of those four has said since that she has written something on that wall herself, in about four years, on a day when that room was empty, and that she has never told anybody in this city that she did it and has not been asked about it.

She has said since that she has stood in front of those words and looked at them and has not been able to say whether they are hers, and that she does not want to be asked again about it.

**Nobody in this city has asked her, and nobody has asked the woman of about sixty whether the words on that wall are the words she wrote in her own kitchen.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-three found that evening that the words on that wall are not in the order the woman of about sixty said them in, and nobody in that room had noticed that before he said so.**

He stood under it at about half past seven on that Friday and read it twice and then read it a third time from further back, and then he said one thing into the middle of that room.

He said: she said the last part first and it is written here in the other order.

**Nobody in that room took him up and nobody in that room put him down, and about four people in that room have since gone and read it themselves.**

A woman of about thirty-eight has said since that she has read it four or five times and that she would not have noticed the order of it in her life, and that she does not now know what the order of it is for.

**Nobody in that room has said since whether the order it is written in makes it better or worse, and this page does not say either.**

---

**A woman of about sixty was not in that room on that Friday and nobody has said where she was, and nobody in this city has asked her about that wall at all.**

The woman of about sixty-two has not been in that room since the Friday before, and nobody in that room has said since whether she has been told where the writing on that wall came from.

---

**There was a man of twenty-two in that room on that Friday, asked nothing and signing nothing, and he stood at the back by the rail because there were nine chairs in that room again and he did not sit in one of them.**

He has said since that he has read it every day since the Monday and that after the first four days he stopped reading it and started looking at it, and that he has not been able to say what the difference is between those two.

---

**What was left of the evening of that Friday went into about two hours in the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A cold room in a workshop had its controller earthed through the flexible conduit that carried its own supply, so that the control was earthed only as far as the flex reached and no further.

He took the earth at the controller end onto the metal case, made it off at the gland, and proved the room at its coldest setting.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A controller earthed through its own flex is a controller earthed to the length of the flex. About four of those controllers in that workshop are earthed that way, and one of them has been a cold room nobody could account for in about four years.**"

A lift in a block of flats had its interlock supply taken off the same way out as the car light, so that a lamp change in the car could drop the interlock out for as long as the lamp took.

He gave the interlock its own way out of the board and proved it by operating the door circuit on its own.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**An interlock sharing a way with a lamp is an interlock held up by a light bulb. About four of those interlocks in that block are on a lighting way, and one of them has been a door that has behaved in ways nobody could reproduce for about four years.**"

A socket in a corridor had been put in behind a radiator that was fixed to the wall on two brackets, so that its earth went back through the brackets and the pipework behind them.

He earthed it to the wall, took it off the brackets, and proved it with a heater on that radiator.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A socket earthed to a radiator bracket is a socket earthed to two brackets and a pipe. About four of those sockets in that corridor are behind radiators, and one of them has been a heater that tripped a whole landing for about four years.**"

A light in a store had no earth of its own at the fitting and was relying on the metal trunking it was screwed to, which was itself broken at one joint about two metres along.

He earthed it to the building, took it off the trunking, and proved it with the store loaded.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A light earthed to its trunking is a light earthed to a joint somebody could open. About four of those fittings in that store are on trunking, and one of them has been a light that went out for no reason anybody could find in about four years.**"

**Forty-four pounds is what those four came to on that Friday, exact.**

---

**By about half past six on that Friday the fourth of those four rooms was shut with a woman of about thirty behind its door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come out, and this page prints no figure for it.**
