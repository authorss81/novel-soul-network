# Chapter 911 — A Monday Of The Ninth Week, A Bench Until Two And A Counter Until About Six, Then About Two Hours Of That Evening In A First Floor Above A Line In Saltmarket With About Nine People In It, Something Written On The Plaster Of That Room Which Nobody Asked For And Nobody Has Taken Down, Four Of Those About Nine Who Have Read It And Five Who Have Not, And Forty-One Pounds For Four Jobs

**The first Monday of that week was a bench until two and a counter until about six, and then about two hours of that Monday evening went into that first floor above a line in Saltmarket, where about nine people were. It was not a sitting and no figure was said out loud in any room in this city.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop between about seven and about half past nine on that Monday, and the shutter came down at about ten.

**The four converted units off that service road had stood one thousand six hundred and eighty-seven days by that Monday, which is two hundred and forty-one weeks to the day. The sixteenth of the nineteen ruled lines on that board had been ruled one thousand four hundred and seventy-seven days before it, which is two hundred and eleven weeks to the day, and nine hands' copies of the front of one page had been taken one thousand two hundred and fifty-three days before that, which is one hundred and seventy-nine weeks to the day.**

---

**The sheet with four headings across the top of it and a fifth heading standing under the fourth went onto that table at about seven the way it goes on it every week, and the fifth heading stood over nothing on all nine of its rows.**

About nine people were in that room. About four of them had stood in it before and about five had not, and the woman of about forty-three was one of the four.

She has said since that she did not know that half of that room had never been above the height of its own shoulders in it, and that she has been not noticing that sort of thing here for about four years.

---

**And there is something written on the plaster of that room, above the rail, and it is not the printed sheet that somebody pinned to a wall of it by two drawing pins, and nobody in this city has said who wrote it or when.**

It is in pencil, and the pencil has been on that wall long enough to have gone most of the way to the colour of the plaster behind it, and it stands in lines that each come to an end a long way before the line beneath it comes to its end, so that nothing in it lines up with anything under it.

**About four people in that room have read it and about five have not, and nobody in that room has asked either group about it.**

About four of the five who have not read it have said since, separately, that they had come up that stair about nine times and had never once looked up at that wall, and that when they did look up on that Monday they had not wanted to read it in front of anybody else in the room.

One of the four who has read it has said since that he has read it four or five times and has not been able to say, when anybody asks him, who else in that room has read it.

---

**A man of about thirty-three came in at about half past seven and stood under that writing and read it from where he was standing, and nobody in that room told him he could not.**

He has said since that he did not know the writing was there before that Monday and that he has been in that room about nine times in four years and had never once looked at that wall.

He has said since that he has gone back over the four or five people who had read it and has not been able to work out which of them is the one who read it first, and that he does not think it matters and that he has thought about it on the bus anyway.

---

**Behind the chair the woman of about forty-three sits in there is a space in the floor and on the wall that has had nothing standing against it since a week in the spring, and nobody has asked her about it and nobody in this city has said a word about it, and this page names it once and puts no figure on it.**

**That ninth chair had its back to that room and its seat against that wall and it did not shift at any point on that Monday, and no page of this movement names whoever would move it.**

---

**He was in that room on that Monday, a man of twenty-two, asked nothing and signing nothing, and he read the writing on that wall twice and said nothing about it to anybody.**

He has said since that he did not know that anything had ever been written on the walls of that room by anybody, and that he had stood in that room about nine times and had looked at the table every one of the nine times.

---

**What was left of that Monday went into about two hours of the same yard and the same building, and four jobs went into it.**

A cooker in a kitchen had been put on the same way out of a board as the lights of that kitchen, so that anything the cooker did was reported as something the lights had done.

He gave the cooker a way of its own and proved the kitchen with a pan of water on it.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A cooker and a kitchen's lights on one way out is a kitchen that reports itself wrongly. About four of those cookers in that block are on a lighting board, and one of them has been a fault nobody could place in about four years.**"

A light in an outside stair had no earth of its own at the fitting and was holding what it had through a length of conduit clipped twice to the wall.

He took the earth off the conduit, put it on the metal box, and proved the stair with everything on it.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A stair light with no earth of its own is a stair light held by two clips. About four of those fittings in that stair are earthed that way, and one of them has been a light out in that stair for about four years.**"

A bell sounder in a corridor had a transformer on a timber partition with its earth taken back through the clip that held the conduit to the timber.

He earthed the transformer to the building, took it off the clip, and proved the bell from the push.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A transformer earthed to the clip that holds it is a transformer earthed to a piece of timber. About four of those bells in that corridor are earthed through their own clips, and one of them has been a bell that could be heard one floor up for about four years.**"

A floodlight on a gantry outside a yard was bonded to its own mounting bracket and nowhere else, so that it was holding itself up and the earth was going back up the same steel.

He earthed it to the gantry, took it off the bracket, and proved it against the gantry itself.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A floodlight earthed to its own bracket is a floodlight on its own bracket. About four of those fittings on that gantry are bonded to the bracket, and one of them has been a yard light out for about four years.**"

**Forty-one pounds is what those four came to on that Monday, exact.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms had its door shut by about half past six on that Monday with a woman of about thirty behind it, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come out, and this page prints no figure for it.**

*914.
Monday of week 309, at ten. That is the two hundred and sixteenth day of this stretch of days. Nine callers came into that shop on that Monday, the last of them at about half past nine.
**No sitting was held on that Monday and no figure was said out loud in any room in this city. About nine people were in that first floor above the line from about half past six, about four of them for the second time or more and about five for the first. There is writing on the plaster of that room above the rail which nobody asked for and nobody has taken down, about four of the nine have read it and about five have not, and nobody in that room has asked the other five about it. A man of about thirty-three read it standing up. That Monday was worth forty-one pounds, exact.**

*Conditions of the close.* **From about half past six until the shutter came down at about ten, five things about that first floor were as they had been in the morning, and none of them was changed by anybody in it: the book in the green binding stood at sixty-nine lines, the tin standing beside it stood at seventy-three with its lid down, and one of those two figures was not got out of the other one.
That ninth chair kept its back to that room and its seat against that wall for the whole of that Monday and no page of this movement names whoever shifts it.
That fourth of those four rooms had a shut door, a woman of about thirty behind it and a binder standing on the shelf at the back, and nothing came off that shelf and this page puts no figure on it.
The room under that building in a first district had nothing burning in it at about eleven on that Monday and has nothing burning in it at this hour.
That register of correct acts that changed nothing stood at four when that shop opened and stands at four at the shutter, and nobody in this city keeps a figure of it, and none of these ten days added anything to it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named in the lines under this heading and no sentence beneath it brings any two of them together. A book in a green binding, shut that evening. A card creased once, holding a doorway open. Nine hand copies of the front of one page, a corner gone off each. A tin with its lid down. A strip of paper cut narrow with one word written across it. A board hung on two nails with nineteen ruled lines ruled across it. The shutter, down at about ten. A rail with two places cut into it. A ring binder standing on a shelf at the back of a shut room. A sheet with four headings across the top of it and a fifth heading under the fourth, empty on all nine rows.
Three things stand outside all ten of those: the form that came back on a bus, the cage down one side of that counter, and the printed sheet somebody put up by two drawing pins. The four columns and that empty fifth heading belong inside the ten and are not one more than they are, whatever anybody in this city decides to write on a wall of a room it has never been asked to write on.
Behind a shut door in the fourth of those four rooms is a woman of about thirty; she is not counted on this page, not described on it, and no figure for the page in the binder on the shelf behind her is printed here.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this question, and the records behind it are public and disputed and open to anybody who comes for them.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, about four of those about nine had read what was on that wall and about five had not, and nobody in that room has said one word about who put it there.**
