# Chapter 463 — A Price For A Crate And A Card That Is Not A Crate

**The Thursday of week one hundred and seventy-two was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that a man of about fifty-two who drives the line twice a week put a price on the end of a counter in his own words, and the price was a price for a crate and not a price for a card, and he said out loud, once, that if the card went at the crate price then the crate would wait a week and about nine people were waiting on what was in it, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and thirty-one days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and thirty-five days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and the price that was said out loud on that Thursday was said a long way from any card and is not in the rail and is not on anything in this city.

---

The dispute is about a card and about a crate and it has been going for about four days, and the whole of it can be told in about nine sentences by either side and neither side will tell it that way.

A room in one of the four towns has an answer on a piece of card and wants the card carried. The card came off a bus on the Monday, went into a drawer, and came out again on the Thursday, and a person wrote an address on it, and the address is in this city.

**The price is on a card in the cab of a bus that goes twice a week, and it is a price by weight, and it was set for a crate of about nine kilos and not for a piece of card the size of a nail box.**

The card would weigh nothing. The card would go in a coat pocket. The card would take up about the same room as a folded newspaper and about half a second to put down.

**And the price is not about the card. The price is about the space and the weight that the card would take up instead of something else, and the something else is the crate, and the crate is going out on the Tuesday whether or not the card goes with it.**

The man of about fifty-two who drives the line knows this and has never had to explain it before, because nothing has ever come off one of those buses that was lighter than a crate and wanted to go in the other direction.


The telephone call was about four minutes long and it was made from a call box because the room has no line, and the woman who made it read the eleven words off a piece of paper and then read the number on the price card out of a book, and neither of those two things is on anything.

**She did not know what a crate price was. She knew the figure because somebody in that town had told her the figure, and nobody in that town knows who told her, and the figure had been a figure in a book in a cab in this city since long before anybody made that call.**

The driver said the price twice. He said it the way a man says a thing he has said before to a person who has not asked it, and he did not explain it and nobody on that call asked him to.

The room waited about nine days for an answer and then did not wait any more, and the card went back into a drawer for four days and came out again on the Thursday with an address on it in a hand that is not the hand that wrote the eleven.

**Nobody in that room has ever been told that the card went at all, and nobody in this city knows that a room in one of the four towns telephoned a bus, and neither of those facts has ever been in the same sentence as the nine words.**
---

He came in on the Thursday at about half past three with the price card still in his hand and put it face down on the end of the counter, and then turned it over, and then did not push it towards anybody.

He said the room had telephoned. He said that was the first time anybody had telephoned him from one of the four places about anything other than a stop being missed, and that a woman had asked whether one card could go back for nothing.

**He said the answer was no, and that the answer was not about the card, and that he had said the word card four times in about nine seconds trying to make the price make sense to somebody who was not going to be told the price makes sense.**

Nobody at that counter asked him what the price was. Nobody at that counter asked him who had telephoned. Nobody at that counter has ever telephoned a bus.

---

What he said about the crate, once, at the end of the counter, to the counter and not to the man, was this.

**"If that card goes on the Tuesday at the crate price, then the crate does not go on the Tuesday, and the crate has been ready since the Friday, and there are about nine people waiting on what is in it, and one of them is waiting for a part."**

Nobody said that a card was more important than a part. Nobody said that a part was more important than a card. Nobody said that a bus is not the place to carry either.

**Nobody said the price was fair and nobody said the price was not fair, and about four people at that counter over the day have formed an opinion about it, and none of the four has said it out loud and none of the four is going to.**


He counted his stops on the back of his hand at about half past four while he was talking and nobody asked him what he was counting and he did not say, and it was nine, and it is nine on a Tuesday and it is not nine on a Thursday because he does not run a Thursday.

**He runs the line out on the Tuesday and back the same day if the weather is on side. Everybody in the four places knows he does not run it on the Thursday, and about four of them have asked him about it, and he has given the same one-line answer four times.**
He did not say it was a bad price. He did not say it was the only price. He said it was the price on the card and the card is in the cab and the card goes out on the Tuesday and comes back on the Tuesday and nobody has ever written down anywhere what happens to a price when it is wrong.

**The nine people waiting on the crate is a number he gave from memory in a cab on a Tuesday morning, and nobody has counted them, and this case cannot count them, and the figure is not a rate and is not on a form and is not about the four rooms in four towns at all.**

---

The room that telephoned is the room that wrote the first answer. It is four feet of shop with a door that sticks and about eleven people in it on a Wednesday evening, and it has a kettle, and it has never been asked anything by anybody with a job.

**It telephoned because a card had to go back and it could not walk it and it could not put it in a postbox and it had never in its life put anything on a bus.**

Nobody told it that the price existed. Nobody told it that a card weighed nothing. Nobody told it that the crate goes on the Tuesday whatever else is on the bus, and about four people in that room have worked out that the crate is the reason, and one of the four has asked the woman who keeps the only written record in that town whether the crate is coming back, and she has said she does not know.

---

He put the price card back in his coat and went out to look at a wheel and came in again and did not finish the first conversation.

He said that the price was not his. He said it twice, which is once more than the same true sentence usually needs, and he said it to the counter and not to the man, and he did not say whose it was, because there is nobody in this case who can be named as the person it belongs to.

**He is owed nothing. He is not owed a price. Nobody in this city has agreed what he is owed and nobody has asked him and he has not asked.**

The shutter came down at about ten and he was still in the room off that service road when it did, and about four people came in for the temperature and none of them was asked anything, and the cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing, and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Thursday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-eight weeks and one day. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-two weeks without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**Nobody in that room knew that a price had been said out loud at a counter in another district, and nobody in that room is owed anything by anybody in it, and the man of about thirty-three's hold is going into its fifty-third week without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

About four of the nine who came in said nothing and stood by the door and then left, and nobody asked them why.

**Nobody in that room was told that a price had been said out loud, and nobody in that room was told that a room four days of bus travel away had telephoned a bus, and neither of those two facts has ever been in the same sentence in front of anybody.**

The room is four rooms and a back one and the middle one is the one that fills, and about four people came in on the Thursday evening and about four of them were not the same four as the Wednesday before, and nobody keeps a list of which.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and there is a fortnight on nobody's account that has her handwriting on the envelope and nothing else, and it is not the fortnight of a heating in any year and it is not on the same sheet as anything said in this case.

**The nine people waiting on that crate are not the four hundred of them and are not the about nine of a Wednesday evening and are not the about nine on that board, and about four people in this city use the word nine for four different things without ever putting two of them in one sentence.**
