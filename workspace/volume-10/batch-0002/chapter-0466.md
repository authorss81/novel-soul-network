# Chapter 466 — Who Is Asking, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Eight Who Says About Nine

**The Wednesday of week one hundred and seventy-three was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that the third of the four rooms asked who was asking and did not answer at all, and the woman who carries the question could not say, because the room that gave it to her would not say either, and on the same afternoon a woman of about thirty-eight who delivers to a building on that service road was asked who is in the room in this city and said about nine, and she was not thanked.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and thirty-seven days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and forty-one days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and the third of the four rooms in the four towns gave no number at all and gave a question instead, and a question is not on the rail and is not on the card and is not on anything in this city.

---

The third room is a room at the end of a corridor in a school and it meets on a Wednesday evening and there are about four people in it and about nine come in a term.

It was asked the same eleven words by a person with an ordinary job who had been told the question by a room and had not been told by whom.

**It said, in about nine seconds, in about nine words: who is asking. Nobody has said who is asking.**

That is the whole of the third of the four answers. It is not a number and it is not a refusal and it is not a joke, and the person who put the question wrote it down and carried it back, and the nine words are the only one of the four sets of nine words that is made of a question.

The corridor at the end of that school is about nine doors long and about four of the nine are classrooms on a Wednesday evening and the room at the end is the one with the outside door, and about four people are in it and about nine come in a term.

**The person who put the question there stands in that corridor and says the eleven words to whoever opens the door, and has done it once, and did not go back, and about four people in that town know it was asked and none of them knows what the answer was.**
---

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record in one of the four towns was asked, in a kitchen, on the Wednesday, by the person who had carried the question to the room at the end of the school corridor.

She said: I was given the words.

She was asked who gave them the words. She said: a room, and not a person, and the room would not say, and I did not push it because pushing it is how a question turns into an argument, and I have got about nine things to do before the bus.

**That is the first time in this case that anybody has walked the question backwards, and it ran out after two rooms, and nobody anywhere has been found, and nobody in this case is going to be found, and the four rooms are still four rooms and not a chain and not a group.**

Nobody said she should try harder. Nobody said she had let it go. Nobody said the room that gave her the words had any obligation to her, and the room has none, and it never signed anything, and there is no list.

**The three of the four answers that exist are on three objects in three towns and none of them has a sender on it, and the fourth has not been given yet, and nobody is keeping a count of the four and there is no column anywhere for the count.**

---

The woman of about thirty-eight delivers bread on a round that takes in a converted shop unit off a service road in this city, and she has been on that round for about four years, and she has been in the doorway of that building about nine times a week for about four years and has never been past the second door.

She came into the shop on the Wednesday at about half past four because she had a box back and the box back is a thing that happens on a Wednesday.

A man of about forty-seven who drives a van for a firm that is not named was in the shop and had been there about ten minutes, and he asked her a question that was not about the bread.

**He asked her who is in the room in this city. He said it the way a man asks a thing he has been carrying about for a week, which is quickly and without any of the words around it.**

She said about nine.

**She said it without hesitating and without counting and without looking at him, and she said it in about nine seconds, and she said it in the voice of a person giving a thing she gives about nine times a week and has never had to think about.**

Nobody said nine what. Nobody said the room. Nobody said that a room off a service road in this city has about nine people in it on a Monday evening, and the woman who said it was not asked to explain where she had heard it and did not explain.

**He did not thank her. She did not wait to be thanked. Neither of them said the word, and about four people at that counter over the day did not know the exchange had happened, and the parts cage man worked with his back to the room the whole of it.**

She has been on that round for about four years and she takes in about nine doors on that service road and one of the nine is a converted shop unit with a door that does not close in the wet, and she has knocked on that door about nine times a week for four years and has never been past the second one.

**She has never wondered what the room is for. She has wondered who is in it twice, on a Tuesday and on a Wednesday, and the second time was this week, and she has not mentioned either time to anybody on that round and will not.**

The man of about forty-seven wrote nothing down. He had a notebook and it was shut, and about four people at that counter have seen that notebook shut about four times a week for about two years and none of them has ever seen a page of it.
Nobody improved on it. Nobody said the figure was a good one. Nobody said it was low and nobody said it was high, and about four people at that counter have wondered since what the nine was a count of and none of them has ever asked her.

**She was right and she was not thanked and she has not been asked since and she goes into that doorway about nine times a week and has never been past the second door and is never going to be, and none of those four things has ever been in the same sentence in front of her.**

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and about four went out again.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-nine weeks. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-two weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**Nobody in that room knew that two things had happened about its size on the same afternoon, and about four people in it have said since that it is a big room, and it is not a big room and nobody in it has ever counted anybody in it.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

The corridor at the end of that school has about nine doors and about four of them are shut on a Wednesday evening and the one at the end is open, and the room at the end has about four chairs and a table with a leg that has been propped with a folded newspaper for about two years.

**Nobody has ever replaced the folded newspaper and nobody has ever asked why it is there, and about four people in that town have said since that the room is the only warm one at the end of that corridor, which is not the reason anybody is in it.**

About four of the nine who came in said nothing and stood by the door and then left, and nobody asked them why.

**A question asked at the end of a school corridor in one of the four towns and a number given at a counter in this city are two different things that happened on the same afternoon, and the woman who did the second of them has never heard of the first.**

The middle one is the one that fills and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and about four went out again, and the cook of twenty-seven washed about nine things and made no cup.

The shutter on that service road came down at about ten, and about four people at that counter over the day had seen a man of about forty-seven ask a question and a woman of about thirty-eight answer it and neither of them had said anything else, and the two of them had gone out into the street separately about four minutes apart.

**Nobody thanked her. Nobody thanked him. Nobody at that counter told the other one anything about what the other one had said, and about four people at that counter have said since that the shop is a good place for a thing like that, and nobody has said what the thing was.**
