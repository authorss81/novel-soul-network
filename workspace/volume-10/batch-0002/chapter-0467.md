# Chapter 467 — The Fourth Friday Of Ten, Forty-One Weeks, And A Man Who Asked Nothing

**The Friday of week one hundred and seventy-three was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and he was not in Crown Terrace and the shop did not know and would not have known, and the one thing that is true of it is that about eleven people were in a room on a second floor for about nine minutes and the post by the corridor end has now been bare for forty-one weeks, and the man of about thirty-nine who buys training sat at the back for the fourth Friday running and asked nothing at all, and the two numbers that were given to him three Fridays ago are still on the back of his left hand and have never been next to anything.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and thirty-nine days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and forty-three days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and it is in a building on the other side of this city from a second floor in another building and nothing on that Friday joins the two of them and nobody crossed it.

---

The room is the same room. About eleven chairs out of a cupboard and a table at the front and a laptop on it connected to nothing in this case. The chairs were stacked against the wall when the first person came and were unstacked by the first person who came, and that is the fourth week running that two different people have done the two halves of that, and nobody has ever asked which two people those are.

About eleven people came, which is the same figure as the three Fridays before, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the figure is a figure and not a rate and is not on a form.

The woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard said one word and pointed at the chairs and said nothing else, and about nine minutes went the way they go.

**Four Fridays is not a run. It is four Fridays, and anybody who wants a run out of it has to wait, and there are six more Fridays after this one of which this case has seen none.**

The post by the corridor end is bare forty-one weeks, which is two hundred and eighty-seven days from the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, and nobody in that room knows either of those figures.

**The card is still in it and the number on the card is still the number and the box is at the same height on the same wall, and the second box at the near end of the same corridor is still not empty, and the two are about nine feet apart and nobody in that building has ever been asked whether they are a set.**

The second box is a different colour and it is a different make and it is about nine feet from the first, and it has something in it most Fridays, and nobody in that building has ever put anything in the first one while somebody was standing there.

**About four people on that floor have looked at the first box in passing for years and about four of them have looked at the second one and thought about the first one, and none of the four has ever said the thought out loud to anybody.**
---

The man of about thirty-nine who buys training sat at the back for the fourth Friday running and did not ask anything.

He had asked something different on each of the three before it. On this one he sat with his hands on the table and he did not write on the back of his left hand and he did not ask whether the post was used and he did not ask what the company counts on a Friday and he did not ask about a room in another building.

**Nobody in that room noticed that he had stopped asking, because nobody on that floor has ever kept a note of what he asks, and a man who asks three different things in three weeks and nothing in the fourth is a man who has had a Friday.**

The woman who supplies the training said one word at the front and pointed and did not say how long it would be and nobody asked and the nine minutes went.

**The two figures are on the back of his left hand, low down near the wrist, in biro, in the ordinary place a man writes a thing he is going to lose. Eleven, and then nine, in that order, with a gap between them, and he wrote them three weeks ago and he has not washed them off and he has not written a third one and he has not looked at them once in this room.**

Nobody has ever asked him for either number. Nobody has ever asked him which room either of them is for. Nobody has ever asked him why he wrote them down at all, and about four people on that floor have wondered since what the two figures are, and none of them has said anything to him and none of them is going to.

**The two figures have been in the same breath once and they have never been in the same sentence, and the person holding both of them does not know which of them is which room, and the person who gave them does not know that anybody does not know. Two counts of the same eleven is a fact about a Friday and not about a company, and this case has no instrument for the difference and says so rather than making one up.**

The woman of about thirty-six who gave them is about four feet from him when she gives them and she does not lean in and she does not lower her voice, and about nine people are in the room and about four of them are close enough to have heard.

**Nobody has ever established whether the other eight heard. Nobody has ever asked, and the two figures have never been in front of anybody but the two people who were there, and one of the two wrote them on his hand in the ordinary place a man writes a thing he is going to lose.**
---

A woman of about thirty-one who works nights was at the back and said nothing for the whole nine minutes and had said nothing on the two Fridays before it, and about four people on that floor know which four Fridays those are and have never been told and have not asked.

There was a man of about twenty-three who has now been to four Fridays and has asked one ordinary question in all of them, and that question was asked three Fridays ago, and he has not asked a second one and has not said anything else on any of the four.

**Nobody in that room said thank you, and about four people on that floor have said since that the four Fridays were the quietest they can remember, and nobody in that room knows what the quiet was about, and the woman who called them in does not know either and has not been asked.**

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the man of about fifty-one had his tool bag at his feet.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Friday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-nine weeks and two days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-three weeks and one day without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**He had been at the bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at that counter from about two until about six on the Friday, and the shop did not know that a man had stopped asking questions in another building, and nobody in the room off that service road knew that a post on a second floor had been bare for forty-one weeks.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

The middle one is the one that fills and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in it for about three hours and said nothing to anybody, and nobody asked her a question and nobody thanked her and nobody apologised to her, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread.

**A woman of about thirty-one who works nights and a woman of about thirty in a chair nearest a door in another district are two people and not one, and the two have never met and no sentence in this case has ever put them in one room, and this one is not going to either.**

About four of the nine who came in said nothing and stood by the door and then left, and nobody asked them why.

**Two figures in biro on the back of a left hand and about eleven people in a room on a second floor are two different things that happened in the same week, and the person who holds the two figures does not know what either of them was a count of and has not been asked.**

The middle one is the one that fills and about four people came in on the Friday evening for the temperature and about four went out again inside twenty minutes, and the cook of twenty-seven washed about nine things and made no cup.

Nobody in that room knew that a man had stopped asking questions in a building in another district, and about four people in that room have said since that a quiet week is a week somebody has had, and nobody has asked who.

**The rail at the end of that counter holds two cards and has held two for about eight years, and the man of about fifty-three at that end of it does not know that a post in another building in this city has been bare for forty-one weeks, and has never asked what the cards in that rail are for.**
