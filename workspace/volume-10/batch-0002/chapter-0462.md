# Chapter 462 — The Thirty-Second Sitting, A Count Of Thirty-Seven, And A Book At Fifty-Five

**The Wednesday of week one hundred and seventy-two was the thirty-second sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and the one thing that is true of it is that the book stood at fifty-five lines when the morning began and it stands at fifty-five lines now, the tin did not move, the count came out at thirty-seven of which thirty-two correspond, and the ask is four hundred and twenty days old on that Wednesday, which is sixty weeks to the day, counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since, and in the same week, in four towns, four rooms each answered a question in about nine words and none of the four knows the other three exist.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and thirty days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and thirty-four days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and the room above a line in Saltmarket is in a fourth building and is not this room and is not in this building and nothing on this Wednesday joins the two of them.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and they came down at about eleven, and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. It did not move in about four hours. Nobody in that room looked at it more than once, and its mover is not named and is not going to be named.

There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, open for about four hours and not shut once.

There were about eleven people, and about four of them are not described this morning.

**She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.**

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece, and none of the six is described here, and that is not a change and is not a pattern and is not a rule and is not a judgement and is not in anybody's mouth.

The first was a question. The second was a question. The third was a person who did not ask one and said nothing for four minutes. The fourth was the same as the third. The fifth was nothing anybody has reported. The sixth was nothing anybody has reported.


There is one flight of stairs and it is up, and about four of the about eleven come up it and about seven come on foot from a different door, and nobody has ever worked out why and the two ways of arriving have never been compared in that room.

**The tin has no lock and has never had one, and it has stood on that table for nineteen years in the same place within reach of her right hand, and about four people in that room have wondered why it is not locked and none of them has ever asked.**

The book has four columns and about four of the eleven could name three of them, and nobody has ever been shown the fourth, and nobody at that table has ever said what it is for.

The shutters go up at about ten and down at about eleven and they are not the same shutters that were there nineteen years ago, and the frame is, and about four people in that room have noticed the frame and none of them has said anything about the shutters.

**Six items at four minutes each is about four hours, and the room has run to about four hours for as long as anybody in it has been counting, and nobody in that room has ever suggested stopping early and nobody has ever suggested going on longer.**
**Nobody said a thing to her face that was not a question, and that is the whole of why the book is where it is, and it has been the whole of it at every sitting for nineteen years, and nobody has ever told her that and nobody is going to.**

**The book is at fifty-five lines. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced thirty-seven times. The whole business is nineteen years old. Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and every one of the four is the whole of itself, and none was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the first two of them is not a number and was not given as one, and it has never been a number in nineteen years and it is not going to be one.**

The count went up by one. It goes up by one at every sitting whether the book opens or not, and thirty-two of the thirty-seven correspond to the thirty-two Wednesdays this book holds, and five of them predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody and has never been asked about twice.

**Nobody asked her for the rule about turning one figure into another.** She has not given it in nineteen years and she promised, in front of nine people, to say it for the last time, and she kept that promise again this morning, and nothing was put in the space it left and nothing was offered in its place, and nobody in that room wanted anything offered.

**No number was given into the tin this morning, and none has been given into it on any day since the year began, and the tin stands at seventy-three, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved.**

Nobody thanked her. Nobody stood up. Nothing about any sitting before this one has ever been said in that room.


Nobody said anything to her about the count. The count is announced and it is not discussed, and the number that came out of it this morning is thirty-seven and about four people in that room have said since that it has gone up every year and none of them has said how they know.

**She did not say the number. She said it to the room the way she has said it at every sitting, and the room heard it, and about four of the about eleven wrote nothing down, and one of them wrote it on the back of a hand about two years ago and has a different hand.**
---

The ask was made by a man of about forty-four at the seventeenth sitting, on a Wednesday, and it is four hundred and twenty days old today, which is sixty weeks to the day, and it is counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since.

**It is not fifty-nine weeks, which was the figure last time and belongs to a Wednesday last time and is not this one, and any sheet that carries that one and not this one is carrying a number for a different day.**

Nobody in that room put the ask. Nobody in that room has put it at any sitting this year, and the man who put it has not put it since, and no one else in that room has put it since either, and she has not offered it once and nobody has asked her for it, and it is still open and it is still not going to be answered and nobody has said it will not be.

---

He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at a counter from about two until about six, and the rail of about forty card slots is along the front of that counter and it holds two, and a man of about fifty-three was at that end with a tray and did not look at it and has never been asked to.

**Nobody in the building in Saltmarket knows that a man of twenty-two spent that morning at a bench, and nobody at that bench knows that a book was open on a table for about four hours in another district, and the two facts have never been in the same sentence in front of either man.**

About nine people came to that counter between about two and about six. About four of them were told no to something. Nobody came in between about half past five and about six, and the shutter came down to the height of the counter, and the light over the bench was the only light on the street he could see from where he was standing.

**Nobody at that counter said anything about a count of thirty-seven, and about four people there have said since that a number of thirty-seven is a lot for a Wednesday, and they do not know what it is a count of and have not asked.**

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-eight weeks. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-one weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**The separation is four months and nineteen weeks and two days old on that Wednesday and it is not shorter and it is not ended, and the figure is counted from its own chain of figures and not from anything that happened on a landing in another district, and the cook of twenty-seven said nothing about it and was not asked and no one in that room was told any of it.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

About four of the nine who came in said nothing and stood by the door and then left, and nobody asked them why.

**The page that is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf in that room was not read on Wednesday, and the binder is on a shelf and not in a drawer and about four people in that room have taken it down and put it back without opening it.**

The landing is one flight up and there is a light on the half-landing that is on a timer and has been on a timer since before the shutters went up, and about four people in that room have wondered about it and none of them has ever asked.

The book went back in the same place within reach of her right hand, and the tin went back in the same place, and the chair against the wall is against the wall and its mover is not named and is not going to be named, and about four people in that room have wondered in nineteen years who put it there and not one of them has ever asked.

**Nobody in that room was told about the four towns and nobody in that room was told about the card, and about four people in that room have said since that something is going on in the districts, and there is nothing going on in the districts that they could name.**
