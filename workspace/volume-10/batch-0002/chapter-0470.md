# Chapter 470 — Five Hundred And Eighty-One Days Old On A Day That Is Five Hundred And Eighty-One

**The Thursday of week one hundred and seventy-four was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that the fourteen lines on a hardboard in a room off that service road are five hundred and eighty-one days old, and five hundred and eighty-one is the day a landlord in an earlier year put a heating on, and the heating in this case is a closed thing with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it, and a man of about thirty-four who loads bread named out loud, once, what it would cost him to be the one who took the four places on a Thursday, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and forty-five days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and forty-nine days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a day in an earlier year when a landlord turned a heating on is not a card and is not a figure of days off for anything and is on nothing and is not in this case's arithmetic.

**The fourteen lines on the hardboard are five hundred and eighty-one days old, and five hundred and eighty-one is the day a landlord in an earlier year put a heating on in a building in this city.**

Those are the two halves of one number and they are not the same thing. The first is an age: it is how long a line of promise has been on a board on two nails in a room off a service road. The second is a day: it is an evening in an earlier year when somebody who is not in this case now turned a heating on for a set of rooms and it stayed on.

**Five hundred and eighty-one is the age of the fourteen lines and it is also the day the heating went on, and a person reading it is holding both at once, and the two readings are both true and neither of them is about a board or about a fortnight.**

**The heating in this case is a closed thing. It went off and a landlord put it on, in that order, and the last figure of it was printed in an earlier year and is not printed again, and there is no figure of days off for it anywhere in this case — not in anything anybody said, not in a record, not on a form, and not on a wall. There is no column for it and there is not going to be one.**

The heating that is on now, and dearer, and four names on a lease dearer still, is a different sentence from the day a landlord turned it on, and about four people in this city pay for it every week and about nine of them have said so in a shop or a kitchen and nobody has written any of it down.

**A hot-water cylinder in a building in the second of the four districts is not that heating. A Friday afternoon in another district is not that heating. A bus that takes most of a day is not that heating. The fortnight a cook of twenty-seven laid out and nobody repaid is not that fortnight, and the two are not on the same sheet and no sentence in this case may put them there.**


Four names on a lease cost more than they did and about four people in this city pay for the thing that is on now every week and one of the four has said out loud, in a shop, that it is dearer, and nobody wrote it down and nobody asked which four and nobody is going to.

**A fortnight a cook of twenty-seven laid out is on nobody's account and has been on nobody's account for four years and it is not the fortnight of any heating in any year and the two are not compared by anybody, including in this record.**
The other seven lines on that board are five hundred and sixty, five hundred and seventeen, five hundred and thirty-five, five hundred and sixteen, four hundred and sixty-three, four hundred and forty-one and six hundred and sixteen, and none of those seven is a day that anything on that board was written and none of them is a day that a heating went on.

---

The man of about thirty-four who loads bread was in the shop on the Thursday at about half past four, having been in it three Thursdays before with a question about four vans.

He came back to say that one of the four places had told him, through a person who had been told by a person, that they would take their own paper if it was on the Thursday.

**He said, once, at the end of the counter, to the counter and not to the man, that a Thursday is not a day he runs, and that it is about nine stops out of his day, and that the other three vans would be late, and that he would be doing it, and that nobody is going to thank him for it and he is not asking anybody to.**

Nobody said that was generous. Nobody said that was not the point. Nobody said that nine stops was a lot or a little, and nobody said that about nine is a small number of stops when there are more than nine, and nobody named the person in the other town who had said it or the person who had passed it on.

**He is not owed anything. Nobody in this city has agreed what he is owed and nobody has asked him and he has not asked and he said so himself, once, in about nine seconds, and then went out to look at a wheel.**

He did not say that he would keep doing it on Thursdays after this one. Nobody asked him whether he would and nobody ever will.


He goes to the four places on the Friday and the Thursday run is not a run and it is a thing he has agreed to do once, and about four people in this city know that he has said he would do it and none of them has told anybody else.

**He was told no in about four seconds three Thursdays ago when he asked whether the four rooms were the four towns, and he has not asked again, and the thing he has offered since is not an answer to the question he was refused and nobody has pointed that out.**
---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the kettle went on twice and one cup was made and it was not made for anybody in particular.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Thursday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about fifty weeks and one day. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-four weeks without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**The separation is four months and twenty-one weeks and three days old on that Thursday and it is not shorter and it is not ended, and the figure is counted from its own chain of figures and not from anything that happened on a Thursday in an earlier year in a building where a landlord turned something on, and the cook of twenty-seven at that sink was not asked about it and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked about it and neither of them has ever been asked about either of them.**

The shutter came down at about ten.

The middle one is the one that fills and the man of about fifty-one was at the wall with his tool bag at his feet and had said nothing for about fifty weeks and one day, and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in it for about three hours and said nothing.

**Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.**

About four of the nine who came in said nothing and stood by the door and then left, and nobody asked them why.

**The fortnight a cook of twenty-seven laid out and the day a landlord put a heating on are two closed things in two buildings and neither of them has a figure of days off for it on any surface in this case, and the fortnight is unpaid and the heating is on.**

The middle one is the one that fills and about four people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and about four went out again, and the shutter on that service road came down at about ten.

Nobody in that room knew that a number on a board two nails off had been its own day, and about four people in that room have said since that a line on a board is a line on a board, and that is the only thing anybody in that room has ever said about that board.

**Five hundred and eighty-one is the age of the fourteen lines and it is the day a heating went on in an earlier year and the two are in the same token on this page and in no sentence anybody in this case has said, and the person who has read this sentence is now holding both and cannot put one down and is not going to be able to.**
