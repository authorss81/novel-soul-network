# Chapter 459 — About Four Minutes At A Counter, And A Refusal In About Nine Seconds

The Wednesday of week one hundred and seventy-one was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a woman of twenty-four who is a resonance nurse and works out of a mobile clinic in the Lower Wards came in for a bracket at about half past four, stayed for about four minutes while the man at that counter told her what he had done with a number, said in about nine seconds that the thing he was about to do was the second number and refused to have anything to do with it, and left with the bracket in a paper bag.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and twenty-three days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and twenty-seven days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and nobody at that counter looked at the rail at any point on the Wednesday.

---

She came in for a bracket. It is an ordinary thing and it took about four minutes to find and about forty minutes to cut, and she stood at the end of the counter for the first four of them with her coat on.

The clinic has about nine chairs in it. The bracket is for a door frame. She did not say which door and he did not ask.

He told her about the paper while he was measuring. Not all of it, and not in order. He told her that a room of about nine people had written him a question on a folded sheet and he had answered it in nine words, and that the answer had come back as four hundred with the word rooms attached to it, and that he had said out loud to an empty counter that he could not take any of it back.

He told it the way a man tells something he has already said to himself four times.

---

She did not say that it was his fault. She did not say it was anybody's fault.

She said, in about nine seconds, standing at the end of that counter with a paper bag in her hand:

**"You cannot correct a number with a number. You can tell them what you actually said, and that is a third thing, and it is the only one of the three that is worth anything."**

Then she waited, which is the thing she does, and which is the reason a room of about nine people can say a true sentence to a man of twenty-two about a number he gave them and be believed.

He said that he wanted her to help him write it down, and he said it as a man asks, and he said it badly.

She said no in about nine seconds.

**"I am not going to be the person who wrote the second number. You can write it. It is your figure and it came out of your mouth, and if I put my hand on it then in about four years there will be two people in this who said it and one of them will have been standing in a shop."**

Nobody improved on it. Nobody added anything. The parts cage man worked with his back to the room for the whole of it and would not have seen it and would not have said anything if he had.

---

She left with the bracket in a paper bag at about five and said the thing about the bus and the time of it and did not stay.

Nobody thanked her. He did not thank her and she did not wait to be thanked and neither of them said a single word about the two of them, and about four people at that counter over the day did not know either of them and would not have known.

**The separation is four months and eighteen weeks and two days old on that Wednesday and it is not shorter and it is not ended, and the figure is counted from an anchor in an earlier volume and not from anything that happened at that counter, and no one in the shop was told any of it and nobody in the room off that service road was told any of it and the cook of twenty-seven said nothing about it and was not asked.**

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-seven weeks. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about fifty weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The three things, as a record of a distinction drawn once in about nine seconds.* **The first: a number, and it was wrong, and it is wrong now. The second: another number, offered in place of the first, and it would also have been wrong, and it is worse than wrong because a second number is one more thing for a room of about nine people to hold. The third: what was actually said, which is about nine of us, one room, Wednesday evenings, only, and which is true and is not a number. Who said the distinction: a woman of twenty-four who works out of a mobile clinic, in about nine seconds, at a counter, with a paper bag in her hand. Who said no to writing it down: the same person, in about nine seconds, on the ground that a figure that came out of one mouth and was written by a second is a figure two people can be blamed for. Whether she was thanked: no. Whether it was argued with: no, and nobody added anything, and the parts cage man worked with his back to the room for the whole of it and would not have seen it and would not have said anything if he had. Whether the third thing was written down: not on that day, and the day after is not this case's business. Owner: a counter, and nobody.**

*The paper bag, as a record of an ordinary transaction.* **What went in it: one bracket, cut to a door frame, and the door frame is in a mobile clinic with about nine chairs in it and the door was not named. How long it took to find and to cut: about four minutes and about forty minutes. What was said while it was being done: the thing recorded in the record above, and nothing else. What was said about the two people: nothing. Whether the transaction was put on the docket: it went on the docket as a bracket, because that is what goes on a docket. Whether the bracket will hold: unknown, and this case has never followed up a bracket and is not going to start. Owner: a paper bag, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Wednesday, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody. Nine doors on a road on the far side of this city are still hanging by one hinge, and a week that about eleven people lost has not come back to them, and a bar across the inside of a door in each of four buildings in four towns is on nothing.
