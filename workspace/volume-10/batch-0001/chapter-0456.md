# Chapter 456 — A Woman Of About Forty-One Who Counted The Counter Herself

The Wednesday of week one hundred and seventy was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a woman of about forty-one came off the morning bus with a shopping list in her hand, counted about nine people at a counter in about six hours on her own without asking anybody, wrote it on the back of the list, was refused the names of everybody in this case in about nine seconds, and said out loud that a room of about nine people would read that as contempt, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and sixteen days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and twenty days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and the shopping list with a number on the back of it is a tenth object and is not on anything anybody in this case has seen before.

---

She came in at about ten and stood at the end of the counter with her coat on, which is what a person does who is not sure she is going to stay.

She runs a shop. It is not a shop anybody would write about. It is a shop with a counter and a card for one thing and a queue on a Saturday.

She said she had come on the bus and would go back on the evening one, and she said it as a fact about the day and not as a complaint, and nobody said she should not have come.

She said the ninth line of a piece of paper was a fair question and she wanted to say so to the man who wrote the answer before she said the rest.

---

Then she asked for the names.

She asked for the names of everybody who comes to the room off that service road on a Wednesday evening. She asked for them the way a person asks for a phone number, and she had a piece of paper out, and there was a space on it.

He said no in about nine seconds. There was no reason and no condition and no invitation to write instead and no offer of anything else, and the nine seconds were the same nine seconds a person spends deciding not to make a joke.

**She did not argue about the nine seconds. She asked a second question, which was how she would know whether a thing had happened, and the answer was that she would not, and she wrote that down too, and it took about four seconds to write and it is the only thing in this case anybody has ever written down from a stranger's mouth on the back of a shopping list.**

She counted the counter herself while she was there.

Nobody asked her to and nobody stopped her. She stood about four feet back from the end of it with the list folded in her fist and she counted, and about nine people came to that counter in about six hours, and about four of them were told no to something, and she wrote the nine down and did not write the four down because the four is not a thing anybody can use.

---

Then she said the thing, at the end, on her way to the door, and she said it once and she said it flat.

**"I have to go back and tell them there are no names, and they will read that as contempt, and I am telling you that so that you know I know it."**

Nobody said that it was not contempt. Nobody said that the room would understand. Nobody said that a room which wants names is a room that has been let down before, because that was true and it was not said.

He said one thing back and it was about the bus and it was about what time it went, and she said she knew, and she went, and he did not say anything else, and about four seconds of the shop's afternoon were spent on that and nobody at the till heard it.

**The names are not on her list. The number of about nine a day is on her list in her own hand and it is the second number about this city to exist in a place four days of bus travel away, and the first one was written on the back of a sheet of paper and the second one was counted, and neither of them came from anybody who was asked.**

He went to the room at about half past six. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-six weeks, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-nine weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The two numbers, as a record of a thing that happened twice and to nobody's advantage.* **The first: about nine of us, one room, Wednesday evenings, only, written on the back of a folded sheet in the middle, unnumbered and unsigned, in a different building, on a different day, by a man who was not asked for a count and did not take one. The second: about nine, counted by a woman standing about four feet back from the end of a counter over about six hours, in her own hand, on the back of a shopping list, on a different day, by a person who was not asked to count and was not stopped. **THE TWO FIGURES ARE THE SAME NUMBER AND THEY ARE ABOUT DIFFERENT THINGS**, and the first is about a room in the evening and the second is about a counter in six hours in a street in this city, and no person in this case has put them together and no person in this case ever will. What the first one became is not in this record and is four days away. Whether either of them is a rate: no, and neither is on a form, and neither is a headcount, and neither is a membership. Whether the woman of about forty-one was thanked: no. Owner: two backs of two pieces of paper, and nobody.**

*The refusal of the names, as a record of nine seconds.* **What was asked: the names of everybody who comes to the room off that service road on a Wednesday evening. What was given: nothing. How long the deciding took: about nine seconds, and the same figure is the caption of about four other refusals in this case and none of them is this one and they are not to be added. Why no reason: a reason given would have been an offer, and an offer of a name is an offer of a person, and the man at that counter does not hand over a person. Whether a substitute was offered: no, and not a form, and not a place to write, and not a person to ask. What the person asking said she would have to do with it: tell a room of about nine people that there are no names, and be read as contempt, and say that she knew that before asking. Whether anybody said it was not contempt: no. Whether it was contempt: this case has no instrument for that and says so, and the not-knowing is not a loose end. Owner: a counter, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Wednesday, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody. Nine keys are needed and two exist, and the first of the nine still cannot be asked back. Nine doors on a road on the far side of this city are still hanging by one hinge, and a week that about eleven people lost has not come back to them, and a bar across the inside of a door in each of four buildings in four towns is on nothing. The fortnight a cook laid out is still on nobody's account. The Exchange did not sit, its book did not grow, and no number was given into its tin. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 459.**
