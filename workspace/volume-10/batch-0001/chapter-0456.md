# Chapter 456 — A Woman Of About Forty-Nine Who Counted The Counter Herself

The Wednesday of week one hundred and seventy was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a woman of about forty-nine came off the morning bus with a shopping list in her hand, counted about nine people at a counter in about six hours on her own without asking anybody, wrote it on the back of the list, was refused the names of everybody in this case in about nine seconds, and said out loud that a room of about nine people would read that as contempt, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and sixteen days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and twenty days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and the shopping list with a number on the back of it is a tenth object and is not on anything anybody in this case has seen before.

---

She came in at about ten and stood at the end of the counter with her coat on, which is what a person does who is not sure she is going to stay.

She stood there for about four minutes before she said anything, and about four of the other people who came in that day stood where they stood and not where they would have stood if they had been sure.

**Nobody moved her along. Nobody brought her a chair. Nobody at that counter has ever had anything for somebody to wait on, and there is nothing in that building for a person to wait on, and she did not look for anything to lean on.**

She runs a shop. It is not a shop anybody would write about. It is a shop with a counter and a card for one thing and a queue on a Saturday.

She has run it for about nine years and she does the card herself on a Saturday and she is the only one who does it, and about four people in that shop have said since that she counts more carefully than she needs to and have never said it to her.

**She has asked him one question before and got one answer, and that was on the bus four days of travel away and it was a question with a full stop at the end of it, and she came a long way to say thank you for a number and then asked for something else.**

She said she had come on the bus and would go back on the evening one, and she said it as a fact about the day and not as a complaint, and nobody said she should not have come.

She said the ninth line of a piece of paper was a fair question and she wanted to say so to the man who wrote the answer before she said the rest.

She said it in about nine words and she said it before the names, which is not the order a person asks in.

**She thanked him for the number before she asked for the names, and the thank you was the shortest thing anybody said at that counter that day, and about four people at that counter have since said that the two of them were talking about two different things.**

---

Then she asked for the names.

She asked for the names of everybody who comes to the room off that service road on a Wednesday evening. She asked for them the way a person asks for a phone number, and she had a piece of paper out, and there was a space on it.

**The piece of paper was a shopping list with about eleven things on the front of it and a space on the back of it, and the space was not ruled and she did not rule it and she put the nib on it before she finished the sentence.**

He said no in about nine seconds. There was no reason and no condition and no invitation to write instead and no offer of anything else, and the nine seconds were the same nine seconds a person spends deciding not to make a joke.

**She did not argue about the nine seconds. She asked a second question, which was how she would know whether a thing had happened, and the answer was that she would not, and she wrote that down too, and it took about four seconds to write and it is the only thing in this case anybody has ever written down from a stranger's mouth on the back of a shopping list.**

She counted the counter herself while she was there.

She counted from about four feet back, which is far enough to be out of the way and near enough to see faces, and she counted with her chin and not with a finger, and she was not hiding that she was doing it.

**Nobody asked her to stop and nobody asked her to say what she was doing, and about four people at that counter over the day did not notice that a woman at the end of it had been counting them.**

She wrote the number on the back of the shopping list and wrote it the way a person writes a number they are going to use again, which is with a ring round it.

Nobody asked her to and nobody stopped her. She stood about four feet back from the end of it with the list folded in her fist and she counted, and about nine people came to that counter in about six hours, and about four of them were told no to something, and she wrote the nine down and did not write the four down because the four is not a thing anybody can use.

---

Then she said the thing, at the end, on her way to the door, and she said it once and she said it flat.

**"I have to go back and tell them there are no names, and they will read that as contempt, and I am telling you that so that you know I know it."**

Nobody said that it was not contempt. Nobody said that the room would understand. Nobody said that a room which wants names is a room that has been let down before, because that was true and it was not said.

**Nobody said it was not contempt, and nobody said it was, and the second silence is not smaller than the first and about four people at that counter have thought about the second one since.**

The door has a bell on it and the bell went when she went, and about four people at that counter have heard it and one of them is the man of about fifty-three and he did not look up then either.

He said one thing back and it was about the bus and it was about what time it went, and she said she knew, and she went, and he did not say anything else, and about four seconds of the shop's afternoon were spent on that and nobody at the till heard it.

**What he said back was the time of the bus and nothing else, and it was the only thing he said to her all day, and the only sentence in about six hours that was about helping anybody, and it was not.**

Nobody at the till heard it and nobody at the bench heard it and the parts cage man had his back to the room and would not have heard it if he had.

**She wrote the time of the bus under the nine and underlined the nine, and there are two numbers on the back of that list now, and only one of them came from anybody who was asked.**

**The names are not on her list. The number of about nine a day is on her list in her own hand and it is the second number about this city to exist in a place four days of bus travel away, and the first one was written on the back of a sheet of paper and the second one was counted, and neither of them came from anybody who was asked.**

He went to the room at about half past six. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing.

**He was at the counter from about two until about six and about nine people came in and about four of them were told no to something, and one of the four was a woman who wanted a card for a Saturday and was told a card could be had on the Friday and not before, and that went on the docket, and about four people at that counter did not know that either of the two refusals on that Wednesday had happened.**

A woman of about forty-nine had been in that building for about six hours and had been told no in about nine seconds and had left with a number in her own hand, and the number is on a piece of paper in a pocket in a shop four days of travel away and the pocket is not in this city and is not on a list.

**The cook of twenty-seven washed about nine things that evening and made no cup of anything and was not spoken to about a woman who had counted a counter in a different city, and there was no reason on earth for her to be.** In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-six weeks, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-nine weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
