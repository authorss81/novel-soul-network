# Chapter 455 — Nine Lines With One Question In The Ninth

The Monday of week one hundred and seventy was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a sheet of paper folded in four came in on the morning bus with nine lines written on it in one hand and nothing else on it, and the ninth line was a question, and he wrote nine words on the back of it in the afternoon and put it back on the counter for the bus, and the nine words were about nine.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and fourteen days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and eighteen days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and the sheet of paper that came in on the morning bus is a ninth object and is not any of the six surfaces anybody in this case has been keeping apart.

---

It came off the bus and not out of a post. That is a thing worth saying because everything else in this case that has travelled has travelled by post, and this did not.

It was folded in four and it had no envelope and it had no name on it and it was not addressed to anybody, and the man on the counter took it off the pile of things people bring in and put it on the bench face down and left it there until about eleven.

**It is nine lines. It is in one hand, and the hand is not steady, and the eight lines above the ninth are about things a room needs and not about people: a door that sticks, a light that goes, a kettle, a bench, a floor that is cold in one corner, a key that has not been copied, a person who is not coming back, and a Sunday.** Nobody signed it. There is no room on it. There is no name on it. There is a place-name at the top with a spelling that is either right or is a different place, and this case cannot tell which, and the person who wrote it is not on the page.

**The ninth line is a question, and it has a full stop at the end of it, which the person who wrote the other eight did not do to any of them.**

It says: how many of you are there.

---

He read it twice and put it face down again and did not say anything about it for about two hours.

At about half past three he turned it over and wrote on the back of it, in the middle, nine words, and he did not number them and he did not sign them and he did not put a date on it and he did not write a name at the top.

He said out loud, to the end of the counter and not to anybody, that the number was going to be wrong by Friday, and that when it was wrong it would not be his fault, and that neither of those things would help.

**Nobody said that it would be the right number. Nobody said that it would not be a number at all. Nobody improved on it, and the man of about fifty-three at that end of the counter did not look up, and about four people came and went over the rest of the afternoon and none of them knew that a piece of paper had been folded in four on a bench under a lamp.**

He put it on the end of the counter at about five, in the place where things go that go back on the bus, and it went back on the bus in the evening and the bus goes twice a week and there was no copy and no carbon and no photograph of it and no second one.

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-five weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-nine weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The nine words, as a record of an answer given to a question nobody signed.* **What was asked, in nine lines' worth of a room's needs and then a ninth line: how many of you are there. What was answered, on the back, in the middle, in the same hand as the man at the bench: about nine of us, one room, Wednesday evenings, only. How many words: nine, and nobody counted them but him, and the counting was not for anybody. What the answer was not: it was not a headcount, and it was not a number of people who live in the city, and it was not a membership, and it was not a promise about a Wednesday in any particular week, and it was not a figure anybody in this case is entitled to carry forward. What was said about it out loud, once: that it would be wrong by Friday, that it would not be his fault, and that neither of those would help. Whether it was correct on the day it was written: no, because about nine is not a count of anything, and it was the only available true thing. Whether anybody improved on it: no. Whether anybody thanked him: no. Owner: the back of a sheet of paper, and nobody.**

*The prediction, as a record of a man telling a room something he cannot do anything about.* **What was predicted: that the number would be wrong by Friday. What was said alongside it: that when it was wrong it would not be his fault, and that neither of those things would help. Who it was said to: the end of the counter, and not to anybody, and the man standing at that end of the counter did not look up and has never repeated it. Whether the prediction was made to warn anybody: no, and no person in this case was warned, and no person in the four places whose names were on an envelope or on nine exercise books was told anything at all. Whether it was true: it came back wrong inside a fortnight, and the way it came back wrong is at the chapter after the next and not here. Owner: nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Monday, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody. Nine keys are needed and two exist, and the first of the nine still cannot be asked back. Nine doors on a road on the far side of this city are still hanging by one hinge, and a week that about eleven people lost has not come back to them, and a bar across the inside of a door in each of four buildings in four towns is on nothing. The fortnight a cook laid out is still on nobody's account. The Exchange did not sit, its book did not grow, and no number was given into its tin. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 458.**
