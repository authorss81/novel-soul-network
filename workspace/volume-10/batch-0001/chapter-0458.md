# Chapter 458 — Four Hundred, And A Second Figure Underneath It In A Second Line

The Monday of week one hundred and seventy-one was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that **the number about nine came back on the morning bus written on the front of a piece of paper in a different hand, and under it, in a second line, was a second figure, and neither of the two could be taken back, and on the same day in one of the four towns a man of about thirty-six wrote the right figure on a second sheet and did not send it and said out loud that it would change nothing.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and twenty-one days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and twenty-five days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and the piece of paper on the counter and the piece of paper four days of bus travel away are two different objects and are not one object.

---

It came off the bus at about ten and it was not folded and it was the same size as the one that went out and it was in a different hand.

Nobody brought it in. It was on the pile with about nine other things and it was the only one of the nine that had been folded and unfolded, and the creases had gone soft, and about four people at that counter handled that pile over the course of the day and none of them turned a second sheet over.

**There is no envelope and no name and no addressee and no signature on it, and there is nothing on the front of it but two lines and a place-name at the top with a spelling that is either right or is a different place, and this case cannot tell which and is not going to be able to.**

The hand is not the hand that wrote the nine words, and the pen is not the pen, and about four people have wondered since whether the person who wrote it is the same person who asked the question, and there is no way on earth of finding out and nobody has tried.

**The first line said that there are four hundred of them. The second line said that there are four hundred rooms.**

Both of those were written on the same sheet, in the same hand, on the same morning, by somebody who had come from a room of about nine people and had done the arithmetic out loud in front of them.

It was done on a Sunday, which is the day a room with a door that sticks on the list has least to do, and it was done by somebody who had been in that room twice, and the person who wrote it did not put their name on it and was not asked to and would not have.

**The arithmetic was not hidden and it was not done in a bad faith. That is the part of it that has no defence in it: nobody in that room had a reason to stop, because the only reason anybody could have given to stop it would have sounded like somebody from this city telling a room what a room was.**

About nine had gone to four hundred, and four hundred had picked up a word it was never given, and there were about nine people in one room and there are now four hundred rooms in a place that has about four hundred people in it in total, and this case cannot say whether that is true and is not going to find out, and **the second line is the part that will still be wrong in a year, because a number of rooms does not go anywhere once it has been written down and repeated.**

He read it twice. He put it on the bench under the lamp, face up, for about an hour, and nobody at that counter came near it and nobody asked what it was.

**Four hundred of them was the first line and it was the line that would be repeated, and the second line was underneath it in the same hand and the same pen and was written after a pause that the person who wrote it did not mark on the sheet.**

Nobody at that counter asked. A man of about fifty-three was at that end with a tray and did not look, and the parts cage man worked with his back to the room, and about four people came and went within the hour and the sheet lay face up under the lamp the whole of it and was picked up once by the man who had put it there.

---

At about one he said the only thing he said about it, out loud, to the end of the counter and to nobody.

**"I said nine and somebody made it four hundred, and I have no way to take any of it back, and that is the whole of what I know about it."**

Nobody said that the man was wrong. Nobody said that four hundred was too many. Nobody said that a number can be corrected by a later number, because that is not true and nobody in that building knows it is not true either.

He did not write a correction. He did not write anything at all that day except the docket, and the docket said *paper, in*, which is what goes on a docket, because there is no column on a docket for what a piece of paper is about.

**He did not telephone anybody, and there was no number to telephone, and about four people at that counter have wondered since whether there was anybody he could have telephoned and nobody has ever established that there was.**

He did not write on the sheet. It went into the tray under the till with about nine other things and it is not on the docket and the docket does not mention it, and the tray is emptied on a Friday and it was not emptied on the Monday it arrived.

**The sheet came back four days later with nothing on the back of it, and nobody checked the back of it, and about four people at that counter do not know that they did not check it and would not call that a decision.**

**A correct thing is possible and it is not the same as a correction, and the two are kept apart in this case because a correction is a second number about the same thing and two numbers about the same thing are how the first one became the second one.** That is not an opinion anybody in this case has expressed. It is a thing that happened between Monday and the Monday before, on a bus, in a room of about nine people, and it is on the front of a piece of paper under a lamp.

**A correction is cheap and it is available and it costs a person who is owed nothing nothing at all, and that is the whole reason there is not one, and nobody in this city has ever been told that there is not one.**

---

**In one of the four towns, on the same day, a man of about thirty-six who works in a kitchen wrote eleven on a sheet of paper and folded it and put it in an envelope and did not post it.**

The kitchen was doing about nine hundred covers and he was on the cold side of it, and about four people were in that kitchen when he wrote it, and one of them asked what he was writing and he said the number in this room, and that was the whole of the conversation.

**He wrote it in about nine seconds and he did not take a second opinion and he did not ask anybody whether eleven was right, and the number in his own room is eleven and this case has not checked it and cannot.**

He had listened to the arithmetic. He had watched a room of about nine people do four hundred out loud and had seen the moment it stopped being about the room and started being about the city.

The moment was not a moment anybody could point at afterwards. It was somewhere in about four minutes of a Sunday evening and it was not the four hundred and it was not the rooms, and it was the point at which one of about nine people in that room said a number out loud for the third time and got louder each time, and nobody told him to stop, and he did not.

**He wrote eleven because eleven was the number in his own room and because a number that is about a room is the only kind he trusts, and he was right about that and it made no difference whatever.**

He wrote the number that was in his own room, which was eleven, and he wrote it on a second sheet, and the second sheet was better than the first one, and it was correct, and it was in his hand, and it went in an envelope.

**He said out loud, to about four people in that kitchen, that sending it would change nothing, and about four of them agreed and about four of them did not say anything, and nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him, and the envelope is in a drawer in a kitchen four days of bus travel away and it is not on anything and it is not going to be posted and this case knows that and cannot prove it.**

A correct action that changes nothing, performed by a person who is not a villain and is owed nothing, in a room that has never met this one.

**About four of the people in that kitchen agreed with him and about four of them said nothing, and one of the four who said nothing has since put the sheet in a different drawer without looking at it, and nobody in that kitchen knows he did it and nobody ever will.**

He went to the room at about half past six. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-six weeks and four days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about fifty weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*About nine and four hundred, as a record of a figure changing hands.* **What was given: about nine of us, one room, Wednesday evenings, only, nine words, on the back of a folded sheet, in a different building, to nobody. What came back: four hundred of them, in the first line, and four hundred rooms, in the second line, in a different hand, on a bus, inside a fortnight. What happened in between: arithmetic, done out loud, in a room of about nine people, and nobody in that room stopped it because there was no reason anybody could have given to stop it that would not have sounded like somebody from this city telling a room what a room was. Whether the four hundred is a count of anything: no, and it is not a headcount, and it is not a membership, and it is not a rate, and it is on nothing. Whether the four hundred rooms is a count of anything: no, and a number of rooms is the figure in this record that will still be wrong in a year, and the reason it is printed here rather than left is that nobody in this case will ever be in a position to say it is wrong. What was done about it in this city: one sentence said out loud to the end of a counter, and nothing else. Owner: a piece of paper under a lamp, and nobody.**

*The eleven that was not posted, as a record of a correct thing.* **Who did it: a man of about thirty-six who works in a kitchen, holds no post, is owed nothing, and has not been thanked. What he wrote: eleven, on a second sheet, in his own hand, and the second sheet was better than the first one. Whether it was correct: it is the number in his own room and this case has not checked it and cannot. Where it went: into an envelope. Whether the envelope was posted: not, and this case cannot prove that and does not pretend to, and the two are different and the not-proving is not a hole in the record. What he said: that it would change nothing, once, to about four people, and about four agreed and about four said nothing. Whether anybody improved on it: no. Whether anybody is ever going to know he did it: no, and the sheet is not on anything and it is not going to be found by anybody, and that is what makes it a correct thing rather than a thing anybody can point at. Owner: a drawer, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Monday, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut — a different drawer, in a different building, four days of bus travel away, and the two are not to be mentioned in one sentence — a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody. Nine keys are needed and two exist, and the first of the nine still cannot be asked back. Nine doors on a road on the far side of this city are still hanging by one hinge, and a week that about eleven people lost has not come back to them, and a bar across the inside of a door in each of four buildings in four towns is on nothing. The fortnight a cook laid out is still on nobody's account. The Exchange did not sit, its book did not grow, and no number was given into its tin. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 461.**
