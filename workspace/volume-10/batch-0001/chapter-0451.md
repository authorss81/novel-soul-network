# Chapter 451 — Nine Exercise Books And About Four Seconds

The Monday of week one hundred and sixty-nine was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about fifty-two put nine exercise books on that counter at about ten past one, one for each of nine places, and was told no in about four seconds, with no reason and no condition and no request to come back, and he said out loud that the bus had cost him a day and a half, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him, and along the front of that counter a rail of about forty slots holds two cards where it held two for eight years and one for a hundred and nineteen days.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and seven days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in that rail is seven hundred and eleven days old, which is four days more than the room is, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up and does not start again when a card has been put down, and it was seven hundred and eleven days old on the hundred and nineteenth day of its being in a coat pocket and it is seven hundred and eleven days old now, and the rail holds two and nobody in that building has ever been told which slot either of them came out of.

---

The books were the cheap kind with a printed cover and a space on the front for a name, and somebody had written nine place-names on nine fronts in nine hands that were not all the same hand, and one of the nine had been spelled wrong and then corrected in the same pen.

The shop at about ten past one on a Monday is the shop it is on a Tuesday, and about four of the things about it are the same every day of the week, and not one of them is there for anybody. There is a bench under a lamp with a docket on it. There is a parts cage with a man in it who works with his back to the room and has for years. There is a shutter that comes down at the end of the day and does not go down the whole way, so that a person can be spoken to across it. There is a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it. There is a wall list in a plastic sleeve with about nine names on it that are the names of things and not of people. And along the front of the counter there is a rail of about forty card slots, and about thirty-eight of them have been empty for years and nobody has ever taken the rail off the counter to see what is behind it.

**He came in with the bag over his shoulder and put it down on the floor by his foot before he took the books out, and he did it in a way that said he had decided where the bag was going to be before he came in, and it was wrong, and it was left where he put it for the whole of the visit.**

He put them down in a row along the front of the counter and squared them up with his knuckle before he said anything, and the squaring took longer than the whole conversation did.

He said he drove the line. He said it went twice a week, out on the Tuesday and back the same day if the weather was on side, and did not come back at all if it was not. He said there were nine stops and about four hundred people got off at them in a week, and that he knew that better than anybody because he counted them, and that he was not counting for anybody.

**He wanted to know what this city does on a Friday.**

Not what it is called. Not who is in charge of it. Not whether it is any good, and he did not ask that either, and the not-asking was the most careful thing about the whole visit. He wanted the thing that happens, so that the nine front covers could have it on them by the end of the month and about nine schools in nine places could set it as a piece of work.

He said the schools had asked for it in the autumn and nothing had come, and he said that in the flat way a man says a thing he has already said once and did not like the sound of it the first time.

He said the nine places were not towns, and then said that they were, and then said that was not the difference he had meant, and did not finish that sentence and did not appear to know how he was going to.

He said the covers were going out at the end of the month and that after the end of the month there would be no more of them, and that this was the part he had not wanted to say on a bus and was saying at a counter instead. He said the bus back went at about six and that saying so was not a complaint about the bus. Nobody at that counter took him up on any of the three things.

---

He was told no in about four seconds.

Four seconds is not a long time. It is long enough to say a word and it is not long enough to have a reason ready, and the reason there was not given is not that there was not one.

**The four seconds were spent on the word. Everything else in that answer came afterwards and did not come.**

There was no reason given. There was no condition. Nobody said come back on Thursday and nobody said write to somebody and nobody produced a form, and nobody at that counter looked at him while it happened in a way that would have made it longer.

**The reason there was not given is that the room off that service road is not a practice in that department and has never been one, and a man of about twenty-two does not teach, does not demonstrate, does not assess and does not assist anybody, and there is no version of this counter on which a list of what he does is a thing he hands over.**

A reason would have been an offer. That is the whole of it, and it is a rule in this case and it is not a rule anybody has written down.

He said the no twice, once as a word and once with his hand, and then he put his hand flat on the nearest book and left it there for about a second and a half, and the parts cage man had his back to the room and did not turn round.

He asked one more thing after that, and he asked it well, which is to say he asked it in the tone of a man who had already decided the answer and wanted to hear it said. He asked whether the answer would have been different if he had not brought the books. About four seconds went on that one as well, and the answer was the same length as the first one and was not a different word.

Nobody at that counter enjoyed it. There is nothing in this case to enjoy and nobody at that counter takes anything off anybody, and a man who has been told no in about four seconds and has named what the trip cost him is not a man anybody has to be careful with afterwards.

He said the trip had cost him a day and a half. He said it plainly, at the counter, to the man standing at the end of it, and he did not say it as a complaint and he did not say it twice, and about four people at that counter over the day did not know it had been said.

Nobody said that it was a lot. Nobody said it was fair. Nobody said it was not the man he should have asked, and nobody named a man he should have asked, because there is nobody in this case who can be named as the man to ask.

---

The books went back into a bag.

He did not take them one at a time. He put the bag on the counter, put the books in off the front in the order they had been in, and squared the bag up, and the squaring was a habit and not a message.

**Nobody helped him. The bag was heavy and it was about nine books and he managed it in one go and nobody at that counter moved to make it easier and nobody thought of not moving.**

They were told no to a Saturday, and to a cheaper thing, and to being put on a list, and to a phone call that will not be made. None of the four said the word contempt and none of the four named anybody, and one of them said thank you and one of them said nothing at all and two of them said the word sorry, which was for arriving and not for asking.

About four people came to that counter between about two and about six. About four of them were told no to something, which is a thing that happens at that counter on an ordinary Monday and is not a policy and has never been a policy and is not on a form.

The fifth was told a thing could be had on Thursday and not before, and that went on the docket as well, because there is one docket and it is on the bench under the lamp and it is the only record of the day and it is not sent to anybody.

**Nobody at that counter takes a second sheet. There is one docket, it is on the bench under the lamp, and it goes into the tray under the till at the end of the day, and the tray is emptied on a Friday.**

**Nine came in and about four were told no to something, and the four were four different things, and the man who told them is twenty-two and has been at that counter for about four years.**

**Nobody came in between about half past five and about six, and the shutter was down to the height of the counter, and the light over the bench was the only light on the street he could see from where he was standing.**

---

He walked to the room at about half past six and sat at the end of the second bench and did not stay long.

The room off that service road is four rooms and a back one and the middle one is the one that fills, because the door on it sticks when it is wet and because there is a light in it that comes on when the other two do not. There is a floor in the corner of it that stays cold whatever the heating is doing, and there has been for years, and a mat has been put over the cold corner and the mat moves and the cold does not.

**Nobody put anybody's name anywhere, and about nine people came in and went out again inside twenty minutes, and the kettle went on twice and one cup was made and it was not made for anybody in particular.**

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. She has been at that sink on the Monday of this case for a long time and has never asked him for anything and has never been asked for anything, and there is a fortnight on nobody's account that has her handwriting on the envelope and nothing else.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-four weeks and five days, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it.

A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-eight weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him, and it is going to go on.

The rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds two cards and has held two for about eight years, and a man of about fifty-three has stood at that end for about eight years, and he was not in that room and has not been in it for about two years and holds no post in it.

The rail along the front of the counter holds two cards and has held two for about eight years. A man of about fifty-three has stood at that end of it for about eight years. He locks the shutter, he takes the lamp off the bench, he puts the docket face down under the till tray and he does not read it, and nobody has ever asked him to read it and nobody has ever asked him what the two cards in the rail are for.

**He was not in that room and has not been in it for about two years and holds no post in it, and the two facts have never been in the same sentence in front of him.**

**Nobody knows that a man put nine place-names on nine cheap covers on the counter he stands at, and he was about eleven feet away when it happened, and he was looking at the till.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
