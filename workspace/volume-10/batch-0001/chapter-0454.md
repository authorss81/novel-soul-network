# Chapter 454 — The First Friday Of Ten, And A Post That Has Been Bare Thirty-Seven Weeks

The Friday of week one hundred and sixty-nine was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, and he was not in Crown Terrace and the shop did not know and would not have known, and the one thing that is true of it is that **about eleven people were in a room on a second floor for about nine minutes, called at about half past seven from the front by a woman of about thirty-six wearing a lanyard, and nobody said how long it would be, and nobody asked, and the post by the corridor end has been bare for thirty-seven weeks, and nobody in that room was told.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and eleven days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and fifteen days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and it is in a building on the other side of this city from the second floor of another building and nothing on that Friday connects the two of them and nobody crossed it.

---

The room is on a second floor. There are about eleven chairs and they are the sort of chairs that come out of a cupboard. There is a table at the front with a laptop on it that is not on the internet and is not connected to anything in this case.

It is a room with one corridor, about nine doors along it, a fire door at each end and the far one propped with a folded sheet of paper that has been there long enough to have a grey mark on the floor under it. There is one stair and one stairwell light and the stairwell light works.

The chairs are the kind that come out of a cupboard and go back into it, and they are stacked against the wall before they are put out, and the stacking is done by whoever arrives first and has been done by about four different people over the years. There is a table at the front with a laptop on it, and the laptop is not on the internet and is not connected to anything in this case, and about four people on that floor have asked at some point whether it works and been told that it does something.

**Nobody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it is for, and no sheet and no form and no line on any wall in that company says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one.**

About eleven people came. That is the number and it is the same number it is on every Friday in this city, and it is not a rate and it is not on a form, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all.

There were about eleven of them and they were not late, and being not late is not a rule and has never been a rule, and the only person who could have said something about it was at the front saying one word.

About four of the eleven are on no rota at all and have been for longer than anybody has kept a count of, and they come, and nobody checks whether they are on anything, and about four people in that room have worked out over the years that they are on nothing and have not said so out loud.

**Nobody signed anything and nobody signed anything on any of the Fridays, and there is no sheet on that floor with a name on it, and there has not been one for longer than the post has been empty.**

**Nobody took a count. The number about eleven is not a count that anybody took; it is what a person who was in the room every week for a year would say, and the person who would say it has not said it to anybody.**

The woman who called them in was about thirty-six and had a lanyard on and did not say how long it would be and did not say what it was. She said one word and pointed at the chairs.

**She did not say good evening. She did not say sit down. She said one word that about four people on that floor have never been able to repeat and have all described the same way, and she pointed.**

**About nine minutes, from the front, in the way a person calls a room that has not started, and about nine people in that room noticed the length of it and about four of them have said since that they did not know it was nine minutes, and nobody in that room has said out loud what a person is supposed to do with nine minutes of somebody else's time and nobody has ever been asked.**

---

The post by the corridor end of that floor is a black box on the wall and there is a card in it with a number on it and there is no list anywhere of what the number is for, and it has been empty for thirty-seven weeks.

It is a black box about the size of a house brick with a slot in it and a card in the slot with a number printed on the card. The number is not a room and it is not a floor and it is not a date, and nobody in that building has ever said what it is.

**The card has been in the box since before the box was empty. Nobody has taken it out. Nobody has put anything else in, and nobody has ever taken the box off the wall, and the reason it is still on that wall is not a decision anybody in that company can remember making.**

There is a second box at the near end of the same corridor and it is not empty, and the two boxes are about nine feet apart, and nobody in that building has ever been asked whether they are a set.

Thirty-seven weeks is from the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two. Nobody in that room knows that. Nobody in that room has asked, and a man of about thirty-nine who buys training and sits at the back has asked the person who supplies the training whether the post is used, and was told it is not, and did not ask why it is still there.

**The material for that week had gone out in one envelope with four names on it, one for each of four places, and one of the four was spelled wrong, and the person who sent it has not been told, and nobody in that room noticed and nobody in this case ever will.**

The envelope went out on the Monday in a bundle of about nine other things that are not evidence of anything, and about four of those nine have gone to the same four places, and about four have not gone anywhere at all.

**Three of the four names on it are spelled the way the places spell them. The fourth is not, and the spelling is not a stranger's spelling, and it is the kind of mistake a person makes who has been writing the name from memory and not from a list.**

Nobody telephoned. Nobody wrote back. The person who sends the material has been sending it for about nine weeks and has never been told that one of the four has not been receiving it correctly, and there is no list of the four anywhere, and there is no way to check, and that is not a thing anybody decided.

There was a man of about twenty-six who works in a canteen four days a week and asked one ordinary question about a form and got an ordinary answer and said thank you and that was the whole of his participation.

The question was about a box on a form and whether a crossed-out figure counted, and the answer was about nine seconds long and was yes if it was not in the box, and the man of about twenty-six wrote nothing down and did not ask a second question and said thank you once and sat down again.

**That is the only exchange on that floor that week in which anybody said a word of thanks, and the man of about thirty-nine who buys training sat about two rows behind him and said nothing, and nobody has ever said that he noticed.**

Nobody thanked the woman with the lanyard. Nobody has ever thanked her in any of the weeks, and about four people on that floor have said since that they did not know her surname, and they have not asked.

Nobody improved on anything. Nobody thanked anybody except that one man, and he was thanked for asking a question and not for anything else.

**The fourteenth line on a hardboard in a room off a service road is five hundred and forty-seven days old on that Friday, and five hundred and forty-seven is the day the fifteen lines were written on that same board. The fifteen lines are five hundred and twenty-six days old on the same day, and five hundred and twenty-six is the day the fourteen were written.** Both of those figures are ages and not dates, and nobody in either building said either number, and they are set down here because a record carries them and a record is not a conversation, and whoever is holding this sentence is now holding two numbers that are each other's birthdays and neither of them has anything to do with a room on a second floor in Crown Terrace.

He went to the room at about half past six. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing.

He had been at the bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at that counter from about two until about six on the Friday, and the shop did not know that any of it had happened anywhere else and would not have known and nobody in the room off that service road knew that a room on a second floor in another district had about eleven people in it at about half past seven.

**The counter on a Friday is shorter than the counter on a Wednesday and nobody in that building has ever worked out why and nobody has asked, and about four people came in over the whole afternoon and two of them were told no to something.** In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Friday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-five weeks and two days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about forty-nine weeks and one day without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**The man of about fifty-one had a tool bag at his feet and had it at his feet on the Monday and on the Tuesday and on every evening since, and nobody has asked him what is in it and he has not said and it is not a tool bag anybody has ever been asked to open.**

**The man of about thirty-three is not in this city and has not been in this city for most of the hold, and the four buildings and the about nine hundred doors go on without him, and nothing is asked of him and nothing is given to him, and the fifth week of that hold began on the Friday.**

Nobody in that room knew any of that. About four people came in for the temperature, and the cook of twenty-seven washed about nine things and made no cup, and the shutter came down at about ten.

Nobody thanked anybody, except the canteen man once. The shutter came down at about ten.
