# Chapter 453 — A Cylinder, A Landlord, And An Honest Answer About Money

The Thursday of week one hundred and sixty-nine was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, at a counter until about four, and at the end of a counter in a building in the second of the four districts until about half past four, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about forty-four who is the caretaker of that building was told what his hot-water cylinder would cost to put right and how long it would take and neither of those figures was the figure anybody has been owed in this case for four years, and the two are not on the same sheet, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and ten days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and fourteen days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and nobody in that building has ever been told which slot either of them came out of.

---

He came in at about ten past three and asked for about ten minutes and got about ninety.

The building is a building. It has a caretaker because somebody once decided that a building with a caretaker was a building people would stay in, and the deciding is not on anything anybody in this case has seen. There are about nine flats in it and four of the nine are empty in the winter, and the empties are not the point and were not the point of the visit.

The cylinder is at the back of a cupboard under the stairs and it is about eleven years old. **It is not the heating. It is not the heating in that building and it is not the heating in this case, and the heating in this case is a thing that went off on a Monday in an earlier year and was put on by a landlord on a Wednesday in a later one, and it is a closed thing in this case and there is no figure of days off for it anywhere in it, in prose, in a page of a record or on any surface, and a cylinder under a stair is not that thing and must never be made to be that thing.**

He wanted it looked at because the hot water at the top of that building goes cold at about seven in the evening and has been going cold at about seven in the evening for two winters.

He said that the landlord had been told in the autumn. He said that the landlord had sent a form. He said the form came back with a tick on it and nothing else, and he said that last part the way a man says the part that has been sitting on him for a month.

---

Marek looked at it for about twenty minutes and did not take anything apart.

He looked at the pipe above it and the isolation valve and the thing behind the panel, and he turned the water off at the valve and on again, and he wrote nothing down, and there was no docket for it because it was not a job.

**"That is a part and a labour and it is about two hundred and forty pounds, and it is about a fortnight, and it is not the landlord's problem until he is told."**

That is the whole of what he said, and it took about nine seconds, and he said it standing up with his hands wet.

He did not say the landlord would pay. He did not say the landlord would refuse. He said it was not the landlord's problem until he is told, which is a true sentence and is not advice, and there is a difference between the two that most people who say true sentences do not make on purpose.

Then he said nothing else, and the man asked three more questions and got three shorter answers, and one of the three was a number and one of the three was about a day and one of the three was the word depends, and nobody improved on any of them.

**Nobody told that caretaker to write to anybody. Nobody told him not to. The day ended with two hundred and forty pounds and about a fortnight sitting in a man's head and nothing on any paper anywhere.**

---

He left at about twenty to five and the shutter was up and the light outside was the colour it is at that hour in that street, and nobody watched him go, and he did not ask for the number of anybody.

The thirteen lines on the hardboard in the room off that service road are five hundred and eighty-one days old on that Thursday, and five hundred and eighty-one is the day a landlord put the heating on in an earlier year, and the figure is an age and not a date.

Nobody in that room said the number out loud and it is printed here because a page of a record carries it, and a page of a record is not a conversation.

**A number that is an age and not a date, in the week a man is told two hundred and forty pounds and about a fortnight, is a number a person can be caught by, and this one is caught here on purpose, and the sentence that catches it belongs to a room and not to a file: the fortnight that was said on the Thursday is a fortnight for a cylinder under a stair, and the fortnight that has been on nobody's account for four years is a fortnight a cook laid out, and the two are not the same fortnight, they are not in the same building, they are not owed by the same person, and no sheet in this city has both of them on it.**

He went to the room at about half past six and sat at the end of the second bench and did not stay long.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and was not spoken to about the fortnight, and she has a fortnight on nobody's account and it is a different fortnight and nobody has ever put the two in a sentence including her.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Thursday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-four weeks and six days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-nine weeks without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The honest answer, as a record of three figures given to a person who did not ask for them.* **Who asked: a man of about forty-four who is the caretaker of a building with about nine flats, and he asked for about ten minutes and got about ninety. What was wanted: the hot water at the top of that building to stop going cold at about seven in the evening, for two winters. What was given: a part, a labour, about two hundred and forty pounds, about a fortnight, and the sentence that it is not the landlord's problem until he is told. In what form: about nine seconds, standing up, hands wet, no note taken, no photograph, no second copy, and nothing on a docket anywhere. What was refused: nothing, and nobody refused that man anything, and this is the second day running on which a person has been refused nothing except a fact about the future. What was not said: whether the landlord would pay, whether he would refuse, and what to write, and this is recorded because a record that reports only what was said is a record that hides a decision. Whether the caretaker will write: not known, and not going to be known in this case, and the fortnight is his and not anybody's. Owner: a cupboard under a stair, and nobody.**

*The two fortnights, as a record of a thing that is not a thing.* **The first: about a fortnight, for a cylinder, said on the Thursday, in the second of the four districts, costed at about two hundred and forty pounds, and owed by nobody and to nobody at the hour it was said. The second: a fortnight, laid out and paid for by a cook of twenty-seven, four years old now, unpaid by anybody, unresolved, and not mentioned on the Thursday by anybody including the man who said the first one. The two are not the same fortnight, they are not in the same building, they are not about the same thing, and no sheet in this city carries both of them. A person who puts them in one sentence has restarted a closed series, and the closed series is the heating, and the heating went off on a Monday in an earlier year and was put on by a landlord on a Wednesday in a later one, and it is a closed thing in this case and it has no figure of days off anywhere in it. Owner: nobody, and the not-being-anybody's is the point.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Thursday, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody. Nine keys are needed and two exist, and the first of the nine still cannot be asked back. Nine doors on a road on the far side of this city are still hanging by one hinge, and a week that about eleven people lost has not come back to them, and a bar across the inside of a door in each of four buildings in four towns is on nothing. The fortnight a cook laid out is still on nobody's account and was not spoken about. The Exchange did not sit, its book did not grow, and no number was given into its tin. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 456.**
