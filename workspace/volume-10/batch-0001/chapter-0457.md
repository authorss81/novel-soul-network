# Chapter 457 — The Second Friday Of Ten, And Two Counts Of The Same Eleven

The Friday of week one hundred and seventy was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and he was not in Crown Terrace and the shop did not know and would not have known, and the one thing that is true of it is that **about eleven people were in a room on a second floor for about nine minutes and the post by the corridor end has now been bare for thirty-eight weeks, and a man of about thirty-nine who buys training asked a person with a lanyard what the company counts on a Friday and was given two numbers, and neither of the two was ever put to anybody in the other.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and eighteen days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and twenty-two days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and it is in a building on the other side of this city from a second floor in another building and nothing on that Friday joins them and nobody crossed it.

---

The room is the same room. About eleven chairs out of a cupboard and a table at the front and a laptop on it connected to nothing in this case.

It is the same corridor and the same stair and the same stairwell light and the same fire door at the far end with the same folded sheet of paper propping it. The chairs were stacked against the wall when the first person came and were unstacked by the first person who came, and that is the second week running that two different people have done the two halves of that.

**Nobody has ever asked who is meant to stack them and nobody has ever asked who is meant to unstack them, and about four people on that floor have worked out that they are the one who does it and have not said so.**

About eleven people came, which is the same figure as last Friday and is a figure and not a rate, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and that has been the case in this city for a long time and is not on a form and is not a form.

The woman of about thirty-one who works nights was there and had been at the back both Fridays. The man of about twenty-six who works in a canteen four days a week was not, and he had not said he was coming, and nobody noticed for about nine minutes and then about four people noticed at the same moment and nothing was said.

**Two Fridays is not a run. It is two Fridays, and anybody who wants a run out of it has to wait, and there are eight more Fridays after this one of which this case has seen none.**

The post by the corridor end is bare thirty-eight weeks, which is two hundred and sixty-six days from the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, and nobody in that room knows either of those figures.

**The card is still in it and the number on the card is still the number and nobody has replaced either of them, and the box is at the same height on the same wall and has not been repainted round, and the paint round it is a different colour from the paint above it.**

Seven days is the whole of the difference between the two Fridays, and the post did not change on either of them and nobody looked at it on either of them.

**A man of about thirty-nine who buys training asked the person who supplies the training whether the post is used, and was told it is not, and did not ask why it is still there, and the same question was asked by the same man three weeks before and got the same one-line answer, and nobody has joined those two asks together.**

---

The man of about thirty-nine who buys training sat at the back again and asked a question this week that he had not asked last week, and the question was what the company counts on a Friday.

**He had asked something on the Friday before, and it was about a different subject, and nobody on that floor has ever kept a note of what he asks and he has never been asked whether he means to.**

The woman with the lanyard answered in about nine seconds. She said the company counts two things and gave both figures, and one of them was eleven and one of them was nine, and she did not say which room each of them was for and was not asked twice.

**She gave the two numbers in that order and did not pause between them, and the order has never been established and about four people have since given the order their own way, and nobody has asked her which order was hers.**

He wrote them on the back of his hand and has still got them, or has not, and nobody saw him do it and nobody has ever asked him for either number.

He wrote them with a biro on the back of his left hand, low down near the wrist, in the ordinary place a man writes a thing he is going to lose, and about four people have seen him wash his hands at the end of a Friday and about four of them have thought about the two figures since and none of them has said anything.

**Nobody in that room knows that the two numbers do not agree, including the man holding them, and the woman who gave them does not know that anybody does not know, and the two of those facts are the only things anybody in that building could be said to have got wrong.**

**The two figures have been in the same conversation twice and have never been in the same sentence, and the person who holds both of them does not know which of them is which room, and the person who gave them does not know that anybody does not know. Two counts of the same eleven is a fact about a Friday and not about a company, and this case has no instrument for the difference and says so.**

---

A woman of about thirty-one who works nights asked whether the nine minutes is the same every week, and was told that it is, and said that she has four of them in a month and that four of them is about six hours standing in a corridor for nothing, and said it as a fact and not as a complaint, and nobody improved on it and nobody said she was right.

She works nights and comes in on the Friday she is off and has done for long enough that about four people on that floor know which four Fridays those are without being told, and nobody has ever asked her how she arranges it and she has never said.

**The six hours are hers and were named by her, once, out loud, to nobody in particular, after the answer and not before it, and nobody improved on them and nobody said she was right and nobody has asked her a second time.**

She sat down at the back and she was not late and she was not thanked, and about four people in that room have since said that they did not know what the six hours were for either.

There was a man of about twenty-six who works in a canteen four days a week and did not come, and nobody noticed, and he was not on a rota.

He was not on a rota and had not asked to be and had told nobody he was coming and had said nothing to anybody about not coming, and there was no list anywhere that said he was expected, because there is no list, and that is the whole of why nobody noticed for about nine minutes.

**A man of about thirty-nine who buys training was at the back again and had asked something different again, and nobody on that floor has ever kept a note of what he asks, and the three things he has asked were not about the same subject.**

**She said the two numbers and left, and about four people in that room have since said that they did not know what either of them was a count of, and nobody has asked her and nobody is going to.**

He went to the room at about half past six. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing.

**He had been at the bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at a counter from about two until about six and neither the bench nor the counter had anything on it about a second floor, and the shop did not know that a man of about thirty-nine had been given two numbers that week.**

Nobody in the room off that service road knew any of it, and about four people came in for the temperature, and the man of about fifty-one had his tool bag at his feet, and the shutter came down at about ten. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Friday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about forty-six weeks and two days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty weeks and one day without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
