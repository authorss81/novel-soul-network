# Chapter 489 — The Thirty-Fourth Sitting, Six Items At Four Minutes, And Nothing Said To Her Face

**The Wednesday of week one hundred and eighty was the thirty-fourth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and the one thing that is true of it is that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record in one of the four towns was in the room and said six items' worth of other things and said nothing at all about a figure of about nine people that she had told a room of about nine people in her own town in about a fortnight, and the book stood at fifty-six lines when the morning began and it stands at fifty-six lines now, the tin did not move, the count came out at thirty-nine of which thirty-four correspond, and the ask stands at four hundred and seventy-six days, which is sixty-eight weeks, counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since, on that Wednesday.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about two and a half and then a room from about half past six, and he was not in the room in Saltmarket and has never been in that room. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook laid out is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and eighty-six days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and ninety days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a book of four columns on a table in a room above a line in Saltmarket is a different object from a rail of about forty card slots in a shop in another district, and nothing in this entry joins them.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and they came down at about eleven, and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

**There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. It did not move in about four hours. Nobody in that room looked at it more than once, and its mover is not named and is not going to be named.**

There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, open for about four hours and not shut once.

There were about eleven people, and about four of them are not described this morning, and there was one who had not been in that room before.

**The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record in the second of the four towns was in that room for the second time. She came in on the bus on the Tuesday and goes out on it on the Thursday. She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.**

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece, and all six of them are the kind of thing that has happened at every sitting for nineteen years, and that is not a change and is not a pattern and is not a rule and is not a judgement and is not in anybody's mouth.

**None of the six was a thing about about nine people, and none of the six was a thing about a figure of about nine, and nobody in that room asked her about a figure of about nine, and she did not offer one.**

She had told a room of about nine people in her own town, about a fortnight ago, that a man in this city had said a room of about nine people was about nine. She had said it out loud in that room. About four of the nine had heard all of it and about four had heard some of it and nobody wrote any of it down.

**She came into this city on the Tuesday afternoon bus with that number in her head and she sat in that room for about an hour on the Wednesday morning and said nothing to the woman who holds the room about the number at all, and the reason is not that she was hiding it and the reason is not that she was sorry and the reason is that she had nothing to say to her face about it, and that is the whole of it.**

Nobody in that room knew that. About four of the about eleven noticed that she said less than the last time and about four did not notice and about four noticed something else entirely and have not said what.

**The book was at fifty-six lines when the morning began and it is at fifty-six lines now, and no line went in, and nobody in that room put anything in it and nobody in that room could have.**

Nobody asked her for a name. Nobody asked her which town. Nobody asked her about the room of about nine people, and she was not asked, and she did not say, and she did not say it afterwards either, in the room or on the bus or in this city at any point in those two days.

The count went up by one. It goes up by one at every sitting whether the book opens or not, and thirty-four of the thirty-nine correspond to the thirty-four Wednesdays this book holds, and five of them predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody and has never been asked about twice.

**No number was given into the tin this morning, and none has been given into it on any day since the year began, and the tin stands at seventy-three, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved.**

**Nobody asked her about turning one figure into another.** She has not given that in nineteen years and nobody in that room has asked her for it in nineteen years, and nothing was put in the space that question leaves and nothing was offered in its place.

**Nobody said anything to her about the count. The count is announced and it is not discussed, and nobody in that room said a word to the woman of about thirty-four about the count or about the book or about the figure of about nine or about anything else except the six items.**

**Nobody thanked her. She was not thanked. She did not wait to be thanked and did not look round the room afterwards, and about four of the about eleven have said since that it was a lot to ask of somebody and none of them has said it to her, and about four others have said that she came a long way for it and neither group has spoken to her.**

**She sat down afterwards on the chair at the end of the curve, which is not the chair against the wall, and she stayed for the rest of the morning, and when the shutters came down at about eleven she went out first.**

---

That same woman said the nine lines out loud in the third of the four towns about a fortnight ago, to about four people, and a man of about thirty-three who works at a weighbridge wrote them out on the back of a hire sheet, and she said nine days in the fourth of the nine, and she has never been asked about the fourth line by anybody in this city and was not asked on the Wednesday.

**Nobody in that room knew that three rooms in three towns have each had the same nine lines said out loud in them by the same woman in about a fortnight, and that one of the four rooms came out of the four about a week ago in about nine words, and that about four people lost something by it, and that one of the four who lost something named a cost out loud in a kitchen and was not thanked.**

---

He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at that counter until about half past two and then the rest of the day was not a counter, and nobody in the building in Saltmarket knows that a man of twenty-two spent that morning at a bench, and nobody at that bench knows that a book was open on a table for about four hours in another district, and the two facts have never been in the same sentence in front of either man.

About four people came to that counter between about two and about half past two and about four of them were told no to something. Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for fifty-five weeks and six days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for fifty-nine weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**Nobody in that room knew that a woman who had been in a room above a line in another district at about eleven that morning said nothing, and nobody in that room has ever been told how many lines are in that book, and the cook of twenty-seven at that sink was not asked and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked.**

*The four copies, as a record of four objects in four towns on which nobody has written a name.* **What they are: nine lines written out by hand by four people, on the back of a timetable in a school in the fourth of the four towns by a man of about thirty-one who works at a school gate, on the side of a chopping board in a kitchen in the second by a woman of about forty-three who runs that kitchen, on the back of a hire sheet in a hall in the third by a man of about thirty-three who works at a weighbridge, and on the back of a rent book in a shop in the first by a man of about forty-seven who keeps that rent book. Whether anybody has signed any of them: nobody, and the four are on nothing. Owner: four objects, and nobody.**

**AND WHAT IS NOT IN THAT RECORD AND IS NOT ON ANYTHING ELSE EITHER: the sheet the nine lines were said off is a sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts of this city, it has no name on any of the nine, one of the nine is a refusal, nothing has been adopted and nothing has been signed, and it is not one of the four copies and it is not on the back of a timetable or a chopping board or a hire sheet or a rent book, and the four people who copied the nine lines out have never seen it and have never been told where it is.**
