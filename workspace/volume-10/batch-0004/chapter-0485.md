# Chapter 485 — The Ask At Four Hundred And Sixty-Seven Days, Which Is The Day A Heating Went Off, And A Woman Who Says She Was Refused And Was Not

**The Monday of week one hundred and seventy-nine was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that the ask stands at four hundred and sixty-seven days, which is sixty-six weeks and five days, counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since, and four hundred and sixty-seven is the day a heating in a building in this city went off in an earlier year, and the figure here is an age and not a date, and the heating in this case is a closed thing with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it, and a room in the third of the four towns has come out of the four, and about four people have lost something by it and nobody has improved on them and nobody thanked them.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and a room in a town four days of bus travel away that has come out of something is not a room in this city and nothing in this entry joins it to one. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook laid out is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and seventy-seven days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and eighty-one days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a room in a town is not a room and a rail is not a room and the two are in different buildings about four days of bus travel apart and nothing in this entry joins them.

---

**The ask is four hundred and sixty-seven days old, and four hundred and sixty-seven is the day a heating in a building in this city went off.**

**Those are the two halves of one number and they are not the same thing. The first is an age: it is how long a question has been open, counted from the day it was asked in front of about nine people and never from any sitting since. The second is a day: an evening in an earlier year when a heating in a set of rooms in this city went off and stayed off until a landlord put it on.**

**Four hundred and sixty-seven is the age of the ask and it is also the day the heating went off, and the figure here is an age and not a date, and a person holding it is holding a question about a room and a winter in a building and is holding both at once.**

**The heating in this case is a closed thing. It went off and a landlord put it on, in that order, and the last figure of it was printed in an earlier year and is not printed again, and there is no figure of days off for it anywhere in this case, and that is not in anything anybody said, not in a record, not on a form, and not on a wall. There is no column for it and there is not going to be one.**

The other thirteen figures in this entry are the room, the card, the hold, the man of about fifty-one, the nine hand copies, and the eight lines on the hardboard, and none of those thirteen is four hundred and sixty-seven on this day and none of them is a day the heating went off.

**A hot-water cylinder in a building in the second of the four districts is not that heating. A heating in a school in the first of the four towns that went off on a Wednesday is not that heating. A Friday afternoon in another district is not that heating, and a bus that takes most of a day is not that heating, and a fortnight a cook of twenty-seven laid out and nobody repaid is not that fortnight, and the two are not on the same sheet and no sentence in this case may put them there.**

---

The third of the four towns has a room in it that four people use after a shift and that nobody has ever named, and on the Monday morning a woman of about thirty-four who works in that town told about four people in it that the room was not going to be one of the four, and she said it once and she gave no reason and she was not asked for one and about four of the four said nothing and about nine people in that town have heard about it since and about four of those nine have said she was wrong to do it and about four have said she had her reasons and neither group has asked her and she has not said.

**She had said the nine lines out loud in that room about a fortnight ago and one of the four wrote them out afterwards, and she is the same woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record and she has now said the nine lines in three of the four towns and taken one of the four out, and nobody in any of the four towns has been told either fact about the other three.**

About four people lose something by it and the four are not in this city. One of them is a man of about fifty-two who drove a van to that room on the Thursday and had a lift back from it and has about nine miles of walking in the dark to find out this week. One is a woman of about thirty-eight who works nights and used that room to sit in at about four in the afternoon between shifts and now has about four hours of a building with nothing on the walls in it. One is a man of about forty-five who reads meters in that town and had put a chair in the corner of that room for people to wait in. One is a man of about thirty-seven who has been eating there on a Thursday for about two years and has not said so to anybody.

**Nobody in that room went to a different room and told them what had happened. About four of the four have said since that they would not go and find another one, and about four have said nothing, and nobody in that town has gone and asked whether they are all still going.**

**The one who has been eating there on a Thursday for about two years found out on the Saturday and has not been back, and nobody asked him why and he has not said, and about four people in that town know about the Thursday and about four do not, and the two groups have never spoken about it and are not going to.**

**Nobody has replaced the four with anybody. There is no fifth room, no sixth, no waiting list and nobody waiting to be told, and about nine people in that town have said that if a room wants to be one of four there is a form, and there is no form, and the nine have not been shown where the form is because there is not one.**

Nobody improved on them and nobody thanked them and nobody in that room has said she did the right thing and nobody in that room has said she did the wrong thing and she did not ask and was not asked.

**This is what it is and it is not a vote and nobody took a count and there is no list of the four and no list of the three and no minute and no form, and the room that came out is still a room and still lets four people sit in it and the woman who said it is still a person who keeps the only written record in a town four days of bus travel away.**

She said it standing up, which about four people in that room have said since she does not do, and she did not put her bag down while she said it, and about four of the four noticed the bag and about four did not, and the four who noticed have not mentioned it to anybody and the four who did not have not been told.

**There is a kettle in that room and a table with a burn ring on it and about nine chairs that do not match each other, and the woman of about thirty-four had been in that room about nine times in about a year and had not written a line down in it on any of the nine, and about four people in that town have said that she keeps records and about nine have said that she is the last person anybody would expect to be the one who says no, and the two things are both true and the room has not asked her about either.**

Nobody in that room has ever been told that the same nine lines were said out loud in three other towns in about a fortnight by the same mouth, and she has not said so and has not been asked and is not going to be told, and about four people in that town know that she goes in and out of this city and about nine know where she goes and about four have asked her and about four have not.

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and none of the four had been in that room the week before and he did not ask any of them about it and has not asked any of them since.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for fifty-four weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for fifty-eight weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**Nobody in that room knew that a room in a town four days of bus travel away had come out of four rooms in about nine words on a Monday morning, and about four people there have lost a lift and a chair and an hour of a building and a Thursday meal, and nobody in this city has the names of any of them and this case has not gone and asked for them.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
