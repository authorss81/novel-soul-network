# Chapter 483 — Two Figures That Are Each Other's Days, And A Third Copy On The Back Of A Hire Sheet

**The Thursday of week one hundred and seventy-eight was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that two figures stand on the same day and each of them is the day the other object was written, and the thirteen lines on the hardboard in a room off that service road are six hundred and forty-four days old, and six hundred and forty-four is the day the eighteen lines were put on that board, and the eighteen lines are four hundred and ninety-one days old, and four hundred and ninety-one is the day the thirteenth line was written, and a third copy of the nine lines was made in a hall in the third of the four towns and in its fourth line the figure is right, and nobody has compared it with either of the other two.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and a hall at the back of a building in the third of the four towns is not a room in this city and nothing in this entry joins it to one. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and seventy-three days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and seventy-seven days old, and that figure is not the day anything on the hardboard was written and is not the day the room opened, and the hardboard and the rail are two surfaces in two buildings and nothing in this entry joins them.

---

**Two figures stand on this Thursday and each of them is the day the other object was written, and here is which is which about each of the two, because a person reading them in a row will be holding both at once.**

**The thirteen lines on the hardboard in the room off that service road are six hundred and forty-four days old. Six hundred and forty-four is the day the eighteen lines were written on that same board, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

**The eighteen lines on that board are four hundred and ninety-one days old. Four hundred and ninety-one is the day the thirteenth line was written, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

Those are two halves of two figures and neither of them is a mistake, and a person holding both at once is holding an age about a board and a day about the same board, and the same board in both.

**Nobody in this case has ever printed these two on one line before, and this is the first time, and a person who has read this cannot put the two figures down separately again.**

The other six lines on that board are six hundred and ninety-three, six hundred and nine, five hundred and eighty-eight, five hundred and sixty-three, five hundred and forty-five and four hundred and sixty-nine, and none of those six is the day any of the four lines above was written and none of them is a day the heating in this city went off or was put on.

**A card in a rail is not a hardboard. A hardboard is not a room. A room is not a question. Nobody in this case may put two of the four in one clause, and the record below names them one at a time for that reason.**

The room off that service road is seven hundred and seventy-three days old and three hundred and fifty-eight is the day the card went into the rail and neither of those two figures is on the same line as either of the two above, and about four people in this city who have been in both buildings have never put any of the four together.

---

The third of the four towns has a hall at the back of a building and the hall is let to anybody who asks for it and the keys for it hang in a kitchen in that town and about four people in that town know where the keys are.

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record said the nine lines out loud for the third time in about a fortnight on the Thursday morning, in that hall, to about four people, and a man of about thirty-three who works at the weighbridge in that town wrote them out afterwards on the back of a hire sheet with a biro on about nine minutes.

**He wrote the fourth of the nine with the figure in it the way it had been said and neither of the other two people who have those nine lines written down in a different town has ever been asked what they wrote in that line, and the figure in this copy is nine days and it is the same figure as the copy on the back of a timetable in the fourth of the four towns and neither of those two people has ever seen this one.**

Nobody in that hall wrote anything down while she was saying them and nobody in that hall has ever counted anybody and about four people in that town have heard nine lines and about nine have not.

**Nobody in that town knows that the same nine lines are on the back of a timetable in a school four days of bus travel away and on the side of a chopping board in a kitchen four days of bus travel the other way, and there are three copies now and about nine people have seen one of them and about four of those nine are the same four people and nobody in any of the three places has been told that there are three.**

**The man of about thirty-three who works at the weighbridge put the hire sheet back on the table it came off and squared it against the edge, which is a thing he does with everything that comes through that office, and about four people in that town have said that he is careful and about nine have not said anything about it.**

He has weighed about nine hundred loads in that town and has never once written down how many people were in any of the places the loads came from, and he was not asked on the Thursday and he has not been asked since, and the nine lines he wrote out that morning are the first thing he has ever copied out in his own hand for anybody.

**Nobody in that hall has ever asked him whether the nine lines are the same as any other nine lines, and he would not know, and the woman of about thirty-four who said them out loud has not told him and has not been asked and is not going to be told.**

The hire sheet went back on the table it came off and the keys went back on the hook in the kitchen and nobody in that town has a list of what has been said in that hall and about four people in that town have said that the hall is the only room in it anybody can book, and about nine have said nothing.

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and none of the four was talking about anything when he came in or when he went.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Thursday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for fifty-four weeks and one day. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for fifty-eight weeks without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

The tool bag is at his feet and there is a folded cloth in it and a bar in it and about nine other things, and about four people in that room have looked in it over about a year and about four have not, and he has not opened it in front of anybody in this case in any of the weeks he has been in it.

**Nobody in that room knew that six hundred and forty-four and four hundred and ninety-one were both on the same day, and neither of those two figures is a day anything happened to the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door, and she was not asked about either of them and would not have known what they were about.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The two figures, as a record of two ages that are each other's days.* **What they are, first: the age in days of the thirteen lines on a hardboard in a room off a service road, six hundred and forty-four. Second: the age in days of the eighteen lines on that same hardboard, four hundred and ninety-one. Which of them is also a day: both, being six hundred and forty-four the day the eighteen lines were written on that board and four hundred and ninety-one the day the thirteenth line was written on it. Whether the two objects are the same object: yes, and that is what makes this row the hard one, and a person reading either figure is holding an age about a board and a day the same board was written, and the two halves cannot be put down separately. Whether either figure is a day the heating in this city went off or was put on: no, and four hundred and ninety-one is the day a line of promise went onto a board in a room off a service road and nothing else. Whether the other six lines collide with either: no, and they are six hundred and ninety-three, six hundred and nine, five hundred and eighty-eight, five hundred and sixty-three, five hundred and forty-five and four hundred and sixty-nine. Whether anybody had ever put these two on one line before: no, and this is the first time and it is a record and not a conversation. Owner: a board on two nails, and nobody.**

*The third hand copy, as a record of nine lines written on a hire sheet with the right figure in the fourth one.* **Where: a hall at the back of a building in the third of the four towns, on the Thursday morning, a hall that is let to anybody who asks for it and whose keys hang in a kitchen in that town. Who wrote it: a man of about thirty-three who works at the weighbridge in that town, afterwards and in about nine minutes, with a biro. What the fourth line says in his hand: that a room that has not sent anything in nine days has not said no. Whether that is the figure that was said out loud in that hall: yes, and it is the same figure as the copy on the back of a timetable in the fourth of the four towns, and the two have never been compared and neither man knows the other exists. Which of the three copies has a different figure: the one on the side of a chopping board in a kitchen in the second of the four towns, which carries four days, and about three people have noticed on their own and about six have not, and nobody in this hall has been told. Whether anything was written down while the nine lines were being said: no. Whether anybody in that hall has counted anybody: no, and about four people in that town have heard the nine lines and about nine have not. Owner: a hall, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Thursday, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts of this city have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody. The four rooms in four towns have still not been introduced to each other, and three of them have now had the same nine lines said out loud in them by the same woman in about a fortnight, and no two of the four has been told that the others have. Nine hand copies of the front of a page with about eleven lines in it are in their forty-ninth week and about eight of the nine are unfinished, and the first disagreement in the fourth line of those has not been found, and a figure of days in the fourth line of a chopping board or a hire sheet or a timetable is a different fourth line and is not the same disagreement and is not compared with any of them anywhere. Nine keys are needed and two exist, and the first of the nine still cannot be asked back. Nine doors on a road on the far side of this city are still hanging by one hinge and no wage pays for a mender, and a week that about eleven people lost has not come back to them, and a bar across the inside of a door in each of four buildings in four towns is on nothing and appears in no list of anything. A printed form is on about nine tables in this city and its second column is empty in every one of them. The fortnight a cook laid out is still on nobody's account.
