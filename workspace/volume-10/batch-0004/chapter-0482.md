# Chapter 482 — The Second Copy, Written On A Kitchen Side, With The Figure In The Fourth Line Transposed

**The Wednesday of week one hundred and seventy-eight was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record in the second of the four towns said the same nine lines out loud a second time, to about four people in a kitchen in that same town, and a woman of about forty-three who runs that kitchen with a bed in the back of it wrote the nine out by hand on the side of a chopping board in about nine minutes afterwards, and in the fourth of the nine she wrote a figure that is not the figure, and about three people have noticed that on their own since and about six have not, and the two figures are four and nine and both of them are real numbers of days and nobody has put them side by side.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and a kitchen in a town four days of bus travel away is a room and is not this room and nobody in that kitchen has ever been told a thing about this one. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and seventy-two days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and seventy-six days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a chopping board with nine lines of handwriting on the side of it is a ninth object and is not the card and is not the hardboard and is not a timetable and is not the sheet.

---

**The kitchen in that town is the back half of a house on a road that goes nowhere anybody in this case has to be, and there is a table in it and about six chairs and a bed through a curtain and a kitchen boiler that works.**

**The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record was in that kitchen on the Wednesday evening at about half past seven with a cup in her hand and she said the nine lines out loud, in the same order, at the same speed, to about four people, and about three of the four heard all nine and about one came in half way through the fifth.**

Nobody in that kitchen had heard the nine before and nobody in that kitchen has heard them since, and the kitchen four days of bus travel away where the same nine lines were said on the Monday is in the fourth of the four towns and this kitchen is in the second and nobody has ever been in both.

She said the fourth of them and this is what she said:

**"A room that has not sent anything in nine days has not said no."**

And then she put her cup down and said the fifth and the sixth and the seventh and the eighth, and then the ninth, which is the refusal, and about four people in that kitchen heard all of it and nobody wrote anything down.

**Nobody in that kitchen has ever been asked where the nine lines came from and nobody asked and she did not say, and she said again that she was not going to write them out for anybody, and about four people in that kitchen have said since that she says that every time and about four have said she does not, and neither group has asked her.**

**There is a fridge in that kitchen with a magnet on the door and about nine things held up under it**, and one of the nine is a list of about four names in a hand that is not the woman of about thirty-four's and not the woman of about forty-three's, and nobody in that kitchen has ever asked whose hand that is, and about four people have looked at it every day for years and about four have not noticed it is there.

**That list is a list of about four people who eat there and it is on a fridge in one kitchen and it is not the four rooms in four towns and it is not on a wall and it is in no building but that one, and nobody in this case has asked whether the two are the same kind of thing and they are not.**

---

The woman of about forty-three who runs that kitchen does the cooking and the washing up and the books and she has a bed through a curtain and she is not owed anything by anybody in this case.

She wrote the nine out on the side of a chopping board with the back of a spoon handle because a pen had gone missing, and she wrote them in about nine minutes, and she did it after the woman of about thirty-four had gone because about four of the four said that was better and about one did not say anything.

**In the fourth of the nine she wrote four days and not nine days, and she wrote it in the same hand as the other eight and in the same line and with the same comma at the end, and the figure she wrote is a real number of days and the figure that was said is a real number of days and the two are not the same figure and no sentence in this case may make them the same figure.**

**Nobody has compared that chopping board with the timetable in the fourth of the four towns or with the sheet in the second of the four districts of this city, and nobody in either of those places knows the other exists.**

The chopping board is on a shelf above the kitchen boiler in a kitchen in the second of the four towns and about nine people in that town have seen the clean side of it and about four have seen the side with the writing on it and about nine have seen neither.

**About three people have noticed the figure in that fourth line is not the figure they were told, on their own, and about six have not, and all nine of them know the kitchen and not one of them has ever seen the other three.**

**The three are a man of about thirty-one who drives a minibus in that town**, a woman of about twenty-nine who works at a school there and is not a teacher, and the woman of about forty-three who wrote it. None of the three told either of the others, and the man of about thirty-one has said out loud once that a room which goes quiet for four days is a room that has gone, and the woman of about twenty-three who was in that kitchen has said that four days is a Tuesday and nobody has argued with either of them and nobody has corrected either of them.

**Nobody has said the figure out loud in that kitchen a second time in front of two people who were both listening, and that is the only reason the figure is still four, and it is not a decision anybody took and it is not a rule and nobody in that kitchen knows it is the reason.**

---

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record was in this city on the Wednesday morning and was in her own town on the Wednesday night and was in this city again on the Friday, and nobody in this city asked her anything on the Wednesday morning and the man at the counter in Lattice Ward was behind the bench and did not look up.

Nobody told the man at that counter that nine lines had been said out loud twice in four days of bus travel away and that a second copy existed, and he has not been told, and he was not on a rota for it and would not have been told if he had been.

**Nobody in that kitchen in the second of the four towns knows that a man of about thirty-one who works at a school gate has the same nine lines on the back of a timetable in the fourth of the four towns, and nobody in that school corridor knows that a woman of about forty-three who runs a kitchen has them on the side of a chopping board, and the two of those are about four days of bus travel apart and the bus goes twice a week.**

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the middle one filled.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for fifty-four weeks. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for fifty-seven weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
