# Chapter 481 — Nine Lines Said Out Loud At The End Of A School Corridor, And One Hand Copy On The Back Of A Timetable

**The Monday of week one hundred and seventy-eight was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record in one of the four towns said nine lines out loud at the end of a school corridor in the fourth of the four towns for the first time in this case, and about four people heard her say all nine, and one man of about thirty-one who works at a school gate wrote them out by hand on the back of a timetable in about nine minutes afterwards, and nobody asked her where she had got them and she did not say, and the sheet they came off is in a room in the second of the four districts of this city and is on nothing and is signed by nobody.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and nobody in that shop knew anything about a corridor in a school four days of bus travel away. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and seventy days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and seventy-four days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts of this city is a seventh object and is not the card and is not the wall and is not the page and is not the list, and the copy on the back of a timetable in the fourth of the four towns is an eighth and is not any of them either.

---

The room at the end of that corridor has about nine chairs in it and a table and a wall with nothing on it, and the door is not locked and has never been locked, and about four people in that school know the room is used and about nine do not.

There were about four of them in it on the Monday evening at about seven, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record in the second of the four towns had come in on the bus that morning and had gone out on the bus on the Wednesday and had about two days in this city in her.

**She said them once and she said all nine and she said them slowly, and she said them from memory, and about four people in that room heard every word of them and nobody wrote anything down while she was saying them.**

She said, and this is the fourth of the nine:

**"A room that has not sent anything in nine days has not said no."**

She said, and this is the ninth of the nine:

**"Nobody in a room has to be told who lets a thing be left in it."**

**The ninth of the nine is a refusal and it was put there by a man of about forty-two who fits kitchens, who turned it down in about four seconds in a room in another district of this city, gave no reason, made the not-giving the condition of it, was not improved on and was not thanked, and nobody in that corridor four days of bus travel away has ever been told any of that and would not have understood it.**

Nobody asked her where the nine lines came from and she was not asked and she did not say, and about four of the four repeated one word back to each other afterwards and about four said nothing, and the woman of about thirty-four said that she was not going to write them out for anybody and that if anybody wanted them they were to write them out themselves.

**That is the whole of how this happened. A woman who would not write nine lines down said nine lines out loud four times in four towns over about a fortnight, and each time about four people heard her, and each time one of the four wrote them out afterwards on the back of whatever was on that table.**

There is a chair in that room with a split in the seat and a strip of tape over the split that somebody put there and nobody has ever taken off, and the woman of about thirty-four sat on the good side of it because she has sat on that side of it before and because the bad side is nearer the door and she does not sit nearer doors in rooms she has been in once.

She had her hands flat on the table in front of her the whole time and she did not look at them and she did not use them to count, and about four people in that room have said since that she counted the nine on her fingers under the table, and she has not said whether that is true, and about four other people in that room have said that she did not, and the two groups have not spoken to each other about it.

**Nobody in that room has ever been asked how many people are in it, and nobody in that room has ever counted, and the room has a wall with nothing on it and has had one since before any of this and a wall with nothing on it is not a list.**

The door of that room has glass in the top half of it and it is frosted from about waist height up and clear below that, and anybody in the corridor can see shoes and nobody in the corridor can see faces, and about four people in that school have said that this is deliberate and about nine have not thought about it.

**There is a radiator under the window in that room that has never worked and there is a plug in the wall by the table that has never been used, and about four people in that room have said one of those two things is a thing somebody should see to and about four have not, and none of them has seen to either and nobody has rung anybody about it and nobody in this case went and looked.**

**She finished at about half past seven and put her coat on before anybody had said anything about how long she had been there, and the man of about thirty-one who works at a school gate was the only one who stood up, and he stood up because she was going and not because of anything else, and about four people in that room noticed that and about four did not.**

**Nobody in that room has written down that it happened and nobody in that town knows that about three other rooms in three other towns have heard the same nine lines from the same mouth, and no two of the four rooms knows any of the other three exists, and nobody has introduced them and nobody is going to.**

---

**The man of about thirty-one who works at a school gate opens that gate at about seven in the morning and closes it at about half past four and he was in that room because he eats in that building and he had come in out of the rain.**

He wrote them out on the back of a timetable, and the timetable was a week of that gate's on the back of which was a grid, and he wrote the nine lines in a column down the grid in about nine minutes, and he wrote the fourth of them with a comma at the end of it and did not notice the comma.

**Nobody told him it was a copy. Nobody told him it was not a copy. Nobody asked him afterwards to read it back and he has not been asked, and it is on the back of a timetable in a pocket of a coat on a hook in a corridor in a school in the fourth of the four towns, and about four people have seen the front of that timetable and about nine have not seen either side.**

He wrote the ninth of the nine last and he wrote it slower than the other eight, and nobody has ever asked him why and he has never said.

---

The man of about thirty-four who loads bread was in the shop on the Monday at about half past three and he bought a roll of tape and he did not mention the Thursday and nobody asked him.

**Nobody at that counter has ever asked him whether the Thursday he agreed to run once went, and he has not said, and about four people at that counter have noticed that he comes in and buys one thing now and has not bought one thing in about nine weeks before that, and one of the four has worked out that the weeks he stopped buying are not the same weeks as anything anybody has said in that room, and has not said so to anybody and is not going to.**

The drawer under the till was not opened and the form in the envelope is still on about nine tables in about four places in this city and its second column is empty in every one of them, and the man of about thirty-seven who drove to an open afternoon with nothing in his hands has not been asked to do anything about it and is not on anybody's list and is not going to be.

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the middle one filled.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for fifty-three weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for fifty-seven weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

The tool bag is at his feet and it has a folded cloth in it and a bar in it and about nine other things, and nobody in that room has ever asked him what is in it.

**Nobody in that room knew that nine lines had been said out loud at the end of a corridor four days of bus travel away, and nobody in that room has ever heard of the sheet they were said off, and the sheet is in a room in the second of the four districts of this city and has been there for about a year and a half and has no name on any of the nine and one of the nine is a refusal and nothing has been adopted and nothing has been signed and it is on nothing.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
