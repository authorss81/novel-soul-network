# Chapter 491 — Three Rooms, Three Things, And Nobody Who Wrote Anything Down

**The Monday of week one hundred and eighty-one was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that three people in a room about four days of bus travel away read out loud, one after another and not in the same breath, the three things that nine lines they had each copied out by hand come to, and the three things are that the three of them will work to one standard, that any one of them can stop on its own without asking the other two, and that whoever holds a week when something goes wrong holds it for four weeks and hands it on, and nobody wrote any of the three down, nobody signed anything, and the nine lines are on four different things in four towns and one of those four towns is not one of the three any more, and the hardboard on two nails in a room off a service road in this city is sixteen lines five hundred and eighty-one days old, and five hundred and eighty-one is a day in an earlier year when a landlord put a heating on in a building two streets from that counter, and the figure in this entry is an age and not a date.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and three people in a kitchen about four days of bus travel away are three people in a kitchen and are not the people at that counter and nothing in this entry joins them. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook laid out is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and ninety-one days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and ninety-five days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and nine lines on the side of a chopping board in a kitchen four days of bus travel away are not the card and are not on the rail and are not in the rail and have never been near it.

---

**The sixteen lines on that board are five hundred and eighty-one days old, and five hundred and eighty-one is a day in an earlier year when a landlord put a heating on.**

Those are the two halves of one figure and they are not the same kind of thing. The first is an age, and it is how long sixteen lines have been on a hardboard on two nails in a room off a service road in this city, the sixteenth of them being a woman of about sixty-eight's. The second is a day, and it is an evening in an earlier year when a heating in a set of rooms two streets from that counter came on and stayed on.

**The figure here is an age and not a date. The heating in this case is a closed thing with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it, and the last figure of that heating was printed in an earlier year and is not printed again, and nothing anybody said in any room about it is in this entry, and no sentence in this case may put a board and a winter on the same sheet.**

A hot-water cylinder in a building in the second of the four districts of this city is not that heating. A heating in a school in one of the four towns that went off on a Wednesday is not that heating. A Friday afternoon in another district is not that heating, and a bus that takes most of a day is not that heating, and the fortnight a cook of twenty-seven laid out and nobody repaid is not that fortnight, and there are five things in this case that are not that heating and no two of them is the other one.

The other figures on that board on this Monday are seven hundred and eleven for the twelve lines, six hundred and sixty-two for the thirteen, six hundred and twenty-seven for the fourteen, six hundred and six for the fifteen, five hundred and sixty-three for the seventeen, five hundred and nine for the eighteen and four hundred and eighty-seven for the nineteen, and none of those seven is a day anything happened to a heating in this city, and the nineteen is the only line on that board that has ever had a zero and it has none now.

---

The second of the four towns has a kitchen that runs in the evenings and about nine chairs that do not match each other, and a woman of about forty-three who runs it and a woman of about thirty-six who helps on a Tuesday and a man of about thirty-five who comes in at about ten most nights and has never once been asked to leave earlier.

Three of them were in it on the Monday evening at about seven, and they had about nine lines between them and no two of the nine lines was on the same thing, and one of the three had written hers out on a chopping board in that kitchen in about nine minutes with the back of a spoon handle and had not read it back since.

**Nobody in that kitchen has ever been told that there are four of them. Two of the three knew there was another kitchen with the same nine lines on something in it, and the third did not, and nobody has said which.**

The woman of about forty-three read the first of the three out loud. She said that the three of them would work to one standard, and that she meant a standard they could all say out loud in a sentence and not a standard written down anywhere, and nobody asked her what the sentence was and she did not give one.

**"We will not be different from each other and we will not be the same as each other, and that is a thing you can say out loud twice without writing it."**

The man of about thirty-five read the second of the three out loud, and he read it the way a man reads something he has thought about on a walk and not the way a man reads something off a page, and it took him about nine seconds and he got to the end of it.

**"Any one of us can stop on its own. Not the other two, not her, not anybody. We do not have to tell the other two first and we do not have to give a reason and nobody in this kitchen is going to be asked for one."**

The woman of about thirty-six read the third of the three out loud, and she read it with the cloth in her hand and she stopped twice in the middle of it.

**"Whoever has the week holds it for four weeks and then it moves. It goes in a drawer and it comes out again when the four weeks are up, and it is not on a wall and it is not a rota and it is not anybody's job."**

**Nobody wrote any of the three down. Nobody signed any of the three. Nobody photographed any of the three, and nobody asked whether they wanted it photographed, and about four people in that town have said since that the three should be written down and about nine have said nothing, and the three have not been shown where the paper would go because there is no paper.**

Nobody in that kitchen has ever been asked how many of them there are, and nobody in that kitchen has counted, and one of the three has said out loud that if anybody counts them then it is a list and it is not a list, and about four people in that town have heard her say that and about four have not.

**Nobody in that kitchen said the word covenant and nobody in that kitchen has been asked whether what the three of them are doing is one.**

The man of about thirty-five put his cup down after he had read the second of the three and he did not say anything else for about nine minutes, and about four of the three have said since that he looked like a man who had expected to be asked something and was not, and about four have said he looked like a man who was glad, and neither group has asked him which it was.

**About four of the about nine in that kitchen have said since that a woman who washes up there has been in that kitchen for about two years and has never once been asked her name by anybody who has read what she said on the Wednesday, and about four have said the same about the man who comes in at about ten, and neither of those two has been told that anybody said anything.**

The woman of about thirty-six who helps on a Tuesday took the cloth off the table when she had finished the third of the three and folded it and put it in a pocket, and nobody asked her whether she was going to write any of it down, and she did not, and about four people in that town have said that she is the one who would have and about four have said she is the one who would not, and the two have never spoken about it.

**Nobody in that kitchen has ever counted who comes, and the door has a hook on it with about four coats on it and about four of the coats belong to people who come every night and about four belong to people who have not come for a week, and nobody in that kitchen has ever asked the second set why.**

---

Nobody in this case has ever asked him to read three things out loud in a room, and nobody in this case has ever put three rooms in three towns in one sentence and called them anything, and he was at a bench in Lattice Ward at about seven that evening and about four miles of it on foot in the dark with a bag and he did not know that three things had been read out loud about four days of bus travel away.

**One of the three was asked, by a person who has not been named to anybody, whether the three of them were a thing that could be joined to anything. He said no in about four seconds, gave no reason, and made the not-giving the condition of it, and nobody improved on him and nobody thanked him, and about four people in that room have said since that it was quick and about four have said he should have said why, and neither group has asked him.**

That is the whole of what he did with three rooms on the Monday.

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the middle one of the four filled and went out again inside about nine minutes.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for fifty-six weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for sixty weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody in that room knew that three things had been read out loud about four days of bus travel away, and nobody in that room had ever been told how many rooms there are, and the cook of twenty-seven at that sink was not asked and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked.

**Nobody in that room knew that on a Monday evening in a kitchen in the second of the four towns a man of about thirty-five had said out loud that any one of three people could stop on their own without giving a reason, and about four people in this city who would have agreed with him have never been asked what they would do if they were one of three, and this case has not gone and asked them.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
