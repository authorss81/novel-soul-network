# Chapter 496 — Three Rooms Say Three Things, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Five Says A Fourth Thing Nobody Asked Her To Say

**The Wednesday of week one hundred and eighty-two was a bench until about two, a counter until about half past two, and then a room, and the one thing that is true of it is that the same three things were said out loud in three rooms in three towns on one morning without any of the three knowing about the other two, and that a fourth thing was said once, by somebody nobody had asked.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about two and a half, and then a room from about half past six, and he was not in any of the three rooms and has never been in any of them. The rate was at nine and ten and the counter was thin for the first two hours. **It is on, it is dearer, the four names on that lease are dearer, and the fortnight a cook laid out is still unpaid and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is eight hundred days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is eight hundred and four days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a form with two columns on it on a table in another district is neither the card nor the rail and nobody has ever put one in the other.

---

**Two figures stand on this Wednesday and each of them is the day the other object was written, and here is which is which about each of the two, because a person reading them in a row will be holding both at once and will not know which is which.**

**The sixteen lines on that board are five hundred and ninety days old. Five hundred and ninety is the day the seventeen lines were written on that same board, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

**The seventeen lines on that board are five hundred and seventy-two days old. Five hundred and seventy-two is the day the sixteen lines were written on it, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

Those are two halves of two figures and neither of them is a mistake, and a person holding both at once is holding an age about a board and a day about the same board, and the same board in both, and the sixteen are a woman of about sixty-eight's and the seventeen are a clerk of forty-four's and neither of those two people is in this city and neither has been asked about anything this week.

The other six lines on that board are seven hundred and twenty, six hundred and seventy-one, six hundred and thirty-six, six hundred and fifteen, five hundred and eighteen and four hundred and ninety-six, and none of those six is a day any of the four lines above was written and none of them is a day the heating in this city went off or was put on.

**The room off that service road is eight hundred days old and three hundred and fifty-eight is the day the card went into the rail, and neither of those two figures is on the same line as either of the two above, and about four people in this city who have been in both buildings have never put any of the four together.**

---

The proposal was on the table in the office above a fourth shop from the Wednesday of the previous week and it had to be answered by nine on the Friday morning, and nobody answered it, and nobody wrote a letter about it, and nobody rang anybody about it, and at nine on the Friday morning it was still on the table with the second column empty.

**Nobody in that office had told the three rooms in three towns that the proposal was on that table, and two of the three rooms had been to that office and neither had been told there was a proposal on it, and the third had not been there at all and was not told anything.**

Nobody answered it and nobody wrote a letter about it and nobody sent anybody to ask about it, and at nine on the Friday morning it was still on the table with the second column empty, and the woman of about thirty-nine who lets that office came in at about half past eight and looked at it and put two cups down and did not move it.

**The proposal was the whole of a private answer to a question three rooms had not been asked, and it was refused by nobody and beaten by nothing and it went in a drawer on the Friday morning because nobody was in the room, and about four people in that building have said since that nobody refused it and about four have said that not being in the room is a kind of refusing, and neither group has been asked and neither group will be.**

At about seven on the Wednesday morning the three of them said the three things, in three rooms, in three towns, in the same week, and none of them knew the other two were doing it, and the reason they all said them on the same morning is not a reason anybody agreed on and is not a pattern and has not been put to anybody as one.

**Nobody counted. Nobody took a vote. Nobody wrote a minute, and nobody in any of the three towns has been told that the other two said the same three things on the same morning, and about four people in each of the three towns have said since that something is going on, and none of the three has asked whether the other two said anything.**

The deadline passed at nine and the folder with the nine forms went into a drawer in an office above a fourth shop, and the drawer has a lock on it and the key to that lock is on a hook inside the door, and about four people in that building have said out loud that a room you can lock and then leave the key inside it is not locked in a way that matters, and none of the four said it to a person who could have had the hook moved.

**The offer was a takeover that was never completed, and it was not completed because nobody filled in a column, and about four people in four towns have said out loud in about four rooms over about a fortnight that a thing that answers in about nine minutes and a thing that answers in about nine seconds are not two things, and none of the four has ever heard the other three say it, and none of the four was in any of these three rooms, and this entry does not join them to a form or to a drawer or to a week that passed at nine.**

---

**A woman of about twenty-five who washes up in the kitchen in the second of the four towns said a fourth thing on the Wednesday morning that nobody had asked her to say, and she said it once, and it was nine seconds long, and about four of the about nine in that kitchen heard all of it.**

**"Those three are three things you decided you could say," she said, with her hands in the water and not looking round. "There's a fourth thing. The fourth thing is that whoever holds the week can be anybody. Anybody at all. Somebody who's never been in this room and never copied a line off anything. And if that part's true then he's got to be able to be told. When something's gone wrong. And there's no way to tell him."**

**"No, there isn't," the woman of about thirty-six who helps on a Tuesday said.**

**"No," the woman of about twenty-five said, and dried her hands, and went back to the sink, and the woman of about thirty-six did not say it again and has not said it since, and the man of about thirty-five who comes in at about ten heard the end of it and not the beginning.**

She said that the three things were three things people who had decided could say, and that there was a fourth thing, and that the fourth thing was that the person holding the week could be anybody at all, including somebody who had never been in any of the three rooms and had never copied anything out, and that if that was going to be true then the person holding the week needed a way of being told when something had gone wrong, and there was not one.

The woman of about thirty-six had been one of the three who said the three things on the Monday, and the woman of about twenty-five had washed up on the Monday and had not been in the room when any of it was said, and nobody asked her afterwards what she made of it and nobody has asked her since, and about four people in that town know that the two of them are in the same kitchen most mornings and about four do not know that they are.

**Nobody improved on her. Nobody added anything. Nobody said she was right and nobody said she was wrong and about four of the about nine have said since that it was a good question and about four have said she should have said it to somebody and neither group has asked her and she has not said.**

Nobody in that kitchen has been told that the three things were said in two other towns on the same morning, and she has not been told, and about four people in that town know that there is more than one kitchen with the nine lines on something in it and about four do not, and neither group has been told what the other group knows.

**She is owed nothing. Nobody in this city knows her name. This case has not gone and asked for it, and nothing she said on the Wednesday has been carried anywhere, and the fourth thing she said is not on anything and is not on the three things and nobody has adopted it and nobody has written it out.**

---

He came in at about half past six, and there was nothing on that Wednesday he could have done anything about, and he left. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the middle one of the four was a man who had been in the room off that service road twice and had not been in about nine weeks and did not say so.

**He noticed the man's hands, which were the hands of somebody who does a thing with them, and the man noticed him noticing, and neither of them said anything about it, and it was the first time in a fortnight that anybody in that room had looked at him for longer than it took to get past.**

**On the way out he worked out that he had spent the day in three buildings and had been the only person in all three of them who was keeping a record of anything, and that the record had a column of his own that nobody had asked him for, and that he had added to it on the Wednesday as well.** He put his hand flat on the shutter for a second before he pulled it, the way you would on a door in a house you might have to come back to, and the shutter came down at about ten.

Nearest the door, in the chair that is always that chair, there was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread, and Wednesday was like the rest.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for fifty-eight weeks. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for sixty-one weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**Nobody in that room knew that three rooms in three towns had said the same three things in the same hour on the same morning, and nobody in that room has ever been told that a woman of about twenty-five who washes up in a kitchen said a fourth thing, and about four people in that room would have said the fourth thing themselves and about four would not and neither of those two groups has been asked.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
