# Chapter 495 — A Plumber And A Woman Who Reads Water Meters Find A Pattern In An Old Exchange

**The Monday of week one hundred and eighty-two was a bench until about two and a counter until about six and then a room, and the one thing that is true of it is that two people lifted a floor in a building on a road that goes nowhere on purpose and found something at the end of a cold pipe, and that about a fortnight later the same thing turned up in three other towns, and that none of the four has ever been connected to any of the others.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and a building on the far side of the road is not a building in this city and nothing in this entry joins them. The rate was at nine and ten and he had stopped expecting it to move. **Heating on, up again, four names on a lease up again. The fortnight that cook laid out is still on nobody's account and still unresolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and ninety-eight days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is eight hundred and two days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a floor in a building four days of bus travel away is a fourth place and the four of them are not in one room.

---

The building is on a road that goes nowhere anybody goes on purpose, and it has four rooms and a corridor and a set of steps down to a lower floor, and the lower floor has been shut since before either of the two people who went into it was working, and the key for it was under a mat at the top of the steps, and nobody has ever asked why a mat is there.

The floor that came up was two boards in the corridor and about nine square feet of concrete under it, and the man of about fifty-three who fits plumbing had gone in to look at a cold water line that nobody in that building has had on since before the year began, and the woman of about thirty-five who reads meters had gone with him because she reads the meters in that building and always has.

**Under the concrete, at about two feet down, there is a length of iron pipe with a plate of iron with four bolts in it at each end, and the plate is fastened on one side and not the other, and the two plates are fastened in the same order, and the pipe goes out through the wall and into the ground.**

**Neither of them had seen one before. He had fitted one in a different building about nine years ago and had thought nothing of it and had not told anybody, and she had not seen one at all, and neither of them said a word about it on the Monday and neither of them has said a word about it since to anybody in this case.**

**"That's a plate," she said, from the step.**

**"That's a plate."**

**"I know it's a plate. I read meters. I've never had one of them come up out of a floor in my life."**

**"I've fitted one," he said. "Different building, about nine years back. I put it in and I thought nothing of it and I never told anybody, and I've been standing here since about ten o'clock trying to remember what I actually know about it."**

**"Do you want to photograph it?"**

**"No," he said, and then said, "Yes," and then took a phone with a cracked corner out of his pocket and looked at it for about four seconds and put it back, and neither of them has said why and the meter woman has said since that the four seconds were longer than four.**

There is a folded cloth in the tool bag of a man of about fifty-one in a room in this city and a bar and about nine other things in it, and nobody in that room has ever asked him what is in it, and this entry is not about that tool bag and nothing in it is joined to what two people found under a floor about four days of bus travel away.

**They were there about four hours. They did not take a photograph. They did not write it down. About nine people in that town have said since that two people were in that building on the Monday and about four have said what for, and nobody has been told where the plate is and both of them have said the same thing if anybody asks, which is that they were in there about the water.**

**Nobody in that town has asked what was under the floor, and nobody in this city has been told there was a floor, and the two of them have not said it to each other in anybody's hearing since the Monday, and about four people in that town have said that the plumber and the meter woman do not talk to each other and about four have said that they do, and neither group has asked.**

**The key for the lower floor is still under the mat at the top of the steps, and the door is not locked, and about four people in that town have said the mat should go, and it is still there and nobody has ever asked why a mat is there.**

**Nobody in this case has ever told him any of it and he does not know it happened. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two on the Monday and at that counter from about two until about six and did not go near a road that goes nowhere anybody goes on purpose.**

---

About four days later, and this is a thing that is being told and not a thing anybody has done, the two of them found that the same plate is at the end of a pipe in a second building in a second town, and about a week after that in a third building in a third town, and then in a fourth.

**Each of the four is fastened in the same order, and each of the four goes out through a wall and into the ground, and the pipe in each of the four is cold, and none of the four has ever been used, and none of the four is connected to anything in any of the other three.**

Nobody asked the four people who own the four buildings why. The man of about fifty-three who fits plumbing asked one of them, in the first of the four towns, and the answer he got was that the building came that way, and he did not ask a second question and has not been asked one.

**The finding is a fact about plumbing. It is not a threat, it is not a pattern anybody has named, it is not in anybody's mouth, and about four people in that town have said it is interesting and nobody has said anything else about it.**

**About four people in the first of the four towns have asked the man of about thirty-eight who repairs refrigeration whether he has ever seen one, and he has not, and he has said so once and has not been asked again, and the woman of about thirty-five who reads meters has not been told that anybody asked anybody.**

Nobody carried it anywhere. Nobody wrote it down. Nobody put it in a list, and about four people in that town have said that if somebody wants to know about it they should ask the plumber, and the plumber has not told them and has not said no.

**Nobody in this case has ever asked what the plate does, and nobody in this case has ever said what it is for, and nobody in that town has either, and the two plates in the building on the road that goes nowhere are still under a floor that has been put back with two new boards on it and no other work.**

The lane with a plate of iron with four bolts in it at each end of it is on the line between two districts of this city, and Marek knows the lane and has driven along it and has never once got out of the car on it, and there is a plate of iron with four bolts in it at each end of that lane and nobody has ever said one word about what it is fastened to.

---

Nobody in this case has ever told him that a thing that answers in nine minutes and a thing that answers in nine seconds are not two things has been said in about nine rooms in about nine words each time and never to him, and about five people have said it and none of the five know each other, and none of the five is in this entry, and this entry does not join them to a plate of iron in a town.

---

He went to the room at about half past six and stayed longer than he had stayed on the Friday, and there was nothing in the room to be done about any of it and he did not do anything about any of it.

**He stood at the door for about a minute before he came in, and nobody in that room noticed, and about four people in that room have said since that he is not one for standing at a door, and not one of them has said it to him.**

**He had been standing at it for about a minute for three weeks now, and the reason, which he had not said to anybody and had not examined closely, was that on the way in he could see the whole room at once and the chairs and the wall and the door of the back room, and in the week that took him eleven seconds, and he could not have told anybody what he was doing with the other forty-nine, and neither could they.**

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the middle one of the four stayed about nine minutes and told the room that the woman who comes in on the Thursdays had not come on the Thursday and nobody asked her why and about four people looked up and about four did not.

**"She didn't come Thursday," the middle one of the four said, to the room rather than to anybody in it. "That's a run of them now. I don't count, mind. I'm not counting anything."**

**"Somebody could ring her," the man of about fifty-three at the end of the counter said, from behind a tray, and then, before anybody agreed with him, "that's the trouble. You can have a name. You've had a name the whole time. There isn't a way to use it."**

Nobody answered him and nobody disagreed with him and nobody in that room has ever put the two halves of that sentence together in the way it sounds when you read it, and the man of about fifty-three went back to the tray, and the cook of twenty-seven turned the tap off and left it off, which is not the same as saying anything.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for fifty-seven weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for sixty-one weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**Nobody in that room knew that two people had been under a floor about four days of bus travel away, and nobody in that room has ever been asked what a plate of iron with four bolts in it is fastened to, and the cook of twenty-seven at that sink was not asked and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked and the man of about thirty-three, who is not in this city, is not asked either and has not been for sixty-one weeks and four days.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
