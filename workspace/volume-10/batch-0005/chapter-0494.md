# Chapter 494 — The Ninth Friday Of Ten, And Two Figures That Are Each Other's Days

**The Friday of week one hundred and eighty-one was a bench until about two and a counter until about six and then a room, and the one thing that is true of it is that about eleven people sat in a room on a second floor for about nine minutes for the ninth Friday running, and that a woman drove in from the third of the four towns to ask one question in an office above a fourth shop and did not get it answered.**

The fortnight that cook laid out is still paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and ninety-five days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Friday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

**Two figures stand on this Friday and each of them is the day the other object was written, and here is which is which about each of the two, because a person reading them in a row will be holding both at once and will not know which is which.**

**The thirteen lines on that board are six hundred and sixty-six days old. Six hundred and sixty-six is the day the nineteen lines were written on that same board, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

**The nineteen lines on that board are four hundred and ninety-one days old. Four hundred and ninety-one is the day the thirteenth line was written on it, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

Those are two halves of two figures and neither of them is a mistake, and a person holding both at once is holding an age about a board and a day about the same board, and the same board in both, and the nineteen is the only promise on that board and the only line on it that has ever had a zero and it has none now.

The other six lines on that board are seven hundred and fifteen, six hundred and thirty-one, six hundred and ten, five hundred and eighty-five, five hundred and sixty-seven and five hundred and thirteen, and none of those six is a day any of the four lines above was written and none of them is a day the heating in this city went off or was put on.

**The room off that service road is seven hundred and ninety-five days old and three hundred and fifty-eight is the day the card went into the rail, and neither of those two figures is on the same line as either of the two above, and about four people in this city who have been in both buildings have never put any of the four together.**

The room is the same room. About eleven chairs out of a cupboard and a table at the front and a laptop on it connected to nothing in this case. The chairs were stacked against the wall when the first person came and were unstacked by the first person who came, and that is the ninth week running that two different people have done the two halves of that, and nobody has ever asked which two people those are.

**There is a radiator under the window that has been cold on every one of the nine Fridays, and about four of the about eleven have sat away from it, and nobody in that room has ever asked the woman who calls them in why it is cold in a room with a radiator in it.**

About eleven people came, which is the same figure as the eight Fridays before, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the figure is a figure and not a rate and is not on a form.

**The woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard said one word and pointed at the chairs and said nothing else, and the nine minutes went the way the eight of them before them went, and she did not say how long it would be and nobody asked her and nobody has ever asked her.**

**"Chairs," she said, and pointed, and that was the whole of what she said for the ninth Friday running, and about four people on that floor have said since that she has never once said good evening and about four have said she has never once needed to.**

A man of about twenty-four came in last and put his coat over the back of a chair, and the radiator under the window was cold against his shins, and he moved the chair about four feet along the wall without saying anything about it, and about four of the about eleven moved theirs that week and about four did not.

**"It's been cold since May," the man of about thirty-nine who buys training said, from the back of the room, to nobody, on the ninth Friday of a thing he had not once asked about.**

Nobody picked it up. She looked at her watch, and said one word, and that word was nine, and the chairs went back on top of each other in the two halves by two different people as they had on the eight Fridays before it, and about four of the eleven were aware that the word was the ninth one and about four were not.

Nine Fridays is not a run. It is nine Fridays, and anybody who wants a run out of it has to wait, and there is one Friday after this one of which this case has seen none.

**The post by the corridor end is bare forty-nine weeks, which is three hundred and forty-three days from the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, and nobody in that room knows either of those figures, and this figure is a different one from the one that stood in that corridor four Fridays ago and the two are not to be reconciled with any other count of the same run.**

**The card is still in it and the number on the card is still the number and the box is at the same height on the same wall, and the second box at the near end of the same corridor is still not empty, and the two are about nine feet apart, and nobody in that building has ever been asked whether the two are a set.**

**The man of about thirty-nine who buys training sat at the back for the ninth Friday running and he asked nothing at all, and about four people on that floor have noticed that he asks nothing, and nobody on that floor has ever kept a note of what he asks on which Friday.**

**Nobody said thank you. The room emptied in about nine minutes the way it does.**

**The two figures in biro on the back of his left hand are still there, eleven and then nine, and there is still no third one, and he has not washed them off and he has not looked at either of them in that room and nobody has ever asked him for either.**

**The corridor outside that room is about four times as long as the room is wide and there are about nine doors along it, of which about four stand open and about four are locked and one is a fire door that does not shut, and nobody in that building has ever gone down it and looked at the backs of the locked ones.**

**Nobody who was in that room on the Friday has ever been in a room in any of the four towns, and nobody who was in that room knew that three people in a kitchen about four days of bus travel away had read three things out loud on the Monday, and nobody has introduced them and nobody is going to.**

**On the Friday morning, before any of that, a woman of about thirty-three who drives a van in the third of the four towns came into this city on the bus and went to the office above the fourth shop and was in it for about an hour, and about four other people were in it, and she did not bring anything with her and did not take anything away.**

She was the fourth person in about three weeks to walk into that office out of one of the four towns, and the two rooms that have been there have never asked her which room she came from and she has not said, and the woman of about thirty-nine who lets the office has not asked either and has told nobody.

**Nobody in that office on the Friday was told that there are four hand copies of nine lines in four towns, and nobody in that office has ever seen a timetable or a chopping board or a hire sheet or a rent book, and nobody in that office has been told that one of the four carries a different figure in its fourth line from the other three, and about four people on that floor have said since that three rooms have been coming for a fortnight.**

**"Is the second column ever going to be filled in," the woman of about thirty-three said. "That's the question I came four days on a bus for."**

**"No," the woman of about thirty-nine said. "Nobody has ever filled it in."**

**"That is not what I asked you."**

**"It is what I said," the woman of about thirty-nine said, and squared the folder on the table without opening it again, and the woman of about thirty-three stayed sitting, which two of the about four others in that office had already got up to do.**

**"Then I'll ask you again the next time I come."**

**"The office is on Tuesdays and Thursdays," the woman of about thirty-nine said. "This is Friday, and you have come four days on the bus on a Friday, and I am not going to be told what my column is for by somebody standing up."**

**"You're not answering me."**

**"No," the woman of about thirty-nine said, and put her thumb flat on the drawer, and left it there, and nobody in that building has ever asked her what the drawer is for, and the van woman did not ask her either, and got up, and went out and down, and the door at the bottom of the stairs does not shut on its own and did not shut on its own on the Friday.**

She asked, on the Friday, whether the second column on the form was ever going to be filled in, and the woman of about thirty-nine said that nobody had ever filled it in, and the woman of about thirty-three said that was not what she had asked, and nobody asked her what she had asked, and she did not say then and has not said since.

**Nobody improved on that and nobody is going to, and about four people on that floor have said since that the van woman from the third of the four towns came a long way to ask a question that was not answered, and none of them has said the question to her, and she has not said it to anybody in this city, and this case has not gone and asked for it.**
