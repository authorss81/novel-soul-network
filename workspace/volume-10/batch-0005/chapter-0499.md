# Chapter 499 — The Thirty-Fifth Sitting, And One Ordinary Sentence Said To Her Face

**The Wednesday of week one hundred and eighty-four was the thirty-fifth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and it was a bench until about two, a counter until about half past two, and then a room, and the one thing that is true of it is that a woman came in on the Tuesday bus and said one ordinary sentence to the woman who holds that room, to her face, and a line went into the book, and he was at a counter two districts away and did not know it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about two and a half and then a room from about half past six, and he was not in the room in Saltmarket and has never been in that room. The rate was at nine and ten and he looked at it last and looked away again. **Heating on and up, the four names on the lease up, and the fortnight a cook laid out still owed to nobody by nobody.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and fourteen days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is eight hundred and eighteen days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a book of four columns on a table in a room above a line in another district is a different object from a rail of about forty card slots in a shop, and nothing in this entry joins them.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and they came down at about eleven, and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

**There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. It did not move in about four hours. Nobody in that room looked at it more than once, and its mover is not named and is not going to be named.**

There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, open for about four hours and not shut once.

There were about eleven people, and about four of them are not described this morning, and there was one who had not been in that room before.

**The woman of about forty-three who runs a kitchen in the second of the four towns was in that room for the first time. She came in on the bus on the Tuesday and goes out on it on the Thursday. She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will, and nobody in that room asked her which town she was in and she did not say.**

She had about two days in this city in her and about four days of bus travel each way, and nobody in that room knew either of those figures and nobody asked.

---

Eight items, at four minutes apiece, and eight of them are the kind of thing that has happened at every sitting for nineteen years, and the ninth was not, and the ninth was nine seconds long.

**The ninth thing was a sentence about a tap. She said it to the woman who holds that room, to her face, across a table, at about twenty to eleven, and the woman who holds that room wrote it in the second column of that book with a pen she had in her hand the whole morning, and the line went in, and that is the whole of the rule and there is no rule.**

**"The tap in that kitchen has been cold on the Mondays for about a year and I have said so to nobody, and I am saying it to you."**

**Nobody said anything back. Nobody said thank you. Nobody asked her anything about the tap or about the kitchen or about the nine days, or about the nine lines, or about the four copies, or about about nine people, and she was not thanked and she did not wait to be thanked and did not look round the room afterwards.**

About four of the about eleven noticed that something had gone into the book and about four did not, and about four have said since that the book has not had a line in it for a long time and about four have said they did not notice anything.

The woman who holds that room did not explain the line to anybody. She did not read it out, and she did not say whose it was, and she has not said since, and nobody in that room has asked her about it and she has not been asked twice.

**The book was at fifty-six lines when the morning began and it is at fifty-seven lines now, and one line went in, and it is the first line that has gone in since a woman of about thirty-four said one ordinary sentence at the thirty-third sitting, and the two of them are not compared with each other in that room and were not compared this morning.**

Nobody asked her for a name. Nobody asked her which town. Nobody asked her about a room of about nine people and she was not asked and did not offer one, and she has not said anything to anybody on the bus or in that town about what she said this morning.

The count went up by one. It goes up by one at every sitting whether the book opens or not, and it came out at forty of which thirty-five correspond, and thirty-five of the forty correspond to the thirty-five Wednesdays this book holds and five of them predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody.

**No number was given into the tin this morning, and none has been given into it on any day since the year began, and the tin stands at seventy-three, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved.**

**Nobody asked her about turning one figure into another.** She has not given that in nineteen years and nobody in that room has asked her for it in nineteen years, and nothing was put in the space that question leaves and nothing was offered in its place.

**Nobody said anything to her about the count. The count is announced and it is not discussed, and nobody in that room said a word to the woman of about forty-three about the count or about the book or about the line or about the tap or about anything else except the eight items and the nine seconds.**

**She sat down afterwards on the chair at the end of the curve, which is not the chair against the wall, and she stayed for the rest of the morning, and when the shutters came down at about eleven she went out first.**

---

**Nobody in that room knew that the woman of about forty-three had nine lines of handwriting on the side of a chopping board in a kitchen in a town, and nobody knew that the fourth of the nine on that board says four days, and about four of the about eleven were told nothing and the eleven were told nothing, and about four people in that room have said since that somebody said something and about four have said they did not hear what.**

Nobody in that room knew that three things had been read out loud in three rooms in three towns over about a fortnight and that one of the three rooms had come out of four, and nobody in that room was told any of it, and about four people in that room have said since that something is happening on the far side of the road and about four have said the word is not in this case and have not said it.

---

He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at that counter until about half past two and then the rest of the day was not a counter, and nobody in the building in Saltmarket knows that a man of twenty-two spent that morning at a bench, and nobody at that bench knows that a book was open on a table for about four hours in another district, and the two facts have never been in the same sentence in front of either of them.

**The rest of the day was not a counter and he had not arranged for it not to be one.** He carried the day's parts back to the bench in two crates and put them down in the order they came off, which is not the order he wants them in, and stood looking at the wrong order for a while without correcting it, and then went back to a room he rents and slept for two hours with the light on and woke up not knowing what day it was for the better part of a minute, and got up and made the walk back in.

About four people came to that counter between about two and about half past two and about four of them were told no to something. Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

**The counter was down to nothing for about ten minutes before the woman of about twenty-four came in on the Tuesday and it was down to nothing again for about ten minutes after she left, and the whole of the conversation on that Wednesday morning was in a different building in a different district about four days of bus travel away and neither man has ever heard of the other.**

He got to the room at about half past six and did not stay long, and the counter had been down to nothing for the best part of an hour by then. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty, and nobody asked her a question, nobody thanked her and nobody apologised to her, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for sixty weeks. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for sixty-three weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**Nobody in that room knew that a woman had been in a room above a line in another district at about eleven that morning and had said nine seconds about a tap, and nobody in that room has ever been told how many lines are in that book, and the cook of twenty-seven at that sink was not asked and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked.**

*502. Wednesday of week 184, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is the thirty-fifth sitting with the book at fifty-six lines before the morning and at fifty-seven lines after it, seventy-three in a tin, forty announced of which thirty-five correspond, nineteen years, a chair against a wall that did not move, an ask at seventy-two weeks, a woman who came in on the Tuesday bus and said nine seconds about a tap, nineteen lines on two nails with the newest of them five hundred and ten days old, and nobody thanked anybody.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about half past two and then a room from about half past six, and he has never been in the room in Saltmarket. That shop, for the half hour he was at it: a bench, a parts cage with a man who works with his back to the room all day, a shutter, a tool roll with a fourth pocket, a wall list in a plastic sleeve, a shelf of finished work along the far wall, and a rail of about forty card slots along the front of the counter holding two cards. People who came to that counter: about four, of whom all four were told no to something. The day's work: eleven things, four of them no. The rate: nine and ten, uncorrected, told to nobody. The heating: on and dearer, the four names on the lease dearer, and the fortnight a cook laid out still on nobody's account and unresolved. The room off that service road: eight hundred and fourteen days old, four rooms and a back one, warm. The hardboard, two nails: the twelve lines seven hundred and thirty-four, the thirteen six hundred and eighty-five, the fourteen six hundred and fifty, the fifteen six hundred and twenty-nine, the sixteen six hundred and four, the seventeen five hundred and eighty-six, the eighteen five hundred and thirty-two, and the nineteen five hundred and ten, and the nineteen is the only line on that board that has ever had a zero and it has none now. The wall: it was neither written on nor cleaned that week, and there is still no twentieth line. The ring binder: on a shelf in that room, taken down and put back without being opened. The woman's page: the eighth of the eight in that binder, still unread, and no question has reached her about it and no apology has. The master's fifth line: blank. The Exchange: the book at fifty-seven lines and the tin at seventy-three and a count announced of forty of which thirty-five correspond, nineteen years, and none of the four of them is convertible into another and the difference between the first two of them is not a number. The separation: four months and thirty weeks and two days, not shorter and not ended. Whether anybody thanked anybody: no. Owner: fifty-seven lines, and nobody.**

*The nine seconds, as a record of a line that went into a book because somebody said a thing to her face.* **Where: a room above a line in a building in another district, on the Wednesday morning at about twenty to eleven. Who said it: a woman of about forty-three who runs a kitchen in the second of the four towns, in that room for the first time, having come in on the bus on the Tuesday and going out on it on the Thursday, and not on any sheet of it. What she said, to the woman's face across a table, in about nine seconds: that the tap in that kitchen has been cold on the Mondays for about a year and that she has said so to nobody and is saying it to her. What went in: one line, in the second column, written with a pen that was in that woman's right hand the whole morning. What the book stood at: fifty-six lines before and fifty-seven after. Who was asked what: nobody asked her a name, nobody asked her which town, nobody asked her about a room of about nine people, and she offered nothing and said nothing else. Whether she was thanked: no, and she did not wait to be thanked and did not look round the room, and she sat afterwards on the chair at the end of the curve and went out first when the shutters came down. Whether the woman who holds that room explained the line: no, and she did not read it out and has not said since, and nobody in that room has asked her about it. How many noticed: about four of the about eleven, and about four did not. What the count came out at: forty, of which thirty-five correspond, and it goes up by one at every sitting whether the book opens or not. What the tin stood at: seventy-three, and no number was given into it, and nobody asked why. Whether anybody asked about turning one figure into another: no, and she has not given that in nineteen years and nobody has asked her for it in nineteen years. Owner: a tap, and nobody.**

*The thirty-five and the thirty-six, as a record of a form a company answers a public question on.* **What it is: a printed form with two columns and a company mark, of which about four are on about nine tables in about four places in this city and the rest are in a drawer in an office above a fourth shop in another district. What the first column is for: what a room has decided this week. What the second column is for: whether that room still wants to be doing this at all. How many times either has been filled in: never. How many rooms have signed: none, and nothing has been adopted and nothing has been signed, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is on nothing. What was offered on the Wednesday of the week before last: one address and one place, quicker than three towns, in exchange for the form, with a Friday morning on it. What happened to the offer: it went into a locked drawer at nine on the Friday morning with the second column still empty, and the key to that drawer is on a hook inside the door of that room. Whether anybody in this room has ever been asked about any of that: no, and nobody in that room has been told how many of them there are. Owner: two columns, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **The ninth line of the ring binder stayed unread, and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts of this city have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody, and the four hand copies of it in four towns are on nothing either and no two of them has been compared with any other. The four rooms in four towns are three and not four and have not been introduced to each other and have not been told how many of them there are, and nobody in the building in Saltmarket knew any of that, and would not have been told. Nine hand copies of the front of a page with about eleven lines in it are past their fifty-fourth week and about eight of the nine are unfinished, and the first disagreement in the fourth line of those has not been found, and a figure of days in the fourth line of a timetable or a chopping board or a hire sheet or a rent book is a different fourth line and is not the same disagreement and is not compared with any of them anywhere. Nine keys are needed. Two exist, and the first of the nine cannot be asked back. Nine doors on a road on the far side of this city are still hanging by one hinge and no wage pays for a mender, and the week that about eleven people lost has not come back to them. A bar across the inside of a door in each of four buildings in four towns is on nothing and appears in no list of anything. A printed form is on about nine tables in this city and its second column is empty in every one of them, and the office above a fourth shop is not on a rota and is not a registry. A sheet of paper with one name on it is in a drawer in a van in a town and the four weeks on it are not over. The fortnight a cook laid out is still on nobody's account. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 502.**
