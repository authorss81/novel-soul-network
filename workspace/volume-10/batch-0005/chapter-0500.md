# Chapter 500 — The Thirty-Sixth Sitting, And A Wednesday Like Every Other Wednesday

**The Wednesday of week one hundred and eighty-eight was the thirty-sixth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and the one thing that is true of it is that a man of about thirty-one who works at a school gate in the fourth of the four towns came into this city on the Tuesday bus and sat in that room for about an hour on the Wednesday morning and said one ordinary sentence to the woman who holds that room, to her face, and a line went into the book, and the book stood at fifty-seven lines when the morning began and stands at fifty-eight lines now, the tin did not move and stands at seventy-three, the count came out at forty-one of which thirty-six correspond, and the ask stands at five hundred and thirty-two days, which is seventy-six weeks, counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since, on that Wednesday, and the shutters came down at about eleven and the room off that service road was warm at about half past six the same evening and about four people came in for the temperature and nobody thanked anybody.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about two and a half and then a room from about half past six, and he was not in the room in Saltmarket and has never been in that room. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook laid out is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is eight hundred and forty-two days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is eight hundred and forty-six days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a book of four columns on a table in a room above a line in another district is a different object from a rail of about forty card slots in a shop, and nothing in this entry joins them.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and they came down at about eleven, and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

**There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. It did not move in about four hours. Nobody in that room looked at it more than once, and its mover is not named and is not going to be named.**

There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, open for about four hours and not shut once.

There were about eleven people, and about four of them are not described this morning, and there was one who had not been in that room before.

**The man of about thirty-one who works at a school gate in the fourth of the four towns was in that room for the first time. He came in on the bus on the Tuesday and goes out on it on the Thursday. He was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried his name and nothing ever will, and nobody in that room asked him which town he was in and he did not say.**

He had about two days in this city in him and he was not asked about either of them.

---

Nine items, at four minutes apiece, and nine of them are the kind of thing that has happened at every sitting for nineteen years, and one of them was not, and that one was about nine seconds long and was said at about ten to eleven.

**It was about a bench. He said it to the woman who holds that room, to her face, across a table, and she wrote it in the second column of that book with the pen that was in her right hand the whole morning, and the line went in, and there is no rule and there has never been one.**

**"There is a bench outside that gate that gets wet and nobody in that school has ever said so out loud, and I have said it four times to nobody, and I am saying it to you."**

**Nobody said anything back. Nobody said thank you. Nobody asked him about the gate or the school or the timetable or the nine lines, or about the fourth of the nine, and he was not thanked and he did not wait to be thanked and did not look round the room afterwards, and about four of the about eleven noticed that something had gone into the book and about four did not.**

The woman who holds that room did not explain the line to anybody. She did not read it out and she did not say whose it was, and nobody in that room has asked her about it and she has not been asked twice, and about four people in that room have said since that two lines have gone into that book in four weeks and about four have said they did not know that.

**The book was at fifty-seven lines when the morning began and it is at fifty-eight lines now, and one line went in, and the book of four columns on that table has been open for about four hours twice in four weeks and shut neither time.**

Nobody asked him for a name. Nobody asked him which town. Nobody asked him about a room of about nine people and he was not asked and did not offer one, and he has not said anything on the bus or in that town about what he said this morning.

The count went up by one. It goes up by one at every sitting whether the book opens or not, and it came out at forty-one of which thirty-six correspond, and thirty-six of the forty-one correspond to the thirty-six Wednesdays this book holds and five of them predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody.

**No number was given into the tin this morning, and none has been given into it on any day since the year began, and the tin stands at seventy-three, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved, and the difference between the first two of the four figures on that table is not a number and was not printed as one this morning and is not printed as one here.**

**Nobody asked her about turning one figure into another.** She has not given that in nineteen years and nobody in that room has asked her for it in nineteen years, and nothing was put in the space that question leaves and nothing was offered in its place.

**Nobody said anything to her about the count. The count is announced and it is not discussed, and nobody in that room said a word to the man of about thirty-one about the count or about the book or about the bench or about anything else except the nine items and the nine seconds.**

**She sat down afterwards on the chair at the end of the curve, which is not the chair against the wall, and she stayed for the rest of the morning, and when the shutters came down at about eleven he went out first.**

---

**Nobody in that room knew that a man of about thirty-one had nine lines of handwriting on the back of a timetable in a school in a town, and nobody knew that the fourth of the nine on that timetable says nine days, and nobody knew that the fourth line on a chopping board in a kitchen in another town says four, and nobody in that room has ever heard of either figure, and about four people in that room have said since that somebody said something and about four have said they did not hear what.**

Nobody in that room knew that a woman of about forty-three had been in it four weeks before and said nine seconds about a tap, and about four of the about eleven this morning were in that room then, and nobody put the two of them together and nobody asked.

**Nobody in that room was told how many lines are on the hardboard on two nails in a room off a service road in this city, or how old any of them is, or that the room it is in is eight hundred and forty-two days old, and nobody in that room has ever been in that room.**

---

He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at that counter until about half past two, and then the rest of the day was not a counter, and nobody in the building in Saltmarket knows that, and nobody at that bench knows that a book was open on a table for about four hours in another district.

About four people came to that counter between about two and about half past two and about four of them were told no to something.

**Four days of bus travel from that counter, in a room above a line, a book of four columns stood open on a table from about ten until about eleven and one line went into it, and at this counter the shutter came down at about ten, and the two of those facts have never been in the same sentence in front of either man and are not in this one.**

He went to the room at about half past six. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the middle one of the four said the word warm and said it to the room and not to him.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for sixty-four weeks. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for sixty-seven weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**Nobody in that room knew that a man had been in a room above a line in another district at about eleven that morning saying nine seconds about a bench, and nobody in that room has ever been told how many lines are in that book, and the cook of twenty-seven at that sink was not asked and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked and the man of about thirty-three was not asked and has not been for sixty-seven weeks and six days.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The nine seconds, as a record of a line that went into a book because somebody said a thing to her face.* **Where: a room above a line in a building in another district, on the Wednesday morning at about ten to eleven. Who said it: a man of about thirty-one who works at a school gate in the fourth of the four towns, in that room for the first time, having come in on the bus on the Tuesday and going out on it on the Thursday, and not on any sheet of it. What he said, to her face across a table, in about nine seconds: that there is a bench outside that gate that gets wet and that nobody in that school has ever said so out loud, and that he has said it four times to nobody and is saying it to her. What went in: one line, in the second column, written with a pen that was in her right hand the whole morning. What the book stood at: fifty-seven lines before and fifty-eight after. Who was asked what: nobody asked him a name, nobody asked him which town, nobody asked him about a room of about nine people, and he offered nothing and said nothing else. Whether he was thanked: no, and he did not wait to be thanked and did not look round the room, and he sat afterwards on the chair at the end of the curve and went out first when the shutters came down. Whether she explained the line: no, and she did not read it out and has not said since, and nobody in that room has asked her about it. How many noticed: about four of the about eleven, and about four did not, and about four of this morning's eleven were in that room four weeks ago and nobody put the two mornings together. What the count came out at: forty-one, of which thirty-six correspond, and it goes up by one at every sitting whether the book opens or not. What the tin stood at: seventy-three, and no number was given into it, and nobody asked why. Whether anybody asked about turning one figure into another: no, and she has not given that in nineteen years and nobody has asked her for it in nineteen years. Owner: a bench, and nobody.**

*The three things, as a record of what three rooms said out loud in one morning and wrote down nowhere.* **Where: three rooms in three towns, on a Wednesday morning in the week before last, at about seven, in each of them, one after another and not together. Who said them: a woman of about forty-three who runs a kitchen, a man of about thirty-five who comes in at about ten most nights, and a woman of about thirty-six who helps on a Tuesday, and one of the three rooms is not one of the three any more and said nothing this morning. What the three are: that they will work to one standard, that any one of them can stop on its own without telling the other two and without a reason, and that whoever holds a week holds it for four weeks and then it moves. Where the name of the person holding the week is: on a sheet of paper in a drawer in a van in one of the four towns, and the four weeks on it are three-quarters over at the close of this entry. Whether anybody heard the other two: no, and about four people in each of those three towns have said since that something is going on and about nine have said nothing. Whether anything was written down: no, and nobody signed anything, and about four people in each of those towns have said that the three should be written down and about nine have said nothing. Whether any two of the four hand copies has been compared with any other: no. Owner: three things, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Wednesday, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts of this city have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody, and the four hand copies of it in four towns are on nothing either and no two of them has been compared with any other. The four rooms in four towns are three and not four and have not been introduced to each other and have not been told how many of them there are, and about four people who had been coming to an office above a fourth shop have not come since, and about four of those four are on no rota at all.
