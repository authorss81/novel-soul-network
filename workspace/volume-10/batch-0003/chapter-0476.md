# Chapter 476 — The Thirty-Third Sitting, One Ordinary Sentence, And A Book At Fifty-Six

**The Wednesday of week one hundred and seventy-six was the thirty-third sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and the one thing that is true of it is that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the only written record in one of the four towns said one ordinary sentence to the holder's face, and the book stood at fifty-five lines when the morning began and it stands at fifty-six lines now, the tin did not move, the count came out at thirty-eight of which thirty-three correspond, and the ask stands at four hundred and forty-eight days, which is sixty-four weeks to the day, counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since, on that Wednesday.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about two and a half and then a room from about half past six, and he was not in the room in Saltmarket and has never been in that room. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and fifty-eight days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and sixty-two days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a book of four columns on a table in a room above a line in Saltmarket is a different object from a rail of about forty card slots in a shop in another district, and nothing in this entry joins them.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and they came down at about eleven, and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. It did not move in about four hours. Nobody in that room looked at it more than once, and its mover is not named and is not going to be named.

There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, open for about four hours and not shut once.

There were about eleven people, and about four of them are not described this morning, and there was one who had not been in that room before and who had come in on the bus on the Tuesday and who was going out on it on the Thursday.

**She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.**

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece, and five of the six are the kind of thing that has happened at every sitting for nineteen years, and that is not a change and is not a pattern and is not a rule and is not a judgement and is not in anybody's mouth.

The sixth was about four minutes and it was a woman of about thirty-four saying one sentence, and what she said was this:

**"I have got the same nine words written down in two towns and nobody over there knows about the one down here."**

**Nobody in that room thanked her. She did not wait to be thanked and did not look round the room afterwards, and about four of the eleven wrote nothing down, and one of them wrote it down afterwards on the back of a hand.**

**The book was at fifty-five lines when she began and it is at fifty-six lines now, and a line went in, and nobody in that room put it in and nobody in that room has ever put anything in it, and the whole of what that line says is the ninth line of a form that nine other people in this city have seen and none of them has copied out.**

Nobody asked her for a name. Nobody asked her which two towns. Nobody asked her where the words came from and she was not asked and she did not say.

She sat down afterwards on the chair at the end of the curve, which is not the chair against the wall, and she stayed for the rest of the morning, and about four of the about eleven looked at her about four times and about four looked at her about nine times and none of them said anything to her, and when the shutters came down at about eleven she went out first and stood on the landing for a moment in the rain.

**The book opens when somebody says a thing to her face, and that has been the whole of it for nineteen years, and it was the whole of it this morning, and nobody in that room has ever told her that and nobody is going to.**

The count went up by one. It goes up by one at every sitting whether the book opens or not, and thirty-three of the thirty-eight correspond to the thirty-three Wednesdays this book holds, and five of them predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody and has never been asked about twice.

**No number was given into the tin this morning, and none has been given into it on any day since the year began, and the tin stands at seventy-three, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved.**

**Nobody asked her for the rule about turning one figure into another.** She has not given it in nineteen years and she promised, in front of nine people, to say it for the last time, and she kept that promise again this morning, and nothing was put in the space it left and nothing was offered in its place.

Nobody said anything to her about the count. The count is announced and it is not discussed, and the number that came out of it this morning is thirty-eight and about four people in that room have said since that it has gone up every year and none of them has said how they know.

---

That same woman said nine words on the Tuesday, in her own town, in the room above the kitchen, to about nine people, and about four of the nine had come from the Wednesday before and about four of the nine had not.

What she said was this:

**"Nine minutes and nine seconds, one answer, not two."**

**The room did not want it. She said that afterwards, on the Tuesday, to nobody, and she has said it once in this case and not twice, and nobody in that room asked her a follow-up question and nobody argued with her and nobody has repeated it back to her since.**

She said the nine words out loud and then she said that she was going into the city on the bus on the Tuesday afternoon and would be back on the Thursday, and about four of the nine did not ask what for, and the one who did was told a sheet to hand to a company and was not told anything else.

**She named the cost out loud, once, at the end of that Wednesday morning, in the room in Saltmarket, to about four of the eleven and to the room.** She said that this was the third time she had carried a question to rooms on foot and on a bus, that it was two days of work and a fare she is not going to get back, and that she has now said a thing out loud in a room and got a line written in a book, and that nobody is going to tell her what to do with the line, and that she did not expect anything and is not going to ask.

Nobody said she was right. Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her, and about four of the eleven have said since that it was a lot to ask of somebody, and none of them has said it to her.

---

He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at that counter until about half past two and then the rest of the day was not a counter, and the man of about thirty-seven was not in the shop on the Wednesday and the form is not in the drawer under the till.

Nobody in the building in Saltmarket knows that a man of twenty-two spent that morning at a bench, and nobody at that bench knows that a book was open on a table for about four hours in another district, and the two facts have never been in the same sentence in front of either man.

About four people came to that counter between about two and about half past two and about four of them were told no to something. Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about fifty-two weeks. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-five weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**Nobody in that room knew that a line went into a book in a building in another district at about eleven on the Wednesday morning, and nobody in that room has ever been told how many lines are in it, and the cook of twenty-seven at that sink was not asked and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked.**

A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over.
