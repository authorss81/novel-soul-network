# Chapter 475 — The Room At Seven Hundred And Fifty-Six Days, And A Monday With No Sitting On It

**The Monday of week one hundred and seventy-six was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that the room off that service road is seven hundred and fifty-six days old, and seven hundred and fifty-six is the day the man of about fifty-one's run of days was counted from, and the man of about fifty-one has now said nothing for fifty-one weeks and five days, which is three hundred and sixty-two days, and three hundred and sixty-two is the day the room opened, and each of those is an age and the other one is a day, and nothing sat this week on this Monday because the Exchange sits on a Wednesday and this was not one.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and there is no sitting on this Monday and there has not been one on a Monday in nineteen years. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and fifty-six days old, which is one hundred and eight weeks, and is warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and sixty days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and three hundred and sixty-two is the day the room opened and is not a day the card was put in the rail, which is three hundred and fifty-eight.

---

**The room off that service road is seven hundred and fifty-six days old, and seven hundred and fifty-six is the day the man of about fifty-one's run of days was counted from.**

Those are the two halves of one number and they are not the same thing. The first is an age: how long four rooms and a back one have been standing off a service road in this city, warm, costing more. The second is a day: a Wednesday in an earlier year when somebody began counting a different man's silence from.

**The man of about fifty-one has now said nothing for fifty-one weeks and five days, which is three hundred and sixty-two days, and three hundred and sixty-two is the day the room off that service road opened.**

**Those are the other two halves of the other one, and the first is an age and the second is a day, and the room and the man are in two different buildings in two different districts of this city and nothing in this entry joins them except the number.**

**A room is not a man and a man is not a hold and a hold is not a question, and the four of them are four things with four days of their own, and a person reading two numbers in a row is holding two of them at once.**

The card in the rail is the one object in this entry that is four days older than the room, and it is not a third pair with anything, and the number that is its own day is three hundred and fifty-eight, which is not a figure on this Monday.

**Nothing sat on this Monday. The Exchange sits on a Wednesday, four weeks apart, for nineteen years, and there is no sitting on this Monday and there is not going to be one, and the next one is in two days and nobody in this city has been told that in advance by anybody.**

---

The third of the four towns has a hall at the back of a building in it, and the hall is let to anybody who asks for it and the keys for it hang in a kitchen in that town and about four people in that town know where the keys are.

A woman of about thirty-three who sets out the chairs in that hall was in it on the Monday afternoon with about four others, counting chairs again, because that is what she does.

She said it once, and what she said was nine words long, and what she said was this:

**"Nine minutes or nine seconds, it is one answer."**

**She said it while stacking the last four chairs, and about two of the four heard it and about two were talking about a hire for the Saturday, and neither of the two who heard it said anything back to her.**

Nobody in that hall wrote it down. The chairs were stacked, the floor was swept, and the key went back in the kitchen, and about four people in that town have been in that hall and do not know that about eight other rooms in four towns have said the same nine words in a fortnight.

The hire for the Saturday was for a birthday and the people who had come to talk about it argued about the number of chairs for about nine minutes and then agreed on a number, and the woman who stacks the chairs wrote it on a card in chalk on the edge of the table, and the card is still in the dustpan in that hall, and nobody in that town has ever looked at it.

**Nobody in that hall has ever been asked how many people were in it, and nobody in that town has ever been told what the number would be for, and the woman who stacks the chairs has never said a number in that hall in her life and was not asked for one.**

---

The man of about thirty-four who loads bread came in at about half past three on the Monday and looked at the shelf of finished work for a while and did not buy anything.

Nobody asked him about the Thursday. He did not mention it. About four people at that counter have noticed that he has been in twice in a fortnight and has not mentioned it, and none of them has asked, and one of them has said to another of them that if she asks him he will say he is fine and she will not learn anything.

**He is not owed anything. Nobody in this city has agreed what he is owed and nobody has asked him and he has not asked, and he said so himself once in an earlier fortnight in about nine seconds, and he has not said it since and nobody has asked him to.**

The man of about thirty-seven did not come in on the Monday. The drawer under the till was not opened. The form is on about nine tables in about four places in this city and it has not been on a counter since the Wednesday and the second column is empty in every one of the nine.

About four people came in between four and five, which is the dead half hour at that counter, and the man of about thirty-four who loads bread stood at the end of it looking at the shelf of finished work and said nothing for about nine seconds, and then said that the four stops on that side of the city had all been on time for a fortnight, which is a thing he would know and which nobody had asked him, and he said it in about nine seconds as well and went out to look at a wheel.

**Nobody at that counter said thank you for that either, and about four people there have said since that a man who tells you his stops are on time is telling you he is not behind, and nobody has ever asked him what he is behind on.**

**Nobody in this city has written down what is on that form, and nobody has told the man of about thirty-four who loads bread that it exists, and the two of them stand at the same counter about four times a fortnight and have never said a word to each other about anything but bread.**

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and the middle one filled and about four people came in for the temperature and about four went out again inside twenty minutes.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about fifty-one weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-five weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Nobody in that room knew that the room they were sitting in is the same age as the day the man at the wall with the tool bag started being counted, and he did not say so, and nobody asked him, and he has not said anything for fifty-one weeks and five days and the room he is sitting in is three hundred and sixty-two days old and the two numbers are the same number.**

**A bench in Lattice Ward and a counter in the same building are one working day and are not the day, and the day is seven hundred and fifty-three and the day is also the day the man of about fifty-one's count began, and nobody in this case is going to say the two out loud in the same breath again.**

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six. That shop: a bench, a parts cage with a man who works with his back to the room all day, a shutter, a tool roll with a fourth pocket, a wall list in a plastic sleeve, a shelf of finished work along the far wall, and a rail of about forty card slots along the front of the counter holding two cards. People who came to that counter: about nine, of whom about four were told no to something, and one of the nine bought nothing and mentioned nothing. The day's work: eleven things, four of them no, and the drawer under the till was not opened. The rate: nine and ten, uncorrected, told to nobody. The heating: on, dearer, four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved. The room off that service road: seven hundred and fifty-six days old, which is one hundred and eight weeks, four rooms and a back one, warm, and seven hundred and fifty-six is the day the man of about fifty-one's run of days was counted from. The card in the rail: seven hundred and sixty days old, four days more than the room, in a rail of about forty slots that holds two, and the day the card went into it is three hundred and fifty-eight and that is not a figure on this day. The hardboard, two nails: the twelve lines six hundred and seventy-six, the thirteen six hundred and twenty-seven, the fourteen five hundred and ninety-two, the fifteen five hundred and seventy-one, the sixteen five hundred and forty-six, the seventeen five hundred and twenty-eight, the eighteen four hundred and seventy-four, and the nineteen four hundred and fifty-two, and the nineteen is the only line on that board that has ever had a zero and it has none now. The wall: unchanged, no line added, no line rubbed off, no twentieth line. The ring binder: on a shelf in that room, taken down and put back without being opened. The woman's page: the eighth of eight things in that binder, unread, and nobody has asked her a question about it and nobody has apologised to her. The master's fifth line: blank. The Exchange: the book at fifty-five lines and the tin at seventy-three and a count announced of thirty-seven of which thirty-two correspond, nineteen years, and none of the four is convertible into another and the difference between the first two is not a number, and it does not sit on a Monday and the next sitting is in two days. The hold: three hundred and eighty-nine days, which is fifty-five weeks and four days. The man of about fifty-one: three hundred and sixty-two days, which is fifty-one weeks and five days, and three hundred and sixty-two is the day the room opened. The nine hand copies: three hundred and twenty-two days, which is forty-six weeks, and about eight of the nine are unfinished and the first disagreement in the fourth line has not been found. The ask: four hundred and forty-six days, which is sixty-three weeks and five days, counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since. The separation: four months and twenty-three weeks, not shorter and not ended. The man of about thirty-four who loads bread: came in, bought nothing, mentioned nothing, and was not asked. The printed form: on about nine tables in about four places and its second column empty in every one of them, and nobody in this city has written down what is on it. Whether the four rooms in four towns have been introduced to each other: no. Whether anybody thanked anybody: no. Owner: a Monday, and nobody.**

*The pair, as a record of two figures that are each other's days.* **First: the room off that service road, seven hundred and fifty-six days old, which is one hundred and eight weeks, and seven hundred and fifty-six is the day the man of about fifty-one's run of days was counted from. Second: the man of about fifty-one, three hundred and sixty-two days, which is fifty-one weeks and five days, and three hundred and sixty-two is the day the room opened. Whether the two are the same thing: no, and the first is an age and the second is a day, and the second pair is the same two numbers the other way round. Whether the two objects are in the same building: no, and one is off a service road in one district and the other is a man with a tool bag in a second district, and about four people in this city have been in both and none of them has ever put the two figures together. Whether the card in the rail is a third of this: no, and the card is four days older than the room and its own day is three hundred and fifty-eight, which is not a figure on this day. Whether the Exchange sat: no, and it has not sat on a Monday in nineteen years and the next sitting is in two days. Owner: a room, and nobody.**

*Nine words in a hall, as a record of a sentence said once while chairs were being stacked.* **Where: a hall at the back of a building in the third of the four towns, on the Monday afternoon, with about four people in it. Who said it: a woman of about thirty-three who sets out the chairs in that hall. How many words: nine. What they were: nine minutes or nine seconds, it is one answer. How many heard it: about two, and about two were talking about a hire for the Saturday. Whether anybody said anything back: no. Whether it was written down: no. Whether anybody in that hall has ever been asked how many people were in it: no, and the woman who stacks the chairs has never said a number in that hall and was not asked for one. Whether that hall knows about the other rooms: no, and about eight other rooms in four towns have said the same nine words in a fortnight and not one of them knows any of the others. Owner: a hall, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Monday, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts of this city have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody. The four rooms in four towns have still not been introduced to each other, and about nine rooms across those four towns have now said nine words in a fortnight and not one of them knows any of the others. Nine keys are needed and two exist, and the first of the nine still cannot be asked back. Nine doors on a road on the far side of this city are still hanging by one hinge and no wage pays for a mender, and a week that about eleven people lost has not come back to them, and a bar across the inside of a door in each of four buildings in four towns is on nothing and appears in no list of anything. A printed form is on about nine tables in this city and its second column is empty in every one of them, and a man of about thirty-seven has not been back to the counter since the Wednesday he stood in a room with about nine people in it. The fortnight a cook laid out is still on nobody's account. The Exchange did not sit this week and does not sit on a Monday, its book did not grow, and no number was given into its tin. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 478.**
