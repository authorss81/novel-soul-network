# Chapter 472 — A Form With Two Columns, And Nine Words At The Back Of A Garage

**The Wednesday of week one hundred and seventy-five was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that a company answered a question in one sentence with a printed form that has two columns on it, and about nine people in this city have now seen the form, and nobody has ever filled in the second column, and a form that nobody can rely on is still the answer of a company and not a smaller thing, and in a room at the back of a garage in the third of the four towns a man of about forty-five who reads meters said nine words once and nobody improved on it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and the drawer under the till at that counter was opened once and shut again. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and fifty-one days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and fifty-five days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a printed form that came back in an envelope on the Wednesday morning is a fifth object and is not the card and is not the hardboard and is not a page and is not a list.

---

The envelope came on the Wednesday morning and it is A4 and it has a window in it and there is a form inside the window and the form has a logo at the top and a reference in the corner.

**It is not a document anybody in this case may rely on, and the reason is not that it is false, and nobody has said that it is false, and nobody in this city has yet found one thing in it that is not true.**

It answers the one sentence the man of about thirty-seven wrote at a kitchen table on the Sunday before last. It is four paragraphs long. It gives a figure, which is a figure of days and not a figure of money, and it gives a date, and it gives a sentence about what happens next.

**The second column on it is headed with one word, and that word is a word for the person who is expected to bring it back, and nobody has ever filled it in, and about four people in this city have looked at that column and about four have not.**

The first column has a tick in it. Somebody put the tick there.

**Nobody knows who put the tick there. It is not signed, and it is not initialled, and it is not dated, and it is the only mark on the whole of the form that anybody can see is a person's.**

The man of about thirty-seven came at about half past two and read it standing up and then sat down on the low stool by the parts cage, which is not a seat anybody has ever been offered at that counter and which he was not offered.

He turned the sheet over once and turned it back and did not take anything out of his pockets, which is what a man does when he has thought about bringing something and has not brought it, and about four people at that counter noticed the pockets and about nine did not.

**He read it twice. He did not say whether it was right. He said, once, that it answered the sentence and that it answered a different one, and he did not say which of the two it had done.**

Nobody at that counter said which either. Nobody improved on his reading and nobody has read the form since except the people he handed it to.

**He gave it to about four people over the rest of the afternoon and by the end of the day about nine people in this city had seen it, and it was on a table in a kitchen in the first of the four districts by about five, and it was on a counter in the second by about half past five, and it has not left either.**

**Nobody in this city has copied it out. Nobody has photographed it. Nobody has put it in a file, and the company that sent it keeps no record of having sent it, and that is a thing the man of about thirty-seven did not find out and this case did not go and ask.**

**He said, once, that he would go to the open afternoon, and that he would take nothing with him, and that if he took nothing with him then nobody could say afterwards that he had come with an answer already written, and he said that to the counter and not to the man and about four people there heard it.**

**Nobody told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong, and the sentence has been in about four people's heads since and none of them has acted on it, and one of them has a form on a table in a kitchen and has not put it in her bag.**

---

The third of the four towns has a garage in it with two ramps and a counter and a back room that is not a workshop and does not have a bench in it, and four people meet in the back room when the work lets them and not on any day anybody has settled on.

There were about four of them at about eight on the Wednesday evening and the man who reads meters had come in from a house on the far side of that town and had not eaten.

He said it once, and what he said was nine words long, and what he said was this:

**"Nine minutes, nine seconds, one answer, not two things."**

**He said it while taking his coat off, which meant that about two of the four heard the whole of it and about two heard the end of it, and the two who heard the end of it have not asked the other two what the first part was.**

One of the four works nights and had about four hours' sleep in him. One is a woman of about fifty-three who does accounts for two businesses and who has never once said how many people are in anything. One was the person who brought him in.

**The fourth of the four had come about nine minutes after and was told what he had said by somebody who heard it, and repeated it back, and the version that came back had the nine seconds in the wrong place in it, and nobody corrected it.**

Nobody in that back room wrote it down. Nobody in that back room has ever written anything down, and there is a hook on the wall by the door with nothing on it and there has been nothing on it for years.

**The garage next door to that back room has a weighbridge office on the other side of the road and nobody in that office has ever been in the back room, and about four people in that town who have been in both have never put the two together because there is nothing in either of them that would go with the other.**

**The man who reads meters has read meters in that town for about nineteen years and has been in about nine hundred houses and has written the number in a book in each of them, and he has never once written down how many people are in any of them, and nobody has ever asked him to.**

**Nobody in that town knows that a woman of about twenty-four said the same nine words on the Monday in a shop four days of bus travel away, and nobody in that shop knows that a man of about forty-five said them on the Wednesday in a garage, and neither of those two people has been introduced to the other and nobody is going to.**

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the middle one filled.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about fifty-one weeks. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-four weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Nobody in that room knew that a form with two columns had been on about nine people's tables since the morning, and nobody in that room has ever been asked to sign one, and about four people in that room have said since that a company answering you on a printed sheet is better than a company not answering you at all, and neither of them is going to find out whether it is.**

**A man of about fifty-one at a wall and a company on a second floor in another district are two rooms and not one, and the post by the corridor end in that other building has been bare a long time and nobody in the room off that service road has ever been told it.**

**The second column on the form is headed with one word and that word is not a name and not a number and not a date, and about four people have looked at it and about four have not, and nobody in this city has asked anybody in a company what it is for, and this case has not gone and asked either.**

*The form, as a record of a company's answer to a question asked in one sentence.* **What it is: one printed sheet in an envelope with a window in it, four paragraphs, a figure of days, a date, a sentence about what happens next, a first column with a tick in it and a second column with a word for a visitor over it. Who asked the question: a man of about thirty-seven who drives vans, who wrote it at a kitchen table on the Sunday, crossed out two goes at it, and paid for the post himself. What the company did: answered on a printed form rather than to a person, which is an ordinary thing for a company to do and is not a thing anybody has objected to in public. *Nine words at the back of a garage, as a record of a sentence said once and repeated back wrong.* **Where: the back room of a garage in the third of the four towns, at about eight on the Wednesday evening. Who: a man of about forty-five who reads meters, in from a house on the far side of that town, and he had not eaten. How many words: nine. What they were: nine minutes, nine seconds, one answer, not two things. How many of the four heard all of it: about two. How many heard the end of it: about two. What happened next: a fifth person came in about nine minutes late and was told what he had said and repeated it back with the nine seconds in the wrong place in it, and nobody corrected it, and that wrong version is the one that is in circulation in that room.
