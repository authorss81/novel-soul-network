# Chapter 478 — Four Bolts On A Hinge At A Boundary, And Nobody Who Fitted It

**The Monday of week one hundred and seventy-seven was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that a man of about fifty-eight who works a barrier on a lane that runs along the line between the second and the third of the four districts of this city said, once, in about nine seconds, that the hinge on his gate and the hinge on the gate on the other side of that line are the same hinge, and it is a shape and not a person, and nobody has ever written down who fitted either of them, and it changed nothing at all and nobody thanked him.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and a boundary between two districts of this city is not a room and has never been one. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and sixty-three days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and sixty-seven days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a plate of iron with four bolts in it is a thirteenth object and is not the card and is not the hardboard and is not a key and a key is not a card.

---

The lane runs for about a quarter of a mile along a line that is the edge of one district and the start of another, and the line is not marked on the ground and is not on a plan anybody in this case has seen and is not a thing either district has ever put a name to.

There is a gate at each end of it. The man in question has opened and shut the one at this end every working day for about nineteen years. He is about fifty-eight. He is not a villager and he is not a villain and he is owed nothing by anybody.

**The hinge on that gate is a plate of iron about the width of a hand with four bolts in it and a pin, and the pin has a flat on it where somebody has been at it with a spanner in about four different decades.**

**The gate at the other end of the lane belongs to somebody else and the man has never opened it and has never been asked to, and its hinge is the same plate, with four bolts, and the pin has the same flat on it in the same place.**

He said it once. He said it at about eleven on the Monday morning to about four people who were waiting at the barrier, in about nine seconds, and what he said was that the two hinges are the same and that he has never seen anybody fit either one.

**Nobody at that barrier said anything back. One of the four said that was interesting and then said nothing else, and the other three were waiting for the lane to clear.**

**Nobody wrote it down. There is a hut at that barrier with a window and a radio in it and a book for vehicle numbers, and the book for vehicle numbers is the only book in that hut and it has one column in it and that column is for vehicles.**

About four people pass that barrier every day in cars and about nine of them pass it in vans, and the man has never once asked any of them which side of the line they sleep on, and about four of the about nine have noticed that he has never asked, and one of them has said so to another of them in a kitchen and neither of them has said anything to him.

Nobody in that hut has ever written anything else. Nobody at that barrier has ever rung anybody about a hinge and nobody has ever reported it and there is no list anywhere of the gates on that lane and not one of the two is on it.

**It is a shape and not a person. Nobody in this case knows who fitted either hinge, and nobody ever will, and there is no document about either one, and the four bolts are the whole of the fact and they have been there through four winters.**

**It changed nothing. The gate still opens when he opens it. The lane is still closed when the barrier is down. The boundary is still a line that is not marked on the ground, and the two districts are still two districts and have still never met, and the four rooms in four districts of this city have still never met either, and no sentence in this case joins the lane to any of them.**

---

The second of the four towns has a room over a shop in it that four people use after a shift and that nobody has ever named, and the shop under it opens at seven and shuts at six, and the room has a hatch in the floor and a table with a burn ring on it.

A man of about thirty-six who drives a minibus in that town was in it on the Monday evening at about eight with about four others and had come straight off a run.

He said it once, and what he said was nine words long, and what he said was this:

**"Nine minutes and nine seconds are all one answer."**

**He said it to about four people and he said it while putting his coat over the back of a chair, and about two of the four heard it and about two had already gone down to the shop, and neither of the two heard it has been told.**

Nobody in that room wrote it down. The hatch went down at about ten and the man who has the key to the shop under it was already asleep, and nobody knocked.

The minibus man had done two runs that day and about nine stops on each and he counted the stops out loud in the yard afterwards because counting them was the only thing left to do, and about four people in that town have heard him do it and about four of them have said that he counts them because nobody else would.

**Nobody in that town knows that about nine rooms in four towns have said the same nine words in a fortnight, and the man who drives the minibus has been in three of the other towns in his working life and has been in none of their rooms.**

---

The man of about thirty-four who loads bread was in the shop on the Monday at about half past two and did not mention the Thursday and nobody asked him, and he bought a box of screws, and the man at the counter did not look at the rail and did not look at him.

The man of about thirty-seven came in at about four and said that he had been to the open afternoon and that he was not going back, and that the form was still on his kitchen table, and that he had not thrown it out either.

**Nobody at that counter said what he should do with it. Nobody said keep it and nobody said bin it, and about four people there have said since that a form on a table is a thing a person does not think about again, and one of them has moved hers into a drawer.**

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the middle one filled.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about fifty-two weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-six weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Nobody in that room knew that four bolts in a plate of iron on a gate at one end of a lane are the same four bolts as the four bolts at the other end, and the man who said so does not know that room exists and has never been asked about it and is not going to be.**

**A correct thing and a wrong thing can both be said in about nine seconds at the same barrier on the same morning, and this case has no way of telling anybody which was which, and the man who said the true one was not thanked for saying it and the man who said the other one was not there.**

Who said they were the same: a man of about fifty-eight who has opened and shut his end every working day for about nineteen years, once, at about eleven on the Monday morning, to about four people waiting at the barrier, in about nine seconds. *What the day did not touch.* **Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Monday, and the woman's page is the eighth of the eight and is unread. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over. The four rooms in four districts of this city have still never met, and the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is still not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody. The four rooms in four towns have still not been introduced to each other, and about nine rooms across those four towns have now said nine words in a fortnight and not one of them knows any of the others. Nine keys are needed and two exist, and the first of the nine still cannot be asked back. Nine doors on a road on the far side of this city are still hanging by one hinge and no wage pays for a mender, and a week that about eleven people lost has not come back to them, and a bar across the inside of a door in each of four buildings in four towns is on nothing and appears in no list of anything. A printed form is on a kitchen table in this city and on about eight other tables, and its second column is empty in every one of them, and the man who wrote the sentence that asked the question has not copied a word of it out. The fortnight a cook laid out is still on nobody's account. The Exchange did not sit this week, its book did not grow, and no number was given into its tin. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody.
