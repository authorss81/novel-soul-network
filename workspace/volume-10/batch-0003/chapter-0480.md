# Chapter 480 — Five Hundred And Eighty-One Days Old On A Day A Landlord Turned A Heating On

**The Thursday of week one hundred and seventy-seven was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that the fifteen lines on the hardboard in a room off that service road are five hundred and eighty-one days old, and five hundred and eighty-one is a day in an earlier year when a landlord in this city put a heating on, and the figure here is an age and not a date, and the heating is a closed thing with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it, and nine words have now been said in nine rooms in four towns and not one of the nine people who said them knows the other eight exist and none of it has ever come near him.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and this was the tenth day of this week and the tenth week and nothing about it was different from any other Thursday in that respect. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and sixty-six days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and seventy days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a day in an earlier year when a landlord turned a heating on is not a card and is not a figure of days off for anything and is on nothing and is not in this case's arithmetic.

---

**The fifteen lines on the hardboard are five hundred and eighty-one days old, and five hundred and eighty-one is the day a landlord in an earlier year put a heating on in a building in this city.**

Those are the two halves of one number and they are not the same thing. The first is an age: it is how long a line of promise has been on a board on two nails in a room off a service road. The second is a day: it is an evening in an earlier year when somebody who is not in this case now turned a heating on for a set of rooms and it stayed on.

**Five hundred and eighty-one is the age of the fifteen lines and it is also the day the heating went on, and a person reading it is holding both at once, and the two readings are both true and neither of them is about a board or about a fortnight.**

**The heating in this case is a closed thing. It went off and a landlord put it on, in that order, and the last figure of it was printed in an earlier year and is not printed again, and there is no figure of days off for it anywhere in this case — not in anything anybody said, not in a record, not on a form, and not on a wall. There is no column for it and there is not going to be one.**

**A hot-water cylinder in a building in the second of the four districts is not that heating. A heating in a school in the first of the four towns that went off on a Wednesday is not that heating. A Friday afternoon in another district is not that heating. The fortnight a cook of twenty-seven laid out and nobody repaid is not that fortnight, and the two are not on the same sheet and no sentence in this case may put them there.**

The other seven lines on that board are six hundred and eighty-six, six hundred and thirty-seven, six hundred and two, five hundred and fifty-six, five hundred and thirty-eight, four hundred and eighty-four and four hundred and sixty-two, and none of those seven is a day that anything on that board was written and none of them is a day a heating in this city went on.

**Four names on a lease cost more than they did and about four people in this city pay for the thing that is on now every week and one of the four has said out loud, in a shop, that it is dearer, and nobody wrote it down and nobody asked which four and nobody is going to.**

---

The first of the four towns has a lock-up in it that a woman of about forty-three rents by the week, and about four people use it in the evenings because there is nowhere else cheap to sit, and it has a table in it and a heater that works about half the time and no window.

There were about four of them in there on the Thursday evening at about eight and the woman who rents it was in with a cup in her hand.

She said it once, and what she said was nine words long, and what she said was this:

**"Nine minutes and nine seconds, one answer, not two."**

**She said it to about four people and she said it while filling the heater, and about two of the four heard all of it and about two were talking out loud across the room to a person four days of bus travel away in a voice that carries.**

**Nobody in that lock-up has ever heard of the other eight rooms and nobody has introduced them, and the ninth of nine has been said in a room about four feet by about nine feet with a heater in it and a table and no window and a door that does not shut properly in wet weather.**

Nobody in that lock-up wrote it down. There is nothing in that lock-up to write it down on and nobody in that town has ever given anybody a piece of paper for saying nine words in a room.

The lock-up is let by the week and the woman who rents it pays for it out of a wage she earns four days a week, and about four people in that town know that and about nine do not, and nobody has ever asked her why she rents a room she does not appear to use for anything in particular, and she has never said.

About four of the people who use it have keys and about nine do not, and the door does not shut properly in wet weather, and the woman who rents it has said that she is not going to get it fixed because the man who would fix it is the man on the other side of the line and she has never spoken to him.

**Nobody in this city knows that nine words have been said nine times in four towns in about a fortnight, and nobody in this city has been told where any of the nine was said, and the man at the counter in Lattice Ward has not been told and could not be reached and nobody in this case went and told him.**

---

The woman of about thirty-nine who lets offices came in on the Thursday at about one and left her envelope on the counter and did not ask for anything and did not say good afternoon, and the man of about fifty-three at that end with a tray did not look at her.

**Nobody at that counter asked her how the four rooms were getting on and nobody has seen the two of them in the same building and nobody has put them together, and the two evenings a week she named out loud on the Wednesday are not on any rota in this city and are not on anything.**

The man of about thirty-four who loads bread came in at about half past four and bought nothing and said nothing and went out, and the Thursday he agreed to run once has now been and has not been run, and nobody at that counter asked him whether it went and nobody ever will.

**He is not owed anything. Nobody in this city has agreed what he is owed and nobody has asked him and he has not asked, and nobody in this case has ever found out what happened on that Thursday and is not going to go and find out.**

About four people at that counter have noticed that he has come in three times in a fortnight and bought nothing twice, and one of them has worked out that the day he did buy something was the day after he was told something, and has not said so to anybody and is not going to.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the middle one filled.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Thursday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about fifty-three weeks and one day. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-seven weeks without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

The tool bag is at his feet and it has a folded cloth in it and a bar in it and about nine other things, and about four people in that room have looked at the bag and about four have not, and he has not opened it in front of anybody in this case in any of the weeks he has been in it.

**Nobody in that room knew that nine words had been said in nine rooms in four towns in about a fortnight, and the four answers those nine rooms were attached to still disagree with each other, and the one woman who has been in this city stood in a room above a line and got a line into a book and has not been introduced to the other three rooms and does not know that any of the other three exist.**

*Five hundred and eighty-one, as a record of one figure that is an age and a date in the same token.* **What it is, first: the age in days of the fifteen lines on a hardboard in a room off a service road, on the Thursday of week one hundred and seventy-seven. What it is, second: an evening in an earlier year when a landlord in this city put a heating on in a building and it stayed on. Whether the two are the same thing: no, and the first is an age and the second is a date, and a person reading the figure is holding both without being able to put one down. Whether either of them is about a board: the first is and the second is not.
