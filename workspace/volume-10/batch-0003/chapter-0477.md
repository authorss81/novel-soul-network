# Chapter 477 — The Sixth Friday Of Ten, Three Hundred And Eight Days, And Two Boxes About Nine Feet Apart

**The Friday of week one hundred and seventy-six was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and he was not in Crown Terrace and the shop did not know and would not have known, and the one thing that is true of it is that about eleven people were in a room on a second floor for about nine minutes for the sixth Friday running and the post by the corridor end has now been bare for forty-four weeks, which is three hundred and eight days, and the man of about thirty-nine who buys training asked about the second box at the near end of the same corridor and about four people had already thought about the first one and none of the four ever said the thought out loud.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and the door on that second floor has a fire door on it that does not shut and has not shut for years. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and sixty days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and sixty-four days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a black post box on a wall on that second floor is a different object and is about nine feet from a second box and nobody in that building has ever been asked whether the two are a set.

---

The room is the same room. About eleven chairs out of a cupboard and a table at the front and a laptop on it connected to nothing in this case. The chairs were stacked against the wall when the first person came and were unstacked by the first person who came, and that is the sixth week running that two different people have done the two halves of that, and nobody has ever asked which two people those are.

The cupboard the chairs come out of has a lock on it and the key for the lock is on a hook inside the door of the room, and about four people on that floor have said that a room you can lock and then leave the key inside it is not locked in any way that matters, and none of them has said it to anybody who could change it.

There is a radiator under the window that has been cold on every one of the six Fridays, and about four of the about eleven have sat away from it, and nobody in that room has ever asked the woman who calls them in why it is cold in a room with a radiator in it.

* * *

About eleven people came, which is the same figure as the five Fridays before, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the figure is a figure and not a rate and is not on a form.

**The woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard said one word and pointed at the chairs and said nothing else, and about nine minutes went the way they go, and she did not say how long it would be and nobody asked.**

Six Fridays is not a run. It is six Fridays, and anybody who wants a run out of it has to wait, and there are four more Fridays after this one of which this case has seen none.

The post by the corridor end is bare forty-four weeks, which is three hundred and eight days from the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, and nobody in that room knows either of those figures.

**The card is still in it and the number on the card is still the number and the box is at the same height on the same wall, and the second box at the near end of the same corridor is still not empty, and the two are about nine feet apart.**

The second box is a different colour and it is a different make and it has something in it most Fridays, and nobody in that building has ever put anything in the first one while somebody was standing there.

The card in the first box is a folded rectangle of stiff card with a number typed at the top of it and nothing else on it at all, and about four people on that floor have picked it up in the last year and put it back the same way, and not one of them has turned it over, and there is nothing written on the other side that anybody has ever looked at.

The first box was put on that wall before the second one was, and about four people on that floor remember the second one arriving, and about four do not remember it arriving and have assumed it was always there, and the two groups have never discussed it and are not going to.

**The man of about thirty-nine who buys training sat at the back for the sixth Friday running and he asked one question, and what he asked was whether anybody in the building has ever put anything in the second box and watched somebody take it out.**

Nobody answered him. The woman of about thirty-six with the lanyard said the one word and pointed at the chairs, and about four people on that floor looked at the first box while she was pointing and about four looked at the second one.

**He asked it in about nine seconds and he did not ask it twice, and about nine of the eleven heard it and about two did not, and nobody has told the two since and nobody is going to.**

**About four people on that floor had already thought that thought about the first box while looking at the second one, and none of the four has ever said the thought out loud to anybody, and one of the four was standing in that room on this Friday.**

**The two figures on the back of his left hand are still there, eleven and then nine, and there is still no third one, and he has not washed them off and he has not looked at either of them in that room.**

The room emptied in about nine minutes the way it does, and about four people on that floor have said since that the six Fridays had been the quietest they can remember, and nobody on that floor knows what the quiet was about.

A woman of about thirty-one who works nights was at the back and said nothing for the whole nine minutes and has said nothing on five Fridays now, and about four people on that floor know which five and have never been told and have not asked.

She has been on the late shift on all six Fridays and has been in that room on all six, and about four of the about eleven have worked with her and have never once asked her what she does in the day, and she has never once told them, and neither of those two facts has ever come up in a sentence in that room.

There was a man of about twenty-three who has now been to six Fridays and has asked one ordinary question in all of them, and it was asked four Fridays ago, and he has not asked a second one and has not said anything else on any of the six.

**Nobody in that room said thank you, and about four people on that floor have said since that nobody has ever written a line down in that room in six Fridays, and the laptop on the table at the front is connected to nothing in this case and nobody in that room has ever said so out loud.**

The corridor outside that room is about four times as long as the room is wide and there are about nine doors along it, of which about four stand open and about four are locked and one is a fire door that does not shut, and nobody in that building has ever gone down it and looked at the backs of the locked ones.

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature and the man of about fifty-one had his tool bag at his feet.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Friday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about fifty-two weeks and two days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-six weeks and one day without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**He had been at the bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at that counter from about two until about six on the Friday, and the shop did not know that a man had asked about a second box in another building, and nobody in the room off that service road knew that a post on a second floor has been bare for three hundred and eight days.**

**A question about a box on a wall and a question about a column on a printed form are two questions asked in two buildings by two men, and neither of them was asked to the man at that counter and neither of them reached him, and about four people on that second floor have never heard of the form and about nine people in this city have never heard of the box.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
