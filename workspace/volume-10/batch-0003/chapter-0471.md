# Chapter 471 — Four Hundred And Sixty-Seven Days Old On The Day A Heating Went Off

**The Monday of week one hundred and seventy-five was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that the eighteen lines on the hardboard in a room off that service road are four hundred and sixty-seven days old, and four hundred and sixty-seven is a day in an earlier year when a heating went off in a building in this city, and the figure here is an age and not a date, and in the first of four towns on the far side of the road a woman of about twenty-four who works at a till said nine words once to about four people behind a counter, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked her.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and nobody in that shop knew anything about the four feet of shop-front this entry is about. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and forty-nine days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and fifty-three days old, which is four days more than the room is, and the rail holds two, and a card the size of a nail box on a shelf under a lamp in this city is a different object and is not in the rail and has not been in it since it came back from the far side of the road with nothing new on it.

---

**The eighteen lines on the hardboard are four hundred and sixty-seven days old, and four hundred and sixty-seven is the day a heating in a building in this city went off in an earlier year.**

Those are the two halves of one number and they are not the same thing. The first is an age. The second is a day, and it belongs to a heating and to nothing else that anybody has ever written down.

**Four hundred and sixty-seven is the age of the eighteen lines and it is also the day the heating went off, and the two are in the same token here and in no sentence anybody in this case has said, and the two are an age and a date and not one thing wearing the other's coat.**

The heating in this case is a closed thing. It went off and a landlord put it on, in that order, and the last figure of it was printed in an earlier year and is not printed again, and there is no figure of days off for it anywhere in this case — not in anything anybody said, not in a record, not on a form, and not on a wall.

The other seven lines on that board are five hundred and sixty-four, five hundred and twenty-one, five hundred and thirty-nine, six hundred and sixty-nine, four hundred and forty-five, six hundred and twenty and five hundred and eighty-five, and none of those seven is a day that anything on that board was written and none of them is a day a heating in this city went off.

---

The first of the four towns has a shop in it with four feet of shop-front, and behind the four feet there is a counter and a till and a back room with a table in it and a kettle, and the back room is a room and it is not a kitchen and it is not the room above a kitchen and nobody in that town has ever said otherwise.

There were about four people in the back room at about half past eight on the Monday evening and the woman at the till was about twenty-four and had been on the till since two.

She said it once, and what she said was nine words long, and what she said was this:

**"Nine minutes and nine seconds, one answer, not two."**

**She said it to about four people and she said it to a room, and the room did not repeat it, and nobody asked her where she had got it, and she did not say.**

One of the four had a hand on a bag of flour and did not look up. One was putting a chair away. One said *right* and went to a door. The fourth did not hear it and has not been told.

**Nobody in that room improved on it. Nobody in that room wrote it down. Nobody in that town knows that about nine other rooms on the other side of that road have said the same nine words in the same fortnight, and no two of those rooms has ever heard of any of the others, and nobody has introduced them.**

The card that came back from that town says eleven and not nine, and it is on a shelf under a lamp in this city, and it is the size of a nail box, and it has been on that shelf since the Monday before last, and nobody at that counter knows that a woman about twenty-four said nine words in a back room four days of bus travel away on the Monday evening.

**The back room has a window that does not open and about four chairs and a table with a cloth on it that has been there longer than anybody can remember, and the kettle in it is the one thing in that room that anybody has ever repaired.**

**Nobody in that town has written down the nine words, and about four people in it have heard them, and there is no second copy of them anywhere on that side of the road, and a person who was not in the back room on the Monday has no way of knowing that anything was said in it.**

**The four towns have a shop, a weighbridge, a kitchen and a school corridor in them and about nine rooms between them, and not one of those nine has ever been told the name of any of the other eight, and four people have each carried a question to some of them on foot and on a bus and two of those four have lost a day of work doing it.**

---

A man of about thirty-seven who drives vans came to the counter at about ten past four with a letter in his hand and put it on the counter face down, which is a thing about four people a week do at that counter and about four a week do the other way up.

He said he had written to a company. He said he had asked them a question in one sentence and put it in the post and paid for the post himself. He said he was not asking the man at the counter to answer it and he was not asking him to read it.

**Nobody at that counter read the letter. It went into a drawer under the till at about ten past four and it is in that drawer, and the man of about fifty-three at that end with a tray did not look at it and has never been asked to.**

He said the company holds an open afternoon once a month in a room in the first of the four districts, and that anybody can walk in off the street, and that he had heard about four people went last time, and that he had never been.

He gave his name to the man of about fifty-three and not to the man at the bench, and the man of about fifty-three said nothing back and put a tray down on the counter about a foot from the envelope, and the envelope was under the tray for the rest of the afternoon and came out again at about six when the shutter came down.

The letters in that drawer are about nine months of letters and none of them is opened at that counter and none of them is ever thrown out, and about four people who work in that building have said since that the drawer is where things go when nobody knows what else to do with them, and none of them has ever looked in it.

Nobody said he should go. Nobody said he should not. He put his hand flat on the counter for a moment and then took it off and said the post was going to cost him about a pound and that he had not been paid for the week the question is about.

**That is a cost and he named it out loud, once, at the end of a counter, and it is not one of the two this week is carrying and it is the first time anybody at that counter has said a price for a stamp in about four years.**

He said he had written it at the kitchen table on the Sunday and that it had taken him about four goes at it and that he had crossed the first two out, and that what is in the envelope is the third one and it is one sentence long.

**Nobody at that counter said anything about the sentence, and about four people there have said since that asking a company a question in writing is a thing a person does alone at a kitchen table on a Sunday, and none of them has done it and none of them has said they would.**

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and the kettle went on once and no cup was made.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about fifty weeks and five days. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-four weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

About four of the nine who came in said nothing and stood by the door and then left, and nobody asked them why.

The middle one is the one that fills and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was in it for about three hours and said nothing to anybody, and about four people came in for the temperature and about four went out again inside twenty minutes.

**Nobody in that room knew that nine words had been said four days of bus travel away, and about four people in that room have said since that a number is a number, and a week is a week, and nobody has put the two together and nobody is going to.**

The eighteen lines are four hundred and sixty-seven days old and the heating went off on a day that is four hundred and sixty-seven, and the cook of twenty-seven at that sink has not been told either of those and was not asked and the woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door has not been told either of those either.

**Nobody in this case has found out who turned about nine into four hundred, and nobody in this case has asked, and the woman who carries a question to four rooms on foot and on a bus does not know either and would not say if she did.**

Who said it: a woman of about twenty-four who had been on a till since two. How many words: nine. What they were: nine minutes and nine seconds, one answer, not two. *Four hundred and sixty-seven, as a record of one figure that is an age and a date in the same token.* **What it is, first: the age in days of the eighteen lines on a hardboard in a room off a service road, on the Monday of week one hundred and seventy-five. What it is, second: a day in an earlier year when a heating in a building in this city went off. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over.
