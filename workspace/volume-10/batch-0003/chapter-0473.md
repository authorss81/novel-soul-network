# Chapter 473 — Four Numbers That Are Each Other's Days, And An Open Afternoon With Nine People In It

**The Thursday of week one hundred and seventy-five was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and the one thing that is true of it is that four figures stand on the same day and three of them are the day another thing was written, and the card in the rail is seven hundred and fifty-six days old, and the man of about fifty-one has been counted for fifty-one weeks and one day, and the ask is four hundred and forty-two days, which is sixty-three weeks and one day, and the twelve lines on the hardboard are six hundred and seventy-two days old, and a man of about thirty-seven stood in a room with about nine people in it on the Wednesday and was handed the same form again with a second column still empty.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six, and a form in a building in the first of the four districts was on about nine tables by the end of the day. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and fifty-two days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the rail is seven hundred and fifty-six days old, and that figure is the day the man of about fifty-one's count began, and the two are an age and a day and are not one thing, and the hardboard and the rail are two surfaces in two buildings and nothing in this entry joins them.

---

**Four figures are on this Thursday and three of them are the day another thing was written, and here is which is which about each of the four, because a person reading them in a row will be holding all four at once.**

**The card in a rail in Lattice Ward is seven hundred and fifty-six days old. Seven hundred and fifty-six is also the day the man of about fifty-one's run of days was counted from, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

**The man of about fifty-one has now said nothing for fifty-one weeks and one day, which is three hundred and fifty-eight days. Three hundred and fifty-eight is also the day the card in that rail was put in it, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

**The ask is four hundred and forty-two days, which is sixty-three weeks and one day. Four hundred and forty-two is also the day the twelve lines were written on the hardboard in the room off that service road, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

**The twelve lines on that hardboard are six hundred and seventy-two days old. Six hundred and seventy-two is also the day the ask was put, in front of nine people, on a Wednesday, and the first of those is an age and the second is a day.**

Those are four pairs, and every figure in every pair is a real figure and not a mistake, and the two halves of each pair are an age and a day and are not the same thing.

**Nobody in this case has ever printed those four on one line before, and this is the first time, and a person who has read this cannot put the four figures down separately again.**

The other six lines on that board are six hundred and twenty-three, five hundred and eighty-eight, five hundred and sixty-seven, five hundred and forty-two, five hundred and twenty-four, four hundred and seventy and four hundred and forty-eight, and none of those six is the day any of the four above was written and none of them is a day the ask was put.

**A card in a rail is not a hardboard. A hardboard is not a hold. A hold is not a question. Nobody in this case may put two of the four in one clause, and the record below names them one at a time for that reason.**

---

The open afternoon was on the Wednesday in a room on the ground floor of a building in the first of the four districts, and the door was open, and about nine people went in over an hour and a half.

The man of about thirty-seven was one of them and he took nothing with him.

**He said, in the room, in front of about nine people, that he had asked one question in one sentence and had paid for the post himself, and that he wanted it read out, and about four people in that room heard him and the rest were talking.**

Nobody read it out. A person behind the table, who gave no name and was not asked for one, put a printed form on the table in front of him and it was the same form, and it had the same two columns, and the second column was still empty, and there was a tick in the first one that had not been there on the Wednesday morning.

**He said the tick had moved. Nobody in that room said it had or had not, and about four people looked at the first column and about four looked at the second one, and nobody said anything about either.**

He was in that room for about an hour and he was given nothing else, and he did not ask for anything else, and about four people in that room have said since that it was a wasted afternoon, and one of them has said that it was the right thing to have done, and the two of them have not spoken since.

He waited until about four of the nine had gone before he stood up. The person behind the table took the forms off it and squared them and put them in a folder and carried the folder out through a door at the back that is not the door the nine came in by, and he saw her go and said nothing to the door.

He said, on the step outside, to nobody, that he had come with one sentence and had left with a sheet that had two columns in it and that the two of those were not the same size, and about four people who were leaving at the same time heard him and about nine did not, and none of the four has said since that they knew what he meant, and he has not been in that room since and has not said that he will not be.

**Nobody in that room was thanked by anybody for coming, and nobody's question was read out, and the company kept no record of the afternoon and this case did not go and ask for one.**

**A form that has been on a table in a kitchen, on a counter and in a room with about nine people in it is still not a document anybody may rely on, and nobody has found a thing in it that is untrue, and the tick moving is the only change in it that anybody has seen and nobody can say who moved it.**

---

The fourth of the four towns has a hired hall in it that people pay for by the hour, and the hall has about nine chairs in it that do not match each other, and a woman of about twenty-seven who does the paperwork at a haulage office in that town had it booked from about half past seven.

There were about three other people in it, which is fewer than the chairs.

She said it once, and what she said was nine words long, and what she said was this:

**"Nine minutes and nine seconds are the same answer."**

**She said it to about three people and she said it while counting chairs out of a cupboard, and about two of the three heard it and one of them has said since that she thought the woman was counting something.**

Nobody in that hall wrote it down. The chairs were counted and stacked and the hall was locked and the key went back on a hook by the door, and the hook is the ordinary kind of hook and not a box of any kind.

**Nobody in that town knows that about nine other rooms in four towns have said the same nine words in a fortnight, and no two of those rooms knows any of the others, and the haulage office where that woman works has never heard of the four rooms in four districts of this city and is not going to.**

---

He went to the room at about half past six and did not stay long. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing and about four people came in for the temperature.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Thursday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about fifty-one weeks and one day. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp, no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about fifty-five weeks without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

**Nobody in that room knew that three hundred and fifty-eight is the day a card went into a rail on the other side of this city, and nobody in that room has ever asked the man of about fifty-one when he started, and he has never said, and nobody has ever noticed that he has never said.**

**The middle one is the one that fills and about four people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and about four went out again, and the cook of twenty-seven washed about nine things and made one cup and it was not made for anybody in particular.**

A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and a folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields was not turned over.
