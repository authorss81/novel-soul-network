# Chapter 222 — Eight Sittings And A Print Run

The back room with the shutters up had its eighth sitting on the Wednesday morning of week seventy-six, and the book did not open, and in the four feet of a print shop in the Quiet Quarter a woman of fifty-eight and a woman of forty-four found out what was on page two of a training pack, and neither of them was in that room and neither of them was thanked.

---

The shutters went up at about half past nine and the room was hot by eleven, and the tin went into a drawer with no lock on it at eleven, because a hand went to the shutter at eleven and that is the signal and it has not changed.

Eight chairs round the table. A ninth has been carried in by hand and set against the wall facing away from the room by a young woman of nineteen who has been coming to that room since she was fifteen and who has never once been asked to move it and never once been named in it.

There have been eight sittings. There have been seven gaps of four weeks. The Wednesday of week seventy-two is exactly four weeks back, which is the only interval in that room that anybody can check.

The rule is given in one go at every sitting of that room. It is not abridged and it is not improved upon.

"**A book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call.**"

She put her hand on the tin.

"**Eight sittings, seven gaps, and the run of sittings at which this book has not been opened is seven and a half. And there is still nobody in this city who can tell you what a half is meant to mean, and I have not gone to find out.**"

---

"**Thirty-five**," she said, at about half past ten, and nobody had asked.

"It was twenty-nine on the Wednesday of week sixty-four and thirty-one on the Wednesday of week sixty-eight and thirty-three four weeks ago."

"And I have said that number out loud in this room thirteen times, and eight of those are the eight Wednesdays in this book and the first five are not in it and are not going to be." She did not look up. "I have said the arithmetic out loud once and I am not going to say it again, and I am saying the number, and the number is not the thing."

Somebody said, "What is the thing."

"**That two people telephoned in the four weeks and I told both of them to come on a Wednesday, and I am not going to say which of the four callers in twelve weeks was in this room, and the reason I gave in the spring is a reason I have now given twice and I am not going to give it a third time.**"

"And the other one," said a man of about thirty.

"**I have not telephoned her.**"

---

The book has four columns and forty-five lines and it did not open, and this is the first time it has been shut since a woman stood up in it and signed her own name under her own words.

"**It is shut because it grew in the spring and I have been asked since when it is going to grow again.**"

Nine or ten people in the room said nothing.

"**I do not know. I have had about nineteen years of being able to answer any question anybody asked me in this room, and there is one I cannot, and I am not going to open a book on a table in front of nine people on a Wednesday when the answer to the only question there is no.**"

---

There were two items and four minutes each and neither of them went in the book, and there is no line in this case that says what either of them was beyond what the people said in the room.

The first was a woman of about thirty-one who wanted to know whether a room that has been photographed is a different room, and she had come because she has read about four hundred words and had been shown a second picture on the same morning.

Bea Nunn gave her the answer in about four minutes and it was not about photographs.

"There have been two cameras in this room in nineteen years and both of them belonged to a man from a paper who wanted a picture of a queue, and I said no to both, and I was right twice, and each of those two times it made me a smaller woman in this room for about a month afterwards and I could not make it back." She put the tin back in the drawer. "A room with the shutter up and about nine people in it and a book that is not open is not worth photographing, and I have had the chance twice to prove it and I took it."

The second was a man of about forty-one who wanted to know whether a thing his employer had printed can be taken off it after the fact, and he was told what she tells about four hundred people in nineteen years, and he wrote nothing down, and neither did she.

And a man of twenty-two was in the corner with a docket and had said nothing for about two hours, and she did not thank him, and she has not thanked him, and the arrangement is the arrangement.

---

Four feet of shop in the Quiet Quarter, at about half past four on the same Wednesday.

The shop is four feet wide and it has a counter, a press, a rack of about nine hundred leaflets that came off it that morning, a card of the shop's own with a quarterly return on it, and a till with a hundred and forty-one pounds in an envelope under it that has no name on it, cannot be sent back, and has not moved since the spring.

The printer is fifty-eight. She has been doing this for about forty years. She is the woman who wrote *it will not be printed* on a piece of hardboard in week seventy and named the cost of that line out loud in front of about eight people.

A clerk of forty-four came in at about half past four to have a card printed and had a file under her arm.

She works at a counter in a public building in Lattice Ward. She has been in the room off the service road twice, in the ordinary way, and has not asked for anything on either occasion, and she is not on the wall and not in the binder and her name is in no file in this case.

---

The printer had four things on the counter, which is one more than a person who prints for a living usually has, and she was moving between them.

"There," she said. "**That is the pack. That is the page that goes in it on page two. And that is nine hundred leaflets that came off this counter this morning and are on a pallet in a room with a door.**"

The clerk looked at the page.

It is a photograph on a printed page and it is a photograph of a counter with a ring binder standing open on it, the pages standing above the sleeve, and a shutter up behind, and a window, and a hardboard on the wall by the door, and about six shapes behind the counter that are not faces.

"That is our counter," the clerk said. "**I have been in it twice.**"

"Yes."

"You printed it."

"**I printed it.**"

---

And then the clerk said the other thing, which was the reason she had come in with a file under her arm at half past four on a Wednesday and not on a Tuesday.

"There is a pack," she said. "**It is on a shelf in the building where I work. About four days. Somebody gave one to me out of a stack and it has the same picture on page two and under the picture it says *our method*, and the method is the one where a room asks a person what they do and writes it in their own words.**"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"And there are about nine hundred of the leaflets," said the printer, "**and they have got that picture on them, and they have got those words on them, and the leaflets say *our method* and I have run nine hundred of them off that counter in one morning, and I did not write the words.**"

"No," said the clerk. "**And I am going to say the rest of it, because you are the only person in this city who can tell me whether it is true, and because I have had it for about four days and I have not asked anybody else.**"

"Go on."

"**I can read it.**"

---

The printer put her hand flat on the page and did not say anything for about nine seconds.

"That is my hand," she said. "**That is the page in the sleeve and the top of it is mine, and it is nine words, and it is about a proof and a line that will be read wrong, and I wrote it in a ring binder in a room off a service road in the first week of this month, and a camera was in that room, and I did not know, and I have printed about nine hundred leaves of a pack with my own handwriting nine inches tall on the top of page two.**"

The clerk said: "I am sorry."

"**Do not be. I want to say what it is and I have had about two hours to think about it and this is it. A page in a binder is a thing you can take out. A page in a binder in a photograph is a page in about nine hundred pairs of hands, and I am the only person in this city who could tell you with certainty that it cannot be un-printed, and I have been wrong about that four times in forty years and I am not wrong now.**"

She looked at the rack of about nine hundred leaflets.

"**And nobody is going to ring me about it. There is no number on that pack that is mine.**"

---

And the clerk of forty-four named her own cost, which nobody had asked her for, and she named it in about nine seconds and she did not improve it.

"**I took it off a shelf and I have been askable about it since. I work at a counter. I have had four people ask me where I got it and one of them was a man who wanted a discount because I had not come through a form. I am not a special case and there is nothing about me. If I had left it on that shelf I would have been askable about it for about four more days, and then somebody would have taken it, and then somebody else would have been askable, and that is how four hundred words and a photograph get to be a thing four hundred people have read.**"

Neither of them was thanked. The card got printed, which is what she came in for, and it took about four minutes, and she paid for it.

And the printer, at the door, with the press cooling behind her, said the sentence she had written on a wall in six weeks ago and had never once improved on, and she said it to a shutter and not to anybody.

"**It will not be printed.**"

---

He got to the bench in Lattice Ward at about ten that night with a word that had been at ten all day and two keys in a pocket and a shutter in another building that was up because it was a Wednesday and nobody opens on a Wednesday after eleven.

He wrote one thing.

*225. Wednesday of week 76, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a fourth-column book that stayed shut at its eighth sitting, and a print run of about nine hundred.*

*The run, as an arithmetic and not as a mood.* **Eight sittings. Seven gaps. Every gap is four weeks and the most recent one is the only interval a person in that room can check unaided. Six of the eight passed with the book closed; the seventh added one line; this is the eighth and it is closed, and the tally of sittings at which nothing was added is therefore seven and a half, a figure the woman of sixty-one volunteered for the second time together with the statement that nobody in this city can tell her what a half signifies and that she has not gone to look. The book stands at forty-five lines. The tin carries no lock, was carried to a drawer with no lock at about eleven because the shutters had been up since half past nine, and stayed shut. The reason for the shutter going up at eleven on a hot morning is nineteen years old and is not a decision anybody takes.**

*Three figures and a reconciliation, given by the person who owns them.* **The telephone count has moved at every one of the last three sittings and stands at thirty-five. Two callers in four weeks, both told to come on a Wednesday, one of them in the room and one of them absent, and she will not say which of four callers in twelve weeks it was. She has now announced that number in that room thirteen times; eight announcements belong to the eight Wednesdays the book itself holds; the first five predate the book and will not enter it; she performed that subtraction aloud, unasked, and then declined to repeat it.The number is a count of her own announcements and not a count of the sittings, the room predates the book, and no entry in this movement may convert it into one. She has not made the call she declined to make in the spring, has given her reason for that twice, and refused it a third time in the same words.**

*The rule, whole, and the two items that did not enter the book.* **Four columns; a caller's own words in the first; the caller's own name in the fourth and nobody else's; four minutes an item; no vote for anybody. The rule was given complete and unaltered. Two items were taken and neither is written down anywhere: one concerned whether a photographed room is a different room, and was answered from the fact that two cameras have been in that room in nineteen years, both belonging to the same man from a paper, both refused, both refusals correct, and each refusal followed by a month of being taken less seriously in that room; the other concerned whether printed matter can be withdrawn after printing, and received the answer that room has given about four hundred times in nineteen years. A man of twenty-two occupied a corner for roughly two hours and said nothing; he was not thanked and the arrangement between them is unchanged and is not described further in any file.**

*The shop, inventoried.* **One counter, one press, one rack, one card of the shop's own with a quarterly return on it, and one till. Under the till, one envelope, no name, unrecoverable, unmoved since the spring, and the figure is a hundred and forty-one pounds. The proprietor is fifty-eight and has printed for about forty years; one of the wall's ten lines is hers and that line names her own money as its price; one of the four hands on a composite sheet in the room's binder is hers; one page in that binder is hers alone, being about nine words on a proof and a line that will be read wrong; and a quarterly return in another building carries the description of four feet of shop in the district of her as whoever set the type in type.**

*What was found, in which building, and by whom.* **A training pack carries on its second page a photographic reproduction of the room's counter, taken from the doorway, showing the open binder, the raised sleeve, the shutter, the window, the unscrewed hardboard, and six unresolvable figures behind the counter. The same image appears on a leaflet of which about nine hundred impressions were run from that counter on a single morning, and beneath it the words *our method* describe, in the second person of a company, a practice belonging to six people in a room. The identification was made in the shop, in the Quiet Quarter, at about half past four on this day, by the proprietor and by a second person who brought a file down to be printed and was still waiting for it.The second person is a clerk of forty-four at a counter in a public building in Lattice Ward, has twice been in the room in the ordinary way, has asked nothing on either occasion, holds no key, no post, no title, no standing, appears on no line of that wall, appears in no sheet of that binder, and is named in no document in this case. She removed the pack from an open shelf in her own building, has carried the question for four days, and has been asked about the source by four people, one of whom requested a discount on the ground that she had not come through a form.**

*The single fact the chapter turns on.* **The topmost sheet standing above the binder's sleeve is legible in the reproduction, and the hand on it belongs to the printer of fifty-eight. Her own nine words, put in that binder by her own hand, are reproduced about nine hundred times inside another company's property, and she is the only person in this city able to state as a certainty that a printed sheet cannot be unprinted, having been wrong about that four times in forty years. She stated also that the pack carries no number of hers, so no enquiry will arrive at four feet of shop.The clerk of forty-four priced her own part without being asked: a fortnight of being askable, four enquiries, one of them commercial, and a mechanism by which a thing left on a shelf is taken by a person and then another person is left askable, which is the whole of how a set of printed words becomes a thing a great many people have read.**

*Refused, withheld, and the two objects kept apart.* **The room was not told any of this by anybody in the room, and the wall was not read aloud; the wall is ten lines in six hands and seven names and is not the hardboard by the door, and neither is the binder. Nobody answered, refused or withdrew a leaflet, a prospectus or a pack. The caption beneath the picture is not reproduced here, the firm is not named, the course is not named, the city is not named, the woman who telephoned the plate is not named, the day of the exposure is not set in any file, and the person who held the camera is not in this movement.Nothing in this chapter is a share, a relay or a practice, and not one person in it taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything. The condition is not quoted anywhere in this entry. The standing question is not stated on this page, nothing has been invented about who may be put to a question, and no figure is attached to one. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, and the box in pencil in another building still holds one name and was not touched. Entry 225..**
