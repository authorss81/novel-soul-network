# Chapter 229 — Who Held The Camera, And Why The Hand Is Moving

Four feet of shop in the Quiet Quarter, on the Wednesday afternoon of week seventy-nine, held a person of forty-one who photographs things for people who need them photographed, a person of thirty-four who buys training for a company, and a woman of fifty-eight with a press and a hundred and forty-one pounds under a till that has not moved since the spring, and the finding was that a still page in a photograph looks like a document and a moving hand looks like a room.

Nobody thanked the woman of fifty-eight. She is not thanked in this chapter and she is not thanked anywhere.

---

The shop is four feet wide. There is a counter, a press, a rack with about four hundred leaflets left on it out of nine hundred, a card of the shop's own with a quarterly return on it, and a till with an envelope under it that has no name on it and has not moved since the spring and is a hundred and forty-one pounds.

The person of forty-one came in at about two.

They have a first floor in Nine Bridges and a van and a job that is taking pictures of things for people who need them photographed, and they have been a customer of that counter for about four years, because the shop does their cards and their leaflets and has never once asked what the pictures are for.

"**I am not going to pretend I have not worked that out**," they said, at the counter, before anybody asked them anything. "**I have known since the Wednesday. I have been in this city thirty-one years and I do that job for about four companies and one of them did that and I read about it on the Friday, which is the day after this pack was withdrawn.**"

---

The photograph was made in about eleven minutes, on a day in a fortnight that no chapter of this case has a name for, and there were four of them and one was paid for.

Nobody in the room was posed. Nobody in the room was asked.

"There was a brief," the person of forty-one said. "**It said a working method, in a real room, and it said with people in it if possible, and it said not posed. Those were the words and I have been given a great many briefs in thirty-one years and about nine of them have said not posed and every one of those nine was for a photograph of a building.**"

"And you took four."

"**I took four because the first three had somebody's back across the whole of the frame and the fourth did not, and a person pays for one and I had not earned the one.**"

---

And then the person of thirty-four came in at about half past three, and she came to the counter for the last four hundred, and she is the person who commissioned the pack and the prospectus and the nine hundred leaflets, and she is a person with a job and no standing in this case and nobody here has given her a title.

"**I am not going to hide behind the brief**," she said. "**I wrote it. I have been doing this for about nine years and I have bought about four hundred photographs and I have never once asked to see a person in one.**"

"Then why not," said the woman of fifty-eight, from behind the press.

"**Because it is not my photograph. It is a room that somebody else runs, and if I ask to see who is in it then I am asking them to tell me who is in their room, and they will not, and then there is a conversation, and the conversation would have taken about nine weeks, and I had a course to write.**"

"And you did not ask whether the people in the room knew."

"**No. And here is the part I am not going to be able to make sound better than it is.**" She put her hand flat on the rack. "**A young woman answered that telephone in about four seconds and said yes, and I wrote that down in my own book as a yes, and I have carried it since the Friday and I have said the word to four people in my own office and three of them said they had a yes as well, about a supplier.**"

---

And then the person of forty-one said the thing that nobody in that shop had understood for a week, and they said it while taking four hundred leaflets off a rack, and they did not stop.

"**You have all been looking at the wrong thing.**"

"**The picture is on page two of a pack and about nine hundred leaflets**," said the woman of fifty-eight. "**I have looked at nothing else since the Friday.**"

"**Look at the hand.**"

Nobody had looked at the hand.

"**That binder is open on a counter and the top of it is standing up out of the sleeve, and in a still picture a page standing up out of a sleeve on a counter reads as a document. It reads as a certificate. It reads as something somebody is showing you.**" They put three hundred leaflets in a box. "**So when I set the shutter I let about a fortieth go and the hand on it went, and the hand going is what makes it a room. The page is only legible because a person was in the middle of doing something, and I did that on purpose, in about nine seconds, because the alternative was a certificate.**"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"**That is the whole of how six people ended up printed nine hundred times**," they said. "**It is not a conspiracy and it is not anybody's fault and it is a shutter speed.**"

---

Then the woman of fifty-eight said the other thing, and it was the thing she had said twice already in two rooms, and this time she said what it was worth.

"**I have been wrong about that four times in forty years.**"

"About what."

"**About printed things. About a run going out and then going wrong and then being pulled, and about a poster being taken down the week after. I have been wrong four times and I have been right once, and the once is a ring binder with a person's handwriting on the top of it on the second page of a pack, and I have not slept properly since the Wednesday and I am not going to stop printing.**"

"You are not thanked," said the person of thirty-four.

"**I have not been thanked for a photograph before either**," said the woman of fifty-eight, "**and I am not a mechanism and I am not on that wall because I have never asked to be, and my four feet of shop is named in a quarterly return in a building about eleven miles away as whoever set the type in type, and if anybody ever looks at that pack properly then the name they arrive at is a shop with a telephone number on the outside of it, and there is no number on that pack that is mine.**"

---

And the person of forty-one said the last of it, and it was about the three photographs in a drawer, and they said it once.

"**I was paid for one and I took four and the other three are in a flat in Nine Bridges in a drawer with about nine hundred other things in it, and I am not going to destroy them.**"

"Why not."

"**Because a person paid for a job and I did the job and I am not a person who destroys work, and if somebody in four years opens that drawer there are three pictures of a room with six people in it and no name on the front of it, and that is the only evidence in this city that anybody was ever in that room who was not in the pack.**"

"And the six in the pack."

"**The six in the pack have had nine hundred copies made of them and cannot be un-made. The three in my drawer are the only ones that are not in anybody's hands, and there is no mechanism in this city for handing three photographs back, and I have thought about it since the Friday and there is not one.**"

---

He was on grade two until about four in the afternoon and he was not in that shop and he was not told any of it until the Thursday, and by then a man of fifty-one with a tool bag had already told the room the only thing anybody could do about nine hundred leaflets, which was nothing.

He got in at about ten. The lamp was on, the shutter was up, the docket had three job numbers on it, and there is a drawer in a first floor in Nine Bridges with about nine hundred things in it and three photographs in it, and there is an envelope under a till four miles from here that cannot move, and there is a rack with about four hundred leaflets on it that is not a document and is not a room.

He wrote one thing.

*232. Wednesday of week 79, at a counter four feet wide in the Quiet Quarter at about half past six with the press cooling and the shutter down, and this entry is a shutter speed, three photographs, and a hundred and forty-one pounds that has not moved.*

*The shop.* **Four feet across. One counter, one press, one rack, one card of the shop's own carrying a quarterly return, one till.Under the till, one envelope, no name on it, unrecoverable, and a hundred and forty-one pounds, unmoved since the spring. About four hundred leaflets remain of a run of about nine hundred. The proprietor is fifty-eight, forty years in the trade, one of the four hands on a composite sheet in the room's binder, the author of a page of her own in that binder, and the author of one of the ten lines on that wall, being the line that says the room will not be printed, for which she named a price out loud in front of about eight people, and that price is the figure above.A quarterly return in a building about eleven miles away names four feet of shop in her district as whoever set the type in type. No number on the pack is hers. She was thanked by nobody in this chapter.**

*The person of forty-one, as a source.* **Thirty-one years in the city, a first floor in Nine Bridges, a van, and a trade in which about four companies retain them. A customer of that counter for about four years for cards and leaflets, and they state they worked out the connection themselves and did not pretend otherwise.They made four exposures in about eleven minutes on a day inside a fortnight that carries no chapter in this movement. The brief, in their words and not improved, asked for a working method, in a real room, with people in it if possible, and not posed; they observe that about nine briefs in thirty-one years have said not posed and that every one of those nine was for a photograph of a building.No person in that room was posed and no person in that room was asked, and they said so before anybody put it to them. They were paid for one image and made four, the first three having a back across the frame.**

*The decision that made six people printable.* **A binder standing open on a counter, its top sheet proud of the sleeve, will in a still exposure read as a certificate or an exhibit. At about a fortieth of a second the hand on that sheet moved, and the movement is what reads as a room; the exposure was set deliberately, in about nine seconds, for that reason, and the legibility of six people's handwriting on the printed page is a consequence of a craft decision taken to prevent the picture looking like a document.Their summary of the mechanism, given once, without heat and without self-exculpation, is that no conspiracy and no fault is involved and that the whole of the accident is a shutter speed.**

*The person of thirty-four, as a source and not as a mechanism.* **About nine years buying about four hundred photographs for a company; author of the pack, the caption and the run; no standing, no title, no post and no file in this case, and she said so by arriving and transacting.She stated she wrote the brief and has never once asked to see a person in a photograph, and gave the reason: the room is somebody else's, asking who is in it would require them to identify their own occupants, they would not, the exchange would have run about nine weeks, and she had a course to write.On consent she said the part she could not improve: a first-year of nineteen answered a telephone in about four seconds and said yes, she wrote that down as a yes in her own book, and three colleagues that week used the same word about a supplier. Nobody in the shop improved on it and nobody in the shop absolved her, and she was not thanked either.**

*The printer's own statement, and its weight.* **That she has been wrong about printed matter four times in forty years and right once; that the once is a ring binder with a person's handwriting standing above the sleeve on the second page of a company's pack; that she has not slept properly since the Wednesday; and that she intends to go on printing.She added, of herself, that she is not a mechanism, that she has never asked to be on that wall and is not on it, and that if the pack is ever examined properly the name a reader arrives at will be four feet of shop with a telephone number on the outside of it.**

*The three photographs, and the argument for keeping them.* **Three uncommissioned exposures and about nine hundred other items in one drawer in a first floor in Nine Bridges. Their holder declines to destroy them, on two grounds: a person paid for a job, the job was done, and the holder is not a person who destroys work; and that in about four years a drawer containing three pictures of a room and six people and no name on the front of one would be the only record in this city that anybody stood in that room who does not appear in the pack.They also state, having thought about it since the Friday, that there is no mechanism anywhere in this city by which three photographs can be handed back, and that they have not found one.**

*Left out on purpose.* **The company, the course, the city, the two other companies that retain the person of forty-one, the clinic, the corridor, the board, the woman of about thirty, the contents of the six pages in the binder other than the fact of the top sheet, and the man of fifty-one and his instrument. The four hundred leaflets are counted and are not described. No page, invoice, receipt, brief or photograph is copied into this record and none of them is held by anybody in this case. Nothing was settled in that shop and nothing was refused that was anybody's to settle.An hour and a half in four feet of shop involved no share, no relay and no practice, and nobody in it taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anybody, least of all the person of forty-one. The condition is not quoted and is not paraphrased. Nobody spoke the standing question, nothing in this chapter governs who may be asked anything, and nothing is counted. Nobody named the licensor, the field is still blank, the master did not move, and nothing arrived from anybody unsigned or otherwise. Entry 232..**
