# Chapter 225 — Our Method

On the Thursday evening of week seventy-eight, in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards, a woman of fifty-eight with four feet of shop and a woman of forty-four with a counter read out the four lines printed under a photograph of a ring binder standing open on that counter, and not one of the four lines said *we* or *our* or *us*, and the two words set above them were *our method*, and about nine hundred leaflets had gone out with them, and a man of twenty-two was in the room and could not get to the sentence.

Nothing was stopped. That is the finding and it took ten days to arrive at.

---

The pack came in at about half past seven in a plastic bag with a strap on it and it was about forty pages, stapled in the corner, and it is a company's document and it is not the only copy and it is not a form.

The clerk of forty-four brought it. She works at a counter in a public building in Lattice Ward, she has been in that room twice in the ordinary way, and she has asked nothing on either occasion, and she put the bag on the counter and took a step back from it before she said anything.

The printer of fifty-eight was already there. She had come on her own time and she had not brought the pack and she had not been asked to bring anything.

About eleven people in the room. Nine chairs in a curve and four of them standing in about two feet of floor between the counter and the hardboard, which is how that room is now.

---

Asha Reed opened it on the counter, and the second page is a photograph, and it is the photograph about four hundred people in another city have read.

And under it, in a company's typeface at about nine point, four lines.

"**Read it**," said the clerk of forty-four. "**I have read it about nine times and I cannot say it out loud, so I am going to make myself say it in front of people.**"

And the printer of fifty-eight read it out, in her own voice, at a counter, with a shop four feet wide and a hundred and forty-one pounds under a till behind her.

"*A working method, in a real room. The room asks a person what they do and records it in their own words. Nothing is claimed, nothing is standardised, and the practitioner can withdraw at any time.*"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**Say the second one again**," said the cook of twenty-seven.

The printer of fifty-eight said it again.

---

And then the man of twenty-two, who had been at the back with a docket since about seven, said something and did not finish it.

"**It is not ours and it is not wrong.**"

"Go on," said the clerk of forty-four.

"**It is the wall.**"

"**It is not the wall**," said the printer of fifty-eight, before he had got a second sentence out, and she had not been asked and she was not looking at him. "**That is the whole of it and I have had the four days since the Wednesday to arrange to say it and I am going to say it now while it is in the room, and you can all do what you like with it afterwards.**"

Somebody said, "Say it."

"**There are ten lines on a piece of hardboard on that wall, and they are in six hands, and the first one is a cook's and it says the room will not keep anything it cannot take back, and the second one is a first-year's and it says the room will not say that anybody has got better at anything.**"

"And?"

"**And somebody has printed the two of them in a company's typeface on page two of a pack with a photograph of the counter on the top of it, and put the word *our* in front of them, and run about nine hundred of the leaflets off one counter in one morning.**" She had her hand flat on the bag. "**I set that type. I did not choose the words and I did not write the sentence underneath the picture and I cannot tell you who did, and there are about nine hundred people who have read them and none of the six of you has.**"

---

The clerk of forty-four said the other half, and she said it to the room and not to the printer, and she did it in about nine seconds and did not improve it.

"**I have been askable about a piece of paper since the Wednesday before last, and four people have asked me where I got it and one of them wanted a discount because I did not come through a form, and I gave him the discount.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And I want to say the part that is mine. I took that pack off an open shelf in a building where about nine hundred leaflets are held, and I did not leave a note, and I have not told the person whose shelf it was, and if I had left it on the shelf then in about four days somebody else would have been askable instead of me, and I decided in about four seconds that I would rather it was me.**"

"**That is the first sensible thing anybody has said in this room for ten weeks**," said the cook of twenty-seven.

---

And then a man of fifty-one with a tool bag said the only thing anybody said about the nine hundred, and he had been in the room for about half an hour and had not asked for anything.

He is a field service engineer. He puts numbers on about nine hundred doors in four buildings in this city and cannot change one of them, and he has been on the record about that in an earlier volume and has not been thanked for it then and is not thanked for it now.

"**A plate is inside a building**," he said. "**You can go into a building. Nine hundred plates are nine hundred buildings you have to be let into, and I have never once been refused, because a plate is a service and a service has an engineer.**"

"And a leaflet."

"**A leaflet goes in a bag and a bag goes in a pocket, and about nine hundred of them are in about nine hundred pockets, and I cannot go and look in nine hundred pockets, and neither can any of you, and if somebody telephoned the room to ask them to come and take it out, then the answer is that about nine hundred people have to turn up at a service road and about four buses a day go down one.**"

He put the tool bag down.

"**That is the whole of what I know about it and I have not been thanked for it and I am not going to be.**"

---

And then the first-year of nineteen said the thing that is the finding of this chapter, and she said it standing up, and about four people were standing already and nobody asked her to sit down.

"**I am not taking the leaflet down.**"

Somebody said, "It has your name on it."

"**It has my name on it and I put it there on purpose in week seventy after two people told me that a description of a room is a description of the people in it, and nobody can take a name off a piece of paper in this city, and I said that out loud at a market and I was right.**"

"Then take it down."

"**I cannot. About eleven hundred pockets, and there is no mechanism, and the day I stand up in that market and say I have taken it down is the day there is a woman in this city who has taken a thing back off a leaflet, and she will be me, and about four people will come to this room over the next year and ask me how to do it, and I have not got the least idea how I did it.**"

She put her hand on the counter.

"**And I said yes to a photograph. In week seventy-two, in about four seconds, to a woman I have never met, and six people were standing in this room and I asked not one of them, and I have been carrying those four seconds for three weeks and I have not told anybody until last Friday, and I am telling them now because they are the six.**"

"You do not know that they are the six," said a nurse of twenty-four, from the back.

"**No. And that is worse.**"

---

And the nurse of twenty-four said her one sentence, which she gave to the room and not to anybody in particular, and it was about consent and not about a technique and it is the shape of her own work.

"**A person can agree to a photograph of a room and be in the photograph.**"

"That's true."

"**It is true and it is the only sentence I have got and I have said it in about nine rooms this year and I have never once had to say it in a room that had a wall with a line about promises on it.**"

Nobody improved on it and nobody wrote it down.

---

And then somebody asked what the room was going to do about it, and about four people looked at Asha Reed, and she said no before the question was finished.

"**We are not writing to them.**"

"That is not a decision anybody can stop."

"**No, and that is not why. We are not writing to them because a letter from four people in a building with a telephone number on the outside of it is a document about this room, with our four names on it, and it goes into a company and in about four years somebody can say that a room off a service road wrote to a company about a photograph, and that is a different sentence and it is a worse one and it will be true.**"

"Then what are we doing."

"**Nothing. For about a week.**" She shut the bag's strap over. "**And the man of fifty-one has just told us the nine hundred is a thing that cannot be got back, and he is right, and the woman of fifty-eight has told us that a printed page is the only object in this city that cannot be un-printed, and she is right, and there is no action.**"

She looked at the wall.

"**It is the eleventh time somebody in this room has done the right thing and nothing has happened, and the wall does not have a line about it because it is not a thing the room will not do.**"

---

The woman of about thirty was in that room for the whole of the evening with her bag on her lap.

She is not in that photograph, and she is the only one of the seven this room is made out of whose face is not in it, and she is sitting in the room while the four lines are read out, and nobody has ever asked her why she comes. She is not on the wall, is not in the ring binder, is on no document in this case, and nobody in that room asked her a single question about any of it, and about four people wanted to and did not.

The binder is on the shelf behind her. Seven things. Six of them are in six hands and the seventh is in a hand that has never stood in that room, and he is on six of the seven.

He got in at about ten. The lamp was on, the shutter was up, the docket had four job numbers on it, and in the fourth pocket of the tool roll there is a corner of card about two inches by three with a fifth line on it that has been blank for a term and a half and that nobody has ever asked him to fill in.

He wrote one thing.

*228. Thursday of week 78, at a bench in Lattice Ward at about ten, shutter up, the pack in a plastic bag on a counter four miles away, and this entry is four lines in a company's typeface with two words above them that are not in the four lines.*

*The object, as an inventory and not as an account of it.* **One plastic bag with a strap. One pack of about forty pages, stapled at the corner, at least two copies of which exist outside that room and neither of them is held by anybody in this case. Page two carries a photographic reproduction of that room's counter, taken from the doorway, and beneath it four lines in a company typeface at about nine point. The pack is a company's document. It is not a form, a minute, a notice, a report, committee paper, a registration or a constitution, and nothing in this movement was settled by it, refused by it, or answered in writing by anybody.**

*What the four lines claim, in their own words.* **A working method, in a real room. The room asks a person what they do and records it in their own words. Nothing is claimed, nothing is standardised, and the practitioner can withdraw at any time. The possessive is the company's own, stands above the four lines rather than inside them, and is used in its own mouth about a public thing. It occurs nowhere in the four lines themselves: those say the room, a person, the practitioner and stop there, and the harm is that the company supplied the pronoun the room never wrote. Roughly nine hundred impressions of a leaflet carrying that same caption and that same picture were run from one counter in the Quiet Quarter on a single morning and are in about nine hundred pockets. The room did not write the lines, cannot stop them, and has no mechanism by which it could.**

*Who said the thing out loud, and the order of it.* **The printer of fifty-eight read the four lines aloud twice from the counter, unasked, having taken the four days since that Wednesday to arrange to say them; the second reading was requested by a cook of twenty-seven. The clerk of forty-four brought the pack, stepped back from it before speaking, said she had been unable to say it aloud to herself and would make herself say it in front of people, and then said the other half: that she has been askable about a piece of paper since the Wednesday before last, that four people have asked her where she got it, that one wanted a discount because she had not come through a form, and that she gave him the discount; and that she removed the pack from an open shelf without leaving a note and has not told whose shelf it was, and chose in about four seconds to be the askable party rather than to leave a pack on a shelf for a week. Neither of the two women is thanked in this chapter, and neither holds a key, a post, a title or any standing, and the clerk of forty-four appears on no object in that room. A man of twenty-two, at the back with a docket, began a sentence — that it is not theirs and it is not wrong, and then that it is the wall — and did not finish it, and the printer of fifty-eight took it out of his mouth and finished it in about forty words. He is not the person who said the thing.**

*What the wall says against it.* **Ten lines, six hands, seven names, on a piece of hardboard that is not screwed to anything and that carries a tenth line in a hand already on it. The first line is a cook's and concerns withdrawal; the second is a first-year's and concerns claims of improvement. Both of those subjects appear in the four printed lines, in a company's voice and with a company's pronoun, above a photograph of the counter taken on a day no chapter records. The room's rule is the first line and the tenth: the first is that it will not keep anything it cannot take back; the tenth, added on the Monday of week seventy-three and now about five weeks old, is four words and a conditional, that it will take it back if you ask. Neither of them is evidence of anything in any proceeding and neither is enforceable against anybody who did not write it.**

*The nine hundred, and the only two facts anybody in the room has about them.* **From a field service engineer of fifty-one, on the record and not thanked: a plate is a service item inside a building and about nine hundred of them may be reached, while a leaflet travels in a bag and about nine hundred of them may not, and he cannot retrieve them and neither can the room, and the practical consequence is that recovery would require about nine hundred separate visits to a service road that four buses a day serve. From the printer of fifty-eight, who set the type and cannot recall a print run: a printed page is the only class of object in this city that cannot be un-printed, a claim she has made wrongly four times in forty years and made once correctly, and she noted that no number on the pack is hers, so no enquiry will arrive at four feet of shop.**

*The first-year's own part, and what she refused to do.* **She declined to remove a leaflet that carries her name in her own hand from a market table, and gave two reasons: that no mechanism exists by which a name or a sheet can be withdrawn from about eleven hundred pockets, and that a public withdrawal would convert an untakedownable sheet into a thing one identifiable person had succeeded in taking back, which would make her the only person in this city anybody could consult about it. She then stated, standing, in front of about eleven people, that she agreed to a photograph in week seventy-two in about four seconds to a woman she has never met, that six people were in the room, that she asked none of them, that she had carried the four seconds for three weeks, and that she did not know whether the six in the picture are the six in that room. She was told, correctly, that she does not, and that this is worse.**

*The two sentences from the back of the room, and the decision.* **A nurse of twenty-four, once: that a person may consent to a photograph of a room and be inside the photograph; and that she has made the same point in about nine rooms that year and has never had to make it in a room with a line about promises on the wall. The decision against writing to the company was taken in about four minutes, by a person who did not ask anybody, on the ground that a letter from four signatories in a building with a company's number on the outside would enter a company's building as a document about that room and would in about four years be repeatable as the true version. Nothing was sent, nothing was drafted, and nothing was minuted.**

*What is held back from this record.* **The caption on the leaflet beyond the four lines; the company; the course; the city; the woman who telephoned the plate; the day the exposure occurred; and the person who held the camera, who is not in this chapter. The woman of about thirty sat in the third chair for the whole of the evening, is not one of the six figures in the picture and is the only one of the seven whose face is absent from it, was asked nothing by anybody, and is not named on any object in that room. The ring binder holds seven things and this record does not reproduce the contents of any of them. Nothing here is a share, a relay or a practice, and no person on this page taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything, in that room or on that night. The condition is not quoted and is not paraphrased. The standing question is not spoken, no rule about who may be asked has been invented in this batch, and no figure is attached to one. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, no message arrived from anybody, and the box ruled in pencil elsewhere still holds one name and was not touched. Entry 228.**
