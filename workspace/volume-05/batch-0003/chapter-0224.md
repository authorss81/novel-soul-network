# Chapter 224 — The Room Is Open And It Costs Somebody A Friend

A man of thirty-one came into a room off a service road in the Lower Wards on the Monday morning of week seventy-seven in the fourth week of a strike, and nobody asked him which side he was on, because nobody in that room had a side, and the answer was correct, and by about four o'clock in the afternoon it had cost a woman of nineteen the ability to be a friend to him.

Nobody was asked to leave. Nobody was asked anything. That is the whole of the room's part and it is the easiest thing that room has ever done.

---

He came in at about ten past ten on a Monday with a folded paper under his arm and he was not on anybody's list.

Asha Reed said the thing that room says to anybody who comes through the door, and the thing is not a welcome and is not a rule and is not on the wall.

"**Sit down. The kettle goes on at half past eight and it is half past ten and it has been on.**"

"**I am on a strike**," he said. "**I have been on one for four weeks. I am telling you because I have been in three rooms this month and two of them wanted to know which side I was on before I had my coat off.**"

"Then you have put your coat on again and you can go and stand on the service road," said the cook of twenty-seven, without looking up, and did not look up, and the man laughed, which nobody had expected.

---

He is thirty-one. He works at a depot on the far side of the city where about four hundred people load wheeled bins onto about ninety vehicles and go out on rounds. He has been on the same round for about nine years and he is not a supervisor and has never wanted to be.

The dispute is about a check.

There used to be a walk round the back of a vehicle at the start of a load, with a torch, to see what was hanging off the load and what was loose on the tail. It takes about four minutes a vehicle. It has been removed because it takes four minutes, and about nine people stopped going in.

"**And it is not a strike about money**," he said, twice, and both times to nobody. "**I want that on the table because everybody assumes it is about money.**"

Nobody put it on a table.

---

And the room was open, and it stayed open, and that was correct.

Nobody in that room asked him whether he was on strike or not on strike. Nobody asked him whether the check should come back. Nobody read a line off the hardboard at him, and Asha Reed, who has read two lines off that hardboard at men in their thirties before, looked at the wall once and then looked at her feet and did not do it, and about four people in the room noticed her not doing it.

A woman of about thirty sat in the third chair from the door for the whole of the day with her bag on her lap. Nobody asked her for anything, she is on nothing in that room, and nobody in this city has ever put her down as a reason to be anywhere.

---

He stayed until about half past three and he brought the folded paper out at about eleven and read two paragraphs of it to about four people, and it was the terms his own shop had put to the other side, and he read it in a flat voice, and then he put it away again.

Somebody asked him if he was a union man.

"**I am not anything.**"

Then: "**Sorry.**"

"**No. Ask it again in an hour and I will have an answer, and the answer will be worse.**"

---

At about half past eleven a woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary came in and did the door, and the sounder, and the outside of the shutter, which is what she does on a Monday and on a Wednesday, and she did it while there were nine people in the room instead of the four she expects, and nobody asked her to and nobody helped her and the shutter is the same in the morning.

That is another thing done correctly in that room by somebody who is owed nothing that changed nothing at all, and nobody said so out loud.

---

And then the thing that cost happened, and it happened in about nine minutes, and it happened because the room was right.

The man asked her a question. Not about the strike. He asked her how she had ended up here, and she answered it, and the answer was about a leaflet and a market and a man with a clipboard, and then she said the sentence she has said to four people in this city, and it is on the page and it is nine words long and nobody in this case has improved on it: *Nobody sent me. I read it and I came.*

They talked for about two hours.

That is the whole of it. Two hours, in a room with nine chairs, about a leaflet and a bus and a kitchen that opens in the afternoon and a course that costs money. At about two she walked him to the bus stop, which does not come to a service road, and came back and washed her hands.

---

At about four o'clock she was at the sink and she said it, out loud, to the room, and it took her about nine seconds and she did not improve it afterwards.

"**I have been friendly with him for about five hours and I am going to have to stop.**"

The cook of twenty-seven did not stop what she was doing.

"**One of my four employers takes about nine people a week off an agency and the depot where he works is on that agency, and a woman with a clipboard asked me in a corridor about three weeks ago whether I was friendly with anybody on strike, and I said no, and I was not lying, and now I would be.**"

"Will they sack you."

"**They cannot sack me, there is nothing in writing anywhere, and that is the entire problem and it is also the reason it works.**" She dried her hands. "**I am nineteen. I am on nine Fridays a week across four employers and none of the four knows about the other three, and if I am friendly with a man on strike then in about four years somebody can say in a corridor that a first-year of nineteen spends her Fridays in a room with people she should not be friendly with, and I will not be able to prove that the only thing we did for five hours was talk about a leaflet.**"

Somebody said, "That is a lot to give up for a leaflet."

"**It is not a lot to give up. It is the only thing I have got and there is no name on it.**"

---

The man of twenty-two was at the back with a docket for the whole day.

He said one thing, at about half past four, to about nine people, and it was not wrong and it was not enough, and the woman of nineteen told him so before he had finished the second sentence.

"**You have been standing at the back of this room for about six hours and a man has been in it since ten past ten and nobody has said his name or asked him anything, and that is the right answer, and it is not a kindness, and if you go home and think that was a kindness then you have not understood what this room is.**"

"I have not thought that."

"**Then say what it is, because I have to walk to a bus stop in about ten minutes and I would like to have heard it from a person.**"

He took a while.

"**It is that nobody here can be asked whether he is right.**"

"Yes," she said. "**And I am the one who has to be his friend, and being the one who has to be the friend is a job, and nobody in this room is paid for it, and I am the only person here who cannot afford it.**"

She went and got her bag.

---

He got in at about ten. The lamp was on, the shutter was up, the docket had two job numbers on it and there is a woman of sixty-four in another district who telephoned a company's number on a plate three days ago and got through to a ring file, and a binder four miles away with seven things in it, and a wall with ten lines in six hands and seven names, and about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard.

He wrote one thing.

*227. Monday of week 77, ten at night at a bench in Lattice Ward with the lamp on and the door open at the end, and this entry is a man of thirty-one in the fourth week of a stoppage, an answer that was correct, and the loss of a friendship that had lasted about five hours.*

*The man, as a source and not as a case.* **Thirty-one; nine years on the same round; a depot on the far side of the city carrying about ninety vehicles and about four hundred loading crew; not a supervisor, has not wanted to be one, and describes himself as nothing, in answer to a direct question, without embarrassment. The subject of the stoppage is the removal of a pre-departure walk-round of the rear of a loaded vehicle with a torch, an operation of about four minutes per vehicle, discontinued on grounds of time, with about nine people out in the fourth week. He stated twice and unprompted that the dispute is not about money, and no one in the room repeated the claim or recorded it.**

*What the room did and did not do.* **The room was open and stayed open. Nobody asked which side he was on, nobody asked him whether the check should be reinstated, no line of the wall was read at him, and about four people in the room watched a first-year of nineteen decide in front of everybody not to reach for the wall, which she has done twice in a month in the other direction. A folded paper was brought out at about eleven and two paragraphs of the stoppage's own shop terms were read in a flat voice to about four people and put away again; no copy exists in this case and no one wrote anything down. A direct question about affiliation was put to him, answered with a flat no, followed by an apology from the asker and a refusal to be asked the same question again for an hour. The wall is ten lines in six hands and seven names, the tenth a cook's, and it is a different object from the ring binder and from the folded paper and from the man.**

*What was already in the room and went on being there.* **A woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary opened the door, tested the sounder and did the outside of the shutter at about half past eleven with about nine people present where she expects four, unaided and unasked, and the shutter was identical the following morning. That is a correct action performed by a person who is owed nothing and it altered nothing, and it is one of a run of them in that room that the room has never once counted out loud. A woman of about thirty sat in the third chair from the door for the whole of the day, was asked nothing by anybody, appears on no object in that room, and has still never been asked why she comes.**

*The five hours, and the price, in her own words.* **A conversation of about two hours about a leaflet, a market, a bus, a kitchen that opens in the afternoon, and a course that costs money; followed by a walk to a bus stop that does not come to a service road. She then announced at a sink, unasked, that she was going to have to stop. Her grounds, given in about nine seconds: one of her four employers draws about nine people a week from an agency, that depot is on the same agency, and about three weeks ago a woman with a clipboard asked her in a corridor whether she was friendly with anybody on strike; she answered no and the answer was true at the time. She then set out the reason there is nothing to be done about it: no employer holds anything in writing, which is the whole difficulty and the whole of why it works. On her own side she gave a count and not a claim — nineteen years old, nine Fridays a week across four employers, none of whom knows of the other three — and said that in about four years somebody could say in a corridor that a first-year of nineteen spends her Fridays among people she should not be friendly with, and that she could not then prove that five hours consisted of talk about a leaflet. She was asked whether that was a lot to give up for a leaflet and answered that it is not a lot to give up, it is the only thing she has, and there is no name on it.**

*The two sentences spoken by a man of twenty-two, and the answer.* **He stood at the back with a docket for about six hours and said one thing at about half past four, which was not wrong and was not enough, and was interrupted before he had finished the second sentence by the woman of nineteen. Her account of what the room had done: a man had been in it since a quarter past ten and nobody had said his name or asked him a question, that this is the right answer, that it is not a kindness, and that a person who goes home thinking it was a kindness has not understood the room. Asked to put it in his own words, he said that nobody in that room can be asked whether he is right, and she accepted that and added that being the one who has to be the friend is a job, that nobody in the room is paid for it, and that she is the only person there who cannot afford it. There is no scene in this chapter and no declaration and nothing was said by either of them that was about the two of them.**

*Absent, withheld, or deliberately not done.* **No copy of the folded paper, no record of the visit, no category for a person on strike, no question asked of him about the thing he is on strike about, and no demonstration, teaching, assessment or assistance by anybody in this chapter, in that room or on that day. The stoppage itself is not resolved, and no employer, official or company appears in it. The condition is not reproduced and is not paraphrased. The standing question is touched and left alone, and there is no rule in this chapter about who may be asked anything, and no figure is set against one. The licensor stays unnamed, the field stays blank, and the box in pencil in another building was not touched. Entry 227.**
