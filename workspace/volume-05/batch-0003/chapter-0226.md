# Chapter 226 — Six People And Five Of Them Cannot Be Found

On the Friday morning of week seventy-eight a company telephoned the number on the plate outside a converted shop unit in the Lower Wards and asked a correct question, and by lunchtime the room had answered it as well as it is able to answer anything, and the answer was that five of the six people in a photograph could not be found and the sixth was a man of twenty-two.

The Friday ran as the Fridays run.

---

He got to the depot at about twenty to eight. Eleven on the docket and nine in date: a communal light in a bin store was a repeat from last week and was a timer, a callout to a car park where the barrier had been left low, two lift calls, three landing lights, an emergency lighting pack in a stair, a tap, and a cupboard light off a service road.

The emergency lighting pack took forty minutes and it is another thing in this case that has been done correctly and changed nothing. The unit was passing its test and the cells in it had been out of date for about two years, and the job sheet said to check the function and he checked the function and the function was fine, and he replaced the pack anyway because a cell that is out of date is the thing that will be a problem in four years, and now that stair will never be examined again, and he will not be the person anybody asks.

Nobody in that yard asked where a release would be coming from, and there has not been anybody in that yard he could telephone about it for a term and a half.

At about one the word went from nine to ten and it stayed at ten.

---

The call came to the office at about half past nine in the morning and it took about nine minutes to reach the room, and none of the nine minutes was anybody's fault.

A letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file answered it first, because the number on the plate is a company's number and there is no name on the plate. She gave the sentence about a vacant frontage, and the caller said she was not asking about the unit, and the clerk said hold on.

Somebody in that office asked whether the room had a telephone.

"No," said Asha Reed. "**Tell them the number on the plate and say it twice.**"

---

The woman on the telephone gave her name and her company and the reason for the call and all three of them were ordinary, and then she asked the question, and it was a good question, and it was the right one, and it was the question that should have been asked on the day.

"**Who is in the photograph, and did they agree to it.**"

The room got quiet in a way that is not describable and is not a rule.

"I am not going to answer the first one from memory," said Asha Reed. "**Give me four minutes.**"

She counted them out loud at the counter with a docket of somebody else's on top of the bag, and about nine people watched, and nobody helped her, and at about two and a half minutes somebody said *it's six* and she said *I know it's six, I am counting the ones I can name.*

---

Here is what the room could establish in four minutes, and it is the whole of what that room is.

One. The cook of twenty-seven. Four people in the room know that she is there on a Monday, a Wednesday and a Friday, and two of the four did not know that until that morning, and the other two have never been able to say which days.

Two. A first-year of nineteen, who is nineteen and is the only nineteen-year-old in that room, and who put her name at the bottom of about eleven hundred leaflets in a market in week seventy and cannot take it off.

Three. A printer of fifty-eight with four feet of shop in the Quiet Quarter, who has a page of her own in the ring binder and a line of her own on the wall, and whose own nine words are legible on the top sheet in the photograph.

Four. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, on his employer's Friday list, who has a page in that binder that is the shortest thing in it, about nine words, and who is on a rota in a building about eleven miles away and is not in the same place on two Fridays running.

Five. A woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary, who runs an exchange room on a first floor in another building, who has been told twice this term that she has no standing, and who handed over the second key in this case on a sheet of typing paper in her own hand.

Six. A man of twenty-two with a card in a rail in a building in Lattice Ward with a plate on the outside of it, on a lease with three other names on it, and the seventh name on a piece of hardboard inside by the door of this room.

"And the seventh," said Asha Reed, "**is a person with a bag on her lap who came in one night because it was raining and has come on every working day since, and she is not one of the six in that photograph, and there is no name for her, and there is no telephone number for her, and she is not in any of it, and I have not asked her and I am not going to, and I am telling you about her on a telephone because somebody has to and it is never going to be her.**"

Silence on the line, and then the woman said:

"**I am sorry. I am not going to push you on the sixth. I want to tell you what we are going to do.**"

---

"**We are withdrawing the pack**," she said. "**From sale, from the next printing, and from the course. That is ours to do and we can do it today.**"

"That is correct," said Asha Reed, "**and it will change nothing, and I am going to say why before anybody says it for me.**"

"Go on."

"**You have about nine hundred of them.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**In about nine hundred pockets.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**And a woman of fifty-eight in this city is the only person who can tell you that a printed page cannot be un-printed, and she is not in this room and she has told us the same thing twice, and you have just done the right thing and the right thing is worth about nothing and you did not know that until this morning and you are not a villain and you did not plan any of it.**"

There was a pause of about four seconds.

"**I did not plan any of it**," the woman said. "**I am going to say something and I want it written down as coming from me and not from a room.**"

Nobody wrote it down.

"**I read a leaflet in a market in the spring and I put it in my bag and it sat in my bag for about a fortnight and then I had a course to write and I needed a room with a method in it and I telephoned the number on it and a young woman answered and said yes in about four seconds, and I have not thought about it since until about nine minutes ago, and I have cost somebody a thing that cannot be got back, and I am the fourth person in my own office to say that sentence this week and the other three said it about a supplier.**"

---

And then somebody handed a telephone to a woman of twenty-six who had taken a piece of paper off a table in a market in week seventy and had gone on her way, and had telephoned the number on the plate on the Tuesday because she wanted to know what was in the room in the picture.

She had a bag. She had not been in that room and she was not going to be.

"**Is it true that what people say in that room stays in that room.**"

He said nine words, and they were counted on the page, and they are the third set in this case and they are not an improvement on either of the first two, and he said so himself before anybody else could.

"That photograph is not of a room any more."

She waited about nine seconds.

"**That is not a no.**"

"No," he said. "**It is nine words about a picture. The first time I said nine words to somebody in a market it was about the room and the second time it was about myself, and this is the third and it is about a thing that has already happened, and I am not going to improve on two worse answers and I would like it noted that I said that before anybody said it.**"

"**Noted**," said the woman of twenty-six, and she did not say anything else, and she was not asked who she was, and she rang off.

---

The clerk of thirty-nine came back in at about half past eleven and put the receiver down and said her sentence about a vacant frontage, out loud, to a room with about nine people in it, because that is what she does.

Then she said the thing she wanted to say and nobody had asked her for it.

"**That is the third time in about two months that the number on that plate has been the way somebody found that room, and I have a ring file and I have said the sentence about the frontage to four sets of people and I am not going to say it to a fifth, and I would like somebody in this room to know that I have a piece of paper with all three of those telephone calls on it and that I have not thrown it away and that it is mine and I am not going to bring it here.**"

"Give it to us," said somebody.

"**No**," said the clerk of thirty-nine, and went back to her office about eleven miles away.

---

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, four of them off any list, the count being on the back of a woman's own card and nowhere else. A woman of about thirty was in the third chair from the door on that Friday afternoon and nobody asked her anything.

He got in at about ten. The lamp was on, the shutter was up, the docket had eleven job numbers on it of which about four were nothing, and there is a plastic bag on a counter four miles away with a company's document in it, and a pack that a company has withdrawn and about nine hundred people are carrying, and a ring binder with seven things in it of which he is on six.

He wrote one thing.

*229. Friday of week 78, at a workbench in Lattice Ward at about ten with the roller shutter up and the docket face down beside the lamp, and this entry is a telephone call, a correct decision by a company, and a count of six.*

*How the call arrived, and how long it took.* **A call to a company's number on a plate with no name on it, at about half past nine, answered by a letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file, who gave the sentence about a vacant frontage and was told it was not about the unit. The room has no telephone and never has, and the number was given twice down a line, and the whole of the delay was about nine minutes and none of it was anybody's fault.**

*The question, and that it was the right one.* **Who is in the photograph, and did they agree to it. Both halves asked by a person who gave her name and her company and the reason for the call first, and both halves ordinary. The first half was not answered from memory. Four minutes were counted aloud at the counter by a first-year of nineteen with somebody else's docket on top of the bag, and about nine people watched and nobody helped, and at about two and a half minutes somebody in the room said the number of figures and she replied that she knew the number and was counting the ones she could name.**

*The six, as a count of findability and not as a cast.* **A cook of twenty-seven, present on three days a week of which two of four people in the room were unaware until that morning. A first-year of nineteen, who is the only nineteen-year-old in that room, whose name is on about eleven hundred leaflets in a market and cannot be taken off any of them. A printer of fifty-eight with four feet of shop, a page of her own in the binder, a line of her own on the wall, and nine legible words of her own on the top sheet in the picture. A man of twenty-nine on an employer's Friday list, whose sheet in that binder is the shortest in it and who is not in the same building on two Fridays running. A woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary who has been told twice this term that she has no standing. And a man of twenty-two, findable by a card in a rail in a building with a plate on the outside of it, by a lease with three other names on it, and as the seventh name on an unscrewed piece of hardboard inside by the door. The six figures are therefore those six people and the count closes, and of the six, five cannot be located by anybody outside that room and the one who can be located is the man who held a docket at the back. The seventh person in that room, a woman of about thirty who has been on about nine visits since the Friday of week sixty-nine with a bag on her lap, is not in the picture, was not in the room on the day, and is the only one of the seven whose face is not in it; the first-year said on an open line that she was not going to ask her about anything, the caller did not ask either, and nobody in that room has ever asked her why she comes.**

*What the company decided, and why it is worth nothing.* **Withdrawal of the pack from sale, from the next printing and from the course, offered by the caller as hers to do and capable of being done the same day. The room accepted the decision, described it as correct, and stated the reason before anybody else could: about nine hundred copies are already in about nine hundred pockets; a printer of fifty-eight has twice told that room that a printed page is the only class of object in this city that cannot be un-printed; and the caller had not known that until that morning. The caller stated her own part, asked for it to be attributed to her and not to a room, and nobody wrote it down: a leaflet read in a market in the spring, carried in a bag for about a fortnight, a course to be written, a need for a room with a method in it, a number telephoned, a young woman who said yes in about four seconds, and four colleagues that week using the same sentence about a supplier.**

*Nine words, counted, and the third set.* **A woman of twenty-six, unlisted, holding a bag, who had taken a piece of paper from a market table in week seventy and telephoned the plate on the Tuesday; she had never been in the room and was not going to be, and she was not asked who she was. She asked whether what people say in that room stays in that room. He answered in nine words, counted on the page: *That photograph is not of a room any more.*He stated, before anybody else spoke, that this is the third such answer in this case, that the first concerned the room and the second concerned himself, that this one concerns a thing which has already happened, and that he is not going to improve on two worse answers. She waited, said that this was not a no, was told that it was not, and rang off without another word.**

*The clerk, and her three.* **She returned to the room at about half past eleven, repeated the sentence about a vacant frontage aloud to about nine people, and then stated unasked that this is the third occasion within about two months on which that plate was the route by which somebody found that room; that she holds a piece of paper with all three calls on it; that the paper is hers; and that she is not bringing it there. Asked for it, she refused in one word and returned to her office about eleven miles away. She is thanked by nobody in this chapter.**

*The Friday, as a count.* **Eleven on the docket, nine in date, grade three from twenty to eight, one repeat of a bin-store timer, one car-park callout, two lift calls, three landing lights, one emergency lighting pack, one tap, one cupboard light. The pack passed its test and its cells were about two years out of date; the work was unnecessary, was done anyway, and is now the reason that stair will not be examined again. Nobody in the yard asked where a release would come from, and there has never been anybody there to ask. The word ran at nine until about one and at ten afterwards and was told to nobody. About eleven people a week are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, about four of them off any rota, the figure being on the back of a woman's own card and on nothing else.**
