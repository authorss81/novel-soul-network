# Chapter 223 — Nine Steps That Belong To Somebody Else

A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip brought nine steps into a room off a service road in the Lower Wards on the Thursday afternoon of week seventy-six, and the steps were his sister's and she had never been asked, and the room said no and then spent the rest of the evening being slow on purpose, which is the only instrument it has.

Somebody was right and somebody was wrong and neither of them said so out loud, and the two who were most right were a man of fifty-eight and a woman of nineteen and they were right about different sentences. That is the whole of it and it took eight weeks and three days to arrive at.

---

He came in at about half past two and he came in slowly, and he put his hand on the door frame on the way, which he did not need to do and which he went on doing for about four seconds after he was inside it.

He is fifty-eight. His hip was done about four years ago and it did not go right, and he is not on a rota and not in a class and came on a bus and got off one stop too far and walked the rest of it.

Asha Reed asked him what it was for, which is the room's whole method, and he said he had not come to be taught anything and had not thought about being asked.

"**I have brought the words.**"

---

Nine steps, and they are about eleven seconds, and they are somebody else's.

You do not put your weight on the leg. You put your hand on the roof. You turn to the hip that is bad. You count to two, which is a habit and not a rule, because his sister counted to two and her hip is the right one. Then you go back and forward, not up, and you let the chair take you at the end of it and you do not sit down on the way.

He said it in about nine sentences without stopping, and twice he said a word and then replaced it with a different one that was more accurate, and the two words he replaced are the two that matter.

"There is a chair," he said. "**At the end of it. If there is no chair at the end of it then there is no such thing.**"

---

And then he said the part that is the chapter, and he said it before anybody asked for it, and he said it in about four sentences.

"**That is not mine.**"

She put the pen down.

"**I did not learn it. She did not teach me. She is sixty-four and she had hers done two years before I had mine and she wrote it out for me on the back of an envelope in a car park in about nine minutes because I asked her to, and I have had that envelope in a drawer for about four years.**"

"You want it in the binder."

"**I wanted somebody in this city to have it in writing. I do not want it in writing. I want it to be hers and I want it to be somewhere, and I have brought it to a room because you are the only room in this city I have heard of that writes down what a person says.**"

---

The cook of twenty-seven had her hands in a sink.

"It will not keep anything it cannot take back," said Asha Reed, and she had not gone to the wall, and nobody interrupted her.

"Go on," said the man of fifty-eight.

"**That is the first line on that wall and I did not go and read it, and it is about your sister and not about your words.**" She had the binder out of the shelf and she did not open it. "**If we take that page then the only person in this city who can take it back is a woman of sixty-four in another district, and nobody has asked her, and I am not going to put a thing on a shelf in a room with a door in it that belongs to somebody who does not know it is here.**"

The room was quiet for about four seconds.

"**And you are right**," she said, "**and I am sorry, and both of those are the same sentence tonight.**"

---

He took about a minute with it.

Then he said: "**I got it wrong twice.**"

Somebody said, "You said you were not going to be the one holding it."

"**I am not holding it. I am doing it. There is a difference and it is the whole of this room.**" He put his hand on the counter and took the weight off the other side. "**The second time was outside a shop on a Friday and I went down on the pavement in front of about nine people and a woman picked up the shopping and a man I did not know asked me if I was all right and I said yes.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**So it is a technique in your terms. It has an order and a way of being wrong and it hurts somebody. That is what your cook wrote on that wall in the first week and I have read it and I can still remember it.**"

---

And then he named the price, and nobody had asked him, and it took him about nine seconds.

"**Four years in a drawer and I have shown it to nobody, and I have not spoken to my sister since our mother's funeral, which was about nine years ago, because we do not speak, and I came here on a Thursday afternoon because a man in a lanyard told a lift about it and the lift mentioned a room and I thought a room would be easier than a telephone call.**"

"You are going to have to ring her."

"**I am going to have to ring her, and I have not got the number in my phone because I took it out in the spring.**"

---

And that is what the room did, or rather that is what it did not do, and it is the only instrument it has.

It did not take the page. It did not tell him to come back. It did not put the page in a folder, because a folder is a place a page goes when nobody has decided about it.

It said the offer would be held open for a week by being not held at all.

"**Nothing happens in this room on Thursday and nothing has happened since you came in.**" Asha Reed had shut the binder. "**We are not going to write it down and we are not going to keep it. If you want it in that binder you will have to come back with her, and if you cannot do that then it is hers and it stays in a drawer in your house and I am not going to be the reason it is in a room she has never seen. It has been in that envelope for four years and it can stay there for another four.**"

"And if I do nothing."

"**Then nothing happens, which is what this room has been doing for eight weeks and nobody has yet died of it.**"

---

At about five he went out into a wet afternoon and up the service road to a bus stop one stop too far, and about four people watched him do it slowly and none of them offered an arm.

And a man of about sixty-four came in at about ten past six with a trolley and a folded mat and moved the table out of the way of the door and put it back, because somebody at the back had been catching a heel on the lip of the floor all week, and nobody asked him to and nobody paid him and the table is in the same place it was in at six.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and about four of the eleven are not on a rota, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card and is on no form.

---

The woman of about thirty was in the third chair from the door for the whole of the afternoon and was asked for nothing and is not on the wall and is not in the binder and nobody has ever asked her why she comes.

The man of twenty-two was at the back with a docket for the whole of it and said about four sentences and none of them was about the nine steps.

---

At about seven that evening a woman of sixty-four telephoned the number on the plate outside, which is a company's number with no name on it, and it goes to an office in another building, and a letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file answered it and said her sentence.

"**The unit has been vacant on this side of the row for a long time and I have given that answer to four sets of people.**"

"I am not asking about the unit," the woman said. "**I am asking about a room off a service road that has a piece of hardboard on the wall.**"

There was a pause of about four seconds.

"Hold on," said the clerk.

---

Asha Reed took the call standing up, beside the counter, with the binder shut on the shelf behind her and about nine people in the room and the kettle going at the point in the day it goes.

"**My brother telephoned me at about six**," the woman said. "**He has not telephoned me in about nine years and he telephoned me at about six and he said he had offered me something to a room and that I should be angry, and I am not going to tell you what I said to him because it was not for you.**"

"Go on."

"**Is it in there.**"

"**No. It was offered and we did not take it, and the reason is you.**"

"**Say it again.**"

"**We would not be able to take it back off you, because you are in another district, and nobody had asked you, and there is a line on that wall that is about exactly that and it is the first one and it is a cook's.**"

There was a pause of about nine seconds.

"**Then I am going to tell you what it is, and then you can decide whether I have given it away, and if you have I want to hear you say so.**"

---

It took about four minutes. She said the nine steps and she said them faster than her brother and she used the other word for the count, and she added two things he had not said, and one of them is that the chair is not optional and the other one is that if the person is being helped then they are not the one counting.

Then Asha Reed took a clean sheet out of the ring binder, and wrote it in the woman's own words in about four minutes, and put the pen down, and said this out loud in a room with about nine people in it before she wrote another word.

"**I am about to do the thing that woman of sixty-one's rule is against, and the rule is a book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call, and I have heard that rule in a back room with the shutters up and I agree with it, and I am doing this anyway.**"

"Do it," said the cook of twenty-seven, from the sink, and she did not turn round.

Asha Reed wrote it, and her hand got smaller towards the end of it, and she did not tidy it, and it went into the binder as the seventh thing, and nobody in that room read it out.

The only difference she gave, when somebody asked her for one, was about nine words long and she said it once.

"**She said it to a person, and not to a tin.**"

"And that is a difference."

"**That is the only difference I have got, and I cannot prove it, and I have decided it.**"

---

He got to Lattice Ward at about ten and the lamp was on and the shutter was up and the docket had four job numbers on it and there is a binder four miles away with seven things in it, six of them in six hands and one of them in a hand that has never been in that room, and the wall has ten lines in six hands and seven names, and the tenth is a cook's and it reads that it will take it back if you ask.

He wrote one thing.

*226. Thursday of week 76, on the wet service road outside a unit whose shutter is up, at about ten, docket under an arm, and this entry is nine steps that are not his, a refusal, and a telephone call to a plate with a company's number on it.*

*What was offered, as a shape and not as a transcription.* **Nine steps and roughly eleven seconds for getting out of the front seat of a car when one hip will not take weight and a person is alone. An order, a habitual count that is not a rule, a substitution of a chair as a necessary terminal object, and a fault condition that has been realised twice: once in a car park and once on a pavement outside a shop in front of about nine bystanders, the second requiring help from a stranger and a denial. It is a technique by that room's own working definition, in that being wrong with it hurts a person. The offerer is fifty-eight, four years after a hip that did not go right, not on a rota, arrived by bus and walked one stop too far, and said before anybody asked that the words are not his and that he is not the person to hold them.**

*Whose words they are, and on what paper.* **A woman of sixty-four in another district had hers replaced two years before he had his, wrote the sequence out for him on the back of an envelope in a car park in about nine minutes at his request, and has not been in that room. The envelope has been in a drawer in his house for about four years and has been shown to no one until this afternoon. He stated that he did not learn the sequence, that he was not taught it, and that the difference between performing it and holding it is the whole of what that room is; and that the reason he came was that a man in a lanyard had described it to a lift engineer, who mentioned the room, and that he had supposed a room would be easier than a telephone call. He also stated that he has not spoken to his sister since a funeral about nine years ago, and that her number is no longer in his telephone.**

*The instrument, and it is the first line of that wall and nobody walked to it.* **A first-year of nineteen put the binder on the shelf without opening it and gave the reason without going to the hardboard: the sole party capable of withdrawing a page is a person in another district who has not been asked, and no page will be placed on that shelf on the strength of an unasked party. She said the offer was right and the refusal was right in the same breath, and did not improve either. The man agreed in about four seconds and did not argue. Nothing was written, nothing was filed, and no folder was used: the offer was kept open by the room doing nothing at all, which is the only instrument it holds, and the first-year said so in nine words in front of about nine people.**

*The cost, in the offerer's own words, unasked.* **Four years of a drawer, no showing, and a sister in another district he has not spoken to in about nine years. He put the price of the sequence himself: two performances, the second on a pavement in front of about nine people, attended to by a stranger and denied by him. He then said out loud that the sequence qualifies as a technique by that room's own definition and that he can still remember the wording of the definition from the wall in its first week, and nobody had asked him to check whether he had understood it.**

*The call, and what crossed it.* **At about seven in the evening a woman of sixty-four telephoned the number on the plate outside, which is a company's number with no name on it, and an office answered. A letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file gave her the sentence about a vacant frontage that she has given to four sets of people, and was told it was not about the unit. The clerk held the line for about four seconds. This is the third occasion on which that plate has been the route by which something in this case was found by somebody who works for a company, and the clerk is not thanked, has said nothing beyond her one sentence, and remains a person with a ring file and not an instrument. The woman then gave the sequence in about four minutes, faster than her brother, using a different word for the count, and added two things he had not said: that the chair is not optional, and that where another person is helping, the count is not taken by the person being helped.**

*The seventh thing, and the rule it breaks.* **A clean sheet, written in the woman's own words in a first-year's hand in about four minutes, untidied, with the hand contracting at the end, and placed in the ring binder without being read aloud to anybody. It is the first page in that binder written down by a person who was not in the room, and the first page in it about something its writer did not do, and the envelope is in a drawer four miles from a counter and has been for about four years. It is also the first breach of the rule in force in the other room in this city, that a book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call, and the first-year announced the breach aloud in front of about nine people before making it, was told to go ahead by a cook of twenty-seven, and afterwards named the whole of her justification in about nine words: the words were given to a person and not to a tin. She added, unasked, that she cannot prove the distinction and has decided it, and no person in that room improved on it.**

*What the room did not do.* **It did not take the page, did not tell the offerer to return, did not open a file, did not make a copy, and did not settle anything by writing one word down about a person. Nobody in this chapter taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything, and the offerer was not taught his own sister's sequence, and a share, a relay and a practice are absent from all four of the pages above. The man of twenty-two was at the back for the whole of it and said about four sentences, none of them about the sequence. A woman of about thirty sat in the third chair for the whole of the afternoon, was asked for nothing, and is not on the wall, is not in the binder, is in no file in this case, and has not been asked why she comes. The wall is ten lines in six hands and seven names; the tenth is a cook's and is four words and a conditional; the ring binder now holds seven things; the board above the door is a different object from both and from the envelope, and no two of the four are together in a sentence in this record. The condition appears nowhere in this entry and nobody's mouth paraphrases it. The standing question is referred to and not stated; no rule about who may be asked exists; no number is attached to one. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, the master did not move, no message came from anybody, and the exchange book was not opened on this day. Entry 226.**
