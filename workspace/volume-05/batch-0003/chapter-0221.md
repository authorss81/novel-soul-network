# Chapter 221 — About Four Hundred People Have Read It

On the Friday evening of week seventy-five a woman of thirty-four with a district jacket and a clipboard laid eight printed pages on a counter in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards, and on the inside cover of the second one there is a photograph of that counter, and about four hundred people in another city have read it.

Nobody in the room had been asked to be in it. That is the whole of this chapter and the rest of it is a Friday.

---

He got to the depot at about twenty to eight. Eleven on the docket and eight in date: a communal light in a bin store, a lift call, a handrail bracket on a second landing, a fan in a stair that runs on a timer, an emergency light, a tap needing nine turns more than it got, a fire door closer, and a light in a cupboard off a service road that the job sheet calls a light in a cupboard.

The bin store one was the first thing that took any time. A timer, a lamp that is on, a fuse that is not, and a man of about thirty-eight in a district jacket who had written the fault on his own sheet in a box that gets a description in it.

He tested it with his own meter and put it back and wrote down what the meter said.

"That is not the fault."

"That is not the fault," the man of thirty-eight said, "**and I did not say it was, and you are the third person this month and the other two were also right.**"

He wrote that down as well, because it is what the sheet says to write down, and the bin store is lit at six the next morning.

Nobody in that yard asked him where a release was going to come from, and there has not been anybody in that yard he could telephone about it for a term and a half, and he has never once wanted to.

At about one the word went from nine to ten and it stayed at ten.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, about four of the eleven are not on a rota, the number is on the back of a woman's own card and is on no form, and a Friday is a Friday.

---

The room was ready. Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, the counter cleared to a strip, the hardboard turned on its two nails so the writing faced into the room, and the kettle on at about half past eight at a point in that room's day which has not moved since the first week.

The woman of thirty-four came in at about seven with her coat already off and put the eight pages down square with the edge of the counter and squared them again.

She is a district nurse. She has a card in a plastic wallet and eleven names on it and she has never given it to anybody. She put a trestle in a market about five weeks ago for reasons she gave in one sentence and has not repeated, and she has not been thanked for it and was not thanked for this either.

"I have been asked," she said, "**whether that is the room.**"

She turned the second page round so it was the right way up for the people on that side of the counter.

---

It is a picture of that room taken from about where the door is.

A shutter up. A floor that is not level and shows it. Nine chairs in a curve. A window with a wall about four feet away behind it. A piece of hardboard about the size of a tea tray on the wall by the door with four words on it in a sign-writer's hand, and there is sandpaper on the counter in the bottom corner of the picture.

And on the counter, in the middle of the picture, standing up out of its rings with the pages in it standing above the sleeve, is a ring binder.

And behind the counter, out of focus because the light was behind them, are people. You cannot tell one face from another. You can tell that there are six of them, because six of them are separate shapes and you can count.

Asha Reed looked at it for about eleven seconds.

"That is our counter," she said. "**That is our binder.**"

---

Nobody said anything for about four seconds, and then the woman of thirty-four said the thing she had come to say, which was that she had read it twice and that the third time she stopped reading the words.

"There is nothing on that page about a room," she said. "**There is a photograph of one, and a photograph of a room is a different thing, and about four hundred people have got it, and I am the ninth person this week who has asked me.**"

The cook of twenty-seven had her hands in a sink and had not turned round.

"Who took it," she said.

"That is the other question and I do not have it either."

---

And then Asha Reed said it, and it took her a while, and she did not improve it, and nobody in that room helped her.

"I said yes," she said. "**To the photograph. I said yes in about four seconds and I did not ask anybody.**"

"Go on," said the man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, from the fourth chair, where he had been since about seven with a bag of spanners he has never opened in that building.

"There was a woman," she said. "**She telephoned the number on the plate. I answered it, on a Tuesday, about three weeks ago, and she said she had a leaflet, and the leaflet was mine, and she had taken it out of her bag in a market and it had been in her bag for about a fortnight before she rang anybody.**"

"And she wanted a photograph."

"**She wanted a picture for a prospectus. There is a certificate course in another city and about four hundred people are going to read it and it has to have a room in it, and she said a picture of a table is not what anybody pays for, and she was right, and I said yes in about four seconds, and I have been carrying those four seconds for about three weeks.**"

Nobody asked her why she had not asked anybody. She said it before anybody could.

"**Because it was a photograph of a room and I was the person who had the room and I did not think of six people standing in it. I have said for two years that nobody in a room has to say anything to be described by the person who has the room, and I said it on a floor in week sixty-eight and on a market table in week seventy, and it is my sentence and I have not been careful with it.**"

Dessa Kwan said one word from the sink.

"Right," she said. "**Well.**"

---

And then somebody asked the question that was the reason the woman of thirty-four had come, and she asked it herself, at about ten past seven, to nobody in particular.

"Is it on the leaflet?"

"No."

"Then why has she got your number off it."

"**Because the leaflet has the number on it and I put the number on it**, and about eleven hundred people have one, and that is not a thing I decided this week either."

---

The man of twenty-two was at the back with a docket. He had been in the room for about half an hour and had not said anything at all, and about four people had looked at him twice.

He said one sentence, and it was the wrong one, and he knew it before he had finished it.

"That is not a picture of a room any more, it is a picture of a room that has been described by somebody who was not in it."

"That is what it is," said Asha Reed. "**And you have just said the thing you have been saying since week sixty-eight in a room with about nine people in it and a wall on the wall, and I want to say one thing back and then I am going to go and wash up.**"

"Go on."

"**It is not a picture of a room that has been described by somebody who was not in it. It is a picture of a room that has been described by a person who was in it and did not ask six other people. That is worse and it is different and I do not know what to do with it.**"

She went and washed up.

The photograph lay on the counter with about nine people looking at it and nobody touched it and nobody turned it over, and the page it is printed on has about four hundred words on it, and about four hundred people have read the words.

---

He got back to Lattice Ward at about ten. The lamp was on, the shutter was up, the docket had eleven job numbers on it in a shorthand nobody else in this city can read, and there was a piece of card about the size of a playing card in an inside pocket of a coat on the back of a chair and the original was in the fourth pocket of the tool roll with nothing written on the fifth line of it, and nobody has ever asked him about that line.

He wrote one thing.

*224. Friday of week 75, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is eleven jobs, eight in date, a bin store, and eight printed pages.*

*The docket as a count of items and not as a rate.* **Eleven on the docket, eight in date, grade three from twenty to eight. In date: one communal light in a bin store, one lift call, one handrail bracket, one fan in a stair on a timer, one emergency light, one tap, one fire door closer, one light in a cupboard off a service road. Not in date: three, being a callout a man in a district jacket had already closed on his own sheet, and two items where the equipment was not on the site. About four hundred closing notes of that last kind go into that company's system in this city every week and no part of this entry expects one of them to be read. About eleven people a week are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, about four of them not on a rota, the figure being on the back of a woman's own card and on nothing else, and no chapter in this movement puts it anywhere else.**

*The bin store, and the fourth visitor.* **A timer, a lamp that is on, a fuse that is not, and a fault reported in a box that receives a description. Tested with his own meter and put back, the reading written down, and a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket saying out loud that he had not said it was the fault and that this is the third such caller in a month and the other two were also right. The man of thirty-eight has said that sentence once and will say it again, because a sheet asks for a finding and the finding is that the thing is not the fault. The store is lit at six the following morning and nothing about it is different. It is the first correct action in this movement that changed nothing and it is on the page because the bin store is the reason the man of thirty-eight came.**

*The word, as it stands.* **Nine a minute until about one, ten from about one, ten at about ten at night. Nothing was done about it then or at any other time and it has not been told to anybody.**

*The prospectus, as an object with a day on it and not as an author.* **Eight pages, printed, on the second one an inside cover carrying a photograph taken from about where the door of that room is. In the picture: a shutter up, a floor that is not level, nine chairs in a curve, a window and a wall about four feet behind it, a piece of hardboard about the size of a tea tray by the door with four words in a sign-writer's hand, a piece of sandpaper on the counter, and a ring binder standing open on the counter with its pages above the sleeve. Behind the counter, out of focus and lit from behind, six separate human shapes. About four hundred people in another city have read those eight pages, and a certificate course is described on them, and the company that issued them is not the author of anything in this case and has never been. The photograph was made on a day inside a stretch of about a fortnight that carries no chapter of its own anywhere in this case, and no day has been set for it in any file. The photographer is not named in this entry, has not been in that room, and is not in this chapter.**

*The woman who brought it, and what she asked.* **Thirty-four, a district nurse, a district jacket and a clipboard, a card in a plastic wallet with eleven names on it that she has never given to anybody. She has put a trestle in a market about five weeks ago and has not been thanked for that either. She was asked, at work, whether the room in the picture was the room, and she brought eight pages to find out, and she said that she had stopped reading the words on the third reading and that she is the ninth person that week who has asked her. She did not have the second question, which is who took it, and the cook of twenty-seven asked that one from a sink and did not get it either.**

*What a first-year of nineteen said, twice, in her own words, and was not helped with.* **That she said yes to the photograph in about four seconds on a Tuesday about three weeks ago to a woman who telephoned the number on the plate outside; that the woman had a leaflet of hers out of her bag, taken from a market about a fortnight before she rang anybody; that the woman wanted a picture for a prospectus and said that a picture of a table is not what anybody pays for; and that six people were standing in the room when it was taken and she asked not one of them. She said out loud that nobody in a room has to say anything to be described by the person who has the room, that she has said that sentence for two years, that she said it on a floor in week sixty-eight and at a market table in week seventy, and that she has not been careful with it. The cook of twenty-seven said one word and went on with the sink, and the word is not a sentence and has not been improved on.**

*The one sentence the man of twenty-two said, and the answer it got.* **He said that the photograph is not a picture of a room any more but of a room that has been described by somebody who was not in it. He was told, in front of about nine people, that it is not that, and the difference given was that it was described by a person who was in it and did not ask six other people, and that this is worse and is a different thing, and that the person who said it does not know what to do with it. Neither improved on the other and the chapter does not report what he thought about it afterwards because he did not say.**

*What is deliberately absent from this record.* **The four hundred words on that prospectus, the name of the course, the city, the woman who rang, the company that sent the eight pages, the day the photograph was taken, and the name of the person who pressed the shutter. No page, sheet, file or copy of any part of the prospectus is held by anybody in this case and nobody in that room wrote a word down. Nothing was taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted by anybody in that room or on that Friday, and the woman of thirty-four was not taught what the room is for and did not ask. A share, a relay and a practice are all absent from this chapter. The condition is not quoted in this entry and is not paraphrased. The standing problem is referred to and not said, and there is no rule anywhere in this batch about who may be asked. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, and the clause behind the public version is not named. The master did not move. The woman of about thirty sat in the third chair from the door for the whole of it, was asked for nothing, is in no file in this case, and has not been asked why she comes. Entry 224.**
