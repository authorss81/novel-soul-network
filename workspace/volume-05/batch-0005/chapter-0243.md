# Chapter 243 — One Name For A Whole City

On the Monday morning of week eighty-four a person of thirty-four who buys training for a company came into a cold room off a service road in the Lower Wards and asked the man of twenty-two to be the person a flood telephones, and he said nine words, and then he said the thing he has been saying for two volumes about a room of four rooms, and about nine people did not improve on it, and at about twenty past seven that evening a woman in another building said the word *hold on* for the sixth time in about twelve weeks.

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date, and it has rained for two days, and the water in a basement where he had stood in nine inches on the Friday was nineteen and the pump was still not the fault and the description box said so again.

The word was at nine all morning and eleven from about one in the afternoon and it stayed at eleven.

---

The rain had stopped on the Thursday and come back on the Sunday and the room had four people in it at half past seven on the Monday, and the tally on a scrap beside a kettle said four on the Saturday, four on the Sunday and four on the Monday, and the cook of twenty-seven had stopped reading the numbers out loud and had not stopped keeping them.

She was in at half past seven making tea for people who had come in out of the wet and had not come in because of anything the room does.

---

The woman of thirty-four came in at about ten past eight and had been in that room twice and had asked for nothing both times and holds no key and has no title and is in no file in this case.

She said the useful part first, which is a thing she does.

"**I was asked on Friday for a name. Not a room, not a board in a corridor, a name, and there is a flood coming in three districts in about four days that I know about and the man who asked me does not know the date and neither do I, because I asked him twice and he would not give it to me.**"

"Somebody has to be able to ring somebody."

"**Somebody does have to be able to ring somebody and I have been in three buildings this week looking for a name and there is not one, and I want to be careful here, because the answer I got was that there is no such person, and that answer is correct, and it is correct in the way that a locked door is correct.**"

---

"**I am going to say nine words and I am going to say first that I have said nine words to a person from outside this room four times in this case, and that I am not going to improve on the other four, and that I am saying this first so that nobody afterwards can treat it as a sentence.**"

The room stopped.

"**One name for a whole city is a post.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and then a cook of twenty-seven, with a cloth in her hands, said the thing that nobody else was going to say.

"**That is five words and it is the right nine and you have said it before about one room.**"

"**I have said it about a room with a door and nine chairs and a bell-push, and a city is the same object with about a thousand more chairs in it, and I have known that for about a term and I have never had anybody ask me, and I have been waiting about three weeks for somebody to ask me and I want it noted that I have been waiting, and I would like that noted by somebody other than me.**"

---

Then he said the long half, and it took about four minutes, and he had a docket in his hand and looked at it twice while he said it, and about nine people were in the room and none of them interrupted him.

"**The whole of the argument of that room is that a key is not a post. A key is a thing you can be given. A key is not a post because the person who handed it to you can take it back, and the reason that matters is that the person holding one can be relieved, and the reason a post is a post is that nobody can relieve him of it. I have one key. Nobody can be asked to take it back, because a company is not a person, and I have said that out loud in this room twice and I have not had it fixed.**"

"**A name is a key that has been left out on a counter. The only thing that makes a name not a post is that a person can say I was never in that room, and about four hundred people in this city have said that sentence about themselves this year and about four hundred of them could not take it back afterwards, and I know that because I was one of them and I put a card in a rail with my own hand on it and it is the only thing in five volumes I have made.**"

"**And a person who answers a line is a person who cannot be relieved of answering it. You cannot say to a river, I did not answer for this. You can say to a room, this is not my district, and I have said it four times, and the reason I am not the person for a city is that I would have to say it about nine hundred times, and by about the fourth time it stops being true and starts being a thing I say.**"

He stopped and put the docket in his pocket.

"**That is the whole of what I have and I would like somebody to find a hole in it and about four of you are going to try and I would rather you did it here than on a service road.**"

---

The woman of thirty-four said: "**Then what do I tell them on Wednesday.**"

He said the sentence that has cost him more than anything in this volume, and he said it before he was asked twice.

"**Tell them the number on the plate. Not mine. Not a name. Tell them there is a room on that row with a number on the outside of it and whoever is in it answers it, and tell them the woman who answers it will not know their name and will not ask for it, and tell them that if they want a person they cannot have one and they are going to have to be a room.**"

"That is not an answer," said somebody. "That is a room with a door in it and a bell-push that does not ring by itself."

"**That is a room with a door in it and a bell-push that does not ring by itself and about nine people in it who can each say no, and I have never claimed it was better than that and I am not going to start.**"

---

A cook of twenty-seven put the cloth down and gave the consequence out loud, which is what she does, and which nobody in that room has ever once improved on.

"**Then on a Tuesday at two in the morning, in a flood, the person who picks that telephone up is whoever is standing in this room, and on a Tuesday at two in the morning the person standing in this room is going to be me, and I am a line cook, and I have never run anything in my life, and I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to wash up.**"

"Go on."

"**I said no to that offer of money on the Friday of week eighty-two in about four minutes and I said the heat was what the refusing costs, and I was right, and it is still off, and it is a week into it, and what I have just agreed to is a bigger version of the same thing and I have not got nine words for it, I have got a cloth and a kettle, and I would like somebody to write down that I noticed, and nobody will.**"

Nobody wrote it down. She picked the cloth up and washed up.

---

At about twenty past seven in the evening the telephone on the plate outside that room rang for the sixth time, and a woman of thirty-nine in another building answered it and gave a sentence about a vacant frontage that she has now given to six sets of people, and then said the word.

"Hold on."

It was the sixth time in about twelve weeks. She has said it five times before and the first was a Tuesday in the week of seventy-two.

Nobody in that room thanked her and she is not thanked on any of the five and has said three times that she will not be.

The man on the other end of it was wet and he had a dog and he had rung the only number on any door in a stretch of about four hundred metres, and the plate outside that unit carries a company's telephone number and no name, and he said so, and a man of twenty-two in a cold room said back that he was sorry and did not say anything else, and gave him the address of a church hall with a hole in the roof and said it was two streets and that he should tell anybody there that it is dry at the front, and the dog went to the front.

There is no record in this city of that call except in the ring binder of one person, which is not a system and cannot be emptied.

---

He wrote one thing, on a service road in the wet, at about ten, with the shutter up and the lamp off and the room behind him still cold.

*246. Monday of week 84, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one request, nine words, and a telephone that somebody answered in another building.*

*The day, itemised.* **Eleven on the docket, eleven in date, grade three from twenty to eight. A standpipe valve seized, a stair light, a door that will not latch on a set of steps that is not part of this contract, the same basement pump reported a second time inside four days, a lift call, a bin-store timer that is not the fault, an emergency pack, and a cupboard light.** **Standing water in that basement was nineteen inches at the second attendance, the pump still not the fault, the description box recording the fault as the wall and the source as not known. The city-side unnameable rate stood at nine until about one and at eleven from about one, and was eleven all night. The standing weekly figure is unchanged, is not a rate, is written on the back of a woman's own card, and is on no form.**

*What was asked, and by whom.* **A person of thirty-four who buys training for a company, in the room twice before and asking for nothing in either, asked for a name. The request came from inside her own organisation on the Friday before, for a flood affecting three districts in about four days, of which she does not know the date and has declined to press for. She reported that she had asked in three buildings and that the answer given to her in each was that no such person exists, and that the answer is correct in the way a locked door is correct. She asked the man of twenty-two in front of about nine people, in the plainest words available, and it was not a trap and she was not a mechanism and she is owed nothing.**

*Nine words, and the count.* **Said by the man of twenty-two, after the disclaimer, which he gave first: that he has said nine words to a person from outside that room four times in this case, is not improving on the four, and says so first so that nobody may afterwards treat what follows as a sentence. The words: one name for a whole city is a post. Counted, nine. It is the fifth such answer in this case. A cook of twenty-seven counted it aloud as five words and identified it as the ninth of a sentence she has heard him begin, and she was not the person he was talking to and he did not answer her.**

*The longer argument, and what it cost him.* **About four minutes, with a docket in his hand, to about nine people, uninterrupted. That the case for a key over a post is that the giver can take it back and therefore the holder can be relieved, and that his own key cannot be taken back by anybody because a company is not a person, and that he has said that twice here and it has not been fixed. That a name is a key left out on a counter. That about four hundred people in this city have said the sentence I was never in that room this year and about four hundred of them could not take it back afterwards, and that he is one of them and that the card in the rail is the only thing he has made in this case. That a person who answers a line cannot be relieved of answering it, and that he would have to say this is not my district about nine hundred times before it stopped being true. He then invited four people to find a hole and said he would rather it happened in that room than on a service road. He was not thanked and he was not answered, and the invitation was not taken up by anybody in this entry's hearing.**

*What he offered instead, and the consequence named by somebody else.* **The number on the plate: not his, and not a name,** and whoever is in the room answers it, and the person answering will not know a caller and will not ask for one, and a caller who wants a person cannot have one and must be a room.** A cook of twenty-seven named the consequence aloud and unasked: on a Tuesday at two in the morning in a flood, the person who picks that up is whoever is standing there, which on that hour is her, and she is a line cook who has never run anything, and she has refused an offer of money ten days ago and said then that the heat was what the refusing costs, and that what she has just agreed to is a larger version of the same and that she has a cloth and a kettle and not nine words. She asked for that to be written down by somebody other than her. It was not written down by anybody.**

*The sixth hold on.* **A call to the plate at about twenty past seven, the sixth such call in about twelve weeks, the first having been a Tuesday in the week of seventy-two. A letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file gave the standing sentence about a vacant frontage and then said hold on and put the call through to that room, which has happened five times before and not in this form. The caller was wet, had a dog, and said the plate was the only number on any door for about four hundred metres. He was given the address of a hall with a hole in the roof and was told to say that the front of it is dry. He said the dog was going to the front. Nothing was recorded. The clerk was not thanked, is not thanked on any of the five, and has said three times that she will not be, and holds a sheet of her own paper with five calls on it that she will not bring to that room.**

*Standing state and boundaries.* **Seven days into an unheated fortnight. Four present at half past seven; the milk-order scrap reads four, four and four for the last three days and its author has stopped reading it aloud. Twelve lines on the wall, thirty-two days old. Seven items in the ring binder. The counter bare. The room's own age: a hundred and twelve days. A card in a rail in Lattice Ward, a hundred and sixteen days, and that is a third object and is neither on this wall nor in this building. The board over the door carrying four words in a sign-writer's hand and eight in a cook's, the upper of which is untrue and has not been altered. No teaching, no demonstration, no assessment and no assistance was given or received by anybody, and a room off a service road is not a practice in that department. No page was made. The standing question is untouched and unnumbered. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank and no message has arrived. Entry 246.**
