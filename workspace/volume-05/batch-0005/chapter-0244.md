# Chapter 244 — The Tenth Sitting

The tenth sitting of that Exchange fell on the Wednesday morning of week eighty-four, four weeks to the day after the ninth, and the book did not open, and the reason it did not open was about a plate on a door in a district that takes water off a hill, and the count of telephone calls moved from thirty-seven to forty-one, and a woman of sixty-one said the number out loud and then did the subtraction aloud and then refused to do it again.

---

The back room with the shutters up is above the line. That is a fact and not a claim and she has been in it for nineteen years and has never once said it out loud in that room until this morning, and she said it because on the Friday a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket told nine people in another part of this city that three districts take water off that row.

The nine chairs were in their curve with the two ends turned in. The ninth of them was against the wall facing away from the room, where it has been since the sitting before last, and nobody moved it, and nobody has ever asked the person who put it there why she put it there.

---

"**A book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call.**"

She has said that at every sitting of that room. It has now been given ten times, asked for nine times in nineteen years, and twice it has been given to a room that had not asked for it, which is this morning and the one four weeks ago.

Nobody asked her to say it. Somebody said thank you, and she said: "**Do not thank me for the rule, thank whoever asked for it, and nobody has since about nine years ago, and the two people in this room who were in the room then are the two people in this room now who are not the book.**"

---

The number came at about twenty past ten, unasked, as it has come at nine of the last ten sittings and at five sittings before this book existed.

"**Thirty-seven to forty-one. Four in four weeks. That is the highest it has gone in a four-week period since I started keeping it, and I am telling you that because I have never told you that before, and I am telling you it is about the number and not about the room.**"

"Give us the rest of it," said somebody.

"**I have announced this number fifteen times. Ten of those are the ten Wednesdays this book holds and five of them predate it, because I kept a count on a piece of paper before there was a book, which is a thing I have never said in this room and have now said.**"

She did the subtraction on her fingers and got the same answer twice and stopped.

"**I am not doing it again. I did it four weeks ago and I did it four weeks before that and the figure is about me and has never once been about the room, and the room is older than the book, and if anybody wants the arithmetic they can have it off me individually and not in front of these nine.**"

---

Then she gave the reason, which was the tenth reason that room has had for not opening a book, and the first one that is about a door rather than about a person.

"**On the Friday, on a day I have already said was a Friday, a man telephoned this desk and asked whether a room on a service road in the Lower Wards was in this book. He did not say the room's name. He said there was a number on a plate on the outside of a door and no name on the plate, and that a woman in an office had said the two words hold on to him, and he wanted to know whether the people who go in there are people who ring here.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I cannot answer him without opening the book, and there is one way to open this book, and it is on a table in front of these nine, and I am not going to do that to find out whether a man in a wet street is on a page.**"

She put her hand flat on the cover of it and did not move it.

"**That is the whole reason and I have had it since about eleven o'clock on Friday and I have not slept properly and I have not told anybody, and you are all going to say that I should have rung him, and I am not going to ring him, and if somebody in this room rings him I will know and I will not thank them for it.**"

---

A man of about thirty-one in the back row said the thing that had been sitting in the room for two weeks and that nobody had put on the table.

"**Mrs Nunn. When the water comes, this room is going to be a room people want to telephone, and everybody in it is going to tell them to telephone a room off a service road, and you are the only person in this city who is going to be asked to be a person about it instead. I want to know now whether you will do it.**"

"I have thought about it," she said, "**and the answer is no, and I am going to say it in more than nine words so that nobody counts them.**"

"**I will be the one who knows that it is a room and not a person.**"

Somebody said, "That's fifteen."

"That is fifteen," she said, "**and it is the longest sentence I have said in this room in about four years, and the two of you who were in the room four years ago know that, and the seven of you who were not, do not, and I am not going to explain the difference, and it is a book.**"

---

The room was quiet for about a minute and then a young man of twenty-one who has been coming to that room since he was seventeen said that the ninth chair had been moved again before he got here, by somebody who has not been here since he was fifteen, and that he was not reporting it, he was saying it out loud because there was a person in the room, and Bea Nunn said that a chair is a chair and that anybody who wants to move a chair in this room may move a chair in this room, and that is the whole of what she has to say about furniture, and the young man said that was the fourth time she had said it and he was keeping count, and she said she was not going to confirm that.

The chair was against the wall at the end of the morning and nobody had asked her to move it and nobody has.

---

The book stayed shut. It is at forty-five lines and has been since a caller came and was not asked to sign and said she would have said yes, which is the only thing that has ever grown it.

Nobody signed anything. Nobody was described. The thirteen words on line forty-five are not in this chapter and are not in any record of this sitting and were not quoted by anybody in that room.

Before she left she said one more thing to the room and it was the only thing anybody wrote down afterwards, in a notebook that is not the book and is not in the room.

"**Forty-one calls and forty-five lines. The book is ahead of the telephone for the first time since I began keeping both, and anybody who tells you that is a coincidence is telling you about a coincidence. It is not a coincidence. It is that I have not opened it and it does not fill while I do not, and the day I open it is the day the room starts filling and neither of those is a thing I can hold and put down.**"

She has said before that a book that grows is a book she can no longer refuse. She said that on a Wednesday in week seventy-two and she did not say it today.

He wrote one thing, on a service road, at about ten that night, at a shutter that was down and cold on the outside of it.

*247. Wednesday of week 84, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is one sitting, one reason, and a number that went up by four.*

*The sitting.* **The tenth, on the Wednesday of week eighty-four, four weeks after the ninth to the day. Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in; the ninth against the wall facing away from the room, where it has stood since the sitting before last, put there by a person who has not been in that room since she was fifteen and who was not asked about it then and is not asked about it now and gave no reason and has not been named in that room in this chapter. Nothing was signed. Nobody was described. The book was not opened.**

*The rule.* **Given unasked and whole: that a book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call. Given ten times at ten sittings, asked for nine times in nineteen years, and given on two occasions to a room that had not asked for it, which are the ninth and the tenth sittings. A thank-you was offered and refused, on the ground that the rule should be thanked to whoever asked for it and that nobody has for about nine years.**

*The figures, and the arithmetic done aloud.* **The telephone count moved from thirty-seven to forty-one, four in four weeks, and this is the highest four-week movement since the count began. Announced unasked at about twenty past ten, on the standing practice. Stated then: that the figure has been announced fifteen times; that ten of the announcements correspond to the ten Wednesdays this book holds; that five predate the book and were kept on a separate sheet, a fact never before stated in that room; that the subtraction was performed on the fingers twice and produced the same figure; and that it will not be performed again in front of nine people. The figure is stated by its author to be about herself and never about the room, and that the room is older than the book.**

*The reason the book did not open.* **The tenth such reason and the first about a door rather than a person. On the Friday before, a man telephoned that desk and asked whether a room behind a door carrying a plate with a telephone number and no name on it was represented in that book, having been put through by a clerk in another building who said hold on. He gave a street and not a room's name. The holder of the book cannot answer without opening it, and there is one way to open it and it is on a table in front of nine people, and she declined to do that in order to find out whether a wet man is on a page. She has had the reason since about eleven o'clock on the Friday, has not slept properly, told nobody, and has stated in advance that she will not telephone him and will not thank anybody in that room who does.**

*A request refused, and a figure of it disputed.* **A man of about thirty-one in the back row asked in advance whether she would be the person a flood telephones instead of a room. Her answer: that she will be the one who knows that it is a room and not a person, and she said she would say it in more than nine words so that nobody counted. Two persons counted, one at fifteen and one at fourteen; she confirmed fifteen, said it was the longest sentence she has spoken in that room in about four years, said the two people who were in the room four years ago know that and the seven who were not do not, and declined to explain the difference, and called it a book.**

*The ninth chair.* **A young man of twenty-one, in attendance since he was seventeen, stated aloud that the chair had been moved again before his arrival by a person not in attendance, and stated that he was not reporting it and was saying it because there was a person in the room. The chair is a chair; anybody may move a chair. The young man said he was keeping a count and was not to be confirmed. It remained against the wall at the close. It has not been asked about and does not move.**

*The last sentence of the morning.* **Spoken to the room and written afterwards in a private notebook which is not the book and is not kept in that room: that at forty-one calls and forty-five lines the book stands ahead of the telephone for the first time since both were kept; that it is not a coincidence; that it does not fill while it is shut; and that the day it is opened is the day the room begins to fill, and that neither is a thing she can hold and put down. She did not repeat on this occasion the statement, made on a Wednesday in week seventy-two, that a book which grows is a book she can no longer refuse.**

*Position of the run, and boundaries.* **Ten sittings; nine intervals of four weeks; the tally of sittings at which nothing has been added is now nine. The book stands at forty-five lines and has not opened since the caller who was not asked to sign. The lines on that page remain unquoted, unparaphrased and uncounted in this entry. The room above the line was stated to be above the line, by its own holder, for the first time in nineteen years, and was not enlarged upon. No teaching, no assistance, no relay and no comparison were performed by anybody, and the department's conditions are neither quoted nor paraphrased here. The licensor is unnamed, its field is blank, and no message has been received. Entry 247.**
