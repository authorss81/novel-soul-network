# Chapter 250 — Forty-Six Lines

The eleventh sitting of that Exchange fell on the Wednesday morning of week eighty-eight, four weeks to the day after the tenth, and the book opened for about eleven seconds, and one line went in, and the person it belongs to was not described and was not counted and asked for nothing, and it is forty-six lines, and a woman of sixty-one said the number of telephone calls and would not do the subtraction again, and in the evening nine people in a cold room off a service road learned the address of a ring binder that nobody had told them about.

---

The book is at forty-six lines, the second of them put in on a Monday in the week of seventy-one and not looked at since, the fortieth is three years old, and the forty-fourth has a hole in it where a woman of sixty-eight in bay four should have been, because the caller's words went in and the caller's words have a hole in them, and it is B. Nunn's name in the fourth column.

The room above the line is above the line.

---

"**A book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call.**"

It has been given at every sitting of that room. It has now been given eleven times, asked for ten times in nineteen years, and given unasked on two occasions.

"Nobody is going to thank me for that," she said, "**and if anybody does I will ask them to stop, and the reason I say that is that somebody thanked me last month and I have not got over it.**"

---

The count came at about ten past ten, unasked, as it comes.

"**Forty-one to forty-four. Three in four weeks, which is less than the four we had and more than the two we had in the spring, and I am telling you the direction and not the number because the number is about me.**"

"Give us the rest of it."

"**I have announced it sixteen times. Eleven of those are the eleven Wednesdays this book holds and five predate it, and there is a sheet of paper with the five on it that I have not shown anybody and that I am not going to.**"

She put two fingers on the cover and left them there.

"**Forty-four calls and forty-six lines. The book is two ahead of the telephone and was one ahead four weeks ago, and the gap has not closed, and I do not know what makes a gap, and I have not looked into it and I am not going to in front of nine people.**"

---

"**Now I am going to tell you why it opened, and I have been not telling you for eleven minutes, and four of you have been watching me not tell you.**"

She took her hand off the cover.

"**A man came on the Friday. He telephoned the plate on a door first, on the Tuesday of the water, and was put through to a room with about four people in it, and was given the address of a hall. He telephoned this office after that and asked me whether the people who go in that room are people who come here, and I told him what I told you last month, which is that I cannot answer him without opening this on a table in front of nine people, and that is true, and it is also a way of not doing anything.**"

"And now he has come."

"**Now he has come and stood in that doorway and said about nine sentences and then said he would like to be in this book, and he did not ask me whether he could, and he did not ask whether he had to, and I did not ask him to sign anything and he did not offer, and I wrote what he said in his own words because he said it to my face, which is the only condition this rule has ever had and which I have never once said out loud in nineteen years.**"

"How many words."

"**I am not going to tell you how many words. You are not going to ask me how many words. It is not a number, it is a line, and it is the second one in this book that has gone in with a person standing in front of me and the first in four years, and the reason the first one took four weeks and this one took eleven seconds is that the first one came and I asked her whether she was sure, and that was me.**"

Nobody was described. Nobody was counted. The book is shut.

---

A young man of twenty-one who has been coming to that room since he was seventeen said the ninth chair had been moved again, for the second time he had noticed, and Bea Nunn said that a chair is a chair, and that she has said that four times, and that if anybody wants a fifth time they can move the chair themselves.

He said: "**You have said it four times and I have kept the count and you have never once confirmed the count and I have never once been asked to stop keeping it.**"

"**Then don't stop,**" she said. "**I have not confirmed it because confirming it is a minute, and I do not take minutes, and that is the one thing in this room I have never been tempted about.**"

The chair was against the wall at the end of the morning.

---

She said one more thing before the shutters came down, and it was to the room, and about four of the nine wrote it down afterwards in notebooks that are not the book and are not in that room.

"**I said a thing in this room in the week of seventy-two about a book that grows and what it does to the person who has to hold it. I have not repeated it. I am not going to repeat it today, when it would have been about nine times easier, and I would like about four people in here to notice that I have not, and I am not going to explain why I have not, because explaining it is a minute.**"

Somebody said: "**Does the book have to be carried anywhere?**"

"**Does it.**"

"**Is there a room in this city it could be carried to.**"

"**There are about nine and I have not been to any of them and I am not going to be carried anywhere by anybody who has not asked me first, and if somebody carries me to one without asking, then this book is a thing being moved, and I have watched about four things in this city be turned into things being moved in my time and I did not notice while it was happening and neither did anybody else.**"

---

At about half past four in the evening there were about eleven people in a cold room off a service road in the Lower Wards and the board over the door carried four words in chalk and a grey ghost of four that could not be got off, and a hardboard on two nails carried thirteen lines, and the ring binder was on the shelf with seven things in it.

A man of about thirty-one, who had not been in that room before, stood at the counter and said he had heard there was a book.

"**A book of what people said.**"

"**A ring binder with seven things in it. It is not a book and it is not a system and it cannot be emptied and nobody who wrote in it can be found in it. You can take it off that shelf and read it now, and if you are the person who wrote one of the things in it you may take that page out and nobody will stop you and nobody will be told.**"

"**Who says I am allowed to read it?**"

Somebody at the back said: "**Nobody. Come and read it.**"

"**Then who is not allowed to know that I read it?**"

"**That is a different question and nobody here has an answer to it and about nine of us have been asked it in the last fortnight and there is no paper anywhere in this city that says, and that is not an oversight, we decided it in about nine days and nobody in the city agreed to it.**"

The man of about thirty-one read the binder for about nine minutes standing up, and did not write anything down, and did not ask anybody anything, and put it back on the shelf, and said one sentence on his way out that about four people heard and nobody wrote down.

"**I have been in about nine rooms in this city with a book in them and I have never once been told to put it back.**"

---

Talia Venn was in the corridor at about half past five for about four minutes and he was in it for the same four minutes, and neither of them said anything that either of them would say again, and neither of them said nothing.

"**I have been asked to carry that binder somewhere,**" he said. "**By a woman with a diary who has not been asked to be carried anywhere in nineteen years and who has now been asked by somebody she has never met, and I do not think it should go, and I have thought about it for about an hour, which is the longest I have thought about anything this year.**"

"**You have not asked her.**"

"**No.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because she said a thing a fortnight ago in that room that she has not repeated since, and I am not going to be the reason somebody asks her again.**"

She looked at him for about nine seconds and then said: "**That is the first correct thing you have said about this in a fortnight and I am not going to do anything with it, and if you tell anybody I said that I will deny it and you will not be able to prove it, and that is a thing I have wanted to be able to do for about a year and it has not come up.**"

Then she went out into the rain and he went to a shutter, and neither of them said one word about any of it to anybody afterwards, and neither of them has said it again.

---

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card and is on no form, and about nine people in the last fortnight have learned the address of a ring binder in a room off a service road, and a woman of thirty-one with a diary has been asked to be moved and has not been asked again, and a board over a door says in a hand that is not a sign-writer's that anybody wet may come in, which is true about eleven days a year, and a hardboard on two nails says thirteen things of which the first is that the room will not keep anything it cannot take back, and a woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door with a bag on her lap and has not been asked anything since the Monday of week seventy and says that anybody who wants to ask her again can come and ask once and she will answer once.

The heating is still off. The shutter came down at about ten and the room was cold.

He wrote one thing, on a service road, at about ten, in the wet.

*253. Wednesday of week 88, at about ten on a service road in the wet, and this entry is one line, one number, and a ring binder off a shelf.*

*The sitting.* **The eleventh, on the Wednesday of week eighty-eight, four weeks to the day after the tenth, in a room whose holder states that it is above the line. Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and the ninth against the wall facing away from the room. The book was opened for about eleven seconds. One line was entered. It is the forty-sixth. The book was shut again. Nobody was described, nobody was counted, and the number of words on the new line was not given and was not asked for by anybody in that room.**

*The rule, and what nobody had been told about it.* **Given unasked and whole: that a book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call. Given eleven times at eleven sittings, asked for ten times in nineteen years, given on two occasions to a room that had not asked for it, which are the ninth and the tenth. A thank-you was offered, refused, and the refusal was attributed to a thank-offered last month. The holder then stated, unprompted, a condition that record shows she has never said aloud in nineteen years: that the rule permits writing what a caller said only where the caller said it to her face, that this is why the first such line took four weeks and the second took eleven seconds, and that the first took four weeks because she asked the caller whether she was sure, and that was the act, not the hesitation.**

*The figures.* **Telephone calls from forty-one to forty-four, three in four weeks; announced unasked at about ten past ten; direction given, number withheld on the ground that the figure is about the person keeping it. Announced for the sixteenth time in total, eleven of those being the eleven Wednesdays this book holds and five predating it and remaining on a separate sheet not shown to anybody. Stated in the room: that the book stands two lines ahead of the telephone count, that it was one ahead four weeks earlier, that the gap has not closed, and that she does not know what makes a gap and has not looked and will not look in front of nine people.**

*The delay, named by the person who caused it.* **The holder stated that the book had not opened for the first eleven minutes of the sitting and that about four people had watched her not open it. Her account: that a man telephoned the plate on a door in the Lower Wards on the Tuesday of the water and was put through to a room of about four people and given the address of a hall; that he then telephoned that office and asked whether the people who go in that room are people who come there; that she told him, as she told the room four weeks earlier, that the question cannot be answered without opening the book in front of nine people; that this is true; and that it is also a way of not doing anything. He came in person on the Friday, said about nine sentences, and asked to be in the book without being asked whether he could or had to. He was not asked to sign and did not offer. She wrote his words in his own words.**

*The ninth chair.* **A young man of twenty-one, in attendance since he was seventeen, reported for the second time that the chair had been moved again by a person not in attendance. The holder's answer, unchanged and given for the fourth time: that a chair is a chair. He said he keeps a count of how often she has said it, that she has never confirmed the count, and that he has never been asked to stop; and was told not to. The chair was against the wall at the close. It was not asked about, and its mover was not named, and no reason was given, in this entry or in that room.**

*The sentence not repeated.* **Having been given in that room in the week of seventy-two concerning what a growing book does to the person who must hold it, it was not repeated on the occasion on which it would have been about nine times easier. She asked that about four people notice the non-repetition and gave no reason, on the ground that giving a reason is a minute, and that she has never taken a minute. She was then asked whether the book has to be carried anywhere: it does. She was asked whether there is a room in this city it could be carried to: there are about nine, she has been to none, she will not be carried anywhere by anybody who has not asked her first, and if that happens the book becomes a thing being moved, which she has watched happen about four times in her time in this city, in each case without noticing while it was happening and without anybody else noticing either.**

*The binder, off the shelf.* **At about half past four, in the room off the service road, a man of about thirty-one not previously in attendance asked for a book of what people had said. He was told it is a ring binder with seven things in it, that it is not a book and not a system and cannot be emptied, that no person who wrote in it can be found in it, that he may take it off the shelf and read it there and then, and that if he is the person who wrote one of the seven he may take that page out, that nobody will stop him and that nobody will be told. Asked who says he is allowed to read it, the answer from the room was: nobody, come and read it. Asked who is not allowed to know that he read it, the answer given was that it is a different question, that nobody in that room has an answer, that about nine people have been asked it in the last fortnight, and that there is no paper anywhere in this city that says, and that this was decided in about nine days and nobody in the city agreed to it. He read for about nine minutes standing up, wrote nothing, asked nobody anything, replaced the binder on the shelf, and said one sentence on his way out that about four people heard and nobody wrote down.**

*A corridor, four minutes, and nothing.* **At about half past five he said to a woman of twenty-four that he had been asked to carry the binder somewhere, by a woman with a diary who has not been carried anywhere in nineteen years and who has now been asked by a stranger; that he does not think it should go; and that he has thought about it for about an hour. He was told he had not asked her, that the reason had not been given, and that he has not asked her because she made a statement in that room a fortnight ago that she has not repeated since and he will not be the reason anybody asks her again. She said that was the first correct thing he had said about it in a fortnight, that she was not going to do anything with it, and that if he tells anybody she said so she will deny it and he will not be able to prove it, and that she has wanted to be able to do that for about a year and it has not come up. She then left into the rain. No scene, no declaration and no kiss; neither said a word about it afterwards to anybody; neither has said it again. Four minutes in a corridor, and a bench that is not reopened, and a Tuesday that is not revisited, and a page that stays closed.**
