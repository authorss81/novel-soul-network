# Chapter 249 — Four Words In Another Hand

On the Friday of week eighty-seven a woman of about sixty-eight who had been in that room four times in three weeks and had not said anything in any of them took a thumb to four words in a sign-writer's hand and put four words of her own in their place, and said out loud what it cost her to be a person who can do that, and at about four in the afternoon a woman of twenty-four gave her name for the first time in that room and said the two sentences that the flood had made necessary, and neither of them was about the flood.

---

Ten on the docket and nine in date, and a fire door in Nine Bridges came up a seventh time and he did not go, and wrote in the description box that the fault is the door and that he has not attended, and a district man of thirty-eight read that and said nothing, which is the first time that has happened in about five months.

The word was at nine and ten all day. The heating in that room was off on its thirty-second day.

---

A woman of about thirty-four with a ring file telephoned the plate at about eleven in the morning and gave the sentence about a vacant frontage, which she has now given to six sets of people, and nobody in that room has ever asked her a question, and then she said one other thing, which she had not said before and did not present as an answer to anything.

"**Our landlord has not asked about that unit once since the water. I checked on Friday and I checked again this morning and there is nothing in that file about you people, and that is the sixth time I have said the other sentence and I am not going to say it a seventh, and I am telling you the absence because nobody asks me for absences and I have about nine years of them.**"

"**What does that mean.**"

"**It means that if you ever need the reason that the first year is free, the reason will not be in that file, and it has never been in that file, and I have told you that for about sixteen weeks and I am not going to be asked about it by anybody in this city.**"

She was not thanked. She is not thanked on any of the six and has said she will not be.

---

The woman of about sixty-eight came in at about half past twelve and sat in a chair by the wall and was there at four o'clock and has been in that room four times in three weeks, twice during the fortnight when the water was at the end of the road, and on both of those occasions she was given a cup of tea and a chair and nothing else, and on both of those occasions she said nothing.

Nobody knows what brought her in the first time and nobody has asked.

At about a quarter past four she stood up and went to the board over the door, which for about four weeks has carried four words in a sign-writer's hand and nothing else, and she put her thumb against the first of them and rubbed, and the letters came off, and about nine people watched her do it, and it took about four seconds and it left a grey ghost of the word underneath, which cannot be got off a board.

She did not apologise and nobody asked her to.

Then she took a piece of chalk out of her coat pocket and wrote four words underneath the ghost, in chalk, in an ordinary hand, and put the chalk back in her pocket.

"**Anybody wet, come in.**"

---

She said the cost of it out loud before anybody could ask her what she thought she was doing, and it took about eleven seconds, and she said it to the room and not to the man of twenty-two.

"**I am sixty-eight and I have been in about four rooms in this city in about nine years where there was a sign on a door saying what a person was allowed to do in there, and in three of the four I could not take the sign down, and in the fourth I could and I did not, and that is the whole of my experience and I have not said any of it to anybody.**"

"Why now."

"**Because that is the only one of the four that was wrong. The other three were right. I could take this one down with a thumb and I have taken it down with a thumb and I would like somebody in this room to understand what I have just become, which is a person who can take a sign down, because in about four years somebody is going to stand in that doorway and ask me to put a different one up, and I am going to have to be the one who knows where the chalk is.**"

Nobody improved on it.

"And the four words are true about eleven days a year and false the other three hundred and fifty-six, and a sign does not have to be true all year. **It has to be true on the day somebody is standing in the rain with nothing.** That is all I have got and I am not going to say it twice."

---

A cook of twenty-seven came out of the back and looked at the board for a while and then said the whole of what she had to say about it, which was about nine words.

"**That is four words and mine were eight, and I got mine wrong, and she has not said so and I am not going to say so for her, and I am going to wash up.**"

The sign-writer was about sixty, was paid four pounds, has not been thanked, is not a character in this room and is not going to be, and is not in this chapter, and nobody in that room said his name.

---

At about four in the afternoon a woman of twenty-four came in and gave her name at the door, which is Talia Venn, and about four people in that room heard it, and the man of twenty-two was at the counter and did not turn round and did not say anything, and nobody remarked on either.

She had been in that room five times in a term and a half and had been described in it by a number.

She said the first of the two sentences without sitting down.

"**That thing is still able to be taken over, and I am going to say that in a room where the person it is most likely to be taken over by is standing with his back to me, and I am not saying it to be cruel and I am not saying it because I have a position here, because I have not got one and have not asked for one.**"

"Go on."

"**It will not be taken by a villain. There is no villain in this, there is a company and a district and a woman with a clipboard, and all three of them are right, and what takes a thing like this is a Tuesday, and a person who is tired, and a version of it that is obviously better for about nine people, and there is nothing in this room that can stop any of those three things and there never was.**"

"**And the second thing. It cannot cross a district line. Not because of a rule, because there is no rule anywhere in this city about a room, and not because of money, because a room in a hall with a hatch in it has been doing it since the flood with a cook's numbers and a man's list of buildings.**"

"Then what is stopping it."

"**You,**" she said. "**There is a man of twenty-nine who can get into four hundred buildings and a cook who can put four hundred and eleven cups into wet hands and a first-year who can be asked to stop, and there is no fourth thing, and the fourth thing is nine keys, and there are two.**"

---

He turned round at that point and said the count himself, in about nine seconds, and it is the first time in this volume that the two have been said aloud in the same room as the number.

"**Nine are needed and there are two. One of them is in my pocket and nobody can be asked to take it back, and one of them has been in the pocket of a woman of thirty-one since the Thursday of week eighty-two, and that is the entire reason I am not a post, and I have been saying that for a term and a half and this is the first time the numbers have been in one sentence.**"

"And the other seven."

"**Do not exist. I have never met anybody who has one and I have never said that out loud in this room and I am saying it now because a woman of twenty-four has just said the number and I would rather it were said in front of the people who own the other two.**"

---

He then asked the question that has been on the back of a docket for about fourteen weeks, out loud, in that room, of a woman of thirty-one with a diary who had come in her own coat and had not brought anything.

"**You can be asked to take it back. About nine seconds. Is that still true.**"

"**It has been true since the Thursday you gave it to me and it will be true on a day you have not chosen.**"

"And if somebody in this room asked you to."

"**They would have me in about nine seconds and I would not have said no and I would not have had to think about it, and I would come in my own coat and I would not bring anything, and you know all of that because you have seen it once.**"

"Would you mind."

"**I would mind about nine seconds' worth and then I would be in a coat.**"

---

A first-year of nineteen asked whether any of that could be written down, and the woman of thirty-one said no in about four seconds and gave the reason, and the reason is the wall's first line and it is the first time in this volume that the standing question has come up in a room in the shape of an answer to a practical question.

"**You could write it. It would go on that wall and anybody could read it, and then this room has got a rule about who may be asked to take a key back, and a rule about who may be asked is a rule about who is findable, and there is no such rule in this room and I am not going to help write one on a wet Tuesday for a reason that will feel like tidiness in about four years.**"

"That is nine words and one of them is wet."

"**That is nine words and I have not counted them and I am not going to.**"

Nobody wrote anything down. The binder has seven things in it and did not get an eighth.

---

At about half past five the telephone on the plate rang and a man of twenty-one answered it and it was a woman asking whether a room was still open and he said it was, and asked her nothing, and told her where the dry ground was, and she said thank you, and he said he was welcome, and nobody in the room heard either of those two sentences.

The shutter came down at about ten and the board over the door carried four words in chalk and a grey ghost of four in a sign-writer's hand, and there was nothing else on it.

He wrote one thing, at about ten, at the end of that service road with the shutter behind him and the cold coming off the metal, and he wrote it standing up.

*252. Friday of week 87, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one thumb, four words, and the number of keys.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Third Friday rota of this movement, grade three from twenty to eight. Ten on the docket and nine in date. The tenth is a stair fire door in Nine Bridges, the seventh report of the same fault in about a month; he did not attend, recorded that the fault is the door and that he had not attended, and a district man of thirty-eight read that entry and said nothing, which is the first silence in about five months. The rate was nine and ten throughout the day. The heating in that room is on its thirty-second day off and unpaid. The city's weekly figure is unaltered, is not a rate, and stands on the back of a woman's own card.**

*One telephone call and one absence.* **At about eleven a letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file gave the standing sentence about a vacant frontage, the sixth giving. Nobody in that room has ever asked her a question about it. She then volunteered, unasked and not as an answer, that the landlord has not asked about that unit once since the water, that she checked on the Friday and again that morning, that there is nothing in that file about the people in that room, and that this is the sixth time she has said the other sentence and she will not say it a seventh. Her stated meaning, asked for and given: that if the reason the first year is free is ever needed it will not be in that file, that it never has been, and that she has said so for about fifteen weeks and will not be asked about it by anybody in this city. She was not thanked, is not thanked on any of the six, and says she will not be. The reason remains unrecorded in this case after a seventh refusal of the question.**

*The board, and the hand that changed it.* **At about a quarter past four a woman of about sixty-eight, in four attendances in three weeks and silent in all of the other three, including two during the flood when she was given tea and a chair and nothing else, removed the four words in a sign-writer's hand with her own thumb in about four seconds, in front of about nine people, without apologising and without being asked to. A grey ghost of the first word remains and cannot be removed from that board. She then wrote four words in chalk, in her own hand, and put the chalk away: that anybody wet, come in.** **Her stated cost, given before she was asked and in about eleven seconds, addressed to the room: that she is sixty-eight, that she has been in about four rooms in about nine years where a sign on a door said what a person was allowed to do, that in three of them she could not take the sign down and in the fourth she could and did not, and that this is the whole of her experience and she has not said it to anybody; that she did it now because that is the only one of the four signs that was wrong; and that she has become a person who can take a sign down, and that in about four years somebody will stand in that doorway and ask her to put a different one up, and she will be the one who knows where the chalk is. She added that the four words are true about eleven days a year and false the other three hundred and fifty-six, that a sign does not have to be true all year, and that it has to be true on the day somebody is standing in the rain with nothing, and that she will not say that twice.** **A cook of twenty-seven, about nine words, from a sink: that the new line is four words and hers were eight, that hers was wrong, that the woman of sixty-eight did not say so and she will not say so for her, and that she is going to wash up. The sign-writer is not named, not thanked, and not in this chapter, and no person in that room said his name.**

*The capture, and the city line, said by somebody else.* **A woman of twenty-four gave her name at the door as Talia Venn, which is the first time in this room, and about four people heard it and the man of twenty-two did not turn round. Two statements, both standing.** **First, that the thing is still able to be taken over, said to a room in which the person most likely to be taken over by is standing with his back to her, and prefaced by her own statement that she holds no position in it and has not asked for one: that it will not be taken by a villain, that there is no villain in it, that there is a company, a district and a woman with a clipboard and all three are right, and that what takes a thing of this kind is a Tuesday, a person who is tired, and a version of it that is obviously better for about nine people, and that nothing in that room can stop any of those three and never could.** **Second, that it cannot yet cross a district line, and not because of a rule — there is no rule in this city about a room — and not because of money, since a room in a hall with a hatch in it has been doing it since the flood with a cook's numbers and a man's list of buildings. Asked what is stopping it, she answered: that there is a man of twenty-nine who can get into four hundred buildings, a cook who can put four hundred and eleven cups into wet hands, a first-year who can be asked to stop, and no fourth thing, and that the fourth thing is nine keys, and that there are two.**

*The count of keys, said aloud together for the first time.* **Stated by the man of twenty-two, in about nine seconds: that nine are needed and that there are two; that his cannot be asked back by anybody; that the other has been in the pocket of a woman of thirty-one since the Thursday of week eighty-two; that this is the entire reason he is not a post; and that the other seven do not exist, that he has never met a person holding one, and that he is saying so in front of the two people who own the other two. No other figure of keys was produced in this entry and none may be inferred from it.**

*The docket question, answered aloud.* **About fourteen weeks old, on the back of a docket, four people had answered it and nobody had written the answers down. It was asked aloud for the first time in that room of a woman of thirty-one with a diary, who came in her own coat and brought nothing: that she can be asked to take the key back, in about nine seconds, that it has been true since the Thursday it was handed over and will be true on a day he has not chosen, and that if anybody in that room asked her she would have it in about nine seconds without thinking about it, would come in her own coat, and would bring nothing. Asked whether she would mind, she said she would mind about nine seconds' worth and then she would be in a coat.** **The answers were not written down. The ring binder holds seven items and did not gain an eighth, and a first-year of nineteen asked whether they could be and was refused in about four seconds, on the ground that a written answer would put a rule about who may be asked on a wall, that a rule about who may be asked is a rule about who is findable, and that there is no such rule in that room and she is not going to help write one on a wet Tuesday for a reason that will feel like tidiness in about four years. Nine words were identified aloud in that sentence and were not counted by anybody else.**

*The last call of the day.* **At about half past five the plate was answered by a man of twenty-one who asked the caller nothing, gave her the location of dry ground, and exchanged two sentences of nothing. Nobody in that room heard them. No record of the call exists in this city.**

*State and boundaries.* **Four words in chalk and one grey ghost on a board over a door. Thirteen lines on a hardboard on two nails, the first twelve of them fifty-seven days old and the thirteenth eight days old. Seven items in a ring binder. The counter bare.
