# Chapter 242 — A Sump Pump That Was Not The Fault

The first Friday rota of this movement ran on the Friday of week eighty-three, four days into a fortnight in which the heating in a converted shop unit off a service road in the Lower Wards was off and nobody had paid for it, and by that Friday it had rained for four days and the tally on a scrap of kitchen paper beside a kettle had gone from eleven down to seven and back up to eight, and the eight was not eight people who wanted to be there.

---

Ten on the docket and nine in date, and one of the nine was a sump pump in a basement in a district whose name is not in this book, which had run for nine hours on a night when nothing came down heavy enough to need it, which had a float switch that had been set by somebody who is not named here and was never asked, and which had been reported by two people into the same box inside a week, both of whom were right and neither of whom was the fault.

He went at about half past four in the afternoon, in the rain, and there was about nine inches of standing water in there and nothing in it to do with the pump.

He wrote in the description box that the fault is not the pump, and that the water in that room is about nine inches deep and is coming through a wall that has no opening in it on this side, and he did not write where he thinks it is coming from, and a district man of thirty-eight said that it was the first entry in about five months that had stated what the fault was not and had declined to state what it was.

"That's the first one that has told me it doesn't know," he said. "Everybody else tells me what it is."

---

The rain came on the Monday and it did not stop until the Thursday afternoon and by the Friday evening the hardboard on the two nails had a run of water across the bottom third of it that nobody wiped, and a cook of twenty-seven said that she was not wiping it because a wall that has been rained on should look as though it has been rained on, and that this is a thing she knows and is not going to explain to anybody who is going to write it down.

The room was cold. Nine people in it at half past seven, which is the most there has been on a Friday since the fortnight before the shutter went down the first time.

---

At about ten past eight a man of fifty-one with a tool bag sat down with his coat still on and said that he had come in out of it and that the low end of his patch is the low end of three districts, and that he had put about nine hundred door numbers on buildings in this city over about nine years and that a number on a door is a thing you can find a building with and not a thing you can find a person in, and that in about four years somebody will put a number on this door that means a person, and that he would like it on the record that he said so first.

"**I have wanted somebody to write that down for about four years and I have never once wanted it written down more than I wanted it not to be true, and I am aware of that, and I would like it written anyway.**"

A cook of twenty-seven said one word about it, and the word was not agreement, and she did not say which word.

---

At about half past eight a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket came in off the wet and stood by the door with his hood down and said the useful part before anybody had asked him for any of it.

"**Three districts take water off that row and two of them take it off a river that is about nine miles from here and the third one takes it off a hill. I am not going to tell you when. I have been wrong about that twice and I am not going to be wrong about it a third time in a room. What I will tell you is that the only list in this city that is worth anything in a fortnight's time is a list of who is in which building, and that I could produce one of those in about a day, and that I am not asking you for one, and that I have wanted to ask you for one for about four years.**"

Somebody said, "Why not ask."

"**Because you have got nine people in there and every one of them could be found in about a day, and I do not know that, and I do not want to know it, and there is a difference and I have thought about it in a car for about a week and the difference is that not knowing is the only thing I have got.**"

---

A first-year of nineteen said the rest of it, standing, in about nine seconds, and it was the thing she had been saying since the week of seventy and had said eleven times in a corridor badly.

"**We could write a list of everybody who has ever come in here and it would take a person about a day and it would be about four hundred names and about half of them could not tell you what district they sleep in. And the first line on that wall behind you says the room will not keep anything it cannot take back, and a list of four hundred people cannot be taken back by any of them, and you could not take it back either, because there is no version of you that could be made to forget a thing you have written down.**"

"**That is right,"** said the man of thirty-eight, and put his hood up, and left.

Nobody thanked him. He has never asked to be thanked and a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket is not a mechanism.

---

A man came in at about half past nine with his coat streaming and stood just inside the door for a while and would not say what he had come out of, and the cook of twenty-seven gave him a cup of tea and a chair and did not ask him anything else, and he was still there when the shutter came down at about ten and he went home into it.

He was not asked for anything and nothing was written about him and there is no record in this city that he stood in that room.

The tally on the scrap said eight cups on the Friday and seven on the Monday and the cook said out loud that the eight was a number of cups and not a number of people and that she keeps it for the milk and that anybody who wants to make it a headcount is welcome to come and count the room themselves on a Tuesday, and that nobody ever has.

---

The shutter came down at about ten and the room was cold and had been cold for four days and would be cold for ten more, and the man of twenty-two was not there, and the ring binder had seven things in it and the counter had nothing on it, and the board over the door still had four words in a sign-writer's hand and eight in a cook's, and the eight were untrue.

He wrote one thing, on a service road in the wet, at about ten, standing under a downpipe.

*245. Friday of week 83, at about ten on a service road in the wet, under a downpipe, and this entry is one pump, one list nobody asked for, and a tally of eight.*

*The night, itemised.* **Ten on the docket, nine in date, grade three from twenty to eight. A stair landing, two tap washers, a bin-store timer that is not the fault, an emergency pack, a light over a service door, a fault on a cupboard circuit reported by a person who is not the fault, and a sump pump in a basement running nine hours on a night with no rain heavy enough to call for it.** **The pump was attended in about four inches of standing water in the rain at about half past four. The description box records that the fault is not the pump and that the water stands about nine inches deep against a wall with no opening on this side. No source was stated, at the reporter's own request and with the reporter's agreement, and a district man of thirty-eight said it was the first entry in about five months that had recorded what the fault was not and had declined to record what it was. The city-side unnameable rate was ten all night. The standing weekly figure is unaltered, is on the back of a woman's own card, and is on no form.**

*Conditions in the room.* **Four days into an unpaid fortnight; cold throughout; about nine present at half past seven, the largest Friday attendance since the fortnight before the shutter was first closed. Rain since the Monday. A hardboard on two nails with a run of water across its lower third, left unwiped on a cook's stated principle. The board over the door carrying four words in a sign-writer's hand and eight in a cook's, the upper line untrue since seven o'clock on the Monday. Twelve lines on the wall, twenty-nine days old. Seven items in the ring binder. The counter bare since the Friday of week eighty-two. The room itself was a hundred and nine days old, which is the Monday it opened and not the Friday. The card in a rail in Lattice Ward was a hundred and thirteen days in, and it is neither of the two things on this wall.**

*Three statements made to the room, in order, none of them asked for.* **A man of fifty-one with a tool bag, on his coat and from the wet: that he has put about nine hundred door numbers on buildings in this city in about nine years; that a number on a door finds a building and not a person; that in about four years somebody will put a number on this door that finds a person; and that he has wanted it written down for about four years and has never once wanted it written more than he wanted it not to be true, and is asking for it anyway. A cook of twenty-seven said one word and did not say which word. A man of thirty-eight in a district jacket, hood down: that three districts take water off that row, two off a river about nine miles off and one off a hill; that he will not give a date, having been wrong about that twice; that the only list worth anything in a fortnight's time is a list of who is in which building; that he could produce one in about a day; that he is not asking; and that not knowing who is in a room is the only thing he has got. A first-year of nineteen, standing, in about nine seconds: that such a list could be written in about a day and would run to about four hundred names of which about half could not give a district; that the first line on the wall forbids it; and that no version of the man in the doorway could be made to forget four hundred names. He agreed, hood up, and left. Nobody thanked him.**

*The tally and its limits.* **A cook of twenty-seven's milk-order scrap, by her own statement a count of cups and not a headcount: eleven on the Friday of week eighty-two, seven on the Monday of week eighty-three, eight on this Friday. She stated the reading herself, that the movement in the number is a movement in the number of people who are cold rather than in the number of people, and that the same has now been said twice. She invited any person to come and count the room on a Tuesday. Nobody has taken her up and this entry does not treat the figure as an attendance count, and no name of any absent person is held anywhere.**

*One arrival, unrecorded by choice.* **A man came in at about half past nine with his coat streaming, declined to say what he had come out of, was given tea and a chair, said nothing further that is recorded, and left into the rain at about ten. He was asked nothing. Nothing was written. There is no record of him outside this entry and he is not on any list because the room keeps none.**

*What this entry does not contain.* **No date for the water, because no person in this case has one. No source for the water in the basement. No name of the person who set the float switch, and no date for the setting of it, because that person is not a person in this case. No attendance figure, because the room does not count people and the one number available is a milk order. The first year of that unit remains unexplained after a seventh refusal of the question. The licensor is unnamed and the field is blank. Nothing here is signed, and nothing here binds anybody, and no rule of any kind was made in that room on this day. Entry 245.**
