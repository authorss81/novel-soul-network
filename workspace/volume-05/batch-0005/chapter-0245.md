# Chapter 245 — Three Pieces And A Refusal On Purpose

On the Thursday evening of week eighty-four the water came into the low end of three districts in a city, and four people in a cold room off a service road each took one piece of it, and no person in that room took all of it, and it took about eleven minutes to agree that, and the first of the two refusals that were going to happen on purpose happened at about half past eight while about nine people were watching.

---

The rain started again at about four in the afternoon and did the thing it does, which is to arrive at a low end and stay there.

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag came in at about half past six, wet to the thigh, and said that the low end of the row was under about four inches of pavement and rising about an inch an hour, and that about nine hundred buildings in four districts were behind that pavement, and that the pump in the basement had been reported for the fourth time that week and was still not the fault, and then he put his coat back on and went out again and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

The tally on the scrap beside the kettle said six.

---

A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was in that room before anybody else and had a page of his own in the ring binder and has never asked to be taught anything and is on an employer's Friday list that is not his and that he does not own.

He said his piece first, in four sentences, and it was the only time in this volume that anybody laid out a piece of a thing in a room and it was four sentences long.

"**There are about four hundred buildings on that row that people cannot get out of on their own, and about nine hundred that they can, and the ones they cannot get out of are the ones with a stair in them that a person of seventy-four would have to get down four flights with a bag of water round the bottom landing. I can get into the four hundred. I cannot get anybody out of them. That is the piece and I will not do the other half of it.**"

Somebody asked him what the other half was.

"**Sitting on a landing for eleven hours being fine at it, and not letting anybody see me being fine at it, and being the man in the lift when somebody comes up and wants to know whether the water has stopped.**"

Nobody improved on that either.

---

A cook of twenty-seven said her piece and it took about eleven seconds and nobody understood it until a first-year of nineteen said it back.

"**I have got a kettle, a gas ring that came with the unit, about nine hundred cups, a table and a chair, and I am going to make about four hundred cups of tea in about two days and I am going to put them where the wet people are, and if I run out of cups people are going to drink out of their hands, and that is the whole of it.**"

"That is not a piece of a flood," said somebody. "That is tea."

"**That is exactly the point and I have been trying to say it since the week of seventy and it is the reason I will not have the kettle called a technique, and today it is the reason about four hundred people are going to be standing somewhere dry with something hot in them, and if somebody wants to write that down in a book, I would rather they wrote down what a chair is.**"

A first-year of nineteen said it back in nine words and nobody improved on her either: "**That is the piece. That is all of her piece.**"

---

A first-year of nineteen took the door. The plate on the outside of that unit carries a telephone number and no name, and about six calls have gone into that room in about twelve weeks and every one of the six began with a woman in another building saying the word *hold on*, and the first-year said her piece in the plainest sentence anybody had used in that room in a term.

"**I will answer it. I will not ask anybody who they are and I will not write anything down and I will say where the dry ground is, and if it is me on it I will be the one saying it, and if it is somebody else at two in the morning they are going to say it badly, and I am not going to be angry about that on Wednesday.**"

"And you can be relieved of it?"

"**I can be asked to stop. That is not the same as the telephone, and the telephone is not the same as a key, and I have been in this room fourteen weeks and I have watched about nine people confuse all three and I am not going to be the fourth.**"

---

A woman of twenty-four was in that room by then and had not been in it since the Thursday before the fortnight began. She is a nurse and she has a page in a drawer in a building in another district with her own name at the bottom of it and nobody's at the top, and she has a clipboard she was not asked to bring and there is nothing written on it.

She said her piece as about thirty words and did not soften them, and then said the half of it that was about herself, and it is the reason the four of them are four and not one.

"**I can get a person out of a bed and down about nine stairs and into a dry hall. I cannot do a building, I cannot do a street and I cannot do a night. I have a list of people who cannot be got out of their own homes and about nine of them I have spoken to this year and one of them is a man in a first-floor flat in that row who will not open his door to a stranger and I have not solved that and I am not going to solve it tonight with a kettle.**"

"And if they want a person to ring," said a man of twenty-nine, "and not a room."

"**Then they will get whoever is in the room, and I will say that again in about two days, and by then it will be a different person, and that is the entire difficulty and it is not a moral difficulty, it is a rota, and nobody in this city has a rota for a thing nobody has agreed is a thing.**"

---

About nine people had been in that room since half past six and the agreeing took about eleven minutes and it produced four pieces and no owner of any of them, and at about half past seven a man of twenty-two stood with his back to a wall and said the part that is about him, which was that he was not running a piece, and why.

"**Because a man with one key in his pocket who is also the only person anybody can find is one thing, and if I am on a line in three districts then I am a second thing, and I have had one of those for about a term and it cost me everything I had and I am not doing it again on a Tuesday.**"

"Nobody asked you to."

"**I know. I have been waiting about two days for somebody to ask me and I want it on this record that I waited and nobody did, because the day somebody does, the answer is the same and I would like to have said the waiting part before the day.**"

---

At about eight a first-year of nineteen went out to the service road and telephoned a man of thirty-four who works nights, and she came back and said he would be here at about nine, and that she had not asked him to do anything and had told him what the room was doing, and he came at nine and was wet through and had his coat still on.

"**You rang me.**"

"**I rang you because a cook of twenty-seven said she would ring you on a Tuesday at about ten and it is Thursday, and I would like it on this record that I stole it, and I am not sorry.**"

"I said I wanted about four seconds."

"**You wanted about four seconds and nobody was going to give you them for about a year, and there is a flood on and I gave you the whole of a telephone call and I am not going to apologise for it, and I am not going to be one of the things you are angry about afterwards.**"

He said: "**I am not angry. I want to say the other half of that and I have never said it out loud. I wanted somebody to telephone me, and a telephone call is not what I wanted, and I have known that since about the spring and I have not said it because it sounds like a complaint. It is not a complaint. It is that I have been on my own with a thing for nine years and I did not want company, I wanted a witness, and those are different and now I have got neither and I have got a first-year of nineteen on a service road in the rain, which is worse.**"

Nobody improved on it, and the cook of twenty-seven said one word that was not agreement, and the word was *late*.

---

At about half past eight the man of twenty-nine who works on lifts put a folded page on the counter, and it was his employer's, and it was a list of names, and it was on a Friday list, and he put it down face up, in front of about nine people, on purpose.

"**I am going to say no to this out loud so that everybody hears it, because if I say it quietly then in about four years one of these people will find out I was asked and did not say.**"

"Which part."

"**All of it. I will not read a single name off that page in this room, I will not take it off my employer's book, I will not photograph it, and I will not put a single person from it on a list that anybody in this room makes. There are about eleven people a week on that page. They are not on it because they did a thing. They are on it because a system in about nine places chooses job numbers, and my name is on it, and the man with the docket is on it, and the woman who answered a telephone in that other building is not and I checked.**"

"And names?" said somebody. "For a flood, we would want—"

"**We would want names and I am not giving you any and I am not saying no to the flood, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what I have for about nine years and I have never said it in a room and I have said it now.**"

He picked the page up and put it back in his coat.

"**And I want it recorded that I said no on purpose, on a night, in front of witnesses, when saying yes would have been easier and would have worked.**"

---

At about nine the man of twenty-nine was still in the room and the man of thirty-four who works nights was still in the room and the cook of twenty-seven had made about ninety cups of tea and had run out of cups at about a quarter to nine and said so out loud, and the tally on the scrap beside the ring said eleven, and eleven is the same figure as the Friday of week eighty-two and has not been beaten since, and the cook of twenty-seven said that about eleven cups on a night with water at the end of the road is not a good sign and that she would rather it was four, and that she is glad it is eleven, and that she is not going to be one of the two people in this room who say both of those things out loud in the same minute.

The shutter came down at about ten and the room was cold and the water was still rising at the low end and the eight words over the door were untrue and nobody had rubbed them off.

He wrote one thing, on a service road, in water about four inches deep over the pavement, at about ten.

*248. Thursday of week 84, at about ten on a service road in about four inches of rising water, and this entry is four pieces, one owner of none of them, and one refusal made on purpose.*

*Conditions.* **Ten days into an unheated fortnight. The room cold throughout. Water standing over the pavement at the low end of three districts and rising at about an inch an hour, of which no person in this case has a first-hand figure and which is the figure of a man of fifty-one with a tool bag who came in at half past six, was wet to the thigh, said it, went out again, and was not thanked. Eleven on the docket, eleven in date, grade three from twenty to eight. The rate was at nine until about eleven in the evening and at eleven for the final hour. The city's weekly figure is unchanged and is on no form and is written on the back of a woman's own card.**

*Four pieces, agreed in about eleven minutes, none of them owned.* **A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, who is on an employer's Friday list and is not a practitioner: about four hundred buildings on that row that a person cannot get out of on their own, and about nine hundred that they can, and he can get into the four hundred and cannot get anybody out of them, and he declined in the same four sentences to sit on a landing being fine at it where a person might see him being fine at it.** **A cook of twenty-seven: a kettle, a gas ring that came with the unit, about nine hundred cups, a table and a chair, and about four hundred cups of tea in about two days put where the wet people are, and if the cups run out, hands; and the finding, restated by her and then said back in nine words by a first-year of nineteen, that the whole of it is a kettle and a chair, is not a technique, has not been called one since the week of seventy, and is the reason about four hundred people will be standing somewhere dry with something hot in them.** **A first-year of nineteen: the door and the telephone on the plate; she will answer, will not ask who is calling, will not write anything down, will say where the dry ground is; and the three distinctions, that answering a telephone is not being a key-holder, that a key is not a telephone, and that she can be asked to stop.** **A woman of twenty-four, a nurse, with a clipboard nobody asked her to bring and nothing on it: about nine stairs from a bed into a dry hall, and not a building, not a street, not a night; a list of people who cannot be got out of their own homes; about nine spoken to this year; one man in a first-floor flat in that row who will not open his door to a stranger, unsolved and not to be solved that night with a kettle; and the statement that what a flood will actually want is a person and not a room, and that this is a rota and not a moral question, and that no one in this city has a rota for a thing nobody has agreed is a thing.**

*No owner of the line.* **The man of twenty-two declined a piece and gave the reason twice, in public: that a man with one key in his pocket who is also the only person anybody can find is one thing, and a second thing, in three districts, on a line, is the thing that cost him everything he had about a term ago; and, unprompted, that he has been waiting about two days for somebody to ask him to run one and that nobody did and that the answer would have been the same and he would like the waiting on this record before the day. A person of thirty-four who buys training was not present and has asked for nothing since the Monday.**

*The telephone call, and the man who came.* **A first-year of nineteen telephoned a man of thirty-four who works nights at about eight from the service road, having been told by a cook of twenty-seven at half past seven that a standing Tuesday at about ten had been offered to him; she said on return that she had taken the offer and was not sorry. He arrived at nine, wet through, in his coat. He said he wanted about four seconds of telephone and had said so on the Monday. He then said, unprompted and at some length, that a telephone call was not what he wanted, that he had known that since the spring, that what he wanted was a witness and that company and a witness are different things, and that he has had neither and has instead got a first-year of nineteen on a service road in the rain, which he described as worse. A cook of twenty-seven said one word, which was not agreement, and the word was late. Nobody improved on any of it.**

*The refusal, on purpose, in public, witnessed.* **At about half past eight a man of twenty-nine placed his employer's folded Friday list face up on that counter in front of about nine people and declined it aloud: he would read no name off it, take it off his employer's book, photograph it, or enter any person from it on any list that room makes. He gave the grounds, that about eleven people a week appear on that page and do not appear on it for anything they have done, that a system in about nine places chooses job numbers, and that his own name is on it and the man with the docket is on it and the woman who answers a telephone in another building is not on it and he has checked. He stated that he was saying no on purpose, at night, in front of witnesses, when saying yes would have been easier and would have worked, and that in about four years somebody will find out that he was asked and did not say. He replaced the page in his own coat. No list was read. No name left that room in this chapter.**

*Standing state.* **Eleven cups, the same figure as the Friday of week eighty-two and not beaten since, and the cook of twenty-seven's own reading, that eleven on a night with water at the end of the road is not a good sign, that she would rather it was four, that she is glad it is eleven, and that she is not going to be one of the two people in that room saying both halves out loud in the same minute. Cups ran out at about a quarter to nine. Twelve lines on the wall, thirty-five days old. Seven items in the ring binder, the counter bare again by nine. A hundred and fifteen days since the room opened. The card in a rail in Lattice Ward has been in that rail a hundred and nineteen days, and no person in this room has touched it and nobody here knows that to be true. The board over the door carries four words in a sign-writer's hand and eight in a cook's, the upper line untrue for ten days and untouched. No teaching, no demonstration, no assessment, no assistance and no relay. No page, list, minute, notice or report was made in that room today; the only list touched was somebody else's, and it was declined. The licensor is unnamed and its field is blank. The standing question is unaddressed and carries no number. Entry 248.**
