# Chapter 246 — The Evacuation Still Works

On the Friday of week eighty-four the second Friday rota of this movement ran while water stood at the end of a service road in three districts, and about eleven people were taken out of about eleven buildings by a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, and a woman of twenty-four refused in front of about nine people to be the person the flood telephones, and said what the refusal cost, and nobody argued with her and nobody thanked her, and about two hundred cups of tea went into about two hundred hands out of a room with a cold kettle, and nobody died.

---

Eleven on the docket and ten in date and the eleventh was a fire door in a building in Nine Bridges whose closer he adjusted for the fifth time in about a fortnight, and he went at about seven in the morning in about nine inches of water and did not go into the building, and wrote in the description box that the fault is the door and that he could not reach it, and a district man of thirty-eight read that on a screen in a different building and said out loud to nobody that this was the first entry in about five months that had stated a reason it had not been done, and he has said before that it was the first entry that had stated what the fault was not, and neither of those things was said in order to make anybody feel better.

The word was at nine all morning and at ten all afternoon and it did not go to eleven for the first time in about a term and a half.

---

A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in the room at seven in the morning, at nine in the morning, and at about two in the afternoon and was not there for most of the time between, and was not thanked for any of the three occasions, and said one thing on the first of them, which is that a door closer is a service item inside a building and a person is not, and that about nine hundred buildings on that row are going to need a person tonight, and that he is one of them and has a coat.

He was gone by half past seven and he did not come back until the water had stopped.

---

A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip came in at about nine in the morning with a plastic bag, and he had come four floors down in the dark, and he had the shoes in the bag, resoled eleven times on the left and not on the right, cut by hand.

He put the bag on the counter and said he wanted to give them away.

"**To somebody with wet feet who is standing in this room and has not got any,**" he said. "**That is what I have come with. That is all I have come with and I have been thinking about it since Friday.**"

A cook of twenty-seven looked at the bag and did not touch it and said the sentence she said about a pair of shoes about six weeks ago and that she has not improved on since.

"**We cannot give them to anybody. We can only hand you the bag.**"

"I know that. I heard it. I have been in a room with about nine people in it for a fortnight listening to that sentence and I have come anyway because I did not want to arrive and not have tried."

"**Then you take the bag and you walk it to whoever you have got in mind, and the room has nothing to do with it, and that is not us being careful, that is us being the only thing in this building.**"

He took the bag. He did not put it on the counter and he did not leave it there and nobody offered to carry it for him and he was not asked to.

He came back at about six in the evening and said the man who got them had a size of his own and no shoes, and that he was not going to say where he had taken them, and that this room does not need to know, and that a first-year of nineteen said so first.

---

The second refusal of this movement happened at about eleven in the morning, in public, in front of about nine people, and it was made by a woman of twenty-four who had taken a piece of the four the night before and was on her feet for the second day.

She did not refuse to do her piece. She refused a different thing, and she refused it before anybody asked for it, and she said the first part fast.

"**I am not going to be the person this telephone reaches. I want to say that before anybody works out that I am the obvious one, because I have been the person in a room that things happen to for about six years and I have got very good at it and I am putting it down.**"

"You have not been asked."

"**I am putting it down first. If anybody asks me after nine o'clock tonight, which they will, because that is what I am for, then the answer is the same and it will be quicker, and the reason is that I have one of the few lists in this city and about nine people on it are people who cannot be got out of their own homes, and one of them is a man in a first-floor flat who will not open his door to a stranger, and if I am on this line then in about four years somebody in an office will use me as a reason and he will not open the door either.**"

---

She then named the cost of the refusal herself, which nobody asked her to do and which she did in about nine seconds and which is the only part of it that anybody wrote down.

"**The price is about four minutes. It takes about four minutes to say what I have just said and for about four minutes nobody could have rung me about a man and a first-floor flat. I am going to carry those four minutes and I am not going to give them to anybody to hold, and I am not going to check them, and I am not going to be the sort of person who is thanked for a thing that cost her nothing.**"

A cook of twenty-seven said: "**You have just cost that room its evening.**"

"**Then I will be the one who says it cost somebody something and everybody else in here can be quiet, and that is a job somebody has to do and it will not be me on Thursday.**"

A man of about thirty-one in the back said that she was wrong about one half of it, and that the four minutes was not a price but a fact, and that a price is something somebody else pays, and that she had paid it and was calling it a price so that she would be able to tell whether she had been right afterwards.

Nobody improved on either. She sat down and then stood up again about a minute later because somebody had come in with a wet child.

---

By four in the afternoon the water at the low end had stopped coming in from the hill and started going back down, and a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts had been into about eleven of the four hundred buildings, and a woman of twenty-four had got about nine people out of about nine rooms, and about two hundred cups of tea had gone out of a room with no money in it, and a man of twenty-one had been sent out four times with a box of cups and had come back four times and had not been asked to sign anything.

A man of thirty-four who works nights came in at about two in the afternoon, on his way to work, and said that somebody at his building had given him a job for the night, and that the job was to be in a building with about nine hundred doors and let people in, and that he had said yes, and that he had said yes to a person he did not know, and that he had done it before and had not been asked to before.

"**They gave me a bit of paper with about nine addresses on it and I said yes, and I have never in nine years said yes to a bit of paper, and I am telling about nine people in a cold room and not telling my own employer, and I do not know why I am telling this room.**"

"**Because this room has not asked you for anything in nine visits**," said somebody.

"**Yes. I had worked that out about half a mile back.**"

---

Nobody died. That is said once and it is true and there is a woman of about thirty-four with a district jacket and a clipboard who was on the road at half past four and who said afterwards that in about nine years of doing that job she has never once been able to say that sentence and mean it, and that on this occasion she was able to say it and could not enjoy it, and that she has thought about the two things not fitting together every day since.

The evacuation that four people each ran one piece of worked, and what it consisted of was: about eleven people out of about eleven buildings where nobody could have got out on their own, about nine people out of about nine rooms, about two hundred cups of tea, one man on an employer's Friday list who would not read a single name off it, one woman who would not be on a telephone, one night man who said yes to a bit of paper, one man of fifty-one with a tool bag and a coat, one lift engineer with about nine years of a thing nobody would write down, and one man of twenty-two who was asked twice for a decision and answered both times with the same sentence.

"**Ask whoever is running that piece,**" he said, at about half past two, and at about five.

Both times it was obeyed, and the first time was a woman of twenty-eight with two children, and the second time was a man of about sixty with a dog.

He has said that sentence twice in one day and has not been asked about either.

---

At about eleven at night the water had gone back down about a foot and a half and the shutter came down at about ten and the room was cold and the tally on the scrap beside the ring said four hundred and eleven, and the cook of twenty-seven would not have it.

"**Four hundred and eleven is the number of cups I have made since Wednesday and I am not going to have that be the number of people, and I am also not going to be coy about it, because four hundred and eleven cups is the best thing this room has ever done and about nine people in here know that and I have said it out loud once and I am not going to say it again.**"

She wrote the rest of it on the scrap, which is her milk order, and which is in no file, and which nobody has asked to see.

He wrote one thing, on a service road, in about six inches of falling water, at about ten, standing in a doorway.

*249. Friday of week 84, at about ten on a service road in six inches of falling water, at a doorway, and this entry is one day, one public refusal, and four hundred and eleven cups.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Second Friday rota of this movement, grade three from twenty to eight. Eleven on the docket and ten in date. The eleventh is a stair fire door in a building in Nine Bridges whose closer he has reset five times in about a fortnight; he attended at about seven in the morning in about nine inches of water, did not enter, and recorded that the fault is the door and that he could not reach it. A district man of thirty-eight read that entry on a screen in a different building and observed, to nobody, that it is the first in about five months to give a reason for something not done.** **The rate held at nine all morning and ten all afternoon and did not reach eleven for the first time in about a term and a half. The city's weekly figure is unaltered, is not a rate, is on the back of a woman's own card and on no form. The room's heating has now been off for eleven days and was not restored, and no person in this case paid for it.**

*What was carried out and by whom.* **About eleven persons taken out of about eleven buildings in which they could not have got out on their own, by a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, on his own piece and without a list, having declined the previous evening on the record to read any name from his employer's Friday list. About nine persons brought down about nine stairs and into a dry hall by a woman of twenty-four, on her own piece, within the limits she stated in advance and did not exceed. About two hundred cups of tea carried out on foot by a man of twenty-one in four trips with a box, at the instruction of a cook of twenty-seven, who was not thanked. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag in attendance at about seven, at about nine and at about two, absent between, coat on, not thanked on any of the three, who said that a door closer is a service item inside a building and a person is not. A man of thirty-four who works nights in attendance at about two on his way to work, who accepted a night of door-opening work from a person he does not know, given on a sheet of about nine addresses, which is the first such acceptance in about nine years and which he reported to that room rather than to his employer without being able to say why. One man of twenty-two, who ran no piece, was asked twice for a decision, at about half past two and at about five, and gave the same answer both times: ask whoever is running that piece. Both were obeyed; the first by a woman of twenty-eight with two children and the second by a man of about sixty with a dog. He has not been asked about either.**

*The second public refusal.* **At about eleven in the morning, unasked, in front of about nine people, by a woman of twenty-four who had taken a piece the previous evening: that she will not be the person the telephone on that plate reaches, given before anybody had worked out that she was the obvious candidate, on the stated ground that she has been the person in a room that things happen to for about six years, that she holds one of the few lists in this city, that about nine people on it cannot be got out of their own homes, and that one of them is a man in a first-floor flat who will not open his door to a stranger; and that if she is on that line then in about four years somebody in an office will use her as a reason and he will not open the door either.** **She then named her own cost, unasked: about four minutes during which nobody could have telephoned her about a man and a first-floor flat; and that she will carry them, will not hand them to anybody to hold, and will not check them. A cook of twenty-seven told her she had just cost that room its evening. She answered that the four minutes is a fact and not a price, a price being something somebody else pays, and that she had paid it and was calling it a price so that she would be able to tell afterwards whether she had been right. A man of about thirty-one in the back disagreed and was not improved upon.**

*The bag on the counter.* **A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip came at about nine having come down four floors in the dark, with a plastic bag containing a pair of shoes resoled eleven times on the left and not on the right and cut by hand, and asked that they be given to somebody in the room with wet feet and none. A cook of twenty-seven did not touch the bag and stated that the room can only return it, not transfer it, which is her own rule about objects with no words on them, unaltered and unimproved. He took the bag, declined to leave it, was offered no help and asked for none, and returned at about six in the evening to say that a man whose size it was and who had no shoes now has them, that he is not going to say where, and that a first-year of nineteen said the same thing first. The bag did not stay on that counter and no object was left in that room in the course of this day.**

*The figure that is not a figure of people.* **Four hundred and eleven cups since Wednesday, recorded on a scrap kept for a milk order, in no file, not requested by anybody. Its author refused the reading of it as a headcount and refused, in the same breath, to be coy about it, on the ground that it is the best thing that room has ever done; she said that once and stated she would not say it again. She has now said the same shape of thing three times in this volume, on the Monday of week eighty-three, on the Friday of week eighty-three and here, and each time it has cost her something and none of the three was recorded by anybody else.**

*Outcome.* **The evacuation worked. It was run by four people on four pieces with no owner of any of them, and it consisted of about eleven people, about nine people, about two hundred cups and a number of small correct acts. Nobody died. A woman of about thirty-four with a district jacket and a clipboard was on the road at about half past four, said afterwards that in about nine years of that job she has never been able to say that sentence and mean it, that on this occasion she could say it and could not enjoy it, and that she has thought about the two not fitting together every day since. This entry does not resolve the two.**

*Boundaries, and the state left behind.* **The heating is off and was not restored. Twelve lines on the wall, thirty-six days old. Seven items in the ring binder. The counter bare. The board over the door carries four words in a sign-writer's hand and eight in a cook's, the upper line untrue for eleven days and untouched by anybody including its author. No list, page, form, minute, notice, report or register was made in that room on this day, and the one list available was somebody else's and was declined the previous evening. No teaching, demonstration, assessment or assistance by anybody; the department's condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased. The relay does not appear and its word is not used. The standing question is not touched and no number is attached to it. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, and no message has come from anybody. Entry 249.**
