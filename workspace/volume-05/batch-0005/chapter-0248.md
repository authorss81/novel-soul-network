# Chapter 248 — Thirteen Lines

On the Thursday afternoon of week eighty-six, twenty-four days into an unheated fortnight that nobody had paid for, nine people sat in a cold room off a service road in the Lower Wards with a list in front of them and took no minutes, and a first-year of nineteen wrote a thirteenth line on a piece of hardboard on two nails, and a cook of twenty-seven rubbed off eight words over the door with her own thumb, and a woman of about thirty who has never been asked anything in fifteen days said one sentence and it was about herself.

---

The room had been asked to be on a board in a corridor in three buildings since the flood, twice by a company and once by a district, and it had refused all three in about nine seconds each, and a person of thirty-four who buys training said in the middle of the second refusal that the refusals were the reason the room could now be found by people who can act, and she was right, and that was on the record from the Monday of week eighty-two and has not been improved on.

Two other rooms had asked to do what this room does, in two other districts, one of them on a Tuesday and one of them at the back of a hall with a hatch in it, and nobody had told this room about either until the Tuesday of week eighty-six, when a woman of about thirty-four with a district jacket said it in about nine words and then said the rest of it, which is the thing that made the room stop.

"**They are not asking you to teach them. They cannot be taught by you or by anybody you have ever met. They are asking whether they may do the thing you do, and the answer is that they may, and the only thing you have is the answer and there is nothing else in this room to give them.**"

---

So nine people came in on the Thursday afternoon and there was a list, and it was a list of the things that room would not do, written out in the order they had happened, and the first item on it was the first line on the wall, and the twelfth was the twelfth, and there was no thirteenth on the list because the thirteenth did not exist yet when the list was made.

A man of about fifty-two in a plain coat said at about half past two that if nine people were going to sit in a room with a list then somebody ought to write down what was decided, and that a decision nobody can read back is not a decision, it is a mood, and that he was not being difficult.

A first-year of nineteen said no in about nine seconds and gave the ground.

"**Nothing is decided in this room. You are going to be told nine things and you are going to be asked whether anybody objects and about nine of you are going to object to something and then at four o'clock everybody is going to stand up and go, and there will be no record of it, and I am telling you now so that you are not surprised at four o'clock and so that nobody later can say they were not told.**"

"And if we are wrong?"

"**Then in about four years somebody will be able to say there was a list and nine people and no record, and that is a thing they can check, and it will be the same list, and it will not have your coat on it.**"

He said: "**I have been in about nine meetings of this shape and every one of them had minutes.**"

"**Then you know what a minute is. It is a page with four names on it and a decision on it that four people made about nine people who were in the room, and the four names are the ones who can be found.**"

The man of about fifty-two said, "**That is fair**," and did not ask again, and he is not thanked, and he is not a mechanism, and a person with an ordinary coat is not a post.

---

A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts spoke for about four minutes and said what he had been able to carry out of that room and what he could not, and the thing he could not was about eleven buildings, and he named the reason out loud, which is that in about four years somebody will use a list, and a list of buildings is a list of people, and he has a page of his own in a ring binder and has decided that is the most he is going to be on.

A cook of twenty-seven spoke for about nine seconds and said that the piece she had is a kettle and a chair and a gas ring that came with the unit and about nine hundred cups, and that the other room with the hatch has asked her for the numbers and that she has given them away and that the numbers are not hers.

"**They have got the numbers. They do not have the four hundred and eleven and I did not give them the four hundred and eleven and if anybody rings me and asks for the four hundred and eleven I am going to tell them it was a woman of about thirty-four with a district jacket and she was in a car and she had not slept.**"

"And if they need it?"

"**Then they will count their own and they will count it wrongly and it will be the right order of wrong, and that is the correct outcome and I would rather it than be handed to them by me.**"

---

At about three o'clock somebody asked what a person was supposed to do in a room like this if they came in with nothing, and about nine people looked at each other, and a first-year of nineteen got up and went to the hardboard on its two nails and counted the lines on it, which is twelve, and then said that she was going to write a thirteenth and that she was going to say the cost of it out loud first because that is what happens here.

"**It is this. You may say you were never here, and the room will not argue.**"

Somebody counted them and said thirteen.

"**It costs this,**" she said. "**It is the first thing I have written down in this room that is about a person being in the room. It is on a wall that anyone may photograph, and I have been on about eleven hundred leaflets and I know exactly what a sentence on a surface does to a person over about four years, and I am writing it without asking the person it is mostly about, and I have done that before, in a market, with a stall-holder's licence, and it is the same thing and I am not going to pretend it is a smaller one.**"

She wrote it on the hardboard in about nine seconds and it is thirteen words and it is in her hand and it is the thirteenth line.

---

A woman of about thirty was in the chair nearest the door with a bag on her lap, as she has been at half past seven on every working day since the Friday of week sixty-nine, and she has not been asked anything by anybody since the Monday of week seventy, and on the Thursday afternoon of week eighty-six she said one sentence, out loud, standing up, to about nine people, and it was this.

"**You did not ask me.**"

And a first-year of nineteen said: "**No.**"

"**That is the right answer and I am going to say it anyway, because if nobody in this room says it then in about four years there will be a line on that wall about a person who sits in that chair, and it will be a good line, and I will not have been asked, and I will not be able to tell anybody that I was there.**"

Nobody apologised to her, and that was correct, and an apology would have been the second time she had been asked something, and a cook of twenty-seven said so out loud without turning round from a sink and did not improve it.

Then the first-year of nineteen asked her, once, whether she wanted the line kept, and the woman of about thirty gave one answer and gave it once.

"**You are not allowed to keep asking. That is not me saying no. That is the line.**"

Nobody asked her a second time, and nobody has asked her anything since, and the thirteenth line is on the wall, and it is not about her, and she was not asked.

---

At about half past three the cook of twenty-seven came back from the sink and looked at the board over the door for a while and then brought a thumb up to it.

She did it in about four seconds, with her own thumb, in front of about nine people, and she took out the eight words she had written herself on the Thursday of week eighty-two, which is *it is warmer in here than out there*, and which had been untrue for twenty-four days, and she left the four words underneath in a sign-writer's hand and did not replace anything.

"**I wrote those. I knew they were wrong the morning after I wrote them and I said so in a room and nobody made me take them down. A line somebody wrote by hand costs the writer something and it can be taken off by the writer and there is nobody else in this city entitled to take it off, so it is mine to take off, and I am taking it off, and I would like somebody to say that it took about four seconds and not about a term.**"

Somebody said, "What do we do about the sign-writer's four."

"**Nothing. He was sixty and he was paid four pounds and he is not a character in this room and he has not been thanked and he is not going to be. They are true.**"

Nobody improved on either half. About nine people watched a woman rub off her own sentence and nobody said anything at all, and that is the whole of the event, and it is in this chapter because it happened in that room and not because it was about anything.

---

There was a public lesson in the same week and he was not in it.

The man of twenty-nine who works on lifts and a cook of twenty-seven gave one in that room on the Wednesday afternoon, about forty minutes, to about nine people, and about four hundred people in a hundred and forty years have been given a demonstration by somebody and had it go wrong, and nothing about it was written down anywhere, and the man of twenty-two was on a job in another district for the whole of it and telephoned at about half past two to ask whether it was happening.

Somebody told him it was. He asked whether it had worked and he was told that it had not finished yet, and he said all right, and he did not telephone again.

At about nine that night a first-year of nineteen told him in the doorway of that room, in about nine seconds, four things, in this order: that the lesson had been given; that it had been given by a man of twenty-nine and a cook of twenty-seven and not by her; that she had not been asked to be in it and had not wanted to be; and that there were two people who had asked her, separately, on the way out, how it had gone, and that she had told both of them the same thing, which was that it had gone the way things go.

"You did not ring back."

"**There was nothing to ask. I asked whether it was on and you said it was. I was not going to ring a second time to be told the same sentence by a person who is nearly nineteen.**"

"**You are not allowed to keep asking me that either,**" she said. "**That is not me saying no. That is the line, and it is a few hours old.**"

---

Nobody in that room knows what the man of twenty-two would have done with forty minutes and about nine people, and about four of them have been asked and have not said, and the question was not asked of him because nobody in that room would have answered it.

He wrote one thing, in the doorway of a building whose water had gone, at about ten, standing in the cold off the wall about four feet away that that room's window looks at.

*251. Thursday of week 86, at about ten on a service road in the dark, and this entry is one list, no minutes, a thirteenth line, and a thumb.*

*Conditions.* **Twenty-four days into an unheated fortnight. Eleven on the docket and ten in date, grade three from twenty to eight, and a landing light in a building whose water has gone. The rate was nine all morning and ten all afternoon. The city's weekly figure is unchanged, is on the back of a woman's own card, and is on no form. About four hundred and eleven people received a drink out of that room in two days and the room has no record of a single one of them and could not produce one.**

*The council that is not a committee.* **Nine people in that room with a list, on a Thursday afternoon, which was the first time nine people and a list have been in that room for any purpose other than the room's own work. Two other rooms had asked to do what this room does, one in a hall with a hatch in it and one on a Tuesday, and the request came to that room by a woman of about thirty-four with a district jacket, who stated that neither of them can be taught by anybody in this case and that the only thing on offer is the answer. A man of about fifty-two in a plain coat proposed minutes, on the ground that a decision nobody can read back is a mood, and was refused in about nine seconds on the stated grounds that nothing is decided in that room, that nine things are said and about nine objections are taken and at four o'clock everybody stands up, and that no record will exist of any of it; and was told in advance, deliberately, so that the absence of a record could not afterwards be attributed to surprise. He accepted it, was not thanked, and did not raise it again. No minute was taken. No committee exists. No office, title, post or register was created and none was proposed by anybody.**

*What two of the nine said.* **A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, four minutes: what he had been able to carry out and what he could not, the latter being about eleven buildings, the reason being that in about four years somebody will use a list, and that a list of buildings is a list of people, and that he has decided the page in a ring binder is the most he is prepared to be on.** **A cook of twenty-seven, about nine seconds: that her piece is a kettle, a chair, a gas ring that came with the unit and about nine hundred cups; that the room with the hatch has asked her for the numbers; that she has given the numbers; that the four hundred and eleven are not hers and were not given; that if anybody rings her for that figure she will say it belonged to a woman of about thirty-four with a district jacket who was in a car and had not slept; and that if the other room needs a figure it will count its own, wrongly, in the correct order of wrong, which she prefers to handing over.**

*The thirteenth line.* **Written by a first-year of nineteen at about three in the afternoon on a hardboard on two nails, in about nine seconds, in her own hand, after announcing the cost and before making the mark: that a person may say they were never here and the room will not argue. Counted, thirteen words, and it is the thirteenth line on that wall.** **Her stated cost, unasked: that it is the first thing she has written in that room that concerns a person merely being in it; that it is on a surface anybody may photograph; that she has been on about eleven hundred leaflets and knows what a sentence on a surface does to a person in about four years; and that she wrote it without asking the person it is mostly about, having done the same thing once before at a market on a stall-holder's licence, and that the two are the same size.** **The wall's first line is unaffected and remains the first line, and the twelve lines above the new one are forty-nine days old and were not touched.**

*The one sentence spoken by a woman of about thirty.* **Spoken standing, to about nine people: that nobody had asked her. The first-year of nineteen answered no, and the woman said that this was the correct answer and that she was going to say it anyway, on the ground that in about four years there will be a line on that wall about a person who sits in that chair, that it will be a good line, and that she will not have been asked and will not be able to tell anybody she was there. Nobody apologised to her, which was correct, and a cook of twenty-seven stated out loud from a sink that an apology would have been a second question. The first-year of nineteen then asked her once whether she wanted the line kept. The answer given, once: that they are not allowed to keep asking, that this is not a refusal, and that this is the line. She was not asked again and nothing has been asked of her since. She is on no object, in no file, and is described in this entry by a count and by nothing else, and the line on the wall is not about her.**

*The thumb.* **At about half past three a cook of twenty-seven removed her own eight words from the board over the door in about four seconds, in front of about nine people, using her own thumb, leaving the four words in a sign-writer's hand beneath it and replacing nothing. Her grounds, given: that she wrote them, that she knew they were untrue the morning after she wrote them and said so in that room and was not made to take them down, and that a hand-written line is removable by its writer and by nobody else. She asked that it be recorded as taking about four seconds and not about a term. Asked what was to be done about the sign-writer's four words, she said nothing, on the ground that the man was about sixty, was paid four pounds, has not been thanked, is not a character in that room, is not going to be, and that the four words are true. Nobody improved on either. Nothing was said after it and this entry records only that it happened in that room and does not assert what it was about.**

*The public lesson he was not in.* **Given on the Wednesday afternoon by a man of twenty-nine and a cook of twenty-seven, about forty minutes, to about nine people, on the same terms as the lesson of about twelve weeks earlier: nothing shown worked, four of the nine had been asked to do it badly on purpose, and the thing that worked was a kettle and a chair. Nothing was written down. The man of twenty-two was on a job in another district throughout, telephoned once at about half past two to ask whether it was taking place, was told that it was, asked whether it had worked, was told it had not finished, and did not telephone a second time. At about nine that evening he was told in the doorway, in about nine seconds, four things in order: that it had been given; by a man of twenty-nine and a cook of twenty-seven and not by the speaker; that the speaker had not been asked to be in it and had not wanted to be; and that two people had asked her separately how it had gone and she had told both of them the same sentence. He said there had been nothing to ask, and was told in return that he is not permitted to keep asking, and was told by which of the two statements that restriction is. Nobody knows what he would have done with forty minutes and about nine people and about four of the nine have been asked and have not said.**

*Boundaries.* **Twelve lines were forty-nine days old at the close of this afternoon and a thirteenth is new. Seven items in the ring binder, unchanged this month. The counter bare. The board over the door carries four words in a sign-writer's hand and nothing else. No minute, notice, report, register, form, leaflet or constitution exists or is proposed. No teaching, demonstration, assessment, assistance or relay by anybody; the condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased. The heating is off and unpaid on the twenty-fourth day and is not resolved. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, no message has arrived, the master is in a tool roll with its fifth line blank, and the standing question is unaddressed and unnumbered. Entry 251.**
