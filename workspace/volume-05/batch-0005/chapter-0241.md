# Chapter 241 — The Heat Is Off

The heating in a converted shop unit off a service road in the Lower Wards was off at seven in the morning on the Monday of week eighty-three and nobody had paid for it and nobody was going to, and the eight words a cook of twenty-seven had written over that door on the Thursday before were untrue from the first hour of the day, which is what a first-year of nineteen had said out loud on the Friday and would not be reminded of.

The room was open all day, and it was cold from seven in the morning, and the ones who came in out of the weather left again inside about a quarter of an hour, and nobody asked them to stay.

---

Grade three from twenty to eight, nine on the docket and nine in date, and the docket had been in date every Friday for about a term and a half and he had stopped noticing.

A landing light in Bower Street, a tap, a bin-store timer that is not the fault, two lift calls, an emergency pack that did not need replacing and was replaced, a cupboard light off a service road, and a stair fire door in Nine Bridges whose closer he adjusted for the fourth time in about a fortnight, and which came up with a stamped number in the description box again, and which he did not go and look at this time because he had looked at it three times and the frame is as he wrote it.

The word was at nine all morning and ten from about two in the afternoon and it stayed at ten.

---

The kettle went on at half past eight at the point in the day it goes, and it was a kettle and not a radiator, and the cook of twenty-seven said so before anybody asked her anything.

"**I am going to say this once and then I am going to wash up, and I am not going to say it again this week, and if somebody asks me on Thursday I am going to tell them what I said on Monday.**"

Nobody asked her on Thursday.

She had a scrap of paper by the kettle with a tally on it that she keeps for the milk order, and on the Friday of the previous week the last number on it had been eleven, and on Monday it was seven, and she said the two things about the seven that she was going to say.

"**Eleven cups on a Friday and seven on a Monday is not a fall in the number of people, it is a fall in the number of people who are cold, and I want somebody to write that down who is not me, and nobody is going to, and I have now said the same thing twice.**"

---

A woman of about thirty was in the chair near the door at half past seven with a bag on her lap, where she has been at half past seven on every working day since the Friday of week sixty-nine, and the room was cold, and nobody said anything to her about it, and nobody has said anything to her since the Monday of week seventy, and that is not an oversight anybody in that room is going to correct this morning.

There were four people who came into that room most weeks because it was warm.

Three of them did not come on the Monday, and none of them has come since, and nobody in that room has said that out loud, and there is no way of asking.

The cook of twenty-seven knows the shape of it because the milk order has a number on it, and she did not say any names, and there are no names.

---

A man of thirty-four who works nights came in at half past seven on his way to work and had been in that room nine times now and had never brought anything and had never been asked for anything.

He had asked for one thing on the previous Friday and had not been given it, which was that somebody should telephone him, and this morning he asked for a different thing, which was smaller, and he said so himself before anybody could tell him it was smaller.

"**I am not asking for the thing I asked for. I am asking for about four seconds.**"

"Go on."

"**Somebody rings me on the morning it goes off. Not to ask me anything. Just to tell me. I would then not have to come in here at half past seven on my way to work to find out about it, and I would not have had to be the person who walked in.**"

He said that last part as though it were the whole of it, and it was.

A cook of twenty-seven, at a sink, with her hands in the water, said: "**You want a Tuesday. I am in here on a Tuesday about ten and I will do it and I am not going to be thanked and you are not going to be able to stop me doing it.**"

"**I did not say I could stop you.**"

"**No. I have known that about you for about three weeks.**"

---

A man of about sixty, who had not been in the room, stood in the doorway at about ten past eight with a leaflet in his hand, one of about eleven hundred, and said he had read about the room in a market and that he had come because it was warm, and it was not, and he stood in the doorway for a while and then came in and sat down and stayed about an hour and did not say anything else that anybody wrote down.

He read the board over the door on the way past it, both halves of it, and the sign-writer's four words and the cook's eight, and he did not comment on either and nobody asked him to.

---

The man of twenty-two came in at about half past nine with no docket, which was the third Friday running and which nobody remarked on and about four people noticed.

He stood with his back against the wall by the counter and looked at the eight words over the door for a while, and then at the hardboard with its twelve lines on two nails, and then he said the thing that had been true since the Friday before and that he had not said on the Friday because it was his own sentence and he had not wanted to hear it in his own voice in a room.

"**That is a line somebody wrote by hand, and it cost her something she has not said out loud, and it is not true, and a removable line is removed by its own writer, and I am not going to be the person who rubs it off for her, and I would like it recorded that I said I would not, and there is nowhere to record it.**"

Asha Reed, who had been sitting on the end chair with her boots off, said: "**Then don't stand near the board. You are the only person in this room anybody would look at twice.**"

He moved about four feet.

---

Nobody wrote anything down on Monday. The board over the door had twelve words on the wall behind it and eight on the board and none of them was altered, and the ring binder had seven things in it, and the counter had been empty since the Friday of week eighty-two, and the shutter came down at about ten and the room was cold and it was going to be cold on the Tuesday and the Wednesday and for a fortnight after that.

He wrote one thing, on a service road in the dark, at about ten, at a shutter that was down and cold on the outside of it.

*244. Monday of week 83, at about ten on a service road in the dark, at a shutter that is down and cold on its outer face, and this entry is a fortnight, a milk order, and a kettle.*

*The night, itemised.* **Nine on the docket and nine in date, grade three from twenty to eight. A landing light, a tap, a bin-store timer that is not the fault, two lift calls, an emergency pack replaced although it did not need replacing, a cupboard light off a service road, and a stair fire door whose closer he has now reset four times in about a fortnight, which arrived carrying the same stamped identifier for the fourth time and which he did not attend to this evening, having attended three times and confirmed on each occasion that the frame is as he has described it in the description box.** The unnameable rate was nine all morning, ten from about two, ten all night. The city's standing weekly figure is unaltered and stands on the back of a woman's own card and on no form, and about four of those people used to come to that room on a Friday for the temperature, of whom three did not come on this Monday and none has come since, and nobody in that room has said so.

*Standing in the room.* **Open. Cold from seven. Half past seven: the kettle not yet on, about five people, and a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door with a bag on her lap, present on every working day since the Friday of week sixty-nine and asked nothing by anybody. The board carried four words in a sign-writer's hand and, above them, eight in another, the upper line untrue from seven o'clock that morning. The hardboard carried twelve lines, twenty-five days without addition, on two nails. The ring binder carried seven items. The counter had been bare since the Friday before. The room had been open a hundred and five days, which is counted from the Monday and not from anything done inside it. In Lattice Ward a card had been standing in a rail a hundred and nine days, and that is a third object and is not in this room. The kitchen-paper leaf stood in a drawer in a kitchen that opens in the afternoon, eleven lines and eight names, one line with a thumb's worth of daylight through it.**

*The figure, and who supplied it.* **A cook of twenty-seven set her kettle going at the fixed point in the day and, without stopping work, announced the distinction once: a kettle is a kettle; the room is not warm; the room is open; and she was not going to stand in a cold room and pretend otherwise. She then gave two numbers off a scrap she keeps for the milk order — eleven cups on the Friday, seven on the Monday — and stated the reading herself, that this is not a fall in the number of people but a fall in the number of people who are cold, and that she had said the same thing twice and would say it a third time only if somebody wrote it down who was not her, which nobody did.**

*The count that is not a count of people.* **Four persons attended most weeks for the temperature. Three did not attend on this Monday and none has attended since, and no person in that room has stated that aloud, and there is no instrument there by which it could be established. The only figure available is a figure about a milk order: eleven cups on the Friday before, seven on this morning, which is a lower bound and not a measurement, since it does not distinguish a person who stayed away from a person who was elsewhere. Its author has said so. No name was spoken and none is recorded here, because the room holds none and its first line on the wall would not let it.**

*Two requests made in the room.* **First, from a man of thirty-four who works nights, on his ninth attendance and his fifth unasked-for offering of nothing: not the request he made on the previous Friday, but a smaller one, namely about four seconds in which somebody telephones him on the morning the heating goes off, not to ask him anything, so that he need not be the person who walks in and finds out. He was not asked for anything and has never asked to be taught anything. A cook of twenty-seven, at a sink, with her hands in the water, offered a standing Tuesday at about ten, refused thanks in advance, and said she had known for about three weeks that he could not stop her. Second, from the man of twenty-two, who came at about half past nine with no docket, on the third such Friday: he would not rub off the upper line on the board, would not stand near it, and wanted it recorded that he had declined, and there is nowhere to record it. He moved about four feet when asked. He was not thanked for declining and nobody had suggested he would be.**

*The doorway.* **A man of about sixty, not previously in attendance, carrying one of about eleven hundred leaflets, stated that he had come for the temperature, observed that there was none, stood in the doorway, then entered and sat for about an hour. He read both lines on the board and offered no comment on either and was not asked for one. Nobody asked him who he was, nothing was written, and no record of his visit exists outside this entry.**

*Not established this evening.* **The reason that unit's first year is free remains in no file and is now recorded as unresolvable seven times. A line on a company's file continues to record a refusal without a reason. Nothing was decided, minuted, posted, published or registered, and the room's two places where a thing is written down remain a wall of twelve lines and a leaf of eleven. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, and the fifth line of the master is still blank in a tool roll in another building. The standing question is untouched and carries no number. Entry 244.**
