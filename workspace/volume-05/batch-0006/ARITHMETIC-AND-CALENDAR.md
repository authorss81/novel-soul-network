# Arithmetic and calendar — Volume 05

**This file exists because four state files and five batch summaries in Volume 05 said the volume's calendar lived in a document that did not exist, and this is the seventh time a state file in this repository has pointed at a missing one. It is a table and a set of series. It is not a prompt, it plans nothing, and it derives nothing.**

**Four rules, and they are the same four as Volume 04's file.**

1. **No day number may be printed in a chapter.** No month-name, no month-date, no day-date, no year. The only calendar nouns permitted are the season words, the week and the day inside the week, and the two inherited years from `chapter-0053.md` and `chapter-0056.md`, which stand and are not extended. **All fifty chapters of Volume 05 are clean and this file writes no chapters.**
2. **A day is assigned only by the live batch prompt's day map, which is fixed and used and is not to be derived.** Where this file and a batch prompt disagree on a day, the batch prompt is right for its ten days and this file is right for the run.
3. **Weeks run Monday to Sunday. Week 68 = Mon 362 – Sun 368. Week 83 = 467–473. Week 88 = Mon 502 – Sun 508.** Volume 04 ended on the Thursday night of week sixty-seven; Volume 05 opens on the Monday of week sixty-eight and closes on the Wednesday of week eighty-eight. The last dated scene in Volume 05 is the Wednesday evening of week eighty-eight in a cold room off a service road and, at about half past five the same day, a corridor.
4. **The interval map's two-day offset from the Monday of week thirty-one is inherited, is recorded for the twelfth time, and is not to be re-derived from the anchor. The Thursday of week 36 is day 141 and not 139, and that correction stands. No chapter in this manuscript has ever printed a day number, so no prose depends on any of it.**

## 2. The three interval series a writer keeps getting wrong

**The room's age, the age of the wall, and the age of the heating are the three figures that ten repair passes across this volume found wrong most often, and every wrong one of them was a figure carried forward from the chapter beside it rather than recomputed. They are recomputed from the day map above and not from the chapter before. The volume's own convention is the difference between two named days and not an inclusive count.**

**And the two endpoints the Movement V batch prompt printed — 151 for the room and 155 for the card, both at Chapter 250 — were nine days high, because the span from the Monday of week eighty-three to the Wednesday of week eighty-eight is thirty-seven days and the prompt's endpoint assumed forty-six. The prompt was corrected in place. Carry the series below and not the prompt's.**

**The book opened in two of Volume 05's six sittings: Chapter 217, for about eleven seconds, which took it to forty-five, and Chapter 250, for about eleven seconds, which took it to forty-six. It stands two lines ahead of the telephone and was one ahead four weeks ago; the holder says out loud that it is not a coincidence, that she does not know what makes a gap, and that she will not look into it in front of nine people. She will not open it on a Friday.**

- **The telephone count has been announced sixteen times, of which eleven correspond to the eleven Wednesdays this book holds and five predate it and remain on a separate sheet she has never shown anybody.**
- **The rule — a book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call — has been given eleven times, at every sitting.**
- **It has been asked for ten times in nineteen years.**
- **It has been given unasked on two occasions, the ninth and the tenth sittings.**

**No writer may convert the last three figures into one another, or into the number of sittings, and a previous pass did and it was a defect.** Eleven givings and eleven sittings coincide and are two different counts; ten asks is a figure about nineteen years and not a sitting count and was never the tenth sitting; two unasked occasions are the ninth and tenth sittings and are not a third of anything. **The holder reconciled the twelve announcements of the telephone count against the book's seven sittings herself, out loud and unasked, in Chapter 217, and said that the twelve is a figure about her and not about the sittings, and that the room existed before the book did. A later writer must not read it as a sitting count.**

## 4. Two figures that are inherited and are not this volume's

- **The two years from Chapters 53 and 56.** Inherited from Volume 02, standing, and not extended. Volume 05 adds none: one year was written in Chapter 242 during the first drafting pass, both instances were removed in the review repair pass, and the batch summary's claim that it had added none was false and is corrected.
- **The four hundred and one replies, the eleven a week, and the nine hundred doors.** The four hundred and one replies and the nine who got the company answer are from a company's postbox and a minute; **about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card; and about nine hundred doors in four buildings carry a number off a different line of the same company's list, a room has never had its own number on the door, and Petar Vance can say how many doors his own company's list has put a wrong number on and cannot change any of them.**

**None of them is a rate and none of them is on a form.** That is not a formality: the eleven is on the back of a woman's own card, the nine hundred is a man's own count of his own work, and the four hundred and one is a number a man of forty-four marked as not sought.

## What a later writer must not do with any of this

Do not print a day number, a month-name, a day-date or a year. Do not re-derive the map from the anchor and do not re-base the two-day offset. Do not move the Exchange off its four-week Wednesday spacing, do not put it on a Friday, and do not put it anywhere but the next Wednesday, which is the Wednesday of week ninety-two. Do not grow the book's forty-six lines without a caller who says it to her face. Do not convert the four figures in section 3 into one another. Do not let the room's age, the card's age, the wall's age or the heating's age be carried forward a chapter; recompute all four from the day map in section 1. Do not let the heating come back on a document, a donation, a fund or a form, and do not have anybody apologise to the woman in the chair. Do not teach, demonstrate, assess or assist anything, do not stage the relay, do not name the licensor, do not fill the field, do not deliver a message, do not open the pencil box, do not move the ninth chair, and do not merge the three boards.
