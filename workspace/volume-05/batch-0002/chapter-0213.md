# Chapter 213 — Nine Chairs And A Cup Of Tea

A woman of thirty-eight with four rooms of folding work offered to show anybody who walked into a room off a service road in the Lower Wards how she folds a sheet, on the Wednesday afternoon of week seventy-one, and about nine people were in the room and the fold did not work for any of them, and the thing that worked was a kettle and a chair.

That is what happened and both halves of it were on purpose.

---

She had not been asked twice. She came in at about twenty past one with a sheet over her arm and asked whether anybody wanted to be shown how she folds one, and the first-year of nineteen said yes on behalf of the room, and that was the last anybody said about it.

She is thirty-eight. She does folding work in four rooms over about eleven miles and she has been doing it for nineteen years and she says the number without being asked for it. She has never taught anybody anything in a room. She offered it in a room the way she would offer it in a corridor.

"Right," she said. "**I have got about four minutes and then I want the kettle, because I have got a bus.**"

The room was ready. Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, a counter cleared to a strip, the hardboard turned so the writing faced into the room, the sounder on the wall by the counter, and the door propped with a wedge of wood because the shutter was up and there is no draught in a room where nobody is being taught anything.

---

About eleven people came through that door in about an hour. Nine of them were in the room at once, which is the number of chairs, which was not arranged and was an accident and is the only thing in this chapter anybody planned.

And four of the nine had been asked, before they came, to do it badly on purpose.

Nobody in that room knew which four, and the woman of thirty-eight who had offered the thing did not know which four, and she has said since that she still does not know and that she would not have known and that this is the only review of the demonstration she is going to get and she is going to take it.

Asha Reed arranged it on the Monday, in about nine words to each of the four, and she gave her reason out loud in the room afterwards and it is the first thing she has said in this case that nobody argued with.

"**Everybody in this city who gets shown a thing is shown it by somebody who can tell whether they did it right, and that is the entire problem and it is not about the fold. So we put four people in a room and made them do it badly on purpose and nobody in the room knew which four, and then we watched what a room does.**"

---

The fold is a sheet corner to corner in three, flat, in about eleven seconds.

She did it four times, slowly, and she talked the entire way through, and she was good at it in the way that people are good at things they have done nine thousand times.

Then nine people did it and not one of them got a square that would lie flat on a table, and about four of them got something that was not a sheet any more, and one of them — a young man who had come in to be warm, as he said, and who was not one of the four — got a triangle and held it up and said "that is a thing," and about four people laughed, and the woman of thirty-eight said yes it is.

And that was the whole of the demonstration. It took about half an hour and at the end of it there was a sheet over the rail of the counter and a heap of nine other things on the floor, and nobody's was right, and it was not anybody's fault, and nothing in the room was corrected.

---

Nothing in that room was corrected, and the two people in it who could have corrected something did not, and one of them has said out loud that it cost her something.

The cook of twenty-seven has been telling people about heat for nine years. She said afterwards, at the sink, that she identified about four wrong folds in the first four minutes and one of them was going to end up as a sheet somebody's kid had slept under, and that she said nothing for about nine minutes, and that it was the longest nine minutes she has spent in that room and she is not going to be told it was the right thing again.

"You cannot go round a room of people saying that," the woman of thirty-eight said. "**You will do it twice and then somebody will come for the correcting and not for the folding, and I have watched it happen to about four other people who taught something and I am not one of them and I would like my bus.**"

And the man of twenty-two was at the back by the wall for the whole half hour with a docket, and nobody in that room asked him a single question about any of the nine folds, and he did not go round the room, and he did not say whether anybody's was right, and nobody thought that was strange at the time and about four people noticed it on the way out.

---

And what worked was the kettle.

The cook had the kettle on at about half past two because the kettle's place in that room's day is fixed and does not depend on anybody's turn, and about nine people were standing in a room with nine chairs in it, and by about ten past three eight of the nine were sitting down.

There were two mugs short. The cook said "**somebody has mugs at home**" and did not make anybody move.

The thing that worked was a cup of tea and a chair. That is the whole of it, and it is not a finding, and nobody wrote it down.

---

And the first-year of nineteen wanted to write it down, and the cook stopped her, and she said why out loud in the room, in front of about nine people, and it is the reason that has shaped the rest of this movement.

"**No.**"

"It worked. Eight people sat down. That is a result."

"**That is a kettle and a chair and it is the whole of what I have got and it is not a technique, and I am not going to have it called one.**" She was already filling it. "**A thing is a technique in this room when you get it wrong and somebody is hurt. That is what the word means here and you worked it out yourself in a list of four words you have not let anybody see. If the kettle goes in the book then in about four years there is a person somewhere who is not doing the kettle right, and the person not doing the kettle right is a person who made somebody a cup of tea, and there is no card anybody can put in front of her that says she failed.**"

"That is not going to happen."

"**It has happened to me about nine times with heat and it happened to a woman I worked with twice and one of those was about a pot and not a knife, and the pot one is on a piece of paper in somebody's office that I have never been able to get back.**"

Nobody argued. The binder stayed shut on the shelf, and about nine people drank a cup of tea in a room with nine chairs in it, and a woman of thirty-eight caught her bus.

---

The four came and said so afterwards, to her, in about four minutes, at the door, and it was Asha Reed who said it and it was the first-year of nineteen who had put the four of them in and then said so to the person whose afternoon it had been.

She said: "Four of us did it badly on purpose."

And the woman of thirty-eight said: "**I know. I did not know which four and I would not have known, and I want to say that I have taught that fold in a room to about four hundred people in nineteen years and in every single one of those rooms somebody was frightened of getting it wrong in front of me, and the fold does not care, and I never knew that until about ten past three this afternoon and a man I have never met put the kettle on.**"

She got the bus.

---

The docket had four job numbers on it when he got in and none of the four was anything, and he sat down at about ten and the word had gone from nine to ten somewhere after one in the afternoon and had not come back down by the time the shutter was half up. It is at nine, ten and eleven, it has never been said in front of another person in this case, and it was not said in that room on Wednesday afternoon either.

He wrote one thing.

*216. Wednesday of week 71, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is nine chairs, a sheet, four people doing it badly on purpose, and a kettle.*

*The offer, as a source and not as a scene.* **One offer, made once, by a person who does folding work in four places about eleven miles apart and has done it for nineteen years, made at about twenty past one on the afternoon, to a room, in the manner of a corridor, with a stated time limit of about four minutes and a bus after it. She got the bus. The offer was accepted on her behalf by a first-year of nineteen and the sentence *I am only going to do this once, because if I do it twice it starts being a thing I do in rooms* is not repeated here because a record does not carry a person's reasons and this one is hers. The subject of the offer is a piece of cloth folded corner to corner in three, flat, in about eleven seconds. It has an order, a fault condition and a stop, and getting it wrong in an ordinary way produces a burn, and that is the whole of why it is in this chapter and not a warmer one.**

*Four of nine, and the arrangement.* **Four of the nine people present were asked, before they came, to fail on purpose. Nobody in the room knew which four. The person offering the thing did not know which four and has said since that she never knew and never would have known, and that this is the only review of the demonstration she is going to get and she is taking it. The four were told at the door at about ten past four, in about four minutes, by the person who had put them in, and the telling is the last act in the chapter and not a confession. The reason for the arrangement was given out loud to the room in about nine words and is the only sentence in this movement that nobody argued with: a demonstration in this city is a demonstration because somebody in it can say whether you did it right, and the test is not the fold. Nine chairs in a curve and nine people in the room at once, and the second number is an accident.**

*What happened, as a count.* **One offer, four demonstrations by the offerer, nine attempts, zero correct, about four results that were not cloth any more, one triangle produced by a young man who had come in to be warm and who said that is a thing, and the offerer said yes it is. Half an hour elapsed. Nothing was corrected by anybody. A sheet went over a counter rail and nine other things went on a floor. About eleven people came through the door in an hour. Two people who had not asked to be in the room noticed, on the way out, that a man standing at the back had not gone round the room and had not been asked anything about any of the nine attempts, and about four people did not notice and about four people were not in the room.**

*Two refusals of an answer, both given at a sink, both about nine minutes.* **A cook of twenty-seven identified about four failed folds inside the first four minutes and said nothing for the length of the demonstration, and afterwards named what that cost and said it was the longest she has spent in that room and that she is not going to be told it was the right thing again. The offerer gave the reason for that refusal in one sentence and would not be drawn further: a room in which a person is corrected is a room people come to for the correcting. The man of twenty-two's position for the half hour is a position and not a service: he was at the back with a docket, was asked nothing by anybody, and did not go round the room, and the chapter does not report what he thought about any of the nine and there is a reason for that which is not modesty.**

*What worked, and what the room said about it, and the reason it is not in the book.* **A kettle, on at about half past two at a fixed point in that room's day that does not depend on anybody's turn, and about eight of the nine people seated by about ten past three, and two mugs short, and a remark that somebody has mugs at home, and nobody moved. A first-year of nineteen asked for it to be recorded and was refused in front of about nine people, and the refusal is the finding of the chapter: a technique in that room is a thing whose wrongness hurts a person, which is a definition that first-year wrote herself in a binder in the second week of this volume as one of four things the room will not use, and if the kettle is recorded then in about four years there is a person somewhere not doing it correctly, and that person is a person who made somebody a cup of tea, and this city has no card that can be put in front of her saying that she failed. The refusal was grounded in her own history: about nine times with heat, twice to a woman she worked with, once of which concerned a pot and not a knife, and the pot is on a paper in an office she has never been able to get back. Nobody argued and the binder stayed shut on the shelf.**

*What this entry leaves standing.* **An empty page, an uncounted room, no record of the four, and a binder that was not opened. Nothing in this movement was settled by a form, a minute, a notice, a report, a committee, a leaflet, a registration or a constitution, and the first-year who said a wall is less than a form has lost that argument a third time in a month. No share, no relay, no practice, and nobody taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything by anybody, and the two people present who could have said whether a fold was right did not say it. The fourth condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased anywhere in this entry. The standing problem is referred to and is not said, and no rule about who may be asked has been invented and no number is attached to one. No name went over the door and no title, office or post went on anybody, including him. The card in a rail is twenty-seven days old and is not referred to; the master did not move and nothing was decided about it; the licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, and no message arrived from anybody. Nobody was asked about the room. The Exchange book is at forty-four lines. Entry 216.**
