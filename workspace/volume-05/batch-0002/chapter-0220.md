# Chapter 220 — Six Things In A Binder

On the Thursday evening of week seventy-three there were about eleven people in the front room of a converted shop unit off a service road in the Lower Wards, four of them standing, and a ring binder came off a shelf and was opened in the middle of the floor, and it has six things in it, five of them in five hands, and one page with a name struck through it in a single stroke and not erased.

That is the whole of what three weeks did.

---

The room got busy on the Thursday and it got smaller at the same time, which is not a contradiction and is the only arithmetic in this chapter.

Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and about eleven people in it at about half past seven, and four of the eleven standing against the wall in the space between the counter and the hardboard, which is about two feet of floor, and nobody asked the four to sit down because there was nowhere to sit.

A first-year of nineteen said the arithmetic out loud to a man of twenty-two with a docket and got it right.

"**It is busier than it was. It is also smaller than it was. Those are both true and about four people are standing up in a room where nobody was standing up a fortnight ago, and a person standing in a room is a person who is not staying.**"

---

The binder came off the shelf at about ten to eight and it was opened in the middle of the floor, flat, on a mat somebody had brought, because there was nowhere else for it to be that nine people could see.

Six things. A first-year of nineteen read the six out loud and did not read the contents of any of them, and said why.

"**Six things. Five of them are in five hands and one of them has four hands on it. And the first one has a name struck through it and I am not going to read that page out in a room, and anybody who wants to know what is on it can go and look at it, and the man whose name is in the middle of it is not in this city.**"

Nobody asked to look.

---

Five of the six have an answer on them to a question that one man asked, and the sixth has not got his name on it anywhere, and that is the finding of three weeks and it belongs to him and he does not want it.

He said it himself, without being asked, at about ten past eight, and he said it to the binder and not to the room.

"**This room's whole method is a question. Somebody walks in and we ask them something, and the only person in this city who can be asked to answer for it is me, so the answers end up on the pages. Five of these five are on five of these six pages, and the reason is a fortnight and a half old and I said it on this floor the day I signed that wall.**"

"And the sixth."

"**The sixth has four hands on it and none of them is mine. I was in that room for twenty-two minutes with a docket and I was not asked to take part and I did not offer, and a cook of twenty-seven told me why, and she was right, and nobody wrote me on the page, and I would not have let them.**"

---

And then the woman of about thirty said the thing, and she said it once, and she said it to him and not to anybody else, and nobody in that room had asked her a question for about a month.

"**Can I say the one thing.**"

"Go on."

"**You are on five of six pages in that binder, and you are the only one of the six of us whose name anybody in this city can go and find.**" She had her bag on her lap and both hands on it, the way she has had it every time. "**And there are nine keys in whatever this is going to be, and one of them is yours, and nobody knows who holds the other eight, and that is the whole of how a man with a key is not a post. It is not that he is a good man. It is that nobody can produce the list.**"

"I know."

"**Then you have just put the same name on five of six pages in a binder, and nobody does that by accident, and in about four years somebody is going to put the five pages together and find one name on all of them. A man with one of nine keys is a man with a door. A man on five of six pages is a document.**"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"**And there is no version of this that is not a trade**," she said, "**because if yours is not on them then somebody else's has to be, and I have sat in this room and listened to a cook give her reason and to a printer of fifty-eight give hers, and neither of them is putting theirs on, and neither am I, and I have not been asked for anything since the Friday of week sixty-nine and I have come every working day since.**"

---

He did not answer it, and Asha Reed did not write it down, and it is not on the wall, and it will not be on the wall, and nobody in that room asked her a single question about why she was there.

Somebody said: "Is that the finding?"

"That is the finding," said a nurse of twenty-four, who was on her knees with a mat and had not been in the room for the whole of it, and who has not said one word about anything anybody brought in this volume and is not going to.

---

The rest of that evening was small and it was the shape of a room.

The cook of twenty-seven put the kettle on at about half past eight and the kettle's place in that room's day has not moved since the first week and about four people a week are somebody doing it alone on a Tuesday with no hot water. The man of twenty-nine who works on lifts sat down in the fourth chair at about nine with a bag of spanners he has never opened in that building, and nobody asked him a question, and it was the best hour and a half he has had in four years and he has said that once and is not going to say it again. A printer of fifty-eight with a shop four feet wide was not there and had sent nothing.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and about four of the eleven are not on a rota, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card in this city and is not on a form and is not a rate, and nobody in that room said otherwise, and a name on five of six pages is not a rota.

---

And at about half past nine a man of about thirty-eight asked him something, on his way out, at the door, and it was not about the room.

He was the man who came to the Exchange eight days ago with four minutes and wanted to know what happens if he does not sign something at work.

He said: "**I signed it. On the Thursday. And I have not slept.**"

"I am sorry about that."

"**Do not be, that is not what I came out here for.**" He had his coat on and he had it half on. "**I came out here because you are the only person in there anybody could ask, and I have spent eight days deciding whether to ask you, and I want you to know that I got to the door and you were about nine feet away and you did not come over, and I want to say that that is the first useful thing anybody has done for me in about a month.**"

He said: "I heard you."

"**Right. I will tell you one thing for nothing, because I am in a mood. When I signed it, the woman who gave it to me said *that is all right, we all sign them*, and I said that to myself about four times on the bus, and I have been saying it to myself in a flat for about five nights, and the thing I have not been able to work out is that there is a room four miles from here with a wall on it that says it will not put a promise on a wall, and about four people in it know what that costs.**"

He did not answer that. He could not, and nobody else in that room could have, and the reason nobody in this city can tell the two apart is a thing he has said out loud in public twice and is not going to say a third time, and about eleven people a week are being taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard.

He said: "Goodnight."

"**Goodnight. Sorry about the binder.**"

"It is not yours," said the cook of twenty-seven, from the sink. "**It is nine people's and about half of it is in your hand. You are the only one of us who is on five of the six and you are the only one of us who has somebody ringing us, and if that is a fault then it is a fault about this city and not about you, and I have said all of it once and I am going to bed.**"

---

He got back to Lattice Ward at about ten. The lamp was on, the shutter was up, the docket had three job numbers on it, the drawer under the bench still had five things in it, and on the chair at the back was a coat with a corner of card about two inches by three in the inside pocket of it, and the original was in the fourth pocket of the tool roll with a fifth line on it that has been blank for a term and a half and that nobody has ever asked him to fill in and that nobody decided anything about tonight.

He wrote one thing, and it is the last line of the run and the run does not end here.

*223. Thursday of week 73, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is six things in a binder, five of them in five hands, one page with a name struck through it in a single stroke and not erased, and a woman of about thirty who is the only person in that room not on anything.*

*The binder, as an inventory of hands and not as a list of things.* **Six things, opened flat on a mat in the middle of the floor at about ten to eight, with about eleven people standing round it. One sheet carries eleven items in a cook's hand, the eleventh of them being a person's own name spelled as its owner spells it and written in a second hand at her asking because she cannot spell it, and that eleventh is now crossed in a single stroke from top left to bottom right, not erased, not gone back over and not made neater than it was, by a man of twenty-two with a biro out of his own pocket on the Monday of week 73, in front of about nine people. One sheet carries four words in a first-year's own hand, being the four things the room will not call a technique. One sheet carries four hands, being the first composite thing anybody has done in that room, and about nine minutes of argument over two of its words. Three sheets carry one hand each: a printer of fifty-eight's page, about a proof and the line on it that will be read wrong; about nine words from a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts; and a page from a woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary. The last three were written on days that are not in any chapter of this movement, in their own hands, by nobody who was asked, and no day has been set for any of them in any file. Five of the six are in five hands; one has four.**

*Why nobody read the contents out.* **A first-year of nineteen announced the number of things, the number of hands, and the strike, and declined to read the contents of any of the six, and said that anybody who wants to know what is on the struck page can go and look at it and that the man whose name is in the middle of it is not in this city. Nobody asked to look. The binder was closed at about half past nine and has three things fewer in it than it had in the middle of the floor.**

*Where his name is on the six, and where it is not, and why the room works that way.* **The method of that room is a question asked of a person who brings something to it, and the only person in this city who can be asked to answer for the room is him, and he said that out loud on that floor a fortnight and a half before this when he signed the hardboard. Five of the six sheets therefore carry the answer to a question of his, which is a consequence and not a design and nobody designed it. The sixth has four hands on it and not one of them is his: he was in that room for twenty-two minutes with a docket, was not asked to take part, did not offer, was given the reason for staying out by a cook of twenty-seven, agreed with it in about four seconds, and would not have permitted himself to be put on the page, and nobody did. So he is on five of the six, and he is the only one of the people whose hands those five are in that anybody in this city can go and find.**

*The sentence, said once, by a woman of about thirty, and its four parts as claims and not as wording.* **That he is on five of the six and is the only findable one of them. That the plan has nine keys, that his is one of them, that nobody knows who holds the other eight, and that this — not his character — is the whole mechanism by which a holder of a key is not a post, because nobody can produce the list. That five of six pages bearing one name is not an accident and will be legible as a document in about four years, by a route she set out in order. That if his name is not on them, somebody else's must be, and she has listened to the reason given by a cook and by a printer of fifty-eight and neither is going to give it, and neither is she, and nobody in that room has asked her for anything since the Friday of week sixty-nine and she has come on every working day since. Nobody wrote it down, it is not on the wall, it will not be on the wall, and nobody asked her a question. A nurse of twenty-four, who had been on her knees with a mat and had not been in the room for any of it, said that that is the finding, and has said nothing else in this movement about anything anybody brought in.**

*The room, as a count.* **Nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, about eleven people present at about half past seven, four of them standing in about two feet of floor between the counter and the hardboard, and nobody asked them to sit because there was nowhere to sit. A first-year of nineteen did the arithmetic out loud and got it right: busier than it was, smaller than it was, both true at once, and a person standing in a room is a person who is not staying. The room has been open five weeks and three days. The kettle went on at about half past eight at a point in that room's day which has not moved since the first week, and about four people a week in this city are somebody doing that alone on a Tuesday with no hot water. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts sat in the fourth chair at about nine with a bag of spanners he has never opened in that building and was asked nothing for about an hour and a half, and has said once what that hour and a half was worth to him and is not going to say it again. A printer of fifty-eight with a shop four feet wide was not there and sent nothing. About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, about four of them are not on a rota, the figure is on the back of a woman's own card and is on no form and is not a rate, and a name on five of six pages is not a rota and nobody in that room said otherwise.**

*The door, at about half past nine, and the only question anybody asked him in this movement.* **A man of about thirty-eight who took four minutes at the Exchange on the Wednesday of week 72 and wanted to know the consequences of not signing something at work signed it on the Thursday of week 72 and has not slept since. He came to the door having spent eight days deciding whether to ask the only person in that room anybody could ask, and reported that he reached the door with the man about nine feet away, and that the man did not come over, and that this is the first useful thing anybody has done for him in about a month. He then gave, for nothing, three facts: that the person who handed him the paper said something about everybody signing them, that he has been repeating it to himself on a bus and in a flat for about five nights, and that the thing he cannot resolve is that a room four miles away has a wall on it with a particular line on it, and that about four people in that room know the cost of that line. He could not answer that and said goodnight. **A cook of twenty-seven told the man at the door that the binder is not his, that it belongs to about nine people and about half of it is in his hand, that he is the only one of the six on five of the six and the only one the telephone rings for, that if that is a fault the fault is about this city and not about him, and that she had said all of it once and was going to bed.

*What this movement did not do, listed so that a later writer can check it rather than infer it.* **It did not say the standing problem, and it invented no rule about who may be asked, and attached no number to one. It did not open the Exchange book anywhere but Chapter 217 and did not have a caller sign anywhere else; the book is at forty-five lines and the run of shut sittings is six and a half out of seven sittings. It did not put a name over the door, a title on anybody, an office on anybody, a post on anybody, a committee in the room, a minute of anything, a notice, a report, a leaflet that is not the market one, a registration or a constitution; nine keys are needed and two exist and the first of them cannot be taken back by anybody. No share, no relay, no practice, and nobody taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything in any of the ten, and the condition is quoted in none of them and paraphrased in none of them. It did not name the licensor, name the person who inserted the page, name the clause behind the public version, or let any message arrive from anybody, and the two names that belong to that history are in no file in this movement. It did not empty the box in pencil, which still has one name in it, and did not touch it; it did not let the master leave the pocket and nothing was decided about it. It did not find the woman in this city who has been waiting since the spring, and she is not written down anywhere. It did not stage the relay, or use the woman who carried it, or put a courier in a room, or name a man of sixty-one, or say the retired sentence of a first-year, or write that word, or invent a second nineteen-year-old. Entry 223, and the run is continuous from fifty and it does not end here.**
