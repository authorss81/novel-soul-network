# Chapter 215 — Eleven Jobs And A Woman On Foot

The docket on a Friday has eleven jobs on it and nine of them are in date, and on the Friday of week seventy-one it was thirty-four degrees in a stairwell in Nine Bridges and the word went from nine a minute to ten at about one in the afternoon, and nobody asked him a single question about any of it.

---

He got to the depot at about twenty to eight. The man of fifty-two who manages it did not read the five jobs off the front of a leaflet, which is a thing he stopped doing in the spring and has not gone back to, and there was a wind coming off the water and about four men in the yard doing about nothing in particular.

The nine in date were a stair light, two lift calls, three lights on landings, a fitting on a stair in a block off a service road, a cold-store door, and a tap. The other two on the docket were a callout that had already been done by a man in a district jacket and a job for a pump that was not there any more and would be closed that afternoon with a note.

Nobody asked him where a release was going to come from. That has been true on every Friday of this case for a term and a half, and it did not stop being true because he has a card in a rail, and there is nobody in that yard he could telephone about it, and he has never once wanted to.

---

The cold-store door was the first thing that took any time.

A cold store off a service road, a man of about forty-four in a white coat, a door that closes, a thermometer on a dial, and a piece of paper on a clipboard that says the temperature is to be held at a number.

The door was closing properly. The temperature was where the paper said it should be. He checked it twice with his own instrument because he always does, and both readings were right, and he wrote the two readings on the docket and signed it, and the man in the white coat said the number had been doing that for a month.

"**Then the door is not the fault.**"

"**The door is not the fault and nobody said it was the fault. You are the fourth person to come about that door in a month and the other three were right as well.**"

He wrote that down too, because it is what the job sheet says you write down, and it is a true sentence, and the building is no different in the morning.

---

The stair in a block off a service road took about forty minutes and it is the second thing in three weeks in this case that has been done correctly and changed nothing.

The fault was recorded as a lamp out. The lamp was not out. He put a new meter on it and it was inside tolerance, and he took the lamp out and put a new lamp in and it made no difference at all, and he signed the docket, and the lamp that is now in that stair is the third he has fitted to that fitting in a year and the second and the first were also fine.

The reason he fitted it is that the fitting is a type that has not been made since before his apprenticeship and there are about nine hundred of them in four buildings in this city and about nine hundred of the new ones have already gone in over about two years because somebody changed what a stair is supposed to be.

Nobody asked him to change the fitting. The job sheet said the lamp. He changed the fitting anyway, because the old one is the thing that is going to be a problem in four years, and that is a correct action and it will be the reason nobody ever looks at that stair again, and he will not be the one who is asked about it.

---

The three lights on landings took until about half past four and two of them were not faults. That is not unusual on a Friday and it is not a complaint about anybody, and he has never once said a word to a tenant about which of the eleven was which.

The tap needed nine turns more than it got and got them.

And the job for the pump that was not there any more was closed with a note that said the equipment was not on site and there was nothing to do, and the note is true, and about four hundred of those notes a week go into that company's system in this city and nobody reads any of them.

---

At about one the word went from nine to ten and it stayed at ten for the rest of the day, and he noticed it at about one and there was nothing whatever to be done about it then or at any other time, and he has never told anybody and is not going to.

At about twenty past four he was in a canteen off a service road where the tickets are on a spike and the tea comes in a jug, and there were about nine people in it and none of them knew anybody else, and nobody said a word to him.

At about half past seven he went through a shutter in the Lower Wards and put a docket down on a counter.

---

The man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was already in the room.

He had come in at about seven on his own time, on a Friday, which is the day his employer's list runs on and the day he has been coming here since the first week the room was open, and he had a bag of spanners with him that he has never once opened in this building.

He had nothing. He was not carrying a thing and he was not wanting to say a thing and nobody asked him for either, and about half an hour went by in which four people talked about a bin collection that has not been done in about eleven weeks.

At about eight he sat down in one of the nine chairs, which were still in a curve with the two ends turned in, and he stayed until about half past nine, and in that time nobody asked him one question.

"That is the best hour and a half I have had in four years," he said, at the door, to nobody, and did not explain it, and has not been asked.

---

And a woman of about sixty-eight came in on foot at about ten past eight, on the Friday evening, and she had read a leaflet.

The leaflet has one sentence on the front of it and a first-year of nineteen's name at the bottom in her own hand, and it has been in about eleven hundred pockets in about a fortnight because the room has a market and a market has a table, and nobody in this case put it in a single one of them.

She said, in the first minute, before she sat down, that she had walked from a bus stop because the bus does not come to a service road, and that she had wanted to see whether the room was a real size, and that it was.

Then she asked him a question, and he was nine feet away with a docket, and he has never in his life not come over.

"**I have read that paper twice and there is nothing on it about what happens in there.**"

"**No.**"

"**Can you tell me?**"

And what he said was nine words, and they were counted on the page, and they are not an improvement on the nine words he said in a market ten days ago and he said so himself before anybody else could say it.

"**I could not tell you what happens in there.**"

---

It is nine words. It is true. It does not answer her question, and it is a different set of nine words from the nine in the market, and it is not a better one, and the reason he gave for that out loud is that the first set was nine words about the room and this set is nine words about himself, and a man who has run out of sentences about a room starts talking about himself, and that is the whole of the difference between the two.

She looked at the room for a while.

"Then I have come to the wrong place."

"**You have come to a room with nine chairs in it and about four people in it who have not asked you a single question, and if you want to know what happens in it, that is what happens in it.**"

She said, "**That is not much of a thing to put on a piece of paper.**"

"**No. It is the only sentence I have got that is both true and short.**"

---

She stayed about forty minutes. She did not ask for anything. She drank a cup of tea out of a mug that was not chipped, which the cook of twenty-seven noticed and has never mentioned to anybody, and she watched about nine people not do anything, and at the end of it she said one thing that the first-year of nineteen wrote nowhere.

"You lot need a worse room than this," she said. "**You have got a good room. Do you know what happens in a good room? Nothing happens in a good room.**"

And then she went out into a wet evening and down a service road to a bus stop, and about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and there were nine people in that room at the time and none of them was taught anything.

---

The page stayed empty. That is the second time in three weeks and it is the second different reason and Asha Reed gave it in about nine words with the binder shut.

"**The first time it was because a person came in wet and I would have had a category. This time it is because the woman was right and there is nothing to write down about a person being right, and there is no column in this city for that either.**"

She shut it and put it back on the shelf, and the binder has three things in it.

---

He got back to Lattice Ward at about ten. The lamp was on, the shutter was up, the docket had eleven jobs on it and about four of them were nothing, and the tool roll was on the bench and there was a piece of card in the fourth pocket of it with nothing written on the fifth line of it, and nobody has ever asked him to fill that in.

He wrote one thing.

*218. Friday of week 71, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is eleven jobs, a stair fitting nobody asked him to change, a man with nothing, and a woman on foot.*

*Nine in date, as a count of items and not as a rate.* **Eleven on the docket, nine in date, grade three from twenty to eight, thirty-four degrees in a stairwell in Nine Bridges, about nine men in a yard at twenty to eight. In date: one stair light, two lift calls, three landing lights of which two were not faults, one fitting on a stair off a service road, one cold-store door, one tap. Not in date: one callout already completed and written on by a man in a district jacket, and one job for equipment that is not on the site, closed the same afternoon with a note. About four hundred notes of that last kind go into that company's system in this city every week and nobody reads any of them, and this is the seventh correct action in three weeks in this case that has changed nothing.**

Two readings taken with his own instrument because he always takes two; both correct; both written down; the man in the white coat said the figure had held for about a month and that this is the fourth visitor in a month and the other three were also right. A stair off a service road, forty minutes, recorded as a lamp out and not a lamp out: the lamp inside tolerance on a meter, a new lamp fitted, no change, signed, and the third lamp fitted to that fitting in a year of which the first two were also sound. A tap needing nine turns more than it got and getting them.**

*The fitting, and why it is the reason nobody looks at that stair again.* **The fitting is a type not manufactured since before his apprenticeship. About nine hundred of the old type stand in four buildings in this city and about nine hundred of the current type have already gone in over about two years, because somebody changed what a stair is required to be. Nobody asked him to change it. The job sheet asked for a lamp. He changed the fitting because the old one is what will be a problem in four years, and it made no difference that day, and it is the reason nobody will ever examine that stair, and he will not be the person anybody asks. This is a correct action, performed correctly, by a man who is not a villain, and it changed nothing except a future that has not happened.**

*Two absences from the depot, both load-bearing.* **The manager did not read the five jobs off the front of a leaflet, which is a practice he stopped in the spring and has not resumed, and the reason is not in this entry because he has not given it. About four people in a canteen off a service road at about twenty past four, tickets on a spike, tea in a jug, none of whom knew anybody else and none of whom said a word to him. Nobody in that yard asked him where a release would come from; the same has held on every Friday in this case for a term and a half and there is nobody there he could telephone about it, and there has never been anybody there to telephone.**

*The word, as it stands and not as a story.* **Nine a minute all morning, ten from about one in the afternoon, ten at about ten at night. Nothing was done about it then or at any other time, it has never been told to anybody, and it was not said in that room on the evening either.**

*The man of twenty-nine and an hour and a half.* **He works on lifts, is on his employer's list for Fridays, arrived at about seven on his own time carrying a bag of spanners he has never opened in that building, had nothing and wanted nothing, was asked nothing, and sat down in the fourth chair at about eight and left at about half past nine. **The one remark he made was that this is the best hour and a half he has had in four years, which he did not explain, and which has not been asked about, and which he has said once. This is the eighth correct action in three weeks in this case that changed nothing and it is the only one of the eight in this movement that was wanted by the person it happened to.

*Nine words, counted, and the comparison made in the room.* **A woman of about sixty-eight, on foot, from a bus stop, because the bus does not come to a service road, having read a leaflet that carries one sentence and a first-year of nineteen's name at the bottom in her own hand, in about eleven hundred pockets in about a fortnight, put in none of them by anybody in this case. He said nine words, counted on the page: *I could not tell you what happens in there.* He then said out loud, before anybody else spoke, that it is not an improvement on the nine words he said in a market nine days before, and gave the difference: the first set was nine words about the room and this set is nine words about himself, and a man who has run out of sentences about a room begins talking about himself. She said she had come to the wrong place. He was told that she had come to a room with nine chairs in it and about four people in it who had not asked her a question, and that if she wants to know what happens in it, that is what happens in it. Her second remark was that the room is a good one, and that what happens in a good room is nothing, and that they need a worse one.**

*What stayed unwritten, and the second empty page of a month.* **She stayed about forty minutes, asked for nothing, drank from the one mug in that building that has no chip in the handle, which the cook noticed and has never mentioned to anybody, and watched about nine people not do anything. The page stayed empty, for the second time in a month and for the second different reason, and the reason was given with the binder shut: the first time a category would have been created and this time there is no column in this city for a person being right, and the first-year of nineteen said the word *right* in the way she says the other four. The binder has three things in it and has had three things in it since the Thursday before last, and no fourth thing went into it on a Friday and no fifth thing was written and taken out and no page exists anywhere in this case for either.**

The four people in that room did not teach a woman of sixty-eight anything, including what happens in the room. No share, no relay, no practice, and nobody taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything.
