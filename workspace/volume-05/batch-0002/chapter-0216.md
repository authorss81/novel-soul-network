# Chapter 216 — The First Line, Read Aloud

A man of forty-nine offered a thing on the Monday morning of week seventy-two to a room off a service road in the Lower Wards, and it was refused before anybody touched it, and the instrument was the first line on a piece of hardboard and the instrument was a woman of nineteen reading it out loud.

That is the seventh thing anybody has brought into that room. Five of them are in a ring binder. One of them went back into an inside pocket.

---

He came at about ten and asked whether there was a person who ran the room, and Asha Reed said there was not, and he said that was fine, he had been in a canteen for nine years and he did not expect a person to run a room.

He is forty-nine. He works in a canteen in a building off a service road that has about eleven hundred people in it, and he has been there nine years, and for six of them he has taught anybody who starts a shift how to carry four hot plates at once.

"Two hands each," he said. "**You do not get four up off the pass in one go, everybody tries it, and everybody's wrists go. Two, and then two, and the second two start before the first two land, and it is a rhythm and not a trick, and I have taught it to about a hundred and forty people and about a hundred and twenty of them can still do it.**"

That is a real thing. It has a beat, a boundary, a stop, and it is the kind of thing a person can be shown wrongly and get a burn on.

---

He had it on paper and he put the paper on the counter, and nobody touched it.

"It is not mine," he said. "**That is the bit I have to say first. That is our sheet. It has got two things on it and one of them is the plates and the other one is a notice about the floor, and it has been on the wall by the pass in about nine canteens in this city for six years and it is not a poster, it is a laminated thing, and I wrote the plates part in about nine sentences in a week in my first year because somebody asked me to write it down and I did not know it was going on a wall.**"

"And what is on it now is the same as what you said to me," said Asha Reed.

"**It is not, no, and I could not tell you in a sentence which bit differs, and the bit that differs is the reason.**"

---

And then he gave the reason, and he gave it without being asked, and it is the whole of this chapter and it is structural and not a matter of feeling.

"**Two versions.**"

He put two fingers on the laminated sheet without picking it up.

"**If it is in that book in here, in my words, then there are two of it. And the one in here is the one with a person's name on the top of it and the one out there is the one on a wall by a pass, and everybody in this building has read the one on the wall by a pass about nine hundred times without ever deciding to. So the one in here is the correction.**"

"Who would correct it."

"**Nobody. That is what a correction is, it is a thing that exists whether or not anybody says it out loud. And then in about four years somebody in an office finds out that their laminated sheet is not the only one, and they will not be angry, they will do what you do when you find out a thing is not the only one, which is change it. And then the sheet on the wall is out of date and about nine canteens have a wall with a thing on it that has been superseded, and about a hundred and forty people were taught by a man who is not the man on the new sheet.**"

"And your name is not on the new sheet."

"**My name is not on either of them. I have never wanted it on either of them.**"

---

Asha Reed put her hand flat on the ring binder, which was still on the shelf behind her, and she did not open it and she did not touch it.

Then she went and stood in front of the piece of hardboard by the door, nine lines in six hands, four of them hers, and she read the first one.

"**It will not keep anything it cannot take back.**"

And she said, over her shoulder, to the room: "**That is what it is for and it is not about him.**"

---

And then she said the other half, and the other half is the reason the room works and the reason it is smaller than anybody wanted.

"He knew that before I got to the wall. He said it to himself at that table and I watched him say it. **So I am not obeying him, I am saying it first, and there is a difference and it is the only difference I have got.**"

"That is fair," said the man of forty-nine. "**I would have said it about nine minutes ago if this room had been a wall.**"

And nobody argued, and nobody touched the laminated sheet, and it went back into his inside pocket where it had come from, and the ring binder stayed on the shelf with three things in it.

---

He named his own cost at the door and nobody had asked him for it, which is the fourth time in five days that somebody in that room has done that.

"**Six years and about a hundred and forty people and it is on nine walls and in my head, and the one thing I have wanted out of all of it is to have it somewhere in my own words, and I have now asked for it four times in about four years and this is the fourth and I am going out with nothing.**"

Then he stopped in the doorway and gave the other half of it, and this is the finding of the chapter and he gave it before anybody in that room thought of it.

"**And I want to say the thing about your wall, because I have been in this room once before and I read it and I did not understand it and I have understood it standing here.**"

"Go on."

"**It has got no force. None of the nine lines has any force. There is nobody in this city who can make me obey that, and if I had walked in and taken that book and read your sheet out of it in about nine canteens, there is not one person anywhere who could have stopped me and I would have been within the law for about four years.**"

"Yes," said Asha Reed.

"**And it works. You have just refused me with a thing you cannot enforce, and I have not argued with it, and the only reason I have not argued with it is that the woman who wrote it down had a man in this room last week who found somebody else's page and took it out of that book, and I do not know his name, and I do not need to, and that is why I believe you.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**That is not a rule that works**," she said at last. "**That is four people who happened to be in a room. It has worked twice and one of the times it cost the person it was done to.**"

"**Then it has worked twice,**" he said, "**and I am going to go and be a canteen.**"

---

The woman of about thirty was in the third chair from the door for about two hours and nobody asked her anything, and the room had about eleven people through the door that morning in about three hours, and the binder was on the shelf, and nine lines in six hands were on a wall that cannot enforce anything, and Marek Senn was standing at the back with a docket for the whole of it and was not asked a single question by anybody and said nothing.

At about twenty past twelve the cook of twenty-seven said the thing about the nine lines, which is the thing the movement exists for and which she has said in about four different ways since the Thursday of week seventy.

"**Nine lines. One of them has stopped somebody today and it is not a sign that anybody put up, and it is not enforceable, and it is the only thing in this building that has ever stopped anybody.**"

---

He got back to Lattice Ward at about ten that night. The lamp was on and the shutter was up and the docket had two job numbers on it, and there were nine lines in six hands on a wall four miles away and about nine hundred lifts in this city, and a key in his pocket that nobody can take back, and a word that had been at ten since Monday.

He wrote one thing.

*219. Monday of week 72, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a laminated sheet that stayed on a counter, and the first line of a wall.*

*The seventh thing, as a man and as an offer.* **Forty-nine, nine years in a canteen in a building off a service road with about eleven hundred people in it, six years teaching anybody who starts a shift to carry four hot plates at once: two hands each, never four up off the pass in one go, the second two starting before the first two land, a rhythm and not a trick, taught to about a hundred and forty people of whom about a hundred and twenty can still do it. He asked whether there was a person who ran the room and was told there was not, and said that was fine. He had it on a laminated sheet, put it on the counter, and said before anybody touched it that it is not his.**

*Why it is not his, in his own words, and the reason.* **The nine sentences about the plates were written by him in about nine sentences in his first year because somebody asked him to write it down, and they have been on a wall by a pass in about nine canteens in this city for six years on a laminated sheet that also carries a notice about the floor, and about a hundred and forty people have been shown it. The structural reason he gave before anybody asked him for one: if the room holds it in his own words then there are two versions, and the version in a ring binder with a person's name at the top of it is the one that will read as the correction, and nobody has to say that out loud for a correction to exist; and in about four years somebody in an office finds out that their sheet is not the only one and changes it, and about nine hundred reads of a wall by a pass become a wall with a superseded thing on it, and the man who taught it is not the man named on the new sheet. He has never wanted his name on either sheet.**

*The refusal, and the order of it.* **A first-year of nineteen put a hand flat on the ring binder on the shelf behind her and did not open it, went and stood in front of the nine lines in six hands on the hardboard by the door, and read the first one: that it will not keep anything it cannot take back. The man had worked it out at the table before she reached the wall and she said so out loud, and said that this is why she said it anyway: saying it first is the only difference the room has. He said that is fair and that he would have said it in about nine minutes if the room had been a wall. The laminated sheet went back into the inside pocket it came from. Nobody argued, nobody touched it, and the binder stayed on the shelf with three things in it.**

*What he said at the door, unasked, and it is the finding.* **Six years and about a hundred and forty people, a thing on nine walls and in his head, one request in about four years made four times, and he is going out with nothing. And then: the nine lines on that wall have no force, there is nobody in this city who could make him obey them, and if he had taken that book and read his sheet out of it in about nine canteens he would have been within the law for about four years and nobody could have stopped him — and he did not argue with a wall, and the only reason he did not is that in the previous week a man took somebody else's page out of that binder, and he does not know the man's name and does not need to. A first-year of nineteen answered him in about nine words: that is not a rule that works, that is four people who happened to be in a room, it has worked twice, and one of the times it cost the person it was done to. He said then: then it has worked twice, and he is going to go and be a canteen.**

*What he did not do, and the cost each of the four took.* **A cook of twenty-seven, at about twenty past twelve, said out loud that there are nine lines on that wall, that one of them stopped a man that morning, that it is not a sign anybody put up, that it is not enforceable, and that it is the only thing in that building that has ever stopped anybody. A man of twenty-two stood at the back with a docket for the whole of it, was asked nothing by anybody, and said nothing. A woman of about thirty sat in the third chair from the door for about two hours, was asked nothing and asked of nothing, and is not on the wall and is not in the binder and is not in this entry. And the seventh offer is not the sixth, and it is not a repeat of the fourth, and the difference is that nobody in that room argued and nobody improved on anybody and the instrument was a line read by a first-year of nineteen off a piece of hardboard that is not screwed to anything.**

*What this entry refuses.* **Any record of the seventh offer, any list of what has been brought to that room, any count of seven, and any improvement on a refusal made by a first-year of nineteen reading a line off a piece of hardboard that is not screwed to anything. No share, no relay, no practice, and nobody taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything, and the man of forty-nine taught nobody in that room, which is what his six years are about. No form, minute, notice, report, committee, registration or constitution, and nothing over the door, and no title, office or post on anybody, and a woman of thirty-one with a key is not one. The condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased. The standing problem is not said, no rule about who may be asked has been invented, and the Exchange book is at forty-four lines with the last shut sitting just over three weeks back. The card in a rail is thirty-two days old and is not referred to. The master did not move and no decision was taken about it. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, and no message arrived from anybody. Nobody was asked about the room. Entry 219.**
