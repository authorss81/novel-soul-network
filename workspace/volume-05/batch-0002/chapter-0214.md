# Chapter 214 — The Sixth Person Brings Something

A man of thirty-three who works on heating came into a room off a service road in the Lower Wards on the Thursday afternoon of week seventy-one with nine minutes' worth of a thing he does every working day of his life, and it went on a page in his own words in about four minutes, and it came off that page in about ninety seconds, and the reason is the wall.

He is the sixth person to bring something into that room. The fifth went home with his own.

---

He had been in twice before and had not brought anything, and both times he had stood at the back with his hands in his pockets and left before the kettle went on.

He is thirty-three. He works on heating and he is on a company's rota and he has been on it nine years, and the company prints a job sheet, and the job sheet has a box at the bottom of it for a name, and his name has been in that box since he was twenty-four.

"Right," he said, about two minutes in. "**I am only going to do this once, because if I do it twice it starts being a thing I do in rooms and I have enough of those.**"

"Go on," said Asha Reed.

---

His thing is the first nine minutes of a job, and it is a real thing and it takes about eleven minutes to say.

You come in the door. You do not go to the boiler first. You stand in the hall for about four seconds. You look at the ceiling in the hall, then you look at the outside of the nearest radiator, then you find the consumer unit and read the labels on it without touching anything, and only then do you go and find whatever it is you came for.

He said it in about nine sentences, in his own voice, without hesitating, and twice he said a word he then replaced with a different word that meant the same thing and was more accurate.

It is the kind of thing that cannot be taught because nobody has ever met anybody who could not already do the first four seconds, and everybody who can do it says it the way he said it.

He stopped and looked at the ceiling of the room off the service road, which is a ceiling with a light on it and a window four feet from a wall, and he laughed at himself for about a second.

"**You would not do that in here.**"

"**I would do that in here**," said the cook of twenty-seven, who was at the sink.

---

Asha Reed wrote it down. That is what she is for and she is the only person in that room who is fast at it.

She wrote about nine minutes of it in the order he said it, in his own words, and the two words he had replaced were the two she kept, and she did not tidy the sentence about the four seconds, and she did not put a full stop after *hall* where he had put one.

He read the page back. It took him about ninety seconds and he read it twice.

"**That is what I said.**"

"**That is what you said**," she said. "**It is in your hand and it is on a page and it is the fourth thing in this binder, and that is the whole of what happens in this room to anything anybody brings here.**"

The page went in. It was the fourth thing in a ring binder, and it was in his own hand, and about eight people were in the room, and nobody had asked him a single question about whether he was allowed to.

---

And then it stopped being about a hall.

"There is a box at the bottom of the job sheet," he said.

"**I know there is. I have seen about nine of them on people's counters in this city.**"

"**My name is in it.**" He did not take the page out. "**And the nine minutes I just said are the nine minutes that are printed on that sheet, in a box, with my name under them, and the reason they are on the sheet is that I wrote them out for them in my second year and they printed it and they have never changed it since and it is on about eleven thousand pieces of paper in about nine hundred vans.**"

The room got quieter in a way that is not describable and is not a rule.

"**So that page is my employer's page. I said it in my own voice, and I have said the same nine minutes in my own voice in about four hundred houses, and the second they go on that page they are that sheet, and the sheet is the thing I cannot take back, and I did not know that until about ninety seconds ago when you put it in a binder and shut it.**"

---

He did not stop there, and the second half of it is why this chapter is not a fight about property.

"There is the other half and I would rather somebody else said it."

Nobody said it.

"**I have been in this room twice. I have got here on a Tuesday off the rota and once on a Monday in the middle of a job. I could not tell you who is in it. I do not know whether the woman who sold me the kettle is here three days a week or one. I could not tell you whether the four people in this room work for anybody, and I have looked, and the answer is that two of you are written down somewhere and two of you are not.**"

Asha Reed said, "Go on."

"**So if I put my words on that page then there is a piece of paper in a room that I cannot describe, in a building that has a telephone number on the outside of it and no name, and if my company ever hears that their job sheet is on a shelf in the Lower Wards then the first thing that goes is that number, and the second thing that goes is a room that four people have signed a year of.**"

---

Asha Reed read the wall out loud, twice, and she did not read all nine lines and nobody asked her to.

She went and stood in front of the piece of hardboard and she said the seventh line and then the ninth.

"**It will not keep a copy of a thing nobody asked to keep.**"

And then: "**It will not be printed.**"

And then she said: "**Both of those are about you and neither of them is about the thing you brought, and that is what a wall is for.**"

"He agreed in about four seconds," said the cook of twenty-seven, from the sink, and nobody improved it.

---

He took the page out of the binder himself. He did it the way a man takes a receipt out of a book, one handed, and he folded it once and put it in his inside pocket, and it is the second thing in a term that has gone back into an inside pocket in this case and neither of them was in his hand at the start.

"That is right," he said. "**And I am not going to argue with a wall. I have read nine of them on a building site and every one of them was somebody's handwriting and none of them had my name at the bottom, which is why I have never once believed one, and this one is in a different language and I believe it, and I cannot tell you why and I have not tried.**"

And then he named his own cost, which nobody had asked him for.

"**There is a difference between the nine minutes as they are on that sheet and the nine minutes as I said them to you just now, and it is about four words, and the sheet is the one with my name in the box under it, and I am the only person in this city who knows what the four words are, and at ten o'clock this morning I was the only one and now four of you are.**"

He said that last part as though it were a bill and not a complaint, and then he said: "**I am not saying take it out again. I am saying do not let anybody put a leaflet out on Wednesday without reading this chapter out to them first.**"

Nobody has put a leaflet out on a Wednesday.

---

And the first-year of nineteen said the last thing in that room and it was about herself and it was the fourth time in three weeks that somebody in that room had been proved right by a stranger.

"That is my argument," she said. "**A description of a room is a description of the people in it. I said that on a floor three weeks ago and on a counter a week ago and I have lost it twice. I am not going to stand here and act as though a man of thirty-three has just taught me something.**"

"You are not," he said. "**You wrote it on a bit of hardboard seven days ago and it is on the wall in your hand, and I read it when I came in, and it is the reason I told you about the telephone number on the outside before I told you about my name at the bottom.**"

And she did not say anything to that and about four people in the room watched a nineteen-year-old not be able to answer, which is a thing that happens to her about twice a term and she has never once enjoyed it.

---

At ten that night the docket in Lattice Ward had three job numbers on it and there was a ring binder on a shelf four miles away with three things in it instead of four, and nine lines on a piece of hardboard by a door, and a folder on the same shelf with a question on the back of a docket in it, and about nine hundred vans in this city with four words on a job sheet in about eleven thousand copies that four people in a room in the Lower Wards now know the difference between.

He wrote one thing.

*217. Thursday of week 71, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a page that went in and came out in ninety seconds, and two lines of a wall.*

*The sixth person, as a man and not as a case.* **Thirty-three, heating, nine years on a company's rota, in twice before the Thursday and brought nothing on either visit, standing at the back with his hands in his pockets and leaving before the kettle. He said in advance that he would do it once, because doing it twice would make it a thing he does in rooms and he has enough of those. The room had about eight people in it. Nobody asked him a single question about whether he was allowed to put it down, which is itself the finding of the chapter and is the reason it works: the room's whole method is a question, and on this page the question was asked by a first-year of nineteen and the answer came straight out of the man who does the thing.**

*What the thing is, in facts rather than in words.* **The first nine minutes of a job, in about nine sentences, in his own voice, with two words replaced by more accurate ones as he said them. Stand in the hall about four seconds. Look at the ceiling in the hall. Look at the outside of the nearest radiator. Find the consumer unit and read the labels without touching anything. Only then go and find what you came for. It took about four minutes to write and about ninety seconds for him to read it back, twice, and he said twice that it was what he had said, and the first-year kept the two words he had replaced and did not tidy the sentence about the four seconds. The page was the fourth thing in a ring binder and it was in his own hand and it was in for about four minutes in total.**

*Why it came out, and the wall, and the two lines.* **The job sheet his company prints has a box at the bottom for a name and his name has been in it since he was twenty-four, and the nine minutes are printed on that sheet, in a box, with his name under them, because he wrote them out for the company in his second year and nobody has changed it since, and it is on about eleven thousand pieces of paper in about nine hundred vans. The second and larger reason is his and not the company's: he has been in that room twice and could not tell anybody who is in it — whether the cook is there three days a week or one, whether two of the four people present are written down anywhere and two are not — and a piece of paper in a room he cannot describe, in a building with a company's telephone number on the outside of it and no name on it, is a page he cannot take back, and he worked that out in about ninety seconds. The two lines of the wall read aloud by the first-year, in that order and nothing else: that it will not keep a copy of a thing nobody asked to keep, and that it will not be printed. He agreed in about four seconds, took the page out of the binder himself, one handed, folded it once, and put it in his inside pocket.**

*The cost, in his own words, unasked.* **He is the only person in this city who knows the four words by which the nine minutes as he says them differ from the nine minutes as they are printed on a sheet with his name at the bottom of it, and at ten that morning he was the only one, and four people in a room in the Lower Wards now are, and he said it as a bill and not as a complaint. He also named the cost of the room's existence in front of eight people: if his company hears that their job sheet is on a shelf in the Lower Wards then the first thing that goes is the telephone number on the plate outside and the second thing that goes is a year that four people have signed. He said he is not asking for the page back again and asked instead that no leaflet goes out on a Wednesday without that chapter being read to the people putting it out.**

*What the first-year said about herself, and what she could not answer.* **She said out loud that a description of a room is a description of the people in it is her own argument, made on a floor three weeks ago and on a counter the week before, and that she lost it twice, and that she is not going to stand in a room and act as though a man of thirty-three has taught her something. He agreed, said the line is in her hand on a piece of hardboard seven days old, and said that it is the reason he mentioned the telephone number on the outside of the building before he mentioned his own name at the bottom of a sheet. She had no answer and about four people in that room watched a nineteen-year-old not be able to answer, which happens to her about twice a term.**

*Not in this entry.* **A page, a count of what has been offered to that room, a list of people, a copy of nine minutes, or any record of the offer: the binder holds what it held on the Thursday morning and a sheet is in an inside pocket four miles away. A company's job sheet is not a share, and about eleven thousand of them in about nine hundred vans are not shares either. No share, no relay, no practice, and nobody taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything. Nothing went over the door, and no title, office, post, committee, minute, notice, report, registration or constitution exists anywhere in this movement; the wall it was refused by is nine lines in six hands and is a different object from the binder and from the folder. The condition is not quoted and not paraphrased. The standing problem is not said, and no rule about who may be asked has been invented, and no number is attached to anything. The card in a rail is twenty-eight days old and is not mentioned. The Exchange book is at forty-four lines and has been shut six times. The master did not move and the licensor is unnamed and the field is blank and no message arrived from anybody. Nobody was asked about the room. Entry 217.**
