# Chapter 207 — It Was Warm In There

A room off a service road in the Lower Wards has a shutter that goes up by hand at seven and down by hand at ten, and on the Friday evening of week sixty-nine a man of twenty-two came through that shutter at about a quarter to nine with a bag of two lamps in it, and there was a woman sitting in the third chair from the door who had not been let in by anybody.

The kettle was on. It had been on since about half past seven and it was on because a woman of twenty-seven had put it on at half past seven for a reason she has never given to anybody.

---

Nobody knows how long she had been there and nobody asked, and that is the first fact about her and it stayed the first fact about her for the rest of her life in this building, which is about nine weeks.

She is about thirty. She was not on a rota and she was not on a course and she was not referred and she did not have a technique. She had a carrier bag with two things in it that stayed on her lap the whole time, and one of the two things was a card from a supermarket that she looked at about nine times, and neither of the two things ever came out of the bag.

"Have you got a cup for me," she said. "I have not got a cup."

"You have got a cup," said Dessa Kwan. "**That is the whole of what has happened in this room so far and I would like it noted before anybody makes it into more than it is.**"

---

There were three people in that room and one of the three was doing nothing at all, on purpose, which is a thing that has to be learned.

Asha Reed had the ring binder open on the counter. She had been in the room on the Wednesday, and she had written two things in it in her own hand, and both of them were in the words of the person who does the thing, and one of the two was eleven items long.

A man of twenty-nine was on the floor with a hand plane. He had come in at about eight, and he works on lifts, and he is on his employer's Friday list, and he did not ask to be introduced and nobody introduced him. He took about a millimetre off the top edge of the door of the back room with a plane, in about four minutes, because a man of twenty-two had said on the Monday that it wanted that and nothing else, and nobody asked him to and nobody paid him and there is no line anywhere with his name on it that says he did it.

The door closes now. That is all he did in the first week and it is the reason the room has a back room anybody can be in.

---

The woman of about thirty said the reason she came, and she said it to nobody in particular, and it took about eleven seconds.

"I walked past at about half seven and it was warm in there and it has been raining since about four. **That is it. I did not know this was anything. I thought it was a letting agent's office that had had the lights on.**"

She said the last part in a voice that was not defensive and was not apologetic either.

"We are not a letting agent's office," said Asha Reed.

"I did not think you were," said the woman of about thirty. "**I want to say the true thing about that, which is that I did not think anything, and a person who has been in a room for twenty minutes and been given a cup of tea has not thought anything, and if you want somebody to have thought something then you are going to be disappointed and I would rather say it now.**"

---

Dessa Kwan made the tea and put it in front of her in a mug with a chip in the handle, which is the same mug out of four that had a chip in the handle on the Monday of week sixty-eight, and the chip is on the same side, and nobody has ever asked which of the four she chose.

She put milk in it without asking, and then stood there and watched it, and then said one thing that she has repeated about four times since and has never made longer.

"**Nine years, that is what a cup of tea is. Nine years of doing that for people who are somewhere they did not plan to be, and not one of them has ever been able to pay me for it, and not one of them has ever been taught it by anybody, and about four of them a week are somebody who is doing it alone on a Tuesday with no hot water.**"

She did not say the word *technique* and nobody in that room said it for her.

---

Then the man on the ladder came.

There is a man from the letting agent's own list, and he was about forty-one, and he had come on the Friday because there was an item on a schedule, and the item was the bell-push by the door, which had been on a schedule as a defect for about four years, twice a year, and had been given a number each time, and about eleven of those numbers are in that company's system and are marked closed.

The bell-push is a brass thing on a short stalk four inches to the left of the door. It has never been connected to anything.

He came on a ladder at about ten past nine and he had a bell-push in a bag and a length of two-core and a small transformer, and he did the whole thing in about forty minutes, and he did it properly, and he was not a villain and nobody had asked him to be careful.

He made the second bell-push ring.

It is a sounder inside on the wall by the counter, eight feet from the counter, and it is loud, and it is audible in all four rooms, and it can be heard from the service road.

Nobody in that room was in the room the first time he pressed it, because the four of them were in the front room and the sounder is in the front room, and they heard it, and a woman of about thirty in the third chair from the door said "oh" and put her hand flat on her knee.

---

And it changed nothing, and this is the fifth correct action in this batch that changed nothing, and it is the one that was done to the room rather than by it.

A sounder on a wall makes a noise in a room when somebody presses a switch. It does not make anybody come. It is not connected to a telephone, or to a bell wire that goes anywhere, or to a person. It is a noise that a room makes at a man on a step, and the man on the step has about nine seconds of deciding what to do with it and this is a decision nobody has ever written down anywhere and about four thousand of them happen in this city every day.

"**That is the best thing anybody has put in this building and it has not told anybody anything,**" said Asha Reed, and she wrote it down, and she wrote it down on the day, in the ring binder, in her own hand, and it is the only sentence in that binder that is not in the words of the person who does the thing, and about four weeks later somebody was going to notice that and it was not going to be a big thing.

The man wrote a card. The card said the item was done and the item was external push, sounder added, tested from outside only. Nobody tested it from inside. He had tested it from outside because from outside is where you are when you press it.

---

The woman of about thirty left at about half past nine and nobody asked her anything and nobody walked her to the door and the door does not stick any more, so it was easy, and she put the carrier bag on her lap into the carrier bag and went out into the rain.

Nobody wrote down that she had been there.

Asha Reed had the binder open and it was the whole of her Friday and she looked at the page for a while with the pen in her hand and then she put the pen down and shut it.

"**I have got two things in here in my own hand and about nine people have been in this room since the Monday and one of the nine was here for twenty minutes and had a cup of tea and she told us the reason out loud and she was right about the reason and I cannot put any of it in this book.**"

"Why not."

"Because **the day I write down a woman who came in because it was warm, then this binder has got a category in it, and the category is the thing I have been trying to get two years, which is ordinary people, and I would be having it because a person of about thirty turned up wet on a Friday and not because anybody came here to be taught.**" She pushed the binder away from her. "And then in about four years the sentence anybody has got is that this room takes anybody, and the word *anybody* is doing a job it cannot do, and the people it is describing are the ones who were not here."

---

Nobody argued with her, and the cook of twenty-seven said "correct," and the man of twenty-nine on the floor said nothing, and Marek Senn wrote nothing down either, which is a thing he has done three times in a term and a half and once in a term before this.

He wrote one thing at half past ten, on the counter of the front room, with the shutter half up, because there was nowhere else and because the bench was four miles away and it was raining.

*210. Friday of week 69, half past ten at night on a counter in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards, with the shutter half up, and this entry is a cup of tea, a bell that rings, and a page that was left empty.*

*The room, on a Friday evening, as a set of facts.* **A shutter up by hand at seven and down by hand at ten. Four rooms and a back one. A door in the back room that sticks a foot into the room, which on this Friday stopped sticking, because a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts and is on his employer's Friday list took about a millimetre off the top edge with a hand plane in about four minutes, having been told the fact of it on the Monday by a man of twenty-two, and nobody asked him to and nobody paid him and no line anywhere carries his name against it. That is the only repair anybody has made to that building in eleven days and it is the reason there is a back room anybody can be in. Two people were in the room at half past seven and three more by eight. A kettle belonging to a woman of twenty-seven, on since half past seven, for a reason she has never given.**

*The woman of about thirty, as an object and not as a case.* **Arrived between half past seven and eight, unlet in by anybody, not on a rota, not on a course, not referred, with a carrier bag containing two things that stayed on her lap for the whole of the visit and neither of which ever came out of it, one of them a supermarket card she looked at about nine times. Stated her own reason in about eleven seconds, unprompted, and the reason is that it was raining and the room was warm and she had thought it was a letting agent's office with the lights on. Stated a second true thing about herself at about twenty minutes in: that a person who has been in a room for twenty minutes and been given a cup of tea has not thought anything, and that a person who wants somebody to have thought something will be disappointed, and that she would rather say it in the first minute than the fortieth. **Asked for one thing in the whole of the visit and got it, and it was a cup.** Left at about half past nine, unaccompanied, through a door that no longer sticks. Not asked anything, not asked to sign anything, not recruited, not given a name, and appears later in this volume as the person who was there when there was nothing in it.**

*The bell-push, as an object, and the repair.* **A brass thing on a short stalk about four inches to the left of the door, present since the Monday, not connected to anything, and on a company's schedule as a defect twice a year for about four years with a number each time and about eleven of those numbers marked closed. On this Friday a man of about forty-one from that company's own list fitted a second push, about nine inches from the first, with two-core and a small transformer, in about forty minutes.** **It rang. A sounder on the wall by the counter, audible in all four rooms and from the service road. It was tested from outside only, because from outside is where a person is when they press it, and nobody was inside, and the card says *external push, sounder added, tested from outside only* and that is a true sentence. A sounder makes a noise at a man on a step and does not make anybody come, and about four thousand of those decisions are made in this city every day and there is no form for one of them. This entry records the repair as correct, done by a man who is not a villain, and changing nothing, and it is the fifth such act in ten chapters.**

*The empty page, as a decision and not as an omission.* **Two items in a ring binder in a first-year's own hand and about nine people through the door in eleven days. She was asked whether to write the twenty minutes down and put the pen down and gave the reason at length and not as a rule: that the day a woman who came in wet gets written down, the binder has a category in it, and the category would be the thing she has been trying to get for two years, and she would be having it because somebody turned up on a Friday and not because anybody came to be taught; and that in about four years the only sentence anybody would have is that the room takes anybody, which is a word doing a job it cannot do, describing people who were not there. The page was left empty. The cook agreed. Nobody argued. The man of twenty-two wrote nothing either, which is the third time in a term and a half and the first in a term before this.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **No attendance, no number of visitors, no list of anybody who came, and no category; the empty page is the whole of that record and it is empty. No name on the front of anything, including the leaflet, which is still in a folder on a counter and is a different object from the board by the door and from the list that is not on the wall yet. No fourth condition, no standing problem and no number on one. No share, no relay, no practice, and nobody taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything: the plane, the second bell-push and the tea are three things done by three people who were not asked to and are not recorded anywhere, and the tea is the only one of the three anybody in this case would call a technique and nobody in the room used the word. The card in a rail is fifteen days old and is not mentioned. Entry 210.**
