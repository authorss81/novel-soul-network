# Chapter 204 — Eleven Jobs

The docket on a Friday has eleven jobs on it and about nine of them are in date, and the docket is a piece of paper about eleven inches long and it has been the same length since the winter before last and nobody has ever asked to make it longer.

He got to the depot at about twenty to eight on the Friday morning of week sixty-eight and the man of fifty-two who manages it had already done the thing he stopped doing in the spring, which is stand at the board and read the five jobs off the front of a leaflet, and he did not do it.

The fifth job on the docket was in a residential block in Nine Bridges on the fourth floor and it was a shared lighting circuit that drops out at about six in the evening, and it has dropped out at about six in the evening for as long as anybody on that floor has been there.

---

The first four were ordinary and took until about eleven.

A stair light in a block off a service road that had been left on all night and that a man of about thirty-eight in a district jacket had already switched off at the isolator and written on his own job sheet, so there was nothing to do and it took nine minutes to do nothing. A door closer on a fire door in a building in Crown Terrace that was being adjusted for the fourth time in six weeks by three different contractors who did not know about each other, and the fourth adjustment was right, and the man who had written the fourth job sheet did not know it was the fourth. A lamp ballast in a stairwell, which took two hours and a half and which he had done four times.

A tap in a canteen off a service road that had a washer out and a stopcock that needed nine turns and a half more than it got.

The word was at nine a minute all morning. He did not write it down. He has never written it down and he is not going to and the reason is that a docket is a document and he does not know what a document is for after the fourth year of it.

---

The fifth job was the fourth floor.

He has been up that stair about nine times since the spring and he has never been up it on a Friday, because Fridays are grade three and the fourth floor's fault is a lighting circuit and lighting circuits are grade two, and a system chose which of the two he was on and neither of them chose him.

There is a man on that floor of thirty-four who works nights and who has been on the same rota for eleven years and who counts to three out loud before he answers anything, and it takes about two seconds, and there are four people on that floor and all four of them are on the Friday list because their employer pays and not because anybody chose them.

The man of thirty-four was not there. It was about a quarter to twelve and he works nights and he was asleep, and one of the four was, and it was a woman of about twenty-six who had a card of her own in her wallet with sixteen Fridays written on the back of it in a hand that got smaller towards the end.

"You want the cupboard," she said. "It is the one by the lift."

---

It took about forty minutes.

It is a lighting circuit, and the fault is a ballast in a cupboard that is the size of a bread bin, and the man of thirty-four showed him where it was eleven weeks ago by writing it on the back of his own hand with a biro and then washing it off, which is the only method anybody on that floor has found for passing information in a building where about nine people have keys to a cupboard.

Marek Senn did the whole thing and it worked the first time and he signed the docket, and while he had the cupboard open he counted the tubes, because he counts, and there were nine working and one not, and the one that was not had a new fitting in it about four months ago, and the new fitting is the fault, and it is on a different contractor's order, and the order is correct.

He wrote that on the docket. He did not write anybody's name on the docket. He wrote *see order, third party* in the box where a job gets described, and *third party* is a thing that about nine thousand job sheets in this city contain and it means a company whose name is on an invoice somewhere and which is not the company that is coming.

Then he shut the cupboard and the woman of twenty-six said, "That is that then," and he said it was, and she said, "We are not going to say anything."

"You do not have to say anything."

"**We are not going to say anything anyway,**" she said. "**I have been on this floor four years and I have watched about nine men come up that stair and not one of them has ever said the thing, and I have said the thing to this floor about nine times, and it has never once cost anybody anything to hear it, and I have got better at saying it and worse at believing it.**"

---

He did not ask her what the thing was, and that is the correct decision and it is also the reason there is a card in a rail in Lattice Ward with his name on it, and he made it in about a second and a half without thinking about it, which is the whole of what a person gets from a term and a half of this.

He said instead: "About eleven people a week get taught that a release waits for a man. That's across about four of your employers. It's not on any form. I know the number because a woman of twenty-nine has it on the back of her own card and she has never given it to anybody."

"Is that you saying it?"

"That is a man of twenty-two saying a number a woman of twenty-nine gave him on a landing once," he said. "**And it is not a rate, and it is not evidence, and if anybody asks you whether you are one of the eleven, the true answer is that you have never been asked whether you are.**"

Nobody had asked her. She looked at the shut cupboard for a while.

"Well," she said. "Now somebody has said it."

---

At about one the word went from nine to ten and stayed at ten for the rest of the day, and he noticed it at about one and there was nothing to be done about it then or at any other time, and he has never told anybody and is not going to.

The sixth and seventh jobs were a lift call and a lift call. The eighth job was a fault on a fire door in the same block as the third one and the door was on a closer that a different contractor had put in in the spring, and the fault was a strip of plastic about four inches long that had been fitted between the leaf and the frame, and it was not a fault, it was a packer, and it was doing what a packer does, and he took it out and put the closer back and the fault came back in about nine minutes, and he put the packer back in and signed the docket and wrote *not reproducible* in the box.

That is a correct action, it changed nothing, and it is the fourth one in four days, and nobody in this case has any way of counting them except a man with a docket.

The ninth, tenth and eleventh jobs were a light in a stair, a light in a stair, and a light in a stair.

---

At about twenty past ten that night he was on the fourth floor of that block again with a bag of two lamps, because the ninth, tenth and eleventh jobs had been on a landing and a landing and a landing and he had come back up to leave one of them where a man of thirty-four had asked for it eleven weeks ago by washing a biro off his own hand.

The man of thirty-four was awake and coming off nights and he was in the doorway of his own flat with his coat still on.

"You're the Friday one," he said.

"That's me."

"Two. Two. Right." He did not ask him in and he did not go out, and he stood in the doorway about nine seconds and then he said a sentence that had been sitting in him since the spring and that he had said out loud on that floor nine times and had never once said to the man it was about.

"**I have been telling four people on this floor for a term and a half that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and I have never once said your name, and if you went under a lorry on that service road tomorrow the sentence would come out of my mouth on Friday exactly the same, and I have known that since about the fourth week and I have not done anything about it, and I have been counting to three about four hundred times since the spring and this is the first thing I have said out loud after a two.**"

Marek Senn stood on a landing with a bag of two lamps in it.

"Right," he said.

"That's it, that's the whole of it, I have been wanting to say it to somebody who is not one of the four and there is nobody else who comes up here."

"Can I take the lamp out of the bag and put it in the cupboard."

"You can do what you like with the lamp," said the man of thirty-four. "**I have never wanted a lamp. I have wanted about four minutes and I have had them and I am going to go and sleep.**"

---

*207. Friday of week 68, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a fourth floor in Nine Bridges, a man of thirty-four, and a sentence that had been sitting in him since the spring.*

*Eleven jobs, as a count and not as a rate.* **Eleven on the docket, nine in date, grade three from eight in the morning. Two of the eleven were a fire door adjusted for the fourth time in six weeks by three contractors who did not know about each other and the fourth adjustment was right. One was a lamp ballast done for the fourth time. One was a stair light that a man of about thirty-eight in a district jacket had already switched off at the isolator and written on his own sheet. One was a tap. Two were lift calls. Three were lights on landings, which is nine out of eleven jobs on a Friday being a light or a fire door, and that is a fact about the job sheet and not about anybody.**

*The fourth floor, as a set of facts and not as a case.* **Four people on the Friday list because their employer pays and not because anybody chose them. A man of thirty-four, eleven years on the same rota, who counts to three out loud before he answers anything and has counted about four hundred times since the spring. A woman of about twenty-six with sixteen Fridays written on the back of her own card in a hand that got smaller towards the end, who said the thing to that floor about nine times and has got better at saying it and worse at believing it. One lighting circuit in a cupboard the size of a bread bin, dropping out at about six every evening for as long as anybody on that floor has been there. Nine working tubes and one not, and the one that is not has a third-party fitting on somebody else's order from about four months ago, and the order is correct. He wrote *see order, third party* in the box where a job gets described and wrote no name.**

*Three correct actions that changed nothing, on one Friday, and this entry counts them because nobody in this case can.* **A fault that was not reproducible because a four-inch plastic packer was doing what a packer does, taken out and put back. A third-party order referred to rather than fixed. And a Friday in which nobody asked a man of twenty-two where a release was going to come from, which has now been true for a term and a half and about nine times, and which nobody is ever going to ask.**

*The sentence, as a sentence, and its shape.* **A man of thirty-four said to a man of twenty-two in a doorway on a landing, in about nine seconds and after a count of two: he has told four people on that floor for a term and a half that a release waits for a man in a lanyard; he has never once said that man's name; and if that man were killed on a service road the next day the sentence would come out of his mouth on Friday exactly the same, and he has known that since about the fourth week and has done nothing about it. He said he wanted about four minutes and had had them and was going to sleep. The number eleven a week was given to a woman of about twenty-six in this chapter by the man of twenty-two, once, out loud, as a number another person gave him on a landing, with the two denials attached to it: it is not a rate, and it is not evidence, and nobody had ever asked her whether she was one of the eleven. **She had not been asked, and she said so, and that is the whole of the consequence and it is not a resolution and it was not one to be had on a Friday.** Nobody was told about the number on that floor and it is on no sheet and it is not in a system and no form anywhere has it.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **No practice run, no share, no relay, no demonstration, and nobody taught, assessed or assisted anything. No number attached to the standing problem and no fourth condition quoted. No share of his own and nobody else's. No name on the front of anything: the docket carried a job number, a description, a third-party note, a *not reproducible* and a signature, and nothing else, and the man of thirty-four's own method of writing a cupboard location on the back of his own hand and washing it off is not a record and is the reason a floor of four people knows where a ballast is. Entry 207.**
