# Chapter 208 — Somebody On The Other Side

A man of fifty-eight came into a room off a service road in the Lower Wards on the Monday morning of week seventy with four steps of a thing he does for a living, and the fourth of them was a person, and the person was not there.

He had a flat cap and a folded sheet of paper with a bus timetable on the back of it and he had been on the same round for thirty-one years and he is an inspector, and there are about nine of them in this city and about four hundred people have been told by one of them to wait.

---

He waited until about eleven, which he did not say he was going to do.

There were three people in the room: a woman of twenty-seven at the counter with a knife and a board, a first-year of nineteen with a ring binder open on the counter, and a man of twenty-two with a docket he had brought and put down.

"You are not on the list," said Asha Reed.

"I know I am not on the list. **I have never been on a list in my life. I am the reason a list exists.**"

He put the timetable on the counter, turned it round so it was the right way up for the people on that side, and put his thumb on the fourth step of a thing he had written out in biro on the back of it in about nine lines.

"**Get a door that has dropped into its own seal. Do not force it, you will take the hinge off. Two hands on the leaf, low, at the hinge side. Three, take the weight of it onto your hip and let it find itself. Four, somebody on the far side takes it off the seal and walks it open.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"That is four steps," said Asha Reed.

"That is four steps and there are only three of them in that room," said the man of fifty-eight. "**I have been doing that since I was twenty-seven and it has never once worked with two people in the same room as the door, and the fourth step is not a step, and I have known that since the first year I was allowed to work without somebody standing next to me.**"

---

Dessa Kwan put the knife down, which she does not do in the middle of anything.

"Go on," she said.

"I will not do it here because I will do it wrong and you will watch me do it wrong, and a man of fifty-eight doing a thing badly in a room in front of a first-year is the only thing in this room that nobody can put back afterwards." He put his thumb on the fourth line again. "**And I want it written down and I do not know how to write it, and that is why I have been sitting here for forty minutes with a bus timetable.**"

---

**And that was the first time anybody said the rule out loud in that room, and it was not said by the man of twenty-two and it was not a rule anybody had decided on.**

Asha Reed put her pen on the page.

"Right. Say it again and I will write it exactly like that."

"Get a door that has dropped into its own seal. Do not force it, you will take the hinge off. Two hands on the leaf, low, at the hinge side. Three, take the weight of it onto your hip and let it find itself. Somebody on the far side takes it off the seal and walks it open."

"**In your words.**"

"That is my words."

"That is four of your words and a half," said Asha Reed, "and the last one is not in your voice at all, it is in mine, and I have been on a course for two years about how to be careful about a thing like that, and I am telling you the difference and I am telling you what it costs. **If I write that down, then in about four years the fourth step is a line on a page, and the line on a page says *somebody on the far side*, and *somebody on the far side* is a man in a lanyard, and I have read a leaflet with five jobs on the front of it and the fifth one has always been a man in a lanyard, and it is never the same man twice, and it is never anybody in the room.**"

---

**That is the cost, and he gave it out loud, and he gave it before anybody asked him for anything, and it is the reason he had brought a timetable.**

"I have watched it go wrong once," said the man of fifty-eight. "**That is not the year I started and I am not going to give you the year, because the year is the only part of it that a person can check and it is the part that is wrong. A lad of nineteen on my round had written his four steps out in a book because he was proud of them, and a man came off another route and read it, and within about two years that depot had a poster with three steps on it and a photograph of a man in a lanyard at the bottom, and the fourth step had come off it entirely, and they were not doing it wrong. They were doing it three-quarters of the thing, faster, and it worked, and about eleven people a week went on waiting for a man, and the fourth step did not go missing in that depot. It went missing in about four hundred depots and there was no man standing in any of them who knew which one they had lost it in.**"

He put his cap back on.

"That is why I have a timetable and not a book. A timetable goes out of date on a Sunday and nobody has ever been proud of a timetable."

---

**And then a line cook of twenty-seven said nine words, and she said them once, and she did not improve them, and she did not explain them afterwards, and the man of fifty-eight did not argue.**

"**You have taught a door to need a person.**"

He stood in a room off a service road for about four seconds and did not say anything, and then he said: "**That is the whole of it and I have not got a better one, and I have had about forty minutes with a timetable to get one.**"

"No you have not."

"No," he said. "**And I am not going to argue with a cook about a door.**"

---

Nobody wrote it down. Asha Reed shut the binder and said that she was not going to write the three steps either, and gave the reason in a sentence: **that the three steps do not belong to this room unless the fourth one is in it, and a page with three quarters of a thing on it is the leaflet, and the leaflet is in about four thousand pockets in this city and it is on the front of it in five jobs and none of them is a room.**

The cook said: "Then it is not a thing you brought me, it is a thing you brought the fourth step to, and the fourth step is not here."

The man of fifty-eight said: "Then I will take it back to the depot and it will be four steps on a timetable in my coat pocket for thirty-one years, and about nine people in this city know it and none of them know it is the same one."

---

**And then the schedule came round, and it is the sixth correct action in ten chapters that changed nothing and it is the one with the most people in the story.**

A woman of about twenty-six came in at about half past twelve with a folder, and she works for the company whose number is on the plate outside, and the folder was a schedule of items for that unit and it is done twice a year and it is a true document.

The bell-push was on it again.

**It was on it as a defect, with a new number, because the item on the schedule is the item on the schedule.** The item reads *external push, serviceable*. It has read that since a man fitted a sounder in about forty minutes on the Friday three days ago, and the sounder is on the wall and it works, and nobody has changed the wording of the item, because a schedule is not a document anybody improves and the man who fills it in the boxes has eleven other units and about four hundred other items.

The woman of about twenty-six said, out loud, in the room, to nobody in particular: "**It is the same number twice. I have written the same number twice on two different days and both of them were true on the day.**"

Nobody said anything about it, and she went back to the folder, and the folder is the eleventh one and it is the first one anybody in this case has seen in a building with a plate on the outside of it.

---

At about four the man of fifty-eight was on a step outside in the rain and Marek Senn went out with him, which he had not planned to do.

"You are going to ask me whether I will be here on a Wednesday," said the man of fifty-eight.

"Go on."

"**I will not be here on a Wednesday, and I am not going to be here on a Monday either, and there is a round that starts at four in the morning and a room that shuts at ten at night, and those two things have never met in this city and they are not going to meet for me.**" He got his cap further back. "**But I will be at the market on a Wednesday, because there is a trestle in that market with an amplifier on it and about forty people go past it in an hour, and I have been past it about nine times and never stopped.**"

"That is not a reason to come to a room."

"It is not a reason to come to a room," the man of fifty-eight agreed. "**It is a reason to be in a sentence somebody has already printed.**"

He walked off up the service road and the sheet of paper with the timetable on the back of it went into his inside pocket, and it went back in the way that a thing goes back in that is going to come out again in about nine weeks, and it does, and it is different by then.

---

The room shut at ten and Marek Senn ate in a canteen off a service road where the tickets are on a spike and the tea comes in a jug, and about four people were in it and none of them knew anybody else, and the whole of what week seventy gave that room was eleven items in a cook's hand and nine words with the wrong door in them.

*211. Monday of week 70, ten at night in a canteen off a service road, with tickets on a spike, and this entry is four steps of which three are portable and one is not, and a schedule with the same number on it twice.*
