# Chapter 210 — What The Room Will Not Do

On the Thursday evening of week seventy a piece of hardboard about the size of a tea tray went on the wall inside the front room of a converted shop unit off a service road in the Lower Wards, under a board with four words on it in a sign-writer's hand, and it is not screwed to anything, and there are about nine lines on it and six people wrote them and a seventh person was in the room and was not asked.

That is the only document the Practice Commons has and it is not enforceable against anybody who did not write it.

---

Two things had happened in that room in nine days and neither of them was planned.

**The first was a woman of about thirty who had been in on the Friday, and who came back on the Tuesday, and again on the Wednesday, and again on the Thursday, and on the Thursday she had been in the room for about six hours across three visits and she had not asked for anything on any of the three days and had not been asked for anything on any of the three days.**

**The second was a market.** Eleven people stopped at a trestle in Saltmarket on the Wednesday and about four of the eleven were spoken to and about seven ate a piece of something and went on their way, and a leaflet went out with a first-year of nineteen's name at the bottom of it in her own hand, and by Thursday morning a woman had telephoned the number on the plate outside the unit and had been put through to a letting clerk of thirty-nine who has said the same sentence about a vacant frontage to four sets of people and to a fifth set who did not know that was what she was doing.

Nobody had sent anybody anywhere. **The room had acquired a person in it and a market had acquired a sentence, and both of those things had happened the way a leak happens.**

---

Asha Reed said the wall was needed and gave the reason in one go before anybody could agree with her.

"**On Tuesday a woman of thirty sat in a chair for two hours and nobody could tell me afterwards whether she had been here or not, and on Wednesday about nine people who do not know us got a piece of food off a table with a leaflet on it. Those are two different rooms and they are about four feet apart and nobody in this city has ever written down a rule about either of them, and the only instrument anybody has ever found for a thing like that is a wall.**"

"And a wall is not a form," said Ianthe Roke.

"No," said Asha Reed. "**A wall is less than a form. That is the entire argument and I have had it twice this week and both times I lost.**"

---

**And then they wrote them, one at a time, out loud, and a line was not a line until the person who had lived it said it.**

The first one was the cook's and it was the first thing on the wall and it is the sentence the whole of this volume is standing on, and she said it with her hands in a bowl of water and did not stop what she was doing to say it.

"**It will not keep anything it cannot take back.**"

Somebody said that was the obvious one and the cook said the obvious one is the one everybody agrees to in a room and this is the one that is true.

Nine lines went on the piece of hardboard in about an hour and a half and about eleven of them were argued about and the nine are what survived. **Two are in the hand of a woman of twenty-seven: that it will not keep anything it cannot take back, and that it will not be warm only when one of us is in it.** Two are in the hand of a first-year of nineteen: that it will not say that anybody has got better at anything, and that it will not put a promise on a wall. Two are in the hand of a woman of thirty-one with a diary and a key: that it will not be the only place a person has to come, and that it will not be open because one person in it decided to open it. One is in the hand of a man of fifty-eight who inspects buses: that it will not keep a copy of a thing nobody asked to keep. One is in the hand of a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts: that it will not ask anybody to come back. And one is in the hand of a printer of fifty-eight with a shop four feet wide, who asked out loud who was going to print it, and who wrote **it will not be printed** and then said the reason for that line out loud to about six people, and it was a hundred and forty-one pounds in an envelope under her till that has no name on it and cannot be sent back and has been there since the spring.

---

**And the person who was not asked is a woman of about thirty who was in the room for about six hours across three days.**

Asha Reed said the reason out loud in the room, which she did on purpose, and it is in the entry because she said it in front of people.

"**You are not on this and the reason is that you came in to sit down, and if I ask you to sign a thing about a room then the wall has got a person on it who was in it because it was raining, and in about four years that is the only line anybody reads out.**"

The woman of about thirty did not take it as a slight and said the reason back, and she said it as though she were returning a cup.

"I came in because it was raining and it was warm and a woman made me a tea and did not ask me a single thing. **If that goes on a wall then in about four years somebody is going to say that this room takes anybody, and I am the anybody, and I was here for a wet Friday.**"

Nobody asked her twice and nobody argued and the pen went past her.

---

And then Marek Senn was asked to sign it, and he had written none of the nine, and he signed it with a pen off the counter, and he is the seventh name on a piece of hardboard and he is the only one anybody can find.

**He said the reason out loud, once, and it was not a reason anybody improved, and there were about six people in that room and the woman of about thirty was one of them.**

"**A card went into a rail in Lattice Ward on the Thursday of week sixty-seven with my name on it in my own hand and a week at the bottom left and nothing on the back of it, and that is the last thing I had after a term and a half of not being findable, and an officer of a fund of forty-four told a room that the reason I gave for it did not help, and he was right.**" He put the pen down. "**So I am the only one of these seven that anybody in this city has learned how to find in about six weeks. The other six are at a counter, a shop four feet wide, a depot, a first floor with a key in it and a kitchen. You would have to already know them. If my name is not on this wall then somebody has to be in this room every day of the week, and I have not met the person who does that, and neither has the woman with the ring file, and she has said so to about five sets of people in nine days.**"

Somebody said that was a reason and he said: "**It is a reason and it is the only thing I have got to put on a wall, and I would like it noted that what I have just done is put my name somewhere findable on purpose, in a room with a door and a bell-push in it, and that I did the not-being-findable for a term and a half and this is the bill, and the bill has come due in a building in the Lower Wards instead of a corridor in Crown Terrace and I did not choose which.**"

---

Nobody thanked him for the six weeks and nobody asked him what a name on a wall is for, and about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and about four of the eleven are not even on a rota, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card and is not on a form and is not a rate.

**A rack is not a rota, and a name on a wall by a door is not a rota either, and nobody in that room said otherwise.**

---

And then the last thing said in that room in week seventy was said by a first-year of nineteen to nobody, with a ring binder under her arm, and it was the finding and she gave it away like something that had fallen out of a pocket.

"**It is much smaller than we wanted it to be. I have been in this room nine days and there are four chairs in the back room and two things in that binder and nine lines on a wall and one of us is on nine Fridays a week across four employers, and every single thing in here is a thing that one person did on purpose and nobody was made to.**" She shut the binder. "**I thought it was going to be bigger. I want that written down somewhere, and I am not writing it on the wall, because it is not a thing the room will not do.**"

---

He got back to the shop in Lattice Ward at ten on the Thursday night. The lamp was on over the bench, the docket had four job numbers on it in a shorthand nobody else in this city can read, and the drawer underneath still held five things, four of which are sentences other people wrote. **On the chair at the back was a coat, and in the inside pocket of it was a corner of card about two inches by three, and the original had lain in the fourth pocket of the tool roll for a term and a half with nothing written on its fifth line.**

He wrote one thing down. It is a piece of hardboard about the size of a tea tray with nine lines on it in six hands and seven names, and it is not screwed to anything.

*213. Thursday of week 70, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is nine lines on a board, in six hands, and a seventh name.*

*The board, as an object, and the two objects on one wall kept apart.* **Hardboard, about the size of a tea tray, inside by the door, under a board four letters high in a sign-writer's hand that reads *Bring what you do* and can be taken off with a thumb. The list is not screwed to anything and neither is the board above it, and they are two objects with two owners and they are not to be put in a sentence with each other. Nine lines, six hands, no date, no number, no reference, no signature block, and nothing at the top. It is not a form, a minute, notice, report, committee paper, leaflet, registration or constitution. It is not enforceable against anybody who did not write it, it can be rubbed off, and it is not evidence of anything in any proceeding, and all four of those were said out loud in the room and the last of them by the printer.**

*What the room would not do, as a count of hands and not as a list of sentences.* **Six hands, nine lines, eleven proposed. Two from a cook of twenty-seven, and the first of the nine is hers and is the sentence this volume is standing on, and she said it with her hands in a bowl of water. Two from a first-year of nineteen, and the second of hers is the reason a first-year is in the room at all, which is that a promise is one of the two things people come into rooms for. Two from a woman of thirty-one with a diary and a key, and both of hers are about the room not becoming the only one, which is a finding made in a corridor in Crown Terrace in an earlier volume. One from a man of fifty-eight who inspects buses. One from a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts, and his is four words long. One from a printer of fifty-eight with a shop four feet wide, and the cost she named out loud in front of about six people for that one line being a hundred and forty-one pounds in an envelope under a till with no name on it that cannot be sent back and has been there since the spring. The nine lines are on the board and this entry does not copy them out a second time, because the board is the document and a record that repeats its own object is not a record.**

*The seventh person and the eighth name.* **A woman of about thirty was in the room for about six hours across three days, asked for nothing and asked nothing of, and was not asked to sign, and the reason was given out loud in front of about six people by the first-year and the reason was given back by the woman in her own words, and the chapter has it in full, and it is that a wall with her name on it is a line about a person who was in a room because it was raining. Nobody asked her twice and nobody argued and the pen went past her.** **A man of twenty-two wrote none of the nine and signed it, and said out loud, once, that he is the only one of the seven anybody in this city has learned how to find in about six weeks, that the other six are at a counter, a shop four feet wide, a depot, a first floor with a key in it and a kitchen and would have to be already known, and that if his name is not on the wall then somebody has to be in that room every day of the week and neither he nor a woman with a ring file has met the person who does that. He added that putting his name somewhere findable on purpose is the bill for a card in a rail, and that the bill came due in a room off a service road instead of a corridor and he did not choose which.**

*What the two unquiet facts were, and neither of them was planned.* **A woman of about thirty came back three times in nine days after coming once because it was raining, and on the last of them was in the room for about six hours in total and nobody can say afterwards what she did there. And on the Wednesday a trestle in a market had eleven people stop at it, about four spoken to, about seven took a piece of food and went on their way, a leaflet went out with a first-year's name on it, and on Thursday morning a woman telephoned the number on the plate outside the unit and was put through to a letting clerk who said a sentence about a vacant frontage to a fifth set of people who did not know that was what she was doing.** **The room acquired a person and a market acquired a sentence, and nobody sent anybody anywhere.**

*The last thing said in the room, and it is the finding.* **A first-year of nineteen, with a binder under her arm, said that the room is much smaller than they wanted it to be: four chairs in the back room, two things in a binder, nine lines on a wall, and one of them on nine Fridays a week across four employers, and every single thing in it a thing one person did on purpose and nobody was made to. She said she had thought it was going to be bigger, that she wanted it written down somewhere, and that she was not writing it on the wall because it is not a thing the room will not do. About eleven people a week are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, about four of the eleven are not on a rota, the number is on the back of a woman's own card, and a name on a wall by a door is not a rota and nobody in that room said otherwise.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **No minute of anything and no minute is taken of any of the nine lines; they were written and argued and four arguments are recorded in a room and not in a document. No attendance, no register, no list of anybody who has come, and no category. No share, no relay, no practice, and nobody taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything in this volume up to this point; a line that would have needed a man in a lanyard was refused with nine words in Chapter 208 and the refusal is not on the wall because it is not a thing the room will not do. The Exchange's book is at forty-four lines and has now not been opened at six sittings running, and the last Wednesday of that book was a fortnight before the market. The standing problem was said in this volume for the eighth time on the Wednesday, in a market, and is not resolved and is not said again. The card in a rail, the master in an inside pocket, the board on the wall, the binder, the leaflet, the lease, the schedule folder and the timetable in an inside pocket on a service road are nine objects with nine owners and no two of them appear in a sentence together in this entry. Entry 213, and the run is continuous from fifty and it does not end here.**
