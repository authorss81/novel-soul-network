# Chapter 201 — A Bell That Does Not Ring

The unit at the back of four rooms off a service road in the Lower Wards has a shutter, a counter, nine chairs, a ring binder on a shelf, a kettle, two plug sockets of which one works, and a bell-push by the door that does not ring by itself.

Nothing about it has been anybody's for four years. There is a plate on the outside of it with a letting agent's telephone number on it and no name, and the number is the number of a company, and the company is nobody's friend and nobody's problem and is not on any list anybody in this case is on.

---

Three people came at about half past nine on the Monday morning of week sixty-eight, and the fourth did not come until the Thursday, and the order of those two facts turned out to be the whole of the first week.

Marek Senn got there first because he had a van key habit and there was nowhere to park on that service road at all. Asha Reed came second with a folder she did not open. Dessa Kwan came third carrying a bag of her own tea, which she put in the plug socket that worked, and the plug socket that works is a thing about this room that will not be said again.

The woman with the key is thirty-nine and she is a lettings clerk and she has a ring file and a coat with the company's name off it.

"You have all read it," she said. "I want to say that first, so that nobody in this room can go home tonight and tell anybody I read it to you in a hurry, because I will be asked that in about a fortnight by somebody who is not in this room and I would rather not have said it at all."

"Nobody has read anything," said Asha Reed. "He told me it was four rooms and a shutter."

"Then that is what you have read," she said, and she put the file on the counter and did not open it. "**Everything else in it is a clause about what happens if the place is used for something the freeholder does not like, and there are nine of those and none of them is about this, and I am not going to read nine clauses at three people on a Monday morning.**"

---

Nobody wrote anything down.

That was the first thing that happened in that room and it happened at about ten past ten and it happened because Marek Senn had a pen out.

He had a docket in the left hand and a pen in the right and he had been standing in the front room with a number of things in his head that he wanted to put on the counter, and a woman of thirty-nine with a ring file said no.

"You are not writing that down."

"I am writing the chairs down."

"**I know which chairs you are writing down.** There are nine. I counted them when I opened the place and there were nine then and there are nine now and nobody has come in and taken one, which in this street in this weather is closer to a miracle than it sounds." She did not move her hands off the file. "**Put it this way. If four people write down what they decided in a room with a door in it, then in about four years that page is the room. Not the room — the page. And the page is a thing that can be carried, and a thing that can be carried is a thing that can be shown to somebody who was not in the room, and then the four of you are the four people who decided it, for ever, in a flat, with no way to say you were young.**"

"The chairs are chairs."

"The chairs are the example," she said. "**Nothing is wrong with the chairs. I have kept about four of these places for a company that has a name on the invoice and I have never once had this conversation in one of them, because in all four of them there was a person whose name was on the lease before there was a conversation.**"

---

So he put the pen away, and that was the first cost, and it was a small one, and he did not know then that it was going to be the shape of everything.

He said the true objection out loud anyway, because he has never in his life let a true thing go unsaid in a room, and it is the reason there is a card in a rail with his name on it.

"If nobody writes it down, it stops when the four of us stop coming."

"Yes," said the woman of thirty-nine.

"That is the whole of the argument for writing it down."

"It is one of the two arguments," she said. "**I have had the other one off a letting agent who did not know it was an argument, and his version was that his office had a fire in it in the spring and about nine boxes of paper went out, and he was the only one who knew which boxes, and it was his own handwriting.**"

Nobody argued with her, and Asha Reed wrote nothing down, and Dessa Kwan put the kettle on.

---

The tea was the first thing anybody in that room made for another person, and it was made in a kettle that belongs to a woman of twenty-seven who brought it in a bag, and about eleven minutes later there were four mugs on a counter and one of them had a chip in the handle.

That is the whole of what happened on the Monday.

---

They walked it, because a room cannot be understood by four people talking about it in the middle of it. The front room took nine chairs and a counter and nothing else. The second room had a window that faced a wall about four feet away, and a light that came on. The third had a floor that was not level and a smell of cold plaster. The back room had a door that stuck, and it stuck about a foot into the room, and there was nothing behind it, and the man of twenty-two put his shoulder into it twice and it gave and he said, without deciding to, that it wanted a planer taken off the top edge and nothing else.

"That is your trade talking," said Asha Reed.

"Yes."

"Then write it on the job sheet and not on a piece of paper about the room," she said, and she did not say it unkindly, and she had a folder under her arm and she still had not opened it.

---

At about eleven the woman of thirty-nine put her ring file under her arm and said the thing she had come to say, which is that she is not a landlord and there is no landlord in the room.

"**The freehold is an entry on a schedule of title. I can describe the entry to you. I cannot describe the person who owns the entry, because I have never met them and I have never been introduced and I did not ask, because when I asked in my second year a manager explained to me for about nine minutes why asking was the same as not renewing, and he was right about the second half of it.**"

"Is there anything wrong with the place."

"Four things," she said, and she held up four fingers without looking at them. "**It has been empty four years. The frontage of this row has been empty on this side for eleven years and there is a plan in a council file about what happens to a frontage that has been empty eleven years, and I have read it and I am not going to tell you what is in it. Nobody has done anything about it in four years. The first year is at no rent and there is no second year price on the page.**"

"Then why is it free?"

"Because the frontage has been empty," said the woman of thirty-nine. "**That is the whole of the reason I have been given and I have given it to four sets of people and two of them are the ones I can name.**"

She looked at the door when she said it, and the bell-push is four inches to the left of it, and it is a brass thing on a short stalk and it has never been connected to anything and it is not a fault, and nobody in that room fixed it, and nobody in that room will.

---

It was ten at night on the Monday, and the bench in the shop in Lattice Ward had a lamp over it and the shutter down, and the docket was clean, because a day spent standing in a room with a plug socket and a door that sticks does not produce a job number.

The word had been at nine all afternoon and at ten by the time he sat down.

He wrote down one thing, because he writes things down.

*204. Monday of week 68, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is four rooms off a service road, nine chairs, a counter, a ring binder on a shelf, two plug sockets of which one works, a bell-push that is not connected to anything, a window onto a wall about four feet away, a floor that is not level, and a door that wants about a millimetre off the top edge and nothing else.*

*The room, as an inventory and not as an offer.* **Four rooms and a back one. Nine chairs, counted twice on the same morning by two people who did not know each other were counting. A counter. A ring binder with nothing in it. A kettle belonging to the woman of twenty-seven. A plate on the outside carrying a company's telephone number and no name. A freehold that is an entry on a schedule of title. A first year at no rent, no second-year price on the page, a vacant frontage of eleven years in one direction and a plan in a council file that the clerk read and would not describe, and four years empty.**

*Why there is nothing in the ring binder.* **A letting clerk of thirty-nine said it out loud at about ten past ten and she was not asked. Her sentence in her own construction: four people writing down what they decided in a room with a door in it means that in about four years the page is the room and not the room, and the page can be carried, and a page that can be carried can be shown to somebody who was not in the room, and the four of them are then the four people who decided it, in a flat, with no way to say they were young. He put the pen away in about four seconds. It is the first cost of this room and it is not the largest and it set the shape of the week.** The counter-argument was made by him and was one sentence long — that if nobody writes it down it stops when the four of them stop coming — and she agreed with it, and gave the other half, which was a fire in the spring in her own office and about nine boxes of paper and one man's handwriting, and then put her file down without opening it. **The chairs are chairs and she said so and this entry does not treat the chairs as evidence of anything.**

*Two figures this entry does not use.* **The freehold is an entry and the person who owns the entry was not described, was not named, and is not a character. The letting clerk is thirty-nine, has a ring file and a coat with a company's name off it, has said the same true sentence about a vacant frontage to four previous sets of people of whom two can be named, and is not thanked and is not going to be. She is a person with a job and a key and she is not a post and nothing in this entry makes her one.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **No lease, no clause, no term and no price, because the lease is Chapter 205 and this entry is the room and the two are different objects with different owners. No key, because no key is handed over in this chapter and the first one is Chapter 206's problem. No name over the door, no board, and no list on a wall: those are Chapters 206 and 210. No card in a rail — the card went into the rail on the Thursday of week 67, four days ago, and it is a third object and it is not the master and the master is not a card. No fourth condition, no standing problem, no number attached to either, and the standing problem is not said in this chapter. No share, no relay, no practice and no page implying one is available. Nobody taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything, in this room or anywhere else, and the room off a service road is not a practice in that department and the reason is not that he is an exception. Nobody's name is on the front of anything in this entry, including his own, and that is the chapter's event and the last of Volume 04's is four days old.**

*Where the next entry starts, in one line, and it is not a document.* **Four people have been in a room with a door and a bell-push and one of them has a folder she has not opened, and on Wednesday a woman of sixty-one will not open a book for the sixth time running, and a first-year of nineteen is going to say a thing about a room in about nine words.**
