**The counting rule, with its word lists, so that a later writer can reproduce it mechanically. This is the corrected rule and it is the only statement of it in the repository.** One hedge per occurrence of *about*, whole word, **case-insensitive**, followed by an optional article (*a*, *an*, *the*) and then **either** (A) **a number word, alone or in a chain joined by a hyphen or a single space** — *one two three four five six seven eight nine ten eleven twelve thirteen fourteen fifteen sixteen seventeen eighteen nineteen twenty thirty forty fifty sixty seventy eighty ninety hundred thousand million* — **or** (B) **one counted quantity** — *half dozen couple fortnight fortnights second seconds minute minutes hour hours day days week weeks month months year years room rooms form forms card cards word words line lines page pages field fields step steps part parts premise premises fold folds pound pounds penny pence job jobs hand hands name names face faces sheet sheets chair chairs seat seats item items entry entries copy copies place places building buildings door doors column columns packet packets envelope envelopes invoice invoices time times people premises men women woman document documents share shares case cases printer printers quarter size thing things*. A possessive form is excluded. Count each occurrence once. Search the whole file, load book included. **The chain must not be permitted to match empty.** A bare number word is sufficient: *about nine* is a hedge and *about nine places* is one hedge. **Word count: split on whitespace with any line consisting only of three or more hyphens removed, and em-dashes left attached to their token.** **Bold: `\*\*(?!\s)(.+?)(?<!\s)\*\*`.**

**Bold emphasis. Hold the batch average at or under 10.75 per thousand and every chapter at or under 12.4.** These are **bold** ceilings and only the bold figure is measured against them; say so in the summary rather than letting a reader assume all three are governed. A bold span marks the beat inside a person's speech; **the elaboration after a speech tag loses the emphasis and the lead-in keeps it, and no line may carry a bold span on both sides of one speech tag**; in a load-book line the first and the last span always survive. Do not use a mechanical longest-span rule across a paragraph. **Nothing in the narration is bolded at all.** **Movement V of Volume 04 finished at 157 and 6.03 with a worst chapter of 8.33, and the order worth keeping is to enforce the rule as the chapters are drafted rather than sweeping afterwards.**

The backwards sweep's method, verbatim, because it is the one that works: **whitespace-collapse both sides, slide an eighty-character window over the new chapter, call it a hit for an earlier chapter when that exact string occurs in it, and define a maximal run as a maximal span over which every consecutive window is a hit for the *same* earlier chapter, its length being that span plus the seventy-nine characters of window overlap the span does not cover.
