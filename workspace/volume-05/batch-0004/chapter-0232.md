# Chapter 232 — A Man Of Thirty-Seven With A Sheet Of Paper

The man who came to that room on the Monday morning of week eighty was a man of thirty-seven with a job and a sheet of a company's paper in his hand, and he was owed nothing and nobody in that room thanked him, and the reason he had come in person instead of telephoning was a sentence about money that was true and that nobody in that room could answer.

The room was eighty-four days old. The two newest lines on the hardboard were four days old and about nine people a week had read one of them and not the other.

---

He came in at about ten past ten on a Monday, and he was not early and he was not late, and he had a coat on that he took off before he had decided whether he was staying, which is a thing people do.

He is thirty-seven. He administers property for a company that has an entry in a schedule of title on a row of service roads in the Lower Wards, and that entry describes this unit and describes nobody, and he has held that job for six years and signs the line that moves money under it.

He had a sheet of that company's paper in his hand. It is not a form. It is not a notice, a report, a minute, a registration or a constitution, and there is no person's name on it, and he put it face down on the counter when he came in and did not turn it over.

Nobody in that room has seen it.

Asha Reed asked him what it was for, because she asks everybody that.

"You have got it the wrong way round," he said. "**I have not come to give you something. I have come because there is a man coming on Thursday and I have signed the paper he is going to read out, and I wanted to have stood in this room first.**"

---

And then he said the true thing, and he said it first, and nobody had asked him for anything at all.

"**There is no money in this room.**"

The cook of twenty-seven had her hands in a sink and did not turn round.

"**I have been in here eleven minutes and I have looked at a shutter, a counter, nine chairs, a window, a kettle, a bell-push and a piece of hardboard, and there is no money in any of it, and I have looked at the floor as well, and there is no money in the floor. And the first year of that unit is free, and it is the only free year anybody in this row has ever had, and I signed the paper that says so, and I have signed it every year for six years, and I have never once been told why.**"

"Have you asked."

"**Twice. Once in the spring of last year and once in the middle of the summer, and both times I was told it is a lease matter, and a lease matter is a ring file, and the ring file is in another building and the woman who has it is not unkind to me.**"

Somebody said, "That is a real answer."

"**It is a real answer and it is not an answer**," said the man of thirty-seven, "**and I want to say the next part before anybody in this room gets pleased with the first part. I have not come here to be helpful. I have come because in about four weeks somebody in a building I have never been inside is going to look at that row of service roads and find a unit that has been open twelve weeks and has cost nothing, and I am not going to be the person who is asked about it, because I have already asked twice and I have already been told twice that it is a lease matter, and I do not know what I would say.**"

---

Nobody in that room could answer him, and about four people in that room tried in different ways over the next half hour and none of it worked, and the reason it did not work is the reason this whole movement is about.

They could not answer because the only person in this city who knows why that year is free has said the same true sentence about a vacant frontage to five sets of people and is not thanked, and holds a ring file in another building, and is not in the room, and has not been asked, and will not be asked.

A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip said: "**I have been coming to this room for about twenty-five days and I have learned one thing and it is that a room will not keep a thing whose words are somebody else's. I cannot tell you why your year is free because that is a thing whose words are somebody else's, and I do not know the somebody.**"

"That is not an answer either," said the man of thirty-seven.

"**No. It is a person telling you he has got nothing, which is the first time in about twenty-five days that anybody in here has said that to anybody and it cost him nothing, because he has nothing to give.**"

---

And then the cook of twenty-seven asked him the question that turned the morning, and she asked it from the sink, and it was not about the year.

"**What is the paper for.**"

"**It is the thing he is going to read out on Thursday. It is one sheet. It has a figure on it and it has five lines on it and there is no name on it and there is not going to be a name on it, and I have read it about nine times and I am not going to read it out in a room before he does.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because if I read it out then it is a thing this room has been told before he walks in, and then it is not the same meeting, and he came on a Monday to a room that had not been primed, and that is the only thing I have organised.**"

Nobody had thanked him for organising that either.

---

The man of fifty-one with a tool bag was in the room for about forty minutes and said almost nothing, and what he said he had said in another room three weeks before, so nobody argued with him about it.

The first-year of nineteen asked one question, near the end, which was: "**If a company has money for this room and the room has nobody to give a name to, who is the person that company writes the cheques to.**"

And the man of thirty-seven said: "**That is the question he is going to be asked on Thursday and he already knows the answer, and the answer is that he will not be asked it, and he wrote a line on that sheet about it, and I have read the line and I am not going to say what the line says.**"

"**So you have read the line and you will not say it and you have come here anyway.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**That is the whole of what anybody in here ever does and it took you six years.**"

He looked at the sheet face down on the counter and did not turn it over.

"**It took me six years and one morning,**" he said. "**And I still have not told you why the year is free and I am not going to, and I have not been thanked and I am not going to be, and I would like to leave now, please, and I would like to leave the sheet, and I would like somebody to tell me that is allowed.**"

---

It was allowed.

That was the whole of what the room did with a sheet of a company's paper on the Monday morning of week eighty. It was left face down on a counter, and the room did not turn it over, and it was the first thing anybody had ever put on that counter that could not be handed back inside a minute, and a cook of twenty-seven said the thing about that out loud about four minutes later and nobody improved on it.

A woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary opened the door, walked the outside of the shutter and put the sounder back where it belongs, at about half past ten, with nobody watching her, and by morning the shutter had not moved.

A woman of about thirty was in a chair near the door for the whole of the morning and was asked for nothing and is on nothing in that room and nobody in this case has ever asked her why she comes, and about four people wanted to and did not.

He got to Lattice Ward at about ten that night in the rain, at a bench with the lamp off and the roller shutter down and the docket face up, and there is a sheet of a company's paper face down on a counter four miles away with nothing written on the back of it that anybody has seen.

He wrote one thing.

*235. Monday of week 80, at a bench in Lattice Ward at about ten, unlit, with the rain coming off the street and the docket face up, and this entry is one sheet of a company's paper left face down on a counter.*

*The visitor, as a source and not as a case.* **Thirty-seven; six years in the job; administers property for a company that appears in a schedule of title on that row; signs the line that moves money underneath it; has signed that line for six years, including the free year. Not a director, not a manager, not on anything, holds no title in this case and is given none here. Owed nothing, thanked by nobody, and not a mechanism. He was asked nothing and answered everything, in that order, and the answering came first.**

*What he said, in the order he said it.* **First and unasked, and it is the sentence the whole of this movement turns on: that there is no money in that room. He then gave the inventory he had gone round — the shutter, the counter, nine chairs, a window, a kettle, a bell-push, a piece of hardboard, and the floor — and had found nothing in any of them. Second, that the unit's first year costs its occupiers nothing; that it is the only free year on that row; that he has signed the paper saying so every year for six years; and that nobody has ever told him why. Third, on being asked, that he had put the same question twice and had it refused twice with the same true sentence. Fourth and unasked, that he was not there to be helpful, and that a building he has never been inside is going to find a unit open twelve weeks at no cost, and that he will not be the one asked about it, because he has already asked and he does not know what he would say.**

*Why he came in person.* **Stated by him: that his signature moves the money; that he wanted to have stood in the room before he moved any; and that in about nine years he has signed about nine arrangements on that row and has been inside one. Nothing in this chapter tests that reason and nothing improves on it.**

*Why the room could not answer, and who tried.* **Two people tried and neither produced an answer. A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip, about twenty-five days in that room, put it as a thing whose words belong to a stranger and said he does not know the stranger; the visitor agreed that this is not an answer; and the man said it is not, and that it is the first time in about twenty-five days that anybody in there has told a stranger he had nothing, and that telling a stranger you have nothing costs nothing, which is the only free thing anybody in this case has ever put on a counter. The other attempts are not recorded because nobody could say afterwards what they were. The person who does know is a letting clerk of thirty-nine in another building, who has now said the same true thing about a frontage five times, who keeps the ring file, who is not thanked, who has not been asked, and who is not in this chapter. Nobody went to her. Nobody has ever gone to her about a year.**

*The sheet, as an object.* **One sheet of a company's paper. No name on it and none to go on it. One figure and five lines, of which the visitor read out neither the figure nor the lines, and said he would not, on the ground that a room primed on the Monday is not the same meeting as a room met on the Thursday. It is not a form, a notice, a report, a minute, a leaflet, a registration or a constitution, and nothing in this chapter was settled by it, refused by it, or answered in writing by anybody. It was left face down and not turned over. It is the first object anybody has put on that counter which cannot be lifted and set down again in about nine seconds by anybody in the room, and a cook of twenty-seven said so out loud about four minutes after he had gone, and nobody improved on it.**

*The one question the first-year asked.* **Who the company writes the cheques to. The visitor said the man coming on the Thursday knows the answer already; that the answer is that nobody is going to put the question to him; that there is a line on the sheet about it; and that he has read the line and will not say what it says. She then said that he had read it and would not say it and had come anyway, and that this is the whole of what anybody in there ever does, and that it had taken him six years. He said it took six years and one morning, and that he still had not answered her first question and was not going to. He asked to leave, he asked to leave the sheet behind, and he asked to be told that was allowed, and he was told it was allowed.**

*What else was in the room, as a list and not as a cast.* **A woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary did the door, the sounder and the outside of the shutter at about half past ten, unaided, unasked, unobserved, and the shutter was identical on the Tuesday morning. A man of fifty-one with a tool bag was present about forty minutes and said one thing he had said in that room three weeks earlier and was not argued with. A woman of about thirty spent the whole morning in a chair near the door with a bag on her lap, was asked nothing by anybody, is not on the wall, is not in the binder, is on no object in that room and is in no file in this case; about four people in that room wanted to ask her something and did not.**

*Absent, withheld, or deliberately not done.* **The name of the company; the figure; the five lines; the name of the man coming on the Thursday, which is not on this page and is not going to be. The reason the year is free, which is in no file in this case and has been recorded as unresolvable five times. No page, form, letter, minute, notice or report was written by anybody in this chapter and none exists. Not one person in that room on that morning taught anybody anything, nor demonstrated it, nor assessed it, nor assisted with it, and an exchange of observations, a relayed plate and a performed practice are all absent from the day. The restriction on what he may do in that department appears in this chapter neither in its own words nor in anybody else's paraphrase. Nobody in this chapter put the standing question, and nothing in it establishes who may be asked anything, and no number is attached to one. Nobody has named whoever wrote the thing, the field on three documents is still empty, the fourth pocket of the tool roll has not been opened, and nothing has come from anybody unsigned or otherwise. Entry 235.**
