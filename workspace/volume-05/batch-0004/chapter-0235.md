# Chapter 235 — What The Room Can Do With It

The second Friday rota of this movement ran on the Friday of week eighty, and the room spent the evening establishing that it could do nothing at all with a piece of paper, and the reason it could do nothing is the reason the refusal in three days' time is going to cost somebody their heat, and a page on a job sheet came up twice in one week and the correct answer to it changed nothing and was written in a box that gets a description.

The room was eighty-eight days old. The sheet of a company's paper was still on the counter, face up, and it had been on that counter for four days and had been looked at by everybody who came through the door and had been read by four people.

---

Grade three from twenty to eight. Ten on the docket and nine in date, and one of the nine was a page that had already been on that sheet.

A fire door on a second landing with a closer on it that had been adjusted in the spring and adjusted again in the middle of the week, and on the Friday it came up a second time in the same week, from a different reporter, in the same box, with a different description, and both descriptions were true and neither of them was the fault.

He went at about half past six with the frame gauge in his bag because a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket had said something on the Wednesday that had been in his head since.

The frame is the door, not the closer. The closer is on the door and the door is in the frame, and the frame had swelled about two millimetres on the hinge side in the wet and had closed on a hinge that was set out of true in a building that was put up before anybody now working in it was born, and the whole of the fault is a hinge in a frame and none of it is a part he is allowed to change.

He adjusted the closer for the second time in four days, and he wrote in the box that gets a description that the fault is not the closer, that it is a frame on the hinge side, and that the frame carries a stamped number on the inside of the head about four feet up, and that anybody reading the sheet is welcome to climb four feet.

The man of thirty-eight read it on the Friday morning and said one thing about it, which was that it was the first time in about four months that anybody had written down what the fault was not.

Nobody in that yard asked him where a release would come from, and there has been nobody in that yard he could telephone about it for a term and a half, and he has never once wanted to, and he will not be the one anybody asks about that hinge.

The word was at nine all morning and at ten from about two in the afternoon and it was at ten all night.

---

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card and is on no form, and a Friday is a Friday and it has been a Friday for thirteen weeks and nothing about it has changed once.

---

The cook of twenty-seven had been thinking about a piece of paper since the Thursday night, and she did the thinking out loud at the sink at about half past seven, in front of about eleven people, and she had it in five parts and she would not let anybody make it six.

"**I have been sitting here since Thursday working out what this room can do with that thing on the counter, and here is the whole of it, and I have not missed anything, and if I have then somebody who has been here longer than me will say so.**"

Nobody said so.

"**One. We cannot take it. Taking it is a thing you do on paper, and there are four names on the lease for this unit and a fifth name on a piece of paper in a building four miles from here is a fifth name anybody can go and look at, and I have watched a woman of nineteen put her own name at the bottom of about eleven hundred leaflets and be unable to take it off, and I am not doing that with a kitchen's money.**"

"**Two. We cannot turn it down. Turning it down is a letter. A letter is a document about this room with our names in it, and a man of fifty-eight said that in here three weeks ago and he was right, and I have not got a better argument than his and I am not going to pretend to.**"

"**Three. We cannot give it away. There is nobody to give it to. A man with a tool bag can get into about nine hundred buildings and he cannot get to one person with a bag on her lap and he has told us that himself and he was right and he is not thanked.**"

Somebody said, "And four."

"**Four. It does not go in the binder, and it does not go on the wall, and both of those are the wall's own two lines and I am not going to read either of them out. And five.**"

She turned round, which she does about twice in a month.

"**Five. The only thing this room can do with it is leave it on this counter where anybody can see it and take it away when the shutter comes down, and I said about four weeks ago that that is a different class of event from a page, and I have just said the same sentence again on a different object, and I am telling the room that I have said it twice and that I am not going to say it a third time, and that whoever wrote down either of those had better be somebody else.**"

Nobody wrote anything down.

---

And then Asha Reed said the thing that the whole evening had been walking towards, and she said it standing up, and about four people were standing already.

"**Which means that this room has to say no to a piece of paper, and there is no version of saying no that is not a document, and that is the first thing in about twelve weeks that this room has not been able to do with something it has been handed.**"

"Does the wall have anything about it."

"**The wall has got eleven other things on it and not one of them is about money, and I am not adding a twelfth for this, and I have been thinking about adding a twelfth for two days and I have not done it and I want that said out loud in this room before anybody decides I have forgotten.**"

Somebody said, "You have not forgotten."

"**No. I have decided that a line about money written by a person who is frightened about money is the worst thing that could go on that board, and the man of fifty-eight proved that in here three weeks ago and I would like it noted that he proved it about a pair of shoes and I have used it about a company.**"

---

The man of thirty-four who works nights came in at about ten past eight and said his two sentences again, as he said he would, and about nine people had heard them once.

The first one about a pump and a building with about nine hundred doors on it and nothing having ever happened in about nine years.

The second one, that he would like somebody to watch him do it, and that he has wanted that for about two terms, and that there is nobody to ask.

He said the distinction again, and the room listened to it again, and nobody improved on it either time.

"**I said last week that I would say it again this week and I have, and I am not going to ask anybody to do anything, because I asked in a corridor about four hundred times and nothing came of it and there is no form, and this room is the fourth place I have said it in and it is the only place anybody has let me finish the sentence.**"

"That is not nothing," said the cook of twenty-seven.

"**It is not nothing and it is not a yes, and I can tell the difference, and I have been able to tell the difference since about the spring, and I have not told anybody I can until tonight.**"

---

And then the man of twenty-two said the only thing he said all evening, and he said it to the counter and not to anybody, at about a quarter to nine.

"**There is no money in that room and there has not been since the year began, and about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and the two facts are in the same building tonight and there is nothing in this room that connects them and there is nothing in this city that connects them.**"

"And when there is."

"**Then it will not be in a room.**"

Nobody asked him what he meant, and he did not say it again, and about four people in that room thought about it afterwards and about four of them got it and about four did not.

---

A woman of about thirty spent the whole evening in the third chair from the door with her bag on her lap. Nobody asked her a single thing, she is on no object in that room, and nobody has ever asked her why she comes.

A woman of thirty-four with a district jacket and a clipboard came in at about half past eight, drank a cup of tea, asked for nothing, and left at about ten to nine, and nobody thanked her for it then or for the other times.

He sat on a step on a second landing in a building in Nine Bridges at about nine that night with the frame gauge still in his bag, and there is a piece of a company's paper face up on a counter four miles away that nobody in this case can pick up and put down again in about nine seconds.

He wrote one thing.

*238. Friday of week 80, at about nine on a second landing in Nine Bridges, a gauge still in a bag and a stair light overhead, and this entry is one page left face up on a counter for three days and one sheet of job paper that arrived twice.*

*The work, itemised.* **Ten on the docket, nine in date, grade three from twenty to eight. The ninth item is a repeat: a stair fire door whose closer was adjusted in the spring and again midweek, reported a second time inside the same seven days by a second person, into the same description box, in different words, both accounts correct and neither of them the defect. What is actually wrong is the frame — roughly two millimetres of swell down the hinge side, in a structure older than anyone now working in it, and not a component he is licensed to replace. He reset the closer for a second occasion in four days, and used the description box to record that the defect lies in the frame and that a stamped identifier is on the inside of the head at about four feet, free of charge to any reader willing to climb. A district man of thirty-eight read it on the Friday morning and observed that it was the first entry in about four months that had stated what the fault was not. Nobody in that yard asked where a release would come from, there is nobody there to ask, and he will not be the one anybody approaches about that hinge. The unnameable rate was nine all morning, ten from about two in the afternoon, and ten all night. The city's standing weekly figure is unchanged and stands on the back of a woman's own card and on no form; the same figure has stood on that Friday for thirteen weeks and has not moved.**

*The page, on its fourth day.* **One page, one figure, five clauses, no name on it. It has lain the right way up on that counter since a man of forty-one read it aloud on the Thursday before last. Everybody through the door has looked at it; four people have read it. In this chapter it is not accepted, not declined, not signed, not sent and not minuted, and its author has not been in the room since Thursday, is owed nothing further, and has not telephoned.**

*What a room can do, in five parts, offered by a cook of twenty-seven without being asked.* **It cannot take it: four names stand on that unit's lease, a fifth appearing on a company's paper in another building is a name that can be looked up, and a first-year of nineteen has already put hers below about eleven hundred leaflets and cannot lift it. It cannot decline it: declining is a letter, a letter is a paper about that room with its occupants' names upon it, a man of fifty-eight proved that in there three weeks back, and she has no answer to it that improves on his. It cannot pass it on: there is nobody to pass it to, a man of fifty-one with a tool bag having told them himself that he can get into about nine hundred buildings and cannot get to one individual, and having been right and thanked for nothing. Neither the binder nor the board takes it, each being barred by one of the wall's own two lines, neither of which was read out. Fifth, and last: the only available act is to leave it visible on a counter and remove it when the shutter goes down, which is the sentence a cook of twenty-seven gave about four weeks ago in respect of a different object. She announced that she has now used it twice, will not use it again, and that whoever wrote down either occasion had better be a different person. Nothing was written down.**

*The decline with no shape to it.* **Stated by a first-year of nineteen, on her feet: that the room will have to turn a page of paper down, and that no turning down of a page of paper is other than a paper, and that this is the first object handed to that room in about twelve weeks it has had no way of holding. Asked whether the wall had anything to contribute: eleven lines, other subjects, none of them about money, and she had thought about a twelfth for two days and had not added it and wanted that said before anybody assumed otherwise. Her ground: a line about money composed by somebody frightened about money is the worst thing that board could carry, the shape of which a man of fifty-eight demonstrated three weeks ago over a pair of shoes, and she has transferred it to a company. No one wrote anything down.**

*The night man, second airing.* **In at about ten past eight and repeated both sentences, having given notice of it the previous Friday. The first is the pump, the plant feeding about nine hundred doors, and about nine years during which nothing whatever happened. The second is the wish to be watched, held about two terms, with nobody to consult. He restated his own distinction between them unprompted; the room heard it twice and improved on it neither time. Added without prompting: that he intends to ask nobody to act, having asked in a corridor about four hundred times to no effect and with no form available, and that this is the fourth room in which he has said it and the first in which he was allowed to finish. A cook of twenty-seven told him that was not nothing. He answered that it is not nothing and is not agreement, that he has been able to tell the two apart since about the spring, and that he had said so to nobody until that night.**

*The docket-holder's single sentence, and the silence after it.* **Spoken toward the counter and not to a person, at about a quarter to nine, to the effect that that room has contained no money since the year began; that the city's weekly figure of people taught to wait for a man in a lanyard is unaltered; that the two propositions occupied one building that evening; and that nothing in the room relates them and nothing in the city does. He was asked nothing, did not repeat himself, and this entry carries no gloss on it. No mechanism, no rule, and no means of distinguishing the two appeared in this chapter, was proposed by anybody, was agreed by anybody, or was written down by anybody.**

*Two others in the room.* **A woman of about thirty, in a chair near the door, bag on her lap, for the whole evening; asked nothing by anybody; on no object in that room; in no file in this case; never once asked why she comes. A woman of thirty-four with a district jacket and a clipboard, in at about half past eight and out at about ten to nine, one cup of tea, nothing asked, thanked by nobody then and on no other occasion of hers.**

*Left out on purpose.* **The figure and the five clauses, which stand in the entry of the Thursday preceding and are not repeated. The gauge. The stamped identifier. The district nurse of thirty-four and every reason she has ever given for coming, which she has never given twice. No page, form, letter, minute, notice or report was made by anybody in this chapter and none exists. The board was not read out and received no new line. That Friday involved no instruction, no demonstration, no assessment and no assistance, and none of the three of a share, a relay and a practice. The terms on which he may not teach, demonstrate, assess or assist are not quoted and not paraphrased anywhere in this chapter. The standing question is not spoken, nothing in this entry governs who may be put to a question, and no figure is set against one. The blank field is still blank and its author is still unnamed; the card stayed in the pocket; and no letter, message or communication of any kind reached anybody in this chapter. Entry 238.**
