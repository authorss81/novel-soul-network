# Chapter 231 — A Man Who Works Nights

The first Friday rota of the fourth movement of this room ran on the Friday of week seventy-nine, a day after the two newest lines went onto a piece of hardboard, and the two of them were one day old that evening, and a man of thirty-four who works nights came in at about half past seven and said two true sentences in about nine minutes, and a company telephoned the number on the plate at about half past eight and asked for a meeting.

Nobody in that room knew what either of those things would be about by the time the shutter came down. That is the shape of this movement and it is the fourth time in a row.

---

Grade three from twenty to eight on a Friday, eleven on the docket, ten in date. A fan in a stair on a timer that was not the fault, a tap needing about four turns more than it got, a light over a fire door that somebody had reported twice in one week and once in the other week, a callout to a loading bay where a light fitting had been knocked, three landing lights, an emergency pack in a stair that was not the fault, a cupboard light off a service road, a door closer on a fire door that was adjusted in the spring and had come back to where it started, and one item where the equipment was not on the site and never had been.

Two of the ten were closed by a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket, who writes the finding in a box that gets a description, and who said out loud that he had not said it was the fault, that this was the third caller that month to be right about that door closer, and that the other two were also right.

The word was at ten from about half past nine in the morning and it was at eleven by about four in the afternoon, and it stayed at eleven until the lamp came on, and nothing was done about it then or at any other time and it was not told to anybody.

Nobody on that site has ever asked him where a release would be coming from, there has not been anybody on it he could telephone about the question for a term and a half, and he has never once wanted anybody to.

---

The room is eighty-one days old. It is a converted shop unit off a service road in the Lower Wards, four rooms and a back one, nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, a counter cleared to a strip, a window onto a wall about four feet away, a bell-push with a sounder behind it that rings and changes nothing, and a piece of hardboard about the size of a tea tray turned on two nails inside by the door.

The two lines on it were put there the evening before and nobody has read them out and about nine people have looked at them.

He was at the back with a docket for the whole of the evening, and that is where he was, and that is the second Friday in a row.

---

The man of thirty-four had been in that room four times in about seven weeks and had never brought anything, and on three of those four occasions he had sat in the fourth chair from the door and said nothing, and on the fourth he had turned the kettle plug off at the end because the cook of twenty-seven was going home and it had been on for six hours.

He works nights. He is not a practitioner in anything that room has ever been about and he is not on a rota and he has never once asked to be taught anything.

He had come in that week from a substation on the far side of the city where the heat comes from before it comes to nine hundred doors, and he works from ten at night to six in the morning four days a week, and he is in that room at half past seven because at half past seven he is on his way to work and that room is the only place between his front door and a substation where nobody asks him a question.

Asha Reed asked him what it was for, because she asks everybody that.

"Nothing," he said. "**I have come in here four times and I have not brought anything four times and I am about to bring nothing again and I would like it noticed that I keep coming.**"

"Go on."

"**I am not going to talk about my job. I am going to say two sentences and I have been holding the first one since the spring and the second one since about the middle of the week, and I have never said either of them in a room and I have said both of them to myself in a corridor about four hundred times.**"

Nobody said anything, and about four people watched him not say anything for about nine seconds, which is the length of both sentences in a man of his trade.

"**At about four in the morning I am on my own in a building with about nine hundred doors coming off it, and there is a pump, and at some point in every shift I take my hand off it and say the thing out loud, and nothing has ever happened. Not once. In about nine years.**"

He put his hands flat on his knees.

"**That is the first one. It is not frightening. It is the only thing I have ever done that I am completely sure of and I have never told one person, and I have not told it here because I want somebody to say something.**"

---

And then he said the second one, and it took him longer, and he did not improve it, and about four people in that room understood every word of it.

"**I would like somebody to watch me do it.**"

Nobody moved.

"**I have wanted that for about two terms. I have wanted it about four hundred times in a corridor. There is nobody in this city I can ask, and there is no form anywhere that asks it, and I have not wanted it badly enough to write to anybody, and tonight I have wanted it badly enough to say it in a room with about nine people in it, and there is still nobody here who can do it.**"

He looked at the floor.

"**I am not asking for it tonight. I am telling you that it is a thing I want, because I have found out this month that I am not the only one, and there is a sentence going round this city about the wanting being taken out of it.**"

Somebody said, "Is it the same sentence."

"**No**," said the man of thirty-four. "**It is not, and I want that said twice, and nothing in this building is written down, so I will say it again next week. Mine is: I would like somebody to watch. The other one is: I need somebody to be there. One of those is about me and the other one is about a person who is not me, and I have never once been in a position to tell the difference, and neither has anybody in a lanyard.**"

Nobody improved on it, and nobody wrote it down, and the cook of twenty-seven said one word from the sink and it was not agreement.

---

At about half past eight the telephone in the office eleven miles away rang, and a woman of thirty-nine with a ring file answered it, and she said the sentence she says.

"**The unit has been vacant on this side of the row for a long time and I have given that answer to five sets of people.**"

The caller said it was not about the unit.

"**It is a company**," the caller said. "**It is a training company and it is not about the unit. I have got about four hundred people who have read a page about a room on that row and I want to come and talk to whoever runs it. I am not selling anything and I have not got a pack and I would like to come on a Monday.**"

The clerk looked at the ring file, which has a schedule of title in it, and the schedule of title has an entry in it that describes this unit as a freehold and does not describe who pays for anything.

"Hold on," she said.

---

What she had done, and what she did not know she had done, is this.

She had put a company and a room on the same morning on the same telephone, and she had not asked anybody's name, because the sentence about the frontage comes before the name in that office and always has, and she has never once asked for a name at that point in nine years.

She had said the word *vacant* to a company about a unit that had been open every working day for twelve weeks.

And then she had said hold on, which she has now said four times, and the first of them was a Tuesday in the week of seventy-two when a young woman said yes in about four seconds to a photograph, and the second was a Thursday in the week of seventy-six when a sister of sixty-four rang about nine steps, and the third was a Friday in the week of seventy-eight when a person of thirty-four rang about a page she had read, and on each of the three somebody in that room came to the telephone afterwards and told the room what had been asked. This time she held the line for about eleven seconds and then rang through.

Nobody has asked her what she has done and she has not been told. She is a person with a ring file and a schedule of title in it, and all four of those telephone calls are on a sheet of paper of her own that she has said three times she is not bringing to that room, and nobody asked her about it again tonight and nobody thanked her.

The room was told one thing about the call and it was this: a man of forty-one wants to come on a Monday, and he is not selling anything, and he has not got a pack.

---

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card and is on no form, and a man of thirty-four who works nights is not one of the eleven and has not claimed to be one of them.

About eleven people were in the room for the whole of it. A woman of about thirty was in the third chair from the door with her bag on her lap and was asked for nothing, and she is not on the wall and not in the binder and is on no document in this case, and nobody has ever asked her why she comes, and she was in that room when a company was told the unit was vacant.

The wall behind the counter had two lines on it that were a day old, one in a hand that got smaller towards the end of it and one in a first-year's own hand, and a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip had read the first of them out loud the evening before in front of about eleven people.

He got to Lattice Ward at about ten with the roller shutter half down and the cold coming off the street, and the docket had four job numbers on it, and in the fourth pocket of the tool roll there is a corner of card the size of a playing card with a fifth line on it that has been blank for a term and a half and that nobody has ever asked him about.

He wrote one thing.

*234. Friday of week 79, at a bench in Lattice Ward at about ten with the shutter half down and the docket under his hand, and this entry is a man who works nights, two sentences, and a telephone call to a plate.*

*The run, as a count of items.* **Eleven on the docket, ten in date, grade three from twenty to eight. In date: one fan on a timer, one tap, one light over a fire door, one callout to a loading bay, three landing lights, one emergency pack, one cupboard light off a service road, one door closer adjusted in the spring, and one item where the equipment is not on the site and has never been on the site. Ten of eleven were in date. Two of those were signed off by a district man of thirty-eight, whose habit is to enter a finding in the description box on the sheet; he stated aloud that he had not called it the fault, that this makes three callers in a month to be right about the closer, and that the other two were also right. The rate of the unnameable thing was on ten from the middle of the morning and on eleven from about four in the afternoon, was told to nobody, and was not acted on at any point. Nobody in that yard asked where a release would come from, and there is nobody there to ask.**

*The room, as a count and as a date.* **Eighty-one days old; four rooms and a back one; nine chairs; a counter; a window; one bell-push, put back on the wall with two screws on the Thursday immediately before this day and tested from the service road; one unscrewed piece of hardboard on two nails, which can be taken off with a thumb. Two lines were added to it on the Thursday immediately before this day and are one day old here: eleven words in a man's hand and fourteen in a first-year's. About eleven people present from about half past seven. A man of twenty-two stood at the back with a docket and said nothing for the whole of the evening, which is the second Friday running and the third evening in a row that he has done it.**

*The man of thirty-four, as a source.* **Four visits in about seven weeks, four offerings of nothing, and one act on a first visit that was to turn off a plug that had been on six hours. Not a practitioner in anything that room has been about, not on a rota, has never asked to be taught anything, and has not offered anything. Four nights a week at a substation serving about nine hundred doors. He is not a company man, is not on any list in this case, is not on the wall, and is not in the binder.**

*Two sentences, and what was done with them.* **The first, held since the spring: that at about four in the morning he is alone with a pump in a building feeding about nine hundred doors, that at some point in every shift he takes his hand off it and says a thing out loud, and that in about nine years nothing has ever happened, and that he has told nobody and came here hoping somebody would say something. The second, formed about the middle of this week: that he would like somebody to watch him do it; that he has wanted that for about two terms and about four hundred times in a corridor; that there is nobody to ask and no form that asks it; and that he said in the room that he was not asking for it that night and only saying that he wanted it. He then distinguished the two sentences himself, unprompted, and asked for the distinction to stand: his is a wish to be watched, and the sentence that is going round this city has the wanting taken out of it and becomes a need for a person to be present, and one of the two is about him and the other is about a stranger, and he has never been in a position to tell the difference and neither has anybody in a lanyard. Nobody improved on him, nothing was written down, and a cook of twenty-seven said one word from a sink that was not agreement.**

*The call, and the four things in it.* **A telephone to a company's number on a plate with no name on it at about half past eight, answered by a letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file. She gave the sentence about the frontage, which she has now given to five sets of people. The caller stated a company, stated that it was a training company, stated that it was not about the unit, stated a figure of about four hundred readers of a page about a room on that row, asked to come and talk to whoever runs it, stated that she was selling nothing and had no pack, and asked for a Monday. The clerk said hold on. What is recorded here, and it is not what the clerk knows, is that the word *vacant* went out of that office to a company about a unit that has been open every working day for twelve weeks, that no name was asked for, because the sentence comes before the name in that office and has done for nine years, and that hold on is now the fourth time that clerk has put two strangers and that room on the same line. She has not been told any of this. That fourth call is written on a sheet she keeps for herself, which she has said three times she will not bring to that room, and she was neither asked nor thanked tonight. The room was given one sentence: a man of forty-one will come on a Monday, he is selling nothing, and he has not got a pack.**

*Kept out of this record on purpose.* **What the man says to the pump. The name of the company, the page, the figure of about four hundred readers, and what the page says. The man's trade, his employer's name, and the four nights. The reason the year of that unit is free, which is not on this page and is not going to be. The room's own eleven-a-week sentence, which is in the prose and is in neither this entry nor this chapter's record of the man. A woman of about thirty spent the whole evening in a chair near the door with a bag on her lap, was asked nothing by anybody, appears on no object in that room, and is in no file in this case; a company was told that unit was vacant while she was sitting in it, and nobody told the caller anything about her. There is no share in this chapter, no relay and no practice, and nobody anywhere on its pages taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted a thing. The condition is neither quoted nor paraphrased here, and nobody in this chapter puts it into words. The standing question is referred to once and is not spoken, nothing in this entry governs who may be asked anything, and no figure is set against one. The licensor stays unnamed, the field is blank, the master did not move, and no message arrived from anybody. Entry 234.**
