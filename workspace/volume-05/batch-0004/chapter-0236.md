# Chapter 236 — Eleven Lines, Nine Arguments, And One Thumb

On the Monday afternoon of week eighty-one nine people wrote eleven rules in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards and about nine of the eleven were argued about, and one of them was rubbed off with a thumb before the shutter came down, and one person in the room was not asked to sign any of them and gave the reason out loud and was not improved on.

Nobody in that room was taught, shown, assessed or helped with anything, and the sheet they wrote on is not a document and does not bind a soul.

---

The room was ninety-one days old, and a first-year of nineteen said so out loud at about half past four, and then said the other half of it.

"**That is a quarter of a year.**"

"Ninety-one days," said the cook of twenty-seven. "**A quarter of a year is ninety-one days and I have counted them twice because I count things, and I am not going to tell you what that is, because I do not know, and I am not going to stand in this room on a Monday afternoon and make a coincidence out of it in front of nine people.**"

Asha Reed wrote the number down on a scrap and put the scrap in her pocket, and nobody asked her why.

---

The cook of twenty-seven had brought the paper.

It is one sheet off a roll in a kitchen that opens in the afternoon, it is not a sheet of that company's paper, there is nothing on it, it is about twice the size of the hardboard by the door, and it is not going on the wall.

"**The wall is a wall**," she said. "**The wall is a piece of hardboard that is not screwed to anything and it can be taken off with a thumb and it has got twelve lines on it in seven hands and eight names, and that is a different thing from this. This is a sheet of paper. And I am not putting a sheet of paper on that wall, and I am not putting a sheet of paper in that ring binder, because that binder has seven things in it and six of them have my words on them and one of them has a woman's words on it in a hand that has never been in this room.**"

Nobody improved on that either.

---

And then a first-year of nineteen said the thing that made it a chapter, and she said it standing up, and about four people were already on their feet.

"**Before anybody writes anything, I am going to say what this is, because about four of you are going to get it wrong tonight and I would rather it was said by somebody who has been wrong in this room before.**"

"Go on."

"**A rule in this building is a line somebody wrote by hand. That is all it is. It costs the writer something to say out loud what it costs, and it is not enforceable against anybody who did not write it, and anybody who wrote it can take it off with a thumb before the end of the evening, and it is not evidence of anything in any proceeding, and there is no proceeding, and there is going to be no proceeding, and if there is ever one then this sheet is worth nothing and I am saying that before we start so that none of you can hide behind it later.**"

"Agreed," said the printer of fifty-eight. "**And I am adding one thing and I am not writing it on that paper: a printed page is the only class of object in this city that cannot be un-printed, and a sheet of paper can be. That is the entire difference between the two and I have been wrong about it four times in forty years.**"

---

They wrote for about an hour and forty minutes and about nine of the eleven lines were argued about, and the arguing was the whole of it, and nothing was minuted.

The first line was the cook's, and it lasted about nine minutes before somebody pointed out that it says out loud a thing that is already the twelfth line on that wall, in a first-year's own hand, eight days old.

"**Then it is on the wall and it does not need to be on a sheet**," said the man of twenty-nine who works on lifts. "**And if it is on a sheet then in about four years somebody will find two versions of the same sentence and not know which one is the old one.**"

"**It is on the sheet anyway**," said the cook, "**because a wall cannot be taken down and a sheet can.**"

The second line was his, and it is that if two of them agree that is not a reason, and he argued for about four minutes that it is the only line on the sheet with teeth in it and that he would like it recorded that he is aware of that and would write it anyway.

---

The line that took the longest was the fifth, and it belongs to a man of fifty-eight who inspects buses, and he is not the man of fifty-eight with the bad hip, and about four people in that room said so out loud while he was getting the pen out.

"**Say the thing about the two of you**," said Asha Reed.

"**I have been in this room twice in about eleven weeks and I have never once been asked to write anything, and I said no the first time, and I have thought about saying no every week since.**" He put the pen down and picked it up again. "**I am going to write one.**"

"**A thing that has gone wrong twice is not a thing anybody should be writing down.**"

Somebody said, "That is about the wall."

"**That is about the wall and it is about about nine buses a week for twenty-two years.**" He did not look up. "**I have the standing to say a thing is not safe. It is the only standing I have ever had and I have never once had it in a room with a door in it. I am going to write a line on a piece of paper that says a man should not write down a thing that has gone wrong twice, and I cannot make a single person in this room do anything, and I am going to say out loud that it cost me twenty-two years to be able to write that sentence, and that is the whole of what it cost.**"

Nobody wrote that down and nobody improved on it.

---

The printer of fifty-eight wrote two and the second one argued for about nine minutes and was argued against by the cook.

"**A page that is a copy is a page somebody cannot take back.**"

"**That is the ninth line on that wall**," said the cook.

"**It is, and I wrote that one in this room in the first week and it has been printed about nine hundred times since and I was right about it every single time and it did not stop a single sheet of anything.**" She did not raise her voice. "**So I am writing it on a sheet of paper, and the sheet goes in a drawer in a kitchen, and in about four years somebody will find it, and that is better than a wall.**"

"That is not a reason."

"**No. It is a thing I want.**"

---

The cook wrote a second line at about half past eight and rubbed it off with her own thumb at about a quarter to nine, in front of about nine people, and left the space.

"**That one goes**," she said. "**I wrote it at about half past eight because I was annoyed, and a line written by a person who is annoyed is a line that will be wrong by Friday, and if a line can be taken off with a thumb then it should be taken off by the person who wrote it and by nobody else, and I would like everybody in this room to notice that I did it myself and that nobody helped me and that nobody has touched that piece of paper tonight except to write on it.**"

Nobody had touched it except to write on it.

---

The last line was written at about ten to nine, after the sheet had been passed round, by a woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary, and she signed it separately underneath the other names, and nobody stopped her.

"**You can ring me about any of this and I will come and undo it.**"

"You do not have to put that on a piece of paper," somebody said.

"**I know. I wrote the same eleven words on a sheet of typing paper about a fortnight ago and gave them to a man with a key on them.**" She signed it. "**That one was about a key. This one is about eleven lines and about a person in the third chair who has not been asked to sign any of them, and I would like it in the same hand and I would like it to be the one anybody rings.**"

That is what it cost her and nobody had to ask.

---

And the one person who was not asked.

A woman of about thirty had been in that room for the whole afternoon in a chair near the door with a bag on her lap, and when the sheet went round nobody put it in front of her, and about four people noticed that nobody had.

Asha Reed said the reason out loud, in the room, to nobody in particular, and the woman gave it back in her own words and it was not improved on by anybody.

"**We are not asking her**," said the first-year. "**She has been in this room since the Friday of week sixty-nine and nobody has asked her one question in about twelve weeks and I am not going to be the person who starts on a Monday afternoon with a piece of paper.**"

"**And I would like to be left in the chair**," said the woman of about thirty, "**and not be the thing a person is careful about, and I would like it noticed that nobody said that for me.**"

Nobody said it for her.

---

And the eighth name signed at the bottom and wrote none of the eleven, as he signed the wall and wrote none of the twelve, and the reason was the same as it had been a fortnight before and he gave it in about nine seconds.

"**I have signed a line on that wall and written none of the twelve and I am signing this one and writing none of the eleven, and I am not going to be asked why twice in a fortnight because I am going to say it once and I am going to say it properly.**"

"Go on."

"**There is no version of a line I write about this room that is not a sentence with my name on the front of it, and there is one of those in a rail in a building in Lattice Ward and it has been there about a term and it cost me the last thing I had, and I am not doing that again for a room, and I would like a first-year of nineteen to notice that nobody in that room has argued with me about it.**"

"**Nobody has**," said Asha Reed.

---

And the man of twenty-two did not read the sheet out and did not comment on it, and at about nine he said one thing about what it is worth, which nobody thanked him for and which about four people thought about afterwards.

"**There is no way in this city to make anybody obey any of those eleven lines and there never will be, and about four of them are already wrong, and a woman of fifty-eight has written the reason down twice now, and the reason a man of fifty-eight in this city could be within the law for four years and still have done what he did is on the sixth line of that sheet in a hand that is not yours.**"

And then the shutter came down at about ten, and there is a sheet of kitchen paper with eleven lines in it in a drawer in a kitchen that opens in the afternoon, and one of the eleven has a thumb's worth of daylight through it.

He wrote one thing, standing at the table, before the shutter.

*239. Monday of week 81, at about ten, on their feet beside a table in the room off the service road, the shutter still up and about nine people gone, and this entry is one sheet of paper with eleven lines and about nine arguments.*

*The object and what it is not.* **A single leaf taken off a roll in a kitchen whose doors open in the afternoon. Not the paper of the company whose offer lies face up on the counter four feet away, unmarked, and roughly double the width of the board by the door. A cook of twenty-seven set the distinction and it was not disputed: that board is a leaf of hardboard, fixed to nothing, liftable with a thumb, carrying twelve lines in seven hands and eight names; that this object is paper, may be taken down, and does not enter the ring binder, that binder holding seven things, six of them carrying that cook's words and one carrying the words of a woman in another district in a hand that has never stood in that room. It is not a document, it binds no one, and no other page in this case refers to it.**

*The account of the practice, given before a mark was made.* **From a first-year of nineteen, standing, having warned that about four of those present would get it wrong: that a rule in this place is a line a person wrote by hand; that writing one costs its writer something, to be said aloud; that it binds nobody who did not write it; that its own writer may lift it with a thumb; and that it is evidence of nothing in any proceeding, there being no proceeding and there never having been one. A printer of fifty-eight added, and did not write it down, that a printed page is the only class of object in this city which cannot be un-printed while a leaf of paper can be; that this is the entire difference between the two; and that she has been wrong on this four times in forty years.**

*What got written, and in whose hand.* **One, the cook of twenty-seven — that the room will say what it will not keep before anything is handed to it. Debated at length on the ground that it restates the board's twelfth line, and written regardless on the ground that a board cannot be lifted and a leaf can. Two, a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts — that agreement between two of them is not a reason. Debated by himself, who observed that it is the only line of the eleven with any bite in it and that he would write it knowing that. Three, a woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary — that a person may withdraw a thing and is not to be called wrong for it. Debated; she named her own cost, which is that the line is about her. Five, a man of fifty-eight who inspects buses, expressly not the man of fifty-eight with the bad hip, a distinction about four people made aloud while he fetched a pen — that a thing which has failed twice is not a thing anybody should be committing to paper. Debated as being about the board; defended by him on twenty-two years of about nine vehicles a week and on being the only standing he has ever had, which he has never had in a room with a door in it; he put the cost of the sentence at twenty-two years. Six, the cook, taken off with her own thumb at about a quarter to nine and left showing daylight. Seven, the printer — that it will not be printed and will not be read by anyone absent from the room; the second half was called impossible. Eight, a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip — that a seat someone can occupy is neither a technique nor the denial of one; debated. Nine, the first-year — that the room will not carry a person's words to a place they cannot be called back from; debated as a duplicate. Ten, the printer a second time — that a duplicated leaf is a leaf nobody may withdraw. Debated against by the cook, who noted that it stands on the board, that the printer has been right about it every occasion, and that about nine hundred impressions of her own handwriting nonetheless left her counter in a morning; the printer replied that a kitchen drawer beats a board, and conceded that this is not a reason. Eleven, written last and signed separately beneath the other names, by the woman of thirty-one: that she may be telephoned about any of it and will come and reverse it. Unopposed. She named her own cost, that eleven identical words on a leaf of typing paper about a fortnight back concerned a key, and these concern one person who was not asked, and that she wants the same hand on it and wants it to be the one anybody rings.** A woman of thirty-four was in that room once that week, asked for nothing, and is not on the leaf and not named in this entry in any other capacity.

*The line that was lifted, and by whom.* **Composed at about half past eight by the cook, removed at about a quarter to nine in front of about nine people. Her ground: it came out of annoyance; such a line will be wrong by Friday; and a line that can be lifted should be lifted by the person who laid it down. She said aloud that she did it herself, that nobody helped, and that nobody had put a hand on that leaf all evening except to write on it. Nobody had.**

*The name at the foot, and the name nobody approached.* **Eight names stand at the bottom. The eighth belongs to a man of twenty-two who signed and wrote nothing, as he signed the board and wrote none of its twelve. He gave the reason once, unasked, in about nine seconds: that no sentence he writes about that room avoids being one with his name across the front of it; that one such sentence has sat in a rail in Lattice Ward for about a term and cost him the last thing he had; and that a first-year of nineteen should notice that nobody argued, and that a first-year of nineteen should notice that nobody argued. A woman of about thirty sat near the door for the whole afternoon and the leaf never reached her. The reason was announced in the room by the first-year and handed back by the woman in her own words: nobody has put a question to her in about twelve weeks, nobody is going to begin on a Monday afternoon holding paper, she would like to be left in the chair and not made into the thing a person is careful about, and nobody said that for her. Nobody did.**

*The number, and the scrap.* **The room was ninety-one days old, a quarter of a year. The first-year said so. The cook confirmed it, said she had counted twice because she counts things, and declined to say what it means, on the ground that she does not know and will not make a coincidence in front of nine people. She wrote it on a scrap and put the scrap in a pocket. The scrap is not reproduced here.**

*What the evening was and was not.* **About nine of the eleven lines were argued about, and the arguing is the content. The leaf carries eleven lines in seven hands and eight names. A first-year of nineteen stated, before anybody else could, that it is not a constitution and is not to be called a set of rules, and that anybody describing it as either in four years is describing nine people sitting in a room on a Monday afternoon. The account of what a rule is here was given before the first line was written and not after, and the leaf is not evidence of anything in any proceeding, there being none.**

*Not on the leaf, and not anywhere else.* **The wording of ten of the eleven lines, given above only where a line is itself the point of the record. The two people in that room who are neither on the leaf nor named in this entry. The scrap. No minute was taken, no notice issued, no report written, no form filled in, no committee constituted and no registration effected, and none of those exists in this city. Nobody in that room on that afternoon taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted a single thing, and what took place there was nine people writing with pens. No chapter-page restates the terms of his restriction, and no person in that room spoke them or a version of them. The standing question is not spoken, no rule about who may be asked anything appears on the leaf or was proposed in that room, and no figure is set against one. This chapter names no author of anything, empties no field, moves no card out of the fourth pocket of the tool roll, and receives nothing from anybody at all. Entry 239.**
