# Chapter 239 — A Key That Comes Back

On the Thursday evening of week eighty-two a man of twenty-two rang a woman of thirty-one in Bower Street and then handed a cut key back into her hand on a counter in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards, and he said the reason out loud in front of about eleven people, and the reason was true, and it was not that anybody had done anything wrong.

There are two keys in this case. One of them went back into a hand at about nine that night and the other one is still in a pocket, and nobody can take it back, and this chapter does not fix that.

---

The room was a hundred and one days old and there is a piece of a company's paper face up on the counter that has been there for fourteen days, and the sheet of kitchen paper with eleven lines on it was in a drawer in a kitchen, and the wall had twelve lines on it that were twenty-one days old and had not been added to since.

There were about eleven people in it at half past eight and four of them were standing.

He did not have a docket with him, which nobody remarked on and about nine people noticed, and which is the first Thursday in a fortnight that has gone without one.

---

He telephoned her from the office eleven miles away at about half past eight, in the corridor, on the number on the plate, and a letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file answered, gave the sentence about the frontage, and said hold on, and put it through, and that is the fifth time and she is not thanked for any of them.

"Ianthe."

"**Go on**," she said. "**I have a diary open and I am not going to close it and I am going to say the eleven words that are on the sheet of typing paper in a drawer in a room off a service road, and then you are going to tell me why you are ringing me on a Thursday evening, because a man who rings me on a Thursday evening is either ringing me about a key or about the heat.**"

"**It is about the key.**"

"**Then I will be there in about an hour and I am not bringing anything.**"

She came at about half past nine. She has a first floor in another building and a diary and she has been told twice this term that she has no standing, and she came, and nobody thanked her, and she was not thanked for coming on a Thursday evening to a service road and she did not appear to expect it.

---

He did not begin at the beginning and he did not apologise, and about nine people in that room had come in expecting something else.

"**I am going to say this once and I am going to say all of it and then I am going to stop, and if anybody in this room has a better reason I will hear it after, and I do not think anybody will.**"

He put a cut key on the counter. It is on a ring, and the paper label tied to it has worn down to a shape rather than to letters, so that it reads to a person who already knows and to nobody else.

"**That is yours. I have had it for about eleven weeks. I want to give it back and I am not doing it as a protest about anything and I am not doing it because of the money, and I want that said before anybody starts arranging their face.**"

---

"**Here is the reason and it is about nine words and then I am going to explain the nine words, which is against the rules I have just signed.**"

Nobody laughed.

"**A key is only as safe as a telephone.**"

Somebody said, "That is hers."

"**That is hers and she wrote it on a sheet of typing paper in her own hand and gave it to me in this room, and it has been the truest sentence anybody has said in this room for about eleven weeks, and I have let it sit there without acting on it for those eleven weeks, which is the whole of what I have done wrong.**"

"And that is the reason."

"**That is the first half of it.**" He turned the key round on the counter with one finger. "**I have two. One came off a counter in this room off a woman with a ring file, and the people behind her are a company, and a company is not a person and cannot be asked to take anything back, and so that one cannot be taken back by anybody, and I have known that for about eleven weeks and I have not done one single thing about it because there is nothing to do.**"

"I asked that on the back of a docket," said Asha Reed.

"**You asked it on the back of a docket about eleven weeks ago and four people answered it and one of the answers was nobody, and nobody wrote the answers down, and the answer about that key is still nobody this afternoon.**"

---

"**So. One key I cannot give back and one key I can. I have been carrying both in one pocket for eleven weeks. And in about four years a man with two keys in one pocket in a city where nobody knows all nine of them is a post, and the only defence this room has ever had against a holder becoming a post is that there is no list and nobody can produce it, and two keys in one pocket is how you start a list, because a person eventually writes down which pocket.**"

Somebody said, "That is about four years away."

"**It is about four years away and there are about nine keys wanted and there are two, and the whole of this room's argument about keys is that the people who handed them over can take them back, and I have been holding the one that proves the argument is a lie and the one that proves it is true, and I would like to be a man who is holding the one it is.**"

---

"**And now the part I have to say out loud, because it is the part that is about the two of you and I am not allowed to get away with only the part that is about me.**"

"Go on."

"**Nobody has done anything wrong.**" He said it to the room and not to the woman of thirty-one. "**Nobody in this room has done anything wrong in about fourteen weeks. A woman of thirty-one wrote eleven words on a sheet of typing paper in her own hand and handed me a key, and that is the best thing anybody has ever done for this room, and I am handing it back, and I want it said in this room that I am not handing it back to her. I am handing it back because of what a key is, and she is not the problem, and she was never the problem, and the woman with a ring file in another building is not the problem either, and neither am I, and I would like that written down somewhere and I know that it cannot be written down anywhere, so I have said it out loud and about nine of you have heard it.**"

The cook of twenty-seven said one word from the sink and it was not agreement and it was not disapproval.

"**What that word was for**," she said, "**is that you have just made yourself less able to open that door and you have done it out loud in front of about eleven people and not one of them is going to tell you it was a good thing, and I am not going to, and I do not think you did it to be good.**"

"No," he said. "**I did it because I could not carry it and I am not able to say why in fewer words than that.**"

---

The woman of thirty-one picked the key up at about ten to ten, looked at the label, and put it in her own pocket.

"**I am not going to make a list of this**," she said. "**I am not going to write it down, and I am not going to tell anybody I have got it, and if you want it again you will ring me, and if a ninth person ever holds one they will have to be nine, and I am not going to stand here and explain that to about eleven people because I have explained it once and it is on a scrap in a folder on a shelf in a room I have never been inside.**"

"What is on the scrap," said the man of thirty-four who works nights.

"**A question one of you wrote on the back of a docket.**"

"**Then it is the best sentence in this room and it is a question**," said the man of thirty-four, "**and I have wanted a person to telephone me for about two terms and I have just watched somebody hand a key back instead, and I am not going to be the one who says which of those is bigger.**"

---

And then the man of twenty-two said the last thing, and it was about tomorrow, and about nine people in that room already knew what he was going to say and about four of them had said it to themselves in the last fortnight.

"**I am going to say no to that piece of paper tomorrow at about half past seven, in about four minutes, and there is nothing on it I can answer, and the man who read it out was right about the one thing he was right about, and I have not slept properly about it.**"

"And the fortnight," said the cook of twenty-seven.

"**The fortnight of what.**"

"**The heat. It goes off on Monday and nobody in this room has ever worked out what it costs to run and a man asked me that yesterday morning and I could not answer him, and I am not blaming you for any of that and I am not letting anybody else blame you for it either, and I would like it written down that I said that, and it cannot be written down.**"

"**It cannot,**" he said. "**That is the correct answer and I have got no better one.**"

---

Nobody thanked him for handing the key back, and about four people said something about it afterwards on the service road, where it did not count.

A woman of about thirty sat in a chair near the door for the whole of the evening with her bag on her lap, was asked nothing by anybody, is on no object in that room, and has never once been asked why she comes, and she was there when a key went back into a hand and she was not asked what she made of it, which is the correct answer and is the whole of what this room has ever offered her.

He wrote one thing, on the floor, at about eleven, with his back against the wall.

*242. Thursday of week 82, at about eleven on the floor of that room with his back to a wall, and this entry is one key returned and one key nobody can reach.*

*The two objects and what each is worth.* **Both are cut keys on rings. One arrived on a counter in that room from a letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file, behind which stands a company; a company is not a person and is not asked to take anything back, so that key has no counterparty and no route out. The finding is about eleven weeks old, nothing has been done about it and nothing can be. The other carries a paper label so far gone that only somebody who knows the old words can read it; it arrived from a woman of thirty-one with a diary, accompanied by a leaf of typing paper on which eleven words in her own hand say that she may be telephoned and will come and remove it from a hand inside a day. That key lay on the counter about four minutes at about ten to ten and went into her own pocket. It is written down nowhere and she announced that she will not record it and will not disclose holding it.**

*The return, as a sequence of about four minutes of telephone.* **A call at about half past eight from a corridor, to the company's number on the plate. A letting clerk of thirty-nine answered, gave the standing sentence about the frontage, said hold on, and connected it. Fifth occasion; thanked on none. The woman of thirty-one took the call with her diary open, declined to close it, recited the eleven words from the leaf, and observed that a man telephoning on a Thursday evening is telephoning about a key or about the heat. He said key. She said she would be there in about an hour and would bring nothing, and arrived at about half past nine, and was thanked by nobody, and appeared to expect it.**

*The argument, in the order it was made.* **That the return is neither a protest nor connected to the money, said first so that nobody need rearrange a face afterwards. That a key is worth exactly as much as a telephone, which is the sentence on that leaf of typing paper; that he had let it sit eleven weeks without acting; and that this is the whole of what he has done wrong. That one key is unreachable because its counterparty is an organisation; that a first-year of nineteen reduced the question to a scrap on the back of a docket about four weeks earlier; that four people answered it; that nobody wrote the answers down; and that the answer for the unreachable key is still nobody. That eleven weeks of two keys in a single pocket is how a list starts, because a person eventually records which pocket; that in about four years a man with two keys in one pocket, in a city where nobody knows all nine, is a post; that the entire argument of that room about keys is that the giver can take them back; and that he has been holding the key that makes the argument false beside the key that makes it true and would rather hold the second. Then, to the room and not to the woman of thirty-one: that nothing done wrong occurred in fourteen weeks; that the eleven words on a leaf of typing paper are the best thing anybody has ever done for that room; that the key is not being handed back to her but because of what a key is; that she is not the problem, that the woman with a ring file is not the problem, and that he is not the problem; and that he would like it on paper and knows it cannot go on paper, and has therefore said it to about nine people.**

*What the room did with it.* **A cook of twenty-seven, from a sink, said one word that was neither assent nor refusal, and then gave its purpose: he has made himself less able to open that door; he did it aloud in front of about eleven people; nobody there will call that a good thing; she will not; and she does not believe he did it to be good. He agreed he could not carry the key and could not put the reason in fewer words. The woman of thirty-one lifted it at about ten to ten, looked at the label, put it in her pocket, and said she will not draw up a list, will not write it down, will not say she holds it; that he rings her if he wants it again; that a ninth holder would have to be the ninth; and that she will not explain any of that to about eleven people, having explained it once, on a scrap in a folder on a shelf in a room she has never entered. A man of thirty-four who works nights asked what the scrap says, was told it is a question one of them wrote on the back of a docket, and answered that the best sentence in the room is a question, that he has wanted somebody to telephone him for about two terms, that he has just watched a key handed back instead, and that he declines to say which of the two is larger.**

*Tomorrow.* **He will decline the paper on the counter at about half past seven on the Friday, in about four minutes; there is nothing on it he can answer; the man who read it aloud was right about the single thing he was right about; and he has not slept properly about it. The cook of twenty-seven then said the heat goes off on the Monday, that nobody in that room has ever established what it costs to run, that a man put that question to her the previous morning and she had no answer, that she is not blaming him and will not permit anyone else to, and that she would like it written down and it cannot be written down. He agreed that it cannot, called that the correct answer, and said he has nothing better.**

*The key that stayed.* **Untouched, unreachable, and raised by nobody in that room that evening except the man carrying it. No holder was invented, no lock fitted, no list made and no mechanism proposed. The question on the back of the docket has four answers, three of them about people and one about nobody, and none of the four was written down. This entry does not treat the fifth as his fault and does not treat it as anything else. The chapter settles nothing about it.**

*Also in the room.* **A woman of about thirty spent the evening in a chair near the door with a bag on her lap, was asked nothing by anybody, is on no object in that room, and is in no file in this case. She was there when the key went back into a hand and was not asked for her view of it, which is the correct answer and the whole of what that room has ever put at her disposal. The kitchen-paper leaf is in a drawer; the company page has lain face up for a fortnight; the board has carried the same twelve lines for twenty-one days and received nothing.** The man of twenty-two carried no docket for the first time in about thirteen weeks, nobody remarked on it, and about nine people noticed.

*The eleven words, the four answers, and the state of a label.* **The wording of the eleven words, which are on a leaf in a folder on a shelf in a building he has never entered. The four answers and the reason none of them was written down. The state of the label. No page, form, list, minute, notice, report or register was made by anybody in this chapter and none exists, and a folder is a folder. No one in that room was taught anything, shown anything, assessed on anything or helped with anything on that Thursday evening, and there was no exchange of observations, no relayed plate and no practice of any kind. The terms of his restriction are neither quoted nor paraphrased in this chapter, and no one in that room said a version of them. The standing question is not spoken, no rule about who may be asked anything was made, and no figure is set against one. The licensor is not named, the field remains blank, the card in the fourth pocket did not move and its fifth line was not discussed, and no message of any kind arrived from anybody. Entry 242.**
