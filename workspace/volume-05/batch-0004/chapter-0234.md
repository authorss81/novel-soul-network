# Chapter 234 — Nine Minutes

A man of forty-one read an offer out loud in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards on the Thursday evening of week eighty, in about nine minutes, and the terms on the sheet were better than anybody in that room had any right to expect, and the argument underneath them was better still, and he was right that there is no money in the room, and nobody in that room could answer him and about four people tried in about four minutes.

The room was eighty-seven days old. A sheet of a company's paper had been lying face down on that counter since the Monday and nobody had turned it over.

---

The room was busy at about half past seven and smaller at the same time, which is what it does now, and about eleven people were in it, and four of them were standing in about two feet of floor.

The cook of twenty-seven had the kettle on at half past eight at the point in the day it goes, and a man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was in the fourth chair with a bag of spanners he has never opened in that building, and a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip was on a chair against the wall, and a printer of fifty-eight with four feet of shop was in the second chair, and a woman of about thirty was in the third chair from the door with her bag on her lap and has never been asked anything by anybody.

A woman of twenty-four was at the back with her hand on the counter and not on the binder, and nobody addressed her and she addressed nobody, and she had not been in that room more than four times since the spring and has never been in a photograph of it, and that is not modesty and not a rule.

And a man of forty-one came in at about ten past seven and gave his name, and it was Vey, and nobody wrote it down.

He was not late and he did not apologise for being early. He had a coat he kept on, which nobody in that room understood until the end.

---

He did not sit down and he did not put anything on the counter, and the first thing he said was the true thing.

"**There is no money in this room.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I have been in this city eleven weeks and I have looked for four months at a thing that does not exist, and I want to say the reason I am standing in your doorway before I say anything else: there is no money in here, and I have not found anybody who thinks there is, and that is the only reason a man of my age is on a service road on a Thursday.**"

"Go on," said Asha Reed.

"**I am going to read you a sheet. It is one page. It has a figure on it and it has five lines under the figure and there is no name on it and there is not going to be a name on it. It will take me about nine minutes and then I will stop talking and you will have about four minutes and I am telling you now that I know four minutes is not enough and I am doing it anyway, because the last thing I want is for anybody here to think I have come to hurry you.**"

---

And he read it out, in a voice that never rose above the noise of the kettle, and about nine people listened and nobody interrupted him once.

"**One. The heat, the light, the water and the two sockets that work are paid every quarter, from the day you opened, and they are paid whether or not anybody comes through that door in the whole of the quarter.**"

"**Two. Nine chairs, and nine more when there are nine people who want to sit down. A floor that does not slope. A back door that does not want a millimetre off the top edge. And a sounder on that wall that somebody standing on that service road can hear.**"

Somebody said, "That is on the sheet."

"**That is on the sheet. Read the rest.**"

"**Three. Nothing that is said in that room is printed, photographed, described, put on a course, or used in any material, by us or by anybody we pay, and that sentence is on the sheet in writing and not a conversation.**"

"**Four. No person in that room gives a name to anybody. No list, no form, no registration, no reference on any paper belonging to this, and no name on any cheque, which means there is no account and the money is paid into a pool that has about nine hundred of your doors behind it already and not one of them knows the other eight hundred and ninety-nine.**"

"**Five. Nobody from where I work will be in that room more than once a month. They will come when they are asked and not otherwise, and they will leave the moment anybody in that room asks them to, and if you ask them to leave twice then nobody comes again.**"

He turned the sheet over and put it on the counter face up, which was the first time that piece of paper had been the right way up in three days.

"**And there is a sixth thing and it is not on the sheet, and you may have it for nothing. You may refuse this in a fortnight. When you do, we owe you nothing, we ask you for nothing, we print nothing about it, and we do not come back. I have put a line in our own file this afternoon and I would not tell you what it says, and it is the only line in that file about this room, and you will never see it.**"

---

The cook of twenty-seven answered that one first, and she answered it from the sink and she did not turn round.

"**The sounder.**"

"Yes."

"**That sounder has been on that wall for twelve weeks. It rings and nothing happens, because a sound at a man on a step does not make anybody come. I put two screws into the top of it myself on a Thursday night with nine people in this room and nobody asked me to and I have not been thanked for it once, and in about four years the reason that stair is never examined again is a pack of cells I replaced when it did not need replacing.**"

"Yes," said the man of forty-one. "**And the sounder is the cheapest line on that sheet and it is the only line on it I would defend in a room like this one.**"

---

Then the first-year of nineteen found the thing that is actually wrong with the sheet, and she took about four minutes, and about four people in that room had already half of it.

"**You have written that no person in this room gives a name to anybody.**"

"That is clause four."

"**Yes. And somebody in your building is going to have to put a number on a piece of paper to get that money into a bank, and the number is going to be ours, because yours already exists and ours does not, and you have not thought about that and neither would I if I had your job.**"

"Go on."

"**Money is a name. It is the only name on this sheet and it is the only one that cannot be refused, and in about four years there is a piece of paper in a building four miles from here with a number on it that has got this room behind it, and a number is findable, and a leaflet is not findable, and a photograph is not findable, and a lease is not findable, and you have just handed us the one kind of paper that is.**"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"**That is not an objection,**" said the man of forty-one. "**That is a description of what happens next and I have not got an answer to it and I have had eleven weeks.**"

---

And then the reasoning came, and it was better than the terms, and it lasted about two minutes.

"**You have a line on that wall in a cook's hand that says the room will not keep anything it cannot take back, and it is the best sentence anybody in this city has written in about four years, and I have not come here to buy it. I have come because of the other thing.**"

"Which thing."

"**That the line has no force. You all know that. Not one of you could make a man of fifty-eight do a single thing with it and he has said so out loud in this room and he was right and it was still the right answer. And I am not a thief and I am not a man in a lanyard, and I want to say the sentence that has been sitting under your whole wall since you opened, and I have not said it in a room before and I am aware that I am saying it to about nine people who did not ask me for it.**"

"Go on."

"**A rule against being bought is worth nothing to a room that has to choose between heat and being bought. In about four years one of you is going to need a wage, and the room is going to need something that is not a chair, and the person who finds the wage is going to own that wall, and they will not steal it and they will not even mean to. They will simply be the one who pays, and a thing that is paid for is a thing that is expected, and I have watched that happen four times and I have been the person who paid twice.**"

The room was quiet in a way that is not describable and is not a rule.

"**That is my whole case and it took two minutes and it is not on the sheet, and I am not going to improve on it if you ask me to.**"

---

Four people tried to answer him in about four minutes and none of it worked, and the fourth of them was the man of twenty-two, who had been at the back with a docket for all of it, and who was the only one who made it worse by about a mile.

"**There is one thing I can add and it is against me and I would rather say it here than have somebody else say it on Friday.**"

"Go on."

"**I am the only person in this city you can telephone about that room. I have known it since the spring. There is a card in a rail in a building in Lattice Ward with my name on it in my own hand and a company's number on the outside of that building, and every person who has found that room in about two months found it through a plate that leads to a woman with a ring file, and every one of those people then ended up wanting to talk to me.**"

He put the docket down on the counter.

"**So if I am the person whose signature moves your money, then the first thing anybody in your building does when the money stops is ring me, and I will be the man who decides whether a room off a service road is a room, and I have been telling this room since the Monday three weeks ago that nobody in here can be asked whether he is right, and I have been wrong about that in one direction only and I did not know which direction until this evening.**"

Nobody improved on it and nobody told him it was all right.

---

The woman of twenty-four said one thing at about half past eight, from the back, to the room and not to him.

"**You have written in clause three that nothing said in that room will be described in any material by anybody you pay. I have spent a year watching people agree to that sentence and then find a way round it that they can defend. There is always a way round it and it is never a lie. I have watched about nine of them and none of the nine was a villain and two of them are people I would have sworn to.**"

"And yours," said the man of forty-one.

"**Mine has not been asked for and I would like it to stay that way and I am not going to explain why, and you will not get an answer out of that, and I am aware that I have just told you there is a way round your clause and then declined to say what it is.**"

"That is fair."

"**It is not fair. It is a warning and I have given about nine of them this year and I have never once given one in a room that had a line about promises on the wall.**"

Nobody wrote any of it down and nobody asked her about it.

---

He left at about ten past eight, which is inside the nine minutes he had allowed himself plus four, and he did not leave the sheet, and the sheet went on the counter face up and stayed face up all night, which is the first time in the history of that counter that a piece of paper had lain on it the right way up and been nobody's business.

A woman of about thirty was in the third chair from the door for the whole of it, and was asked for nothing, and is on no object in that room, and nobody told the man of forty-one a single thing about her, and he did not see her face, because he does the thing he does and the room is not what he does.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card and is on no form.

He wrote one thing, on a chair in that room, at about ten, with the shutter up and nobody else in it.

*237. Thursday of week 80, at about ten on a chair in the room off the service road with the shutter up and the lamp off and about eleven people gone and the sheet face up on the counter, and this entry is nine minutes and one figure.*

*The man, as a source and not as a mechanism.* **Forty-one; name given at the door, unrecorded, not a villain, not a liar, not defeated at any point in this chapter and not thanked. He is not told anything about the licensor, the photograph, the pack, the nine hundred, the woman of about thirty, or the man who works nights, and he says nothing about any of them because none of them is mentioned to him. He has been in the city eleven weeks looking for four months for a thing that does not exist, and he says so before he says anything else.**

*The sheet, as it was read.* **One page, one figure, five numbered clauses, no name on it and none to go on it. One: heat, light, water and the two working sockets, quarterly, from the opening day, payable whether or not anybody comes through the door in a quarter. Two: nine chairs and nine more when there are nine who want them; a floor that does not slope; a back door with nothing off its top edge; a sounder audible from the service road. Three: nothing said in that room to be printed, photographed, described, put on a course or used in any material by his company or by anybody it pays, stated in writing and not as a conversation. Four: no person in that room gives a name to anybody, and no list, form, registration or reference on any paper of his, and therefore no account and no cheque name, the money being paid into a pool already standing behind about nine hundred doors none of which knows the other eight hundred and ninety-nine. Five: no member of his company in that room more than once a month, on invitation only, leaving immediately on request, and never again after a second request. A sixth condition was given aloud and is not on the page: refusal inside a fortnight, with nothing owed, nothing asked, nothing printed, and no return, supported by one line in his own file which he declined to describe and which is the only line in it about that room.**

*The argument, which is the part that held the room.* **He put the wall's first claim against itself. That the line about not keeping what cannot be taken back has no force, that the man of fifty-eight has said so in that room and was right, and that his case is not about the line. His case, in his own construction and not improved: that a rule against being bought is worth nothing to a room that must choose between heat and being bought; that in about four years one of them will need a wage and the room will need something that is not a chair; that the person who finds the wage will own the wall without stealing it and without meaning to, being simply the one who pays; and that a thing paid for is a thing expected, which he has watched four times and paid for twice. He said he was aware he was saying it to nine people who had not asked him and that he would not improve it if asked.**

*The three answers that were tried and the one that failed hardest.* **A cook of twenty-seven took the sounder clause first, described a sounder on that wall that rings and changes nothing, said she put two screws into it herself on a Thursday night with nine people present and unasked and has not been thanked for it, and connected it to a pack of cells replaced when it did not need to be; he agreed the sounder is the cheapest line on the sheet and the only one he would defend in that room. A first-year of nineteen then took about four minutes on clause four: money is a name, the only name on the sheet and the only one that cannot be refused; a number will be needed to move the money, it will be that room's because the other number exists already; and a number is findable where a leaflet, a photograph and a lease are not, so the sheet supplies the one class of paper that is. He accepted the description and said he has no answer to it and has had eleven weeks. A woman of twenty-four then warned, once, that clause three is always circumvented by a route that is not a lie, that she has watched about nine such routes and that none of those nine was a villain and two are people she would have sworn to; she said her own route has not been asked for, declined to describe it, and noted that she has never given that warning in a room with a line about promises on the wall. Nobody wrote any of it down. He then said his case was not improved by anybody.**

*The sentence from the back of the room.* **A man of twenty-two, with a docket, who had not spoken until then, asked leave to add a thing that was against him. That he is the only person in this city who can be telephoned about that room; that he has known it since the spring; that a card in a rail in a building with a plate on the outside of it carries his name in his own hand; that every person who has located that room in about two months did it through a plate and a letting clerk with a ring file and then wanted to talk to him; and that the sentence about nobody in that room being askable was said on a Monday three weeks earlier and has been the room's working assumption ever since. Therefore, if his signature moves the money, the first action in that company when the money stops is to ring him, and he would then be the person deciding whether a room off a service road is a room, and he has been telling that room for thirteen weeks that nobody in it can be asked whether he is right and was wrong in one direction without knowing which. Nobody improved on it and nobody told him it was all right. This entry records the statement and not a verdict, and no rule about who may be asked anything was made, proposed or implied in this room on this day.**

*The counter after he had gone.* **The sheet lay face up until the shutter came down, which is the first time any paper has lain on that counter the right way up. A woman of about thirty sat in the third chair from the door for the whole of those nine minutes, carrying a bag on her lap. Nobody asked her anything. She is on no object in that room and in no file in this case. The man of forty-one was told nothing whatever about her and did not see her face. The kettle went on at half past eight at its fixed point. Nothing was accepted, nothing was refused, nothing was signed, nothing was sent, and nothing was minuted; the offer remains on the counter and no chapter of this movement resolves it except the last.**

*Excluded on purpose.* **The figure; the company; the pool and what stands behind it beyond the count of doors; the wording of the sixth condition; the file and the line in it; what the woman of twenty-four declined to say; the two questions the man of fifty-one with a tool bag asked and did not press, which are not recorded because he did not press them and asked twice and was not thanked for either. Nobody in that room, and nobody on its page, restates or paraphrases the terms of what he may not do in that department. The standing question is referred to and not spoken. There was no teaching that evening, no demonstration, no assessment, no assistance, no share and no practice, and not one person in that room was shown a thing by another. No one in this chapter says who wrote anything into a blank field, the field is empty, the original card remains in the fourth pocket of the roll with its fifth line unfilled, and nothing was received from anyone.**
