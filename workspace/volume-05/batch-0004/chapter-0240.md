# Chapter 240 — A Fortnight Of Heat

The room off a service road in the Lower Wards refused an offer of money on the Friday evening of week eighty-two, in about four minutes, with about eleven people in it, and the refusal cost a fortnight of heating in that room, and the heating is not paid for by anybody in this volume, and it is not resolved, and the person it costs is not the man who said no.

Nobody was thanked. A woman of about thirty sat in a chair near the door for the whole of it and nobody apologised to her, and that was correct, and it was the whole of the movement.

---

Grade three from twenty to eight, nine on the docket and nine in date, and he has had a night when every one of them was in date and he has not thought about that night since it happened.

A bin store timer that is not the fault, a tap, a landing light, a fitting in a stair of a type nobody has made since before his apprenticeship, two lift calls, an emergency pack, a cupboard light off a service road, and a fire door on a second landing in Nine Bridges whose closer he adjusted on the Wednesday of last week, again on the Friday, and again this evening, which is three times in nine days, and which came up this evening for a third time with a stamped number in the description box that he did not write and that a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket had copied out of a frame about four feet up with a step-ladder.

He went and looked at the frame. It is as he wrote it. He adjusted the closer a third time and wrote the word *frame* in the box again, and it will be back on the Monday, and he will not be the person anybody asks.

The word was at nine all morning and at ten from about one in the afternoon and it stayed at ten.

---

The company page had been face up on that counter for fifteen days.

There were about eleven people in the room at half past seven and four of them were standing in about two feet of floor, and a sheet of kitchen paper carrying eleven lines was in a drawer in a kitchen whose doors open in the afternoon, and the hardboard had twelve lines on it that were twenty-two days old, and a board over the door carried four words in a sign-writer's hand and, above those four words, eight more that were not in a sign-writer's hand and that nobody had seen until that evening.

The refusal took about four minutes and about nine people spoke in it.

---

"**I am going to say the thing first so that nobody can say afterwards that they were not told.**" Asha Reed had the page face up in front of her and had not touched it. "**We are saying no. Not because the terms are bad, because they are the best terms anybody has put in front of this room and the man who read them out said so himself before he read them. And not because we do not need the money, because there is no money in this room and a man of thirty-seven told us that in this room on a Monday and he was right.**"

"Then why," said somebody, and it was not an argument, it was a person in a room.

"**Because there is nothing on that page any of us can put a reply to, and I have been in this room every day for about fourteen weeks waiting for a reply to it and it has not come, and I would like it noted that I was not looking for one.**"

---

"**And I want to say my half and I want it said fast**," said the man of twenty-two, from the back, with no docket, for the second Friday in a row.

"Go on."

"**I am not refusing that page because it is bad. I refused to read the thing on it as a question about the offer, which is what I have been doing since the Friday of three weeks ago, and about four minutes ago a first-year of nineteen told me that I have not been looking for a reply and I have been waiting to be asked what I think, and I have not asked, and the two of those are the same thing.**"

Nobody improved on it.

"**And the other half. I have not voted. There are about nine of you in here and every one of you can be relieved of a thing at a telephone inside a day, and I cannot be relieved of this room at all, and I have not been relieved of it tonight, and that is the thing I would like written down and there is nowhere to write it.**"

---

The cook of twenty-seven put the kettle on at half past eight at the point in the day it goes, which is what she has done for about fourteen weeks, and a kettle is not heating and everybody in that room has known it since the second week and nobody had ever said it.

"**I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be argued with and I am going to say one number and then I am going to wash up**," she said. "**A fortnight. The heat goes off on Monday and nobody has paid for it and nobody is going to, and I have not got the money and it is not mine to find and I am not the person who is going to stand in this room on a Friday night and pretend that a kettle is a radiator.**"

Somebody said, "Can we find it."

"**No. And I want to say why no, because about four of you are going to think of a way. The only way anybody finds money for a room like this is a person who wants something, and we have spent fourteen weeks turning down every version of that, and the heat is what the turning down costs, and I have been putting the kettle on at half past eight every day for fourteen weeks and pretending in about nine different ways that I had not noticed.**"

---

At about eight a man of thirty-seven came for the page, and he came in his own coat, and he did not sit down, and he did not say he was sorry.

"**I told you on a Monday there was no money in that room and I have been proved right about a thing I was not talking about**," he said. "**The line in our file is going to say the offer was declined. I have been told I am not permitted to write why and I am not going to write why, and I would like somebody in this room to understand that I have been given an instruction about my own handwriting and I have obeyed it and that is the whole of my contribution to this evening.**"

Nobody thanked him and he was not thanked on the Monday either, and he has a job and no standing in this case and is not a mechanism.

He took the page off the counter and the counter has had nothing on it since.

---

At about a quarter to nine a man of about forty-five stood in the doorway, and it was obvious at once that he had never been in the room, and he said he had read about the room on a piece of paper in about eleven hundred pockets and had never been in it, and he asked one question, and it was one question, and the man of twenty-two answered it in nine words.

"**Will you still be here?**"

"**I have said nine words to a person from outside this room three times in this case**," he said, before the answer, "**and I am not going to improve on the other three, and I am saying that first so that nobody can decide afterwards that it was a sentence.**"

"Nobody knows who will be here in a fortnight."

The man at the door stood there for about nine seconds.

"**That is a no**," he said.

"**It is not a no**," said the man of twenty-two. "**It is nine words and it is the truth, and the truth is not a no, and I am not able to do better than that and I have tried for a fortnight.**"

The man at the door went away and nobody asked him who he was and nobody wrote him down, and there is no record anywhere in this city that he stood in that doorway on that evening.

---

And then somebody looked at the board over the door, which has four words on it in a sign-writer's hand, and above those four words there were eight more.

It is warmer in here than out there.

Nobody had seen them. Asha Reed said so, out loud, standing up, and about nine people stood up, and a cook of twenty-seven at a sink did not turn round and said one thing about them and would not improve it.

"**I wrote them on the Thursday night after the eleven of you had gone, and I came back on the Friday morning, and I am not going to be told off about it. A line somebody wrote by hand costs the writer something and it is not enforceable against anybody who did not write it, and I have not told anybody in this room what it cost me, and I am not going to tonight.**"

"What did it cost."

"**That room is warm on a Friday because I put a kettle on at half past eight every day for about fourteen weeks, and a kettle is not heating, and it has not been heating since the second week, and about nine people in here have known that the entire time.**"

---

And Asha Reed said the last thing anybody said in that room, and she said it standing, and it took about nine seconds, and about nine people heard it.

"**That board is not the wall and the two of them are not the same object. The first line on that hardboard is still the first line on that hardboard and it is a cook's and it says the room will not keep anything it cannot take back, and it is not a rule and it is not enforceable against anybody and it is not evidence of anything in any proceeding. The eight words over that door are not a rule either. And from Monday they are going to be the only line in this room that is not true, and I would like everybody to look at it now while it is still true.**"

Nobody said anything.

About eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card and is on no form, and about four of them come to a room off a service road on a Friday because it is warm, and one of those four has a bag on her lap and has been coming on every working day since the Friday of week sixty-nine and has never once been asked why, and nobody apologised to her, and she was not thanked, and she is owed nothing, and she is the person this fortnight is paid for.

The shutter came down at about ten and the room was still warm and it will not be on Monday.

He wrote one thing, on a service road in the dark, at about ten, at a shutter that was down and cold on the outside of it.

*243. Friday of week 82, at about ten on a service road in the dark, at a shutter that is down and cold on the outer face of it, and this entry is one refusal, one fortnight, and eight words above a door.*

*The night, itemised.* **Nine on the docket and nine in date, grade three from twenty to eight. A bin-store timer, a tap, a landing light, a stair fitting of a type not made since before his apprenticeship, two lift calls, an emergency pack, a light in a cupboard off a service road, and a stair fire door whose closer he has reset three times since the Wednesday, and which arrived a third time this evening carrying a stamped identifier in the description box that he did not write, copied by a district man of thirty-eight off the frame at about four feet with a step-ladder. He went and looked; the frame is as he wrote it. He reset the closer a third time, wrote the word again, expects it on the Monday, and will not be the person anybody approaches. Nine in date out of nine is the second such Friday in about a term and a half and both were accidents.** The unnameable rate was nine all morning, ten from about one, and ten all night. The city's standing weekly figure is unaltered and stands on the back of a woman's own card and on no form, and about four of those people come to that room on a Friday because it is warm.

*What was standing in the room.* **Eleven at half past seven, four of them standing in about two feet of floor. The company page, upper side up, on the counter for a fifteenth day. The kitchen-paper leaf in a drawer in a kitchen whose doors open in the afternoon. The board carrying twelve lines, twenty-three days without addition. The man of twenty-two with no docket, the second Friday running. The board over the door, carrying four words in a sign-writer's hand and, above them, eight in another hand, which nobody had observed until that evening and which nobody removed.** The refusal took about four minutes and about nine people spoke in it.

*How the refusal was reasoned.* **A first-year of nineteen stated it before anybody could compose a face: the answer is no; not because the terms are poor, since the reader said before reading them that they were the best anybody had put to that room; and not because the money is unwanted, a man of thirty-seven having said on the Monday of the previous week that there is no money in that room and being right. Asked why, she said nothing on that page can be answered by anyone in it; that she has been there daily for fourteen weeks awaiting an answer that has not come; and that she was not seeking one. The man of twenty-two added two halves from the back. That he declined to treat the page as a question about the offer, which is what he had been doing since the previous Friday, and that a first-year told him he had been waiting to be asked his opinion and had not asked, and that the two are one thing. And that he did not vote; that about nine of those present can be relieved of a thing by telephone inside a day; that he cannot be relieved of that room at all and was not relieved of it that evening; and that he would like it recorded and has nowhere to record it. Nobody improved on either.**

*The figure, and who supplied it.* **A cook of twenty-seven set the kettle going at its fixed point and then, without stopping work and without being asked, said she would not be thanked, would not be argued with, and would give one figure and then wash up. The figure is a fortnight. The heating goes off on the Monday; nobody has paid for it; nobody is going to; she does not have the money and it is not hers to find; and she is not going to stand in a room on a Friday night and pass off a kettle as a radiator.** Asked whether it could be found, she said no, and gave her ground: the only route by which money is found for a room of this kind is a person who wants something; every version of that has been refused for fourteen weeks; the heating is what the refusing costs; and she has been setting that kettle going daily for fourteen weeks while pretending in about nine separate ways that she had not noticed.

*The page leaving the counter.* **Collected at about eight by the man of thirty-seven, in his own coat, without sitting down, and without apology. He said he had been shown right on the Monday about a matter he had not been discussing; that his file will record the offer as declined; that he is forbidden to record the reason and will not; and that he has been given an instruction about his own handwriting and has complied, and that this constitutes his entire contribution to the evening. Nobody thanked him then and nobody thanked him on the Monday. He holds no standing in this case, and is no part of any mechanism. The counter has carried nothing since.**

*The nine words, and to whom.* **A man of about forty-five at the doorway, who had not been in that room before, said he had read about it on a sheet circulating in about eleven hundred pockets, and asked one question: whether it will still be here. The man of twenty-two announced beforehand that he has said nine words to somebody from off that room three times in this case, that he is not improving on the other three, and that he says so first so that nobody may afterwards treat it as a sentence.** The answer, nine words: nobody knows who will be here in a fortnight. The questioner said it was a no. He was told it is not a no, that it is nine words and the truth, that the truth is not a no, and that he has been trying for a fortnight to do better. The man left. Nobody asked his name, nobody wrote him down, and no record exists anywhere in this city that he stood in that doorway.

*The eight words.* **Seen at about a quarter to nine on a board that has carried four words in a sign-writer's hand since the second week. Above them, in another hand and another ink: that it is warmer in here than out there, which is eight words and which nobody counted.** A first-year of nineteen stated that nobody had seen them. A cook of twenty-seven, at a sink, without turning round, said she wrote them on the Thursday night after the room emptied and returned on the Friday morning; that she is not going to be told off about it; that a line written by hand costs its writer something, binds nobody who did not write it, and that she is not going to say what it cost her tonight. Asked outright, she said the room is warm on a Friday because she has set that kettle going nightly for about fourteen weeks, that a kettle does not heat, that it has not heated since the second week, and that about nine people in that room have known it throughout.

*The final sentence of the evening.* **Spoken by a first-year of nineteen, standing, in about nine seconds, to about nine people: that the board over the door is not the wall and the two are not a single object; that the first line on the hardboard remains the first line on the hardboard, is a cook's, concerns what the room will not keep, is not a rule, binds nobody, and is evidence of nothing in any proceeding; that the eight words above the door are not a rule either; and that from the Monday they will be the only line in that room that is untrue, and that she would like everybody to look at it while it is. Nobody said anything after it.**

*Who bears it.* **The fortnight of heating is not paid for by any person in this volume and is not resolved anywhere in it. No fund, collection, subscription, grant, list, minute or appeal exists, and none was proposed by anybody in that room. The cost falls on the people who come because the room is warm, and among them on a woman of about thirty who was in a chair near the door for the whole evening with a bag on her lap, who has attended on every working day since that Friday in week sixty-nine, who has been asked nothing by anybody in fourteen weeks, who is on no object in that room and is in no file in this case, who was not thanked, who is owed nothing by anybody, and to whom nobody apologised, which was the correct course and is the whole of what this movement cost.** The man who said no does not pay it and is owed nothing by anybody. This entry does not pay it, does not resolve it, and proposes no mechanism for it.
