# Chapter 237 — What This Room Has Got Wrong

On the Thursday evening of week eighty-one a room off a service road in the Lower Wards read out its own record, in about eleven minutes, in a room with about eleven people in it, and the first thing it read was the worst thing anybody in it had ever done, and a woman of fifty-eight read nine words of her own out loud in a room for the first time in her life, and a cook of twenty-seven read eleven items of which the eleventh had a single stroke through it, and the reason the room did it is the only reason anybody in it would come.

---

The hardboard came off the two nails at about half past seven and went on a chair, and about nine people looked at the bare wall behind it, which has had nothing on it since the second week of this volume, and nobody said anything about that.

Asha Reed said the terms of the evening before anybody sat down, and she said them the way she says everything, which is once.

"**We are going to read out what this room has done and what this room has got wrong, and the wrong parts are the point, and I have decided that and I am not taking a vote and there is nothing to take a vote about.**"

"Why the wrong parts," said somebody.

"**Because a record of what a room does right is a document about a room, and we have all learned that this month the hard way. And because about nine of the eleven people here have not been in a room that could tell them what it had got wrong, and I have been in about nine of those, and I did not know that was what was missing until somebody in here told me what they had got wrong out loud with about nine people watching.**"

---

And she went first, and she said before she started that she was going first, and nobody had asked her to, and the reason was hers.

"**In week seventy-two, on a Tuesday, in about four seconds, I said yes to a photograph of this room to a woman I had never met who had telephoned the number on the plate, and six people were standing in this room when it was taken and I asked not one of them.**"

Somebody said, "We know."

"**I know that you know. You have all known for about a fortnight and nobody has made me say it, and I have been carrying four seconds for about seven weeks and I have just decided to say them in a room, and that is the whole of what I have to give and it is not going to get better by being said quietly.**"

"And the cost."

"**About eleven hundred leaflets in about eleven hundred pockets in a market in this city have my name at the bottom of them in my own hand and there is no mechanism in this city that takes a name off a piece of paper, and I put it there on purpose after two people told me that a description of a room is a description of the people in it, and I have been the only nineteen-year-old in this room since the day I walked into it, and about four people in this room have wanted to ask me something for about two months and have not.**"

Nobody asked her anything.

---

The printer of fifty-eight read hers, and it took her a while to get to it, and about nine people knew what was coming and let her take the time.

"**It is nine words. They are on a clean sheet in that binder and they are about a proof and a line that will be read wrong, and I wrote them in this room in the first fortnight, in my own hand, in about nine seconds.**"

She did not look at the sheet.

"**I have never read them out loud in a room. I have printed about nine hundred leaves of a company's pack with my own handwriting on the second page at nine inches high, and about four hundred people have read my nine words inside somebody else's document, and I am the only person in this city who can tell you with certainty that a printed page cannot be un-printed, having been wrong about that four times in forty years and right once, and being right once has cost me sleep and nothing else, because nobody rang the shop and the pack carries no number that is mine.**"

She read the nine words out loud, in her own voice, in a room, and her voice did not go up and did not go down.

And then: "**That is the first time. I have wanted to do that for a fortnight and I have not done it because I did not know whether the room was a place where a woman of fifty-eight is allowed to hear her own handwriting read out by herself, and now I know.**"

Nobody thanked her and nobody improved on it.

---

The cook of twenty-seven read eleven items out loud, and the eleventh had a single stroke through a person's name, top left to bottom right, not retraced, not tidied, done with a biro in about a second and a half, and the whole of that room had watched it happen and nobody had stopped it.

"**Eleven things. The first nine are mine and the tenth is mine and the eleventh is a woman's name that I put there because I thought a name was a courtesy.**"

"Whose name," said somebody, and then said, "Don't tell me."

"**I am not going to.**" She had the sheet. "**I put a sister's name in the eleventh place on a line that is about a pot, and the pot is mine and the name was not, and she telephoned me at work on the Thursday and asked for it to be taken off, and that is the entire content of the telephone call and I have never repeated it in a room and I am not going to now.**"

"It is still struck through."

"**It is still struck through and it cannot be un-struck, because a finger takes the ink off and leaves the paper, and in about four years somebody is going to find a name with a ghost through it and no sign that anybody asked, and the only record of the request is a smudge.**" She put the sheet down. "**And I was told at the time that I was not the person holding the pen, and I was right, and I have thought about that sentence every day for a fortnight, and it is on the wall in a first-year's hand, and it is the only line in this room that has ever cost anybody anything to write.**"

---

A woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary read hers, and it was short, and the cost of it was that she had been findable about one thing for about a fortnight and was now going to be findable about two.

"**A cut key on a ring with a faded label, given on this counter, with eleven words on a sheet of typing paper in my own hand saying that I could be telephoned and would come and take it out of a hand inside a day. And a clean sheet written from a telephone call by a woman in another district, about a man who was standing in this room, which is the only page in that binder nobody in this room can take back, and which exists because a cook of twenty-seven said go ahead and a first-year of nineteen announced out loud that she was about to do the thing the rule in another room is against.**"

"And the cost."

"**I gave a rule away and I wrote the same rule on the end of a sheet of paper on Monday night, so now there is a piece of paper in a kitchen in this city with my telephone habits in it and not one thing on it about a room, and I am going to be findable about two things instead of one, and one of them is a sheet of eleven lines that about nine people in this room have now heard about.**"

---

A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts read the shortest thing in that binder, and it was about nine words, and he said it and then said why it was the shortest thing in there.

"**It is about nine words and it is not a practice. It is a use.**" He had the sheet. "**I am on an employer's Friday list in a building about eleven miles from here and I am not in the same place on two Fridays running, and that sheet is the only page in that binder that says which building I am in, and about four people in this room have read it, and my employer does not know it exists.**"

"Cost," said somebody.

"**About four people.**"

---

A man of fifty-eight with a bad hip read his, and it took him about a minute, and he was the only person in that room who had ever been refused anything by it.

"**I came here on a Thursday afternoon with nine steps that are my sister's, and this room would not take them, and it was right, and I have thought about that for about five weeks now instead of a fortnight.**"

Somebody said, "That is not a failure."

"**It is a failure, and it is the first of mine, and I have written a line on the wall about a thing whose words are somebody else's and I got that line about three weeks after the afternoon it happened, which is about three weeks too late, and I have said in this room that the pair of shoes was the first thing this room was ever able to hand straight back and I was wrong about that as well, because the first thing it was able to hand straight back was an afternoon.**"

---

And then the man of fifty-one with a tool bag said the thing that made the evening a decision and not a reading, and he said it to the floor and not to a person.

"**You have just read a list of about eleven people out loud in a building with a company's telephone number on the outside of it.**"

"So?"

"**So I have spent about nine years putting numbers on about nine hundred doors in four buildings in this city, and I have never once been able to find out who walks through one of them, and I have wanted to for about nine years, and the reason I have not is that a number on a plate is a service and nobody is owed a list.**" He put the bag down. "**You have done in one evening the thing I have wanted to do for nine years, and nobody in this room made a mistake, and I am not going to be thanked for noticing it and I am not going to be the one who says what it is for.**"

"**Say what it is for**," said Asha Reed.

"**A company that can prove a room exists can put that room in a list, and about eleven people a week in this city are already on a list in that same building, and the two lists are not the same list, and the people on them are the same people.**"

---

And the woman of twenty-four asked for one thing, and she asked for it in about nine seconds, and she was at the back with her hand on the counter.

"**I have said three sentences in this room over about three weeks and none of them was written down and I have relied on that, and one of them was that saying a thing badly is the only shape available because doing it well produces a question about how, and there is no how, and the how is a person, and the person is findable.**"

"Yes."

"**And I have never been in a photograph of this room, and that is not modesty and it is not a rule, and it is the only arrangement in this building I have ever been able to rely on, and I am not breaking it and I am not keeping it for nothing.**"

"Which is it."

"**I am asking that nobody repeats it.**" She did not raise her voice. "**And the room will agree in about four seconds because it agrees to things like that in four seconds, and the cost is mine and not yours, and the cost is that agreeing changes nothing, because about eleven people heard me say it in this room on a Thursday night and there is no mechanism in this city that takes a sentence out of a person, which is the sentence a first-year of nineteen said eleven times in a corridor about six weeks ago and got wrong twice and did not fix.**"

Nobody thanked her and nobody wrote it down and nobody repeated it.

---

The wall was read after that, line by line, twelve lines, each one by the person who wrote it, in about four minutes, and the first line is still the first line and it is a cook's, and it says that the room will not keep anything it cannot take back, and about nine people in that room heard a line of it said in a clinic corridor in another district six weeks ago by a girl who was crying and did not know it.

And the binder was closed again, and the hardboard went back on its two nails, and the shutter came down at about ten, and about eleven people a week in this city are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and about four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and the number is on the back of a woman's own card and is on no form.

He wrote one thing, sitting on the floor in the corner with his back against a wall and the tool roll shut beside him.

*240. Thursday of week 81, at about ten on the floor in the corner of that room, the board back on its nails and the roll shut, and this entry is a record spoken aloud in about eleven minutes whose first item is a failure.*

*What kind of record it was.* **A reading, not a copy. The board was unhooked at about half past seven and rested on a chair for the evening; about nine people looked at the bare wall behind it and said nothing. No leaf, form, minute, notice, report or register was made, and what was read exists afterwards only as memory in about eleven people. Conditions were fixed by a first-year of nineteen before anyone sat down, without a vote and with nothing available to vote on: the room would speak its own record, the failures would lead, and the failures were the point.** Her stated reason: an account of a room's successes is a paper about a room, and about nine of those present had never been in a room that would say aloud what it had got wrong, and she had been in about nine such rooms and had not known that was what was missing until somebody said it with witnesses watching. The wall was then read line by line, twelve lines, each by its author, in about four minutes; the cook's first line remains the first line; the board was rehung at about ten; the binder was shut and not reopened.

*Position.* **Seven positions were filled and eight people spoke. Nobody supplied a seventh failure for the man with the docket, and the absence is entered here as a fact and not as a credit. Every item was read by the person it belongs to, which took roughly four minutes a head and about eleven minutes in total, and about four people in that room had known in advance what was coming and let the speakers take the time they took.**

*Class of item, by speaker.* **One, the first-year of nineteen, first by her own choice and without being asked: the four seconds, the Tuesday, the woman telephoning the plate, six people in the room, no request to any of them. Price given by her: about eleven hundred leaflets in about eleven hundred pockets, her own name in her own hand, no mechanism in this city for removing a name from paper, her own decision after being told twice that a description of a room is a description of the people in it, her uniqueness by age in that room, and about four people who have wanted to ask her something for about two months. Two, a printer of fifty-eight: nine words of her own, spoken in a room for the first time in her life, on a clean sheet in that binder, concerning a proof and a misread line, set down in the first fortnight in about nine seconds. Surrounding facts she gave before the words themselves: about nine hundred leaves of another company's pack carrying her handwriting at nine inches, about four hundred readers, four errors in forty years and one correctness, sleep and nothing else as the cost, and no number of hers anywhere on it. Her reason for the delay: she did not know whether that room would let a woman of fifty-eight hear her own handwriting read by herself. Three, a cook of twenty-seven: eleven items, the first nine and the tenth hers, the eleventh a woman's name struck through in one unretraced stroke with a biro in about a second and a half before about nine witnesses, none of whom stopped it or was asked to. She refused the name and said so before being asked twice. Her account of it: a courtesy extended to a relative on a line about her own pot, a request by telephone from work, a mark that cannot be lifted off without leaving paper, a future reader finding a ghost and no trace of a request, and a smudge as the only surviving record. She added that she was told she was not the person holding the pen, that this was correct, and that she has turned it over daily for a fortnight, and that it stands on the board in a first-year's hand as the only sentence in that room ever to have cost its writer something. Four, a woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary: a key, a label, a leaf of typing paper, eleven words and a day; and a page produced from a telephone by a woman in another district about a man present, being the only item in the binder no one there can withdraw. Price: a rule given away and then written down again in a kitchen, a second thing she is findable about, and nothing whatever about a room. Five, a man of twenty-nine on an employer's Friday list: nine words, a use rather than a practice, the shortest item in the binder, the only one naming a building, about four readers, an employer unaware. Price: about four people. Six, a man of fifty-eight with a bad hip: a refusal of nine steps, the correctness of that refusal, and a line he wrote on the board about three weeks after the afternoon in question. He corrected his own earlier statement in that room about an object being the first thing it could hand back. Nobody told him he was wrong.**

*Seven, and what it cost her.* **A woman of twenty-four requested that her three sentences in that room over about three weeks not be repeated, giving as her reason that the only available shape is saying a thing badly, since a good version would produce a question about method, there being no method, and the method being a person, and the person being findable. She added that she has never been in a photograph of that room, that this is neither modesty nor a rule, that it is the only arrangement in that building she has been able to rely on, and that she is neither breaking it nor keeping it for nothing. The room agreed in about four seconds. Her own account of the cost: agreement alters nothing, about eleven people heard her on a Thursday night, and nothing in this city removes a sentence from a person, which is the line said eleven times in a corridor about seventeen days earlier, twice wrongly and unrepaired. Nobody thanked her, nobody wrote it down and nobody repeated it.**

*The price of the reading, as one man with a tool bag put it to the floor.* **That about eleven individuals had just been spoken aloud inside a building bearing a company's number on its outer wall; that he has spent about nine years setting numbers on about nine hundred doors in four buildings and has never been able to learn who passes one, and has wanted to for about nine years, a plate being a service and nobody being owed a list; that the room accomplished in an evening what he has wanted for nine years and that no mistake was made in it; and that he will not be thanked for observing this and will not be the one to say what it is for. Being asked by a first-year of nineteen to say what it is for, he said that an organisation able to establish that a room exists may enter that room in a list, that about eleven people a week in this city are already entered in a list in the same building, and that the two lists differ while their membership does not. Nothing further was said about it that night and no chapter of this movement treats it as settled.**

*Also in the room.* **A woman of about thirty sat near the door for the whole evening with a bag on her lap, was asked nothing, is on no object in that room, and is in no file in this case. A kettle went on at half past eight at its fixed point. The room's own weekly figure stands at about eleven people taught to wait for a man in a lanyard, about four of them on no rota, on the back of a woman's own card and on nothing else.**

*Left out on purpose.* **The struck name. The contents of the binder apart from the items spoken. The words of the man of fifty-eight with the bad hip, which are on a page in that binder and were not spoken by him on this night because they are not his to speak. The man with the tool bag asked for nothing and is thanked by nobody. No record of this evening exists in any form, and opening the record is a different act from writing one; a copy would be a second copy and the persons named in it could not withdraw it. The evening contained no instruction, no demonstration, no assessment and no assistance, and it contained no share, no relay and no practice either, and about eleven people heard their own words read back and nothing else happened that was not already going to happen. The terms of his restriction are absent from this chapter, in quotation and in paraphrase alike, and no person in that room spoke a version of them. The standing question is not spoken, no rule about who may be asked anything was made, and no figure is set against one. Nothing here identifies who wrote behind a blank field, the field remains as it was, the card is where it has been for a term and a half, and no communication arrived from any source. Entry 240.**
