# Chapter 238 — The Same Eleven, And Not The Same List

The price of reading a room's own record out loud arrived in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards on the Monday morning of week eighty-two, and it arrived by telephone, and it is that about eleven people a week in this city are on a list in a company which can now prove that room exists, and the eleven are the same eleven they have always been, and it is not the same list.

Nobody was thanked. A letting clerk of thirty-nine said one sentence and a man of fifty-one with a tool bag said another, and neither of them was thanked, and neither of them asked to be.

---

The room was ninety-eight days old. The hardboard had been back on its two nails for four days with nothing going near it, and the twelve lines on it were eighteen days old, and a sheet of paper with eleven lines in it was in a drawer in a kitchen that opens in the afternoon, and a piece of a company's paper was face up on the counter where it had been for eleven days.

There were seven people in it at about ten past ten and about eleven by eleven, and the difference is the kettle and the hours people work.

A woman of thirty-four with a district jacket and a clipboard sat on a chair by the counter and drank a cup of tea and asked for nothing, and nobody thanked her, and nobody has ever thanked her for any of them.

---

At about eleven the telephone in the office eleven miles away rang, and a letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file answered it, and she gave the sentence she gives, and then she said her one sentence, which is the only one she said all day.

"**On Friday a man from my own floor rang me and asked whether the unit was still vacant, and I told him it was, the same as I have told five sets of people, and he then said that the unit was not vacant, and that he had been told it was not, and that he had been told it by somebody who said they had read it out of a record.**"

Somebody said, "A record."

"**A record of a room in a building in the Lower Wards that reads its own walls out loud to about eleven people on a Thursday night, and the man on the phone could tell me the name of the street and the day of the week, and he could not tell me the name of one person in it, and I said that that was the correct state of affairs and he said it was not his business, and I said no it is mine, because I am the one who tells him.**"

"And your sentence."

"**That I am not thanked, and that I am not going to be, and that I have a sheet of paper with four telephone calls on it and I am still not bringing it there.**"

Nobody asked about the sheet. She has been asked three times.

---

And then the person of thirty-four who buys training for a company spoke, and she was on a line, and she had not been in that room and had not been in that building, and she telephoned the plate on a Friday once in the previous month and a company had withdrawn a print run because of what was said on that call, and she is owed nothing in this case and has said so twice.

"**I am going to say the useful part first and then the part that is about us and I am going to say them in that order, because I have about four minutes and you have a room to be in.**"

"Go on."

"**On Friday I was asked by a person in my own building to put that room in a list of places. Not a document. A list, on a board, in a corridor, the way a person puts up the name of a café.**"

Somebody said, "Whose list."

"**Ours.**" She said it flatly and she did not soften it and nobody in that room said anything. "**And I said no, and I am going to keep saying no, and I am not telling you that I said no because I think well of you. I am telling you because you should know that a company which could not prove that room existed could not put it on anything, and that on Thursday night in a room with about eleven people in it, this room became provable, and I was not in that room and I was told about it afterwards by about four people who were.**"

Nobody asked her how she had been told.

"And the part that is about us," she said. "**About eleven people a week in this city are on a list in that building. They have been on it for years. It is not our list of places; it is a list of people who are sent to things, and about eleven a week go, and nobody at that end of it has ever been in your room, and nobody in your room has ever been on that list, and I have looked at it and I have never seen any of your names on it and I have never seen the room on it until Friday.**"

"Which is not a price," said Asha Reed.

"**It is not a price. The price is that you can now be found by people who can act, and you could not be found by them before Thursday, and a room that can be found by people who can act is a different thing from a room with a company's number on the outside of it and a bell-push that does not ring by itself, and I am not going to stand in your corridor and tell you that is bad news. You already know what a findable room costs. A first-year of nineteen told me the price of one about six weeks ago and she was right about it and I have thought about it since.**"

---

And the man of fifty-one with a tool bag said the other sentence, and it is the only time in this case he has said his own name out loud, and nobody thanked him for it and he did not ask to be.

"**I am on it.**"

The room got quiet in a way that is not describable and is not a rule.

"**I have been on it for about nine years. It is a rota. It has a box in it that says no reason given by holder, and about four years ago I put my own name in that box, and it is still my name in the box, and nobody has ever asked me about it and I have never once wanted to be asked.**"

"You could take your name out of it."

"**I could take my name out of it in about four seconds with a telephone and I have not, and I am not going to, and I want to say that out loud in a room because there are about eleven people a week on that thing across this city and I have never once looked at the other ten, and I have thought about that in about nine years and I have not done anything about it and I am not going to pretend I have.**"

"And the man with the docket," said somebody, and looked at him.

"**He is on it. He has been on it for about two years, on an exception recorded and renewed for the term, in a box that says no reason given by holder, and I know that because I put his renewal through four times and he did not ask me to and I did not ask him why.**"

Nobody looked at the man of twenty-two and the man of twenty-two was in the room and had said nothing for an hour.

And the man of fifty-one put his bag down and said the last of it.

"**So there is a man in this room who is on a company's list and on a card in a rail in Lattice Ward, and he has been both for about a term, and neither of those two people knew about the other one, and that is the whole of what I have come to say and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be asked anything else about it.**"

---

And Asha Reed said the sentence that the whole of this volume has been arriving at, and she said it once, and it is not nine words and nobody counted it.

"**They are the same eleven. It is not the same list.**"

Somebody said, "How do you know that."

"**I do not know it. I have said it and I cannot prove it and a man of fifty-eight proved about a fortnight ago that a line you cannot prove is still worth having, and that is the only authority I have for saying it and I am using it about a room instead of about a pair of shoes and I would like that noticed.**"

Nobody improved on it.

---

The rest of the morning was ordinary and it changed nothing, and two of the things in it were correct.

The door, the sounder and the outside of the shutter were dealt with at about half past ten by a woman of thirty-one who keeps a key and a diary. Nobody watched her and nobody helped. On the Tuesday morning the shutter was the same shutter.

And a man of thirty-four who works nights came in at about half past eleven on his way to a shift and asked one question, which was whether anybody knew what the heat in that room cost to run, and about four people in that room did not, and it is the first time in fourteen weeks that the cost of the heat and the cost of anything else have been in the same sentence in that room, and about nine people heard it and nobody answered him and he went to work.

He got to Lattice Ward at about ten that night and the tool roll was shut on the bench for the first time in a term and a half, which is not a decision and which nobody has asked him about.

He wrote one thing.

*241. Monday of week 82, at about ten at a bench in Lattice Ward, the roll shut beside the lamp and the docket face up, and this entry is one list in a company and about eleven names a week that are not in that room.*

*Position.* **Ninety-eight days old. The board back on its nails four days, after one evening off them, and the twelve lines on it eighteen days old, with nothing added. The kitchen-paper leaf with eleven lines in a drawer in a kitchen that opens in the afternoon, the sixth of them showing daylight where a thumb went. The company's page still face up on the counter after eleven days, read entire by four people and looked at by everybody through the door. Seven present at about ten past ten, eleven by eleven, the difference being the kettle and the hours people keep. A district nurse of thirty-four sat beside the counter with a clipboard, drank one cup of tea, asked nothing, and is thanked by nobody then or on any of her visits.**

*The clerk's single sentence.* **A letting clerk of thirty-nine with a ring file took a call at about eleven. On the Friday a man from her own floor rang, asked whether the unit was still vacant, received the standing answer she has now given five times, and then said the unit is not vacant and that he had been so informed by somebody who had read it out of a record. He could give the street and the day. He could not give the name of one person in the room. She informed him that this was the correct state of affairs; he replied that it was not his business; she replied that it was hers, being the one who tells him. Her one sentence, and the only one she spoke that day: she is not thanked, will not be, keeps a sheet bearing four calls, and is still not bringing it there. Nobody raised the sheet. It has been raised three times.**

*The buyer, in her own order.* **Thirty-four; buys training; no standing and none claimed; telephoned the plate in the previous month; the call after which a company withdrew a print run; owed nothing, having said so twice. She put the useful half first. On the Friday she was asked, by somebody in her own building, to enter that room in a list of places — not a document, a board in a corridor, the manner in which a café is entered. She declined. Her reason for declining is not regard for the room. Her reason is that a company unable to establish that the room exists cannot enter it on anything, and that on Thursday night, in a room holding about eleven people, the room became establishable, and that she was not present and was informed afterwards by about four people who were. Nobody asked by what route. She then gave the half that concerns her own building: about eleven persons a week stand on a list there; they have done for years; it is a list of people sent to things, not of places; about eleven a week attend; nobody at that end has ever been in that room; nobody in that room has ever stood on that list; she has examined it and never seen a name from that room on it, and had never seen the room on it before the Friday. Asked whether that constitutes a price, she said it does not. The price is that the room is now discoverable by persons able to act, having formerly been discoverable only through a number on a plate and a bell-push that does not ring by itself; she declined to call that bad news standing in that room, and said a first-year of nineteen named the price of a findable room to her about six weeks ago and was right.**

*Two statements from a man with a tool bag, and the second of them has never been made before.* **That he stands on the list; that he has for about nine years; that it is a rota carrying a box that reads that no reason is given by the holder; that he entered his own name into it about four years ago; that it is still there; that nobody has ever questioned him and he has never wanted to be questioned. Asked whether the name could be removed, he said it could be done in about four seconds with a telephone; that he has not done it; that he does not intend to; that he has been considering it for about nine years, has done nothing, and will not pretend otherwise; and that about eleven persons a week stand on it across this city and he has never once examined the other ten. Unasked, he then said that the man with the docket stands on it also, for about two years, on an exception recorded and renewed for the term, in the same box, and that he knows because he has processed four renewals that were not requested and for which no reason was asked. He set the bag down and said that a man in that room therefore stands twice over, on a company's roll and on a rail in another district, has stood twice over for about a term, that neither knew of the other, that this is the whole of what he came to say, and that he will not be thanked and will not be questioned further. Nobody thanked him. Nobody looked at the man with the docket, who had said nothing for about an hour.**

*The sentence that came after it, and where its authority comes from.* **Spoken once by a first-year of nineteen: that the eleven are the same eleven and that the list is not the same list. Asked for her grounds she said she has none she can prove, that she has said it regardless, and that a man of fifty-eight established about a fortnight earlier that a line incapable of proof is still worth having, and that this is the only authority she has and she is spending it about a room rather than about an object in a plastic bag, and that she would like that observed. Nobody improved on it. The authority is borrowed and is entered as borrowed.**

*Two correct acts, and the second of them is the first thing in fourteen weeks to put two prices into one sentence.* **The door, the sounder and the outside of the shutter, handled at about half past ten by a woman of thirty-one who keeps a key and a diary, without assistance, without request and without an audience; the shutter was identical on the Tuesday morning. A man of thirty-four who works nights arrived at about half past eleven on his way to a shift and asked what the heat in that room costs to run; about four of those present could not say; until this evening no sentence spoken in that room had carried the price of the heat and the price of anything else together; about nine people heard the question, nobody answered it, and he left for work. Neither act altered anything.**

*Held back on purpose.* **Every name on either list. The name of the man who rang the clerk. The board in the corridor and its upkeep. The contents of the buyer's file. The four people who told the buyer about the Thursday. The number on the plant that serves that room, which no person in it has seen and which is not in this entry. No page, form, list, minute, notice, report or register was created in this city by anybody in this chapter, and the list in question is a company's, eleven miles distant, and is not a document this movement produced. Nobody in that room on that morning taught, demonstrated, assessed or assisted anything; there was no exchange, no plate, and no performed practice; and the three of them are named here because a record that leaves them out is not a record. No person on these pages quotes or paraphrases the terms of what he may not do in that department, and a room off a service road is not one. The standing question is not spoken, no rule about who may be asked anything was made, and no figure is set against one. No author is named in this chapter; the field on the documents in three buildings is untouched; the card is still in the fourth pocket; and nothing came from anybody. Entry 241.**
