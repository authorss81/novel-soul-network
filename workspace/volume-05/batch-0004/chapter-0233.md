# Chapter 233 — Nine Sittings And A Person

The ninth sitting of that Exchange fell on the Wednesday morning of week eighty, four weeks to the day after the eighth, and the book did not open, and the reason it did not open was about one person and had nothing whatever to do with how many people were in the room.

A woman of sixty-one said two things that nobody had asked her for, and the second of them was a number, and she had said that number fourteen times, and the fourteenth saying was hers and not a count of anything.

---

The shutters went up at about half past nine and the room was hot by eleven the way it is hot by eleven, and at eleven a hand went to the shutter and that is the signal and it has not changed in nineteen years, and the tin went into a drawer with no lock on it.

There are nine chairs round the table. The ninth has stood against the wall facing away from the room since the sitting before last, put there by a young woman of twenty-one who has been coming to that room since she was fifteen and who has never once been asked to move it and has never once been named in it, and nobody has moved it back and nobody has said why it is where it is.

There have been nine sittings. There have been eight gaps of four weeks. The most recent gap is the only interval in that room a person can check without a calendar, and it is exactly four weeks, and it is the fourth time in a row that anybody has said so out loud.

The rule is given in one go at every sitting of that room. It is not abridged. It is not improved upon. It is not shortened for the length of a morning. It has been given nine times and asked for eight, and the ninth was given to a room that had not asked for it, which is the sitting this is.

"**A book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call.**"

---

And then, before she had sat down, and before anybody had said anything about a company or about money, she said the other thing, and she said it flatly, to a shutter.

"**I have been asked that before. By a supplier.**"

Nobody had asked her.

"**Not recently. Eleven years ago, and about four years ago, and not since, because I have not told anybody in this room what is in this room since I got it, and the only person who knew was a man who is not alive. A supplier came to this door with a figure and asked me whether the book could be bought, and I gave him the sentence I give about four hundred people in nineteen years, and he asked it again on the way out and I gave him the sentence again, and he did not come back.**"

Somebody said, "What sentence."

"**That it is four words and a rule and it does not belong to anybody who is not in this room.**" She put her hand on the tin. "**And I want to say the rest of it before somebody in here decides I have been mysterious on purpose. He did not come back because I was rude, or because I was clever, or because I was right. He did not come back because there is nothing in this room to buy, and I would not let him find that out from me, and it took him about a week to find it out from the four words.**"

---

Then she said the number, at about twenty past ten, and nobody had asked.

"**Thirty-seven.**"

Nobody said anything, which is what usually happens.

"**It was twenty-nine on the Wednesday of week sixty-four and thirty-one on the Wednesday of week sixty-eight and thirty-three four weeks ago and thirty-five the Wednesday before that, and it is thirty-seven, and two people telephoned me in four weeks and one of them has not come and one of them is in this room.**"

"And the number," said somebody, "is not the thing."

"**The number is not the thing and I have said it fourteen times in this room and nine of those are the nine Wednesdays in this book, and the first five are not in it and are not going to be. It is a figure about me and it has never once been a figure about this room, and the room was here before the book was, and I will not do that subtraction again.**"

She did not do it again. She had done it three times.

---

The one who did not come telephoned on the Monday and was told to come on a Wednesday, and she did not come, and nobody in that room has any idea what she wanted, and Bea Nunn has not telephoned her.

Somebody asked her why not.

"**I gave my reason in the spring. I have given it twice since. I am not going to give it a fourth time in the same words, and nobody in this room has earned it by asking a fourth time, and I would like that noticed, because I am the one who decides what has been earned.**"

---

The one who did come had come in at about half past ten and had sat down and had not brought anything, and she is a woman of about thirty-four who runs a small kitchen with eleven people in it and who had telephoned on the Monday and asked one question, which was whether a room that writes down what people say in their own words can be bought.

She had been told to come on a Wednesday.

Bea Nunn had said no, in about four minutes, and had given her the sentence about four hundred people in nineteen years, and the woman had said thank you and had come.

Nobody asked her to sign anything and nobody offered her the tin, and the tin was in a drawer with no lock on it from eleven, and the book was shut.

"You could have signed," said somebody. "**You came. You said your thing. It is on the page in about four minutes and you could have signed it and it would be the forty-sixth line.**"

"**No**," said the woman of thirty-four.

"Why not."

"**Because nobody asked me.**"

Somebody said, "You would have said yes."

"**I would have said yes. That is exactly why nobody asked me.**"

---

And then Bea Nunn said why the book was shut, and it took her a long time, and it was the ninth reason that room has given for not opening a book, and it is the first of the nine that is about one person.

"**At the seventh sitting this book grew, and I told you what that costs, and I have not opened it since, and I have given you four reasons and every one of them was about me.**"

"Go on."

"**This one is not about me. There is a woman in this city who telephoned this room in the spring about something she had said to me, and she telephoned again afterwards and asked whether it had been written down, and I could not tell her, and I could not tell her in a way that was true, because telling her it had not been written down means opening a book in a room with nine people in it, and if I open it in front of nine people then I have published the only thing she asked me for, which is that it is not there.**"

"And you cannot be sure it is not there."

"**I have not looked. That is the whole of it. In nineteen years I have been able to answer any question anybody has asked me in this room, and I have one I cannot, and it is not about the rule and it is not about a number. It is about whether a person I spoke to on a telephone is owed an answer, and I do not know what I would find, and I am not going to find out in front of nine people.**"

Somebody said, "So it is a count."

"**No**," she said. "**It is a person. I have been asked eight times in nineteen years what my rule is and I have given the rule eight times, and I have never once been asked whether a person was owed an answer, and I have been the woman who answers people for nineteen years, and this is the first time anybody in this room has put the two things next to each other.**"

---

Two items were taken and neither went in the book, and there is no line in this case that says what either of them was beyond what the people said in that room.

The first was about nine minutes, and the second was about a corridor, and neither of them was written down, and one of the four minutes of the first was spent by a man of about thirty-one saying that he had been told by two separate people in the same week that a room like that was a front, and that he had come to see whether it was, and that he did not think it was, and that he was going to be told it was not one, and that he wanted to be the person who had asked.

The second was four minutes and a woman of sixty-eight who wanted to know whether a thing written down by a stranger could be about her, and Bea Nunn told her what she tells about four hundred people in nineteen years, and the woman of sixty-eight said that she had known the answer when she sat down and had wanted it said out loud by somebody who was not her, and left.

A man of twenty-two was in the corner with a docket for about two hours and said nothing at all, and was not thanked, and has not been thanked, and the arrangement is the arrangement.

He wrote one thing.

*236. Wednesday of week 80, at a bench in Lattice Ward at about ten, lamp out, shutter up, rain over, and this entry is a four-column book that stayed shut at its ninth sitting for a reason concerning one individual.*

*The arithmetic of the room.* **Nine sittings. Eight intervals. Every interval four weeks; the newest is checkable without a calendar and is the fourth checkable one in a row. Of the first eight, six passed with the book closed, the seventh added a line, and the last two are closed, which makes the number of sittings at which nothing was added eight and a half. The book stands at forty-five lines and has four columns. The tin carries no lock, was carried to a drawer with no lock at about eleven, and stayed shut. The signal for the shutter is nineteen years old and is not a decision anybody takes. Nine chairs. The ninth has been turned to face the wall since the previous sitting, moved there by a young woman of twenty-one who began coming to that room at fifteen, who has never been asked to move it and never named in it; nobody has turned it back and nobody has given a reason. She is not the first-year of nineteen in the other room and is not that person, and there is one nineteen-year-old in this case and it is the other one.**

*The rule.* **Delivered entire at that room, as at every one of its sittings, given nine times and asked for eight, the ninth having been given to a room that had not asked, and neither abridged nor improved nor shortened for the length of a morning: a book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call.**

*The supplier, and the three facts beneath it.* **Uttered by the woman of sixty-one before sitting down and before any person present had raised a company or a sum of money: that the question has been put to her before, by a supplier. The occurrences were eleven years back and about four years back, and there has been none since, because she has told nobody in that room what is in that room since she came into it and the single person who knew is dead. The supplier brought a figure to the door, asked if the book could be bought, received the standard refusal, asked again on his way out, received it again, and did not return. Her reason, given unasked and not improved: she was not rude, was not clever, and was not right; there is simply nothing there to buy and she declined to be the instrument of his finding that out, and it took him about a week to establish it from four words. She claimed in the same breath to be the person who decides what has been earned in that room.**

*Her figure, and what it does not count.* **Thirty-seven, up two since the previous sitting, announced unasked at about twenty past ten. Announced fourteen times in that room in her life; nine of those correspond to the nine Wednesdays the book holds; the first five are earlier than the book, are absent from it, and will not be entered. She performed the subtraction aloud, refused to repeat it, has performed it three times, and stated that the figure concerns herself and has never concerned the room, and that the room is older than the book. No later writer is to convert it into a number of sittings. Of the two who rang since last time, one was told to come on a Wednesday and did not; what she wanted is unknown to everyone present; she has not rung her. Asked why, the woman of sixty-one declined to give her spring reason a fourth time and asserted that no one in that room had earned it by asking a fourth time.**

*The woman who came, and the line she did not make.* **Thirty-four; a small kitchen; about eleven people in it. Rang on the Monday, put one question in about four minutes, was refused in the standing form, and came. Nobody asked her to sign and the tin was not offered. A person in that room pointed out that she could have signed and that the result would be the forty-sixth line. She declined, and gave the entire ground in one sentence: nobody had asked her. It was put to her that she would have agreed, and she agreed, and said that this is the reason nobody asked. The book therefore closes the day at forty-five lines, no signature was taken in this chapter, and no chapter of this movement raises it to forty-six; the volume's figure of forty-six is a later problem and this is not it.**

*The fifth reason, and how it differs.* **Of the reasons that room has given for not opening the book, this is the ninth and the first that turns on an individual. The growth at the seventh was about the woman of sixty-one's own cost; the four since have all been about her. This one is not. In the spring a woman telephoned that room about something she had said, telephoned again to ask whether it had been recorded, and could not be answered; she cannot be answered truthfully without opening the book in front of nine people, which would make public the one thing she asked for. Nobody has looked. The woman of sixty-one's own position, given without being asked: that in nineteen years she has answered every question put to her in that room and has one she cannot; that the question is whether a person she spoke to on a telephone is owed an answer; that she does not know what she would find; and that she will not find it in front of nine people. She was asked whether this was a matter of numbers and said it was not, and added that she has been asked what her rule is eight times in nineteen years and has given it eight times, and has never once been asked whether a person was owed an answer, and that this is the first occasion on which the two have been put side by side in that room.**

*Two items and one corner.* **About nine minutes and about four minutes; neither entered in the book; neither reproduced in full here. The first turned on a man of about thirty-one who had been told by two separate people inside a week that a room of that sort is a front, who came to see whether it was, who did not believe it was, who expected to be told it was not one, and who wanted to have been the person who asked. The second turned on a woman of sixty-eight who wished to know whether a thing written down by a stranger could concern her, was given the standing answer, and said she had known it on sitting down and had wanted it said aloud by somebody other than herself. A man of twenty-two sat in a corner for about two hours, said nothing, was not thanked and has not been thanked.**

*Excluded, refused, or left out on purpose.* **The name and number of either caller; what the first caller wanted; the contents of the tin; the contents of the book; the woman of sixty-four whose words stand on line forty-five, who is neither named nor described here. Which man came on the Friday, which is not on this page. The four minutes the second caller was given. No page, sheet, card or slip was written by anybody in this chapter and none exists, and no box ruled in pencil in any building in this city was touched. No instruction, no demonstration, no assessment and no assistance was given by anybody in that back room that morning, and neither a shared observation, nor a plate, nor a performed practice appears anywhere in it. He is not described as being stopped from a thing, and nobody in that room paraphrases the terms on which he does what he does. The standing question is not spoken, nothing here governs who may be asked anything, and no figure is set against one. Nobody in that back room named whoever wrote behind a blank field, the field is still blank, the card stayed in the fourth pocket of the tool roll, and no communication of any kind arrived from any quarter. Entry 236.**
