# Chapter 171 — Nobody Has Ever Been Asked

There is a person in this city who has authority over a building's own records, and on the Monday morning of week fifty-five a man of twenty-two had been asking for that person for about nine days, and the answer he had got in three rooms and two corridors was that there is not one.

What everybody he asked said, in three different voices, is that the three people who could have said otherwise had never once been asked to say anything.

---

The first was on a landing in the second building at eleven, with a man of forty-four in overalls and a roll of plates under his arm and a caulking gun he did not put down.

Petar Vance prints them. He services the exchange rooms and he has printed about nine hundred of them and about nine hundred is not a guess, it is what his own schedule says, and he said the number the way a man says a thing he has said twice and been right both times.

"A plate is a service plate," he said. "**It says the number of the service. It has got a manufacturer's line along the bottom and the number is the service number, and the service number is off a schedule of doors, and the schedule is not mine.**"

"Then whose is it."

"The building's. That is what our engineer told a foreman of yours, twice, and he was right, and it is the only sentence anybody in this trade has said to me that is both true and useless."

He turned the roll over.

"**We print what the list says. If the list is wrong then the list is yours and the plates are correct and I am a man with a caulking gun.**"

---

"Whose list."

"There is no list a building gave us. There is a contract and a schedule and a number against every door on the schedule, and the schedule was not made by the building and the building has never seen it, and I have been in this trade twenty-two years and I have never once been asked where a schedule came from and I have never once thought to ask, and that is the answer to your question from a man who is on the schedule."

He said the rest of it in four minutes and he said it once.

"Two of the four are held freehold by a landlord whose name is on a schedule of title and who has a letterhead and a solicitor and no hours, and he has been inside one of them twice that he knows of, and both times it was a plate. One is held by a management company that is not the freeholder and does not know it either. The fourth is the one with the basement in it and nobody in this city has a key to that door except me and a foreman who has not turned his."

---

"A district nurse in this city wants one room put right."

"I know which one."

"Then you will want to know what happens to the other eight hundred and ninety-nine, so I am going to tell you, and I am telling you because you have got a docket and I have got a ladder and neither of us has got the afternoon." He put the roll down on the step. "**In the week of the thirty-seventh there was a fault ticket. It came in with no name on it. Somebody read it against the wall, wrote the number that was already on the wall in the typeface that is already on the wall, and closed it. That was the right thing to do. I have been in this trade twenty-two years and I would not have done it any other way.**"

"And the room."

"**The room is still a room with the wrong number on the door and the right number in a rack inside it, and about forty-one people in that district are on something that cannot be ended, and the ticket is closed, and the closure is correct, and there is no version of the world in which a correct ticket fixes a number.** Nine hundred plates, one closure, no change. That is what I am telling you. You will not be able to put that in a document either, and I have stopped expecting anybody to."

---

"You have said that twice now."

"**I have said it twice because it is true twice. A plate is correct. A schedule is correct. The building's own record is neither of those, and there is nobody in that building whose job it is to hold it, and the only two people in this city who are ever going to look at the building's own record are a man with a caulking gun and a foreman who has not turned his key.**"

---

"About nine hundred doors."

"About nine hundred," said Petar Vance. "**And I cannot change nine hundred of them because a district nurse in this city wants one room, and that is the whole of my trade in this case, and if you print me a piece of paper about that number I will read it and put it in a van.**"

Then he said the thing he had come down the stairs to say, and he said it before he was asked for it.

"The number is nine hundred and I have given it to you once and I would like it not to be given to anybody else, **and I am not going to be thanked, and if I am thanked I will have to think about it for the rest of the week.**"

---

The second was a corridor at half past two and she came out of a stairwell with a trolley and a bucket and four cloths over the side, and she had been in the building since a quarter past one and had forty-one doors to do before she could go home.

Ines Kolar is forty-six and she has signed a condition sheet at the bottom of every Thursday for nineteen years and the sheet has forty-one lines on the front and no column for anything that is not a condition.

He had put the question in a corridor on the Thursday of week fifty and it had taken her three weeks to come back with an answer, and the answer had been that her sheet could not answer it.

He asked her the other one.

"**What I think the sheet is for,**" she said. "You have been four days getting to that one."

"You are the only person in this city who has stood in front of every one of those doors and read it against a list."

"That is not a question, that is a fact, and I have never been thanked for it either."

She moved the trolley two inches to get past him and did not put it down.

"**It is for a Thursday. It has been for a Thursday for nineteen years and there are about a thousand of them in a ring file in a cupboard on a ground floor in another part of this city, and in about four years there will be about two thousand, and nobody has ever opened that cupboard and nobody is ever going to.** That is what it is for. Now put the docket down, because you are holding it like a man who is about to open something."

He put it down on the floor between them.

"That is the first time in nine days that a person in this city has told me to do the correct thing and I have done it," she said. "**And I am going to say the other half, because you did not ask for it and I have not got anybody else to say it to. I did not put the pencil there. Nobody has ever told me what a sheet is for. And the only person in this city who has asked me a question my sheet could not answer is a man of twenty-two with a docket, and he asked it in a corridor on a Thursday and then waited three weeks, and I have had nine days on this one.**"

"Then answer it."

"I am not going to. You did not ask it in the right words, and if I had answered it you would have written it down, and there is no column for it and there is no form in this city that takes one, and I have spent nineteen years putting a thing on the back of a sheet because the somewhere used to be nowhere and I am not going to be the reason it becomes a document." She looked down at the docket on the floor. "**You have got a different question. Ask me that one and then go away, because I have got thirty-one doors after this landing and the light on it is not a condition.**"

He did not ask the different one either, in the end, and she did forty-one doors.

---

The third was not asked, because there was nobody to ask.

The landlord is a name on a schedule of title. The company is a service list and a contract and a man with a caulking gun who has given a number once and asked for it back. And the woman with a trolley has forty-one doors and a sheet nobody opens and has been asked, in the whole of this case, one question about a discrepancy and nothing at all about the sheet she signs at the bottom of forty-one times a week.

That is the three of them. Between them they hold the plates, the freehold and the doors, and not one of the three has ever been asked to decide anything.

---

Nobody in this city can point at a lock, because there is no lock. What a person can point at is a schedule of doors, a schedule of title, and a line at the bottom of a form, and all three of those are in the hands of people who have never been asked a question and who are not, in any sense that would survive a conversation, to blame.

Petar Vance went down the stairs with the roll under his arm and said nothing at the bottom of them. Ines Kolar did forty-one doors. And a man of twenty-two stood in a corridor with a docket he had not written anything on.

There is nobody in this city to ask about a schedule of doors, a schedule of title, or a line at the bottom of a form, and all three of them have gone home for the night, and not one of the three knows that it is the answer.

---

*174. Monday of week 55, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a man of forty-four with a roll of plates under his arm who has printed about nine hundred of them and has asked for the number not to be given to anybody else, and a woman of forty-six with a trolley who has answered a different question than the one I came with.*

*The question I spent nine days on.* **Who has authority over a building's own records. Put that way it is a question with an answer of the word *nobody* and I have known that since the Monday of week fifty-one, when a woman of forty-six stopped in an office and said a man's name out loud, and what I have actually been doing for nine days is going round asking three people to say it, and all three of them have said a different thing and none of them has said the word.**

*What a company is, as a record-holder.* **A service plate, a manufacturer's line along the bottom edge, and a service number off a schedule of doors that no building in this city has ever seen. Twenty-two years in the trade. The engineer's sentence, given twice to a foreman: the schedule is the building's. He called that both true and useless and he was right about both halves.** The freehold of two of the four sits with a landlord on a schedule of title with a letterhead and a solicitor and no hours. One is managed by a company that is not the freeholder. The fourth has a basement with a door that two people have keys to. **No list has ever been traced to a person and nobody in this trade has ever asked, and he said he has never thought to ask, and that is the finding and he is not the man who found it, he is the man who was standing next to it.**

*The cost he named before I asked for it.* **About nine hundred doors, and he cannot change nine hundred of them because a district nurse wants one room. He asked for the number not to be repeated, asked not to be thanked, and said that being thanked would give him something to think about for the rest of the week.** He is on the record in a different building and in a different case and he has one fact here and he has asked for it back and I have written it down, which is a thing I do and which he cannot stop.

*What a sheet is for.* **A Thursday, and a thousand of them in a ring file in a cupboard on a ground floor in another part of this city, two thousand in about four years, and nobody has ever opened that cupboard. She said the question I came with takes her three weeks and the one I actually asked takes her nine seconds, and told me to put the docket down because I was holding it like a man about to open something.** She has been asked one question about a discrepancy in the whole of this case and nothing at all about the sheet she signs at the bottom of forty-one times a week.

*The three, as a list, and it is the whole of the movement.* **A schedule of doors. A schedule of title. A line at the bottom of a form.** Plates, freehold, and the doors themselves. Nine days, three rooms, two corridors, and not one of the three has ever been asked to decide anything, and one of them asked me not to thank him and one of them told me to put the docket down. Entry 174. The book is at forty-four lines and does not move before the Wednesday of the week of fifty-six, and a man of twenty-two is standing in a corridor in a building he has no work in.
