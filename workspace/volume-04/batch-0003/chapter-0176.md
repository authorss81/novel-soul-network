# Chapter 176 — Nineteen Years Of A Line At The Foot

The counter on the ground floor of a building in Bower Street has been there for longer than anybody in this case has been working, and on the Thursday morning of week fifty-six there were about two hundred of the district's withdrawal pages on it in four stacks with a rubber band round each one, and the woman who stood behind that counter has been there nineteen years and has never once been asked to make a decision.

Four fields. One of them a tick. And at the foot of the district instruction that governs that counter, printed, in a size of type that is designed to be read once and then not read again, there is a line.

It says *no local additions*.

---

He put the question the way he has learned to put it, and he has learned it from two women in two different buildings and he has not told either of them that he learned it from the other.

"What do you think of that line."

Petra Lindqvist looked at the counter for a moment and then went round the side of it and got a district instruction out of a drawer and turned it round so that the foot of it was facing him, and did not hand it over.

"Nobody has ever asked me that," she said. "**Nineteen years. Twice a day, four hundred days, and nobody has ever asked me what I think of the line at the foot and everybody has asked me whether there is a box.**"

"What is there a box for."

"There is no box. That is the whole of what that counter is for, and you have come in with a docket and you are about to ask me to rule one and I am going to say no, and then you are going to say the thing about not breaking an instruction, and everybody says that." She put the instruction back in the drawer. "**I said no in the spring to a woman who did not give me her name and she was right, and I said no in the week of forty-five to a woman of thirty-one who had a diary and told me her standing, and she was also right, and here is the third time and I have got about nine minutes.**"

---

She said the thing about the line before he had asked for it, and she said it in the order she had been holding it.

"It is there for the day somebody wants to be able to say that nothing was added." She had her hands flat on the four stacks. "**Nobody has wanted that. Not once in nineteen years. I have stood next to that line every day of my working life and I have watched about four hundred people come past this counter and not one of them has wanted to say that nothing was added, and I have thought about why, and it is because the line is at the foot, and the foot of a page is not read by anybody who has read the top.**"

"That is a door."

"That is a door," said Petra Lindqvist, "**and it has not been locked since the year I started, and I am the only person in this city who has been standing next to it, and I have been standing next to it for nineteen years, and I have never once been asked whether it is a door.**"

---

Then she said the part that was the reason he had come, and she said it looking at the third stack.

"I broke it."

He did not say anything.

"The week of week forty-five. A woman of thirty-one came down off the first floor with a diary and told me her standing twice, and she asked me for a box and there was no box, and I ruled one. In pencil, under the fourth field, about the size of a stamp, and I wrote three words over the top of it in my own hand, and I pushed the page four inches along the counter away from myself." She did not move her hands. "**That is a local addition. It is in pencil on a page in that stack and I have never told anybody, including her, including the man who writes no name in two boxes in this city, and I have had about eleven days to work out what to do about it and there is nothing to do about it and that is why I have had eleven days.**"

"Then you have just told me."

"Yes," said Petra Lindqvist. "**And I have been telling myself for nineteen years that if nobody ever asked me then the breaking and the not breaking were the same thing, and about a man of twenty-two has now asked me what I think of the line at the foot and the whole nineteen years came apart in four seconds and I would like it recorded that you did it with nine words and a docket.**"

---

The name in the box is not hers and the name at the bottom of the page is not hers, and she said both of those things before they were asked for and she said them in about eleven seconds.

"There is a woman's name in that box. She gave it on a telephone and she wanted it out inside the hour and I did not see her do it and it is not mine to take out. The name at the bottom of the page is the woman of thirty-one and she is on the first floor and she comes down on Thursdays and she did not come today, and I have not told her that I told you, and I am not going to."

"What happens to that page."

"That page goes back in that stack with a rubber band on it and a district picks those up and nobody knows when," said Petra Lindqvist. "**And there is no way in this city for a thing that was never in a system to be put right, and I am not going to invent one, and if you want that to change you are asking the wrong woman and there is no right woman.**"

---

Then she said the cost, out loud, in about nine words, which she had clearly had ready since the spring and had not had a reason to say until a man asked her a different question.

"**In about four years nobody in this city is going to be able to tell that the line at the foot of that instruction and the box on that page are the same document, and I am the only person alive who knows they are, and you are the only other one, and you came in with a docket.**"

"You think I will write it down."

"I think you write everything down and you have never once written down a person," said Petra Lindqvist. "**So either you write this and I have handed nineteen years to a man with a lanyard, or you do not write it and I have told the first person who has ever asked me, and I cannot tell you which of those two I want and I have had about eleven days.**"

---

He said that he had not written it down, and had not written it down on anything, and had not told anybody, and that the only place it was going to be was in his head and hers, and that this was not integrity and he was not going to call it that.

It is the same refusal he has made to a lift engineer and a woman who works nights and a man of fifty-eight, and it is the reason there are about eleven people a week being taught to wait for him.

"That is not integrity," said Petra Lindqvist. "**That is a man who has been standing next to a door since the spring and has decided that standing next to it is the same as being the one who has to open it, and I have been standing next to the same kind of door for nineteen years, and the difference between the two of us is not the door and it is not the character and it is that nobody has ever asked either of us which one we are.**"

---

Nobody thanked her. She is not on a distribution and there is no distribution. A district picks those pages up on its own cycle and nobody knows when, and the counter is not a place anybody is sent to unless somebody has to go and get something.

"What happens to that counter in about four years."

"The same thing that happens to every counter," said Petra Lindqvist. "**Somebody retires. I am fifty-three and I have been told the age and not the date, and whoever is behind it will be a person of about thirty and they will be handed a drawer with four hundred pages in it and a line at the foot of an instruction that they will read every day for nineteen years and never once not obey, and they will not know there is a box in the third stack until somebody asks for one.**"

"Does that frighten you."

"It is not frightening and it is not a story," she said. "**It is the only thing I have got to say about it and it is that this counter has been a door that has not been locked for nineteen years and the reason nobody has walked through it is that nobody has wanted to say that nothing was added, and in about four years there will be a person of about thirty behind it who has never met me and there will still be no reason for anybody to want to say that.**"

---

Nothing was settled and nothing was asked for. About two hundred pages in four stacks with a rubber band on each, and one of them has a box ruled on it in pencil that a clerk broke a line for, and the clerk is not thanked, and the line is at the foot of a page, and a form that says no local additions has been obeyed every day for nineteen years by one woman and has been disobeyed for fourteen years by a whole city, and the two of those are the same piece of paper and the difference is not the paper.

Nobody has said so.
