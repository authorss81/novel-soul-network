# Chapter 180 — A Column Heading

A bound register in a canvas cover stands on a table on the ground floor of a building in Crown Terrace, beside a counter, with nothing in front of it and nothing hung off it, and on the Thursday morning of week fifty-seven the table had nobody behind it.

There is a woman of thirty-four who reads it out to anybody who asks, and she is in that room on the Monday, the Wednesday and the Friday, and it is a public document and that is her whole view, and four hundred people in fourteen years have had a line read out to them at that table.

Not one of the four hundred has ever been there on a Thursday.

A woman of forty-one was standing at the other end of it with her coat still on, because she had come on her day off, and she said why before she was asked.

"Because there are four hundred of them in my room and every one of those cards has got a box number and a date and a place and a hand on it in a shorthand I cannot read, **and not one of them has got an authority on it anywhere, and this is the only table in this city where an authority is written down.**"

---

The question that had brought them to it was four words long and had taken nine days.

"Who can withdraw the rule."

Nobody at the table said anything, because there is nobody at the table, and the register does not answer. It is a bound book in a canvas cover, ruled into four columns, about nine hundred lines, going back fourteen years to the week of week thirty-eight.

Date. Place. Hand. Authority.

They read the four headings out themselves, standing up, the way four hundred people have had them read out to them by somebody else.

---

Then the woman of forty-one said the thing that the whole of this volume had been walking towards, and she said it in two sentences and then stood there.

"**This is a register of removals.** It is a book of what was taken out of a room. A binder went down a corridor to a room with a register in it in the week of week thirty-eight and six lines in this book are about it, and that is what this book is."

"Then where is the Compact in it."

"It is not in it," said Wendla Ossick. "**The Compact was not taken out of a room. Nothing in this book has ever been the Civic Compact, and nothing in this book has ever been the authority that made the Compact, and there is no line in this city, on any table, in any drawer, in any system, that says who may change it.**"

---

"You have looked."

"I have looked for a fortnight," she said, "**since a woman of forty-five and a man of twenty-two had two documents out of my room at the same time, and I went looking for the thing that could put them back, and there is not one, and I did not find it by stopping. I found it by going through every list in this city that has got a word at the top of it, and there are about four of them, and three of them belong to a fund, and the fourth one is in this book.**"

Marek Senn said: "There is a man on the floor above."

"Yes."

"He is fifty-eight and he has a soft voice and he keeps a count and he has declined in writing, twice, to have this table moved into a locked room upstairs, and he told nobody until a man with a docket stood in a corridor and he said the reason was that he would rather have a corridor that does not work than a locked room that does."

"He is a person," said Wendla Ossick. "**A person can be asked. He has been asked six times in fourteen years and he has given six counts and he has agreed to nothing, and if you walked up that staircase this morning and asked him whether the requirement stands, he would tell you it stands, and then he would tell you why it stands, and that is not the same as anybody in this city being able to take it away.**"

---

"So he is the authority."

"He is not the authority. He is a man in a building who has kept a table where it is for fourteen years because a locked room would have been the correct decision about the pages and the end of the corridor, and he has told nobody that until a stranger made him tell it." She put her hand on the canvas. "**Nobody has ever asked him to be the authority over anything. He is the only person on this floor and that is not the same as a post, and there is no post on this floor, and there is not going to be one.**"

"Then the rule cannot be withdrawn."

"It cannot be repealed," said Wendla Ossick, "**because repealing it needs an authority, and the only authority that exists in this building is a column heading, and a column heading cannot be asked a question.**"

---

They read the six lines about the binder because there was nothing else to do with a Thursday.

About nine hundred lines, and the fourth column is a job title in every one of them and has never once had a person's name in it in fourteen years, and the hand column is blank in about four lines in ten, and where it is filled in a woman of thirty-four has found out twice and both times the person had left.

Of the six, two have a hand in them.

Of those two, one has an author still in this city, and a man of fifty-eight on the floor above has said so in a corridor once and will not say which and will not say why.

And the fourth column on those six lines is a job title, in every one, and it is the fourth column on all nine hundred, and it is a word at the top of a column on a page in a book on a table by a counter in a public building, and anybody in this city can walk in and read it and four hundred people have had it read to them and not one of them has come back.

---

"There is one more thing and it is the reason I came on a day off."

"Go on."

"**I have spent nine years writing a card for every box in my room, and a card has a box number on it and a date and a place and a hand on it in a shorthand I cannot read and a shelf. And I have read every one of those four hundred cards in about four years, because there is nothing else to do in a quiet Wednesday, and I have never once wanted to know who was allowed to take one of them out.**" She took her hands off the table. "**I have wanted to know that for a fortnight and I have not asked you and I am telling you now so that it is in a head and not in a tray, because if I put it in a tray then this room has got a column and the room does not have a column.**"

---

Nobody settled anything on that Thursday and nobody was asked to.

A rule that has been in print for fourteen years in the original words cannot be repealed because the thing that would have to be asked is a piece of paper with a word at the top of it. About eleven thousand premises pay a levy that bought a third line of nine words. About four hundred and thirty-two cards are in about nine places in one district. A woman of forty-six has forty-one lines on the front of a sheet in a ring file in a cupboard and there is no column on that sheet for anything that is not a condition. A clerk of fifty-three ruled one box in pencil on one page out of about two hundred in four stacks on a counter in Bower Street and told one man about it on a Thursday and it is not written down anywhere. A man of fifty-two has stopped offering to check one thing and has not told anybody that he has stopped.

And a man of twenty-two was told on the Tuesday of the week before last, in a corridor off a service road, by a woman of twenty-four, that there is one of him.

Nobody has said so.

---

At ten at night the docket was on the bench with the job numbers on it in his own shorthand and the word came at nine until about one and at ten after, and the lamp was on, and the shutter was up, and the tool roll was in the drawer under it with the fourth pocket where it always is.

There are five things in that drawer. Four of them are other people's sentences. The fifth is a letter that carries a reference and whose own last line says it should not be produced without that office being asked first, and no proceeding has ever been started on the strength of it, and it is a drawer that has never held anything anybody could produce.

There is a jacket on the back of the chair by that bench and there has been since before the term turned over, and in the inside pocket of it there is a corner of card about two inches by three, and it has not left a room in a term and a half, and nobody has ever held out a hand for it, and the decision about it has not been made and was not made this month. The original is in the fourth pocket of the tool roll and the fifth line on it is blank.

---

In about a month four things have happened in this city and none of them is a document.

A set of plans came out of a drawer on a hook and went back into it. Two sheets of paper that had never been in one room were on one counter at once for eleven minutes on a Wednesday, and a woman of forty-five looked at one of them and would not say why, and a woman of forty-one read the other one out and then said out loud that she has been behind a counter in that room for nine years and has never once opened the book three shelves under her elbow. A clerk of fifty-three was asked what she thinks of a line at the foot of a district instruction and admitted that she broke it once in the week of forty-five and told nobody until a man asked her a different question. And a room off a street in Lattice Ward has nineteen slips in a tray about nothing at all.

Also: a woman of thirty-four in a cardigan has an opinion about a column that anybody can walk into a building and ask her for; a printer of fifty-eight has her name against a number on a quarterly return and is still the only person in this city who decides what a card is for; a man of thirty-four who works nights has nine words in biro that have stopped working and a second no with no reason on it; and a woman of twenty-one with a grey card in her own pocket has not been asked to stop and has not stopped.

And a rule that everybody in this city has been living outside for fourteen years is still in force, and nobody broke it, and nobody can take it away, and the only thing standing in the way of taking it away is a word at the top of a column.

About eleven people a week are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and there are four feet of shop with a name over the door and nineteen slips in a tray in a room off a street and four hundred cards that nobody can take back off anybody, and not one of those is a rota, and nobody in this city has said so in a room.

Thursday of week 57, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a bound register in four columns in a public building on a Thursday, with nobody behind the counter, and a woman of forty-one who came on her day off because it is the only table in this city where an authority is written down.*

*What the table is.* **Ground floor, Crown Terrace, a bound register in a canvas cover ruled into four columns, about nine hundred lines, fourteen years back to the week of week thirty-eight. No rope, no sign. A woman of thirty-four reads it out to anybody who asks because it is a public document and that is her whole view; she is there on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays, and four hundred people in fourteen years have had a line read to them at that table, and not one of them has been there on a Thursday.** The four headings, read out loud by two people standing up: date, place, hand, authority. Fourth column, a job title in every one of about nine hundred lines and never a person's name in fourteen years. Hand column blank in about four lines in ten; where it is filled in, a woman of thirty-four has found out twice and both times the person had left.

*What she went there to look for.* **A rule that has been in print for fourteen years in the original words cannot be withdrawn, so somebody must be able to take it away, so she went looking for the thing that could put two documents back in the same book. She found it by going through every list in this city with a word at the top of it. There are four. Three belong to a fund. The fourth is in this book.** It is a register of removals and it records what came out of a room. What came out of the reading room in the week of week thirty-eight was not a box; it was a binder, and it went down a corridor to a room with a register in it, and six lines in this book are about it. The four volumes in a box on a counter in another part of this city are a working copy, carry a card with a Wednesday on it, and have never been in this room. The Compact was never taken out of a room, so it is not in this book, and no line anywhere in this city says who may change it.

*The man on the floor above, as a record of what was said about him rather than of what he said.* **Fifty-eight. Keeps a count. Two declinations in writing against a locked room upstairs in fourteen years, and the reason given is that a locked room would have been the correct decision about the pages and about the end of the corridor. Six approaches, six counts, no agreement on anything. She separated three things in front of two people standing up: he is a person, he is the only person on that floor, and neither of those is a post, and there is no post on that floor and there is not going to be one. Nobody has ever asked him to be the authority over anything, and if he were asked whether the requirement stands he would say that it stands and say why, and nobody in this city can take it off him.**

*The six lines, read on a Thursday because there was nothing else to do.* **Six lines relate to the binder. Two have a hand in the hand column. One of those two has an author still in this city, said once in a corridor by the man upstairs, who will not say which and will not say why. The fourth column on all six, and on all about nine hundred, is a job title, and it is a word at the top of a column on a page in a book on a table, and anybody can walk in and read it and four hundred people have had it read to them and not one has come back.**

*The finding the movement closes on, recorded as a finding.* **The requirement is in print in the original words and cannot be withdrawn, the only thing that would have to be asked for it is a heading, and a heading is not a person. The sentence itself is in the room on a Thursday and this is what I have instead of it.** No form, minute, notice, report, committee or leaflet settled anything on that Thursday and nobody was asked to.
