# Chapter 174 — Where Does It Come Out

The Friday of week fifty-five was the first of the two. Eleven jobs on the card and nine of them in date, and the count kept on the back of a docket that lives in a jacket on a chair in Lattice Ward.

The word came at nine a minute until one and after one at ten, and on the Friday of week fifty-one it had been nine all day.

Nobody on that card asked where a release was going to come from. Nobody used the word. Five Fridays now, and a man of fifty-two has stopped offering to check it.

The last job of the day was a light on a landing in a block off a service road and a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket was standing on the landing when he got there, and had been, he said, for an hour.

---

"Where is the return path on this floor."

It was the first thing anybody had asked Marek Senn about a return path in four terms, and he had been a repairer for three of them and a student for a term and a half before that, and he had never once been asked it by anybody who was not already in a corridor.

"That is a question about a building and not about a card," he said.

"It is a question about a building," said the man of thirty-eight. "**Second landing, four flats, and there is one way off it after six o'clock and the way off it is through number twelve, and number twelve has a woman in it who is on her feet all day and who is not a way out of a building.**"

He had a job sheet in his hand with nothing written on it.

"That is a stairwell with the wrong direction on it."

"It is a stairwell with the wrong direction on it and a landing door that is the only door and a window that does not open, and I have written all of that on a job sheet and I have read it back to a man in an office and he said he would look at it."

---

He answered it the way a repairer answers it, which is by walking the floor and looking.

"There are four things on this landing that are exits and one that is not," he said. "**The thing that is not is the landing door, and a door is not an exit, it is a way of being in a building. The four are: the stair going up, which is a route and not an exit and I will come to that. The window in the stair, which is a cut in the wall on the inside and is painted over.**"

The landing is nine feet by eleven and there is a window at one end that does not open and a stair at the other that goes up, and the light on it is the fluorescent that had gone and had not yet been changed. He walked the wall and put his thumb beside the window frame, and there was a groove under the paint the width of two fingers, running down to the floor and away from the door.

"**That is a return path. It is a cut somebody made in a surface on purpose, in a place you can find it in the dark with one hand, and it is not an idea and it is not a promise, it is a groove, and it is here and it is about the same age as this frame.**"

"There is one on the third floor as well," said the man of thirty-eight. "**I have put a torch on it twice. There is one on the first and I have never looked at the second at all, and I have not written that down anywhere, and you are the first person I have told.**"

---

"What is the other three."

"The stair going up is a route and not an exit, and I want to be careful about that because it is the one people get wrong. A stair that goes up is a way of leaving a floor. A return path is a way of leaving a *building*, and if your only route is up then you are on a floor with a route and no exit, and on a second landing at two in the morning that is a different thing from what the drawing calls a stairwell." He put his hand on the rail. "**The fire door at the top of it has got a bar on it and the bar is a return path, and the lift does not come past this floor after six, and the fourth is the second stair, which does not exist, and everybody in this block thinks it does.**"

"So there are two."

"There are two that a person can use and there is a third that a person can find in the dark, and the fourth thing is not an exit and I have told you what it is."

"Go and look at the second floor before you write anything on that sheet."

"I have got nine jobs and a Friday," Marek Senn said. "**And if I go and look at the second floor on a Friday then it is a Friday and somebody in a building is going to write Friday on it, and in about four years there is going to be a piece of paper in this city that says a man went up one flight of stairs in about four hours and that is the whole of the record.**"

The man of thirty-eight wrote something else on the job sheet, and did not read it out, and Marek Senn did not ask.

---

"You have not told me whether any of that is legal."

"**No, and I am a repairer and I have got a docket and there is nothing on the docket that says what a building has to have, and if there is something on a form somewhere then it is not my form and I have never seen it.**"

The man of thirty-eight turned the job sheet over.

"Two exits and a groove under the paint and a bar on a fire door, and about nine people a week are being handed a card in this city that says a release waits for somebody." He said it flatly. "**And in four terms, on this floor, in this block, there is a woman who is on her feet all day and who is the way out of a building, and I have written it on a job sheet and a man in an office said he would look at it.**"

"Write the two."

---

The cost of it took two minutes to arrive and it arrived out loud, which is how everything on that landing arrives.

"**A job sheet with four locations on it goes into a district system at the end of the week and nobody reads a job sheet. In about four years the plate on the door of the room that matters in this city is still going to be the wrong number and that groove is going to be under two more coats of paint, and I have got a sheet of paper that is the only piece of evidence in this city that a woman in number twelve is a way out of a building, and it is in a system, and there is nothing anybody can do with it.**"

"That is the correct action."

"I know it is the correct action," said the man of thirty-eight, "**and I have got nine of those this month and every one of them is correct and I would like somebody to tell me the day one of them is going to be a different thing, and not one of them has been, and I am not going to ask you about that, I am asking you about the floor.**"

He wrote the two locations down and put the sheet in his jacket.

---

Then he asked the other thing, at the top of the stairs, with the light still off because a ballast is a ballast.

"I have got a district, and I am going to have to tell somebody about this, and there is nobody in this city whose job it is to be told this. That is not a complaint. **If you say it to me again in a room I will write it down, and if you say it to me in a room with a microphone on it I will not be able to help you, and you have a condition and I have read about it and I have got a file on you in a drawer that is not mine.**"

"I cannot say it again to a room."

"I did not say a room."

"You said a room with a microphone on it and that is the same sentence with the volume turned up," Marek Senn said. "**I have a condition on my share and it says I may not demonstrate, teach, assess or assist at any practice in this department, on its premises, under its insurance, at any time, unlimited by hours, and the department may put four names in front of any of them, and what you have just asked me for is none of those four words, and the reason I can do it is that this is a landing and I am a repairer and there is a fault on my docket. The day somebody asks me to say it twice, or to say it to somebody who was not standing on the landing, that is a different thing and I am not going to do it.**"

---

The man of thirty-eight went down and did not say anything else about it, and the light came on at about four o'clock and he left the panel the way a panel goes back.

And a man of twenty-two wrote four locations on a docket of his own in a shorthand he could read and then did not put them on anything, because a location in a company's system is a way for a floor to be found by somebody who has a reason to find it, and the groove under the paint is not going anywhere and the two exits are not going anywhere and the woman in number twelve is going to be on her feet all day on a Tuesday.

About eleven people a week are still going to be taught to wait for him.

Nobody asked him about that today. A man of thirty-eight asked him about a door, and the door has got a groove beside it under two coats of paint, and two exits, and a fire door at the top of a stair that goes the wrong way, and a job sheet in a district system that nobody reads, and none of the five is his and none of the five is a person, and the fifth line says *if the word does not come, withdraw and report* and he did not improve it and he will not.
