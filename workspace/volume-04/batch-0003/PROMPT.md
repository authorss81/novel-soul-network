**The counting rule, with its word lists, so that a later writer can reproduce it mechanically.** One hedge per occurrence of *about*, whole word, **case-insensitive**, followed by an optional article (*a*, *an*, *the*) and then **either** (A) **a number word, alone or in a chain joined by a hyphen or a single space** — *one two three four five six seven eight nine ten eleven twelve thirteen fourteen fifteen sixteen seventeen eighteen nineteen twenty thirty forty fifty sixty seventy eighty ninety hundred thousand million* — **or** (B) **one counted quantity** — *half dozen couple fortnight fortnights second seconds minute minutes hour hours day days week weeks month months year years room rooms form forms card cards word words line lines page pages field fields step steps part parts premise premises fold folds pound pounds penny pence job jobs hand hands name names face faces sheet sheets chair chairs seat seats item items entry entries copy copies place places building buildings door doors column columns packet packets envelope envelopes invoice invoices time times people premises men women woman document documents share shares case cases printer printers quarter size thing things*. **A possessive form is excluded. Count each occurrence once. Search the whole file, load book included. A bare number word is sufficient: *about nine* is a hedge and *about nine places* is one hedge.**

**Bold emphasis. Hold the batch average at or under 10.75 per thousand and every chapter at or under 12.4.** A bold span marks the beat inside a person's speech; **the elaboration after a speech tag loses the emphasis and the lead-in keeps it, and no line may carry a bold span on both sides of one speech tag**; in a load-book line the first and the last span always survive. Do not use a mechanical longest-span rule across a paragraph. **Nothing in the narration is bolded at all.** Movement I spent four passes getting this right after finding it inverted in fifty-one places, and Movement II enforced it as the chapters were drafted rather than swept afterwards, which is the order worth keeping.

**Every line must carry an even number of `**` and an even number of `"`, and diff the finished files against a pre-edit copy.** Then sweep: **no duplicated paragraph at an eighty-character threshold, no `* * *` marker, no month-name, month-date, day-date, year or day number, and no eighty-character overlap with an earlier chapter's wording for a sentence you are remembering on purpose.** Movement II's sweep against the one hundred and seventy earlier chapters found **two hundred and fifty-eight shared runs of eighty characters or more and every one of them is canon the brief requires word for word, a same-speaker echo that is on purpose, or the volume's inherited refrain**, and **five passages of prose reuse that were none of those were reworded before those files were finished**: a character's description restaged from an earlier chapter's opening, a room's inventory restaged from an earlier room's opening, a rule restaged, and a paragraph of canon restated verbatim. **Assume the sweep is worth running twice, and reword the reuse rather than declaring it canon.**
