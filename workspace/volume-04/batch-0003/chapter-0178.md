# Chapter 178 — Four Feet Of Shop

There are four feet of shop in the Quiet Quarter with a sign over the door that the woman who owns it paid for out of her own money in her first year, and on the Monday morning of week fifty-seven she was behind the counter with a folder of work and a boy of nineteen running a machine he has run once.

There are about four hundred and thirty-two cards in about nine places in one district, and not one of them can be taken back off anybody, and she is the person who decides what a card is for.

---

The first visitor came at half past nine with a form on a clipboard and a district's return in her hand, and she was thirty and she had a field on it that said what a practice was currently in use, and a field for the name of whoever set it in type.

"The coverage of a supervised confirmation where the holder is not a named party to the share," she said. "**It is a return and it is quarterly and there is a box on it that has got to have something in it, and I am not going to leave it blank, because a blank on a return is a question and I have got nine of them a quarter.**"

"What goes in it."

"Whether it is in use. And it is, and there are about four hundred and thirty-two of them out, and about nine hundred rooms have got a leaflet in them that says it, and I am not going to put a number in the box because a number on a return is a number somebody can quote." Sunniva Bellhouse filled it in. "**And the name at the bottom is mine and the shop's, and I am not going to leave that blank either, and you will tell me it is a commercial matter and I will tell you that it is not and you will go away.**"

---

The cost of it did not arrive that morning. It arrives in about four years, and she said so at eleven, to a man of about sixty who had come in with a card in his hand and a question he had been carrying since a Wednesday in a reading room.

"**In about four years somebody is going to walk into a building with a return in their hand and say the shop on that street printed about four hundred and thirty-two, and the shop has a name over the door that a person put there in their first year out of their own money. There is no version of that I can put in a drawer.**"

"You have the name on the back of four hundred."

"I have the name on the back of four hundred," she said. "**The four hundred and thirty-two have got this postcode on them and a printer's shop name on the back, and you are going to ask me what the difference is and I am going to tell you the truth and the truth is that I put the name on the back of four hundred on purpose, on a Monday, in front of that boy, because a card with a shop's name on it is a card a person can be traced on, and in about four years that is going to be the only thing anybody has.**"

---

The man of sixty-odd had one of the four hundred and thirty-two in his hand. He had not given his name at the reading room and had not been asked for it and still had not given it here, and the registrar has said since that she has not known since what it is.

"What I want to know," he said, "is whether it is safe. I have had it since the spring and everybody I have asked has told me that a lot of people have been given it, and that is not the same as it being safe, and I read that sentence in a room and I have thought about it for a fortnight."

"Then I am going to say three things and none of them is an answer and the first one is the only thing I know." Sunniva Bellhouse put her hands flat on the counter. "**One: the fifth line on that card says if the word does not come, withdraw and report. That is the same five words as the one a company prints and it is not the same sentence, and I did not write it and I set it in type in the spring, and I have been told that the difference between the two is who the technique is waiting for, and I do not know whether that is true.**"

"And the other two."

"**Two: the older ones are safer than the company's by the company's own argument and that is why people are using them to come off a link a ward needs, and I have printed four hundred and thirty-two of those and I stopped, and I have not got a way of taking the four hundred and thirty-two back off anybody and neither has anybody else. Three: I am the person who decides what that card is for, and you have come to the right shop for the wrong question, and there is no way in this city for me not to be the person who decides it, and nobody has offered me a way of not being and I have not asked for one and I am not going to ask you.**"

---

The boy of nineteen had the machine running and had stopped it.

"I have got about four hundred pieces of paper with my handwriting on them," he said, "and I am on none of them with my name, and I have been running that machine since the spring and I have not once been on a card with my name on it, and I have started to think about what happens in about four years if somebody in a building wants to know who set that card in type."

"Who does know," said the man of about sixty.

"**The shop knows. The shop has got a name over the door that somebody paid for, and there is a girl in a trade paper in about four years who is going to go round shops asking who prints these, and the answer is going to be that a shop in the Quiet Quarter printed four hundred and thirty-two of them, and that is me and I am not going to be on it and I am also not going to be able to un-ring the bell.**"

"About four hundred cards have your handwriting on them," said Sunniva Bellhouse, without looking round, "**and the four hundred and thirty-two have my postcode, and the two of you are arguing about the same thing from opposite ends of it, and neither of you is going to be right and both of you are going to be findable.**"

Nobody thanked her. She is fifty-eight and she has printed on that street for nineteen years and nobody has thanked her for anything since the spring, and she gave the hundred and forty-one pounds back in a coat to a person in a wet coat, and the money is in that coat with a person nobody can name, and it has not moved since.

"There is a coat on a hook in the back of this shop," she said, unasked, to the boy. "**I have looked at it twice this month and I am going to keep looking at it, and there is a form in this city that says a person can send money back to a person, and I have not used it, and the reason I have not used it is that a form wants a name at the top of it and the only name I have got is a postcode and a wet coat, and I would have to write a stranger's postcode on a district return because that is where a return goes.**"

"So it stays on the hook."

"It stays on the hook, and in about four years I am going to be sixty-two and somebody else will be behind this counter and they will not know whose coat it is, and that is the correct outcome and I have worked it out myself and I am not going to be told it."

---

The man of about sixty put the card back in his pocket and did not leave.

"**If I put it down and I say so, and somebody comes and says there was no report, what happens.**"

"Then there was no report," said Sunniva Bellhouse, "**and that is what the line says and I printed the line and I am not going to tell you whether it is enough, and I have been asked that by about four people in four months and I have given them the same sentence four times, and the sentence is that the fifth line is on the card and not in somebody's head.**"

He left with it. It is still in his pocket and it is about four hundred and thirty-second in a count that nobody in this city can finish.

---

Four feet of shop, a sign a woman paid for out of her own money in her first year, a till, a folder of work, a boy of nineteen and about four hundred and thirty-two cards in about nine places in one district that cannot be taken back off anybody, and a form on a quarterly return with a name at the bottom of it that is correct, and a district that asked four hundred and thirty-two is a number somebody can quote, and a card in about nine places with a printer's shop name in a postcode on it.

And the woman who printed them is the one who decides what they are for, and there is no way in this city for her not to be, and nobody has given her one.

Nobody is going to tell her that she is the one who decides what a card is for, because telling her would also be a request, and she is not going to ask him for one, and in about four years about four hundred and thirty-two cards will still have her postcode on them and four hundred more will still have her name on the back.
