# Chapter 173 — A Drawer On A Hook

The fourth building had a light out on the second landing on the Thursday morning of week fifty-five and it was a fluorescent with a ballast that had gone, and there were three people on that landing at ten because a district service turns up and a lead repairer turns up and a man with a docket on a Friday exception turns up, and the man with the docket was not the district service.

Ines Kolar was there because it was a Thursday and the second landing is on her round and she had been up it at eight with a trolley and had not reported the light because a light is not a condition.

Bryn Maw was there because the circuit is on his schedule and he had come on ahead of both of them with a ring of keys in his hand, which he does.

---

He said the thing he had come to say before anybody asked him anything.

"I have got a key to this building and I have not turned it and I am not going to be the one who turns it." He held the ring up. "**I said that in a room in Lattice Ward a fortnight ago in front of a woman of forty-one and a man with your face on, and I have thought about it since, and the thinking has not improved it.**"

"Then who," said Marek Senn.

"That is not my problem and I have been told it is not."

---

The room is 2-14 on the plate on the door and 2-07 on four hundred leaflets in a rack inside it, and both of those are correct about themselves, and the drawing that would settle which one the room is has been rolled in a drawer on a hook in a basement in this building since nobody in this building could say when.

There had not been a person in that basement since about the spring.

Ines Kolar was asked in the corridor on the Thursday of week fifty and she had looked down the stairs for four seconds and said she had never been down and was not going that day, and she had said it out loud so that it would be a thing she said and not a thing anybody found out.

She was on the landing at ten past ten and she said the rest of it in about nine seconds.

"**No, and I am not going to be the second person in this case to open a door I have told you I am not opening.** I did not put it there. I do not know what is on that hook and I do not know whose hand wrote anything and if I go down there and there is a sheet with a date on it then there is no column for that either, and I have been round this building every Thursday for nineteen years and there is not one thing in it I have ever been asked to find."

"Then who goes down."

She put the trolley against the wall.

"**Not me. Ask somebody whose job is a room.**"

---

There were nine men on that site and none of them had a job that is a room, and the two people standing in that corridor who did have one were the man with the docket and a man of forty-four with a caulking gun who was not in the building that day.

And a woman of forty-five came up the service stairs with a braid of tools in her pocket and a bag, because the light on the second landing had a ballast in it and there is a lead repairer for the second landings in this part of the city and she is the one.

She had been in the building nine minutes and she had already found the fault and had already said it was the ballast.

She said the next part before she was asked for it, and she said it to Marek Senn and not to the foreman, and the reason she said it to him is that he is the one who had asked the question.

"**A basement is a room and I go into rooms, and I have not asked why and you are not going to tell me why, and I want that clear before I go, because if I come up with a sheet and you tell me afterwards that you did not want me to go down there then neither of us has learned anything.**"

"You do not know what is in it."

"No."

"It could be nothing."

"It could be nothing," said Sera Quill, "**and there is a four-foot-one thing in a drawer on a hook and nobody has been in that basement since the spring, and I have been in eleven hundred basements in this city and every one of them has had a pipe in it that somebody had to look at, and that is the whole of why I am going and I am not going to improve it for a corridor.**"

---

Then she said the cost, out loud, on a landing, to a foreman and a woman with a trolley and a man of twenty-two, which is the way things arrive on that site.

"**In about four years somebody can say a woman of forty-five went into a basement in this city on a Thursday and did it for a man she has said no to seven times, and there is no version of that that stays a fact about a drawer. So I am telling you what it costs now while there are three of you in a corridor, and then I am going down, and I am not going to be thanked, and if this becomes a thing where the woman who went down is a person in this case then I will know it inside a month and so will you.**"

"Nobody is going to thank you."

"**I know. I have been in this trade for eleven years and I have never once been thanked and I have never once wanted to be and that is the only reason any of it works.**"

She took the braid out of her pocket and left it on the top step and went down.

---

Nobody went with her. A foreman of fifty-eight stood at the top of those stairs with a ring of keys in his hand and did not go down, and said why, and the reason was that he had said it in a public room and he was not going to be a different man on a Thursday.

A man of twenty-two wrote in his own book in the week of week fifty that nobody in this case has earned a basement, and that a basement is the room where the records are, and that if he is the first person through that door then he is the man who went through a door.

He stood at the top of the stairs and did not go down and did not say that he had written that down, and it was five weeks since he wrote it.

---

It took her six minutes.

There is one drawer on a hook on the wall of that basement and it is not locked and there has never been anything on it to lock. In it there is a set of plans rolled, with a date in ink on the outside of the roll, and the ink is older than any manufacturer's line on any plate in this building, and the plans are the building's own record and they are nineteen years old and nobody in this building has stood in front of them since about the spring.

The room is 2-07 on the plan. The plate on its door says 2-14 and the company that printed the plate did exactly what it was told and has said so twice to a foreman.

The two records have not been in the same room since before a service contract existed.

---

She came up the stairs and put her hand on the rail and said twenty words and then did not say anything for a while.

"Roll of plans, one. A date in ink. Nineteen years on it and the ink is older than the plates." Sera Quill picked the braid up off the step. "**That is what is in it. I am not going to be asked what I think of that and I have not been asked and I am not going to volunteer it.**"

"Nineteen years is before the contract."

"Nineteen years is before the contract," she said, "**and I am a lead repairer and I have been handed a piece of paper in this city twice in eleven years and both times it was in a corridor with nine men in it and I am not doing it a third time in a basement.**"

---

The foreman of fifty-eight was still at the top of those stairs and he had not moved in six minutes.

"I have got a guess," he said. "**I have got a guess that it is a set of drawings for four buildings going back nineteen years with every room on it that anybody ever wrote down, and that 2-07 is on it in a hand that is not a hand anybody has asked about, and that the answer to the plate on that door has been in a drawer on a hook in a basement the whole time and somebody has been wrong for nineteen years in about nine hundred places.**"

"That is a good guess," said Marek Senn.

"It is a guess. **I have not been down and I have said so in a public room and I am not going to start now, and if that is what is down there then the man who wrote it on a plan in nineteen years is a person and he is not me and he is not you and I would like the whole of this corridor to notice that I have just said the useful thing and then refused to be the one who uses it.**"

"He said that on the Wednesday and he said it again on the Thursday," said Ines Kolar, from the landing, without turning round. "**That is twice in a fortnight and neither time was in the same room, and there is a version of this case in which that is the only thing anybody does all term.**"

---

The plan went back in the drawer and the drawer went on the hook and the door was shut and a key turned a lock that four people in this city hold, and nothing in the case moved by one inch, and a foreman of fifty-eight has still not used his, and a woman of forty-six did what she said on the Thursday of week fifty and has not gone down, and a man of twenty-two was not the first person through that door and is not going to be.

A set of plans is nineteen years old and says 2-07 about a room whose plate says 2-14, and both of those are right about themselves, and there is one of each in this city and neither of them is in charge of the other.
