# Chapter 163 — Sixteen Weeks On A Card

He walked four miles on the Sunday to a converted shop unit in Lattice Ward with a folding screen and nine chairs and a district leaflet on a pinboard he has never seen taken down, and he did not go in, and four miles away a woman of twenty-four spent the one clean call she has in a week on a counter in a building in Bower Street, and said a district and nothing else, and neither of them has ever mentioned the other's Sunday to the other and there has been nothing to mention for a term and a half.

On the Monday morning of week fifty-two he walked four miles to Nine Bridges to meet a woman of twenty-nine in a motor room, and she was not there for him, and neither was the woman of twenty-four.

---

The corridor on the ninth floor of a tower block in Nine Bridges has a lift out of service and a woman in it doing rounds with a bag, and she was there because two of the four people she visits in that building live above the eighth floor and cannot get down, and that is not a thing to do on a Sunday and she had come on the Monday morning early.

She saw him in the corridor at about ten and did not stop walking.

"**Three weeks,**" she said.

"I know how long it is."

"I have not said anything about it and I am not going to," said Talia Venn, "**and I am going to say one sentence in a corridor because I do not have time and this is a corridor, and then I am going to go and do my round.**"

"You do not have to—"

"**You have made somebody askable who was not askable,**" she said.

Then she said: "**That is nine words. I have been carrying it since the Thursday and it did not get better and I am not going to improve it now. I have got two visits and a bag and a lift that is out, and that is the whole of what I have come up here for.**"

She went down the corridor and into the flat, and she did not say she had told him to do it, and she had not, and he has never once asked her whether she had.

---

She is twenty-nine, six years in the job, and there are about nine hundred lifts behind her name on a work list, and her employer is what puts her on a Friday and pays for the card, and the card says eleven parts.

The card in her wallet had a piece of cardstock on the back of it, and the cardstock had been cut from something with a printed edge on it, and on it there were sixteen lines in her own hand in biro.

Two numbers on each line. A week, and then what the card said, and then what the building had.

She did not hand it over. She put it on the top of the machine housing face down and then turned it round, and she stood with her back to the door while he looked at it, which she said afterwards she had done on purpose and would have done anyway.

"**Sixteen Fridays,**" she said. "Since the week of the thirty-first. It is not a rate. I am not going to let anybody call it a rate, including you, and I said that to a man with a lanyard on in a canteen seven weeks ago and he wrote it down."

---

"Sixteen lines is a sample," Marek Senn said. "It is not a rate and it is not a number anybody can act on."

"**No,**" said Marguerite Okpara. "It is a list, and a list is the only thing in this city that anybody could look at, and I have had it on the back of my own card since a Monday and I have shown it to nobody, and I am showing it to you, and here is the reason and it took me about three weeks."

"**You are the only person in this city who can say a number out loud in a room, and you are not allowed to be asked about it, and you are not allowed to tell anybody what to do with one. That is why I am giving it to you and not to a district and not to a company and not to a man with a clipboard.**"

"That is a strange thing to do on purpose."

"**It is the first sensible thing anybody has done with a number in a term, and I want you to sit with how strange that is.**"

---

She talked him through the sixteen lines and she did not editorialise once, and the numbers are hers and they are not round.

The card says eleven on every one of the sixteen lines. The buildings had eight, nine, nine, ten, nine, eleven, nine, eight, ten, nine, nine, eleven, nine, eight, ten and nine, and she wrote them down in the order the Fridays came and she did not total them because she said a total is a number somebody can quote.

"There is a fortnight in there where it is eleven both times and I checked twice," she said, "**and that is the part I would like somebody to have an opinion about, and I am not going to have an opinion about it myself because I am not allowed to have one and neither are you.**"

---

Then she said the cost, out loud, in about nine words, and she had clearly had it ready since the Monday.

"It is the back of my own card," said Marguerite Okpara, "**and my employer is on the front of it, and there is no copy, and there is no form in this city that takes a number like this, and if that card is in a pocket in a shop in Lattice Ward for a term and a half then in about four years the sixteen lines are gone and the only thing anybody has got is a man who cannot be asked about them.**"

"You want it back."

"No," she said. "**If I take it back off you then the list is on my own card and I am a lift engineer with a piece of cardstock on a wallet and no reason to be holding it, and that is worse. It is on a docket in a jacket in a room now and that is a better place for it than my wallet, and I have thought about that for three weeks and I am not being brave, I am being tidy.**"

---

He did not write it on the docket, and she watched him not write it.

"You are not going to put it on that."

"**No. If I put sixteen weeks of yours on a docket in a jacket then it is a record with a person in it, and I have spent a term and a half not being a person in a record,**" he said. "And the word on the back of that docket is nine, ten and eleven a minute and it does not stop me hearing a voice and it did not stop me asking a question about one, and I have already cost one person a name by writing a thing down on the right piece of paper."

"**So no. It is in your head and now it is in mine and that is two, and there is a man of thirty-four in this city who has counted about nine twice since the spring and once since the summer, and that is three, and he does not know you have got a list and you do not know he has counted it and I have not told either of you and I am not going to.**"

---

Marguerite Okpara put the cardstock back in the cut edge of the cardstock and the card back in the wallet, and the whole operation took about four seconds and was the only thing she did all morning that she had not done six years of.

"**Right,**" she said. "I have got four buildings before lunch and one of them has a fault on a lift that will be a fault on Friday. That is the whole of my week. I am going to go and be a lift engineer now and I would like it noted that I did not learn anything today and I have not been asked a question and I am going home at the same time as usual."

"You did learn something."

"No, I did not. You have got a list and you cannot use it and I have got a list and I cannot give it away," said Marguerite Okpara, "**and the two of us are the only people in this city who know that, and in about four years one of us will be asked about it and one of us will not.**"

---

There are sixteen Fridays on the back of a card in a wallet in Nine Bridges and there are sixteen Fridays in a man's head in Lattice Ward and there are about nine counted twice and once by a third person who works nights, and none of the three has been on a form, and there is no post whose business it is to hold any of them, and the man in the middle of it may not be asked about any of it, in a room with a microphone or anywhere else.

And a woman of thirty-eight is asleep in the middle of the day in a residential home four miles from a counter, and her name is in a box in pencil on a page in a stack of about two hundred that a district picks up and nobody knows when.

Nobody has said so.
