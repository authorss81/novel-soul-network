# Batch 0002 — Volume 04, Chapters 161–170 ("The Reading Room")

**Movement II of Volume 04. Write Chapters 161 through 170 as complete finished scenes, in order. Ten chapters across the four weeks from week fifty-one to week fifty-four, and the load-book run continues at 164 and ends at 173. `outline/volume-04.md` is the plan and it is not to be re-argued; this batch writes its *Movement II — The Reading Room* and nothing ahead of it.**

**The movement's job, in one sentence: the cost of a room with a counter and a rule is that it becomes a place where a person asks to be told something, and asking is findable, and the people who ask first are the people with the worst thing to lose — and two debts the last volume left open come due, one of them a telephone call that is about nine seconds long and is not a rescue.**

Then this batch must deliver, in this order: **the Thursday in Crown Terrace where Marek Senn asks a woman of thirty-four what she thinks of the fourth column, and the answer is hers and not his; a reading room that becomes a place people walk to, and a lift engineer of twenty-nine who finally shows somebody her list; the man of thirty-four on nights saying why he has stopped saying a thing; the telephone on a wall in a prefab office off the Meridian corridor rung by a person who is not Marek Senn, and a box number and a four-letter post town turning out to be a postbox in a town that is not Nacre; the standing problem a sixth time in a room with a microphone, and not resolved; and a person filling in a request slip in the reading room in the first person, in their own words, and signing it, with a woman of forty-one saying the rule is a rule and not a mood — which is a habit in a room and not a mechanism, and can be stopped by her walking out.**

**And one thing this batch must NOT deliver, which an earlier draft of this prompt got wrong: no caller signs in the Exchange and the book does not go to forty-five. `outline/volume-04.md` forbids it in terms — *a book of what a caller said cannot be written by people who did not call.* The Exchange may be staged on the Wednesday of week fifty-two and Bea Nunn may again decline to open the book, and the reason must be given out loud, and it must be a rule and not a mood, and the book is at forty-four lines at the end of this batch and at forty-four lines at the end of the volume.**

**The *about N* hedge, measured.** The finished Movement I, after the repair pass, is **31,247 words. 340 raw instances of *about* at 10.88 per thousand. 213 true hedges at 6.82. 311 bold spans at 9.95, worst chapter 11.62.** **That true-hedge figure is a hair above the one it inherited — Volume 03's last batch was 6.80 — and it is reported rather than dressed up.** The raw and the bold figures sit in the family Volume 03 finished in. The rule and its word lists are below and in `workspace/volume-04/batch-0001/SUMMARY.md`, and every figure comes from the strict matchers, because a lowercase-literal *about* cannot see a capitalised *About* and the obvious bold regex cannot see a span containing a single-asterisk italic, and **the loose matcher only ever under-reports, and under-reporting is the direction that hides a ceiling.** Hedge the genuinely uncertain and do not hedge the fixed: a character who has just read a clock says ten past five, an age is an age, a measured duration is not approximate. Three passes: clock readings and ages lose the hedge totally; a phrase repeated inside the batch keeps a fixed number of hedges, prose before load book and earliest first; **the canonical quantities keep every hedge** — about eleven people a week, about eleven thousand premises, about four hundred leaflets, about four hundred cards, about nine hundred doors, about nine places, about nine hundred rooms, about two hundred pages, about two hundred lines, about nine hundred cards, about forty-one people, about eleven letters, about four years — **and the four-year horizon is the volume's inherited repetition and is neither to be cut further nor to be increased.** **One figure is printed without a hedge and must stay that way: eleven thousand premises, which Volume 03 fixed in Chapter 141 without one.**

**The counting rule, with its word lists, so a later writer can reproduce it mechanically.** One hedge per occurrence of *about*, whole word, **case-insensitive**, followed by an optional article (*a*, *an*, *the*) and then **either** (A) **a number word, alone or in a chain joined by a hyphen or a single space** — *one two three four five six seven eight nine ten eleven twelve thirteen fourteen fifteen sixteen seventeen eighteen nineteen twenty thirty forty fifty sixty seventy eighty ninety hundred thousand million* — **or** (B) **one counted quantity** — *half dozen couple fortnight fortnights second seconds minute minutes hour hours day days week weeks month months year years room rooms form forms card cards word words line lines page pages field fields step steps part parts premise premises fold folds pound pounds penny pence job jobs hand hands name names face faces sheet sheets chair chairs seat seats item items entry entries copy copies place places building buildings door doors column columns packet packets envelope envelopes invoice invoices time times people premises men women woman document documents share shares case cases printer printers quarter size thing things*. **A possessive form is excluded. Count each occurrence once. Search the whole file, load book included. A bare number word is sufficient: *about nine* is a hedge and *about nine places* is one hedge.**

**Bold emphasis.** **Hold the batch average at or under 10.75 per thousand and every chapter at or under 12.4.** A bold span marks the beat inside a person's speech; the elaboration after a speech tag loses the emphasis and the lead-in keeps it; **in a load-book line the first and the last span always survive.** Do not use a mechanical longest-span rule across a paragraph. **Count bold markers as well as spans and diff the finished files against a pre-edit copy.**

**Every line must carry an even number of `**` and an even number of `"`, and diff the finished files against a pre-edit copy.** Then sweep: **no duplicated paragraph at an eighty-character threshold, no `* * *` marker, no month-name, month-date, day-date, year or day number, and no eighty-character overlap with an earlier chapter's wording for a sentence you are remembering on purpose.** Movement I's first pass found six such overlaps against the 150 earlier chapters and all six were reworded, **including the master in the inside pocket on a chair, which is canon and can be reworded without changing the fact.** Count both figures with the stricter matchers: the strict bold matcher is `\*\*(?!\s)(.+?)(?<!\s)\*\*` and the strict *about* is case-insensitive, **and the loose matcher only ever under-reports, and under-reporting is the direction that hides a ceiling.**
