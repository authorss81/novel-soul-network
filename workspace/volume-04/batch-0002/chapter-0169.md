# Chapter 169 — Eleven Slips In A Tray

Off a street in Lattice Ward there is a room that anybody may walk into and fill in a slip, and the slip wants four answers before it will go in a tray, and the last of the four is a person's name, and there is a bell on the counter and four drawers beneath the counter with about four hundred cards in them and a trolley with a high shelf against the far wall.

She is forty-one. She is in that room on the Monday, the Wednesday and the Friday and on no other morning, and she has been in the building since she was ten and in the room nine, and on the Wednesday morning of week fifty-three there were eleven slips in the tray and she had not put them there.

She has not counted them for nine years. She counted them in four minutes on the Wednesday and she did not tell him the number until she had decided to tell him.

---

"Eleven in three weeks," she said. "**And I want you to understand what I am about to say, because I have been registrar of this room for nine years and I have never once counted the tray.**"

"**In nine years, about forty people have asked to see a binder. I have brought the tray out to them and I have put the cards back and it has taken me about eleven minutes each time and it is the whole of what this room is. About forty people in nine years.**"

"**There have been eleven slips in three weeks and not one of them is about a box.**"

---

Wendla Ossick put her hand flat on the counter and did not pick any of them up.

"I am not going to tell you that I am frightened and I am not going to tell you that this is a good thing," she said, "**because the rule in this room is that a box does not come down without a slip, and a slip is a piece of paper and a person, and that is a rule and it is not a mood, and it was a rule before I came and I would be very surprised if you found a box in here that came down without one. A rule that holds when eleven people want something it was not built for is still the same rule.**"

"Then what is different."

"**That in nine years nobody has ever come in here and asked me a question that was not about a box, and in three weeks four people have, and I have answered all four, and I have not got a rule for answering a question.**"

---

The foreman came in at half past ten with a slip in his hand, and he had filled in one of the four lines and had left the other three, which nobody who has ever seen the sheet does.

He is fifty-eight and he has a carpenter's pencil behind his ear and he was in a prefab office off the Meridian corridor six days before, in the rain, with a heater going.

"I am going to say why I have only done one line," said Bryn Maw, "**and I have thought about it since Monday and I have not got it down to one sentence, so I am going to say both of them and you can have whichever one you want.**"

"**One: I have put a name on the fourth line of a piece of paper in a public room and I have known about nine hundred plates off a list that is not mine, and I am not a careful man, and I want to see what it feels like on paper once.**"

"**Two: I have got a key to a basement and I have not used it, and I would rather ask a person than open a door.**"

---

"Then ask a person."

"That is the whole of it and that is why I am here and I have been about four days getting to it," said Bryn Maw. "**A plate on a door in one of my buildings says a number that is not the room. I have asked a company engineer twice and both times I was told the list is the building's. The building's is a set of plans in a drawer on a hook in a basement in the fourth building, and there has not been a person in that basement since about the spring, and I have got a key to that building and I have not gone down, and there is a room in this city that says a box does not come down without a slip and a slip is a piece of paper and a person, and I have come here because I would like to be told no by a person rather than find out in a basement on my own.**"

Wendla Ossick looked at him for longer than was comfortable.

"I am not going to be able to tell you no," she said. "**There is nothing in this room about a building's own rooms. There are about four hundred boxes and about four hundred cards, one for each box, and every one of those cards has got a box number and a date and a place and a hand on it in a shorthand, and a shelf, and the hand on it is a person who put it there a long time ago. This room is what came out of this room. It is not what went into buildings.**"

"Then what do I do with a slip."

"You have already written on it," said Wendla Ossick. "**What you want, from when, to when, who is asking. You have done one of those and I am not going to add a fifth and I am not going to help you with the other three, and if you leave it like that it is a piece of paper and there is no tray for a piece of paper, and that is nine years of a room with a counter and a bell on it and it is not me being difficult.**"

---

He wrote the other three lines in.

From when: never. To when: still. Who is asking: Bryn Maw, gate, Meridian consolidation point.

And then he said the cost out loud, in a public room, at a counter, to a woman of forty-one and a man of twenty-two.

"That is a name at the bottom of a piece of paper in a tray in a room anybody can walk into," he said. "**I gave a box number to a man of twenty-two in a hut in the spring so that one person would not be a person, and I have said in four buildings since then that about nine hundred doors on a company's list is not a thing that belongs to me. I have said that out loud about four times and I have got no receipt for it, and this is the first piece of paper in this city with his name at the bottom of it about a door.**"

"**And I am not going to take it back, and I want the cost said out loud because I am the one paying it: a slip with my name on it is a thing somebody can point at and say he asked, in about four years, by somebody who has read about nine hundred plates and wants a name to go with them.**"

---

The other three of the eleven were on the counter and Wendla Ossick did not pick them up, and she said what they were, because he asked and because she had decided to stop not saying.

"**One is a man of sixty-ish who came the Wednesday before and asked me whether a thing can be safe because a lot of people have been given it, and I said no, and he said thank you, and he did not tell me his name and I did not ask him for it, and he is not in this room now and I have not known since what his name is.**"

"**One is a woman who wanted to know about a card, and she told me, and I did not write it down because I have not got anything to write it on, that the difference between the two cards everybody is talking about is that one of them has a printer's shop name on the back of it and the other one has nothing, and she said that was the first useful thing anybody has said to her in four months, and she went off with it.**"

"The other one."

"**The other one is a boy of nineteen who asked me whether anybody had ever written down what a person said in a room like this and been believed, and I said I did not know, and he said that is the correct answer and he was right, and he is not the boy on the till in a shop in the Quiet Quarter and I have not asked him anything.**"

---

"You will not count them."

"**I have counted them four times this morning and I have told you the number and I am not going to tell you what it means, because I have got a counter and a bell and a trolley and about four hundred cards and no tray for a piece of paper, and in a month somebody is going to come in here and say that this room does a thing, and I am not going to be the one who says it, and the only thing I have got to stop it with is that I am a woman who works three days a week and I have said so out loud to a man with a carpenter's pencil in his ear.**"

---

There are eleven slips in a tray in a reading room in Lattice Ward in three weeks and not one of them is about a box, and a foreman of fifty-eight has put a name at the bottom of one of them about a door he has not gone and looked at, and a woman of forty-one has said that a rule which holds when eleven people want something it was not built for is still the same rule, and that she is a woman who works three days a week and has said so out loud to the only person in the room with a pencil.

Nobody has said so.

*172. Wednesday of week 53, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a tray with eleven slips in it in three weeks, none of them about a box, and a foreman of fifty-eight with one line of a slip written and three of them blank.*

*What the room is, unchanged.* **Open to anybody who walks in off a street. Four things on a slip: what you want, from when, to when, who is asking. A counter with a bell, about four hundred cards in four drawers under it, one card per box, each with a box number, a date, a place, a hand in a shorthand and a shelf. A trolley with a high shelf against the far wall. She is forty-one, three mornings a week, in the building since she was ten and in that room nine.** The rule is a rule and is not a mood and it was a rule before her: a box does not come down without a slip, and a slip is a piece of paper and a person.

*The number, which she had not counted in nine years.* **Nine years of that room: about forty binder requests, and about a hundred and thirty people a year through the door before the spring. Since the spring: eleven slips in about three weeks, none of them about a box, and about two hundred people.** She counted the tray in four minutes and held the number back until she had decided to. Four questions that were not about a box in three weeks, against none in nine years; four answered; no rule for answering a question, and she said that in those words. **Her stated position, unasked: a rule that holds when eleven people want something it was not built for is still the same rule, and she is not frightened and is not going to tell me this is a good thing.**

*Him, and why he had one line.* A foreman of fifty-eight with a carpenter's pencil behind his ear, who had been in a prefab office off the Meridian corridor six days earlier, on the Thursday of last week. He came in at half past ten with the first line of the slip filled in and the other three left, and gave both of his reasons. One: he has put a name on the fourth line of a piece of paper in a public room and he wanted to see what that felt like on paper once. Two: he has got a key to a basement and has not used it, and would rather ask a person than open a door. His question: a plate on a door in one of his buildings says a number that is not the room; he has asked a company engineer twice and been told the list is the building's; the building's is a set of plans in a drawer on a hook in a basement in the fourth building where there has not been a person since the spring; and he would rather be told no by a person than find out in a basement on his own. **He has not gone down. The plans are not opened in this chapter and are not going to be opened this month.**

*What she said, and what she would not do.* **She cannot tell him no, because there is nothing in that room about a building's own rooms: it is about four hundred boxes and about four hundred cards, one per box, each with a box number, a date, a place, a hand in a shorthand and a shelf, and the room is what came out of this room and not what went into buildings.** He wrote the other three lines: from when, never; to when, still; who is asking, Bryn Maw, gate, Meridian consolidation point. She would not add a fifth thing to the sheet and would not help him with the other three lines, and if he had left it he would have had a piece of paper and there is no tray for a piece of paper, and that is nine years of a room with a counter and a bell on it.

*His cost, out loud, at a counter, in a public room.* **A name at the bottom of a piece of paper in a tray in a room anybody can walk into. He gave a box number to a man of twenty-two in a hut in the spring so that one person would not be a person; he has said out loud in four buildings that about nine hundred doors on a company's list is not a thing that belongs to him; he has got no receipt for any of it; and this is the first piece of paper in this city with his name at the bottom of it about a door. He is not taking it back, and the cost is that a slip with his name on it is a thing somebody can point at in four years and say he asked, by somebody who has read about nine hundred plates and wants a name to go with them.**

*The other three, which she named because I asked and because she had decided to stop not saying.* **A man of sixty-ish who came the Wednesday before and asked whether a thing can be safe because a lot of people have been given it, was told no, said thank you, did not give his name and was not asked for it, and she has not known since what it is. A woman who wanted to know about a card, who was told, and not written down, that the difference between the two cards everybody is talking about is that one has a printer's shop name on the back of it and the other has nothing, and who said that was the first useful thing anybody has said to her in four months. And a boy of nineteen who asked whether anybody had ever written down what a person said in a room like this and been believed, was told that she did not know, said that is the correct answer, and is not the boy on the till in a shop in the Quiet Quarter.**

*What she will not have.* **No second count, and no reading of the number, and no distribution, and no minute, and no fifth thing ruled on the sheet.** The one instrument she has said she will use is her own hours — three mornings a week, said out loud to a man with a carpenter's pencil in his ear — and her stated expectation is that somebody walks in inside a month and calls this room a thing, and that she is not going to be the one who says it.
