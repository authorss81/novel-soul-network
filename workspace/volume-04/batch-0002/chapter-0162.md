# Chapter 162 — The Whole Of The Condition

The Friday of week fifty-one was the first of the two, and there were eleven jobs on the card and nine of them in date, and the word came at nine a minute until about one and then at ten, and the count was on the back of a docket in a jacket on a chair in Lattice Ward.

Nobody asked him where a release was going to come from. Nobody used the word. That is four Fridays and the question has never once been put to him by a person who needed it answered.

---

The last job of the day was on the ninth floor of a block off a service road and it was a corridor job, a distribution board and a light, and the district nurse came up to the ninth floor at half past two because there was somebody on the card for that floor and somebody on the card for that floor had never been.

He did the board first, because that is the half of a callout that decides whether the light comes on. Two of the ways were dead and one was a lamp that had been changed in the summer by somebody who had not told the circuit. He wrote the circuit on the docket in his own shorthand and did not write the floor, because the floor is on the docket already, and he put the panel back with the dead ways marked and did not leave it tidy, because a tidy panel is a panel nobody opens for four years.

She is thirty-four and she has a file with her name at the top of it and she is not disciplined, and she has a way of standing in a corridor that does not take up any of it.

"**Do not start,**" she said. "I have got a form and I have got nine minutes and I am going to use all of them asking you for one thing and you are going to do it, and then you are going to go, and I am not going to thank you and you are not going to offer."

"What thing."

"**Say the condition. Word for word. All of it, including the part about the four names, and I have got it written down here in my own words and I want to see whether mine is the same as yours.**"

---

He said it.

He got as far as *at this time* and then he put the four names in before *at any time*, and he heard himself do it, and the woman of thirty-four was already writing.

"Stop. That is not the condition, that is the condition with a thing moved in the middle of it," she said. "**Start again from the top and do not look at me while you do it.**"

"You have it in front of you."

"**I have my own words in front of me and they have got the order wrong in them as well, because I copied them off a sheet in a room in Crown Terrace in about four seconds with about nine people in it, and I have not had anybody correct it since. That is why I am standing on a ninth floor. Start again.**"

He started again and got it right the second time, and it took about eleven seconds, and it is the fourth condition and it is exactly this: *he may not demonstrate, teach, assess or assist at any practice in this department, on its premises, under its insurance, at any time, unlimited by hours, and the department may put four names in front of any of them.*

"That is the whole of it," she said, "**and it is shorter than the version in the room and it is not a technicality, and I want to say the next part before I say anything else about it.**"

"Go on."

"**If that had an expiry on it, or a review on it, or a person’s opinion on it, then a person could wait for it to lapse,**" said the nurse. "And I have a file with my name at the top of it that I put there myself on a Friday in the spring with my own hand in the box that says completed by, because I did not want a form to go out with nothing on it. Nobody has disciplined me for it and I am not going to be disciplined, and if that condition ever gets a review date on it then I am going to be stood in a corridor by somebody with a clipboard waiting for a date, and there is no version of that where the corridor is mine."

---

She read her own version back to him and it was wrong in the same place.

"Same mistake," she said. "**Both of us. In a room with about nine people in it and about four hundred leaflets by the door, and a man of forty-four read a specification out loud and nine of us did not hear the difference between a line and a section. I have been saying that sentence in my own words to about six people since the spring and I had it wrong until a minute ago.**"

"You want it in writing."

"No," she said. "**I want it in my head and I have now got it in my head. I am not writing it down because a piece of paper with a condition on it is a thing somebody can ask for, and I have spent a term finding out what happens when somebody can ask for something.**"

---

At about ten to three the depot manager of fifty-two came up the stairs, which he does not do, and he had the Friday sheet in his hand and a question of his own.

"I am not here about the board," he said. "I have had a word from somebody in our own office about the condition and I have been told it is not the office's to change, and I have looked at it, and I want to put it to somebody who can put it to somebody. Not to you. Through you."

"Then do not put it through me."

"**You have not asked me why.**"

"You are going to tell me anyway," Marek Senn said. "Tell me and then I will say no to both of us."

"**I have been putting that exception forward for renewal since the start of the term,**" said the depot manager, "and the box it lives in says no reason given by holder, and I have read that box about four hundred times and I have never once put a reason in it, and I have been offered, twice, by two different people, to take it to somebody who could look at the wording."

"No."

"**Say a reason. Not for me. For the next man.**"

"Because the moment somebody starts looking at the wording, it becomes a thing that could be worded differently," he said, "**and the only thing that has kept it honest for a term and a half is that it is a sentence nobody can improve. A rule that can be looked at is a rule somebody is waiting to be told is wrong. There is no version of that condition with a review in it that is not a person in a corridor with a clipboard.**"

The depot manager of fifty-two looked at the sheet for a moment.

"That is the same thing she said," he said. "**Nine words different and the same thing, and you have both said it to me inside four minutes without either of you having heard the other, and that is now twice this term. Right. The sheet stays as it is.**"

---

He went down the stairs and stopped on the fourth landing, because the order of it is the only part of that sentence that matters and he cannot hold it after two o'clock, and he has not been able to since the Friday of week forty-seven, when he wrote two sentences down in the wrong order on the back of a docket and the wrong order made a man who had come to help sound like a man who had come to complain.

There is no form in this city that holds the order two sentences were said in, and there is not going to be one, and the note he wrote that day said ask him again or do not use it, and then he decided the note was not right either.

He said the condition out loud on a fourth landing to nobody, once, all of it, in the right order, in about eleven seconds.

It is the only thing in this case he is still the best person in the city at, and it is a sentence about what he is not allowed to do, and there is no version of a term where that gets better by being worked at.

Then he went down the rest of the stairs, and out to the lift, and the light on the ninth floor came on behind him while he was still in the lobby, which is the part of the job that is not in anybody's line of business.

---

There is a condition on a share and it is in a company's system in a box that says *no reason given by holder*, and it is renewed for the term, and the officer of the levy fund wrote *no name* in two boxes in his own hand in the Monday of week forty-two and is getting letters about it, and none of the three things is the same thing and all three of them are the same habit.

A woman of thirty-four put her own name at the top of a file in the spring and has not been disciplined, and on the Friday of week fifty-one she got a sentence right that she had been saying wrong to about six people since then, and she got it right because a man on a ninth floor was made to say it twice.

The number on the back of the card has not moved and nobody in this city can be shown the shape of it, and the man in the lanyard has just spent a Friday being the only person who can say a sentence correctly from memory, at the request of a person who needed it, and he cannot be the one who says it twice to anybody else.

Nobody has said so.

*165. Friday of week 51, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a woman of thirty-four with a file with her name at the top of it, who had been saying a condition wrong to about six people since the spring, and who has not written it down.*

*What the day was.* **Eleven jobs, nine in date, the eleven parts said eleven times, the word at nine until about one and at ten after, on the back of a docket in a jacket on a chair. Four days a week on grade two and two Fridays at grade three, the exception renewed for the term in a system, in a box that says no reason given by holder. Nobody asked me where a release was going to come from. Four Fridays and nobody has.** The last job was a board and a light on the ninth floor of a block off a service road, and it is a corridor job and the corridor is where this movement keeps putting me.

*What she asked for, and how long she had.* **Say the condition, word for word, all of it, including the four names. Her reason: she has a version in her own words and she wanted to know whether it was the same. She has had nine minutes and used all of them and told me not to thank her and not to offer.** She copied hers off a sheet in a room in Crown Terrace in about four seconds with about nine people in it, in the spring.

*The two things that are canon and were said on that landing.* **The whole of it, and it is one sentence: he may not demonstrate, teach, assess or assist at any practice in this department, on its premises, under its insurance, at any time, unlimited by hours, and the department may put four names in front of any of them. And the sentence she gave about why it must not get a review on it: if that had an expiry on it, or a review on it, or a person's opinion on it, then a person could wait for it to lapse, and a file with a name at the top of it is a corridor somebody stands in with a clipboard waiting for a date.**

*The order, which is the whole of it.* **I got the four names in before *at any time* on the first pass and she stopped me at the word *this*. Both of us had it wrong in the same place. Her copy is a person in a room in Crown Terrace in the spring and mine is a person on a Friday in week forty-seven writing two sentences down in the wrong order, and the wrong order made a man who had come to help sound like a man who had come to complain, and the note I wrote that day said ask him again or do not use it, and then I decided the note was not right either.** There is no form in this city that holds the order two sentences were said in. I said the whole of it on a fourth landing to nobody, once, in the right order, in about eleven seconds, on my way down, and that is the only correct performance of it in this case and there was nobody in it.

*What a man of fifty-two came up nine floors to do.* **He has been putting the exception forward for renewal since the start of the term, has read the box about four hundred times, has never once put a reason in it, and has been offered twice by two different people to take the wording to somebody. He said not to me and through me. I said no before he gave me the reason and he gave me it anyway.** The reason I gave is that a rule somebody can look at is a rule somebody is waiting to be told is wrong, and there is no version of that condition with a review in it that is not a person in a corridor with a clipboard. He said it was the same thing she had said four minutes earlier, nine words apart, and neither of us had heard the other, and he said that is twice this term. The sheet stays as it is and nobody has moved it and nothing has changed.

*Her file.* **Her name at the top of it, in her own hand, in a box that says completed by, put there in the spring because she did not want a form to go out with nothing on it. Not disciplined and not going to be. She is not going to be helped by anybody's condition and she knows it and she is using a sentence of mine as a wall, and I have decided I am not going to be the one who improves the wall either.**

*What she did not do.* **She did not write it down. She said a piece of paper with a condition on it is a thing somebody can ask for, and she has spent a term finding out what happens when somebody can ask for something, and she wanted it in her head and now it is.** Entry 165. The book is at forty-four lines and Bea Nunn does not open it on a Friday, and the Exchange is on Wednesday, and nobody has rung a man of fifty-one since a Tuesday in Volume 03 and a woman of thirty-eight is asleep in the middle of the day.
