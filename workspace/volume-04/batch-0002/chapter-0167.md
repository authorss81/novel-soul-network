# Chapter 167 — What Is On The Hall Table

The eighth of the eleven was on the Friday of week fifty-two, four weeks after the seventh, and it was the same room in Crown Terrace, and there were forty folding chairs in rows again and nine of them empty again, and the stack by the door had been made again, about four hundred of them, and every one of them has a fee on it and the name of the session and a list of five jobs on the front, and nobody has ever asked whether four hundred is the right number for a room with nine empty chairs in it.

Two of the five jobs printed there are him, and a piece of software picked those two out of two job sheets four weeks after the spring, and nobody in the room picked anything, and the microphone was on its stand at the front of the room with nobody behind it.

He was four rows from the back with a docket flat on his knee and a lanyard round his neck that he had not asked anybody for.

---

The woman who types things was at her table by the wall with a folder behind her and she did not stand up, and about nine people did not ask her anything, and she has eleven letters in that folder she is not going to answer and one session left.

The officer of the fund read the specification in the voice he reads everything in and got to the third line, which is not a section, and said the same thing about it he said last time, and four hundred leaflets went out with the last one and the address of that building is on it and a job title is on it and the job title is in four hundred pay packets in one district.

A man in a district jacket was in the fifth row on the Friday of week forty-eight and wrote down three answers in his own notebook. He was not in the room on this Friday, and there was nobody in the fifth row, and there was nobody in the room wearing a district jacket.

---

The woman who asked was forty-three and she had read the leaflet and she had a question and the question was not stupid.

She said her name and where she worked and she said the question in about nine words and then said it again more slowly, which people do.

"**There is a card on the hall table at one of the three addresses I work at. It has got four steps on it and a fifth line. It has got my employer's name at the top. I have read the leaflet and the leaflet says what the fifth line is for. Is that about me.**"

Nobody in the room said anything for four seconds.

Then a woman of about twenty-six at a table by the wall said, "You can ask anything, that is the second half of the evening."

"I am asking him," said the woman of forty-three, "because he is the only person on that sheet who is a person."

---

He answered in nine words and it did not answer her.

"**I do not know, and I am not allowed to teach you how to find out.**"

"That is not an answer."

"No," he said. "**It is the shape of one. I will tell you what I have got. I have not got anything in this city that can tell the difference between a practice that is safe and a practice that has been handed round a lot, and I have looked for four days and I have not found one and I have not been given a second person to ask. And what is on that hall table is a piece of card and the only question anybody can put to it is the one you have put, and I cannot put a hand on it, and I am not allowed to show you how you could.**"

---

"What am I supposed to do then."

"Read the fifth line," Marek Senn said. "**That is not a joke and it is not an answer either, and I would like it noticed that I have given you the only thing I have got and it is that the fifth line is on the card and not in somebody's head. If nobody comes, put it down and say so. That is what the line says and I am not going to improve it and I am not going to tell you whether it is enough.**"

Nobody in the room improved it either.

---

Then he said the thing himself, which he had never done.

He stood up in the fourth row of the eighth session, in a room with a microphone on a stand, in thirty-odd people, and said the condition, and it is his condition, and he said it in the first person.

"**There is a condition on my share. I may not demonstrate, teach, assess or assist at any practice in this department, on its premises, under its insurance, at any time, unlimited by hours, and the department may put four names in front of any of them.**"

He stopped there and it was not a speech and nobody in the room interrupted him, and the microphone was on a stand and not switched off, because nobody had asked for it to be switched off and nobody in this city can ask.

"**That has now been said in public six times and it has not been resolved once, and I am saying it myself because I have been letting other people say it about me for a term and a half and it has never once got me out of a room. It stops me doing the thing. It does not stop anybody asking me about the thing. There is no rule in this company or in this city about who may be asked, and I have checked that four times now, and the answer has been the same every time, and the answer is that there is not one, and the reason there is not one is that a rule about who may be asked is a rule about who is findable.**"

---

The woman of forty-three put her hand up, which nobody expected, and asked the question that is the standing problem and is not a question about anything else in the room.

"**So who is allowed to ask you about this.**"

"Nobody can tell me that."

"Then I am allowed," she said. "**I am a member of the public and I have read your leaflet and I have asked you a question in a room with a microphone on it, and there is nobody here who can stop me, and I would like that written down somewhere and I do not think it can be, and I would like somebody to try.**"

Nobody tried.

---

Then she said her own cost out loud, in the room, in the second half of the evening, and nobody wrote it down, and she had not asked them to.

"On the Monday of this week I did three addresses and tonight I said a name and a street out loud in this room with a microphone on a stand, and the address I work at is on the district leaflet that went into about nine hundred rooms in the spring, which is not the same one as the four hundred on that stack by the door, and there is a hall table with a card on it in that house and I go in that house four days a week," she said. "**I have thought about it on the bus and I have decided I would say it again and I would also like it not to have been a microphone. Both of those are true and I have not been able to put one of them down for the other, and I have been a person who has been asked nothing for nine years and I am a person who has been asked something and it cost me a hall table.**"

---

The woman of twenty-six at the table by the wall wrote nothing down and said why.

"There is nobody in the fifth row this week," she said. "**Last time there was a man with a notebook and he took down three answers and that was in my hearing and I did not stop him and I have thought about it for four weeks. This week there is nobody, and that is not better for the man of twenty-two and it is worse for the woman of forty-three, and I am not going to tell the room which of those two things I think matters more, because I do not know.**"

---

Nobody settled anything that evening. The specification has four sections and three lines and the third one is funded and nine words long, and eleven thousand premises pay a levy that bought it, and the committee does not meet again.

The standing problem was said in public for the sixth time, and not resolved, and no rule about who may be asked was invented, and there is still no mechanism in this city that can tell the difference between a man doing a thing and a man being asked about one.

The microphone was still on its stand at a quarter to eleven and nine chairs were still empty and a man of twenty-two was still in the fourth row of a room in Crown Terrace with a docket in his hand.

Nobody has said so.
