# Chapter 164 — A Note In His Own Hand

The man of thirty-four who works nights was in a stairwell in Nine Bridges at ten past four in the afternoon on the Tuesday of week fifty-two, which is four hours before he starts, and he had asked for the stairwell and not for the canteen, and he had said why on a Friday in week forty-seven in a room with a tray of milk jugs in it.

He has a card in a plastic sleeve on a lanyard and he has had it since the spring, and there is a second one in his wallet with four correct steps on it and a fifth line in a different printing.

He had written something on the back of a work card in the spring, in his own hand, and he had it folded twice in the wallet with the two cards.

---

"You have read it."

"I have read it," he said. "**And I could not do it. That is the thing I have come to tell you and I have been three weeks getting round to it, and I am going to say it badly and then I am going to read it out, and you are going to tell me whether that is the same sentence, and if it is not the same sentence then I have lost something and I would like to know that on a Tuesday and not find out in about four years.**"

"Read it out."

He read it out. It was nine words in biro on the back of a work card and it was in his own hand and the hand was the same hand as the other writing on the card.

It said that he had said out loud, to two men, that anybody handing you the other sort of card is a front, and that he had gone back to a doorway in Nine Bridges in the spring to say it to the man who had said it to him, and that the doorway was a stairwell for a different building, and that he had been stood in about four stairwells since and none of them was the right one.

"That is the sentence," he said. "**That is nine words of mine from the spring and it has not changed and it does not do the work.**"

---

"Which work."

"The work of stopping," said the man of thirty-four. "**In the week of forty-seven I told you in that canteen that I had decided I was not going to be a man with a grudge about a card, and I gave you a reason for it and the reason was a doorway and a man who had gone, and I have been able to hold that reason since the Friday, and I can hold it now.**"

"Then you have not lost it."

"I have lost the other one," he said. "**In the spring I stopped saying it because a man had gone. That was the reason and it was a fact I could put my hand on. In the week of the thirty-eighth a woman with a lanyard came and asked me to take the word on my own floor, for my own four people, so that they would have somebody to wait for, and she was pleasant about it and she gave me about four minutes, and I said no.**"

He looked at the folded card in his hand.

"**And I said no in about a second and a half, and I have not been able to say why since, and that is the whole of what I have come about, and I would have said it in the canteen if the canteen had not been full.**"

---

"Why did you say no."

"**I do not know.**"

"You have had two months."

"I have had two months and I have tried it out loud in a stairwell four times and on a bus once," said the man of thirty-four, "**and the sentence is a different sentence every time and none of them is the one I said in the spring. In the spring it was a doorway and a man who had gone. That was the reason. There is not a second reason. There is a door with something behind it that I cannot get a hand on and I have stopped trying in a fortnight because I was getting angry about it and angry is how I got the first one wrong.**"

---

There was a thing Marek Senn could have done then, and he did it in his head, in about nine seconds, and then he did not do it.

He could have said the thing the man had not said, out loud, in a stairwell, from his own book, in the man's own words, and the man would have had a reason in nine seconds and it would have been a reason that worked in a corridor and in a canteen and in a stairwell, and nobody in this building would ever have known.

That is demonstrating.

"I am not going to do that," he said. "**There is a condition on my share and it says I may not demonstrate, teach, assess or assist at any practice in this department, on its premises, under its insurance, at any time, and unlimited by hours, and what I have just described is all four of those in nine seconds and I have done it in my head and I am telling you that I have done it and I am not going to do it.**"

"**I did not think you were going to.**"

"I want it recorded that I wanted to," he said. "**I have wanted to since the Friday of week forty-seven. I have wanted to in nine rooms and in a car park and on this stairwell and I have not done it and I am not going to and the reason I am telling you instead of doing it is that a sentence of mine in a stairwell is not a reason, it is a sentence, and in four years it will be the only thing in this city saying you were right and that is the worst possible place for a reason to be.**"

---

Then the man asked the thing he had come to ask, and he had it written on the folded card as well.

"**Am I the man on the card.**"

"No."

"Because if I am, then I said no for a reason and I have only lost the words," said the man of thirty-four, "**and if I am not, then I said no for a reason I cannot get at, and I have lost the words and the thing, and I would like to be told which of the two I have got.**"

"**You are not on the card. There is a job title in about four hundred pay packets in one district and it is a man of twenty-two, and he is me, and I am not on the Friday rota in nine rooms, and I have not been in nine of the rooms the card is issued into, and I have never once been the person the word is supposed to come from.**"

"**About eleven people a week.**"

"About eleven people a week, and it is not a rate, and it is on the back of my own card, and about four of the eleven are not on the rota because a company does not hold a course on a Friday and has not held one for eleven years."

---

The man of thirty-four said the number back and Marek Senn wrote it on the docket, and he wrote the wrong one.

He wrote ten, and the man said nine, and he crossed it out and wrote nine, and the man looked at it.

"That is twice," he said. "**I have said it three times now and you have got it wrong twice and I am not being difficult and I want that on the record, because I have been on the other side of that and I know what it costs to be the man who is difficult.**"

"That is the condition I have got and I have not told anybody about it and I am not going to tell you about it, and it is not the same condition and you should not confuse the two."

"I am not confusing anything. I have a card in a plastic sleeve and four correct steps in a wallet and I have had both since the spring and neither one has ever been right," said the man of thirty-four, "**and you have written a number down wrong twice and said it is a condition you cannot talk about, and that is two men in a stairwell with a thing neither of them can hand over.**"

---

At twenty past four he put the folded card away and said the thing he had come to say, which was that he was going to keep both cards, and that he was not going to ask anybody to take either of them off him, and that he has been asked twice since the spring by people with lanyards to hand his card in and hand in a company one instead, and that he had said no twice and had a reason for the first no and had not got one for the second, and that he wanted it noticed that the no came first both times.

"Noticed," said Marek Senn. "**And I have written it down in the order you said it, which is the only part of this that anybody can use.**"

"**I do not want it used. I want it kept. There is a difference and nobody in this city has got a word for the difference and I am not asking you for one.**"

---

A man of thirty-four wrote nine words on the back of a work card in the spring and they are still his and they still do not work, and he stopped saying a thing out loud because a man in a doorway had gone, and he has said no twice to being the man on a card for his own four people and he can say why about one of them and not the other, and there is nothing in this city that can tell the difference between a reason you cannot say and no reason at all, and there is not going to be one, and the only evidence anybody has is that the no came first both times, and that is in a stairwell and not on a form.

On the Wednesday of this week there are nine people in a back room and a tin that is not locked and a book at forty-four lines, and a woman of sixty-one is not going to open it.

Nobody has said so.
