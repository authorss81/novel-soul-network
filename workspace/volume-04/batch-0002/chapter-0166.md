# Chapter 166 — Four Letters And A Counter

Bryn Maw is fifty-eight, there is a flattened pencil behind his ear that has been there most of a shift for about thirty-one years, and he has been on the gate at Meridian consolidation point for all of them, and the office has a telephone on the wall of it that has been on that wall since before the mobile came and that is the only number in that office a voice comes out of.

He has never rung it himself, and nobody has rung it about the thing, and a man of twenty-two told him on the Monday morning of week forty-third that there are two people in this city who could put a hand on it and one of them is him.

It rang at about eleven on the Thursday morning of week fifty-two while Marek Senn had the panel of the second gate lamp open.

---

Bryn Maw said the name of the gate into it, which is what he says, and a voice he did not know asked him whether the yard at Meridian consolidation point was the return point for a consignment, and Bryn Maw said it was on the docket and the docket was the company's.

"I am going to say this slowly," said the voice, "**because I have been on hold twice with a different office and I would like to get this right. Is there a post office box on your paperwork? A number and a town, four letters, not Nacre.**"

"It is on the paperwork."

"**Whose box is it.**"

"Not mine," said Bryn Maw. "**I have got a stranger's box number in a stranger's handwriting and a town with four letters in it and I am not the holder of it. I am the second holder of it. There is one other and he is twenty-two and I have not got him here.**"

A pause.

"I am not going to ask you for that," said the voice. "**I only asked because I have got a field on a return that has not changed in four quarters and I have been told to re-verify it before I can submit it, and there is nobody here to ask.**"

---

The name on the call was Rowan Teale and he is thirty-one and he fills in a box on a stock return every quarter whether anybody asks him to or not, and the box is a return address, and for four quarters it has had the same four-letter post town in it and the same box number under it.

He put both of them on the docket with a finger and he had a printout of four quarters' worth in front of him and he had never asked about either of them because nobody has ever asked him about either of them.

"Now then," he said. "**You have not got to. I am going to do the ordinary thing, which is ring the town and check it is a real address, and if it is a real address then I can submit it, and if it is not then I put a note on it and it goes to a person who does not have my problem.**"

"That is your job."

"**That is my Thursday,**" said Rowan Teale, and put the phone down on the desk in an office a long way from Meridian consolidation point.

---

Bryn Maw put the handset back on the wall cradle and stood looking at it.

"That," he said, "**is the only number in this office a voice comes out of, and I have told one man that in about four minutes in the spring and I have not told anybody since.**"

"You did tell me."

"**I told you because you were the second one. A man at a company is the first one who has rung it, and that is different, and I have not said it out loud and I am saying it out loud.**"

---

What came back from Brin, which is four letters and is not Nacre and is a town with a high street and a sorting office on it, took about nine minutes and went like this.

A recorded voice, which said the office and the day and the hours: Monday to Friday between eight and six, and Saturday morning, and closed on the Sunday, and to collect from a box you must quote the box number and the reference from the card.

Then a woman, whose name is Bettina Rook, who said she was at the counter and could help with that, and who asked him for the box number and the town, and wrote nothing down, because there is no place at that counter to write a stranger's box number down.

"It is a real one," she said. "**It is here. It is on the end wall, third bay, and it has got a card on it, and the card is the only record of who holds it and the card is not something I can look at for a man on the telephone. I am not going to tell you who holds a box and I would not do it if you were standing here.**"

"I only wanted to know it was real."

"**It is real. It is four feet by three and it is a hundred years old and there are about four hundred of them in this building and about nine in this town that anybody collects from, and the rest are company names and a company name is not a person.**"

---

"**It has not been collected from in about two years,**" she said, before the line went.

"How would you know that."

"**Because I empty the row on a Friday and I have emptied it for eleven years and I can tell you the ones that are touched and the ones that are not, and that one is not touched, and I am telling you that on the telephone to a stranger and I have not said your name to anybody and there is no form in this building that takes one.**"

---

Bryn Maw sat down on the chair that is not meant for sitting on and put the carpenter's pencil on the desk and did not pick it up again for a while.

"**Right,**" he said. "Now I am going to say the cost out loud because it has arrived and nobody asked me for it and you are in the room."

"**In about four years a person can stand at that counter with a reference off a card and a person in this city who holds this address will have something taken off them by a person they have never met, on a Friday, with a card, and there is no way to find out that it is happening, because the card is the only record and the card is in a building a hundred years old in a town with four letters in it.**"

"That is a trade," Marek Senn said. "That is what a box is."

"**Yes, and that is not the part that has cost me.**" He turned the pencil over. "**The part that has cost me is that there is a telephone on this wall and it is the only addressable thing about me in this city and I told you that in the spring and chose it on purpose, and a man at a company has now rung it and I have said a stranger's box number out loud in this office with the door propped, and I have said it twice, once to him and once to you, and it is in his ear and it is in yours and it is not in the diary.**"

---

"You did not want the call."

"**I did not want the call,**" said Bryn Maw. "And I did not say so for about two hours and I want to say why, because I have been thinking about it since he put the phone down and it is not a good reason and I am going to give you the bad one."

"If I had said it to him at eleven o'clock this morning then it would have been a foreman of fifty-eight telling a man at a company that a telephone in a hut is not an addressable place, and that is a sentence about a company and he would have had to do something with it, and he has a form and it has got a field in it, and in about four years there is a note in a file about this office. I have watched about four companies put a thing in a file that should have gone in a man's head. I said that in the spring and I am not taking it back."

"**Saying it to you is different and I could say it to you because you have got a book that is not a system and no reference on it. So I did not want the call and I wanted the person who was already in the room, and I have thought about that for two hours and it is the same shape as the thing I did not want to say to him, and I do not have a better account of it than that.**"

---

He put the pencil back behind his ear and got the site diary and turned it round on the desk, and there are ten lines in pencil in it, and every one of the ten is the same entry, and the hand got worse as it went.

"That is still in here," he said, "**and no box number has ever been in there and none is going to, and a site diary goes in a container when this site shuts and there is not one line in this book that is not a person.**"

He squared it.

"**The field on your three documents is still blank and the number is still on nine hundred doors in four buildings and the number came off a room list that is not anybody's, and I have not seen a page in this city with a name at the top of it in about four years, and a town with four letters in it is the first thing anybody has rung in this case and it is a box and it is a hundred years old and there is a woman at a counter who will not say a name and who is right.**"

---

A box number and a post town with four letters in it are in a left-hand page in a book in Lattice Ward in a man's own ink, and a foreman of fifty-eight has been the second holder of it since the Monday of week forty-three with nobody to hand it to, and on the Thursday of week fifty-two a stock clerk of thirty-one rang the only number in that office a voice comes out of and was told by a foreman that there are two holders, and then rang a town with four letters in it and was told by a woman at a counter that the box is real, that it is on an end wall, that nobody will tell a stranger who holds a box, and that it has not been touched in about two years.

Nobody has been told the name of anything. The field is still blank on three documents in three buildings and on a company's own stock docket.

Nobody has said so.
