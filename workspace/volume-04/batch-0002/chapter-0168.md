# Chapter 168 — The Thing About The Call

Roy Amiss is fifty-one, and about a fortnight of stairs is what is left in him, and a building in this city had him for a leg over four weeks in the middle of a run of Thursdays and gave him back a printed card for his own table at home and a line for his employer in somebody's list of things that went right.

Nobody rang him before or after. He said so out loud in a repair shop four miles from where he is now, to a man who was the only one in it, and then he said he was not saying it to anybody, and then he went out with the leaflet and the door took a while.

That was in the week of week forty-six. It is now the Monday of week fifty-three and nobody has rung him in the seven weeks since he said so out loud in a shop.

---

The district nurse of thirty-four has a file with her name at the top of it and she put it there herself in the spring, and in the week of the thirty-fourth there was a woman in that district who promised him a telephone call on the Monday, and there is a form, and nobody rang him on the Monday, and she was not at fault and the other woman was not at fault either.

It is her job and it is not her form.

She has opened a door of her own in that unit, and the door is this: any person may bring an ending to their unit, not a card and not a form, and she will do it or not, and if she will not she will say why in about nine words and the reason will be about the share and not about the man. Nothing goes on anybody else's form, including the nine-field one. She will not put his name on it and she will not put hers at the top of it.

He is in her list of about forty-one people in that district who cannot get out of a thing closed since the Tuesday of week thirty-eight, and he is the one who has never had a call.

---

She rang him from a corridor on the Monday morning of week fifty-three at about eleven, and it took about nine seconds, and she had written nothing down before she made the call and she wrote nothing down after it.

"This is the district service, my name is the nurse of thirty-four and I am the person who did not ring you," she said. "**I have been not ringing you since the Thursday and I have not got anything to give you this morning. I am going to say one more sentence and then I am going to put the phone down and you are not going to be able to have this conversation, and that is correct.**"

---

He said the thing about the call.

He had it ready. He had had it ready since the week of week thirty-four, and he had said a version of it in a repair shop to a stranger, and he had said afterwards that he was not saying it to anybody, and this is the first anybody he has said the whole of it to.

"I read that on the Thursday night," said Roy Amiss, "**and I waited a week for somebody to ring, and nobody rang. I have not rung anybody either and I am not going to now and I have got a phone that works and a hip and a fortnight of stairs in me and I have had three weeks in a row where the only thing I have done with it is wait for a telephone.**"

"That is a true sentence and it is not a request."

"**I know it is not a request. I have not got a request. I have a leaflet on my table at home and it is about a practice and not about me, and I have had it since the Thursday of week thirty-four, and you could have told me what it was about for nine words any day in three weeks.**"

---

"That is fair."

"**It is not fair, it is accurate, and I have been trying to get from one to the other for seven weeks and I have not got it, and I am not going to get it today either, and you have told me in one sentence that there is nothing coming and that is the sentence I have been owed, and it cost you nothing to say and it has taken me seven weeks.**"

Nobody wrote it down. The nurse of thirty-four has a corridor and a file with her name at the top of it and she did not write a word of that call on anything, and the reason she gave, afterwards, in nine words, is that the reason is about the share and not about the person, and a person who has been rung goes on a list, and the list in that district is a piece of paper and a piece of paper is findable.

---

He was not going to be comforted, and he was not going to be thanked, and he had said so in a repair shop in the week of week forty-six, and he said it now in nine words of his own.

"**You have rung me and that is a thing you have done and I am not going to say thank you for it, because a thank you is what a person says when somebody has done something for them, and you have not done something for me. You have done a thing at the same time as you were going to do a thing to about forty other people and I am one of them and I have not been singled out and I have noticed.**"

"That is correct and I am not going to argue with it."

"**I would like to say the other half, because I have been not saying it since the week of week forty-six and I have practised it on a bus.**" He took his time with it. "**I asked a man in a shop whether the thing on my table was about me and he gave me nine true words and they were not the answer, and I have had seven weeks with that. It is not the answer to the thing I asked either. So there is no reason anybody owes me a sentence and I would like that written down in whatever the nearest thing to a record is, which is this.**"

"**There is no record of it,**" she said. "**That is not me being cold. There is a form in this district and the form is how the district finds out about a person, and I have just told you there is nothing coming, and the two of those cannot both be on the same piece of paper.**"

"**Then I have said it into a telephone and a nurse of thirty-four heard it, and that is the whole of the record and it is in a corridor and not in a system, and I have got a worse one of those than a leaflet.**"

"Is that it, then."

"**That is it. There is nothing else and there is not going to be anything else, and I am not going to come and see you and I am not going to send you a leaflet and I am not going to put you in a review.**"

"Right."

"**I am going to say the last of it and it is the only part of this telephone call that is mine, and I would like it heard once and then I am going to stop.**" She took her time. "**There is a form in this district for people who have been rung. I have not filled it in. I am not going to fill it in, and the reason is that the form is how the district finds out about a person, and the whole of the reason I rang you is that the district did not find out about you in seven weeks and should have, and I am not going to fix a thing I have just told you is not fixed by writing it down.**"

---

Then she said her own cost, out loud, in the corridor, to nobody, because she has nobody to say it to.

"**I have never rung one of them before and I have had about forty-one names on that list since the Tuesday of week thirty-eight and I have done it once, today, in nine seconds, and I am going to go back to that corridor on the Tuesday and do the other forty if I can get them, and I have not got a rule for ringing forty-one people in a week and I have not asked anybody for one and I am not going to.**"

"Nobody is going to stop you."

"Nobody is going to help me either and that is the difference and I have known it for a term and a half," said the nurse. "**There is no post. There is no minute. There is a piece of paper about forty-one people and a woman of thirty-four who has rung one of them and the piece of paper has not got a mark on it, and in four years somebody is going to look at that piece of paper and see one gap and not be able to say what it was for.**"

---

The leaflet is on his table at home and it is about a practice and not about a man, and it is still there, and it is still wrong for him to have, and a woman of thirty-four has told him so in nine seconds on a Monday morning and has not taken it off him and is not going to.

He is owed a thing that is not a leaflet. There is no form for it. He was not thanked, he was not found, and he is not going to be comforted, and the payment has changed almost nothing, and the one thing it has changed is that a man of fifty-one in this city now knows that a woman rang and that it was not a rescue and that it was a rule.

About eleven people a week are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard.

Nobody has said so.
