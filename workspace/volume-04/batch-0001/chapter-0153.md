# Chapter 153 — Nine In Date

A Friday is a Friday. There were eleven jobs on this one and nine of them were in date, and he said the eleven parts eleven times, and the word came at nine, at ten and at eleven a minute between half past nine and two o'clock, and the count is on the back of a docket in the inside pocket of a jacket over a chair in a repair shop in Lattice Ward.

Nobody asked him about a fifth line. Nobody asked him whether it was safe. Nobody asked him who they should be waiting for. Nobody said the word.

He had waited for it about eleven times by a quarter past one and it had not come.

---

The man of thirty-four who works nights was on the list because his employer pays for the card, and he was in the canteen off a service road at ten past one with a wallet open on the table and two cards in it.

He had the same one on a lanyard and the same one out of a printer's counter and he had had them since the spring, and he had told two men in Nine Bridges that anybody handing you the other sort is a front.

"**I have stopped saying it,**" he said.

"When."

"Three weeks ago." He did not look up. "**Not because you told me to and not because the other one is worse. Because I said it to a man in a doorway in Nine Bridges in the spring and I went back to that doorway before the summer was out and the man had gone and the doorway was a stairwell for a different building, and I have been stood in about four stairwells since and none of them was the right one, so I have decided I am not going to be a man with a grudge about a card.**"

"**That is a better reason than mine was.**"

"**It is a worse one for the case and a better one for me,**" said the man of thirty-four, "**and I have worked that out and not told anybody, which is a sentence I am going to say once and then never again.**"

---

He put the two cards on the table side by side.

"**I do the job with the lanyard one in my pocket and the printer's one in my wallet, and the reason is that I do not know which one to do it with, and I have not rung anybody, and there is no number to ring, because the card says if no word withdraw and report and I have not got anybody to report to and the word has not come and it is not coming and it has not come for about four months.**"

"Report to who."

"**That is what I am telling you and I have told two men in this canteen and neither of them was in a position to do anything about it,**" he said. "**The card has got a company on it and a register reference and about four of those references are in about nine rooms in this city and I have never rung a room. I have rung a number about four times and it is a company's number and it is a service line and the man on it has asked me for the model and the site and I have given him both and he has told me it is not a fault and to ring the district. That is four rings in four months and about forty minutes of that line in total.**"

---

Somebody behind the servery dropped a tray and neither of them looked round.

"The other half," he said. "**Eleven on the card. About nine on the rota. I have worked that out on my own and I am not saying it as a complaint.**"

"**I know that number.**"

"**I do not know it. I have got a card that says eleven and I have counted about nine on a Friday twice since the spring and once since the summer, and twice is a sample of two and I am aware of what a sample of two is, and I am also aware that if the card is wrong then the four people it is wrong about are standing in rooms waiting for a person who is not on the rota, and I have been one of those people twice and there was nobody there, and I went home both times and I did the job anyway, and doing it anyway is not a system.**"

Nobody said anything for four seconds.

"**So I have got a card that is wrong and a man in a lanyard who is not going to be here on a Tuesday,**" he said, "**and the thing I want to say to you is not a request. It is that you are the only person in this city who has ever said a true sentence to me about a card and you did it in a lift, and I have been thinking about whether to come and find you for three weeks, and I have come, and I have not got anything for you, and that is the whole of it.**"

---

The briefing at two o'clock was four minutes long and it is on a Friday because a company does everything it does on a Friday.

The depot manager is fifty-two and has a board with a list on it and a telephone on a stand with a spiral lead, and a district had telephoned at ten past one with a question, and he read it out because that is the procedure, and the question was for the man in the lanyard.

It was whether the district could put a name against a release on a Friday, and whether the name would be the person who gave the word.

"They want a name," said the depot manager, "**and I am reading it out because it is a written question and not a conversation, and I have told them the same thing I tell everybody, which is that I do not put names on releases and that the card is the card.**"

He looked down the table.

"**You do that on Fridays. Do you want to say anything to it.**"

"Nine words," said Marek Senn. "**A release is finished by the person holding it, not by a person waiting.**"

Somebody at the end of the table wrote it down.

---

And then the depot manager said the thing he says at the end of every Friday briefing, to nobody in particular, and it was a sentence that had been said in a room in Crown Terrace three times.

"**For anybody who has not read it: there is a condition on his share. He may not demonstrate, teach, assess or assist at any practice in this department, on its premises, under our insurance, at any time. That is true and it is not a technicality and I am not going to pretend it does something it does not do. It stops him doing the thing. It does not stop anybody asking him about the thing, and there is no rule in this company or in this city about who may be asked, and I have stopped offering to check it because I have checked it three times and the answer is the same every time and the answer is that there is not one.**"

The room did not do anything.

"Right," said the depot manager. "**Nominations for the Friday of next week are on the sheet by four.**"

---

At half past two he went out to the car park off the service road and stood next to his own van for eleven minutes with a job sheet in his hand.

He was aware, the whole eleven minutes, of not being needed, and it is a thing he has been aware of since the autumn and it has not once turned into a telephone call.

Then he got the docket out and tried to write down the two sentences that had mattered in the last three hours, in the order they had been said in, and he could not do it, and he wrote them down in the wrong order, and the wrong order made the man of thirty-four sound like a man who had come to complain.

He crossed it out and wrote a note along the bottom of the docket instead, in the same hand, and the note said: *cannot hold the order of two sentences after two o'clock. not the words. the order. ask him again or do not use it.*

Then he thought about that for a while, because the note was not quite right either, and there is no version of it that is right, and the word was at eleven a minute at two o'clock and at nine at nine in the morning, and the difference between those two numbers is the whole of what he is and it is not going to be told to anybody.

The master is in the inside pocket of a jacket on a chair in a repair shop in Lattice Ward and the drawer under that bench holds five things, four of them other people's sentences.

There is no form in this city that can hold the order two sentences were said in.
