## What the batch delivers, in order

1. **The box in pencil takes its first name, and the name is written by somebody who is not the person it is for.** A care worker of thirty-eight telephones the forms counter in Bower Street, says she held a share on a bay with four people, says one of them answered and it was not her, says she took it off anyway in four seconds, and is not asked whether she was willing, and is asked nothing else, and gives her name to a woman of thirty-one who writes it in pencil. **Then she asks for it out, and it cannot come out**, and the reason is given in nine words: a name written in pencil is a name on a page in a building, and the page goes back in a stack of about two hundred, and a district picks those up and nobody knows when. **The person who asks to be asked was not asked, and says so, and says she would have said yes.**
2. **A woman of forty-six with a trolley finds out that the number on a door came off a list in a company.** There are two kinds of plate and she is the only person in this city who knows it. A service plate is printed and has the company's name along the bottom edge and the number on it is a service number; **the room in Bower Street has never had its own number on the door, and about nine hundred doors in four buildings are the same, and nothing broke and nobody did anything.** She has signed a condition sheet for nineteen years that nobody has ever asked her to mean anything, and she has a forty-first line for conditions where there are no conditions that nobody has ever used, and **she asks the question of a man of twenty-two that her sheet cannot answer, and there is nowhere to put the answer.**
3. **The Friday rota, eleven jobs, nine in date, the word at nine, ten and eleven a minute, and the standing problem said a fourth time in a depot briefing and not resolved.** A man of thirty-four on nights has stopped telling people that the other sort of card is a front, and says why, and it is not the reason the case would have wanted. **And the word's consequence changes form again: he cannot hold the order of two sentences after two o'clock, and writes a note on a docket saying ask him again or do not use it, and then decides the note is not right either.**
4. **The old network archives open.** A counter, a bell, four drawers of about four hundred cards, a trolley, a woman of forty-one who works three days a week, and a rule in eleven words: a box does not come down without a slip, and a slip is a piece of paper and a person. **The Marrow Falls material is not in the room. It was never in the room. It was a binder that went down a corridor fourteen years ago and the seal is a column in a register in another building, and nothing in that room is locked, and she says so twice, deliberately, watching his face.** A slip needs a name. A slip without one is a piece of paper and she has not got a tray for pieces of paper.
5. **A Continuity Office assessor of thirty-four reads the register out at a counter because it is a public document and that is her whole view. Iven Sore, fifty-eight, asks three smaller questions, refuses, and gives a count.** Six lines relate to the binder. Two were written by a hand rather than a title. One of those two has an author still in this city. **He will not say which and he will not say why, and he says a count is not a line and a count is not a person, and he has been offered in writing by two offices to have the register moved into a locked room upstairs and has declined both, and he has told nobody until now.** The fourth column of that register has a job title in every one of about nine hundred lines and has never once had a person's name in it, and the woman at the table has never been asked what she thinks of that.
6. **The Exchange, the Wednesday of week 48, nine people, the book at forty-four lines, and nobody called.** The book is not opened. The reason is a rule and not a mood and it is given in eleven seconds: **a room of people who did not call is not nine callers, and a line in a book is a promise that somebody stood at a telephone and wanted something, and if she writes tonight then in about four years somebody reads line forty-five and finds out that a woman of thirty-eight who did not want to be found said a true thing in a room with nine people in it, and that is not a book of callers, that is a book of people she liked.** A care worker of thirty-eight talks for nine minutes and none of it is written down. **The book does not go to forty-five.**
7. **The choice, in a corridor, out loud, by him.** A slip needs a name and the only name in this city he could put on it is not his. **He writes two letters and does not write the third, and the woman whose name it is was standing behind him and had read over his shoulder, and she says: *you have just made me the reason*.** He asks her properly, before the pencil, as she told him to on a half landing two days earlier. She says yes. **The cost comes out before the yes, in her own words: she works nights, she is awake in the day, the daytime is the only part of the week that is hers, and if she is a person who has been asked something then the next person this city needs is her, and she will come in the day.** He tears the slip in four on the counter. A registrar watches a better-than-nothing request go in a bin and says so, and then says she has a school group in eleven minutes.
8. **The seventh session of the levy series, in a room with a microphone, and a woman of twenty-six who types things stands up for the first time in three years.** Eleven letters in a folder behind her, all asking a man of forty-four why a levy return went out with two boxes that say *no name*, and she has answered none and nobody asked her to, and she says it is not a decision, there is nothing on the paper to answer them with. **And then the thing nobody in that room had said: those two boxes are the only two on the sheet with words in them, and every one of about eleven letters has gone to her.** A man of forty-four says the other half of his own sentence out loud for the first time in that room: a document with no name on it is a document people get angry at, and four years later four thousand of them went out and about forty people who had written in were told nobody was dealing with it, and everybody in the city has that leaflet, and he has read it as carefully as she has. A man of fifty-one asks for the microphone to be switched off for the second Friday running and is told for the fourth time that he does not have to, and says he is going to take the fee anyway, and asks for it to be on a record.
9. **A printer of fifty-eight, a boy of nineteen, and a hundred and forty-one pounds.** A person in a wet coat wants four hundred more cards. **They are not printed off a photograph this time. The shop's name goes on the back of every one of them, in the same place, the same way, the same size, because a card with that shop's name on it is a card a person can trace** — and about nine hundred plates in four buildings come off a company's list, and the same company is the assembler and the seller and is not the author. **The envelope goes back to the person who paid it, and the cost is said out loud: there is now nothing on that table to take.** The boy of nineteen writes the name on four hundred cards and says he is not going to be on any of them with his name and is going to be on all of them with his handwriting, and she says that is the correct answer and it is worse than hers.
10. **The close.** The forty-first line of a condition sheet has something in it for the first time in nineteen years: **fourteen lines in pencil, on the back, in the space where the conditions go, upside down relative to the front.** Nine are in the building in Bower Street, three in the second building, two in a stairwell in Saltmarket that a company plated in the year the stairwell was new and that are not mistakes. **There is no column for a discrepancy and no form in this city that takes one, so the somewhere is the back of a sheet, and the somewhere used to be nowhere.** In about four weeks a woman of thirty-six will turn that sheet over and ask what the pencil is, and a woman of forty-six will have to say that a man of twenty-two asked her a question in a corridor that her sheet could not answer. **And the price, said on a landing: in four weeks the only thing that has changed in this city is a sheet with pencil on the back of it, and about eleven people a week are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and he is still that man, and he did that on purpose in a room with nine people in it. Nobody has said so.**

## The two re-bases, and what the batch did with them

**Re-base 1, the power state.** `outline/series.md`'s Volume 04 line — *Stage 2, with the first sustained relay* — is **superseded and is not available at any price.** The relay was staged once, in Chapter 136, and the carrier stopped it on the sixth of seven headings, and her round was re-cut, and she is not going to do it again. **Ten chapters of Volume 04 Movement I contain no relay, no practice, no comparison run by him, and no page that implies one might be available.** What the batch shows him doing instead is being in corridors, being wrong about a number, losing the order of two sentences, tearing up a slip, and standing in a room with a woman of thirty-eight who is going to say the thing that matters.

**Re-base 2, the recognition.** *A recognised researcher, not a public hero* is kept and re-based into an object rather than a title. **Nothing in this movement is a recognition and no page anticipates one**, and the batch plants the ground it will be built on: a woman of forty-one says that her room is built out of about four hundred cards in four drawers, that every one of them has a person who put it there written on it in a shorthand, that the reason anybody can find anything in there is that the finding is a card in a drawer, and that the card is not hidden and not locked and is in a building anybody can walk into. **He writes down six shelf numbers and gets one of the six wrong, and the chapter says plainly that the number was not the thing that mattered, and that he did not think about the rest of it until about six weeks later, by which time it was in a book in an inside pocket on a chair and it had cost somebody a name.** That sentence is in Chapter 154 and it is the only forward reach this movement is allowed, and it is not a promise.

## The canon decisions of this batch, recorded so that a later writer quotes them rather than re-derives them

1. **THE BOX IN PENCIL HAS A NAME IN IT AND IT CANNOT COME OUT.** Doreen Abbiss, thirty-eight, a night care worker, is the person who ended the share on the bay on the Tuesday of week thirty-eight. **Nobody has ever asked her whether she was willing and nobody is going to.** She gave her name on a telephone to a stranger in about nine seconds and then asked for it out. **It cannot be taken out of the page, and the page cannot be taken out of the stack, and the stack cannot be taken out of the counter, and there is no mechanism in this city that could do any of the three.** A name written in pencil is the least recoverable object in this case and the only box in it that can take a name.
2. **THE PLATE IS A SERVICE PLATE AND WAS NEVER A BUILDING'S.** The room in Bower Street has never had its own number on the door. **About nine hundred doors in four buildings are the same, and the numbers come off a room list the company does not own and has asked for twice and been told is the building's, and a set of plans in a drawer on a hook in a basement has the rooms, and the two have not met in nineteen years.** There is a clean rectangle in the dust two inches wider than the plate and nobody recorded it coming off and nobody is going to put it back.
3. **A SEAL IS A COLUMN IN A REGISTER, AND A REGISTER IS IN A PUBLIC BUILDING WITH A COUNTER ON IT.** The Marrow Falls material was never locked. Four volumes went down a corridor in the week of week thirty-eighth, fourteen years ago. **The register has about nine hundred lines, four columns, and a hand column that is blank in about four lines in ten.** Iven Sore has declined in writing, twice, to have the register moved into a locked room upstairs, and told nobody until Chapter 155, and does not agree to anything.

A slip with a person on it is a request; a slip without one is a piece of paper, and there is no tray for pieces of paper. **No field was added to anything, no box was ruled, and no second box exists anywhere in this manuscript.**
