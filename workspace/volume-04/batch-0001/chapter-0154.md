# Chapter 154 — A Card Catalogue With A Rule

The old network archives are open to anybody who walks in off a street in Lattice Ward and fills in a slip, and the slip has four things on it, and the fourth one is a person.

What you want. From when. To when. Who is asking.

There is no fee and there is no membership and there is a counter with a bell on it and a card catalogue in four drawers under the counter and a trolley with a high shelf against the far wall.

---

Wendla Ossick is forty-one and she works Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays and she has worked in the building for thirty-one years and in that room for nine, and she told him the rule in the first four minutes because she says it to everybody in the first four minutes and she is not going to make an exception of him.

"**A box does not come down without a slip, and a slip is a piece of paper and a person,**" she said. "**That is the rule. I have not had it in writing and I do not need it in writing and if you ask me whether it is a rule or whether it is how I feel, it is a rule, and it was a rule before I came, and I would be very surprised if you found a box in this room that came down without one.**"

"I am not going to argue with it."

"**Nobody does. That is not a compliment, it is a Tuesday.**"

---

He filled one in.

The bell went and she looked at it and did not touch it.

"The Marrow Falls material," she said.

"Yes."

"There is no box."

He waited for the rest of it and there was not any.

"**I have been in this room nine years and I have never once heard a person ask for the Marrow Falls material and say that and get told there is no box,**" said Wendla Ossick, "**and I want to say the next part properly, because you have got a docket in your hand like a man who is going to be in a hurry about it, and I am not going to be hurried by a docket.**"

"Go on."

"**Nothing was taken out of this room. Things were taken out of rooms. This room has about four hundred boxes in it and the cards in those four drawers are one card per box and about four hundred cards, and if you want a box you ask for a box and you get a box.**"

---

She squared the slip against the marble and left her hand on it.

"**What came out of here in the week of week thirty-eighth, fourteen years ago, was not a box. It was a binder. It went down a corridor to a room in another building with a register, and the register has a line on it, and the line has got a date on it and a hand on it and a place on it, and if you want the binder you do not want a box from me, you want a line in a register in Crown Terrace.**"

"And the line is sealed."

"Nothing is sealed," said Wendla Ossick. "**I am going to say that twice and I am going to say it the same way both times, and the second time is for your face, because I can see you doing it.**"

She said it again.

"**Nothing is sealed. There is no lock in this room. There has never been a lock in this room and there is a sign about eleven feet from the door that says so, and I have never once seen anybody read the sign. What there is, is a column in a register in another building, and a column is not a lock, and a column can be read by anybody who can get into the building it is in, and that building has got a counter on the ground floor and the register is on a table by the counter and anybody can walk in and look at it.**"

"Then why does nobody do it."

"Because nobody has been in a hurry," said Wendla Ossick. "**And because it is a page of a register with a date on it and a hand on it, and to most people a hand on a register is a thing that means there is a person to ask, and there is not one, and that is the whole of what is in front of you.**"

---

He asked the question that turned the morning.

"What happens if I leave the last line off the slip."

She picked the slip up and read it and put it back down at an angle.

"It does not go in the tray," she said. "**There is no tray for a piece of paper. It goes back to you. I have had this conversation with a librarian in Crown Terrace and with a man from a trade paper and with a woman who wanted a bundle and did not know what a bundle was, and nobody has ever asked me what happens, and the answer is that a slip with a person on it is a request and a slip without one is a piece of paper, and I have not got a tray for pieces of paper, and that is not me being difficult, that is nine years of a room with a counter and a bell on it, and I have worked in this building for thirty-one years and there was a room upstairs with a bell in it for the other twenty-two and I did not like that one either.**"

"Do you want one to go in the tray."

"No," said Wendla Ossick. "**I want you to understand what you are about to do and I am not going to help you do it quietly.**"

---

He stood at the counter for a while with the docket on it.

"**There is no name in this city that ought to be on that slip for this,**" he said, "**and I am the name that would go on it if I let it, because I have got a job title in about four hundred pay packets and I have been in a room in Crown Terrace with a microphone pointed at me, and the correct answer is that I put my own name on it, and I have spent a term and a half not doing that.**"

"Then do not do it."

"**If I do not, the line in the register in Crown Terrace does not get looked at, and about forty-one people in a district are on a thing that cannot be ended, and the only route out of that corridor is a counter with about two hundred pages on it and a box in pencil that has got a name in it this week that a woman of thirty-eight put there on a telephone and cannot get out.**"

Wendla Ossick looked at him for longer than was comfortable.

"**I am going to say one thing and I am not going to say it as a favour and I am not going to say it twice,**" she said, "**and it is that you are the first person who has come in here in about four years who has told me what is actually at the end of it. They all want a date. You have told me a corridor and a counter and a woman of thirty-eight, and I have been sitting at this counter for nine years and I would like you to notice that that is a thing about you and not about me.**"

---

At twenty past eleven she went to get the tray out of the back, and he waited by the card catalogue under the counter with the four drawers, because there was nothing else to do and the bell was not ringing.

There were about four hundred cards in the drawers in alphabetical order by the word on the top line, and each card had a box number on it and a date and a place and a hand in a shorthand he could not read, and a shelf.

He wrote down six of the shelf numbers on the margin of a docket, because he wanted to know how the room was arranged.

He got one of the six wrong, and he did not find out for about three weeks, and when he found out he had a bad half hour about it, and the reason it mattered was not the number.

It was that a room like this one is built out of about four hundred cards in four drawers, and every one of them has got a person who put it there written on it in a shorthand, and the reason anybody can find anything in here is that the finding is a card in a drawer and the card is not hidden and the card is not locked and the card is in a building that anybody can walk into, and **nobody has ever asked her to think about that, and he did not think about it either until about six weeks later, and by then it was in a book in an inside pocket on a chair and it had cost somebody a name.**

The name at the bottom of the slip was blank.

There is a card in a drawer in Lattice Ward that says *Marrow Falls, emergency protocol, working copy, four volumes* and the date on it is a Wednesday and there is a hand on it that is not a hand anybody can be asked about, and it is not a lock.

*157. Monday of week 48, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a woman of forty-one who works three days a week and has said a rule out loud in eleven seconds and has said the other thing twice on purpose.*

*What the room is.* **Open to anybody who walks in and fills in a slip. Four things on a slip: what you want, from when, to when, and who is asking. No fee, no membership, a counter with a bell on it, four drawers under the counter with about four hundred cards in them in alphabetical order by the word on the top line, and a trolley against the far wall with a high shelf. Each card has a box number, a date, a place, a hand in a shorthand I cannot read, and a shelf.** She is forty-one, works Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays, thirty-one years in the building and nine in the room, and she has never once been asked to make a decision and has never once had to be asked, because the room decides by the counter and the bell.

*Her rule, and it is a rule.* A box does not come down without a slip, and a slip is a piece of paper and a person. She has not had it in writing and does not need it in writing, and she said the whole of it inside eleven seconds and then told me that if I thought it was a mood it was a rule, and that it was a rule before she came, and that she would be surprised to find a box in that room that came down without one. I said I was not going to argue with it and she said nobody does, and that is not a compliment, it is a Tuesday.

*And the thing she said twice, deliberately, watching my face.* Nothing is sealed. There is no lock in that room and there has never been a lock in that room and there is a sign about eleven feet from the door that says so and she has never once seen anybody read the sign. What there is, is a column in a register in another building, **and a column is not a lock, and a column can be read by anybody who can get into the building it is in, and that building has got a counter on the ground floor and the register is on a table by the counter and anybody can walk in and look at it.** She said the reason nobody does it is that nobody has been in a hurry, and that a hand on a register looks like a person to ask, and there is not one. **That is the second question of this volume in one paragraph from a woman with a bell, and the person in this city with authority over a building's own records does not exist, and the reason does not exist is that the records are a column in a register in a public building and the public building has a counter.**

*What came out, and what it actually was.* **Not a box. A binder. In the week of week thirty-eighth, fourteen years ago, it went down a corridor to a room in another building with a register, and the register has got a line on it with a date and a hand and a place on it, and if you want the binder you do not want a box from me, you want a line in a register in Crown Terrace.** There are about four hundred boxes in that room and about four hundred cards, one per box, and I have never once heard a person ask for the Marrow Falls material and get told there is no box, and she said that, and then she told me to slow down.

*The fourth line on the slip, and what she would not let me do quietly.* A slip with a person on it is a request and a slip without one is a piece of paper, and she has not got a tray for pieces of paper, and that is not her being difficult, that is nine years of a room with a counter and a bell. She has had this conversation three times, with a librarian in Crown Terrace, with a man from a trade paper and with a woman who wanted a bundle and did not know what a bundle was, and nobody has ever asked her what happens. I said there is no name in this city that ought to be on that slip and that I am the name that would go on it and that the correct answer is my own name, and that I have spent a term and a half not doing that. She said then do not do it. **And then she said one thing, once, and would not say it twice, and it is that I am the first person in about four years who has told her what is actually at the end of it, because they all want a date and I told her a corridor and a counter and a woman of thirty-eight, and she would like it noticed that that is a thing about me and not about her.**

The book is at forty-four lines and the Exchange is on Wednesday and does not move before it. A name is in the box in pencil and it cannot come out, nine hundred doors are the wrong number, and there is a card in a drawer in this city with a Wednesday on it and a hand on it that nobody can be asked about, and the hand is not a lock.
