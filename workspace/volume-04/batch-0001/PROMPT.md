# Batch 0001 — Volume 04, Chapters 151–160 ("One Box")

**Movement I of Volume 04, and the volume's first batch. Write Chapters 151 through 160 as complete finished scenes, in order. Ten chapters on ten days across the four weeks from week forty-seven to week fifty, and the load-book run continues at 154 and ends at 163.**

**The volume's job, in one sentence: a document in this city can now say *who ended it*, in pencil, in a clerk's hand, on one page on a counter, and it is empty — and the same city has about four hundred people who have been handed a page with their own date on it and no person at the top of it, and this movement asks what the difference is and who is supposed to be it, given that the person who drew the box was told twice that there was no box for it.**

Then this batch must deliver, in this order: **the box takes its first name and the name is written by somebody who is not the person it is for; a woman of forty-six with a trolley finds out that the number on a door came off a list in a company and that the room has never had its own number on it; the Friday rota and the standing problem said a fourth time and not resolved, and the word's consequence changing form again; the old network archives open, and a registrar with a rule, and the discovery that nothing in them is locked and the seal is a column in a register in another building; a man of fifty-eight in a corridor who answers three questions with three smaller questions, gives a count, and does not agree to anything; the Exchange on the Wednesday of week forty-eight, opening and not opening the book, and a care worker of thirty-eight who will not sign her own words; the choice, made in a corridor, out loud, by him, with a woman's name on a slip and the slip torn up; the seventh session of the levy series and a woman of twenty-four who types things and eleven letters she has not answered; a printer of fifty-eight, a boy of nineteen, and a hundred and forty-one pounds that goes back to the person who paid it; and the close, which is fourteen lines in pencil on the back of a condition sheet and about eleven people a week.**

## Load book

**The run to date is continuous: 50 (Ch 1) … 153 (Ch 150), no gap and no duplicate. Chapter 151's entry is 154 and the run must stay continuous.** **Put at least six of the ten entries on somebody who is not Marek Senn and let the two about him be the choice and the close.** Vary the headings and no two entries in a batch may end on the same heading. **An entry must add or reframe and must never re-argue its own chapter's dialogue.** The house measurement is the eight-word overlap between an entry and its own chapter; **Volume 03's finished batch ran 38 to 284 and the ten entries of this batch run zero, and a reviewer should check that figure because zero is outside the family.**

**The *about N* hedge, measured, and hold or beat the figure you inherit.** The finished Volume 03 batch was **306 raw instances of *about* across 29,868 words, 10.25 per thousand, and 203 true hedges at 6.80 by the rule printed in `batch-0005/SUMMARY.md`**, and that batch's own true-hedge figure did **not** beat the one it inherited, and said so. **Hedge the quantities that are genuinely uncertain and do not hedge the ones the scene has already fixed** — a character who has just looked at a clock says ten past five, an age is an age, and a duration the scene has measured is not approximate. **The volume's canonical quantities keep every hedge, because nobody in the story knows them either, and the four-year horizon is the volume's inherited repetition and is neither to be cut further nor to be increased.** The three passes to use: clock readings and ages lose the hedge totally; a phrase repeated inside the batch keeps a fixed number of hedges, prose before load book and earliest first, so a load book can never out-hedge the scene it restates; and the canonical quantities keep theirs.

**The counting rule, restated so a later writer can reproduce it mechanically, and the word lists are part of the rule rather than an illustration of it.** Count one hedge for each occurrence of *about*, matched as a whole word and **case-insensitively**, immediately followed by an optional article (*a*, *an*, *the*) and then **either** (A) **a number word, alone or in a chain of number words joined by a hyphen or a single space** — *one two three four five six seven eight nine ten eleven twelve thirteen fourteen fifteen sixteen seventeen eighteen nineteen twenty thirty forty fifty sixty seventy eighty ninety hundred thousand million* — **or** (B) **one counted quantity** — *half dozen couple fortnight fortnights second seconds minute minutes hour hours day days week weeks month months year years room rooms form forms card cards word words line lines page pages field fields step steps part parts premise premises fold folds pound pounds penny pence job jobs hand hands name names face faces sheet sheets chair chairs seat seats item items entry entries copy copies place places building buildings door doors column columns packet packets envelope envelopes invoice invoices time times people premises men women woman document documents share shares case cases printer printers quarter size thing things*. **A possessive form is excluded, because a possessive duration is a clock reading.** Count each occurrence once. Search the whole file, load book included. A bare number word is sufficient under (A): *about nine* is a hedge and *about nine places* is one hedge, not two.

**Bold emphasis has a ceiling.** **Hold the batch average at or under 10.75 bold spans per thousand and hold every single chapter at or under 12.4.** The house convention is that a bold span marks the beat inside a person's speech; where one line carries several spans, the elaboration after the speech tag loses the emphasis and the lead-in keeps it, and **in a load-book line the first and the last span always survive so that no load book loses a bolded thesis clause.** Do not use a mechanical longest-span rule across a paragraph. **Count bold markers as well as spans and diff the finished files against a pre-edit copy.**

**Every line must carry an even number of `**` and an even number of `"`, and diff the finished files against a pre-edit copy.** Then sweep: **no duplicated paragraph at an eighty-character threshold, no `* * *` marker anywhere, no month-name, month-date, day-date, year or day number anywhere, and no eighty-character overlap with an earlier chapter's wording for a sentence you are remembering on purpose.** Count both figures with the stricter matchers: the strict bold matcher is `\*\*(?!\s)(.+?)(?<!\s)\*\*` and the strict *about* is case-insensitive, **and every figure you publish must come from those two.** The obvious bold regex cannot see a span containing a single-asterisk italic, and a lowercase-literal *about* cannot see a capitalised *About* opening a load-book label, **and the loose matcher only ever under-reports, and under-reporting is the direction that hides a ceiling.**
