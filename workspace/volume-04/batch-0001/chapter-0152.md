# Chapter 152 — Forty-One Lines On A Sheet

The condition walk goes round four buildings on a Thursday morning and it has gone round them every Thursday for nineteen years, and the sheet it produces has forty-one lines on it, and forty of those lines are a room number and a tick and a date, and the forty-first line is a space for conditions where there are no conditions.

Nobody has ever put anything in the forty-first line.

---

Marek Senn had a docket in his hand on the Thursday morning of week forty-seven and a room on the second floor of the second of the four buildings that he was not going to get into, and the woman with the trolley was on the first landing going the other way.

She is forty-six and she has a cardigan with pens in the pocket and a clipboard that is not a clipboard any more and a trolley with a bucket in it and four cloths over the side.

"**You are not on the round,**" she said.

"No."

"Then you cannot come up."

"**I know I cannot. I have been told there is no visitor column.**"

She looked at the sheet on the board under her arm.

"**There is no visitor column,**" she said, "**and that is not mine, and I have never once broken it, and I have been asked that by two men in nineteen years and both of them were annoyed.**"

---

She read him the sheet instead, and that cost her nine minutes of a morning she does not have, and she read it standing on a landing with the trolley against the wall.

Eleven weeks of the second floor of the second building, and the room on the end of the corridor is 2-14 on the sheet, and the plate on the door says 2-14, and she has signed it eleven times.

"You are going to ask me something," she said.

"I am."

"Then ask it on the landing, because I have got a bucket."

---

"What does the column say on the sheet. The one with the numbers in it."

"Room, condition, date."

"And where do the numbers come from."

"Off the list."

"And what is the list."

"It is the list." She shifted the trolley. "It comes down on a Friday with the other paper and it is a printout and it is four pages and it has got four buildings on it and every room that has got a plate on it. I fill the column in from the list and I walk the rooms and I look at the plates and I write the conditions. That is the round. I have done it since I was twenty-seven."

"And the plates."

"I clean them," said Ines Kolar, "**because the round is a cleaning round and the sheet comes off the cleaning round, and I have cleaned about four hundred of them in nineteen years and I have never once been asked what a plate is for.**"

---

He got a pen out and wrote eleven lines of 2-14 down the margin of the docket.

She watched him do it, and at the ninth line she said: "**That one is wrong.**"

"They are all the same."

"**That one is 2-11 and it has been 2-11 since the summer and you have written two fourteen because the one before it was two fourteen,**" said Ines Kolar. "**You are not bad at numbers. You are bad at nine of the same thing in a row.**"

"How did you know I had got it wrong."

"**Because I have been putting that sheet in a ring file for nineteen years and I have never had to look at a plate to know what it says,**" she said, "**and you are the first person in nineteen years who has wanted to write them down, and I am telling you that about myself and not as a criticism of you, and I would like it noticed that I said it.**"

---

He asked her the question after that, and he asked it well, which was the mistake.

"**What happens if the plate is wrong.**"

"Nothing."

"Who checks."

"**The sheet checks it.**"

"The sheet is made of the list."

Ines Kolar looked at him for four seconds.

"Say that again in a different order," she said, "**because you have just asked me a question my sheet cannot answer, and I have been asked a lot of questions in nineteen years and I have never had one of those, and I am going to think about it for a week.**"

---

Then she told him about the plates, and it took four minutes, and it came out in the wrong order and she let it.

"There are two kinds."

"Two kinds."

"**A building plate is enamel and it has got a number on it and nothing else, and there is one of them in a hallway in four years and when it comes off nobody replaces it because it is not anybody's job.**" She put her hand on the trolley. "A service plate is a printed plate with a company on it in a line of type along the bottom edge the size of my thumb, and the type is theirs and not the building's, and the number on it is a service number, and the company that made it lists every room they have ever put a plate on, and the list is their list and not the building's list."

"Which one is on the door of that room."

"**That is a service plate,**" said Ines Kolar. "**It has been a service plate for as long as I have been cleaning it. It is not a building plate and it never was. I have known that for nineteen years and I have never once said it, and I have never once been asked, and if I had been asked I would not have been sure, because I did not know there were two kinds until a man of fifty-five told me in a basement four years ago while he was doing something to a pipe.**"

---

He said: "So there is no building number on that door."

"**There is no building number on about nine hundred doors in four buildings,**" said Ines Kolar, "**and the room behind the door is 2-07 and it has been 2-07 since before I came, and the number on the door is 2-14 because somebody in a company in another part of this city printed it and put it up, and both of those are true about themselves and neither of them is the building's.**"

Somebody on the second floor opened a door and shut it again.

"And the leaflets," she said. "You did not ask me about the leaflets and I am going to tell you, because you have been standing on this landing for nine minutes and I have got a bucket. About four hundred of them are in a rack in that room and every one of them says 2-07, and they came out of a printer with the room in the address, and the room number on a leaflet is the room number on the plan and not the room number on the door, and nobody has ever asked me to reconcile the two, because reconciling the two is not a condition, and a condition is the only thing on that sheet."

---

She carried the bucket down.

"You are going to come on the round," she said, from four steps below.

"You said there was no visitor column."

"**There is no visitor column and you are not coming on the round,**" said Ines Kolar. "**I am going to walk four buildings with a trolley on Thursday mornings and you are going to walk four buildings with me on Thursday mornings, and on the sheet it will say one name because the sheet says one name, and if anybody asks you why you were in the second building you are going to say you were waiting for a door to open, and that will be true, and it will be a worse answer than the truth and it is the one that keeps you out of a file.**"

---

On the Thursday of the forty-eighth week she walked the wall instead of the list, and she did it for eleven minutes on the fourth building, and the fourth building is not in Bower Street.

The plate on the end of that corridor said 2-03 and the list said 2-09, and she read one off the wall and one off the list and then she put the clipboard down on a windowsill and said the thing she had been carrying since the Tuesday.

"**There is nowhere to put it,**" she said. "**I have been a person who has somewhere to put it for nineteen years and I have got nine of these and I have got a sheet with forty-one lines on it and one of the lines is a room number and one is a condition and the forty-first is a space for conditions where there are no conditions, and that space is two inches high and I have never once had a reason to use it.**"

"Then you have not got a condition."

"I have got a discrepancy," said Ines Kolar, "**and a discrepancy is not a condition, and a condition is the only thing this sheet takes, and I have been trying to make a condition out of a discrepancy for eleven minutes and it will not go in, because the moment it goes in there somebody is going to sign the bottom of that page and the signature means that the room is as the list says it is and it is not.**"

"So what is the answer."

"**There is no answer,**" she said. "**I have run out of places to put it and that is the first time in nineteen years and I have been doing this job since I was twenty-seven and I have never run out before, and I have been thinking about it since Tuesday and I have not slept properly, and I am forty-six.**"

---

He wrote 2-03 on a docket and she watched him do it and did not tell him he had it wrong.

Then she picked the clipboard up, and did the round, and finished at eleven, and the sheet went into a ring file in a cupboard on a ground floor in another building, in a building where nine hundred plates are the wrong number and nobody has ever walked the wall.

There is no form in this city that takes a discrepancy.

---

*155. Thursday of week 47, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a woman of forty-six with a trolley and a bucket and four cloths over the side, and forty-one lines on a sheet of which one is a space for conditions where there are no conditions.*

*What the round is.* **Four buildings, a trolley, a bucket, four cloths, a printout of four pages that comes down on a Friday with the other paper, and a condition sheet with forty-one lines on it: a room number, a condition, a date, forty of them, and the forty-first line is a space two inches high for conditions where there are no conditions, and nobody has ever put anything in it, in nineteen years, and I do not know who wrote the line and I have asked twice and been told it is a standard sheet.** She has been doing it since she was twenty-seven. She cleans about four hundred plates on that round and has never once been asked what a plate is for, and has never once been asked by a man.

*Two kinds of plate, and she is the only person in this city who knows there are two.* **A building plate is enamel, a number and nothing else, one in a hallway in four years, and nobody replaces it because it is not anybody's job. A service plate is printed, with the company's name in a line of type along the bottom edge the size of her thumb, and the number on it is a service number, and the company lists every room they have ever put a plate on, and their list is their list and not the building's.** She knew four years ago, told by a man of fifty-five in a basement while he was doing something to a pipe, and has never said it to anybody because nobody has ever asked. **The plate on the end of that corridor in Bower Street is a service plate and has been for as long as she has been cleaning it, and the room behind it is 2-07 and has been since before she came, and the two of those are both true about themselves and neither of them is the building's, and about nine hundred doors in four buildings are the same.**

*What she gave me, and what it cost.* **She read me eleven weeks of the sheet standing on a landing with the trolley against the wall, which is nine minutes of a Thursday morning she has not got, and I did not ask to be taught and she decided to teach.** I wrote them down in the margin of a docket and got the ninth one wrong and she caught it, and the way she caught it was that she has been putting that sheet in a ring file for nineteen years and has never had to look at a plate to know what it says, and I am the first person in nineteen years who wanted to write them down, and she said she was telling me that about herself and not as a criticism of me and would like it noticed that she said it. **And then she said the thing about the leaflets that I had not asked for, which is that about four hundred of them in the rack in that room all say 2-07, and they came out of a printer with the room in the address, and the room on a leaflet is the room on the plan and not the room on the door, and nobody has ever asked her to reconcile the two because reconciling is not a condition.**

*The first question her sheet could not answer, and what she did with it.* **I asked her what happens if the plate is wrong and she said nothing, and who checks and she said the sheet checks it, and the sheet is made of the list, and she made me say that in a different order and then said she was going to think about it for a week.** On the Thursday of the forty-eighth week she walked the wall instead of the list for eleven minutes on the fourth building, and there was a plate saying 2-03 where the list said 2-09, and she put the clipboard down on a windowsill and said there is nowhere to put it. **A discrepancy is not a condition and a condition is the only thing that sheet takes, and the moment it goes in there somebody signs the bottom of that page and the signature means the room is as the list says it is, and it is not.** She said she had been a person who had somewhere to put it for nineteen years and had run out for the first time, and that she has not slept properly about it since Tuesday, and that she is forty-six. **That is the whole of the movement so far in one sentence from a woman with a bucket, and I have not got a better one and I have been looking for a week.**

*And what she did about me.* **There is no visitor column and I am not coming on the round. She is going to walk four buildings on Thursday mornings and I am going to walk four buildings with her on Thursday mornings, and on the sheet it will say one name because the sheet says one name, and if anybody asks me why I was in the second building I am to say I was waiting for a door to open, and she said that would be true and would be a worse answer than the truth and is the one that keeps me out of a file.** Entry 155. The book is at forty-four lines and does not move before the Wednesday of the forty-eighth. A name is in the box in pencil and it cannot come out, and nine hundred doors in four buildings are the wrong number, and the wrong number is a company printing four hundred plates a year out of a list, and about forty-one people in one district are on a thing that cannot be ended, and the only route out of that corridor is a counter with about two hundred pages on it.**
