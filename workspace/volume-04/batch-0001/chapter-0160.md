# Chapter 160 — The Back Of The Sheet

The condition walk went round four buildings on the Thursday morning of week fifty with a trolley and a bucket and four cloths over the side, and the sheet it produced had forty-one lines on it, and the forty-first line had something in it for the first time in nineteen years.

It was in pencil. It was on the back of the sheet, upside down relative to the front, in the space where the conditions go, and it was fourteen lines long, and it was not a condition.

---

Marek Senn walked with her because he had walked with her for four Thursdays, and there is no visitor column, and the sheet says one name because the sheet says one name.

She did the round. She wrote the conditions, which were four damp ceilings and a loose handrail and a light that had been out since the summer. She signed the bottom of the page.

And then she turned the sheet over and wrote the fourteen lines on the back in pencil, in the space where the conditions go, while he held the trolley.

"**That is not a condition,**" he said.

"No."

"It is on the sheet."

"**It is on the back of the sheet, and the back of the sheet goes in the same ring file as the front, and the ring file goes in a cupboard on a ground floor in a building in another part of this city, and there are about nine hundred sheets in that ring file going back nineteen years,**" said Ines Kolar, "**and there is no column for what I have written and there is no box on it and there is no form anywhere in this city that takes a discrepancy, and I have been a person who has somewhere to put a thing for nineteen years and the somewhere is the back of a sheet.**"

---

Fourteen is not a round number and she had counted them herself.

Nine of them were in the building in Bower Street, and one of the nine was the room everybody had been talking about, and the plate on that door said 2-14 and the plan said 2-07 and both of those were correct about themselves and one of them was on a door.

Three were in the second building. Two were in a building in Saltmarket where a company had put plates on rooms nobody had ever asked it to plate, in a stairwell, in the year the stairwell was new.

"**Two of those are not mistakes,**" she said. "**Those two are a company doing exactly what it was told, and I have put them on the sheet anyway, because a condition is a thing and this is a thing and if I do not write it down then in about four years there will be one number left in my head and I will be sixty-two and I will have moved house.**"

"**You have not moved house.**"

"**I have moved house twice and one of them was to a smaller flat, and that is not a threat, it is a fact about how I would remember things.**"

---

She did the last building and it was the one with the plan in the basement, and the fourth building is not in Bower Street, and there is a set of plans in a drawer on a hook in a basement in it that nobody has stood in front of since the spring.

They did not go down. Marek Senn did not ask, and the reason he did not ask is in his own book and he wrote it down on the Monday: **you do not get to be the man who goes into the basement. Nobody in this case has earned a basement and the case is about records and a basement is the room where the records are, and if I am the first person through that door then I am the man who went through a door, and about eleven people a week are being taught to wait for that.**

Ines Kolar stood at the top of the stairs with the trolley and looked down them for about four seconds.

"**There is a drawer on a hook down there,**" she said. "**I have known that since before I came, from a man of fifty-five who was doing something to a pipe. I have never been down and I am not going today, and I am telling you that I am not going today so that it is in your book as a thing I said and not a thing you found out.**"

---

The sheet went into the ring file and the ring file went into the cupboard, and the cupboard is on a ground floor in another part of the city and nobody asked for it and nobody is going to.

Then she said the thing that is the price.

"**You know what happens to that pencil,**" she said. "**In about four weeks a woman of thirty-six is going to come into that office and do the sheets up, because that is her job and she has done it for about nine years and she does not know me and I do not know her. And she is going to pick up that sheet, and she is going to turn it over, because everybody turns it over, and she is going to see fourteen lines in pencil in the space where the conditions go, and she is going to bring it to me and ask me what the pencil is.**"

"What will you say."

"**I am going to say that there was a discrepancy and that I wrote it down in pencil on my own sheet and that it has not been actioned,**" said Ines Kolar, "**and that is true and it is nine words and it does not help her, because she is going to have to decide what to do with fourteen lines that a woman of forty-six wrote on the back of a sheet instead of in a column, and there is no column, and she will do whatever she does and I will not know.**"

"**And then what happens when somebody asks you about it.**"

"**Then I am going to have to say that a man of twenty-two asked me a question in a corridor in the week of forty-seventh that my sheet could not answer,**" said Ines Kolar. "**And that is your name, and it is the only place your name is going to be in this city as a result of anything I have done, and you did not ask me to write anything down and I want you to know that I worked that out on the Monday and not tonight.**"

---

He said the thing he had been carrying for a week, out loud, on a landing, to a woman with a trolley.

"Four weeks," he said. "**Four weeks since the box went on the counter and since the plate turned out to be a company's and since a woman of thirty-four told me a column has not got a person in it, and the whole of what has changed in this city in four weeks is a sheet with pencil on the back of it, and about eleven people a week are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and I am still that man, and I did that on purpose in a room with nine people in it in the Monday of week forty-two.**"

"Yes," said Ines Kolar.

"**I am not asking you to make it into more than it is.**"

"**I am not going to, and I am not going to pretend either,**" she said. "**Here is what it is worth, and I have had four weeks to work out what it is worth, and it is worth this. Two weeks ago a man in this city could stand in that corridor and say the door and the wall do not agree, and about four people in four buildings would have told him he was talking nonsense. And in about four weeks there is a piece of paper in a ring file in a cupboard on a ground floor that says fourteen times, in a hand, that the door and the wall do not agree. And it is not a form and it is not a column and it is not signed as anything and nobody has asked for it and nobody is going to.**"

She shifted the trolley.

"**That is worth about a fortnight. In about a year it will be worth nothing, because the pencil will come off, and then the only thing anybody has got is a woman of forty-six who says it on a Thursday.**"

---

At twenty past eleven he was in the repair shop in Lattice Ward with the shutter up and the lamp on over the bench, and there were five things in the drawer under it, four of them other people's sentences, and a letter in it that says in its own last line that it should not be produced without that office being asked first, and there is no proceeding.

There was a jacket over the back of the chair by the bench with a corner of card in the inside pocket of it, about two inches by three, and it has been in a pocket in a room since before the term turned over, and nobody has ever held out a hand for it.

On a counter four miles away there is a form with a box in pencil on it, and a woman's name in the box, and there is no reference number on it and nothing was typed and it cannot come out.

About forty-one people in a district are on a thing that cannot be ended, and a plate on a door is a company printing about nine hundred of them a year out of a list, and a set of plans is in a drawer on a hook in a basement that nobody has stood in front of since the spring.

Six lines are in a register in Crown Terrace with a Wednesday on them and a hand on them, and the fourth column of that register has not got a person's name in it in fourteen years, and a woman of thirty-four at a table there has not been asked what she thinks of that, and has said that if he comes back she would like to be.

And on the Thursday of next week a woman of thirty-eight who works nights is going to stand in that corridor with him while he asks it, and the answer will not be about him.

There is no post and there is no office whose business it is to look at a stranger's wrist, and there is not going to be one, and about eleven people a week are still waiting for a man in a lanyard that four weeks of work has not made unnecessary.

Nobody has said so.

*163. Thursday of week 50, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is the back of a sheet, and fourteen, and a woman of forty-six telling me what the pencil is going to cost, and a corridor on Thursday of next week that I am not going to be able to be quiet in.*

*What changed in four weeks, in one sentence.* **The forty-first line of a condition sheet has something in it for the first time in nineteen years, and it is fourteen lines long, and it is in pencil, and it is on the back of the sheet upside down relative to the front, in the space where the conditions go, and it is not a condition.** Nine of the fourteen are in the building in Bower Street and one of the nine is the room everybody has been talking about, and the plate on that door says 2-14 and the plan says 2-07 and both are correct about themselves and one of them is on a door. There is no column for it, no box on it and no form anywhere in this city that takes a discrepancy, and she has been a person who has somewhere to put a thing for nineteen years and the somewhere is the back of a sheet.** Four weeks ago the somewhere was nowhere and she told me that in a corridor and said she had not slept since the Tuesday.

*The basement, and why I did not go down.* **The fourth building has a set of plans in a drawer on a hook in a basement, and a man of fifty-five told her about it before she came, and she stood at the top of the stairs and looked down for about four seconds and said she had never been down and was not going today, and asked me to write it down as a thing she said and not a thing I found out.** I did not ask to go down and the reason is in the book: **nobody in this case has earned a basement, and the case is about records, and a basement is the room where the records are, and if I am the first person through that door then I am the man who went through a door, and about eleven people a week are being taught to wait for that.** That is the best sentence I have got this term and it is in my own hand and it is not clever.

*The price of the pencil, said out loud, on a landing, before I asked.* **In about four weeks a woman of thirty-six is going to come into that office and do the sheets up, because that is her job and she has done it for about nine years and she does not know her and she does not know me. She is going to turn the sheet over, because everybody turns it over, and she is going to see fourteen lines in pencil in the space where the conditions go, and she is going to ask what the pencil is.** And what she will say is that there was a discrepancy and that she wrote it in pencil on her own sheet and that it has not been actioned, which is true and is nine words and does not help, **because the woman of thirty-six is going to have to decide what to do with fourteen lines that a woman of forty-six wrote on the back of a sheet instead of in a column, and there is no column, and she will do whatever she does and I will not know.** And then she told me the other half without being asked: **when somebody asks her about the pencil she is going to have to say that a man of twenty-two asked her a question in a corridor in the week of the forty-seventh that her sheet could not answer, and that is my name, and it is the only place my name is going to be in this city as a result of anything she has done, and she worked that out on the Monday and not tonight.**

*What it is worth, and she said it and I had not asked.* **Two weeks ago a man could stand in that corridor and say the door and the wall do not agree and about four people in four buildings would have told him he was talking nonsense. In about four weeks there is a piece of paper in a ring file in a cupboard on a ground floor that says fourteen times, in a hand, that the door and the wall do not agree, and it is not a form and it is not a column and it is not signed as anything and nobody has asked for it and nobody is going to. That is worth about a fortnight. In about a year it will be worth nothing, because the pencil will come off, and then the only thing anybody has got is a woman of forty-six who says it on a Thursday.** I have written both halves of that down and I have not improved either of them and I am not going to.

*And the part of it that is the price, said on a landing to a woman with a trolley.* **Four weeks since the box went on the counter and since the plate turned out to be a company's and since a woman of thirty-four told me a column has not got a person in it, and the whole of what has changed in this city in four weeks is a sheet with pencil on the back of it, and about eleven people a week are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and I am still that man, and I did that on purpose in a room with nine people in it.** She said yes. I said I am not asking her to make it into more than it is, and she said she is not going to and she is not going to pretend either, and then she gave me the fortnight and the year and I have got both of them. **Entry 163.** The book is at forty-four lines and it does not move before the Wednesday of the week of fifty-two. A name is in a box in pencil and it cannot come out, a piece of paper is in a bin by a bell, a hundred and forty-one pounds is in a coat, six lines are in a register with no person's name in the fourth column, and there are fourteen lines in pencil on the back of a sheet in a ring file in a cupboard, and about eleven people a week are still waiting for a man nobody can find, and on the Thursday of next week I am going to stand in a corridor in Crown Terrace with a woman of thirty-eight who works nights while I ask a question that is not about me, and she is going to be awake in the day for it, and that is the whole of what I have got.
