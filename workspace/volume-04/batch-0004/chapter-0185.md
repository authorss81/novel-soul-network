# Chapter 185 — The Man Who Was Not A Caller

There is a back room with the shutters up in it, and a tin in the middle of a table that has no lock on it, and a book lying in front of Bea Nunn with four columns in it and forty-four lines in it, and the book had not been opened since the Wednesday of week forty-eight.

There are four minutes an item. Nobody has a vote. Nobody in that room is anybody's employer and nobody in that room has ever been paid by anybody who wanted an item.

She said the rule before anybody had asked her for anything, in one go, the way she says it.

"**A book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call.** That is the rule, it is older than me, it was here before the tin, and it is not a mood and I am not going to be asked whether it is a mood. The book is forty-four lines. The fortieth was written three years ago by a man who rang me and said nine words and signed his own name under them. Line forty-four has a hole in it where a woman of sixty-eight in bay four should be, and she was never written down and she is not going to be."

---

There were eleven people in that room on the Wednesday of week sixty and three of them had telephoned her.

She had said the number out loud at half past nine, which is what she does.

"**Twenty-nine.** It was nine in the spring and it was twenty on the Wednesday of the fifty-sixth and it is twenty-nine, and I have said yes twenty-nine times, and I did not know what I was agreeing to for the first eleven and I know now and I am not going to pretend to have worked it out in a better mood tonight." She turned the tin a quarter turn. "**Three of you are in this room. I have not asked the other twenty-six why they have not come and I am not going to, because the only way I find out is by asking, and the second I ask then I am a woman who rings people to come to a room.**"

---

The man of sixty-ish had come at a quarter to eleven and he had asked for four minutes and had got them and had not used them, because he had not telephoned, and he said so in the first nine seconds.

"I have not rung you. I want to say that first because I have read the leaflet and I know what the book is for and I have not done the thing that makes me a caller, and I would like somebody to notice that I noticed."

"You are noticed," said Bea Nunn.

"Then say the rest of it. What is a person in this room if he has not rung."

"**He is a person in this room, and there is no name for him, and there is nothing on the front of this table that says he was here, and in about four weeks there will be nine people in this room who will tell you they were here on the Wednesday of week sixty and one of them will be telling the truth.**"

---

That is the whole of it and it took nine seconds and then he used his four minutes and what he said was about a thing he has been given.

"There is a card going round in three buildings where I do some work and it has four steps on it and a fifth line and the four steps are right," he said. "**And I have been telling people for about a fortnight that a thing can be safe because a lot of people have been given it, and I have said that in a corridor twice and in a canteen once, and I have never once said it into a machine, and I would like somebody to be able to tell me why that makes me different from a man who is being handed leaflets.**"

"Nobody can," said Bea Nunn.

"That is what I have."

---

"Then here is what I am going to say and it is the whole of my case and I have had a month to get it into one piece." She put her hand on the cover of the book. "**That sentence you have just said in a room is now in a room, and a room is the only container in this city that does not keep anything, and about two hundred people have come into a room off a street in Lattice Ward since the spring and left with nothing, and I have been the person standing at the counter when they left, and I have decided that that is the correct thing and I am not going to be talked out of it by a man who has just told me a true thing for four minutes.**"

"You have been talked into it," said somebody at the back.

"**I have been talked into it by twenty-nine telephone calls, and one of them was a man who wanted to be told that it was all right to feel frightened, and I gave him that at about eleven at night in the spring, and there is not one thing in this city that can find that out except me.**"

---

The rule came round a second time, and it came round because somebody asked, and the somebody was a woman of about thirty-four with a notebook who wanted to know what a caller was.

"Is it anybody who asks for four minutes."

"No," said Bea Nunn. "**It is somebody who rang this number, and that is the whole of it, and I have not got a rule for the other half and I am not going to invent one in a room at eleven at night with about eleven people in it, because the second that sounds like a rule somebody in about four years is going to be able to point at a rule I made up on a Wednesday because nine people were in a room and liked me.**"

"Then how does anyone get in."

"**They ring. And about four of them ring twice, and one of them rang in the spring because she was frightened and I have thought about that every week since.**"

---

Nothing was written down.

Nobody signed, the tin went back into the middle of the table with nothing in it, and the fifth chair was against the wall and a man of about sixty had been offered it twice and had taken it the second time and had not said why, and it is still against the wall.

At ten past midnight she said the last thing of the night to nobody, and she said it standing up, and the tin was on the table.

"**Forty-four lines, and the fortieth of them is three years old, and there is a hole in the forty-fourth, and in about four years this room is going to be a thing that happened. And when it is, nobody is going to be able to say what was in it, because the reason there is nothing in it is a rule that is one sentence long and older than the tin, and that same sentence is the only reason anybody in this city is allowed to say a thing in a room without it being used against them, and both of those are the same rule, and the day somebody takes it out for the other reason then this room was a service before it was a room, and it will have been a service for about four years and nobody will have noticed the day.**"

Nobody said anything and the shutters went up and the book did not open.

---

*188. Wednesday of week 60, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a book at forty-four lines that has now not been opened for four sittings, and twenty-nine telephone calls.*

*The room and the rule.* **A back room, shutters up, a tin with no lock, four columns and forty-four lines, four minutes an item, no vote for anybody, and the book shut since the Wednesday of week forty-eight, which is the fourth sitting running. The rule given in one go and not shortened: a book of what a caller said cannot be written by the people who did not call; it is older than her and it was there before the tin. The fortieth line is three years old and was written by a man who rang and said nine words and signed his own name. Line forty-four has a hole in it where a woman of sixty-eight in bay four should be and she is never going to be written down.**

*The number, said out loud.* **Nine in the spring, twenty on the Wednesday of the fifty-sixth, twenty-nine on this Wednesday. She has said yes twenty-nine times and says she did not know what she was agreeing to for the first eleven. Three of the twenty-nine are in the room. She has not asked the other twenty-six why they have not come and is not going to, because the only way to find out is by asking, and asking makes her a person who rings people to come to a room.**

*The item, and why it could not go in the book.* **A man of sixty-ish asked for four minutes and opened by saying that he had not telephoned, that he knew what the book is for, and that he wanted somebody to notice that he had noticed. He was told he was noticed and that there is nothing on the front of the table that will say he was here. His four minutes were about a card with four correct steps and a fifth line going round three buildings where he works, and about a sentence he has been saying for about a fortnight in two corridors and a canteen: a thing can be safe because a lot of people have been given it. He said he had never said it into a machine. He was told nobody can tell him why that makes him different from a man being handed leaflets.**

*The answer, and what it cost, in her words.* **That the sentence is now in a room and a room is the only container in this city that keeps nothing, and that about two hundred people have come into a room off a street in Lattice Ward since the spring and left with nothing, and that she is not going to be talked out of that by a man who has told her a true thing for four minutes. Then, to somebody who put it to her that she had been talked into it: that she was talked into it by twenty-nine telephone calls, one of which was in the spring from a woman who was frightened, and that there is nothing in this city that can find that out except her. The rule came round a second time when a woman of thirty-four asked what a caller is, and the other half of it was admitted in front of eleven people: that she has not got a rule for the other half and is not going to invent one at eleven at night, because the second it sounds like a rule somebody in about four years can point at a rule she made up on a Wednesday because nine people were in a room.**

*The close, said standing up, to nobody.* **That the emptiness of the book and the fact that a person may speak here without it being used against them are the same rule, one sentence, older than the tin, and that the day it is removed for the other reason this room was a service before it was a room and will have been one for about four years with nobody noticing the day.** Nobody signed, the tin went back empty, the fifth chair was taken twice by a man who was not a caller and offered no reason.
