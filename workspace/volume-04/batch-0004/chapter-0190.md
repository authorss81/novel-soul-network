# Chapter 190 — One At A Time

A man of twenty-two has counted exits before entering a room since he was old enough to be told not to, and on the Thursday morning of week sixty-one he counted three doorways in one building and one of them was in a different street, and all three of the things that happened to him that week had already happened.

He did not ask for any of the three. He was let into all three and he was in a corridor for one of them and he was in a kitchen for another and he was on a step for the third.

---

The first doorway was on the ground floor of a building in Bower Street and it stood open onto about two hundred sheets of district paper in four piles of fifty, each one held by an elastic band, and a woman of fifty-three was behind that counter and has been for nineteen years.

He said good morning because he had said it every Friday for a term and a half and he was not going to be the man who stopped.

"Thursday," said Petra Lindqvist. "You do not usually come on a Thursday."

"No."

"Then you have come on a Thursday." She put her hand flat on the second stack and left it there. "**I said a thing to a man in this building five weeks ago and I have had five weeks to find a better version of it and there is not one, so I am going to say it again in the same words because changing the words would be the other thing I have never done. A thing that was never in a system has nothing in it to correct. I am not going to invent a way to correct it. And you are asking the wrong woman and there is no right woman.**"

"I know."

"**Then you are here for the other half of it and there is nothing to have.**"

---

The second doorway was up a flight of stairs in the same building and it was shut, and the room behind it is not where the building says it is, and the key to it is on a ring in a flat because somebody who is dead gave it to somebody who is thirty-one.

She came down to the step with a diary closed under her arm and she did not open it.

"You have not asked me anything since Thursday and I have noticed that, because I have been waiting for it and I would rather say that than have you not ask."

"You have got a week left of it and then I can come in if I tell you."

"**You can come in if you tell me and I will say yes and I will not make you ask twice, and that is the arrangement, and it is the only arrangement in this building that is not on a form, and I have had a diary with nine lines in it since the Thursday of last week and I have thought about tearing them out about four times and I am not going to, because the second I tear them out they are gone and the first time anybody asks me about that week I have got nothing to read out.**"

"You could say it out loud instead."

"**To about four people, once, and then to whoever they tell, and I have watched a woman of thirty-four have her file put in a corridor in this city and I am not doing it for a week.**"

---

The third doorway was on a street in Nine Bridges at half past two in the afternoon and it was the door of a residential home and the man who came out of it was thirty-four and he had been on since a quarter past six in the morning and he had been awake since half three.

He had a work card in his hand and he gave it to the man with the van, because a man with a van is the only person anybody in Nine Bridges hands a piece of paper to, and neither of them said what was on it.

"You have got about four minutes before you have to be anywhere."

"I have got a floor at ten past four and four people on it and I am going to say it four times, and one of them is going to be the fifth step, and I have told them there is nothing coming and I have told them I am not coming either, and both of those are true, and the day somebody comes it will be wrong and I would like it to be about a fortnight away."

"You have got no reason why that is going to happen in a fortnight."

"**No. And I have not got a reason why it is going to happen in a fortnight and I have said the word fortnight in a corridor to two people in about a month and I have still not got a reason, and the not getting a reason is the only thing I have got, and you cannot write it down.**"

---

So: three things, one at a time, and they are not the same thing, and two of them cannot be undone.

**A share** can be ended by the person it belonged to. It was, on the Tuesday of week fifty-nine, in about forty words in biro on the back of a work card, and carried out of a building in a lift engineer's tool bag by a person who was not thanked and did not ask to be. **That one can be put back the same way it was taken out, by the same person, on his own, whenever he decides to, and nobody in this city can stop him and nobody in this city is going to ask him to.**

**A room** can be closed by the person who holds the key. It was, on the Thursday of week sixty, for a week, in a corridor, with the reason given before it was asked for. **That one can be put back the same way, by the same person, with the same key, and she has already said she will not make him ask twice.** The reason it is the only one of the three that can be undone is that it is the only one of the three where the person who shut it is still going to be there in about four years.

**A name**, in pencil, on the top page of the first of four stacks, on a counter in that same building, cannot be taken out by anybody. Not by the clerk who ruled the box in the week of forty-five, not by the woman of thirty-one who wrote it in the spring, not by the woman of thirty-eight it is for, and not by a district that picks those pages up on its own cycle and nobody knows when. **A thing that was never in a system cannot be corrected, because there is nothing in it to correct, and she gave it, and somebody wrote it, and both of those were the correct action, and there is not one person in this city who has done anything wrong.**

---

Which is the whole of it, and it took a term and a half and it is not a mechanism and there is nothing in this city that could do any of the three of them again.

The three people who put those three things there are a man of thirty-four who asked for one of them and was answered, a woman of thirty-one who has a key, and a woman of thirty-one who rang a counter in the spring and was answered by a stranger. **Nobody in this city has the authority to take a share off a person, or a name off a page, or a room off a person who has a key. What there is instead is three people, each of whom can do one of those things to one other person, and each of whom did it, and none of whom is a company and none of whom is a district, and two of the three cannot be taken back.**

That is the whole of what a person in this city can do with a rule, and there is not one item on a list of about nine thousand of them that says so, because nobody made the list.

---

Nobody stopped any of the three. That is not a finding about a failure of nerve and it is not a thing anybody can be blamed for. **It is that a share can only be ended by a person who is in it, and only a key can shut a room, and only the person who answered a question can answer it, and in each case the person who did it was the person who was standing there, and the reason nobody stopped them is that stopping them would have meant becoming the person who does it, and there is no such person in this city and there is not going to be one.**

---

He was at the bench at ten with the lamp on and the shutter down and forty-one jobs on the docket in a shorthand nobody else in this city can read, and the word had been at nine a minute since the afternoon and moved to ten somewhere around the time he put the kettle on.

Under the bench there is a drawer with five things in it. Four of them are sentences that other people wrote and one of them is a letter with a reference number on it whose own closing line asks not to be produced in any proceeding unless that office has been asked first, and no proceeding exists, and that drawer has never once held anything a person could produce in a room.

The chair by the bench has had a jacket over the back of it since before the term changed and nobody has asked about the jacket. Inside pocket: a corner of card, two inches by three, a photocopy of a photocopy, a fifth line in a hand that got smaller towards the end. It has not been out of a room in a term and a half and no hand has been held out for it and the decision about it is exactly where it was before the summer. The original, which is not the same object, is in the fourth pocket of the tool roll and its fifth line has never been filled in by anybody, including him, and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in.

---

In about a fortnight five things have happened in this city and none of them is a document.

A man of thirty-four ended a share of his own and had it carried out of a building by somebody else's hands, and neither of them was thanked. A woman of thirty-eight went into a building she had never been inside and was handed a sheet of paper that is true, and said out loud at that counter that it is not a correction, and went out to catch a bus. A woman of thirty-one turned a key on a room for a week and then told a man on a landing that she was not going to be the reason a frightened person comes to a counter with a bell on it. An officer of a fund read five jobs off a leaflet for ten Fridays and then did not read them an eleventh time, in a room, and gave the reason out loud. And a man of fifty-one said a sentence he does not believe into a microphone in a market in Saltmarket, having asked twice for it to be switched off, and nobody answered him, and there is not one word of that on anything.

Also: about eleven thousand premises pay a levy that bought a third line. About four hundred and thirty-two cards are in about nine places in one district and about four hundred of the newer ones have the name of four feet of shop on the back of them. A woman of forty-six has fourteen lines in pencil on the back of a condition sheet in a ring file in a cupboard that nobody has ever opened. A clerk of fifty-three ruled one box in pencil on one page out of about two hundred and told one man about it on a Thursday and it is not written down anywhere. A book in a back room is at forty-four lines with a hole in the forty-fourth and has now not been opened for four sittings, and a man of fifty-one is going to keep coming.

And a name in pencil on a page in a stack of about two hundred that nobody can take out, and a key on a ring, and forty words in biro on the back of a card in a wallet in a tool bag, and a sheet of paper in a handbag going to a shift in Nine Bridges.

About eleven people a week are still going to be taught to wait for a man in a lanyard, and what this city has instead is four feet of shop with a name over the door, a tray holding thirty-one slips in a room off a street, about four hundred cards in four drawers under a counter, a key on a ring, and a coat on a hook in the back of that shop. None of those is a rota and nobody has stood in a room and said so.

---

*193. Thursday of week 61, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is three operations that are not one thing: a share, a name, and a room, and the three of them done in a fortnight by the three people who put them there.*

*The three, as records and not as scenes.* **A floor share, four parties, running since the spring, terminated on the Tuesday of week fifty-nine by one of the parties. Medium: card stock, reverse side, biro, forty words, no signature. Route out: a fire door and a stairwell at ten past two. Carrier: a lift engineer of twenty-nine, whose own card carries sixteen Fridays in the same wallet. Neither party was thanked; the second did not request otherwise and said why in advance. A room, shut on the Thursday of week sixty for a week, in a corridor, with the reason given before it was asked for, by a woman of thirty-one with a key and a diary and no standing, of which nine lines are in the diary and the diary is a diary. A name, in pencil, under a fourth field on the top page of the first of four stacks on a counter, which the clerk who ruled the box, the woman who wrote it, the woman it is for and a district on its own cycle cannot take out.**

*The asymmetry, which is the finding.* **The share can be put back the same way, by the same person, on his own, and nobody can stop him and nobody is going to ask. The room can be put back the same way, by the same person, with the same key, and she has said she will not make him ask twice, and it is the only one of the three where the person who shut it will still be there in about four years. The name cannot be put back by anybody, and the reason is structural rather than moral: an object with no system behind it carries no record that could be amended, and the three acts that put it there are all still correct, which is the whole of what is obstructing the amendment.**

*Why nobody stopped any of them.* **Not a failure of nerve and nobody to blame. A share can only be ended by a person in it, only a key can shut a room, and only the person who answered a question can answer it, and in each case the person who did it was the person standing there, and stopping them would have meant becoming the person who does it, and there is no such person in this city and there is not going to be one. There is no item on any list of about nine thousand that says any of this, because nobody made the list.**

*What the movement's five documents are, and none of them is one.* **A work card in biro, a key and a diary, a sheet of paper with a name at the bottom of it in a handbag, a face-down leaflet, and a case with a microphone lead coiled in it. Nothing was settled by a form, a minute, a notice, a report, a committee or a leaflet, and there is one box in this manuscript and it cannot be emptied. The standing problem was not said in this movement and no rule about who may be asked was invented. The Exchange's book is at forty-four lines and has not been opened for four sittings. The master is in an inside pocket on a chair with the fifth line blank and nobody has held out a hand for it. Entry 193.**
