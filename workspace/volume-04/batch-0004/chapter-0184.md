# Chapter 184 — Two Ways To Hold A Line

The Friday of week fifty-nine had eleven jobs on it and nine of them in date, and the word was at nine a minute by half past seven and at ten by eleven, and nobody asked him where a release was going to come from, which is seven Fridays.

Four locations were on a docket of his own in his own shorthand and on nothing else. Three of them were still what they had been. The fourth was a cut in the wall inside a stair window, the width of two fingers, painted over, the same age as the frame, and a man of thirty-eight in a district jacket had said in a corridor in the week of the fifty-fifth that in about four years that groove would be under two more coats of paint.

It is under two more coats of paint. It was done on the Tuesday, by a man on a ladder, in half an hour, and nobody who did it knew, and a job sheet with four locations on it went into a district system and nobody read a job sheet.

---

The reading room in Lattice Ward is open to anybody who walks in off a street, and it was half past ten on a Friday, and the woman who ran it had counted the tray twice that week.

Thirty-one slips.

She said the number out loud, once to a man of twenty-two with a docket and once to a man of forty-four who had not been in the room before, and the second time she said it she said it slower, because the man of forty-four had not heard the first one.

---

"Thirty-one," said Corin Vasska. "I have been an officer of a fund for four years and I have never once seen a tray."

"There is not a tray in my job," said Wendla Ossick. "**There is a rule, and the rule is that a box does not come down without a slip, and a slip is a piece of paper and a person, and a slip with a person on it is a request and it goes in the tray, and a slip without a person on it is a piece of paper and there is no tray for that either.** And I have got thirty-one of them in there and not one of them is about a box, and about forty people have asked to see a binder in this room in nine years."

"So the tray is not the thing."

"The tray is the only instrument I have got and it is a drawer."

---

There were four other people in that room and that is what made it a thing that happened. A man of thirty-four who works nights, who had come in on his own time and told nobody. A man of thirty-eight in a district jacket with a folder under his arm. A woman of about sixty with a shopping bag. And a woman of thirty-one with a diary closed under her arm, who was in the building for a different reason and was not in the room and was in the corridor.

Nobody in that room knew that the two of them had had the same argument in a corridor off a service road on the Tuesday of week fifty-five, in front of nine men, and that neither of them had given way.

---

The man of forty-four put the leaflet down on the counter. It had a session title on it and a fee and five jobs on the front of it, two of which were a man of twenty-two, chosen by a system out of two job sheets in the week of the thirty-fourth.

"I am going to say a thing and I am going to be fair about it," said Corin Vasska, "because I have read the specification four hundred times and I have never once thought about how it is read, and I have thought about it for about a fortnight and I would like to know whether I am the only person who has."

"Go on."

"**A rule that can be changed is not a rule. It is a draft. And this specification has four sections and three lines and the third one is funded and the committee met once in the week of the thirty-seventh and it is not going to meet again, and about eleven thousand premises pay a levy for it, and there is not one word of it that anybody in this city is allowed to improve.**"

"That is right," said Wendla Ossick.

"**And it has been in that state for four years and about eleven people a week are still going to be taught to wait for a man, and not one of those eleven is going to be taught anything different, and the reason is not the words. The reason is the copy nobody opens.**"

---

"Then that is your method," said Wendla Ossick, "and I have got the other one, and they are not the same and I am not going to pretend to you that yours is worse than mine, because yours has worked and mine has worked, and I have got four reasons why mine works and one of them is me and one of them is my hours and I am not going to pretend those are the same thing as a levy."

"Tell me the four."

"No. Two of them are about a man of thirty-four on nights who has said no twice and I am not discussing him in a room."

"**Then the first three, and leave him out of it, and I will tell you where I think the argument actually is.**"

---

"The first three are this. A slip is a piece of paper and a person, and the person can be asked a question, and I have been asked four in about six weeks that were not about a box and I have answered all four and I have not got a rule for answering a question, and a rule that holds when thirty-one people want something it was not built for is still the same rule."

"That is a person with hours."

"It is a person with hours and about four hundred cards in four drawers and a bell on this counter that is a push and does not ring by itself."

"And the second."

"**The second is that a line in a specification cannot be asked anything, and I have come to think that that is the whole difference between your thing and mine, and I do not mean it as a compliment to mine.**"

---

"Go on," said Corin Vasska.

"You have got eleven thousand premises paying for a sentence that cannot be changed and that nobody has ever been able to ask a question about. And that sentence has been in that state for four years and nobody has broken it, and about eleven people a week are still going to be taught to wait for a man, and in about four years you are going to stand in a room and read that specification out again and it will be exactly the same and the eleven will be exactly the same."

"That is fair."

"**And I have got four hundred cards in four drawers and a slip, and I have had a tray with eleven in it in the week of the fifty-three and thirty-one now, and a woman of forty-three came in here and asked me whether this is a place a person goes when they are frightened and I said no, and she went, and the thing I have actually achieved is that about two hundred people have come through that door since the spring and I do not know what any of them took out of it.**"

---

The man of forty-four put both hands flat on the counter.

"Then here is where I think the argument is and neither of them is going to win it. **Yours is a rule that is true on a Tuesday in a building with nobody in it. Mine is a rule that is true on a Tuesday in a building with nobody in it and it is a different rule, and there are about eleven thousand of mine and one of yours, and the only thing they have in common is that two people are standing in the room.**"

"That is not an answer."

"It is not an answer and it is the whole of what I have got and I have had a fortnight."

---

The man of thirty-four on nights had been standing at the counter for the whole of it with his hands in his pockets, and he said one thing, and he said it to the two of them and not to the man with the docket.

"**I have got two people telling me about method in two rooms in about a fortnight and neither of them is wrong and I am the one who has to say something on a floor at ten past four in the morning, and I have asked twice now for one of them to be wrong and I am not going to ask a third time, because I have worked out that neither of them is going to and the asking is the part I would like back.**"

Nobody offered him anything.

The man of thirty-four on nights went out and said nothing at all on the way down the stairs, because he had used one of his two sentences on the Monday and the other on the Tuesday and he is allowed each of them once a week, and Friday is in the same week as both of those, and about four years from now there is going to be a person on that floor who has never heard either of them.

---

The man of twenty-two with the docket did not say anything in that room at all.

There is a condition on his share and there is a microphone in a room in Crown Terrace on a Friday with a stand on it and there is no mechanism in this city that can tell the difference between a man being stopped from doing a thing and a man being asked about it, and nobody in the reading room on Lattice Ward that morning was going to ask him either thing, and he has said it in a room twice and he is not going to say it a third time in a better sentence because there is not one and he told the room that on the Friday of week fifty-six.

Seven Fridays on a rota, eleven jobs, nine in date, four locations on a docket of his own, and the count has not moved since the term turned over and nobody in this city has asked him for it.

---

At half past four that afternoon the woman of thirty-one was in a corridor of that building with the diary closed under her arm, and she said to him: "**You were in that room for about nine minutes and there were two people in it who have got a version of this that is working and neither of them has got a version that works in a room on a Tuesday with nobody in it, and neither of them said the word, and I have been in this building nine years and I have never seen two people not say the word in my room.**"

"I noticed."

"**And I have a diary and I did not write it down, and I am not going to, and that is not me being disciplined. That is me finding out what I am when I do not write things down.**"

---

The groove under the stair window was under two more coats of paint, and a woman of forty-six had put fourteen lines in pencil on the back of a condition sheet in the week of the fifty, and a woman of thirty-six who does the sheets up turned every sheet over because everybody does, and in about a fortnight she is going to ask what the pencil is, and a man of twenty-two is going to have to be told about it by somebody who is not him.

None of that is a repair and none of it is anybody's fault and one sheet of paper in a ring file in a cupboard is the whole of the record and nobody has ever opened that cupboard.

*The residue, carried by a man of thirty-four on nights.* **He asked for one of them to be wrong, for the second time in a fortnight, said he is not going to ask a third time, and gave the reason: the asking is the part he would like back. Nobody offered him anything, and he went down the stairs with nothing, having used one sentence on the Monday of that week and the other on the Tuesday and being allowed each of them once a week. This is the first Friday in four terms on which he has had nothing to say and he said so to nobody.**

*What was not said and what was not settled.* **The standing problem was not said and was not asked about, and the count in this volume remains seven, and no rule about who may be asked was invented. Nothing was minuted, nobody filled in a fifth field, and the woman of thirty-one said in a corridor that she did not write the morning down and is not going to, and gave the reason, which is that she is finding out what she is when she does not write things down. Entry 187.**
