# Chapter 189 — Nobody Answered The Question

There is a market on a Wednesday in Saltmarket that runs from eight in the morning until about two in the afternoon, and a trestle table goes up at the end of the middle aisle on Wednesdays, and on the trestle there is a leaflet, a name at the bottom of it, a small amplifier, and a microphone on a stand with a lead in it.

About forty people go past it in an hour. That is the whole of its audience and it has been there for about four years and it is a company that puts it there and nobody is on the trestle as a post.

A man of twenty-two had carried the trestle in at half past seven because he has a van on a Friday and nobody had asked him to on the Wednesday, and he was stood at the end of it for two and a half hours with his hands behind his back and he was not a speaker and was not introduced as one.

---

Peter Amado is about fifty-one. He has said a true sentence into a microphone six times, in a market and in a room, and he has been paid a hundred and forty pounds and travel for it, and he has a wife and a heating bill, and nobody in this city has ever asked him whether he wanted to say it.

He came at half past ten and he had a card in his hand and he did not put it down.

"I have got one item and it is four minutes and I have asked for the microphone to be switched off for it, and I have asked twice before, and I am going to ask a third time, and I would like the answer to be a yes or a no this time rather than nothing."

The woman at the trestle is about thirty-five and she is the day's public face and she has a list of times on a card and she said: "You are on at eleven."

"Right."

"It has not been done the other two times either, and not by me."

---

Nobody switched it off.

At eleven he put his hand on the stand the way a man does when he is about to speak into something, and it was still on its stand with its lead in it, and the small amplifier was making the faint noise it makes when nothing is going into it, and about eleven people had stopped in the aisle, which is more than usually.

He said the sentence he was being paid for.

"**A practice in this city belongs to nobody.**"

And then he said the other one, and he said it fast, and the woman at the trestle did not stop him and there was nobody with a stop in reach.

"**And I am the man being paid to say that, and I said a true thing six times in this city and somebody paid me for each of them, and nobody has ever asked me where I got it, and I have never been asked, and I am not going to be asked now, and I would like it noticed that a man of fifty-one has just said a sentence he does not believe into a microphone that he asked twice to have switched off and that nobody in this market has answered the question about whether it was off.**"

---

Nobody answered it.

A man of about sixty said afterwards that he had been standing about four feet away and could not tell, and a woman of about thirty said the same, and the woman at the trestle said that the switch is on the amplifier and not on the stand and that she had not known it was there until a fortnight ago, and that she has asked once herself and been given nothing.

"So that is four times."

"That is four times, counting mine," she said, "**and I am going to be honest with you, because you have said the price out loud and I have got a list of times on a card and no rule at all. I have not switched it off because the last two times somebody else did, and I did not do it, and I have not worked out yet whether that makes me the one who left it on.**"

---

A man of forty-four was at the other end of the trestle with a stack of leaflets on it, because his fund's assurance is the thing a leaflet in about nine hundred rooms cites as proof, and he had come to look at that and had not expected any of the rest of it.

He said it to a man of twenty-two in about nine seconds, out loud, on the far side of the trestle from the man of fifty-one, in the noise of a market.

"**He has asked twice and both times the asking was in front of people. The first time somebody switched it off and nobody wrote that down. So the only thing anybody can prove about that microphone is that a man asked for it to be switched off. The asking is the part that lasts. In about four years the only sentence anybody can quote of this morning is a man of fifty-one asking for a microphone to be switched off in a market, and he cannot take it back, and neither can the woman at the trestle, and I have just watched a man be paid four pounds to say a sentence he does not believe and the microphone was on for all of it.**"

"Why are you telling me and not him."

"Because he is being paid and I am not going to be a man who says it to him for nothing, **and because about a fortnight ago I stood in a room and a man of fifty-two said out loud that he had stopped asking to be checked, and I have not been able to sit on it since, and you are the only person in this market who is allowed to say something to somebody and is not allowed to say it twice.**"

---

He did not tell him.

He carried the trestle back to a van at about two in the afternoon with the amplifier and the lead coiled and the microphone in a case, and Peter Amado was still there at about twenty past one with a cup of something he had not paid for, and he looked at the man of twenty-two twice, in about four minutes, twice, and did not ask him anything.

That has never happened to him in a market in this city and he wrote it down, and he wrote down the two counts, because he writes down numbers, and he wrote down no names.

---

Nobody offered Peter Amado a way out.

There is not one in this city and there has not been one since the week of the twenty-four and nobody in this case has ever been in a position to offer him one, and on the Wednesday of week sixty-one a woman of thirty-five at a trestle table with a card of times on it told him that the sessions finish after this one and that the fee schedule for the autumn had not come down yet.

"That is not a way out."

"That is not a way out," she agreed, "**and I am not going to pretend it is, and I have said no to about nine people this month who wanted something out of a Friday night and I have said no to all of them, and you are the first one I have said no to who has not asked me for anything.**"

"Then I will come back."

"**You will come back and there will be a fee or there will not be a fee and either way you will be a man who was paid, and in about four years that is the only sentence anybody can say about you, and it will be said about a man who said a true thing six times first and nobody wrote that down either.**"

---

*192. Wednesday of week 61, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a trestle table in a market in Saltmarket with a microphone on a stand and a lead in it, and a man of about fifty-one who asked a question in it and got no answer.*

*The place.* **A market on a Wednesday, about eight until about two, a trestle at the end of the middle aisle put up by a company and not staffed as a post, a leaflet, a name at the bottom of it, a small amplifier and a microphone on a stand. About forty people past it in an hour, four years. The trestle was carried in at half past seven by a man with a van, unasked, and he stood at the end of it for two and a half hours and was not a speaker and was not introduced as one.**

*The question, and the count.* **He asked for the microphone to be switched off for his four minutes and said he had asked twice before and would ask a third time, and asked for the answer to be yes or no rather than nothing. He was put on at eleven. It was not switched off. The amplifier was making the noise it makes when nothing is going into it. Afterwards, two people standing about four feet away said they could not tell whether it had been on, and the woman at the trestle said the switch is on the amplifier and not on the stand, that she had not known it was there until a fortnight ago, and that she has now asked twice and been given nothing. Four times, one of them hers, and no answer on any of them.**

*What he said into it, and the price of it, in his words.* **The funded sentence, that a practice in this city belongs to nobody, and then, fast, that he is the man being paid to say it, that he has said a true thing six times in this city and has never once been asked where he got it, and that he is not going to be asked now, and that a man of fifty-one has just said a sentence he does not believe into a microphone he asked twice to have switched off and that nobody in the market has answered the question about whether it was off. Nobody in reach had a stop.**

*What a man of forty-four said to a man of twenty-two, on the far side of the trestle, in about nine seconds.* **That the asking was in front of people both times, that the first time somebody switched it off and nobody wrote it down, so the only provable thing about that microphone is that a man asked for it to be switched off; that the asking is the part that lasts; and that in about four years the only quotable sentence of that morning will be a man of fifty-one asking for a microphone to be switched off in a market. He said he was telling the man of twenty-two and not the man of fifty-one because the man of fifty-one is being paid and he is not going to be a man who says it to him for nothing.**

*What was not done, and the reason.* **It was not told to him. A man of fifty-one looked at the man of twenty-two twice in about four minutes and did not ask him anything, which is recorded because it has never happened in a market in this city. Nothing was written down, no form was filled in, and the standing problem was not said, and no rule about who may be asked was invented. He was not thanked, he was not found, he was not given a way out, and there is not one in this city. The woman at the trestle told him the sessions finish after this one and the autumn fee schedule had not come down, and said that was not a way out and that she had said no to about nine people this month who wanted something out of a Friday night, and that he was the first who had not asked her for anything.
