# Chapter 186 — She Has Got The Key

Up one flight of stairs in that building there is an exchange room, and it is not on the first floor according to anything anybody has ever written down, and the woman who runs it is thirty-one and called Ianthe Roke and she has a key, a diary and a list and nothing else at all, and she has never had to decide anything with any of the three.

She has had that key for about a year and she has never turned it on anybody, and on the Thursday morning of week sixty she turned it on one person.

A man of twenty-two was in a corridor of that building on a job and was not let up and was not told why and did not ask, which is the whole of his part in it.

---

She did it in the corridor, not in the room, and she said that afterwards was a decision and not an accident.

"You have been in that room nine times since the Monday of last week," she said. "I have written the days in the diary because there is nowhere else to write them and I want you to know that I wrote them."

"Why are you telling me that."

"Because in about four years the only version of this anybody has is that a woman of thirty-one took a key off a man of thirty-four, and I would rather the first version had a diary in it."

---

He was on his way in. It was half past three in the afternoon and he had been up two hours and he had four hours before his shift.

"How long."

"Not a week. **A week, and then it is somebody else's turn to decide whether you can come in, and there is nobody else, so it will be me, and I would rather it was a week than a decision.**"

"You are the key."

"I am the key," said Ianthe Roke. "**I have been the key since the spring and I did not know that until about a fortnight ago, and what happened a fortnight ago is that I started leaving the door on the latch, and I did that because you were in there, and I did not ask myself who the latch was for.**"

---

He did not argue, and that is not a thing to be admired.

"Go on."

"The room is a room where somebody is doing something," she said. "**There is a table in it and there is a plate in the wall and about two hundred of the district's pages come through that counter downstairs four times a year, and there is a woman of fifty-three behind it who has been there nineteen years, and I have made that room into a place where a man can sit down at two in the afternoon because he has got nowhere to sit down at two in the afternoon.**"

"That is not a bad use of a room."

"It is the worst use of a room I can think of and I did it with a key and nobody's permission, and the reason nobody stopped me is that nobody knew, and the reason nobody knew is that it is a room on a floor that is not on the plan."

---

"There is no form for this."

"There is no form for this and I have looked, and the honest reason there is no form for this is that a form would want a reason and I do not have a reason. I have got a fact. **You have been in that room nine times and on eight of them nothing was happening, and a room where nothing is happening is a room where somebody is waiting, and a person waiting in a room is a person in a record, and I am the only one who writes that room down and I have been writing you down nine times without asking you a single question about any of it.**"

---

"And after the week."

"After the week you can come in if you tell me and I will say yes and I will not make you ask twice. **And that is the version that costs me, and I would like it noticed that I have chosen the version that costs me, because the version where you just come in is the version where I am a woman with a key, and in about four years a woman with a key is a post, and there is no post in this city whose job it is to decide who goes in a room, and there is not going to be one.**"

"Who told you that."

"Nobody. A woman of forty-one said a version of it to me in about nine words in a corridor in the spring and I have had a term to work out that it applies to me and it does."

---

Then she went down and told the woman at the counter, and she should not have, and she said so.

Petra Lindqvist listened to the whole of it and then said: "**I have been behind this counter for nineteen years and I have never once been asked to know anything, and I am not going to start now. If you tell me that you have turned a key on somebody in this building then I am a person who knows, and a person who knows is the beginning of a record, and I have spent nineteen years making sure this counter does not have one of those.**"

"Then I will not write it down either."

"You write it in the diary. You have been writing it in the diary for a fortnight and you told him so in a corridor, and I am going to say the one thing I have got and then I am going back to the four stacks. **A diary is a diary. It is not a register and if anybody ever treats it as a register then there is a woman in this building who has written nine lines about a man in a corridor and she will not know why she did it.**"

---

He did not take it well and nobody in that building was told that either.

He stood in the corridor for a minute with his hands in his pockets and then he said: "**I have got a shift, a canteen off a service road, a stairwell four miles away, and now this, and I want it on the record that none of those five things is mine and every one of them was given to me by a person.**"

"None of them is mine either," said Ianthe Roke. "The key is on a ring in a flat because somebody who is dead gave it to me and there is a piece of paper about that too, and I am not going to tell you about that piece of paper because you have a floor at ten past four in the morning and I am not going to be the reason you have to carry a person you have never met."

He laughed, once, and went down.

---

At ten past four the following morning he stood on his own floor and told four people, and then he asked the only question he asked.

"Is the counter still open."

"Every morning it is a woman of fifty-three and four stacks," said Ianthe Roke, from a corridor, where she had come for a different reason and had not planned to be there.

"Right," he said. "Then I have lost a room and I have not lost a floor."

He used the sentence that says a room is a room, and he was allowed it, because it had been a week.

---

At ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward there were five things in a drawer under a bench, four of them other people's sentences, and a jacket on the back of a chair with a corner of card about two inches by three in the inside pocket of it, and the original in the fourth pocket of the tool roll with the fifth line blank.

A man of twenty-two had been in a corridor of a building in Bower Street for about eleven minutes and had not been let up and had not asked, and the reason he had not asked is that three weeks ago he stood in a corridor outside a room in the same building and listened to a telephone ring and did not knock, and the woman on the other end of that telephone has a name in pencil on a page in a stack about four feet from where he was standing, and nobody took it from her.

He is the only person in this city who writes things down, and on the Thursday of week sixty he wrote down that somebody was removed from a room and the name of the person who removed her and the fact that the person who removed her did it with a key and a diary and told him in a corridor, and he did not write down what the man said afterwards, and he has not decided yet whether that was integrity.

---

*189. Thursday of week 60, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is one key, one diary, and a room on the first floor of a building in Bower Street that is not where the building says it is.*

*What the removal was, in the terms the person doing it used.* **Not a ban, not a complaint and not a district. A week, said in a corridor and not in the room on purpose, with the reason given afterwards as a decision rather than an accident. The instrument is a key held by a woman of thirty-one since the spring, and the only record is nine days written in a diary, which is a diary and not a register and is not to become one.**

*Why, in her order, before she was asked.* **That the room had become a daytime sitting room, that she was the one making it one, that she had done it on her own authority and with a key she was given and without asking anybody, and that nobody stopped her because nobody knew, and that the only reason available for nobody knowing is that the room sits on a floor that appears on nothing anybody has ever written down. Attendance: nine times since the Monday of last week, eight of them with nothing taking place. Her account of why that is a fault: an unoccupied room with a person in it puts a person in a record, and she is the only person who writes that room down and had entered him nine times without putting one question to him about any of it. And the other half, which is what it costs her: after the week he can come in if he asks and she will say yes and will not make him ask twice, and that version is the one where she is a woman with a key, and in about four years a woman with a key is a post, and there is no post in this city whose job it is to decide who goes in a room.**

*What the clerk at the counter said, which is the instrument refusing to become one.* **That in nineteen years she has never been asked to know anything and is not going to start, and that being told a woman in this building has turned a key on somebody makes her a person who knows, and a person who knows is the beginning of a record, and she has spent nineteen years making sure that counter does not have one. And: a diary is a diary, and if anybody ever treats it as a register then there is a woman in this building who has written nine lines about a man in a corridor and will not know why she did it.**

*What was said on his own floor, and the one question.* **Five places he has and none of them is his and every one was given by a person, and she refused to tell him about a piece of paper about the key because he has a floor at ten past four in the morning. He asked one question, which was whether the counter downstairs is still open, and was told that it is a woman of fifty-three and four stacks every morning, and said then I have lost a room and I have not lost a floor.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **Nobody stopped it and nobody was asked to. There was no form, minute, notice, report, committee or leaflet, and there is no post in this city whose job it is to decide who goes in a room. The instrument and the holder are the same person, the period is a week, the number of people who know is two, and the third party received a true statement and asked no follow-up. This entry contains no account of what the removed person said afterwards, because he said it in a corridor and the entry is a record and not a transcript. Entry 189.**
