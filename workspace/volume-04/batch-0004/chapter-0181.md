# Chapter 181 — Somewhere Else For Eleven Minutes

The room was on the first floor of a building off a street in Nine Bridges and it had a table in it, two chairs, a window that opened about four inches, and eleven names on a piece of card inside a plastic wallet.

Sinead Corrigan is thirty-four and she is a district nurse and the card is a list she wrote herself and it is not a district list, and she has carried it since the spring in the same wallet as the file with her own name at the top of it that she put there on a Friday with her own hand in the box that says *completed by*, and nobody has disciplined her for either.

A man of twenty-two came in at ten past ten on the Monday morning of week fifty-nine because he had a fault on a docket in that building and the fault was upstairs.

---

She did not ask him to leave.

That is the part that a man who has been in this city for three terms gets wrong, and he got it wrong in his head about four seconds after she said it, and what she said was this.

"**I am not asking you to leave. I am asking you to be somewhere else for eleven minutes and I want you to notice the difference and I am not going to explain it twice.**"

"I can go."

"You can go," said Sinead Corrigan. "**A person who is asked to leave has been asked to leave. A person who is asked to be somewhere else has been asked to be somewhere else, and only one of those two is in a file anywhere, and I would like to know which one I have just done before you go out of the door.**"

---

Then she told him the reason, and she told it to the card on the table rather than to him, which is a thing she does.

"I have got eleven people on this and I have got one question and I am going to ask all eleven of them the same question and it is the only question I have got. **And if you are standing in this room when I ask it then I am not the person who asked it, I am the person who was in the room with the man everybody has heard of, and eleven people are going to answer that question by looking at you.**"

"And if I am in the corridor."

"Then you are in the corridor and eleven people answer a question that I asked, in a room with one person in it, and about four years from now the only record of that in this city is that a man of twenty-two was outside the door."

---

He went out.

There is a landing outside that room about nine feet long with a window at the end of it and a stair that goes down, and he stood on the landing with his docket in his hand, and the door was a fire door on a spring that did not latch, so that it stayed where it was last pushed.

He said he would wait outside. She said nothing back, which is how she said it.

---

Four people went up and down that landing in eleven minutes and every one of them noticed where he was.

The first was a man of thirty-four who works nights, on his way in at half ten for a shift that starts at eleven, who has a stairwell of his own four miles away and who has said no twice to being the word on his own floor and once with no reason on it at all. He looked at the man on the landing, and at the door, and at the man on the landing again, and went down without stopping, and on the second landing he said out loud, to nobody, the sentence that says a room is a room, because he is allowed to use it once a week and it was Monday.

The second was a woman of about thirty-eight in a district jacket with a folder under her arm and the particular way of standing that a person has who is waiting for somebody to be free. She did not go in. She looked at the door and at him and she said: "Is this a queue?" and he said no, and she said "right," and went and sat down on the step at the bottom, and waited there, and was still there when the door opened.

The third was a foreman of fifty-eight with a site diary in his hand who had a reason to be on that landing and did not have it, and who said, out loud, to the man standing there: "**I am going to be in this building in about four hours and I am going to say nothing about what I have just seen, and I would like it noted that I said that now rather than later.**"

The fourth was a boy of nineteen carrying a bag of card offcuts up those stairs because he was going the wrong way on purpose and everybody does it in that building.

---

Behind the door, for eleven minutes, nothing happened that a man on a landing could have found out about, and that is the finding of the Monday morning of week fifty-nine, and it is not a small one.

There is a room on the first floor of a building off a street in Nine Bridges where a woman of thirty-four asked the same question of eleven people, and there was a man in the corridor outside it, and about four hundred people in this city know that there is a room like that in a building like that, and not one of the four hundred has any idea whether the man was outside it.

---

At four minutes past eleven the door opened and the card was in the wallet and the wallet was in her pocket and the table had nothing on it.

"I did not get through the list," said Sinead Corrigan. "**I did five of the eleven in eleven minutes and there are six of them I have not asked yet and I am going back on Monday. Four said yes and one said no, and I am telling you the five and not the eleven, because the five are the ones with an answer against them, and the one who said no is not on that card and is not going to be, and I did not ask her why and I did not offer her a second question.**"

"Was that the right thing."

"There is no right thing. **It is the correct action and it is the correct action because she said no and the only thing anybody can do with a no is stop asking, and if anybody in this city does anything else with it then it was not a no.**" She closed the wallet. "And you were not in the room, and I want to be straight with you about why that mattered, because it is going to sound like a compliment and it is not one."

"Go on."

"**It mattered because with you in there those four said yes in a voice they use on a man they have been told about, and I cannot tell you which of the four would have said it in my voice. I have got four yeses and I do not know how many of them are mine, and I have got six people I have not asked at all, and both of those are things I am going to have to work with for a fortnight and there is no form in this city that takes either of them.**"

---

She named her own cost, and she did it at the door, and she has never once named a cost quietly.

"**I have just done the only version of this I know how to do and I have had to do it alone, and there is a woman in this city whose name is in a box in pencil on a counter four miles from here who gave her name on a telephone and was asked once and did not answer, and nobody took it from her. I have thought about the first half of that every day since the spring.**"

"What half."

"**The half where nobody did anything wrong.**" She did not soften it and she did not raise her voice. "**I have not got the other half and I have had a morning doing this and a car park to get to, and there is a woman of fifty-three behind that counter who has had a pencil of her own on that page for about eleven weeks and has not had anybody to say it to.**"

He did not have an answer to that and he said so, and she said it was all right, because it was half eleven and she had a plastic wallet to get back to a car park in.

---

He went down and collected the fault, which was a lamp ballast on the second landing and a nine-minute job, and he did it, and the four people in the corridor had all gone.

The man of thirty-four on nights had left a folded sheet of paper wedged in the fire door so that the door would stay where it was last pushed, and it was not there in the morning, and nobody in that building has ever mentioned it, and it is not on anything.

---

He got back to the shop at ten and the bench was where it is and the docket was on it with five job numbers in his own hand and one of the five was a lamp ballast in Nine Bridges with a tick beside it. The number ran at nine all afternoon and was at ten when he sat down, and the shutter was up and the parts cage was in the corner where the parts cage is.

He wrote nothing down about the eleven minutes. He has been in this city three terms and he has written down a lift engineer's sixteen Fridays and a clerk's nineteen years and a plate count of about nine hundred, and he did not write down a woman being asked to be somewhere else, and the reason is that there is nothing to write it on and the other reason is that if it were in his book then the only copy of it in this city would be in his book, and he has been trying for a term and a half not to be that.

A man was in a corridor for eleven minutes and eleven people were asked a question they were not asked by him, and there is no version of that which anybody can use, and the whole of what the Monday morning of week fifty-nine produced is that nobody had to be asked about anything.

*184. Monday of week 59, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a woman of thirty-four with a clipboard and eleven names on a list that is not a district list, and a corridor with four people in it who noticed where he was standing.*

*What the room was.* **First floor, a building off a street in Nine Bridges. A table, two chairs, a window that opens about four inches, a plastic wallet with eleven names on a card in it that a district nurse of thirty-four wrote herself and is not a district list, and the same wallet as a file with her own name at the top of it. A fire door on a spring that does not latch, so it stays where it is last pushed, and a landing about nine feet long outside it with a window at the end.**

*What she asked for and what she gave as the reason.* **Not a leaving. A somewhere else, for eleven minutes, and she said out loud that she wanted to know which of the two she had done before he went through the door, and the reason given to the card and not to him: with a man in the room the answer to her one question is given in a voice people use on a man they have been told about, and in about four years the only record of the morning would be that a man of twenty-two was outside the door. He did not argue and he went out.**

*The four who noticed, and what each of them did with it.* **A man of thirty-four on nights going in for a shift, who looked at the door and at him and went down and used the one sentence he is allowed to use once a week, and which is the one that says a room is a room. A woman of about thirty-eight in a district jacket with a folder, who asked whether it was a queue, was told no, sat on the step at the bottom and was still there when the door opened. A foreman of fifty-eight with a site diary and no reason to be on that landing, who said out loud that he was going to say nothing about what he had seen and would rather have said it then than later. A boy of nineteen carrying card offcuts up the wrong stair on purpose.** None of them said anything to the man on the landing about the room, and one of them left a folded sheet wedged in the fire door overnight, and it was gone in the morning and is not on anything.

*The result, as a count and not as names.* **Five of the eleven were asked inside the eleven minutes and six were not, and she is going back on Monday. Four yes and one no, and the no was not followed by a reason and was not offered a second question. She named the cost of the method and not of the answer: four of the four yeses may have been given in a voice she cannot account for, and there is no form in this city that takes an account of that, and she is going to have to work with it for a fortnight.**

*What she named, at the door, which is the hardest thing said in this movement and is said by her and not by him.* **That a woman of thirty-eight gave a name to a form four miles away in good faith, that nobody took it from her, that she has been carrying the first half of that as a grievance for a term and a half, and that she has said out loud that she has not got the other half and did not have time to get it. This entry does not supply the other half, because the counter that box is on is the object of the Thursday and is not dealt with in this entry.**

*What was not done and was not asked for.* **No form, minute, notice, report, committee or leaflet settled anything, and no post was created, and a room with a door that does not latch is a room and not an authority. The master is in an inside pocket on a chair and the fifth line is blank. Entry 184.**
