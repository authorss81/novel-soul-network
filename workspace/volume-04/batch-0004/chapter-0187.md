# Chapter 187 — The Tenth

The tenth of the eleven was on the Friday of week sixty, four weeks after the ninth, in the room in Crown Terrace with the strip lights and the stack by the door, and somebody had made the stack again, and it had about four hundred leaflets on it with a fee and a session title and five jobs on the front of them.

Corin Vasska read the third line out in the voice he reads everything in, and a line is not a section, and about nine people in that room did not hear the difference, which was the fourth sitting running.

He was four rows from the back with his coat over the back of the chair in front of him, because he has carried that coat into about forty rooms this term and put it over a chair in every one of them, and he did not stand up.

---

There is a condition on his share and there is a microphone on a stand at the front of that room and there is no mechanism in this city that can tell the difference between a man being stopped from doing a thing and a man being asked about one, and he had said the whole of that out loud in this room on the Friday of week fifty-six, standing, in the fourth row, with his own mouth, and he had said it again four minutes later when a woman of thirty-four asked him to, and it had not got better the second time and he had not gone home and thought about it and found a better version.

So he sat in the fourth row for fifty minutes and said nothing at all, and nobody asked him anything, and the man of fifty-two in the second row looked at him once at ten o'clock and then looked at the front.

---

There were thirty-one chairs in that room and twenty-one people in it, and Corin Vasska said the numbers out loud at a quarter to ten, which is what the officer of a fund does.

"**Thirty-one and twenty-one. The first one of these had nine chairs and a man in each of them and I have never in four years had a session with fewer than nine and I have never once put that in a minute, and the reason is that it is a Friday at half past nine at night and about eleven thousand premises pay a levy for this series and close at six.**"

"Somebody should tell them the hours," said somebody at the back.

"**I have told them the hours. It is on the front of the leaflet. That is where the hours are, and the five jobs are on the front of the same sheet, and I read that sheet out loud every Friday because a system in a company put two of those five jobs in my hand and did not ask me and I have never been able to find out what it wanted.**"

---

Then he said the thing he had come to say, and he said it at eleven, with the room half empty and the microphone still on its stand and nobody having asked for it to be switched off, which is a thing nobody in this city is able to do.

"I am not going to read the five jobs out loud an eleventh time."

He put the leaflet face down on the desk.

"**And I want to be exact about the reason, because in about four years somebody is going to say that a fund officer took a step back, and the truth is that I have read that sheet out loud ten Fridays and about eleven people a week are being taught to wait for a man and not one of those eleven has ever heard me read it.**"

---

A man of fifty-two in the second row put his hand up and Vasska said yes, and the man of fifty-two had been the depot manager for four years and had been putting an exception forward for renewal since the start of the term and had read the box about four hundred times and had never once put a reason in it.

"I have stopped asking," he said.

"Stopped asking what."

"**Whether anybody is going to offer to take the wording to somebody who could change it. I have been offered twice. I said no twice. I have now stopped expecting it a third time and I cannot tell from inside this room whether that is a decision or whether I am tired, and I am not going to say which in a room with a microphone on it, and I am saying it here because a man of forty-four has just read out a thing that he has done ten times and I would like somebody to have said the other half of it out loud in a room at least once this term.**"

Nobody wrote it down.

---

The woman of twenty-six was at her table by the wall and she had come to nine of the eleven and she had not stood up in any of them, and at five to eleven she put a hand on the folder behind her and said one thing about it to nobody in particular.

"**There are eleven letters in this folder and there are five answers written and none of them has gone out, and the fifth one is better than the first four and I wrote it in about an hour and I am not going to send it either, and I have said that in this room before and I am saying the numbers again because in about four years the only thing anybody knows about me is that there were four.**"

"How many are you going to write."

"**Nine. There are eleven and I am going to write the other four and I am not going to send any of them, and that is not a decision, it is a Tuesday and a pen, and I have a job and a room and a floor and the writing is the only part of this week that is mine.**"

---

At a quarter to midnight the microphone was still on its stand at the front of that room and ten chairs were empty and a man of twenty-two was the only person in the fourth row who had not said anything at all, and there is no record anywhere in this city of that.

A specification runs to four sections and three lines. The third of them is funded. A levy is paid by premises that number in five figures. A committee of about nine met once in the week of the thirty-seventh and has not met since and is not going to. A session is held on a Friday at half past nine at night in a room with thirty-one chairs in it, and twenty-one people were in it, and the officer of the fund has decided not to read five jobs off a leaflet an eleventh time, and nobody in the room stopped him and nobody asked him to.

---

*190. Friday of week 60, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is the tenth of the eleven, four weeks after the ninth, and a sheet with five jobs on the front of it that is not going to be read an eleventh time.*

*The room, as a count.* **Same room in Crown Terrace. Thirty-one chairs, twenty-one people, a stack of about four hundred leaflets with a fee, a session title and five jobs on the front, two of them his, chosen by a system in a company out of two job sheets in the week of the thirty-fourth. The third line was read as the third line and about nine people did not hear the difference, which is the fourth sitting running. The first session of the series had nine chairs and a man in each.**

*The arithmetic the officer of the fund put on the table himself.* **Thirty-one chairs and twenty-one people, the lowest attendance in four years, never minuted, and the reason: a Friday at half past nine at night and about eleven thousand premises paying a levy that closes at six. When somebody said somebody should tell them the hours, he said the hours are on the front of the leaflet, and the five jobs are on the front of the same sheet, and he reads that sheet aloud every Friday because a system put two of those jobs in his hand and nobody has ever been able to find out what it wanted.**

*What was not said, and the reason, which is in the entry because the reason is the finding.* **He did not stand up. He has said the standing problem in this room twice, once standing and once four minutes later when asked, and it did not get better the second time, and he did not go home and find a better version. Nobody asked him anything and a man of fifty-two in the second row looked at him once at about ten and then looked at the front. The count in this volume is unchanged and no rule about who may be asked was invented.**

*The removal, which is the second one in this movement and it is the officer of the fund who makes it.* **At eleven, with the room half empty and the microphone still on its stand, he said he is not going to read the five jobs out loud an eleventh time, and gave the reason: he has read that sheet out loud ten Fridays, about eleven people a week are being taught to wait for a man, and not one of those eleven has ever heard him read it. Nobody stopped him and nobody asked him to.**

*What a man of fifty-two said, out loud, once, in a room with a microphone on it.* **That he has stopped asking whether anybody is going to offer to take the wording of a condition to somebody who could change it, having been offered twice and said no twice, and that he cannot tell from inside the room whether that is a decision or whether he is tired, and that he is not going to say which. His reason for saying it: a man of forty-four has just read a thing out loud ten times and he would like the other half of it said in a room at least once this term. Nobody wrote it down.**

*What a woman of twenty-six said from her seat, and did not stand up for.* **Eleven letters, five answers written, none sent, the fifth better than the first four, and nine to go, and that it is not a decision but a Tuesday and a pen, and that the writing is the only part of the week that is hers. She has been to nine of the eleven.**

*What was not settled.* **Four sections, three lines, one funded, premises in five figures, a committee that does not meet again. A session in a room with thirty-one chairs on a Friday night with twenty-one people in it, and no minute, and no notice, and nobody asked to fill in anything. Entry 190.**
