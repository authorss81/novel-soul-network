# Chapter 188 — None Of It Was Paid Back

The canteen off the service road opens at seven and the woman who runs it had a hospital appointment, and a man of twenty-two on grade two had said he would do the till and had been there since a quarter past six with the urn on.

Five people came in between ten past seven and about ten, one at a time, and none of them knew each other, and every one of them had been somewhere in this city in the last fortnight that cost them something.

---

The boy of nineteen came first, at ten past seven, and he had a bag of card offcuts over his shoulder and he bought a tea and put a pound on the counter and did not take the change.

"That is a pound for a tea of about sixty."

"It is a pound for not having to count change," said the boy of nineteen, "because I am not going to be able to count it at eight this morning and I have got nine words off a form to say to a man before I go to work."

"Whose form."

"**Mine is not to do with it. A district woman has asked me to be a name at the top of a sheet about cards and I have said no, and I said no on Friday, and I have not put anything on it, and I have had about eleven hours to work out what a refusal of that is going to be in about four years.**"

---

He took the tea to the end of the counter and sat down and said the rest of it to the urn.

"There are about four hundred pieces of paper out there with my handwriting on them and I am on none of them with my name, and that was the right answer and she said it was the right answer and it is worse than hers. **And now there is a thing I have said no to, and a no that is written down is a thing somebody can ask you about, and I have never in my life had a no that was written down, and I did not know that was a thing you could have.**"

"You can give it back in a week."

"**I can give it back in a week and then I have got to have a reason, and I have got a reason, and the reason is that four hundred pieces of paper have got my handwriting on them and if I put my name on a fifth one then somebody in about four years has got my name and my hand and my handwriting and there is no version of that where I am a person who only did some of it.**"

---

The man of thirty-four on nights came in at twenty to eight because he could not sleep, which is what he does, and he had been up since half three and had been on his own floor and had not been able to be on his own floor.

He did not say why he was there. He ordered a tea and put it down and then he put a work card on the counter face up.

"**I have got nine words in biro on the back of that and I have used them, and I used them on Friday, and nobody asked me to use them.**"

"On what."

"On the back of a second card." He turned the first one over. "**A man of thirty-four on nights has a floor with four people on it and a release that waits for a man, and I have said the nine words out loud in two corridors and a canteen and a reading room and I have got no way of handing them to anybody, and on Friday I wrote them out on the back of a card because a man of twenty-two in this canteen at about half seven in the morning has put about a hundred and forty leaflets through a hatch and there was a pile of them on the counter and I took one and I wrote on the back of it.**"

---

"You have put your nine words on a leaflet."

"I have put my nine words on a leaflet of about four hundred that a man of twenty-two printed in the spring for a company and did not write, and **it is in the bin behind that door, and it will be in a skip by Wednesday, and that is the only place in this city where a sentence of mine is written down and it is going to be in a skip on Wednesday.**"

He did not sound like a man who minded.

"I have been trying for about two months to find a way of handing nine words to somebody," he said. "**And I found one, and it was on the back of somebody else's card, and it cost nothing, and I did it in about nine seconds, and the reason I have come in here on a Monday morning eight o'clock to tell a man who makes tea about it is that it is the only true thing I have got and I have nobody to give it to and I am not going to give it to the four.**"

---

"You cannot hand that back."

"**I can hand it back. That is the whole of it. I can go and get a leaflet off the skip on Wednesday and I can put it in a different bin, and it is the same nine words and it will be a different piece of paper, and in about four years there will be a skip in this city with a sentence of mine in it and nobody will ever be able to say whether that was a thing I did or a thing that happened.**"

---

The woman of twenty-six came in at a quarter to nine and she had an envelope in her hand and she put it on the counter and did not push it towards him.

"**That has a stamp on it and it is not going in a box. I wrote the answer on Friday night in about an hour and it is the best of the five and I am not sending it, and I have carried it down here in my hand since half five because I wanted to be holding a thing that somebody could see me not do.**"

"You want me to post it."

"I want you to look at it and then I am going to take it away again and put it in my coat, and I would like a witness, and you are the only person in this city I have got and you are a man behind a hatch at about nine in the morning and that is exactly the right amount of person to want."

He looked at the envelope and did not read the address and said that was the correct decision.

"It is not a decision," said the woman of twenty-six. "**It is that there is nothing on the paper to answer it with. That was true in the spring and it is true now and I have got five of them written and I am going to write nine and I am not going to send any of them and I have said that in a room with a microphone on it and now I have said it to a man making tea, and the reason I have said it twice is that the first time about eleven people heard me and the second time one did.**"

---

The printer of fifty-eight came in at ten to ten with a plastic wallet and a bag of card under her arm and she had been in the district since eight for a quarterly return.

Sunniva Bellhouse is fifty-eight and she paid for the sign over her own door out of her own money in her first year, and she has never seen a company's stock, and about four hundred and thirty-two cards went out of that shop in the spring and about four hundred of the new ones went out the week before last with her name on the back of every one of them.

"Four teas," she said, and put four pounds on the counter and did not take the change either.

Then she said the thing she has said in about four rooms and had not said in any of them to a person who serves tea.

"**I am still the person who decides what that card is for. Nobody has offered me a way of not being that and I have not asked for one and I am not going to ask a man behind a hatch for one, and I have got a hundred and forty-one pounds on a coat hook in the back of four feet of shop because a form wants a name at the top of it and the only name I have got is a postcode and a wet coat.**"

"How long is that money been on the hook."

"Since the spring. And the coat is a wet coat and there is a form on top of it in an envelope and I have looked at the envelope about nine times and I have not opened it, because opening it is a decision and I have had enough of those this year."

---

At ten the canteen filled up and he stopped hearing individual people, which is the point of a canteen at ten in the morning.

Five people had come in one at a time between ten past seven and about ten, and every one of them had told a man who serves tea the exact amount that a thing in this city had cost them, and not one of them had been paid any of it back, and not one of them had asked him for anything, and he was the only person in the building who could have heard all five and he could not do a single thing with any of them.

There are still about eleven people a week being taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and the man in the lanyard made five teas that morning and did not take any change and wrote down five numbers and no names, and about a hundred and forty leaflets went through that hatch in about an hour, and one of them had a man's nine words on the back of it and was in a bin by the middle of the day.

---

Ten at night, the shutter up and the bench under the lamp with a day of forty-one jobs on it in his own shorthand, and the number nine all the way through the afternoon and ten by the time he sat down.

The drawer under that bench holds five things, four of them other people's sentences, and the fifth is a letter with a reference on it whose own last line says it should not be produced without that office being asked first, and there is no proceeding.

There is a jacket on the back of a chair with a corner of card about two inches by three in the inside pocket of it, and a decision about it that has not been made in a term and a half, and the original is in the fourth pocket of the tool roll with the fifth line still blank.

He wrote down the five numbers, because he writes down numbers, and he wrote down no names.

---

*191. Monday of week 61, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a canteen off a service road at ten past seven in the morning, and five people who came in one at a time and every one of them told a man who makes tea what a thing in this city had cost them.*

*The room.* **A canteen off a service road on the far side of the city, open at seven, the woman who runs it at a hospital appointment, the till and the urn done by a man of twenty-two on grade two since a quarter past six, and a hatch through which about a hundred and forty leaflets of the spring went in about an hour.**

*The first.* **A boy of nineteen with a bag of card offcuts, who paid a pound for a tea of about sixty so as not to have to count change, and who had said no on Friday to being a name at the top of a district sheet about cards. He named the cost: about four hundred pieces of paper carry his handwriting and he is on none of them with his name, that was the right answer and it is worse than the printer's, and a no that is written down is a thing somebody can ask you about, and he had not known that was a thing he could have. He said he could take the no back in a week and would then have to have a reason.**

*The second.* **A man of thirty-four on nights, up since half three, who had used his nine words in biro on the back of one of the hundred and forty leaflets on the counter, unasked, on Friday, and had come in to tell a man making tea about it because it is the only true thing he has and he is not going to give it to the four. He named the cost: it is in the bin behind that door and it will be in a skip by Wednesday, and it is the only place in this city where a sentence of his is written down. He also named the thing he cannot undo about it, which is that he can take it out and write it on a different piece of paper, and that in about four years there will be a skip in this city with a sentence of his in it and nobody will ever be able to say whether that was a thing he did or a thing that happened.**

*The third.* **A woman of twenty-six with a stamped envelope she did not put down the post, who had written the fifth of five answers and is not sending any of them and intends to write nine. She asked for a witness and specified the amount: a man behind a hatch at about nine in the morning, because eleven people heard her in a room with a microphone on it and she wanted one person to see her not do a thing. Her reason, said twice and unchanged: there is nothing on the paper to answer them with, and that was true in the spring and is true now, and it is not a decision.**

*The fourth.* **A printer of fifty-eight with a plastic wallet and a bag of card and a quarterly return, who put four pounds down for four teas and did not take the change, and who said in a canteen what she has said in about four rooms: that she is still the person who decides what a card is for, that nobody has offered her a way of not being that, that she has not asked for one and is not going to ask a man behind a hatch, and that a hundred and forty-one pounds has been on a coat hook since the spring in an envelope she has looked at about nine times and has not opened, because opening it is a decision.**

*What was and was not done.* **Five teas, no change taken, five numbers written down and no names, no form, no minute, no notice, no report, no committee, no leaflet, and nobody asked him for anything. The standing problem is unchanged and was not said. Entry 191.**
