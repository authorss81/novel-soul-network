# Chapter 195 — Two People He Did Not Choose

The old continuity hall in Crown Terrace has a plant room on the ground floor and a service tunnel under it, and on the Thursday morning of week sixty-five four people went down the tunnel and a woman of twenty-four went down it after them because she had a bag of stock returns for a counter in that building and no reason at all except that there were four of them and she was one of four.

She is a resonance nurse. She works out of a mobile clinic and she is doing a part-time certificate in this city about how to be difficult on behalf of somebody, and she has a consent clipboard in her bag that no institution has ever asked her for, and she had not seen Marek Senn for a term.

"You have got a job," she said, on the landing, in a voice that was not warm and was not cold. "Is that a fault."

"It is a lamp ballast in a stairwell in Nine Bridges and I have got it on a docket and I have got two hours."

"Then you are on a docket and you are going down a tunnel, and those are two different sentences, and one of them is about somebody's money and one of them is about a man who has not told me which is which." She put the bag down. "**I am going down because four people are going down and one of them is a woman of forty-five who has said no to me once and I would like to be in the room when she says it again, and that is not your business and it is not hers either.**"

---

The chamber is called the Crown Vault on the plans that stop at it, and the plans call it that because the plans are from before the hall was a hall, and it is a maintenance chamber under a building and it is about eleven feet by nine and it has a low ceiling and a steel stair down into it and a hatch in the floor of the landing above.

There is a convergence cabinet in it. It is painted the same grey as the wall and it is the size of a wardrobe and it has a plate on the front with a number on it and a keyhole that has no key in it, and there are about eleven conduit runs going into the top of it out of the ceiling and out of the floor, and a man of fifty-eight in a coat that has never been in a workshop looked at it for about four seconds and then did not look at it again.

Nobody locked the hatch behind them because there is nothing on the hatch to lock.

---

There is a shallow drawer in the wall at the end of that chamber and there is a book in it.

**It is a maintenance ledger.** It is ruled in five columns and it is about the size of a telephone directory and the cloth on the spine has gone at the corners, and it is not new and it is not old. **It is partial.** The first part of it is not in that drawer and has not been in that drawer for longer than anybody in this room has been working, and what is left runs for about four years and ends about a year and a half ago with no notice at the end of it, which is not how a book ends, and is how one stops when nobody is writing any more.

He got to the floor of that chamber before anybody else did, and he stayed on the floor of it, and he did not read it.

"You have not read it."

"I have not read it," he said. "**I have got a condition on my share and I have got a hand that has never been any good at a small hand, and the honest answer is that I would have read it in about nine seconds and got two words out of it that I liked and brought you four hundred pages of somebody's arithmetic.**"

---

They read it between them, and he did not choose either of them.

**Sera Quill is forty-five and is the lead repairer at Civic Spine 7 and the hall's Spine plant is on her round, and she has a metal braid of tools under her shirt and she has not held a pen in this case since the Wednesday of week fifty-six.** **Halla Rennick is forty-one and is a records clerk at a counter in a municipal annexe two streets from that hall, and she was asked on the Friday before by a man in a corridor and she said yes and said at the same time that she did not want to, and both of those were true on the same morning.**

Halla Rennick read the columns out, because she reads things out and that is her whole training and she does it in the voice she uses at a counter. Date. Place. Item. Done by. And then a fifth column, and the fifth column had a word at the top of it and the word was a word that means *who gave the word*.

"He is not in that column," she said. "**It is blank in about every line of it. I have been reading columns with things missing in them for twenty-two years and this is the first one I have read where the whole column is the point.**"

---

Sera Quill did not read it out. She turned the pages with two fingers and she did not touch the writing, and about a minute in she stopped with her hand on a page and she did not say anything for about nine seconds.

"Sera."

"**And I am not going to tell you why.**"

That is the same nine words and the same refusal and she did not better either of them, and the room was four people and a hatch and a cabinet, and nobody asked her a second question, because a man of twenty-two stopped asking about nine seconds into it: he has known for a term and a half that the answer to a question in that shape cannot be taken back once it has been said in a room with a floor in it.

The name was not said in that chamber and it was not said in the corridor afterwards and it is not on anything.

---

They took a leaf out about eleven minutes in.

It is a run sheet and it is loose, folded twice, behind the last leaf, and it is a thing a person puts in a book and does not file, and it has got a date on it and a chamber on it and about forty lines on it in that same hand, and about nine of the forty lines are the same eleven words in the same order, and Halla Rennick read the nine lines out and stopped and said:

"That is not an instruction. That is the same sentence written down nine times by nine people."

"Yes," said Sera Quill, "**and that is what it is, and I am not going to say anything else about it, and I would like it noticed that I have not said anything else about it.**"

---

Then he asked her to carry it, and he asked her in front of both of them, and that was the worst thing he did that day and he did it on purpose because there were two other people in the room and he wanted it to be a thing a room had heard.

"Talia. Take the page out of here."

He said it standing on the floor of a maintenance chamber with a docket in his other hand, and she was the only person in that room who had not come down it for the work.

"No," said Talia Venn.

"That is not an answer."

"**I will not carry what I cannot answer for.**" She said it the way she says things in rooms, which is once, and slowly the first time and not at all the second time, and then she went and picked up her bag off the stair. "**And that is about the page and not about you, and if it was about you it would be a different sentence and you would know the difference inside a second because you have spent a term and a half listening to the sound of a person saying no, and that was not it.**"

"Go on."

"There is a piece of paper in a hand I cannot read and it was written by somebody who is not going to be found by asking me where I have been on a Thursday, and the day I carry it up that stair it is in a coat on a bus and then it is in a room that is not mine, and in about four years there will be a woman of forty-five and a woman of forty-one who both know what is on it and neither of them has got it, and that is the whole of what I know about how records die." She got to the third step. "**You are asking me to be the person who happened to be in the room. That is not a reason. It is a coincidence with a strap on, and I have been the cheapest person in a room more times than I can count in four years, and I have said no three of those times, and this is the fourth, and you got the same answer, so put it with the others.**"

---

Nobody argued with her, and nobody said that was fair, and the two people who were left on the floor of that chamber did not look at each other over it.

The records clerk of forty-one put the leaf inside a canvas satchel she had brought with her for her own work and said out loud that she was doing it in front of four people on purpose, because she had been in a building for twenty-two years where nothing anybody carried out of a room was ever said out loud, and she wanted one thing she did on a Thursday to be said in a room.

"**I am putting it in my bag and I am going to say that I am, and I am not signing anything and there is no slip and there is no form and I am not going to be the person who is asked for it in a corridor in a fortnight, and if I am, the answer is that a woman of twenty-four said no to carrying it and a woman of forty-five said she was not going to say why, and that is three people and it is not one.**"

---

He was the last one out of that chamber and he did not lock anything, because there is nothing to lock, and he counted the doors on the way up and there were four and one of them went somewhere he needed to go.

The woman of twenty-four was in a corridor with her bag at her feet waiting for a counter to open and she did not look at him when he came up and she did not go, and about two minutes later she said one thing that was not about the page.

"You did the asking in front of them on purpose."

"Yes."

"**Then do not tell me it was because you trusted the room. It was because you wanted a witness and I was the cheapest one in a tunnel, and I have been the cheapest one in a room four times in four years and I have stopped being it twice, and this is the third time and you got the same answer, so put it with the other two.**"

There was nothing to be said to that and he did not pretend otherwise, and she said it did not matter, because a counter in that building was opening at eleven and she had a bag of stock returns in it that nobody was going to take off her after eleven.

---

At ten at night the docket was on the bench under the lamp and there was nothing on it from that day, because a lamp ballast in Nine Bridges had taken two hours and a tunnel had taken the rest.

Under the bench there is a drawer with five things in it, four of them sentences other people wrote, and the original is in the fourth pocket of the tool roll with the fifth line blank, and a jacket is on the back of a chair with a photocopy of a photocopy in the inside pocket of it, and the decision about it is exactly where it was before the summer and it was not made today by anybody.

He wrote down one thing, because he writes things down.

He wrote down: *four people went down. One page came up. The one who is carrying it is not me.* He did not write down a name and he did not write down a hand, and the drawer under the bench still holds five things.

---

*198. Thursday of week 65, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a maintenance chamber under a building, a book in a drawer in it, and two people who read it who were not chosen by the man who went down.*

*What the chamber is, without its name doing any work.* **A steel stair down from a landing, about eleven feet by nine, a low ceiling, a painted grey cabinet the size of a wardrobe with a plate on it and a keyhole with no key in it, and about eleven conduit runs into the top of it. A shallow drawer in the wall at the end. No lock on the hatch and no lock on anything, as is true of every maintenance chamber in this city. The name on the plans is a historical one and the plans stop at the first chamber, and nobody in this case used the name out loud.**

*The book, as an object and not as a revelation.* **Five columns, ruled. Date, place, item, done by, and a fifth column whose heading is a word meaning who gave the word, and that column is blank in every line. Partial: four years down to about a year and a half ago, and it ends with no notice, which is not how a book ends. No second part was found, no other copy was found, and this entry does not say where the rest of it went.**

*Who read it and who did not.* **A man of twenty-two on the floor of the chamber, who did not read it, and gave the reason: that he would have read it in nine seconds and brought back two words he liked and four hundred pages of somebody's arithmetic. He did not choose the two readers. A lead repairer of forty-five with a metal braid of tools, whose plant round covers that hall, and a records clerk of forty-one from a counter two streets away who was asked in a corridor on the Friday before and said yes and said at the same time that she did not want to, both true on the same morning.**

*Two refusals, each counted.* **The first is nine words and is the sentence she has said for a term and a half, word for word: she would not say why, and the reason given for not saying why is a thing about a person in this city and not about a practice, and nobody in that chamber asked her twice. The second is nine words and was said once, in front of four people, and is the whole of what milestone four of this case cost: I will not carry what I cannot answer for. Nine words, countable, and not one of them is about the two people who said them to each other.**

*What each of them named as the cost, in their own words.* **The nurse: that a piece of paper in a hand she cannot read goes into a coat on a bus and then into a room that is not hers, and that in about four years there are two people who know what is on it and neither of them has got it, and that being the person who happened to be in the room is a coincidence with a strap on. The clerk: that she put the leaf in her satchel in front of four people on purpose, that she is signing nothing, that there is no slip and no form, and that if anybody asks her for it in a corridor in a fortnight the answer is a list of three people and it is not one.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **The authorship, the name of the hand, the reason for the refusal, the other nine lines out of forty, and the rest of the book. No relay, no carrier, no comparison run by him, and no page implying one is available. Nothing in this entry was carried out of the building by anybody: it went into a canvas satchel belonging to the woman of forty-one and stayed in the building, and the carriage is on a Tuesday, and it is done by other people. Entry 198.**
