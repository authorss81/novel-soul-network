# Chapter 197 — One Over The Hundred, The Other Way

The round came on the Tuesday of week sixty-six and it came at about half past six in the morning, and the man with the trolley and the clipboard and the roll of brown tape and the pair of steel weights was in the yard behind the old continuity hall at twenty to seven, and a man of fifty-eight stood in a corridor on the first floor and let it happen.

The hatch in the floor of the landing was open four inches. It had been open four inches on the Monday of week sixty-four, and on the Thursday of week sixty-five, and it was open four inches now, and there is nothing on a maintenance chamber in this city to lock and there never has been.

---

He had authorised it on the Monday and he said so in the corridor, in the first person, at about half past six, to a man of fifty-eight in a coat that has never been in a workshop, who had come down forty steps to see a round go out of a building.

"You are going to want to know who did it."

"I already know who did it."

"Then I am going to say it anyway, because I have noticed something about myself over fourteen years." He did not look at the stairs. "**I authorised it. On Monday, in writing, in one line, and the line is in a file in a locked room on the first floor, which is the only locked room in this building, and there is a thing in it that is about nine hundred sheets of paper and it does not know what is in them.**"

"Are you going to be asked about it."

"I am going to be asked about it by about four people over the next fortnight and I am going to give the same answer to all four, and the answer is that a maintenance record has a life and the life is eleven weeks and a chamber under a building has no lock on it and a piece of paper in a locked room is a piece of paper in a building nobody can count." He put his hands in his coat pockets. "**And I did not sleep badly about it and I am not going to tell you that I did not, because a man of fifty-eight telling a corridor that he did not sleep is a thing a corridor will hold for about four years.**"

---

There were eight bundles in the yard and about nine hundred sheets across them, and there is a woman of forty-one who was there because she holds the disposal book for that annexe, and she had not been asked to be there by anybody.

She was standing in the plant room with a canvas satchel on a chair beside her and the satchel had been on that chair, or on a hook, or in that building since the Thursday before, and nobody had asked her about it and she had not mentioned it.

"You have got that in your bag."

"**I have got a folded sheet of paper in my bag and it has been in my bag for four days and I have not opened it and I have not shown it to a second person, and you are the first person who has said anything to me about it in four days, and that is because you are the only one in this building who could use the sentence correctly.**"

"Are you going to put it back in the drawer."

"No," said Halla Rennick. "**And I want it noted that I said no out loud, in a plant room, to a man with a clipboard, at about ten past seven in the morning, on the day it was going.**"

---

The man with the trolley was in the chamber at about half past seven and he was in there for about forty minutes because there were about nine hundred sheets and he is not a fast man.

He counted with the steel weights. He put them on the top sheet, turned four pages, put the weights back, wrote the number on the corner in a block hand, and put a strip of brown tape across the corner of the top sheet, and the tape went on at about ten past eight, and the count was a hundred and six and the bundle went up the steel stair to the landing and out into the yard.

The line went into the disposal book in the hut: bundle, count, tape, the Tuesday, the item, and a column with a word at the top of it that said *authority*, and in that column a job title went in, because a job title is what a man can write without stopping to think about who he is.

Every part of that was correct. The count was correct, the tape was correct, the line was correct, the item was correct, the eleven weeks were correct, and the man was not a villain and had not been asked to be careful and was going to go home at about one.

---

The leaf was in the drawer in the wall at the end of the chamber and the round took the drawer.

He put the weights on the top sheet and turned four pages and came to a folded sheet that was loose, and unfolded it, because that is what a man does with a folded sheet, and it was about forty lines in a hand and nine of the lines were the same eleven words in the same order, and he read it the way he reads everything, which is quickly.

Then he put it flat on the top of the stack, which is also what a man does.

**Nobody in that chamber had been asked to look at anything. There was no second reading, no check, no witness and no slip. A man of about sixty was doing a routine disposal with a clipboard and a bag of hooks and a folded sheet came out of a drawer and went on the top of a stack and was going to be bound, and the only thing in this case that ever had a hand put on it deliberately was about a hundred and twenty years old and was not in that drawer.**

---

They went up the steel stair at about eleven and the satchel went with them, and it went on the landing, and then along a service corridor with a fire door at the end of it, and then up forty steps, and then it stopped.

It stopped on the second landing. Halla Rennick put a hand on the rail and stood still and said:

"I have got it."

"Have you."

"**Because the last one did.**"

That is the whole of it. It was said once and it was not argued with, and nobody asked her who, and a man of fifty-one with a tool bag standing on a landing about four feet away did not say a word, and he did not say a word afterwards either, and he did not say a word about it in a corridor, a canteen or a yard in the three weeks that followed.

Nobody has ever asked her who the last one was, and she has never said, and that is not a mystery anybody in this case is going to spend anything on.

---

A man of fifty-one took the bag off the rail, checked that it was not his, and carried it the rest of the way up and out through a service door into a yard. A woman of forty-one stayed on that landing until the door had shut, and then went down. Neither of them said one word about the four flights.

The bag was not his and it was not hers. It was on a hook behind the door in the plant room and it belongs to a man who left that building's contract in the spring and whose locker nobody has cleared, and neither of them asked whose it was and both of them knew by about the second landing that they were not going to.

**There is no mechanism in this city that can make a person hand a bag back, and there is no form on which a person can say whose bag it was, and the only thing either of them could have done about it was not pick it up.**

They did not thank each other. Nobody has thanked them since.

---

The skip was filled at about one in the afternoon and the last bundle went in at about twenty past and a man of about sixty took the tape off the last bundle and put the roll in his pocket and wrote a number in the book and said the number out loud to nobody, the way he does.

About nine hundred sheets. Nine hundred and four of them are about relay plates and cable runs and valve schedules and fire doors and a heating circuit in a hall, and every one of them is a record of a thing that was done by a person who was not asked to be careful, and every one of them was destroyed correctly.

One of them is a folded sheet with about forty lines on it in a hand, and it is in a canvas bag on a hook behind a door, and it is the only copy in this city, and a bag on a hook behind a door in a building is a thing that can be found by anybody who has a reason to be in a plant room.

A woman of thirty-nine said that in another city in another year, and everybody in that building knew it while it was happening, and nobody has ever written down that they knew it.

---

*200. Tuesday of week 66, one in the afternoon in a yard behind the old continuity hall in Crown Terrace, and this entry is eight bundles, about nine hundred sheets, a strip of tape, a line in a disposal book, and a folded sheet that went the other way.*

*What the round was, as a record and not as a scene.* **Half past six in the morning. A man of about sixty with a trolley, a clipboard, a roll of brown tape and a pair of steel weights. Eight bundles, about nine hundred sheets. Eleven weeks between rounds, the same item, the same three operations. Steel weights on the top sheet, four pages turned, weights back, the number written on the corner in a block hand, brown tape across the corner of the top sheet. A line in the disposal book in the hut: bundle, count, tape, the Tuesday, the item, and an authority column with a job title in it. Every part of it correct, and the man is not a villain and was not asked to be careful and went home at about one.**

*The thing that was authorised, said in the first person, on the Monday, in writing, in one line.* **By the man of fifty-eight with a soft voice, in his own corridor, at about half past six, to a man of fifty-eight in a coat that has never been in a workshop, and he said he authorised it and then said he was going to say it anyway, because the count is a thing he has and a thing he has not said out loud is only a thing he has. His stated reason: a maintenance record has a life, the life is eleven weeks, a chamber under a building has no lock on it, and paper in a locked room is paper in a building nobody can count. And then the sentence that is not a defence and was not meant as one: that he did not sleep badly about it and that a man of fifty-eight telling a corridor he did not sleep is a thing a corridor holds for about four years.**

*What happened to the one sheet, in the order it happened.* **It was in a canvas satchel on a chair in the plant room from the Thursday before, put there by the woman of forty-one out loud in front of the man of fifty-eight in a chamber, and not returned to the drawer. At about half past seven the man with the trolley came to it in the drawer, unfolded it because it was folded, read it quickly, laid it flat on the top of the stack, and bound the bundle. No second reading, no check, no witness, no slip, and no person in that chamber asked to look at anything.**

*The carrying out, as a route and not as an act.* **Up the steel stair, along a service corridor with a fire door at the end of it, forty steps, and then nothing. The records clerk stopped on a second landing. She said she had got it. Her reason was five words, said once, not argued with, and nobody asked her who; the field service engineer said nothing about it then or afterwards, in a corridor, a canteen or a yard. He took the bag off the rail, checked it was not his, and finished the climb and went out through a service door on foot. She stayed on the landing until the door had shut and then went down. Neither thanked the other. The bag belonged to a man who left that contract in the spring and it was on a hook behind a door, and neither of them asked whose it was.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **No relay, no comparison, no practice, and nothing run by him. It does not contain the reading of the forty lines, the other thirty-one of them, the reason the ninth line of nine is the same as the tenth word of the first, the rest of the book, or the name in the hand. It does not contain a form, a minute, an order in a minute, a committee, a notice or a report, and the one line that authorised the round is not reproduced here. It does not say the round was the right thing or the wrong thing, because both of those were true on the same morning and only one of them is in this entry. Entry 200.**
