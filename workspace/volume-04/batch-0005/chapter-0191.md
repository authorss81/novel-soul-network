# Chapter 191 — One Over The Hundred

There is an item on a maintenance schedule in Crown Terrace that runs on eleven weeks, and on the Monday morning of week sixty-four the item was on the yard list, and a strip of brown tape went across the bundle at about seven o'clock, and a line went into a disposal book at about half past seven, and by nine there were eleven of them standing on a service road behind the old continuity hall and about nine hundred pages in the one that was being filled.

Every part of that was correct.

Nobody in this case has ever found a thing that was not correct. That is the sentence a term and a half has been arriving at and nobody has said it out loud, because a correct thing does not need saying, and because the people who say it out loud are usually about to do something else.

---

Petar Vance has been a field service engineer for eleven years. He services the exchange rooms in three districts and he prints the plates, and about nine hundred of the plates in four buildings came off one line of his own company's list and carry a number off a different line of the same list, and he has given that number out loud in about four rooms and nobody has ever asked him for it.

He was in the plant room behind the wall on the Monday morning of week sixty-four with a tool bag because a heating circuit on that floor had been losing pressure since Friday and nobody had written one word about it.

"You have got a skip outside."

"I have got eleven skips outside," said Petar Vance. "**That is a fortnight's worth and they come on a Monday and the one at the end of the row is the one that goes, and it goes empty, and it comes back at about four.**"

"Who fills them."

"A man called Oduya with a trolley and a clipboard, and he counts, and the count goes on the tape and the tape goes across the bundle, and the line goes in the book in the hut, and every one of those three is a thing a person does and not one of them is a thing anybody has to be brave about." He put a gauge on the pipe. "**It is eleven weeks between rounds and it has been eleven weeks for longer than I have been on this contract, and I could not tell you who set it and neither could he, and he does not need to know because it is on a schedule.**"

---

The bundles were standing against the wall in a hut with a corrugated roof and a hatch in the side of it, and there were nine of them, and they were not the same size.

A man of about sixty was at the hatch with a clipboard and a roll of brown tape and a pair of steel weights, and he counted the way a man counts when he has counted a great many things: he put the steel weights on the top sheet, he turned four pages, he put the weights back, he wrote a number, and he said the number out loud to nobody.

"How many in that one."

"**A hundred and six,"** he said.

Petar Vance was on his way out with the tool bag and he stopped, because a hundred and six is not a round number and he had heard nine bundles counted on two different Mondays.

"Run that again."

"The hundred and six is the hundred and six."

"You counted a hundred and six and you taped a hundred and six."

"I counted a hundred and six and I taped a hundred and six," said the man, "**and the tape is on the corner and the corner is on the top sheet and the top sheet is in, so that is a bundle of a hundred and six, and if the tape is on the corner then the corner sheet is in and you have got a hundred and seven sheets in a bundle of a hundred and six and there is not one thing on this site that can tell you which of those two is true until somebody weighs it.**"

---

Nobody weighed it. That is the whole of it and it took about four minutes and it was done by four people who each did the right thing.

The count came off the clipboard and went onto the tape in the corner in a block hand. The line went into the disposal book in the hut: bundle nine, a hundred and six, tape, the Monday, the item, and then a column with a word at the top of it that said *authority* and in that column a job title went in, because a job title is what a man can write without stopping to think about who he is. The bundle went into the skip behind the wall.

And when the man with the trolley took the fourth bundle in, the top four sheets went over on the corner, and one of them came out of the stack and went down flat on the concrete, face up, in about nine hundred sheets' worth of other people's paperwork.

Petar Vance picked it up because it was face up and he was going that way.

---

It is a service sheet. It is about the same age as a school exercise book and it has got a printed head on it with a box in the top corner that says a site and a system and a location, and the location is not a room number, it is a description: *continuity hall, sub-level plant, chamber 3*.

The line for the item runs down the left of it in a printed column, and there are eleven dates in that column going back a year and about four months, and every one of the eleven has a small mark beside it, and every one of the eleven marks is the same small mark.

He turned it over.

There is nothing on the back.

He read the front again and then he stood in a service corridor with a tool bag at his feet and a sheet of paper in his hand, and the corridor had four doors in it and none of them went anywhere he needed to go, and the door at the end of it was a fire door with a bar on it.

---

He is the only person in this city who can say what a plate belongs to, and he is fifty-one and it is not a thing anybody has ever asked him to say.

"The chamber 3 is not in any of the plans I have seen for that building," he said. "**I have got four of those plans and three of them are contractors' and one of them is a survey from before the hall was a hall, and I have put a wrong number on about nine hundred doors in four buildings and I cannot change nine hundred doors because a district nurse wants one room, and that is the largest thing I have ever been responsible for and it is a list I did not write.**"

"Does your company maintain the plant down there."

"My company maintains the plant in the hall. There is no company contract in this city for anything in a tunnel, because a tunnel is under a building and a building is a landlord, and I have a service list and a contract and a man's own schedule and that is all I have ever had, and I have never once asked for a schedule of the tunnel because a schedule of the tunnel would be a document and a document would be a thing somebody could ask me about." He put the sheet on the sill. "**That is the first time in eleven years I have said that sentence out loud and I have said it to a door.**"

---

He put the sheet back.

That is worth saying plainly because it is the whole of the finding and it took him about eleven seconds: he put it back into the skip, on top of about nine hundred other people's records, and he squared the four sheets whose corner had gone over, and he did not take a photograph of it, and he did not copy it, and nobody watched him do either of those things because nobody was there.

What he did do was write a number in his own book, at about ten past nine, in the margin, in his own hand: *ch 3, sub-level, wk 64*. Four words and two letters and a week. It is the first thing he has put in that book in about four years that is not a part, and his book is his own and it goes in a van and it is not on a system and nobody else has ever held it.

"And that is going to be the only copy of it in about four years unless somebody else writes it down," he said later, in the corridor, to a man of twenty-two who had a fault on a docket in that building and had been standing at the other end of it since about ten.

"You could have taken it."

"I could have taken it. **I have got a tool bag and a van and a company card and I could have put a page in my pocket and taken it out of this city tonight, and that would have been the end of it, and in about four years there would be a man in a shed with a page in a drawer and no way of telling anybody why he had it.**" He closed the book. "It is on a schedule, and there is a strip of tape, and there is a line in a book in a hut, and every one of those three is a thing a person did, and the page is going to be destroyed because that is what the page is for, and if it goes then it goes and somebody will have to find it again."

---

The line in the disposal book is still there. The bundle number is nine, the count is a hundred and six, the tape went on the corner, and the authority column has a job title in it, and the man who wrote the job title did not put his own name there and was not asked to and would not have known what to write if he had been.

The last bundle of that round went out on the far side of the city at about four o'clock in the afternoon, and it was one of about nine hundred sheets, and there is no mechanism in this city that can bring a sheet back out of a container once it has gone in, and there is one that can take a sheet out of a skip on a Monday morning if a man with a tool bag is going that way.

That is the whole of what happened on the Monday morning of week sixty-four and it is eleven weeks long and it happens again, and nobody has to be brave about any part of it.

---

*194. Monday of week 64, nine in the morning in a service corridor behind the old continuity hall in Crown Terrace, and this entry is an eleven-week item on a maintenance schedule, a strip of tape across the corner of a bundle, a line in a disposal book in a hut, and about nine hundred pages going over a wall into a container.*

*What the schedule is.* **An item running on eleven weeks, on a printed sheet with a location in a box in the top corner described in words and not by a room number: continuity hall, sub-level plant, chamber 3. Eleven dated marks down a printed column going back a year and about four months, all the same mark, on one sheet. Nothing on the reverse. Nine bundles against a wall in a hut, counted by a man of about sixty with a clipboard, steel weights and a roll of brown tape.**

*Why one page was in a bundle of a hundred and six, as a record and not as a scene.* **He counted a hundred and six, wrote it on the corner of the top sheet, and taped the corner. Both operations are correct and either one on its own puts that sheet in the bundle. A bundle of a hundred and six with a hundred and seven sheets in it is not an error anybody can see from the outside, and this entry does not say how the seventh sheet got under the corner, because nobody looked and nobody was going to.**

*What the line in the book contains, and what the entry keeps apart.* **Bundle nine. A hundred and six. The tape. The Monday. The item. And an authority column with a job title in it, written by a man who was not asked for his name and would not have known the entry for it. This book is in a hut in a yard and is not the bound register that stands on a table in Crown Terrace in four columns; the nine hundred in that one are lines and the nine hundred in this one are pages, and they are two objects and neither of them is the other.**

*What a field service engineer of fifty-one said out loud, and the number in his own hand.* **That chamber 3 is in no plan he has seen for that building, out of four plans he holds; that he has put a wrong number on about nine hundred doors in four buildings off one line of his own company's list and cannot change nine hundred doors because a district nurse wants one room; and that no company in this city holds a contract for anything in a tunnel, because a tunnel is under a building and a building is a landlord. He put the page back where it had come out of and squared the four sheets that had gone over, and took no photograph and made no copy, and the only thing he made was four words and a week in the margin of his own book, which is the first entry in four years in it that is not a part.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **It does not contain a person taking a page out of that container and keeping it, and there is no relay and no carrier in this entry and nobody carried anything out of a yard. It does not contain the interior of the chamber, the schedule that governs it, the man who set the eleven weeks, or the number of bundles in the fortnight that were not on this list. This entry does not treat that object as a place where a record is lost, because in this entry it is a place where one was found, and the other direction on the same object belongs to other people on other days and is not argued about here. Entry 194.**
