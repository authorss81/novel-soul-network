# Chapter 200 — A Card In A Rack

There is a rail of card slots along the end of a counter in a room off a street in Lattice Ward, and about nine people have put a card in it over nine years, and the cards have a person's name on one side and a day on the other, and the woman who runs that room has never once taken one of them out.

She took one out on the Thursday of week sixty-seven, and it was the first, and she was not at work.

"There is nobody at this counter today," she said, before anybody asked. "**I come on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays, and today is a Thursday, and I am here because I put a folder on the high shelf of that trolley on Wednesday and I have been thinking about it for two days like an unreasonable person, and I would like it known that I came on my day off to look at a shelf.**"

---

There were five people in that room. A woman of forty-one who had come on her day off. A man of forty-four who had put a number on a tray in a fortnight and could not get it off. A woman of forty-six with a trolley and a printed list and nineteen years of a condition sheet at the bottom of it. A courier of twenty-one with three practices in three hands on a sheet of typing paper in a drawer and a worse answer he prefers. And a man of twenty-two with a docket and a piece of card about the size of a playing card.

None of the other four had been asked to be there and none of them knew the other three.

---

He put the card in at about eleven in the morning and he did it in his own hand and he did it on purpose and he did it on the worst possible day in the week, when there was no counter staff and no bell within reach of anybody's hand and nobody in the room who had to do anything at all.

It has one thing written on the front of it. **A name, in a hand that gets smaller towards the end of it, and a week at the bottom left in the same hand.** There is no title on it and no office attached to it and there is a woman of thirty-one with a key to a room in another building who did not agree to anything and is not on it, and there is no office in this city that he is attached to by it.

He turned it over before he put it in. There is nothing on the back of it.

**It is renewable, by him, in his own hand, without asking anybody, by writing the next week on it. It is removable by a clerk, because a clerk can take it out of a rail. Nobody has to do a form about it and nobody has to be told and if he stops coming the card stays until somebody who works there decides to take it down, and that is the whole mechanism and it is a drawer with a rail on the end of it.**

Then he said the reason out loud, to four people, because they were there, and he said it once and he did not improve it.

"**A ledger nobody may read is a ledger a person owns, and a ledger a person owns dies with the person. There is a folder on the high shelf of that trolley and about forty lines on it in a hand, and I did not read it and neither did two of the four people in that chamber, and the only copy in this city is on a shelf in a room anybody can walk into, and if it stays with one person then the day that person stops coming it is in a drawer in a flat and then it is a skip, and I would rather it was a card in a rail in a room off a street.**"

---

"That does not help," said Corin Vasska.

"No."

"I want to be exact about it, because I am the man who has spent four years reading a specification out loud to a room and I have got a rule about a thing being true." He did not put his hands in his pockets. "**That is a true sentence. It is also a sentence that puts your name on the front of a piece of paper in a building with a plate on the outside of it, and in about four years the only thing anybody in this city will be able to say about the case is where to find you, and about eleven people a week are being taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and they have been being taught that since the spring before last, and they are going to be taught it next Friday.**"

"I know the number."

"Then do the arithmetic out loud, because you are the only person in this city who has ever done it on purpose." He waited. "**Eleven a week, and about four hundred and thirty-two cards that cannot be taken off anybody, and a hundred and forty-one pounds under a till that cannot move, and a rule in a specification that cannot be changed, and now a rack. Say what you have bought and what you have sold and do not do it in a room with a microphone on it, because there is not one and that is the only good news available.**"

---

So he said it, and it took him about nine seconds, and there were four people listening to it and one of them was a courier of twenty-one who had a grey card in his own pocket and had not said anything all morning and was not asked anything now.

"**About eleven people a week are going to go on waiting for me, and they have been waiting since the spring before last, and about four of the eleven are not even on a rota, and none of that is because of anything I did. I have traded the last thing I had, which is that nobody could find me, and a man of fifty-eight in a corridor told me a term and a half ago that a person who says a thing is a person who can be asked whether he said it, and he was right, and I have done it anyway, and the arithmetic is mine and I am not going to hand it to a fund.**"

Nobody said that it was the right thing. A woman of forty-six with a trolley said that she had walked four buildings for nineteen years and that the only time anybody has ever asked her a question her own sheet could not answer was about a corridor in the week of the fifty-two, and that nobody thanked her then and nobody has since.

---

Then the woman of forty-one took the card out of the rail and put it on the counter, and that is the first time in nine years that a card has come out of that rail, and she did it with two fingers and she did it slowly.

"**I am not handing this to anybody who has not filled in a slip.** That is the rule and it is older than me, and a slip is a piece of paper and a person, and I am going to keep it in the tray with the fifty-eight, and if you want it out you come with a slip, and if you want it to stop you come with a slip, and I have never let anybody into this room or out of it without one of those two and I am not starting with a man who is good at rooms."

Nobody had an answer to that one, and the man of forty-four said that it was the only thing anybody had said in the room that morning that he could not improve, and the man of twenty-two said that he was not going to argue with it, and that was the end of the sentence.

---

"Who has the folder on the high shelf."

"Nobody has it," said Marek Senn. "**It is on a shelf in a room off a street in Lattice Ward, and the woman who runs that room works three days a week and on Thursday she is not there, and the last person to put anything on that shelf was a records clerk of forty-one who carried it out of a building under Crown Terrace on her own time and is not a post, and the one before her is a woman of forty-four with a metal braid of tools who read the page and would not say why, and the one before her is a woman of forty-five who read the same page and said nine words about it and went back to work.**"

"That is three people and a shelf."

"That is three people and a shelf, and a folder, and no form, and no signature, and no office, and a woman of forty-one who has a rule about slips and has not been asked for one." He turned the card round on the counter. "**And the binder with the page stapled into it is a different object and it is three shelves down from that one, and I am not going to be the man who puts those two in the same sentence, because the day they are in the same sentence somebody will ask which one is a record and the answer will be whichever one a person is standing next to.**"

---

**A rack is not a rota, and nobody in that room said otherwise.**

A man of forty-four had read five jobs off the front of a leaflet ten Fridays and then had stopped, in a room, with a reason. A man of fifty-two had come to a session and not spoken. A man of fifty-eight had retained an authority in a corridor in the first person. A man of fifty-one had asked for a microphone to be switched off three times and had not been answered. A man of twenty-one had three practices in three hands on a sheet of typing paper in a drawer and had said no to being a name at the top of anything and had not changed his mind, and nobody in that room asked him whether he had, and nobody was going to.

And a rack of about nine cards in nine years, of which eight say a name and a day and mean nothing at all, and one of them has a week on it instead of a day, and there is not one item on a list of about nine thousand anywhere in this city that says a man of twenty-two may come into a room off a street and read a folder, and the reason he may is that anybody may.

---

At ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward the shutter was up and the lamp was on over the bench and the docket had nine job numbers on it in a shorthand nobody else in this city can read, and the word had been at nine all afternoon and at ten by the time he sat down.

He has five things under the bench and four of them are sentences other people wrote, and the fifth one carries a reference number and a closing line that says it is not to be produced unless somebody asks that office first, and in a term and a half nobody has ever asked that office for anything at all. It is a drawer that has never held a thing that could be handed to a person in a room, and it is where it was before the summer, and he did not open it tonight.

The original has lain in the fourth pocket of that tool roll for a term and a half, and there has never been a fifth line on it, and nobody has ever held out a hand and asked him for one. On the back of a chair is a jacket with a corner of card about two inches by three in the inside pocket of it, a photocopy of a photocopy, and the decision about it is exactly where it was before the summer and it was not made today and nobody held out a hand for it.

Those are two different objects and they were not confused today, and the card that went into a rail in Lattice Ward is a third one and is about the size of a playing card and has a name on the front of it and nothing at all on the back.

---

And then the rest of it, which is what there is.

On a trolley against a wall, a high shelf, a binder with a page stapled into it, and the staple is older than the print around it, and three shelves down on the same trolley a public copy in print that says every technique must show its exit, and about forty people in nine years who have asked to see a binder in that room and not one of them who has asked for that one.

On a building plan rolled in a drawer on a hook in a basement, a date in ink, and room 2-07.

On a wall in a corridor, a service plate in a contractor's typeface, and about nine hundred of them in four buildings.

A trolley with four sheets on it, and pencil on the back of the week's own sheet in the space where the conditions go.

A hundred and forty-one pounds in an envelope under a till with no name on it, which cannot move, and which stood on a coat hook in the spring and is under the till now, and the only reason anybody has is that water got into the back of that shop over the summer and the hook was above the damp.

A box in pencil on a counter with a woman's name in it, which cannot come out, and a woman of thirty-one with a diary who is not thanked, and a clerk of fifty-three who ruled it and has not mentioned her since.

A book in a back room with forty-four lines in it, the fortieth of them three years old, a hole in line forty-four, and no caller.

A care worker of thirty-eight who will not sign her own words, and whose name is at the bottom of a slip in a tray because a woman of forty-one wrote it and not her.

A man of fifty-eight with a soft voice and a count, who has declined three things in fourteen years, retained an authority out loud in a corridor in the first person, agreed to nothing and refused nothing, and is not defeated and is not turned.

A man of fifty-one who has been rung, and who says the thing about the call, and whom nobody writes down.

A courier of twenty-one with three practices in three hands and a worse answer he prefers, who was in a room that morning and was not asked anything.

And about eleven people a week still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and there is a rack now, and **a rack is not a rota, and nobody in that room said otherwise.**
