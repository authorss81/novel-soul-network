# Chapter 199 — Retained

On the ground floor of a building in Crown Terrace there is a table with a book on it and nobody behind the table on a Thursday. On the Monday morning of week sixty-seven there was a woman of thirty-four behind it in a cardigan with a cup of tea and a folder she had not opened. The book is bound in a canvas cover and ruled into four columns and it holds about nine hundred lines, and the oldest line on the first page is from the week of week thirty-eight. There is no rope round it and there is no sign.

She reads a line out to anybody who asks. She said that before anybody asked, and the reason she gives is that it is a public document and that is her whole view.

Nobody has ever been on that floor on a Thursday. On this Monday there were three people on it and one of them was in a corridor above it and had come down.

---

The fourth column is a job title in every one of about nine hundred lines and there has never once been a person's name in it.

"I am going to ask you the thing I said I would ask."

"You said you would ask me what I think of the fourth column."

"No," said the woman of thirty-four. "**I said that if you came back I was to ask you the first one. I have not asked you the first one and I am not going to, and I want you to know that I noticed.**"

"Ask me the second one."

"The second one is whether the register is going to be moved," she said. "**And I have asked it in a corridor and not at my table, and I have a folder I have not opened, and I have had four years of standing in front of this and I am going to be honest with you about which half of it is mine, and it is mine, and not yours, and I have a table and a bell and about four hundred cards in four drawers and about two hundred people have come through that door since the spring.**"

---

He came down at about half past ten and he did not sit down, and he did not answer the question, and he did not refuse it either.

"**I am not going to tell you the register is staying, and I am not going to tell you it is going, and the reason I am not going to do either of those is that the answer to the first one is not mine and the answer to the second one is a thing about a Tuesday, and I have been in this building for fourteen years and I have watched two offices in it learn that the answer to everything is yes until about four years ago.**"

"That is not an answer."

"It is not an answer and I have given it anyway, and I am going to give you one more thing, and I am only going to give it to you because you have not asked me anything for four years." He put his hands behind his back. "**There were eight bundles in a yard behind this building on the Tuesday morning of week sixty-six and about nine hundred sheets went into a skip and one of them was a folded leaf that came out of a drawer that a man of about sixty unfolded because it was folded, and a records clerk of forty-one carried it out of this building on her own time and put it on a high shelf in a room off a street in Lattice Ward, and I have known that since the Wednesday, and I am not going to ask you for it, and the number of people in this city who could tell me where it is is two, and neither of them works for me.**"

The woman of thirty-four put her pen down.

"You have known since the Wednesday."

"I have known since the Wednesday."

"And you have not asked for it."

"**And I am not going to, and I want the reason on the record in your voice rather than mine, so that it is a thing a room has heard: because a page that has left a building is a page that somebody is carrying, and the second that an office asks for a page is a man in a corridor who has a piece of paper to hand to a person, and then the person is findable, and I have spent fourteen years declining two things in writing and both of the things were about not making a person findable, and I am not going to do it a third time in a fortnight.**"

---

Nobody minuted that, and there is no minute, and there is nobody whose job it is to take a minute of it.

A man of twenty-two was on the landing at the foot of that stair. He had been on a fault in that building since eight in the morning and he had been in a corridor for the whole of it, and he asked one question, and it was the only one he asked, and it was asked badly.

"Does the requirement still stand."

"That is the third question anybody has asked me in fourteen years and the first one that has been the real one," said Iven Sore. "**It stands, it has stood for the whole fourteen years, nothing in this city has ever withdrawn it, and the only way it could be withdrawn is by somebody who can be asked whether they intend to, and there is no such person, and I have been in this building every working morning for fourteen years and nobody has ever asked me whether I am it.**"

"You could be."

"I could be. **That is a sentence about a person and not about a post, and nobody has ever offered me the post and I would decline it in writing, and the reason I would decline it is that the day there is a person whose job is the requirement then the day a person is unhappy about the requirement they have to complain to the requirement, and about nine hundred lines in a book downstairs have a job title in the fourth column instead of a name and that has been the arrangement for fourteen years and it has worked exactly as well as it looks.**"

---

Then he said the other thing, out loud, in the corridor, to the three of them, standing up, and he said it as a man and not as an office.

"**I am retaining emergency authority in this city and I am telling you that in a corridor, in the first person, on the Monday morning of week sixty-seven, and I am not going to soften it and I am not going to put it in a form.**"

"Why in a corridor."

"Because **a form of that is a form, and a form can be produced by anybody who has ever seen one, and in about four years somebody is going to write down that the Continuity Office retained emergency authority on a Thursday, and it will be true, and it will be the only true sentence anybody in this city has ever had about me, and I would rather it was said by three people in a corridor on a Monday and that it is a man saying it and not a stamp.**"

"And you are not going to do anything with the nine requests."

"I have not seen nine requests," said Iven Sore. "**I have seen a tray and I have been told about a tray by a woman of forty-one and I have not been asked anything and I have agreed to nothing and I have refused nothing, and those two are not the same and there is nobody in this city who has to make them the same, and if a person comes to me on a Monday and asks me to look at a book I will not say no, and if a person comes to me on a Monday and asks me to leave a book alone I will not say yes, and I have been like this for fourteen years and I have not been asked to be like this.**"

---

There is a register on a table downstairs with about nine hundred lines in it and a column at the top of it that says *authority*, and the answer to who can withdraw a rule in this city is still a word at the top of a column, and a column heading cannot be asked a question, and about nine people in this city have put a slip with their own name at the bottom of it into a tray in a room off a street and none of them has been answered and nobody has adopted any of them.

The Office agreed to nothing on the Monday morning of week sixty-seven. It refused nothing. It lost nothing. A man of fifty-eight was in a corridor and said one sentence with a count in it, and that sentence is the whole of what changed in this building in a term, and there is a folder on a high shelf in another part of the city that nobody has read.

---

The docket was face up under the lamp with the word still on it from nine in the morning, and there is a jacket on the back of a chair and a piece of card about two inches by three in the inside pocket of it, and the original has lain in the fourth pocket of the tool roll for a term and a half with nothing on its fifth line, and a drawer under the bench holds five things.

He wrote down one number, which was three, because there were three people on that floor, and he wrote down no names, because he has been doing that for a term and a half.

---

*202. Monday of week 67, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a bound register on a table with nobody behind it, a man of fifty-eight in a corridor saying one sentence with a count in it, and an authority retained by a person.*

*The floor.* **Ground floor of a building in Crown Terrace. A bound register in a canvas cover, ruled into four columns, about nine hundred lines, the oldest of them from the week of week thirty-eight, which is fourteen years back. No rope round it and no sign. A woman of thirty-four behind it with a cardigan and a tea and a folder she did not open, reading a line out to anybody who asks, which she says before she is asked. The fourth column is a job title in every line and has never had a name in it. Three people on the floor, and nobody has ever been on it on a Thursday, and one of the three was in the corridor above it.**

*What was asked, and what was not answered.* **She said she had not asked him the first question she had promised to ask him and that she had noticed, and then asked the second one: whether the register is going to be moved. She said, before she asked, that she was the frightened one and not him. He answered neither way and gave the reason: the answer to it staying is not his and the answer to it going is a thing about a Tuesday, and two offices in that building learned that the answer to everything is yes until about four years ago. He said afterwards that it was not an answer and that he had given it anyway.**

*The one sentence with the count in it, given because nobody had asked him anything for four years.* **Eight bundles in a yard behind that building on the Tuesday of week sixty-six, about nine hundred sheets into a skip, one of them a folded leaf that came out of a drawer because a man of about sixty unfolded it, carried out by a records clerk of forty-one on her own time and put on a high shelf in a room off a street in Lattice Ward; he has known since the Wednesday; he is not going to ask for it; the number of people who could tell him where it is is two and neither of them works for him. And the reason, which he asked her to say in her own voice so that it would be a thing a room had heard: a page that has left a building is a page somebody is carrying, and the second an office asks for a page is a man in a corridor with a piece of paper to hand to a person, and then the person is findable, and he has declined two things in writing in fourteen years and both were about not making a person findable and he is not going to do it a third time in a fortnight.**

*What was retained, and by whom, in the first person.* **Emergency authority, retained, stated standing up in a corridor, in the first person, on a Monday morning, and refused a form on the stated ground that a form can be produced by anybody who has ever seen one and that in about four years a true sentence about him will exist and he would rather it were said by three people in a corridor than by a stamp. He also said, in the same corridor, that nobody has ever asked him whether he is the authority over anything, that there is no such person, that he would decline the post in writing, and that the fourth column of about nine hundred lines has held a job title instead of a name for fourteen years and has worked exactly as well as it looks.**

*Agreed to nothing, refused nothing.* **Stated by him and demonstrated on the page: he has not seen nine requests, has been told about a tray by a woman of forty-one, has been asked nothing, and would neither refuse a request to look at a book nor accept a request to leave one alone, and has not been asked to be like this in fourteen years. The Office lost nothing and a folder on a high shelf has not been read and the nine named slips in a tray remain unanswered. The man of twenty-two was at the foot of the stairs on a fault, asked one question badly, was told the requirement stands and cannot be withdrawn, and wrote down the number three and no names.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **No opening of a record, no reopening of a file, no admission, no agreement, no minute, no notice, no report, no committee, no leaflet and no post. It does not contain a defeat, and it does not contain a turn, and it does not say that a man of fifty-eight is a good man, and the only sentence in this entry that is new is one with a count in it. Entry 202.**
