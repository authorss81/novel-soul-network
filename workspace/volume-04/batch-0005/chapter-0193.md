# Chapter 193 — The Fourth Location

The Friday of week sixty-four had eleven jobs on it and nine of them in date, and he was on grade three by eight in the morning, and the word came at nine a minute until about one and at ten after one, and the count is on the back of a docket in a jacket on a chair in Lattice Ward.

Nobody on that card asked him where a release was going to come from, and it is not a number he keeps.

There were four locations on a docket of his own in his own shorthand and on nothing else.

Three of them were what they had been five weeks ago. The fourth was not, and there was no version of the Friday in which that turned into a conversation.

---

He got to the block off the service road at about twenty past ten and the stair window on the second landing had a new frame in it.

It is a different profile. The old one was a steel frame with a putty line and the new one is a uPVC sash with a rubber gasket, and the glass in it is thicker and greener, and the sill has a plastic trim on it, and it is a better window. It was measured and ordered on the Monday and fitted on the Wednesday in about four hours by two men and a boy, and the man who ordered it did it because the old frame was soft at the bottom and let water in when it rained hard, and he did not know what was behind the paint on the inside of the reveal and neither did the boy, and the frame came out whole.

"That is a new window," said Marek Senn.

"It is a new window," said the man of thirty-eight in a district jacket. "**It came out on the Wednesday in about four hours. I was in the building. I put my hand on the old sill on the Monday to see if it was soft and it was, and I did not know there was anything on that reveal at all, and neither did the fitter, and I have written on a job sheet that the window was replaced and I have written nothing else.**"

---

There was a groove in that reveal the width of two fingers running down to the floor and away from the door, and it was the same age as the old frame, and it went out with the old frame in a skip on the Wednesday afternoon.

The record of it is fourteen lines in pencil, put on the back of a condition sheet five weeks ago by a woman of forty-six. That sheet is in a ring file. The ring file is in a cupboard in that basement. Nobody has ever opened the cupboard, and there is nothing else anywhere in this city about that groove, and there never was.

"So that is the second one gone," said the man of thirty-eight. "**I had two. One is under four coats of paint and one of them is in a skip on the far side of the city, and the one on the third floor is the one I put a torch on twice and never wrote down, and you told me to go and look at the second floor and you were right and you did not go.**"

---

He said that on a landing in the middle of the morning, in about nine words, to a man of twenty-two who had a ballast in the same block and who had told him the same thing nine weeks before.

"Go and look at the third floor before you write anything on that sheet."

"I have got eleven jobs and a Friday."

"**You said that on the Friday before last about the second floor and you said it word for word, and I have written it down because I was standing on the landing when you said it, and I am not asking you to have a better reason this time, I am telling you that I have got two sentences from you on this floor and they are the same sentence.**"

"And I have not got a better one."

"No, and I did not think you had, and that is not why I am saying it." He turned the job sheet over. "**Say it again if you like. I have got a file on you in a drawer that is not mine and a district and no way of getting it anywhere, and the only thing I have ever wanted off you is a sentence I can put in front of somebody, and you are the only man in this city who gives me the same one twice and I would like to know what that is worth.**"

---

He did not say it again, and he did not go and look at the third floor, and the reason he did not is in a ring file in a cupboard and not on the page.

What he did do, at about half past eleven, was go and find the woman whose sheet it was, because somebody had asked about the pencil and she was the one who had put it there, and the whole of what he got for that was a sentence about a woman of thirty-six.

---

Ines Kolar is forty-six and she walks four buildings every Thursday with a trolley and a printed list, and she has signed a condition sheet at the bottom of for nineteen years, and that sheet has never once been asked to mean anything.

She was in a basement on the Friday morning of week sixty-four with the trolley against a wall and a ring file open on her knee.

"About four weeks ago," she said, before he had got to it. "**A woman of thirty-six does the sheets up. She turns every one of them over because everybody does, and she turned that one over and read the fourteen lines and came and found me at the counter and asked me what the pencil was.**"

"What did you say."

"I said I would find out." She put the file down on the trolley. "**And I have had four weeks to find out what I am going to say and I have not got it, and I have not told her, and she has not asked me again, and I would like it recorded that she has not asked me again, because a woman who asks twice is doing something different from a woman who asks once.**"

"You could tell her."

"I could tell her what the pencil is. It is fourteen lines about eleven rooms where a sheet and a door do not say the same number, and it is in pencil because I do not have a machine in that basement and I did not want to go up and get a pen." She said it to the trolley. "**And the reason I have not told her is that the day I tell her, that woman is the person in this city who did not turn a sheet over, and in about four years somebody is going to want to know how she knew, and the only version of that anybody will have is me and a trolley.**"

---

"And there is the other half of it," she said, "and I am going to say it because you have come down here on a Friday and there is a woman of thirty-six upstairs who is owed an answer by lunchtime on some other Friday."

"**There is no column on that sheet for what is on the door. There are forty-one lines and a column of room numbers and a column for conditions, and a man of twenty-two asked me a question in a corridor in the week of the fifty-two and I put eleven more rooms in pencil in the space where the conditions go, because there was nowhere else, and the sheet went back on the trolley and it went to a building and nobody has asked for it and nobody is going to.**"

"Who asked you the question."

"A man of twenty-two," said Ines Kolar. "**And I am not going to be asked to say his name to explain the pencil, and if she asks me on another Friday I am going to tell her what the pencil is and stop there, and that is the whole of what I have got and I have been walking four buildings for nineteen years and it is the first time the answer has been about a thing I did.**"

---

He had a ballast at four o'clock in the same block and he did it in nine minutes, and he came out of the stairwell and looked at the new frame on the second landing on the way past, and it is a better window and it is what somebody asked for.

There is a thing a person can leave a building by and it is a groove somebody cut into a surface on purpose in a place you can find it in the dark with one hand, and there was one on that landing for about forty years and it went into a skip on the Wednesday afternoon with a frame and a load of putty, and the man who fitted the new one did nine seconds of work he was not asked about and he is not going to be asked about anything ever.

About eleven people a week are still going to be taught to wait for a man in a lanyard. That number is not a rate and it is not on a form and it is on the back of a woman's own card, and on this Friday nobody said it out loud to anybody.

---

At ten at night the docket was on the bench under the lamp with nine job numbers in a shorthand nobody else in this city can read, and the parts cage was in the corner, and the shutter was down.

Under the bench there is a drawer with five things in it, four of them sentences that other people wrote, and one of them a letter with a reference on it whose own closing line asks not to be produced without that office being asked first, and no proceeding exists, and it is a drawer that has never held anything a person could produce in a room.

He wrote down four locations. Three of them were the three that had not changed and the fourth was a new window, and he did not write down the groove, because there was nothing to write it on, and that was the ninth week in a row he had been in that stairwell and there was a groove in about a quarter of a term of them.

---

*196. Friday of week 64, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is a window that was replaced correctly, a woman of forty-six with a ring file, and a sentence a man gave two people on two Fridays in nine weeks.*

*The docket, as a count.* **Eleven jobs, nine in date, grade three from eight, the word at nine a minute until about one and at ten after one. Four locations in his own shorthand and on nothing else. Three unchanged at five weeks. The fourth has gone: somebody cut a groove into the inside of a stair window about forty years ago, it was two fingers wide, it had been painted over since the summer of the fifty-fifth, and it is no longer in that building, because the frame came out whole on the Wednesday of week sixty-four in about four hours, correctly ordered, correctly measured, correctly fitted, and the groove went out with it in a skip. Nobody knew what was behind the paint on the reveal and the fitter did not know and the man of thirty-eight who ordered the window did not know, and the job sheet says the window was replaced and nothing else.**

*The only record of the groove, stated as an object.* **Fourteen lines in pencil on the back of a condition sheet, in a ring file, in a cupboard that has never been opened, put there by a woman of forty-six in the week of the fifty, in the space where the conditions go because the sheet has no column for what is on a door. That is the whole of the record in this city and it has been the whole of it for about five weeks. This entry does not reproduce the fourteen lines and does not say who asked the question that put them there by name, because a woman of forty-six has declined to be asked for it and is not going to be asked again.**

*The refusal, which is the same refusal.* **A man of thirty-eight in a district jacket asked him to go and look at the third floor before writing anything on a sheet. He gave the sentence he gave nine weeks before, word for word, about eleven jobs and a Friday. The man of thirty-eight told him he had written it down because he was standing on the landing, said he was not asking for a better reason, and gave his own reason for wanting it: two sentences from the same man on the same floor, identical, and a file in a drawer that is not his, and a district, and no way of getting it anywhere. Nobody improved either sentence.**

*The refusal by a woman of forty-six, which is the finding of the day.* **A woman of thirty-six who does the sheets up turned one over because everybody does, read the pencil, and asked what it was, about four weeks before this Friday. She has not asked again. The reason she has not answered: that the day she answers, that woman is the person in this city who did not turn a sheet over, and in about four years somebody will want to know how she knew and the only version available will be a woman with a trolley. And the other half, said on a Friday in a basement: she would not be asked to give a man's name to explain the pencil, and if she is asked again she will say what the pencil is and stop there. Neither of the two women was thanked and neither asked for anything.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **No third floor, no second floor, no second look, no writing down of a groove, and no photograph of a wall. The rota ran and nobody was asked about a release and no number was kept. The door that was a groove in a wall five weeks ago is a window, and that is the correct action, and it changed nothing, and the man who did it is not going to be asked about it. Entry 196.**
