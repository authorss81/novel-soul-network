# Chapter 196 — The Eleventh

The Friday of week sixty-five was the last of the eleven, and there were thirty-one chairs in that room in Crown Terrace and a number under them, and a man of forty-four who has been an officer of a fund for four years said the number out loud at a quarter to ten, which is what he does.

No minute exists and none was made. There has never been a minute of that figure in four years and there is not going to be one now.

---

"**Thirty-one and fourteen.** That is lower than any session in four years and I have never once put it in a minute and I am not going to put this one in a minute either, and the reason is that a minute is a thing a committee reads and this committee does not meet again, and I would be writing a number down for about nine people who will never be in a room together again."

"The reason it is fourteen."

"**The reason is a Friday at half past nine at night, and about eleven thousand premises that pay for this series and shut at six.** And I gave that out loud four Fridays ago in this room and I am not going to give it again, because a reason given twice is a habit and this term has had about four of those in it already." He put the leaflet face down on the desk. "And there is a second reason and it is new and it is about me, and I am going to give it once."

"Go on."

"**A series of eleven that ends because a man of forty-four read a sheet out loud eleven times is not a series. It is a habit. And I have been the habit, and the eleven was a number somebody put in a specification section three years ago, and the specification does not expire and the levy does not stop and the sessions stop, and nothing in this city is going to write down what replaces them, and I have decided that is better than the alternative.**"

---

He read the third line out, as the third line, at about ten past ten, and about nine people in that room did not hear the difference, which is the fifth time that has happened and the third time this term.

The specification runs to four sections and three lines. The third one is funded. A committee of about nine met once in the week of the thirty-seventh, was told two things, settled nothing, and is not going to meet again.

He did not read the five jobs off the front of the leaflet. There were about four hundred of those leaflets by the door and two of the five jobs on the front of them are his, put there by a system in a company out of two job sheets in the week of the thirty-fourth, and he read that sheet out loud ten Fridays and then, four Fridays ago, in front of everybody, said that he was not going to read it an eleventh time.

The reason he gave that afternoon is on the record and it is not repeated here. It is the sentence about about eleven people a week, and it is in a room in a batch a term ago, and a reason that gets said twice stops being a reason and becomes a thing he says at people.

Nobody asked him to read it tonight. A man in the back row said the session was the last one and would he mind reading the times and the fee for whoever came to the door, and he said no, and said why in one sentence, and the sentence was that reading out a fee to people who have already walked in is a thing a person does to feel useful.

---

A man of fifty-two came and sat in the second row and did not speak.

He has been the depot manager for four years and he has been putting an exception forward for renewal since the start of the term and he has read the box about four hundred times and has never once put a reason in it, and four Fridays ago he stood up in this room with a microphone on a stand and said out loud, once, that he had stopped asking whether anybody was going to offer to take the wording of a condition to somebody who could change it.

He did not say it again tonight. He came at half past nine and he left at about eleven and he did not put a hand up and he did not look at the front of the room once, and a man of twenty-two who was sitting in the fourth row with his coat over the back of the chair in front of him noticed both of those things and did not use either of them.

Coming was the whole of it. Nobody has ever written that down in this city and this entry is not going to.

---

Two people said something other than their own business and neither of them asked him anything.

A woman of twenty-nine came in at about twenty to eleven with a card in her wallet and sixteen Fridays written on the back of it in her own hand, and she has carried that card since the spring and has never totalled the sixteen because a total is a number somebody can quote.

"I am on the Friday list," she said, "**and it is not because anybody chose me, it is because an employer pays for the card, and there are about four of the eleven people a week who are not on the rota at all because of that, and I have been on the wrong side of that particular line for six years and I have never once been asked to say so out loud and I have said it now to a room and not to a man, and that is the whole of what I came for.**"

"You have not said what the sixteen is."

"**The sixteen is the number of Fridays I have been on this card and I have not written what it is for, because the day I write what it is for then it is a claim and a claim is a thing somebody can ask me to give up, and I would like to keep the sixteen as sixteen for as long as the card lasts.**"

---

A woman of twenty-six was at her table by the wall and she has been to all eleven and she has not stood up in any of them, and at a quarter to midnight she put a hand on the folder behind her and said the numbers out loud to nobody in particular.

"**There are eleven letters in this folder. There are nine answers written. I have not sent one of them and I did not send one of the five I had written a month ago, and the ninth is the best of the nine and it took me most of a Sunday, and there are two letters left in the folder that I am not going to write anything for.**"

"You have said that twice before in a room with a microphone on it."

"I have said it twice in a room with a microphone and once to a man making tea at about nine in the morning, and about eleven people heard the first time and one heard the second and this is the third and there are about nine in this room, and that is not a reason to stop and it is a reason to count." She put her hand flat on the folder. "**And it is still not a decision. There is nothing on the paper to answer it with.**"

---

At a quarter to midnight seventeen of the chairs stood empty behind a man of twenty-two in the fourth row who had not said one word all evening, and the microphone was still on its stand at the front of the room where it had been all night.

There is no record anywhere in this city of that, and a specification runs to four sections and three lines, and one of the three is funded, and about eleven thousand premises pay for it, and a committee does not meet again, and about eleven people a week are going to be taught to wait for a man in a lanyard.

So: a man of fifty-two came and said nothing, having said the other half of it four Fridays ago in the same room with a microphone on a stand. A woman of twenty-nine announced a number and then refused to say what the number was for. A woman of twenty-six announced the same number for the third time in a term. And an officer of a fund brought eleven Fridays to an end by saying in front of everybody that eleven Fridays of him was a habit and not a series.

None of that is a rule and none of it is anybody's job and nobody in that room was asked a question by anybody and nobody answered one.

---

Nine job numbers were on the docket under the lamp and the word had been at nine a minute until about one and at ten after one, and there is a piece of card about two inches by three in the inside pocket of a jacket on the back of a chair, and the original has lain in the fourth pocket of the tool roll for a term and a half with nothing on its fifth line, and nobody has ever held out a hand for it.

A drawer under that bench has five things in it and has never once held anything a person could produce in a room. Four of the five are sentences other people wrote. The fifth carries a reference number and a closing line asking that it not be produced unless somebody asks that office first, and nobody has ever asked. Nobody has asked him a question in a room with a microphone on it for four weeks and he is not going to miss it.

---

*199. Friday of week 65, ten at night in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and this entry is the eleventh and last of eleven, thirty-one chairs, and a number an officer of a fund said out loud and did not minute.*

*The Friday, as a count and not as a story.* **Eleven jobs on the card and nine of them in date, grade three from eight in the morning, the word at nine a minute until about one and at ten after one, and the count itself on the back of a docket in a jacket on a chair in Lattice Ward. Nobody on that card asked him where a release was going to come from, and this entry does not keep a running total of Fridays because a total of Fridays is a number somebody can quote.**

*The room, as a count.* **Same room in Crown Terrace as every session in the series. Thirty-one chairs. Fourteen people, which is lower than any session in four years. Seventeen chairs empty at a quarter to midnight. A stack of about four hundred leaflets by the door, a fee and a session title and five jobs on the front of each, two of those five his, entered by a system in a company out of two job sheets in the week of the thirty-fourth. A microphone on a stand at the front, on all night, with a lead in it. A specification of four sections and three lines, the third of them funded, a committee of about nine which met once in the week of week thirty-seventh and does not meet again.**

*Four people in that room who are not him, as a list and not as a cast.* **An officer of a fund of forty-four, who is an officer by job and has said four things out loud in this room over a term. A depot manager of fifty-two, who came at half past nine and left at about eleven. A woman of twenty-nine, on a Friday card because an employer pays. A woman of twenty-six, at the table by the wall, at the eleventh of eleven sessions she has attended. None of the four was asked anything by him and none of the four was asked anything by anybody else in that room on that night.**

*Two refusals, recorded by their object and not by their wording.* **A woman of twenty-nine refused to state what a number of sixteen Fridays on the back of her own card was for, on the grounds that stating it converts a number into a claim and a claim is a thing that can be asked to be given up. A woman of twenty-six declined to send the ninth of nine answers written into eleven letters, on the unchanged ground that there is nothing on the paper to answer it with, and did not send the other eight either, and the count of what she has said in rooms about that folder is three this term.**

*One piece of canon, quoted as a canon.* **The third line was read aloud as a third line rather than as a section, and about nine people in the room did not hear the difference, which is the fifth time that has happened and the third time this term. The line is not counted aloud in this entry and no word count for it appears in it. A man of forty-four said of it in Chapter 177 that it is nine words and the printed line is seventeen, and neither figure is repeated here.**

*What the bench held at ten at night, as a list of objects and not a mood.* **A docket with nine job numbers in a shorthand nobody else in this city can read. A drawer under the bench holding five things, four of them sentences other people wrote and one of them a letter with a reference and a closing line that says it is not to be produced unless that office is asked first, and nobody has. A jacket on the back of a chair with a photocopy of a photocopy in the inside pocket, decision unmade, a term and a half old. The original, in the fourth pocket of the tool roll, with a fifth line nobody has ever asked him to fill in. Nobody asked him a question in a room with a microphone on it for four weeks.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **The standing problem was not said and was not asked about, and no number is attached to it here or anywhere in this batch, and no rule about who may be asked was invented or implied. This entry contains no count of how many times the standing problem has been said in this manuscript, because counting it is a thing a later volume can do and not this one. It contains no minute, no notice, no leaflet issued, no attendance figure entered anywhere but a room, no fifth job anybody could put a name to, and no account of what the officer of the fund said about the eleven, which is on the page and not in this entry. The series ended. Nothing replaced it. Entry 199.**
