# Chapter 194 — The Third Time

There is a corridor in a building in Crown Terrace that a man of fifty-eight has walked every working morning for fourteen years, and on the Monday morning of week sixty-five a letter came up it with a company heading on it and a request in it, and it was the third such letter in fourteen years and the first one that anybody had sent to the office instead of to the office upstairs.

The letter is not the interesting part of that Monday. The tunnel is.

---

Petar Vance had a number in a book in a van, and on the Monday of week sixty-five he took it to the hall.

He is a field service engineer and he services the heating plant in that building for his own company, and the plant room is on the ground floor and there is a door at the back of it that is not on any of the four plans he holds, and behind the door there is a service tunnel, and the tunnel goes down.

"You are not going down it."

"I am going as far as the top of it and I am going to stand there and I am not going to be the first person in a term and a half to go into a room nobody has asked me about." He put the bag down on the step. "**I have a service list and a contract and a man's own schedule, and none of those three is a permit, and the fourth of the seven reasons I have got for not going down that tunnel is that the man who goes down a tunnel alone in a building he does not own is a man whose company finds out about it afterwards.**"

---

They went down about forty steps and there is a landing at the bottom with a hatch in the floor of it, an old steel hatch with a lift ring on it, and there was nothing on the hatch and there was no lock on it.

That is the fact about a maintenance chamber in this city and it is not a surprise to anybody who has been down one. **A chamber under a building has never had a lock on anything, and the reason is that it is under a building, and the person who can get to it is a person with a torch and a reason, and there has never in about forty years been an authority in this city that could put a door on one and keep it shut, because the authority to lock a room is the same authority as the authority to say who goes in it, and that authority has never once been written down anywhere in this city.**

The hatch was open four inches on a Monday morning in week sixty-five and it had been open four inches on the twenty-sixth of the week before, and there is a man of about sixty with a trolley who comes into that building every Monday and who goes down that landing to see whether the hatch is where he left it.

---

They stood on the landing and they did not open it any further, and a man of twenty-two counted the doors on the way down and counted four and none of them was an exit, and the fire door at the top had a bar on it, and he said the sentence that says a room is a room, and he was allowed it, and nobody was listening.

"Chamber 3 is under this landing," said Petar Vance. "**The sheet said sub-level plant and it is the only sub-level thing down here, and there is a plan of the tunnel in a drawer on a hook in a basement four miles away that I have seen once and it stops at the first chamber.**"

"How many chambers."

"The plan stops at the first one and the sheet says the third, and those two facts have been sitting in two different buildings since the year before last and neither of them is wrong and nobody has ever put them in the same room."

---

Then they went up and along the corridor to the office on the first floor, because Iven Sore was in it. He is fifty-eight and he has a soft voice and a coat that has never been in a workshop, and he answered the first question with a smaller one, and it was the third time in fourteen years anybody had put that office in writing.

He read the letter out in the first person, which nobody in that corridor has ever heard him do, and he did not read it out twice.

"In writing, and to me, and from a company that holds the cleaning and the paper contract for this building, and what they want is for the table on the ground floor to be moved into a locked room on the first, and the reason they give in the second paragraph is insurance, and the reason they give in the third paragraph is that they cannot insure a document in a corridor." He put it down. "**I am declining it. That is three, and I have not told anybody about the other two and I am telling you about all three, and the count I am going to give you is the count I have given you every time, and I am not going to give it to a man in a corridor and I am going to give it to you.**"

"Go on."

"**Eleven weeks between rounds. Nine hundred and six sheets went into the contractor's yard on the Monday of week sixty-four across nine bundles, and one of those bundles was counted as a hundred and six and had a hundred and seven in it, and the number of pages in that building that anybody can account for this morning is nine hundred and five, and I have not looked for the sixth and I am not going to.**"

---

"Why a locked room is the wrong decision, in his words, the third time."

"Twice I said it was about the end of the corridor and I would rather have a corridor that does not work than a locked room that does, and that is still true and I am not going to improve it." He turned the letter round so the company heading was facing away from the door. "**The third time it is about the count. A locked room is a room that things go into and do not come out of, and the only thing this office has ever been good at is counting what goes in and what comes out, and there is a man in that yard this morning with a clipboard and a roll of tape, and in eleven weeks he will be back, and if the pages are in a locked room on the first floor then in eleven weeks he will come and find that the round found nothing, and a round that finds nothing is the same as a round that did not happen, and I would rather have nine hundred sheets in a skip than a building where nobody knows what was thrown away.**"

"That is the count."

"That is the count and that is the reason, and I have never put the two together before, and I have had fourteen years."

---

He did not ask them for anything, and they did not ask him for anything, and nobody agreed to anything on the Monday morning of week sixty-five.

What he did do, at the door, with the letter still in his hand, was tell them the thing about the round, and he said it the way a man reads out a date off a schedule.

"**The next one is on the Tuesday of next week. It is the same round, it is the same eleven weeks, the man in the yard is the same man, and the item is the same item, and there is a strip of tape and a line in a book in that hut, and if you want to know why I am telling you the date of a routine disposal then I do not know, and I have told you because a count that is not given out loud is only a thing I have.**"

---

A man of twenty-two was on a fault in that building until about half past three and he did not go down the tunnel, and he did not go through the hatch, and he was in a corridor for the whole of the afternoon with a docket and a light.

A hatch in the floor of a landing in Crown Terrace is four inches open and has been for as long as anybody has been down there, and there is no door on it, and there is not going to be a door on it, and on the Tuesday of next week a man with a trolley is going to put about nine hundred pages of other people's records into a skip and a strip of brown tape is going to go across a corner and a job title is going to go into a column with a word at the top of it, and every part of that will be correct.

---

*197. Monday of week 65, half past three in a corridor in Crown Terrace, and this entry is a service tunnel, a steel hatch with no lock on it, and a third declination in writing with a count and a reason in it.*

*Sources, with a date on each.* **A maintenance schedule running on eleven weeks; a printed service sheet with a location described in words rather than by a room number; a plan of a plant room belonging to a contractor; a plan of a tunnel that stops at the first chamber; four plans of a building held by a field service engineer; a letter with a company heading on it; a disposal book in a hut; a bound register in a canvas cover on a table on the ground floor. Seven documents. This entry does not treat any two of them as the same document and the sheet, the letter and the register are three different objects owned by three different people.**

*The tunnel, as a set of measurements.* **Door at the back of a plant room, absent from all four of the engineer's plans. Forty steps. One landing. Hatch: steel, with a lift ring, standing four inches open, no lock and no hasp, and the four inches measured by eye. Conduit: about eleven runs into the top of a painted cabinet, not counted further. Doors counted on the way down: four, and no exit among them, and a bar on the fire door at the top. Nobody opened the hatch. Chamber 3 is named on a sheet and is absent from the only plan that goes below ground, and the two facts sit in two buildings.**

*People, as a count and not as a list of names.* **Two went down. One field service engineer of fifty-one and one man of twenty-two on a fault. One man of about sixty with a trolley comes down that landing every Monday to see whether the hatch is where he left it, which is a maintenance check and not an inspection. One man of fifty-eight was in an office on the first floor. One woman of thirty-four was behind a table on the ground floor. Five people, four of them on ordinary work, and none of them asked to be.**

*The count, as figures and not as a speech.* **Eleven weeks between rounds. Nine bundles on the Monday of week sixty-four. Nine hundred and six sheets across them. One bundle entered in the book as a hundred and six and holding a hundred and seven. Nine hundred and five sheets in that building accounted for at the time of writing, the sixth not looked for and not going to be. One table. One register, four columns, about nine hundred lines, a job title in the fourth column of every line. A fourth column is not a person and this entry does not convert it into one.**

*What changed in that office on that Monday, in one line.* **A third written offer to move a table into a locked room, declined; and a date given out loud to three people that had not been given to anybody. Two actions, one of them ordinary, neither of them recorded, and the office held everything it held on Monday morning.**

*What the entry deliberately does not contain.* **It does not contain the interior of the chamber, the argument for the declination in either of its forms, the name of the contractor that wrote the letter, the schedule's owner, the number of bundles in a fortnight that were not on the Monday list, and the identity of the sixth sheet. It contains no relay and no carrier and no comparison. Nothing was agreed to, nothing was signed, no post was created, and no minute of the third declination exists anywhere. Entry 197.**
