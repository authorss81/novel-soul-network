# Chapter 26 — Nowhere to Put It

On the Thursday morning of week 11 the clinic was in the lay-by outside the wholesale kitchens and a Threadline service engineer was sitting on the folding step with his knees up, drinking out of a paper cup, and Marek Senn knew him by the case and the coat and by the fact that he was the third one to come to that door in three days.

"Senn, mate. Two minutes."

"I've got to see Talia."

"She's in with a woman from the district registry and there's a thing going on in there that I am not part of, and neither are you, and you'll wait." The engineer stood up and put the cup out of reach of a breeze. "Can I say a thing and then go and do my round?"

"Go on."

"I'm not asking for anything. There's no form and there's no signature and I'm not writing anything down." He had a clipboard and he did not put it on his knee. "Last Thursday, the week before, we started calling on the four sites with local holds. Routine. There's a bulletin out on the return contact — it's not a recall, there's no fault, we're not claiming anything, and our office sent it round in the first week of term and it's got a distribution list on it and that's how we find you, that's literally how the job works, that's the only way this company knows where its own plates are."

"And?"

"And every one of the four sites has said no, and that's their business, and I'm not here about the hold, I'm here because the clinic's stock went off the shelf and I've got to photograph the shelf numbers and confirm the count, that's it, that's a warranty job, that's how you get paid." He looked up. "And there's a list, Senn. There has to be a list. A hold's a hold, and a hold gets audited, and the audit needs serials against the people they've gone to. I'm not asking anybody anything. I'm reading a sheet."

Marek Senn stood in a lay-by in the cold and understood, about two seconds before the engineer said the next part, exactly what was about to happen.

"There's a man come in here Tuesday," the engineer said, "who's on the hip list. Farrow, Mal. Comes every few weeks, been coming years. And I've read his name and his serial off a sheet and I haven't got a chart and I haven't got to know him, and I want you to know that I read it off a sheet and I have not put it in a conversation with a patient because there is a person in this building whose job is to stop me, and she is in there now with the door shut." He picked the cup up. "That's it. That's the whole of what I came down the ramp to say and I said it too slowly and now the woman's finished and I can hear her."

He went in. Marek Senn stood outside in the lay-by and read four documents in ten weeks and worked out, in about as many seconds, that the only list of human beings with the specific knowledge to be hurt in this city had been made by four people acting on their own authority with no advice, no indemnity, and no lawyer.

He had spent a fortnight being proud of that.

---

Talia Venn read it standing up, in the treatment bay, with a patient in the chair behind her, in about ninety seconds, and gave it back.

"Sit down."

"I've got about eleven minutes."

"Then sit down for eight of them." She read it again, and then folded it once, which she did not do to other people's paper. "Senn. This is a document about a design. It is the best document anybody in this case has produced and I am not going to say anything else about it, because I have had a district registry nurse in my bay for half an hour and she has not asked me a single question about the eight hip units that went off my shelf onto six people, which means she has been told by somebody with a clipboard that she does not need to."

"Who told her?"

"Somebody with a list," said Talia Venn.

"Ma'am—"

"Sit down." She put her hand flat on the chart, which was Mal Farrow's, and did not close it. "Here is what I am not going to do and I am telling you in advance so that you cannot come back with a better version of it, because you will, and I have five noes on file and I am not making it six by accident. I am not going to help you put this anywhere. Not a form, not a room, not a list of people who would care, not a district registry nurse who happens to be in my bay this morning. No."

"I wasn't going to ask you to—"

"You were going to leave it on the step in about ninety seconds and hope." She looked at him. "I have said no to you five times since the Thursday of week five and every single time you came back with a better version of the same question, and this is the sixth, and the no is the same no, and I have not softened any of the other five and I am not going to start on the day you are holding the best piece of paper of your life."

"I know," said Marek.

"I know you know. That is not the same as being fine."

She put the folded sheet on the counter and put her hand flat on top of it.

"One thing, and I have told you in advance that this is information and not help, because I know what it is going to be for and I am giving it to you anyway." She did not soften it, because she never softened anything. "The Exchange has a monthly meeting on the first Wednesday and a noticeboard a month out of date. I have no vote in it. You will have no vote in it. Anybody who stands up in that room and says *this is important* gets the floor for four minutes and is then ignored by a room of ordinary people who have a fish to sell, and that is not me being unkind to you, that is how a room works, and every good thing in this city that anybody has ever built got built in one of those rooms by people who were extremely boring for four minutes and then very good at something else."

"What do you do about that?"

"Nothing," said Talia Venn. "You stand in it. That is the entire content of what I know about it and I have never been to one. I have a hip in eleven minutes and I am going to go and do it, and Senn — do not bring me the next one either. I know there is going to be a next one. There is going to be a next one for another three months, and I am the person in this city you will want to give things to, and I am telling you now, in advance, while I have got a reason, that you don't get to."

---

Sera Quill was at the bench at Civic Spine 7 at a quarter past one on the Thursday of week 11 with a plate frame in her hand, and Marek Senn took the photocopy out of the inside of his coat rather than out of the satchel, which was a thing he had worked out about himself on the Saturday of week 9 and had not told anybody.

"Put it on the wall," she said, before he had it out of the fold.

"What?"

"Put it on the wall, over there, on the nail, and then go and do the bathroom heaters, and if you tell me it's important I will tell you to take it out of my building." She did not look up from the frame. "I've been in this bay eleven years. I have written warnings on the backs of forms for eleven years because I do not trust a page with a front and no back, and you are standing in my bay with a document that a woman in a cardigan says four hundred people are not allowed to publish, and I am not going to be the second person who is holding it, because the first one is already going to be asked about it by somebody by the end of the month, and if it is in two buildings it is in both of their mouths."

"It isn't anybody's," Marek said. "It's a design approval. There's a function and a reference number and a date and no name on it at all."

"Fine," said Sera Quill. "Then it is nobody's, and you have brought it into a building with a wall of notes on it, and if you have not noticed what I have noticed then you have not been listening to me for nine weeks." She turned the frame round. "I cannot help you with that and you know I cannot, and what I told you in week nine is still true and I am not going to be a person who expands on it. And I would like it noticed that the second time was not softer, and that if you come at me with it a third time I will take it out of my building and you will not enjoy that."

She nodded at the nail on the wall.

"Put it there. Go and do the bathroom heaters on the canal. And Senn—" and she stopped, and did not finish, and went back to the frame.

---

Petar Vance was not at the Threadline depot on the Thursday of week 11, and Marek Senn telephoned the depot at ten past two from the corner of Pell Street and a woman with a switchboard told him three things.

"The run was the twenty-eighth."

"I know it was the twenty-eighth."

"Then you know it's nine days ago and he's not sat in that building waiting for you to decide." She said it without any unkindness, which was somehow worse. "He's in Friday. He does the Cordage and the clinic in the morning and he's back in the office about two. If you want the intake pack for the twenty-eighth, it's been pulled and it's on the second shelf and he's the only one who can sign it out, and there's no need to book."

"There's need to book," Marek said. "I promised a man in a corridor outside a department office on the Friday of week nine to come and find him before the twenty-eighth, in a room, with a witness, and it is the Thursday of week eleven and I have not done it."

There was about two seconds of a switchboard.

"That's a shame," said the woman, and put him through to the Thursday of week 12 instead, which he did not want, and then hung up.

---

He got the envelope out of the side of his bag at about half past three in the afternoon on the Thursday of week 11, at a bench, in a yard off Sennet Row, in the last forty minutes of daylight, having carried it in eleven pockets for a fortnight.

It was a buff service envelope with a printed label on the front that said **M. S. SENN — INSTITUTE — CIVIC SPINE 7**, and below the label there was the pale rectangle where a second label had been and had been pulled off, and it had been pulled off in a hurry, because a corner of it was still there.

He did that. He did it with a fingernail at a counter in Lattice on the Tuesday of week 9, in a depot, with a queue behind him, and he put it in front of him, which is the single most ordinary thing that has ever happened in this case and the only thing in it he has ever done for nothing.

Inside was one sheet.

It was a service bulletin cover sheet, carbon, with a routing stamp across the top right corner, and a printed subject line, and a distribution block, and a copy number, and a carbon sheet still attached at the corner by a small strip of gummed tape that had gone the colour of tea.

*Service bulletin SB-4-19-03.*
*Subject: return contact — revised for district distribution. Not a fault. Do not claim. Prior practice continues unchanged.*
*Issued: the first week of term, three days after the last crate. Regional service office — 2nd line, Ashfields Road, two districts up.*
*Received, Lattice depot: two days later.*
*Distribution: (1) Nacre Institute of Applied Practice, practical department. (2) Cordage evening classes, Saltmarket. (3) Ninth Street rooms, Nine Bridges. (4) Mobile clinic, Saltmarket route. (5) [address: a registered office, four lines, a district, and a postal town that is not Nacre.]*
*Copy 5 of 5.*

On the back of the routing slip, in pencil, in a hand that was not a work hand and had been written by somebody standing up:

*Ask for the distribution list, not the bulletin. The bulletin is what everybody gets. — don't write back to this envelope.*

Marek Senn sat on a bench in a yard off Sennet Row for about four minutes.

Copy one of five had gone to the Institute. Copy two to the Cordage. Copy three to the Ninth Street rooms. Copy four to a mobile clinic on the Saltmarket route. Four sites, four holds, four local authorities, three of them people who had acted on their own initiative with no advice and no indemnity, one of them a woman who had forwarded the government's own letter to three strangers before she had spoken to one of them.

He had spent a week being proud of that.

And copy five had gone out of the city, to an address with a postal town on it that was not Nacre, in the first week of term, and the man at the depot counter had put a copy of a piece of paper into an envelope and written a label on it and taken the label off again, and had written four lines on the back of a routing slip and gone to work.

---


He read the whole page back at one o'clock in the morning on the Friday of week 11, at the table in the room above the laundromat, with the buff envelope flat under his hand and Rafi Pell asleep on the couch with a courier bag over his legs, and then he wrote the last page of week 11, and it is short.

*Week 11, Friday, 01:00.*

*The strongest piece of paper I will ever have, and there is nowhere in this city to put it.*

*Gone into: a filing system in a building that has refused a co-operative three times. Gave me a slip with a date on it. That is the whole of the department.*
*Read on the telephone by a man with four assessors, who said no before I had got to the end of the sentence and then told me the thing I have thought about every hour since, which is that a document with a reference number in it gets read by people who are not us.*
*Read in ninety seconds in a corridor by a woman of forty-five, who said it is not a school matter, and then said the only sentence anybody has said to me in six weeks that was any use: it is a market table in front of anybody who walks in off the street, and it is not in your pocket.*
*Taken off a bench in my own building and put on a nail, and not held, and not expanded, and the same two sentences as week nine, in the same tone, and the second time was not softer.*

*So: I have proved it. I want to write the sentence out once, because in about six weeks I am going to be tempted to be careful about it and in nine months I am going to be tempted to be cool about it: the omission is designed. Somebody chose to leave the fourth beat out and installed the instructor's voice as the replacement, and they wrote down why, and the why is good.*

*And it is four sentences in my own hand with a co-operative member's initials and a date on them, and the only forum I have is about a practice, and nobody in this city can act on a design, and the body that can is not in this city, and the record says the card was not to be issued to houses and about ninety houses had it four weeks later on a form with a dash in it.*

*And now the second thing, and I have been not writing this down since eleven o'clock this morning and it is worse than the first thing:*

*Holding a correct answer makes the people near me findable.*

*1. A service engineer came to the clinic on the Thursday morning on a routine audit with a serial list, and Mal Farrow's name and his hip serial were on it, and that list is the only list that has ever existed in this case, and the list exists because four people acted on their own authority with no advice and no indemnity and no lawyer, and I have been proud of that for a fortnight.*
*2. The same company called the Cordage on the Wednesday to photograph fifteen plates in a bucket off a high shelf that Bo Halloran put there himself, and read the serials off the wall before he could stop him, and it was not malice, it was a warranty job, and that is the sentence I have to write down: it was not malice.*
*3. D. V. has my request for the department's own purchase docket. My name. What I wrote in the purpose box, which is the only part anybody will read, going into a room that twelve people walk through every day, and in three weeks a stranger in that room is going to know the exact sentence I used about a card that had a boy's hand in it.*
*4. There is an envelope in my bag with four Nacre addresses on it and one that is not in Nacre, given to me by a man at a counter who took a label off it with his fingernail, and I cannot check the handwriting, and there is one copy of it in this city and it is in a rucksack above a laundromat.*
*5. Two days ago I was proud of the four holds. This morning I have worked out that the four holds are the only list.*

*Nothing resets. I am not going to write a line in this book in the next four weeks that says somebody has taken a thing off somebody, and there is going to be pressure, and there is going to be a company that is being reasonable with me, and there is going to be a moment in about ten days when it would be easier by a factor of ten to hand somebody the photocopies. And I have got a slip from the department with a date on it and a slip is the only thing I have ever wanted.*

*Rule five, then, and it is new, and I am writing it at one in the morning because that is the only time I write anything that is true:*
*Being right is not the same as being believed, and being right is not the same as being safe, and the second one costs other people and not me, and I have been careful about the first one for ten weeks and I have not once thought about the second one.*

*Do not know: what TRC-419-88 is a reference *to*, in the sense of what a room is. I have a function and a number and a date and the number's two eights are the revision and the other two are the sequence and that is a guess, and I have written down that it is a guess.*
*Do not know: who the member of the Choir is who copied that record into plain language by hand, and was in a room in the middle of August, and has not been named, and would not be printed if they asked, and I am not going to be the man who makes them findable.*
*Do not know: whether being proved right is going to be worth a single thing to the fifty houses, and I do not know how to ask, and the only tool for asking is a form, and a form has everybody's name in it, and that is the entire of it.*

*Tomorrow: the depot, at two, on the Friday. Nine days late on a promise I made in a corridor on the Friday of week nine, to a man who wrote it down twice and gave me the chance to fix it and did not press me when I did not. He is going to say it is late. He is going to be right. I am going to say the sentence anyway, in a room, with a witness, and it is going to be worth less than it was worth on the sixth of this term, and it is the only thing I have left to do that is actually in my control.*

---

Civic Spine 7 at ten past nine on the Friday morning of week 11 was four jobs on the board and Marek Senn did three of them, and there was a hand-copied account of nineteen seconds in a fish office yard folded into a delivery docket in his satchel with a piece of tape on it, because Mal Farrow had written it himself, on the Wednesday, in his own hand, in six lines, and had given it to Tam Arrick to give to Marek because Farrow had a rule about telephones.

*"I watched a lad put his hand on a box and take it off. Nineteen seconds, I counted it. He decided before he did it and I saw him decide. It was not a release and I'm not giving it a name. If anybody wants my name on it, they'll have to come and ask me, and they'll have to come and ask me twice, and I don't answer the second time either."*

Underneath, in a different pen, in the same hand:

*"Tell the third year the Saturday refusal stands. He is not to write me down as a source and he is not to come and find me. I gave him this because I was stood next to the boy and I said so, not to him."*
