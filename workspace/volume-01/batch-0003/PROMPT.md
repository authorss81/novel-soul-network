## Where the story is

Saturday night of week 9. Marek has no license, no technique, no standing and no date. The week-7 assessment is withdrawn. The term practice-variance panel opens **Friday of week 11**. The Continuity Office has declined in writing to trace the licensor and has handed him one sentence instead: a panel can act on a *demonstrated* non-hinged release in a public room with witnesses, and on nothing else in his case. The four sites hold the 4-19 stock on their own authority and can refuse the Office's recommendation later.

Four open threads go into this batch and must be picked up:

- **The Choir of Practice.** Talia named it once, on the telephone, as *information and not help*: a co-operative that publishes lineages in plain language, the only complete record in the city of who wrote a technique, when, and what they changed; copies of every quarterly lie on a table at the Saltmarket Exchange for anybody who walks in off the street. They are not an authority, they will not do anything for Marek, and one of them will tell him to his face that he is wrong in front of nine other people. **Nobody in the case has met a member.**
- **The unopened envelope**, addressed to Marek at the Threadline depot counter since the Tuesday of week 9, delivered to Civic Spine 7 on the Saturday.
- **Petar Vance and the Wednesday of week 10.** Vance has two written lines in a depot book with Marek's initials against them, both saying *I don't remember* about the wax paper. Marek remembers. He has promised to go, in a room, with a witness, before the depot run, and Vance will write *volunteered* next to it.
- **Rusk's unread 2019 stores file** about a set return contact out of a fish crate.

Also live: **the Thursday of week 10 and Ines Sarr's classroom**, which Marek has promised Rafi he will sit in, and which Sarr has said she will take him out of by the arm; **Tam's strap, which comes off in about four weeks**; the term panel on Friday of week 11; the fifty unaccounted household plates; and Deputy Roos, who can close the variance item by signature before the review opens.

## What this batch must do

Movement III turns a school problem into a supply-chain problem, and it pays the family. It must contain, in order: the weekly independent practice in a working kitchen, a fish market, and on a ramp; the evidence that a person who learned it here can stop it here, built from other people's jobs rather than the Institute's; the Choir of Practice and the cost of a published lineage; a family cost, because Nell has been lent a 4-19 kit by a classmate's family and Marek has to take it back; **the Chapter 25 midpoint proof that the omission is designed**; Petar Vance, the wax paper, and the Threadline district depot; Oren Vey, who is helpful, credible, and offering a job and a "compatible" replacement technique; the public refusal of that replacement; and a service bulletin number that becomes the first thread anybody can follow upward.

By the end of Chapter 30 the reader must be able to say: the omission is not an accident in one bundle, it is a decision somebody signed, and the person who signed it is not in this city and cannot be reached from here, and the same gap is sitting in fifty houses and a man's hip pocket and a neighbourhood classroom that no school supplied.

## Hard rules for this batch

- **Do not name the Crown Clause.** Do not let anyone identify the Marrow Falls signature's authorship. **Sera Quill must not say the name on the log aloud.** She may withhold, go cold, or leave the room. She may not explain. She has already said her one operational sentence in Chapter 18: *if that is a pattern, and I am not saying it is, then it is not a school matter.* **Do not let her expand it.**
- **Threadline is the assembler and seller, not the author.** Oren Vey must not turn out to be the person who chose the omission. He is a man who profits from a pattern and reframes it as safety, and the last thing Movement III should leave the reader with is the sense that the mystery has been solved by a company's commercial department.
- **Chapter 25 is the midpoint proof and it is the only place the deliberateness is proved.** It must connect the **instructor script** to a **design-approval entry** and to a **revision history**, and show that somebody chose to leave the fourth beat out and replaced it with the instructor's cue. Up to and including Chapter 24, the four explanations named in Chapter 7 must all still be live in Marek's handwriting, and nobody may find an approval page, enumerate a revision history, or claim a person chose the substitution. **Do not let an earlier chapter spend Chapter 25.**
- **Marek holds no Working Share until Chapter 49.** Every share in this batch runs on a named purpose, a named scope, a person who could stop it, and a written record with a review or destruction date.
- **Movement III needs no System panel at all.** The interface has spoken three times in twenty chapters and has never once helped. If one appears, it appears once, it does not conclude, and it does not tell him where to look.
- **No reset.** Tam's arm, the two tendons, the six weeks before weight, the three months before anything heavy, the lost kitchen wages, the middle finger, Tam's distrust, Marek's transcript line and the two voices in Tam's release all persist. **He is in a dorsal splint, not a cast, and the strap does not come off until the middle of the movement.** He breaks his own rules on the page and writes them down when he does. He is two and a half weeks post-op at the start of this batch.
- **Rafi withdrawing was correct and is not in question.** He is counting to two out loud before he answers anything. Do not have him recover, do not have him forgive Marek on schedule, and do not let him be a yes again on the page.
- **Marek must not become good at something he has not practiced.** He repairs beautifully and cannot perform a release. In Chapter 20 he worked out that he has spent nine weeks being the word in the room. **He now has a written rule: stop arranging, do not chase, ask one person one thing at a time, and stop believing that being right is the same as being believed. This batch is where he has to keep that rule, and where keeping it should cost him something he wants.**
- **Talia is not a reward, a deputy or a romantic payoff.** Her scope is closed and she has told him she is not going to help him find a fourth person. She has given him the Choir of Practice as *information*. Do not reopen the scope, do not soften her, and do not make her available.
- **Nell is an ordinary sixteen-year-old, not a hidden node, an inheritance, or a victim.** The borrowed kit is a family cost and a supply-chain fact, and the cost is that she stops speaking to him for days. **Do not let it resolve inside this batch.**
- **Oren Vey is not the final enemy and does not become one.** He is courteous, specific, and believable, and he should be the most likeable person in the batch. The batch ends with his replacement technique refused in public and a service bulletin number that can be followed upward, not with him defeated.
- **No new final enemy and no new canon rule.** The Choir of Practice is not a secret police; it is a cooperative that publishes lineages and is capable of being wrong, political, and unkind about it.

## Beat sheet (follow the shape; the prose is yours)

- **21.** The week after the recognition. Marek asks for one thing at a time, badly, and finds that a queue of noes is the method and not an excuse. He goes to the Choir of Practice's table at the Saltmarket Exchange and is told, in front of nine other people, that he is wrong about something, and does not know yet which thing.
- **22.** Independent practice in a working building: Dessa's kitchen, a fish market, a courier's ramp, and a fourth body nobody has met. Ordinary people hold a better version of the fourth beat than the Institute does and nobody has taught them to call it that. The material is gathered, not performed, and nobody agrees to be a source.
- **23.** The Choir of Practice properly: what a published lineage contains, what it costs to read one, and the discovery that the Institute's own card is not in it. The family cost lands — **Nell has been lent a 4-19 kit by a classmate's family, and Marek has to take it back, and she does not speak to him for days.** Do not let her forgive him inside this chapter.
- **24.** Tam's plan runs into the strap. A failed attempt and a bad week, and then a **partial success that only works with Tam's own hand in it, after he gives fresh, explicit consent for that named purpose** — larger and harder-won than the eleven seconds of Chapter 16, and still not a technique. **This is where Mal Farrow finally agrees to be the observer, on his own condition, and only for Arrick.**
- **25.** **Midpoint proof.** The instructor script, its design-approval entry, and the revision history together show that the omission is designed: somebody chose to leave the fourth beat out and installed the instructor's cue as its replacement. The bundle's upstream licensor field is blank, which means the vendor is a conduit and not the author, and the next question is a supply-chain question. **And after the proof, nothing.** No institution will act on it. Nobody in this city can be reached with it.
- **26.** The cost of the proof. Marek has the strongest piece of paper he will ever have and cannot give it to anybody who can act on it, and the new danger is that having been right once makes him more dangerous to the people he cares about. Petar Vance, the wax paper, and the **Thursday of week 10 / Wednesday of week 10 depot run.**
- **27.** The Threadline district depot in Lattice. **Petar Vance** at full length, courteous and afraid of his own warranty file. The wax paper, the intake pack, and a lot number that leads somewhere and not to a person. **The service reference SB-4-19-03 becomes the first thread that can be followed upward.**
- **28.** **Oren Vey appears.** Helpful, credible, generous with small favours, and offering a paid job, a reference, and a "compatible" replacement technique that completes the fourth beat the way the kit was always supposed to. He reframes the hinge as a safety feature and does it sincerely.
- **29.** The replacement technique is examined and refused — **in public, and by people who are not Marek.** A working kitchen, a market, a neighbourhood classroom and a courier's ramp all say no to it, each in their own words and for their own reason, and the refusal does not belong to the student. Tam's fresh consent is the hinge of the scene. **Nell does not speak to Marek throughout.**
- **30.** **Mini-climax.** The public refusal of Oren's replacement, the **service bulletin number becoming the first thread anybody can follow**, and the batch's ending turn: the same omission is now confirmed in a work technique nobody at the Institute bought. The week-7 route stays closed, the term panel is three days away or already gone depending on the date you choose, and the demonstration requirement is still hanging with nothing behind it.

## Ending pull for the batch

The mystery has stopped being about a school and become about a supply chain, and the family cost has stopped being abstract. The next question is who licensed the upstream bundle and why the same gap appears in places no school supplied.
