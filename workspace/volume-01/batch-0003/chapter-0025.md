# Chapter 25 — A Function and a Reference

The 2019 stores file was in Davan Rusk's hand at twenty to eight on the Wednesday morning of week 11, in a corridor outside the Institute's copy room, and he was not going to give it to Marek Senn.

"I've read it," he said. "That's the first thing. "What does it say."

"It's a return." He had the file under his arm and he was holding it with both hands, which Marek had not seen him do with anything. "A 4-19 wrist unit came back from the Cordage fish office in the November of 2019 with a bent frame and a return contact that had set. That's the phrase on the form, *return contact that had set*, which is the correct phrase, and I know it is the correct phrase because there is a line under it that says the plate will do it again. Somebody in this department wrote that line. It isn't in my hand."

"Read me the disposition."

"That's the bit I'm going to read you and then I'm going to go to a lesson." He did not open the file. "One line. In my hand. *Not a fault. No claim. Prior practice continues.* Signed, initialled, dated the nineteenth of the eleventh, about nine minutes after the form came in, in a corridor, because I was on my way to a room with a hundred and eleven people in it." He looked up. "And under it, in the department's hand, a query. Eleven of that. *Query raised with distributor. Response: design record not held by distributor; not available. Closed.* And a number, and a date, and it's a service request number and I've never seen it before today because it is a number in a file in a press on a shelf."

Marek Senn stood in a corridor outside a copy room and worked very hard not to reach out.

"The return contact had set," he said. "In 2019. Out of the fish office at the Cordage."

"Out of the fish office at the Cordage," said Davan Rusk. "I saw the plate in 2019. "Then you know what it means."

"I know exactly what it means and I am not going to say it in this corridor." He put the file under his other arm. "I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to go and teach a card with a hole in it to a hundred and eleven people for fifty minutes, because that is my job and I am going to do it today whatever is in that press, and I have decided that just now, standing here, which is the first decision I have made about this since the Thursday of week six."

"Go on."

"I wrote *not a fault*." Rusk's voice did not change and his hands did. "About a copper contact that had taken a shape in the shape of a load. Nine minutes, in a corridor, in a hurry, about a piece of equipment. And I am not giving you the file."

"You don't have to."

"I am aware. I am not giving you the file because it is a department record and I am a member of the department and I am not the one who opens a file to a third-year with no licence on a Wednesday morning, and if I did it today I would have to defend having done it in a fortnight." He turned to go. "There is a number in it. It is a Threadline service request number, and it is from before you were in this building, and it is the first thing in ten weeks that belongs to somebody other than a school or a hospital. You may write that down. I have not given you the file and I have not answered for it and I have told you what's in it, and if anybody asks me I will say I told a student in a corridor that there is a service request number in a stores file, which is true and which is the least I could do with eighteen months of carrying a plate around in my head."

He went into room two. Marek Senn stood in the corridor for a minute and a half and then wrote the number down, and then, in the same breath, wrote next to it: *I asked him for it and he did not give it and I did not ask again. Rule three. Cost: the file, which I want more than anything in this building. And I have just been told the number in it is the first thing that belongs to a company, and I cannot use it, and I am not going to ring it up on my own at eleven o'clock at night and see who answers.*

He did not go home.

He sat on the wall at the canal end until it was dark and read the overleaf again, which he had read nine times by then, and arrived at the thing that nobody had been able to warn him about, because it was not a fact about a company, or a form, or a reference number, or a corridor, and because it was the only part of the day that was not a document.

The reason was good.

That was the whole of it. A competent person had written three paragraphs about why a learner cannot be relied upon to apply a boundary under load, and had cited two real incidents in six months, and had cited one of them as a fourth-year assistant, and had concluded that a single supervised release route should be retained, and had signed a form.

It was the best sentence Marek Senn had read in ten weeks.

He read it a fifth time and understood, sitting on a wall by a canal in the dark with a photocopied sheet in his hand, that he agreed with it. Not all of it. The second incident — a scope applied early, with the subject still in the load, six months apart, in a room, by a student — was a real incident and it had hurt somebody and it was a real argument. The retention of a single supervised release route was a rational response to a department that had been getting that wrong. The withdrawal of the learner's self-initiated release was a considered, defensible, and — he had to write it, he actually had to write it down and his hand went cold — *an entirely reasonable* decision to take, in a building, about a hundred and eleven young people, in a term, by a function with a reference number, four days before nine crates went out to a car park on a Tuesday in the first week of term.

He had spent ten weeks building a case out of a boy with two tendons, a woman with three ribs, a courier with a wall in his shoulder, a man's four-page file, four local holds, and two blank ruled lines on a bundle label in a store room behind a porters' cage.

And the actual answer was three paragraphs in a good hand about two incidents, and the answer had been right, and a hundred and eleven people had been taught it anyway, and it had been issued to ninety households in a revision it said should not be issued to households, four weeks after it was signed, by a company that had asked twice to see the document and been told no.
