# Volume 01 Close — *The Missing Beat* (Chapters 1–50)

Phase: writer, batch 0006. **No prose in this document and none is required.** Volume 01 ran from `batch-0001/chapter-0001.md` to `batch-0005/chapter-0050.md` — 231,481 words in fifty chapters, from the Tuesday of week 1 to the Monday evening of week 15. The term ended on the Friday of week 12, so eighty of those days are in term and eighteen are out of it. This file closes the volume against `outline/volume-01.md`, records what the prose actually delivered, confirms the guardrails, and hands Volume 02 a dated set of open questions.

It is not a re-outline, and it is not a plan for the next volume. Where it says something is missing from the world, the correct sentence is *there is no such thing in this city*, which is a fact of Volume 01 and not an invitation to design one.

---

## 1. What Volume 01 was about

A man proved that a whole city's safety instructions had been quietly rewritten so that one person had to be in the room, and then found that the only way to prove it in public was to stop being that person — and that proving it in public does not fix the instruction, and does not find the fifty houses, and does not take a count out of a woman on a rail, and does not make a man in a doorway say something different.

1. **The Institute's manual was requisitioned in March.** The outline says only that the manual still shows 3-08 and that nobody updated it and nobody asked. ## 10. What Volume 01 was for, to the writer of Volume 02

This is not a puzzle. It is a supply chain with a blank line in it, and the protagonist's problem is not that he cannot find the answer — the answer is a design record with three paragraphs of reason and no name on it, and he has had it since a Wednesday in week 11 — it is that **every document he makes to prove the answer turns a person into a row.** He wrote a request for a register and did not post it because the register is ninety houses. He copied his father's card and filled the fifth line wrongly in his own hand, and a boy made him take the full stop off the end of the third sentence so that nobody in a department in nine years would hear a rule, and neither of them will ever know that either. He got a name printed against his own upheld request in nine hundred addresses, and the only reason he cannot un-ask it is that he asked to be printed, which is the exact mechanism he spent the term refusing to start, arrived at by the one institution he trusted. So the next volume should not be a man looking for a name in a file. It should be a man discovering that the file is only dangerous, and the room is the dangerous thing, and that a list on a wall reaches a hillside in four days while a name in a booking sheet is permanent and public and cannot be taken out by anybody, including him.

**What the reader now believes about him, in one sentence, checked clause by clause:**

> He does not have the licence, he has a narrow one, four people can end it, the four sites still have their stock, the licensor is still unnamed, the man he hurt is a boy who finished his own release, and the room is now something other than a man's.

Every clause is supported by the prose. Two things the reader is also holding, which the sentence does not contain and which the volume insists on anyway: **a girl of nineteen went to hospital with four stitches because of four seconds of his**, and she said in a corridor, before anybody else had, that the first thing she was aware of was not a bar but a man counting. And **a boy of twenty wrote at the top of a page that the hand is going to be told what to do and he is the one who is going to let it, and the fortnight for telling somebody has run out and he has not said it to anyone.** The sentence is true. It is not the whole of what the reader knows, and it is not supposed to be.

## Two things a reviewer should look at, flagged and not fixed
