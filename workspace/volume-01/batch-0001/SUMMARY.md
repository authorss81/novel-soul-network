# Batch 0001 Summary — Volume 01, Chapters 1–10 ("The Drill")

Phase: writer, batch 0001. Chapters written: 10. Total prose: ~34,700 words. (Revised after the phase-002 review; the batch is the same batch, not a restart.)
Files: `chapter-0001.md` through `chapter-0010.md` in this directory.

## What this batch delivers

Marek Senn goes from a failing third-year with a private hunch to a failed licensee holding physical evidence, one injured classmate, and a problem that lives outside his institution. The batch moves the volume's central problem from theory into a body, then out of his body and into a product, then into a stranger's practice, then out into a city full of people the Institute has never taught.

In order, as the batch card required: ordinary work and family life (1); the drill (2); the rescue (3); the cost of the share (4); the failed seal run (5); Talia's correction (6); the structural proof that the beat is missing and the licence field is blank (7); the second rescue in which Marek becomes the key-holder himself (8); the institutional squeeze and **the refusal of the deal, said out loud and written down** (9); the move outside the Institute (10).

## The spine of the hinge, as dramatized

The 4-19 card prints a boundary — *scope: force only, no joint, no sensation* — and prints it honestly. What is gone is not the label. What is gone is the **action under it**: a question the practitioner's own hands must ask out loud, at a point where the answer can still change what happens. In its place the bundle's instructor notes install the instructor's spoken cue as a "support beat… not a step in the technique," and the learner is told not to proceed on a self-initiated release.

This is stated in the prose three times in three registers: Rusk's teaching voice in Chapter 2, the printed script in Chapter 7, and Marek's own mouth on a dock in Chapter 8.

## Power state

Stage 0 throughout, three demonstrations, no capability gain.

- Ch 3: first two-source line, ~4 seconds, unrepeatable, offered badly and agreed to in under four seconds by a man who did not know what he was agreeing to. It yields the shape of the missing beat and a shared aftertaste Marek did not ask for.
- Ch 4: attention debt becomes a tracked physical condition. He starts the **load book**, a cheap spiral notebook with a crane on the front, written by hand in a column headed *LOAD*. This habit persists for the volume and is the batch's signature motif.
- Ch 6: a consent cost, not a power cost. He loses a source of comparison.
- Ch 7: he can name what is missing, which fixes nothing.
- Ch 8: one accidental non-hinged release, produced once, at a price, and only because Tam's own hand on the crate edge supplied the check. Marek does not install it: he asks, and says out loud that he is handing over nothing and that he is the last person in the city who should be saying the word to Tam, and offers to refuse Tam anything he asks for. It is *not* a qualifying scored release: Marek voices Rusk's cadence before refusing it, so the no-cue requirement is not satisfied.
- Ch 10: three independent lines are visible in the city. He has observed all three and directly compared only one (Rafi).

## Relationship changes

- **Marek–Talia**: strangers who have met twice, both times badly. He has twice refused her Thursday-slot invitation without giving a reason and never answered her text. Ch 6 is her correction, in front of a patient, in plain words, unsoftened. He resents her for the rest of the evening and then, at the clinic door, tells her the one true thing he has told nobody: Tam's fear is still in him. She gives him a bounded alternative anyway (two performances, use and remember, no derive, written scope with a date and a review, and she keeps a copy). **Ch 7 is on the Thursday she named: she opens by telling him she is changing the terms of her own written scope, says why, writes a new one in front of him, and makes him sign across the line from her.** No warmth, no softening, no romance. She is the only person who has told him the truth about what he did, including the part he did not know.
- **Marek–Rafi**: Rafi's yes is established early and honestly, and his flaw is named out loud in Ch 4. His balance step carries the same missing fourth beat and he has compensated for two years with his left shoulder against a wall, which he calls technique. He is the batch's first *repeat* victim, not its opponent.
- **Marek–Rusk**: Rusk is a decent man inside a bad procedure. He admits he ordered the obsolete manual himself, tells Marek to take the evidence to somebody who can be harmed by it, and is protected by the department. He is not a villain and must not become one.
- **Marek–Tam**: the batch's central cost. Tam is rescued, then locked, then injured, then he tells Marek to stop speaking for him, then he takes the recovery plan away from the department and writes it himself. He names the observer he wants and makes Marek the one who has to go and find it.
- **Marek–Sera**: she refuses to let the Spine 7 relay be used as laboratory equipment, teaches him that a return path is a physical cut in metal rather than a promise, and refuses to discuss Marrow Falls. He does not give her the log page back.
- **Marek–Deputy Roos**: the institutional squeeze. She offers him the whole thing on paper, tells him honestly what it would cost either way, **makes him refuse it out loud, demands a reason that will survive being written down, records it, turns the form so he can read it, and tells him not to thank her in the corridor.**
