# Chapter 39 — Sitting In It

The night shift at Civic Spine 7 on the Wednesday of week 13 was Marek Senn's shift, and the night list had eight jobs on it and he did six of them, and the two he did not do were a relay cabinet in a building he had never been inside and a lamp in a stairwell in a tower in Broadfields, and both of them were out of area.

Sera Quill came in at half past ten, read the night list over his shoulder, and said, "You're doing six."

"I'm doing six."

"Two of them are out of area."

"Two of them are out of area."

"Twelve hours, six jobs, two out of area, and you didn't tell the list." She put the clipboard back on the nail. "Write it on the list tomorrow, because in about a month somebody's going to notice and then it will look like you hid it, and I am not having a person on my shift who hides things from a list."

Then she went through to the mess room, and he was at the bench with a resistor in his hand, and had the distinct and unpleasant experience of a man who has been carrying a document about for a fortnight finding that he has nothing to carry it to.

He had had it in his coat since the Wednesday of week 11 and it was still in his coat, and she had never asked for it, and he had not brought it, and neither of them had said a word about the not-bringing, and what she had done about it was send him to the Saturday backlog in week nine and say nothing since, which was the whole of the answer and had cost her something the first time.

At about eleven he went past the wall of handwritten maintenance notes on the way to the sink. The Marrow Falls page was still up there, fourteen years old, with its typed block and its one row at 04:20, and it had been up there for twelve weeks and had not moved and had not been asked about by anybody including him.

He washed his hands for longer than his hands needed.

---

Oren Vey rang the bench at ten past eleven and Marek Senn said *that is me* into the receiver in a voice that had not been expecting anybody.

"Senn. I'll be quick, because I can hear a workshop." There was a road in the background. "I've heard there's a date coming. I'm not going to pretend I didn't hear it, because you have never once in this term accused me of saying something I didn't, and I would like to keep that."

"Who from."

"A colleague in a district office who cannot talk about it and who talked about it," said Oren Vey. "I'm not going to be thanked for that and I'd rather you didn't ask me for a name, because I have already decided I don't have one I'd give you."

"What do you want."

"Two things and then I'll let you go." A door shut somewhere. "One. On the Tuesday of last week, in the morning, I put in a request, in writing, internally, logged, in the company's own system, that every supplier pack be issued with section four completed or the pack refused at intake. It has a number on it. It is not a resignation, because I have not resigned, and it is the first time in three years that anything about a design has been in a file with my name on it in this company, and I would be obliged if you did not make a thing of it, because it is not brave and it is not a gift and it does not cost me anything I cannot afford."

"Right."

"The second thing is the card," said Oren Vey. "I am going to be straight with you about the fact that I have been waiting a fortnight for you to ring me and you have not, and I have decided you are not going to, and I am going to make you answer for the trial and not for the job."

"Go on."

"The one-month trial, with the card in your name and the wage at the end of it and my word that it will not be difficult." He said it in about six seconds. "I'm not taking it back, and I'm not going to try again later, and I'm not going to leave it in your coat as a way of keeping you in a room, because that is exactly the thing I watched four people do to four other people in nineteen years and it works and it is foul. Take it or don't. Say the reason."

He was at the bench in a repair shop at ten past eleven at night with a receiver against his ear and a bad shoulder and a card in his coat that he had carried about for a fortnight.

"Because in six days' time I am going to walk into a room in front of a panel, and the only thing anybody in that building is able to act on is that a person finishes their own practice," he said, "and if I am wearing a card that says I may practise under supervision then I am a man with a supervisor, and the panel will be right about me, and I would rather be wrong in a room than right with a lanyard on."

There was a silence of about two seconds.

"No," said Oren Vey. "That's a good reason and it is not the reason."

"Which is."

"The reason is that you'd be able to do it," said Oren Vey. "You'd be able to do it in a fortnight. You'd be in a workshop with a phone and a bench and a wage and a man who'd let you put a plate on a person, and in a year you'd have thirty-one technicians and four of them would be girls of nineteen and you'd be the one who taught them what a boundary is, and it would be the best thing you will ever do, and in about four years there would be two thousand people in this country whose fourth beat is a man in Lattice with a card in his name, and it would be *you*, and you would never once have to be frightened again, and you would not be able to tell the day it happened from any other day." He said it without heat at all. "And you know that, and you have known it since week four, and you have been carrying a card about for a fortnight instead of posting it back, and I have decided that if you want to keep it, you can keep it, and I am not going to be the man who makes you feel clever for handing it in."

"That is a filthy thing to say to somebody at eleven o'clock at night."

"Yes," said Oren Vey. "It is. Come and tell me about it on Tuesday."

"No," said Marek Senn. "You are not coming to that room."

"I was going to offer."

"I know you were, and no, and the reason is not the lanyard. It's that there is going to be one voice in that room that is not mine, and if there are two it will be yours, and you will be a certified completion route with a complaint form standing in a public room, and in nine months somebody will point at you and say *that is the man who showed the panel how to finish somebody else's practice*." He stopped. "And you would not be able to say no to it afterwards, because you would have wanted it, and wanting it is the whole of the argument we had in a café."

"I would not be able to say no to it," Oren Vey agreed. "That is annoyingly well judged and I am going to think about it for a long time. Right. There is a thing I have to say and then I am going to let you go, because I have a train in about an hour and I have been sitting in this station since half past nine."

"Go on."

"There has to be something in its place." He said it flatly, and for the first time in four chapters he sounded tired. "I said that in a market on a Monday and I meant it, and I have spent a fortnight trying to put something in its place, and I have got two things and neither of them is any good. One is a procedure, and I have shown you the procedure and five people who do the work said no to it. Two is a person." A pause. "The two are not the same and everybody in this case has been arguing about them as if they were, and I have watched a market on a Monday and I have watched a ward this week — not by being there, because I have not been there, but because four people in four buildings have stopped giving my engineers reasons, and that is a thing a man in my position notices inside nine days whether he is on the hill or not."

"And the job."

"The job is there at the start of next term," said Oren Vey. "Not before, and not on Tuesday, and not as a way of being in a building. And I am not going to take the card back, and I am not going to chase you, and if you post it back before the term starts I will bin it myself and it will not go anywhere, and if you bring it in on your own two feet on the first morning of the term I will have a desk for you by the Thursday."

"Senn," said Oren Vey, before he put the phone down. "One more thing and it costs me nothing to say. You have spent a fortnight being extremely good at not being the man in the room, and I want you to know that the way you have done it has made you the man everybody rings. Those are the same act. Somebody rang your shop at six this morning and you gave them nineteen minutes and I know because a woman in Nine Bridges rang a number off a wall and it got as far as a man, and that is all I am going to say about how I know, and she is not the only one. I am not saying it to be cruel. I am saying it because I have watched four men do it and every single one of them thought it was modesty."

He put the phone down.

Marek Senn ruled a clean page at about twenty past two in the morning on the Thursday of week 13 at the bench in the relay room, with the door open and the extractor on, and it is the longest entry in this part of the book and it is the only one where nothing on it is a document.

**WHAT I KNOW — five things, and all five of them are people.**

*32. A key-holder without a certificate. Not a hinge and not a card and not a booklet and not a course. I have no licence and no assessment and no room and no list and no wage and no employer and I am nobody's responsibility, and on the Monday afternoon I stood on a bank above a lock for half an hour and a woman of thirty-three came down two steps four hundred more times, and there is no register anywhere in this city on which the words "Marek Senn, key-holder" could be written, which means there is no act by which it can be struck off, which means the only honest description of what I am is a man who is the fourth thing in three working buildings and got there by doing absolutely nothing.*
*33. Asha Reed has the case and has paid for it out loud and knows the price to the penny. Marda Szabo has said no to her in a market and turned it into a yes in a month. Corvin Ashby has said no to me on the pound and he was right and he said the reason better than I have said it to myself in a fortnight. Enid Sarkis has said the thing that nobody in this case has said, which is that a man who is hard to be angry at is the most powerful thing about him and he will not have noticed it happening.*
*34. A woman of sixty-one in Nine Bridges rang a number off a wall at six in the morning and got nineteen minutes out of a repair shop, and she told me not to come, and she was right, and I have written in four books this term that I am not the man who comes round and a woman on a counter has told me the mechanism of why that is not available to me and she is four for four.*
*35. O. V. put a request about section four in his own company's own system on the Tuesday of last week with a number on it, and he has never seen a page of the design record, and he did it because a man who has been the word in a room for a term put a number on a wall, and he has told me he cannot afford it and has not asked me for anything, and he is the second person in a week who has put his own name on a piece of paper and I have put my name on none.*
*36. Oren Vey, at eleven at night, from a station: I would be able to do it in a fortnight, and in four years there would be two thousand people whose fourth beat is a man in Lattice, and I would never be able to tell the day it happened from any other day. He is the most careful man in this case and he has just described my own private argument back to me in one sentence and he does not know that he has done it, and I am not going to tell him.*

**WHAT I WANT — one thing and it has a price on it now.**

*37. Somebody whose job it is. Not a technique and not a room and not a demonstration. A person, in a building, who is paid on a Wednesday to look at a wrist belonging to a stranger. I have wanted that since the fourth week. It is the only thing anybody in this case has ever needed and it is not a thing the Continuity Office can recommend, because it is not a failure of equipment, and it is not a thing the Institute can require, because nobody bought a plate and nobody supervised a fitting and there is no form anywhere on this earth with a name in it, and a woman of sixty-eight in a shop under my bedroom wrote her name and her wrist on a page in my own hand and initialled it and asked for somebody whose job that is, and I have had it in a drawer since Sunday.*

**WHAT IS NOT EVIDENCE — the six from week 9, the one from week 12, the one from the Friday, the one from the weekend, and two from this week, and one of the two is me.**

*38–43. T. V.'s brace. The hip variant. M. F.'s step. R. P.'s step, which does not have one and never did. O. F., who is not a source. The boy in the market who turned his head. 44. Five noes in a market on a Monday and a boy who said stop. 45. A contact that set in the shape of a man's weight in a lane, and a woman telling a stranger in plain words that it was on his hand and not on his wrist. 46. A woman counting to three on a rail. 47. A column in the Exchange book with Bea Nunn's name at the top of it and mine nowhere in it. None of it is evidence. All of it is the case. I have been using the word evidence for a term and the honest word is company, and I had that in week seven and I lost it.*
*48. MINE, and it is in the same list on purpose. Nineteen minutes on a telephone at six in the morning to a woman in Nine Bridges, and then a second thing on the same call, which was a government office and a woman at a till, and I was the best I have ever been at anything, and it is not a technique, it is not a release, it is not a share, and nobody consented to anything because nothing was shared, and if it is on this page at all it is because if I leave it out then the only good thing I did this week will be the thing I did not write down, and that is the way this book becomes useless.*

**WHAT I DO NOT KNOW — four things.**

*49. Whether there is a way to take a count out of a woman on a rail. Nobody in this city has one. The only one anybody has ever had in this case is a boy's two voices, which he got from being taught a card at twenty, and which have got better every week since, and which are the most frightening object I have ever seen, and N. O. does not want it and is right not to and I am not going to look for it.*
*50. Whether four people can be a room. I have handed three of them something to hold and the fourth one I have not got, and one of the three has said yes at a price and one of the three has said no with the best argument anybody has given me all term, and the fourth is a woman at a till who has told me that I am going to keep coming, and I have not said no, because saying no would be a lie, and I have not said yes, because I do not know how to.*
*51. What the Thursday is going to produce. A panel gave the department seven days and a deadline and a diary entry, and the department is waiting on a sentence from a government office with its own timetable, and I did not have to do anything for that, and I have worked out that the most important morning of my life is a thing that happens to me.*
*52. Whether the middle finger has moved. It will not have. Six weeks is his figure and it is not mine and I am not going to have an opinion, and he is not on the Weir round and he is not going to be on anything, and the Friday of week twelve is the day a hand can take a load and not the day a hand can do a practice, and I have written both halves of that sentence down because I am the only person in his life who keeps a book.*

**AND THE ONE THAT IS NOT UNDER ANY OF THE FOUR.**

*There is going to be a room and a date. The demonstration requirement has been hanging since the Friday of week nine with nothing behind it and it is going to have a room and a date in it by tomorrow lunchtime, and I will be able to say the date out loud to a person, and the room is going to be a room I do not control, and I am not going to be able to get into it, and there is no licence, and there is no technique, and the only person in this city who has done the thing that the sentence asks for was at the back door of a fish market at half past six in the morning on the Tuesday of week eleven, with a written consent in his own hand and a witness who put a stool out of sight and then said out loud, in a yard, that it was not a release and would not be given a name.*

*And I am not going to be the man in that room, and I have spent a fortnight finding out that not being the man in the room is not the same as not being the man everybody rings, and both of those are true, and neither of them cancels the other, and I have to go to work at eight.*

*Do not know, and it goes at the bottom because it is the only one I have been avoiding all week: whether I will be in that room on Tuesday. Not speaking. In it. There is a difference and the difference is a plate, and if a plate is fitted on me by an assessment board in a public room on a Tuesday, then the whole of the case I have spent a term on is a man who got the plate off one person and put one on himself, and I do not know how to write that sentence in a book, and I have known since Monday, and I have said nothing to anybody, and I have now written it down four times without acting on it, and I have noticed that counting is a way of not doing it.*
