## What this batch must do, in order

1. **Ch 61 — the four sides come back, and the answer is the same answer.** The Friday of week 18. **He says yes.** The outline's own words: taking the job is the volume's last honest mistake and it is not undone. **The scene must be a shop, a bench and a document, and it must not be triumphant.** He has a wage from the Monday of week 19 and he has said, in his own load book, that if he says yes it will be because a man did three things well and not because he worked out what the job is, and that the second is the only one he could defend to a board. **Vey's line to give him: he is not buying a man who agrees with him, he is buying a man who will write down what he finds, and the two are not the same job.** Also in this chapter: **the flood round, if it falls here, and a second dependence that is not cured.** And **the Exchange's monthly meeting is the first Wednesday of every month — the Wednesday of week 20 is the next one.**
2. **Ch 62 — 07:00 at the depot, and the intake at 09:30, which is a morning and not an evening.** **His first day as a Threadline technician, four days a week, Monday to Thursday, on a rota, with a supervisor who can put a hand on a practice of his.** **He learns what a service engineer already knows and what he has been arguing with for a term: that a standardized technique with a named holder produces fewer reportable incidents, and that this is a true thing, and that it is the true thing the hinge needs.** **And from twenty-five to ten, in the practical hall, forty-one people are taught revision 4-19 out of a manual that says something different from the card, and he is not in the room and is not permitted to be, and he is in the building, two and a half hours into his first morning.** **Rusk teaches it. Marek sits at the back and is not asked to say anything, as agreed. Rusk reads page four or does not read page four — decide it here and make it cost.**
3. **Ch 63 — the work, and the thing he is actually good at.** A technician's week. **Service calls, a van, a work order, and the discovery that a standardized technique is a piece of extraordinarily good engineering done by people who were never told what it was for.** **The fourth condition is load-bearing here and is not renegotiated: he is on company premises, on company equipment, under company insurance, four days a week, and there is no clause anywhere that lets him hold a practice alone, and there is now nobody he could ask.** **He is a key-holder on a company's rota and there is no register on which the fact can be struck off, and the lanyard gets him through doors.** Let him enjoy about four minutes of it before it turns.
