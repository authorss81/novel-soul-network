# Chapter 68 — None Present

She rang the shop at about ten past four on the Sunday afternoon of week nineteen, which was a rule she had set for herself in the second week of the term and had restated once, and which she broke eleven minutes before she said anything about why.

"I know what day it is," said Talia Venn. "I am aware of what day it is. I set the rule and I have not broken it until now and I am going to say the reason in nine seconds and then I am not going to refer to it again, because if we spend nine minutes on the day then I will not be able to say the other thing at all.

"Somebody rang me on Friday. A woman from an office in a building I have never been in, and she has got a title with *continuity* in it, and she has read what I wrote in a box in a form and she wants me to evidence it."

Marek Senn had the load book shut on the bench in front of him and the extractor off, because it was a Sunday and the shop was cold and he had not turned the light over the bench on.

"What did you write."

"None present. You know what I wrote because I told you on Tuesday and you did not write it down, which I have since decided was a kindness and not a lapse, and I am not going to argue about it." He heard a chair. "What she wants is not the count. She has not asked me for the count once. She wants to know how a resonance nurse on the Saltmarket route knows that no licensed holder was present or within reach at the material time on the Tuesday of week fourteen, at about ten past eleven, in a room, in a building, at a distance of about nine feet from a first-year's forearm.

"And Marek, I want you to understand the shape of the sentence she used, because I have been sitting with it for two days. She said, and I am giving you her words: *is there any person you can name who was in that room who could say so.*"

"And what did you say."

"I said I would need to think about it. Which is true, and which is the sentence I have been saying since week fifteen and which is a way of saying *no* with a delay on it, and you know that, and it has been four years' habit, and I heard myself do it."

The line was quiet for a second.

"I can name a person," said Talia Venn. "That is the difficulty and it is the whole of it and I have not said it out loud to anybody. I can name a man who was in that room. He is a member of staff in the practical department, he has been there twenty-two years, he has a certificate in a drawer in his house that he has never produced, and on the Tuesday of week fourteen he said a word out of habit from the front of that room, and a boy in the second row went off his own count because of it, and the front edge of the demonstrator frame came across about four inches at knee height and took the inside of a first-year's left forearm from the wrist to about four inches below the elbow, and she got her arm round about a second after it did, and there are four stitches in it and a review in nine days.

"And that is not a secret. Everybody knows. It was read into a record in a room on the Thursday of week fourteen and I was not in the room and I have read the account of it. So I can name him. I can name him in about two seconds, without checking anything."

"That is not the boy," said Marek Senn. "That boy is in a bed in Nine Bridges with two cut tendons in the same forearm and a middle finger that has not moved since before the term began, and he got those in a drill in the first week, three months before anybody put a rail on a frame, and he has never once been the reason for anything and he has asked for the last line of a card to be in his own handwriting so that it will not be mine. **Toby Ansell is the boy in the second row. Toby Ansell got the notebook out from under that rail with two fingers and pulled her forearm along it in one movement, and he has said in public, standing up, that he was one of about nine people who followed a word, and nobody has ever asked him to say it again.**"

"Then why have you not."

"Because if I put his name in a continuity return as *the person in the room who could say so*, then in four years a document in a regional office says that a member of staff said a word out of habit in a room, and a boy went off his count, and a young woman's forearm is on a form. **And that is a man with a fault on him, in a file, in his own words, and a boy who cannot hold a kettle as the reason.** I am not going to do that to him on a Sunday, or on any day, without him knowing that I am about to, and he cannot be asked, because he cannot be asked, because the man in question is the fourth condition of a share somebody signed."

Marek Senn did not say anything for about four seconds.

"Rusk," he said.

"I am not going to say the name," said Talia Venn.

"You were not going to say the name. I am going to say it. Davan Rusk, twenty-two years in the practical department, and he was in that room, and he said the word, and the boy is Tam Arrick, and the forearm is Rhoda Ilesanmi's, and the transcript line is yours to carry and not mine, and the count is two hundred and six and sixty-one are yours. You have all of that and you have had all of that since week fifteen. I have never once said it in a sentence and I have just said all of it in eleven seconds, so we are both out."

"I have been out since the Tuesday," said Talia Venn.

"Yes."

The shop was very quiet. A bus went past on the far side and the extractor did not come on, because it was a Sunday and it is on a timer and the timer does not know what day it is.

"Now the thing I rang about," she said. "And I am going to ask you for it and I would like you to say no quickly, because I have thought about it for two days and I have already decided I am not going to like the answer."

"Go on."

"The Monday of week nineteen. Nine thirty in the morning. Forty-one people in a hall." Her voice did not change. "**Who read page four out.**"

Marek Senn put his hand flat on the bench, on the shut load book.

"You said on the Tuesday that there was a man in a corridor on the Friday of week fifteen who asked a third-year to read a supplier's page to a room, and that the third-year said no, and you did not say the third-year's name, and I did not ask you for it, and I want to say that I noticed and I am not going to use it, and I am about to use it.

"And that is what I rang on a Sunday about. Not the boy. Not the forearm. **I want to know who read it, so that I can put a sentence in front of a woman with *continuity* in her title and be able to say: there was a member of staff in that building on the Monday of week nineteen who read a supplier's page out to forty-one people and put his own name against it in a file that goes to a regional office, and I can then say to her that a licensed holder was present in that department on the Monday, in a professional capacity, in a room of forty-one people, and the thing that happened on the Tuesday of week fourteen is not a thing that happened in a department with nobody in it who could be named. It is a thing that happened in a department that trains forty-one people a term in the exact sequence and gets it wrong at the front of the room instead of the front of the plate.

"That is a true sentence. It is the only true sentence I have got. And it needs one fact in it, and the fact is a man's name, and the man is a member of staff in the same department as the thing I am writing about, and I am not allowed to have it, and it is not being withheld from me. There is simply no route in this city by which a district contractor on the Saltmarket route is given the name of a member of staff in a practical department, in week nineteen, on a Sunday, and there is no form, and I have looked, and I did the looking on Friday afternoon in a library for about an hour and a half and I would like it minuted that I did the looking and did not ask you to do it."

He said nothing.

"I am going to ask you now," said Talia Venn, "and I want you to notice that I am asking you and not asking the department, and that the reason is that you are the only person I know who would tell me if the answer were no."

Marek Senn looked at the extractor that was not on.

"Yes," he said.

"Thank you. That is all. I am going to put the phone down—"

"No," he said. "You have not asked me and I am going to stop you asking, because I have been sitting here for about four minutes deciding whether to let you get to it, and that is the thing I have been doing since the Wednesday of week seventeen, and you caught me at it on Tuesday and you have caught me at it three times since, and I am not going to let you do it a fourth time in a shop on a Sunday.

"You are going to ask me whether I can get it. And I could."

There was a pause of about a second and a half.

"Say that again."

"I said I could get it. Not today and not this week, and not by asking a man I report to, and not in a way anybody would ever find, because I do not need to be clever. I have been in a depot eight working days. I have been in a building with a second shelf going upward and a visitor column at a gate and a bay with a carton nobody has labelled in it, and there is a person who works there who has a course list, because a company that runs courses keeps a list of who has done them, with names on it, because that is what the product is. **There is a list in the company I signed four sides to, eight days ago, and you have been refused the only version of it that exists in the public system, and I could see it, and neither of us has said that sentence in either direction in about four weeks and I am saying it now because you rang on a Sunday.**"

The line did not go.

"Right," said Talia Venn.

"I am going to say the rest of it and then I am going to be quiet, and I want the whole thing in the same breath, because if I say it in pieces I will get about nine words in and then I will start being reasonable.

"**No.** Not to you, not to a form, not to a woman with *continuity* in her title, not on a Sunday or any other day. And the reason is not the fourth condition and it is not a principle and it is not the lanyard, and if I let you have a version of that sentence then I have not refused you, I have delayed you, and you will be able to hear the difference and so will I, and you said in week fifteen that you would give me the same sentence in writing the day you were asked for anything else, and I would like to be the person who gives you the sentence the first time.

"The reason is that if a company's employee reads a company's list of named holders and then talks to a district contractor about what is on it, that is a leak. It is a small leak, it is a list of people who paid four hundred and forty pounds or were sent on the clock, and it is a leak that would be found in about nine minutes by a woman in a standards office who is good at her job and who would be right to look. And the person it would be found on is a technician who has been in post eight days and who has a contract that ends on the last Friday of week thirty and a fifth clause that says he takes the company's own course in his first month. **I would not be leaking a list. I would be asking a company for a job I have not finished and a card I have not got, in exchange for a thing I am employed to look after, and everybody in that sentence is me.**"

He stopped and let it sit.

"So that is no. And now the part that is worse, and I am going to say it because you have earned it and because it is the reason this is the first time in fourteen weeks I have been frightened of a thing I have done.

"I did not say no because I do not trust you. If anything it is the other way round and I have been living on the other way round for about a year and it is the only thing in this that has not needed any work. You have asked me for a row and a page and a name and I have said no three times and every one of the three was right, and you did not go round any of them, and that is not nothing.

"I said no because in about a month there is going to be a man in a room somewhere in this city with a card with his name typed on it, and the only person in the building who is any use with a fourth beat is going to be that man, and he is twenty and he cannot hold a kettle, and there is a day between now and the Monday he does the course. **And if it gets out that the only person in this city with any use in this is the technician at the supply company, then in about six weeks every district office that needs a release confirmed will want him, and in about four months somebody will want him to say the third step to somebody, and he will do it, because he is good at it and he will not be able to tell you why he cannot.** I have written that down. I wrote it on the Friday of week eighteen in a book in a shop. I have thought about almost nothing else for eight days. And if I hand you a list of names this week then I am a man who was in a company for eight days and came out with a list, and in about two years that is a story about a man who was in a company for eight days and came out with a list, and the story does not need the list to be true to ruin him.

"That is the reason. It is about a boy. It is the only refusal I have given anybody in fourteen weeks that is about a boy, and I did not expect it and I am not proud of it, because a refusal that is about somebody else's future is a refusal I have made on his behalf without asking him, and I have spent fourteen weeks refusing to do that."

The line was quiet for about four seconds.

"You are not to tell him," said Talia Venn.

"I am not going to tell him."

"You are not to tell him, and you are not going to write it down, and I want that understood in both directions, and I am saying it now so that neither of us has to think about it in a fortnight when it is a problem." She had not raised her voice once in nine minutes. "Do not tell him. Do not write it down. And do not do it again in about three weeks to prove that you did not do it in a fortnight."

"I was not going to do it in three weeks."

"I know you were not going to. That is what I am frightened of." A door shut on her end. "Right. I have said the thing and it is nine minutes and I have to go and do a drug chart. I am not going to say the other thing because we both know what it is going to be and we have said it eleven times since the term began and it has never once been a resolution.

"We had an hour."

"We had an hour," said Marek Senn.

"And Marek — one more, and it is the last one, and it is not about the boy.

"There is a box in a form in a regional office with *none present* written in it in my hand, and there is a woman with *continuity* in her title who wants to know how I know. And there is a man in a practical department who read a page out to forty-one people on the Monday and put his name against it. **Those two documents are not in the same record, and they are about the same building, and in about four years two people in two offices are going to try to join them up and one of them is going to manage it, and I would like to know whether you and I are on the same side of that when it happens.**"

"I do not know," said Marek Senn. "That is the true answer and you are not going to like it as much as the other answer."

"I have had nine minutes of not liking things," said Talia Venn, "and I have not once been on the same side of anything as anybody. Go and eat."

The line went.

Marek Senn sat at the bench in a cold shop for about ten minutes with the load book shut in front of him, and then he turned the light over the bench on, and then he turned it off again, and then he turned it on again, and he wrote.

*71. Sunday of week 19. She rang at ten past four and it was nine minutes and it is the first time she has rung me on a Sunday in fourteen weeks and she broke her own rule and told me she had broken it in eleven seconds so that I could not use it.*

*What I said no to, and what it actually was.* She asked me for a name. A man in a practical department who read a supplier's page out to forty-one people in a hall on the Monday of week nineteen and put his own name against it in a file that goes to a regional office. It is Davan Rusk. She had worked out that I had it and had not said it and she was right, and I said it inside eleven seconds with no reason and no cause and got all of it out, her count, her form, the boy, the forearm, and I have no idea why I did that and it was not planned and I have thought about it for two hours.

*And then she was going to ask me and I stopped her asking.* I have been letting her get to it since the Wednesday of week seventeen. Three times she has got to within nine words of it and three times I have said something reasonable first, and she caught me at it on the Tuesday and she has caught me at it three times since. **There is a list in the company I signed four sides to eight days ago with names on it, and she has been refused the only public version of it, and I said out loud on a Sunday that I could see it, and that neither of us has ever said that sentence in either direction in four weeks, and then I said no.** I want both of those on the same page because they happened about forty seconds apart.

*The reason.* Not the fourth condition. Not a principle. Not the lanyard. A leak, found in about nine minutes, by a standards officer who would be right to look, and the person it is found on is a technician eight days into a contract that ends on the last Friday of week thirty, and every single person in the sentence is me. And under that, the thing I am actually frightened of, which I have not written down anywhere until now: **in about a month the only person in this city who is any use with a fourth beat is going to be a boy of twenty who cannot hold a kettle and who will have paid four hundred and forty pounds for it or been sent on the clock, and if it gets out that the one useful person is the technician at the supply company, then in six weeks every district office that needs a release confirmed will want him, and in about four months somebody will want him to say the third step to somebody, and he will do it because he is good at it.** That is the reason. It is a refusal I have made on a boy's behalf without asking him, and I have spent fourteen weeks refusing to do that.

*What we did not say.* She told me not to tell him and not to write it down and not to do it again in three weeks to prove that I did not do it in a fortnight, and she said she said it so that neither of us has to think about it in a fortnight when it is a problem. We had an hour. Neither of us said anything else. I have not said the word sorry and she has not either and we were eleven minutes into a nine-minute call.

*The two records, and she is right, and this is the only thing on the page that is about the case and not about us.* There is a box in a form in a regional office with *none present* in it in her hand. There is a man in a practical department who read a page out to forty-one people on the Monday and signed it. **Two documents, one building, two offices, and in about four years two people are going to try to join them up and one of them is going to manage it, and she asked me whether we are on the same side of that and I said I do not know, and it is the first time in fourteen weeks I have given her a straight no about a fact instead of a principle, and I would like somebody to notice that the straight no is the one that does not cost anybody anything.**

*Do not know, and it is a new one.* Nobody has asked me about the review. Not one person, in three weeks, in a shop, in a depot, in a car park, in a corridor, and I have not told one person that I do not know who calls it, and I have been waiting for about fourteen weeks for somebody to ring me about it and nobody has, and condition six went with condition three on the Wednesday of week sixteen in silence and it was the only thing in this city that ever put in writing that a board would sit at the end of a term and examine me. **It is still sat. It is at the end of term two, in the last week of that term, on the Friday, and there is nobody with standing to call it, and I have got about eleven weeks, and I have written the number down twice this month and it is the only number on this page and I have not done one single thing about it.**
