# Chapter 82 — Not by a Company

The letter was on the mat when he got the shutter up at about ten to seven on the Tuesday morning of week twenty-two, and it had come in an envelope with a district mark on it and it was four paragraphs and it had a reference number in the top corner and it was signed by a man whose title is *continuity and returns*.

He read it standing up in the cold with his coat still on.

It said what the man had said on the telephone on the Sunday. It said it in the way a thing is said when it is going to be looked at twice, which is slower and worse and completely correct. Four thousand one hundred and six registered technique bundles in the district. Nine hundred and forty with a blank upstream field. Two hundred and eleven revised at least once since registration. Four assessors, two of them part time. And then two paragraphs that were not on the telephone, and the first of them was the reason, and the reason was the same and was better on paper than it had been in a cold shop, and the second of them was one sentence.

*I am not able to trace the field and I am not able to say why the field was left blank in any individual case, and I record here that a blank field is a record of an absence and that a record of an absence is the easiest class of record in this district to complete incorrectly. This office has been asked to trace the field on four occasions in nineteen years. On each occasion the request came from a company.*

*This letter records a telephone conversation. It is not a determination, it creates no obligation on any party, and it should not be produced in any proceeding without this office being asked first.*

He read the last sentence three times, standing in the cold, with the shutter motor ticking as it cooled.

That was the whole of it. A man had done a decent thing on a Sunday for no reason that was any use to anybody, had then spent four days turning it into four paragraphs, and had put a sentence in the last one that made the letter worthless as evidence and completely worthless as an answer, and had signed it, and had posted it on the Monday as he said he would, and it had come on the Tuesday.

He read the second paragraph twice, which is the one about the reason, and the reason was better on paper than it had been on a telephone, and he understood why. On a telephone a person can be doubted. On a printed sheet with a reference number on it, in a building, from an office that has been there nineteen years, the sentence *a record of an absence is the easiest class of record in this district to complete incorrectly* stops being a man's opinion at about the fourth word and becomes a fact about the world, and there is no way to write back.

He did the arithmetic anyway, because he could not help it, and it took about four minutes. Four thousand one hundred and six bundles. Nine hundred and forty blank. Two hundred and eleven revised at least once. Four assessors, two of them part time, so on any given Tuesday there are very likely two people in this city who are allowed to sign something about a technique bundle, and both of them are part time, and the reason that matters is not workload. **It matters because there is nobody to contradict them inside the room. A department of four with two of them part time is a department where a sentence said in a corridor on a Tuesday has no other sentence said in the same corridor on the Tuesday after it, and in about four years a document will exist that nobody in the world has ever seen the other side of.**

He also worked out the other thing, which was that the man had told him a number he had no use for in exchange for nothing, and had then spent four days converting it into four paragraphs so that it would exist, and had then put a sentence in the last line that made the four paragraphs unusable. That is not caution. Caution would have been a shorter letter.

He took it into the back and got the bench lamp and pulled the drawer out from under the bench — the shallow one, the one with a bent screwdriver and two grommets in it — and there was page four in there, photocopied, the four-colour diagram gone flat to grey, four numbered steps, and the line in capitals about another voice.

He put the letter in on top of it and shut the drawer.

Two things in a drawer, and one of them was a company's own page with no page before the page on it, and the other was a letter with a reference number in the corner that said in its own last line that it should not be produced in any proceeding without the office that wrote it being asked first. He could produce neither. He could not show the letter to a woman in Bower Street because she would have to write *not by a company* under it and the letter would say *not by this office* and the two of them together would be a sentence about a gap, and a sentence about a gap is a pattern, and a pattern gets escalated, and the first thing that happens to a record with a reference number is that the people who wrote in it find out that they wrote in it.

He wrote the reference number on the inside of the drawer front in pencil, because in about four years he was going to want to know whether the thing had ever been followed up, and a pencil line on a drawer is not a document. Then he shut it and washed his hands, which he does before he writes anything in the book, and it is the only part of the routine that has not changed since the Tuesday of week fourteen.

The second call of the morning came at about ten past nine from a woman who gave her name as **Rae Tolley** and who said she had telephoned the shop on the Thursday of week twenty-one and had not been put through to a person, and who had left a message, and who had then sent a written request for one page of her own file to a private address, and who had then sent the same request to a district office in Crown Terrace on the Friday, which she apologised for and which she said she had done in about four minutes of temper.

Marek Senn stood at the bench and worked out, in about two seconds, which page she meant, and that she was one of the eleven, and that she was not the one in the sling.

"You've asked for it in two places."

"I've asked for it in two places and I have told both of them why." Her voice did not change at all. "**I have read my own line, Mr Senn. That's all of it. I have read a line with my name and my induction date and my site and the word NONE typed into a box, and I have never had a condition in my life, and I have never worked at height above about a ladder.** And now there is a copy of that line on a counter in a market in Lattice and nine men have stood in a canteen and read it, and I do not know one of them and I do not want to know one of them, and I would like it back."

"How many copies do you think there are."

"I've stopped counting. I counted to four and then a woman on my site said she'd seen it in a car park." A door. "I want two things and I'm going to say both of them and then I'm going to let you go, because I am not going to be a person who rings twice. **One. Destroy it. Written, to me, from an address, saying it has been destroyed. I am not asking for it back, I am asking for it not to exist.**"

"That will happen. There is a letter going out to all eleven that says the note we hold about them is destroyed at the end of the term on request in writing, and I have read it, and it is not a policy and the woman who wrote it knows it is not a policy and has been told to expect a question about it from a man with a line manager."

"Thank you." Nothing for a second. "Two. **I want somebody to tell me why it said NONE.** Not you. I have read the box and I know what NONE means in it and what I do not know is whether it is a true thing or a lazy thing, and if it is a lazy thing then somebody in that company is typing NONE into a box on a form for a person they have not spoken to, and that is not a data breach, that is somebody not doing a job, and I would like to know which one it is.**"

He looked at the letter face down on the bench.

"I can tell you that it is a box," he said, "and I can tell you that nobody in that building has spoken to you about it, and I cannot tell you the rest, and the reason I cannot tell you the rest is that the answer is in a system in a building I work in and the only person who can open it is a woman who is between two jobs and who has already done one thing this week that is not in any policy her company has, and I am not going to make her do a second one for a woman she has never met on a Tuesday morning."

"That is a no."

"That is a no with the reason attached in eleven seconds and I am not going to pretend to you that I have not given you four sentences of politeness first," said Marek Senn. "**I would rather you had the true one. The true one is that there is a box and nobody has asked you anything, and the box got what it got because asking you would have taken a phone call and typing is a key on a keyboard.**"

"Right," said Rae Tolley. "That's the first useful thing anybody in that industry has said to me in a fortnight and it is worse than a form." A pause. "I'll be waiting for the letter."

"You will get it."

"And Mr Senn — I am not going to be one of the eleven who rings a company back, and I would like that written down somewhere, because I have decided it and there is nobody to decide it with."

"It is written down," said Marek Senn, "in a book in a shop, and you will never read it, and that is the correct arrangement for everything in this case and it is the first arrangement in a term that has made me feel better about the state of things."

She put the phone down and he wrote nothing down, which was the fourth time in a week and the fourth one that had cost somebody something.

She rang the shop at about half past ten on the Tuesday morning of week twenty-two, and he had the letter face down on the bench and had not decided what to do with it, and she had the sound of a building with a lot of metal in it.

"Two things and they are both mine and neither of them is a question," said Talia Venn. "The first one is that I sent Keogh eleven lines at twenty past eight this morning. Eleven lines, from my own address, dated today, saying that a district practice standards office does not hold company personnel records, cannot produce them and is not going to try, and that if a regional office wants a company register then the route is the company and not me. I sent it before anybody asked me to send it and I have put the date at the top in my own hand, and I am telling you the date for a reason and the reason is that in about four years somebody is going to read it as the first step in something, and I would like it on the record in my own writing that it went on the Tuesday of week twenty-two before anybody had asked me for it."

"That is the right thing to do."

"It is the only thing there was to do and I did it in about four minutes and I have not thought about it since, and if you say *that is the right thing to do* again I am going to hang up." A door. "The second one is mine and it is worse. I have to give a regional office an answer today and I have written it, and I am going to read it to you because you are the only person in this city who will understand what a blank line is, and because I am not going to have written it and said nothing, which is what I did on the Sunday of week twenty-one and it cost me a fortnight."

"Go on."

"It says: *the requested material is not held by a company.* That's the whole of the answer. Eleven words. And then there is a box underneath it that says *reason if not held* and I have not written anything in it, and I have written the date and I have signed it, and I am going to send it at about eleven."

He looked at the envelope face down on his bench.

"You said you would not write the reason."

"I am not writing the reason. **The reason is a fifteen-word phrase in a district minute of the fifteenth week of the term that I wrote myself to keep a man's name out of a document, and it is the only sentence in a file in this city that describes what you are, and it is in a room I have never been inside, and if it is in my return then in about two years a regional office has both halves and the two halves go together and a person who is not independent of the party the request concerns is a phrase that a room can act on.** A blank line in a return is how a whole term started. I know that because I have watched it, and I am choosing it on purpose, and I want you to hear me choose it, because you have been putting things in boxes all term and you have never once had to choose a blank."

"Nobody has standing to—"

"I know. **I have now carried a fact about a review that nobody has standing to know and I cannot hand it to anybody, and I am about to hand this office a second fact I cannot explain, and a woman does not get to keep two of those in a year without becoming the woman who keeps facts.** That is not a complaint. It is arithmetic. I am telling you the arithmetic so that you know what my side of the shelf looks like this morning."

The shop was quiet. Somebody went past on the far side of the road with a trolley.

"Say the other one," said Marek Senn.

"There is no other one."

"You said there were two things and neither of them was a question, and then you told me both of them, and you have not asked me one thing, which means you have been standing in a corridor all morning deciding not to."

Talia Venn did not answer for about two seconds, which was two seconds more than she needed.

"**Saturday," she said. "There is a room on the third floor of a building in Bower Street and there are about twenty women on somebody's books and a woman from a district office has a list with a tick on it and not a name, and I asked her on the Monday whether I could sit in and she said the room was full.** I am not one of the twenty. I am a district contractor on the Saltmarket route and I have asked four times to watch a round in this city and been refused three and once told it would need a name, and I am not going to use the word *independence* in a room I am not in. So I have got nothing to do on the Saturday and I have been not thinking about it since Monday."

"Go on."

"That is all of it. **I was not going to ask you whether you were going.** I want that said first. I have known since the Sunday you were going and I have worked out that you are not going to tell me and I have worked out that if I ask you then in about four years there is a sentence in this city that says a woman in Bower Street put a man in a room on a Saturday, and I have got one of those already and I am not collecting a second." She took a breath. "And I asked anyway. That is the thing I have to say. **I stood in a corridor at about nine this morning and I asked a man who had told me on a Sunday that he was going, and I did it anyway, and I am not going to be a woman who says she did not.**"

Marek Senn sat on a stool in his own shop with a district letter face down in front of him.

"No," he said. "I am not coming on the Saturday."

"Thank you," said Talia Venn. "That is all I needed and it took nine seconds and I am going to spend the rest of the morning on the thing you have just done for me, which is made the fact that you are not coming into something I have to carry, and I want you to have exactly no credit for it, because you have not got a reason and I have not asked you for one, and I am going to go and write *not by a company* in a box with a date on it and that is my morning and it has nothing to do with you."

"Talia—"

"**Do not.**"

"Fine."

"**Do not be sorry at me.** That is not a thing I want. I have got eleven words and a blank line going to a regional office at eleven o'clock and you have given me the truth in one word in a second and a half, and it is the cheapest thing anybody has given me this month and I am not going to thank you for it and I am not going to be gracious about it and I am not going to ring you on Thursday to check you have not been talking.**"

"That was not the first time."

"It was the first time you have said a thing to me that you had already written down on a Sunday and did not tell me, and I am not going to be the woman who makes a list of them, and I have made one, and it has two things on it and one of them is from the sixteenth week." A chair, in a different building. "Ring me on a Tuesday. Do not bring me a document. And Marek — the Saturday is not a favour and it is not a favour you are doing me, and I have known that since Monday, and I asked anyway, and we are both going to leave that alone."

The line went at about ten past eleven.

He sat on the stool for a while. Then he turned the letter over and read the last sentence again, and then he did the thing he had been not doing since about eleven o'clock the previous morning, which was to get the docket out and turn it over and write on the back of it in a small upright hand, four words and a date.

*Tuesday. Form exists. No target.*

He did not write the name. He had given the name away on a Friday morning in a shop to a man of forty-one who said he would not ask for a telephone number, and the name was on a form in a system in a building on the far side of the city, and the question on the form had no target on it, and on the Wednesday of that week a man of forty-one was going to ring a man of twenty-three and ask it.

He had had thirty-one hours to do one thing about it and he had done nothing, and on the Thursday of week sixteen he had refused a boy of twenty on the boy's behalf without asking the boy and had written it down in a book as the only refusal he had ever made that was not for himself.

Ruslan Petric was twenty-three. He had put a hand-written sheet through a box at a post office on Weaver Street in week sixteen and got three words in ballpoint on a playing card, and he had stood up in a market in front of about twelve people and said a thing in the first person with his name at the end, and a woman of sixty-one had invented a rule about the card it went into, and it was in a book in a market at forty lines and it was not going to be read out until the first Wednesday of next month.

Marek Senn put the docket in his pocket and did not ring him, and the reason had changed since Friday, and the change made it worse.

A week ago the reason had been that the boy might be turned into a reason. The reason now was that Petric was not a boy, and Petric did this on purpose, in rooms, for a living, in the first person, with his own name at the end, and a man of forty-one was going to ring him in about thirty hours and put him in a room with a form and no company in it, and there is no version of a warning that is not a man in that room deciding that a technician knew in advance, and that is the exact sentence that did the first damage in this city.

**I did not ring him, and this time it is not the same act, and it is worse, because this time I am protecting a man who would have wanted it.**

He put the docket back in the pocket and got a hand set and did about forty minutes of work that he could do without thinking, and at about half past ten he wrote four lines on the back of the same docket, because the docket was already a document and there was no point in pretending otherwise by using a second one.

*Ring him. Do not ring him. Ring him and say what. Say the fourth thing in a corridor and a woman copies it out.*

Then he tore the docket in half, because a docket with a date on it in this shop is a document, and he had watched a woman in a market on the Saturday of week twenty-one say that she wanted two things in a book and not one of them, and he was not going to be the reason a book in a market had to carry an accident on top of a Saturday.

He put the two halves in the bin and wrote the twenty-five that mattered on the docket he would book the job on, and then he went out to a van in Nine Bridges for the morning.

He wrote the entry from about half past nine at night, and it is the shortest he has written in six weeks, and the lamp stayed on.

*85. Tuesday of week 22, half past nine at night. Three documents moved today and one of them is sitting in a drawer under this bench, and I am going to write about the other two and skip the drawer, and the reason is that the drawer is settled and the other two are not.*

*The letter, and the sentence at the end of it.* Four paragraphs, a reference number in the corner, signed on the Sunday, posted on the Monday, on the mat at ten to seven. **And the last line says: this letter records a telephone conversation, it is not a determination, it creates no obligation on any party, and it should not be produced in any proceeding without this office being asked first.** A man took a thing he did on a Sunday for nothing and made it into something a person could file, and then he put a sentence in the end of it that makes it unusable as an answer and unusable as evidence, and he signed that too. **There are now two things in that drawer. One of them is a company's own page with no page before the page on it.
