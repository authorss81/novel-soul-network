## What the ten chapters did

- **81** — a man asks a company for a door and gets one in three seconds, and the price is a box number a fitter would not write down. A second out-of-hours call, and a man who has decided not to be a man you could ring.
- **82** — a letter from a Continuity Office with a reference on it, whose last line says it should not be produced in any proceeding. Rae Tolley's two requests, one refused and one given the true sentence. **Talia asks whether he is going to be in a room on the Saturday and he says no, in one word, in a second and a half, with no reason — the first outright false sentence he has put into this case, and he has put it in about a person.**
- **83** — a man of forty-one rings a man of twenty-three in the wrong order on purpose, gives him a form with no supplier, no lot and no date range, and no company in the room. **And a prediction written down on the Wednesday night, which Chapter 88 pays off exactly thirteen days later.**
- **84** — the out-of-hours rota, a service callout at a consolidation point, a lot onward, a laminated card on a control post with no name and no date on it.
- **85** — twelve pounds is a card rate and not a release callout, and the difference between the two numbers is the whole reason he is standing at a gate. **Rafi Pell says the true sentence out loud, in the first person, with his name at the end.** A free course on the Tuesday of week 24.
- **86** — the third floor, a rule that is not a rule about a lanyard, and a woman who looks at the lanyard and then at the buttons and says nothing.
- **87** — Enid Sarkis's five lines, a list copied out with no date on it, **and *leave the date off it*, which he uses on a second person nine days later without asking her.**
- **88** — **the movement.** A boy of twenty-one puts his hand up in a room of about eleven, tells nine strangers he has been doing it for two years, and a woman crouches down and says *that is the fourth beat*, and nobody in that room knew. **And the confession: fourteen days after the lie, at ten past ten on a Tuesday night, he tells her he was there.**
- **89** — the Exchange goes to forty-one lines and stops, Ena Truslove's card is read out in four minutes, and a man says the true sentence in a market, once, for nothing, and it goes into a woman who has a cousin and not into a file.
- **90** — **the review is called by a form**, a girl of nineteen decides to read her own two hundred and forty words aloud, a chair is laid by a booking clerk who had never done it in four years, and Talia Venn rings on a Friday and says she is not going to be in the room, and gives him the one sentence he is forbidden to say on the day.
