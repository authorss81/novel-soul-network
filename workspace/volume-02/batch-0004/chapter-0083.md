# Chapter 83 — A Tuesday With No Target

The hand set was a conveyor stop on a line in a kitchen in Nine Bridges and it had nine readings on a piece card and the piece card wanted them in a different order, and he had been on it since half past eight, and it was about eleven minutes past nine when the telephone went.

The practical building is quiet in the mornings for about forty minutes and then it is not, and the forty minutes is when the corridor fills with people carrying things they have been told to bring back, and he had counted eleven of them go past the door of the little room he was in, which had a bench in it and a vice on the end of it and a kettle that made a noise like a man clearing his throat.

He wrote the ninth reading down, put the meter down, and picked the phone up on the ninth ring.

The telephone went at about eleven minutes past nine on the Wednesday morning of week twenty-two, on a bench in the practical building at the Institute with a hand set in pieces in front of him and a piece card with nine readings on it that he had done in the wrong order.

Ruslan Petric said his name into it twice because the first time there was a noise off a corridor.

"Petric. Ruslan Petric. Third year, maintenance track. I did not think this telephone would ring, so if I am in the wrong place—"

"You are not in the wrong place," said the man. "My name is Oren Vey. I am the operations director of a supply company that has spent four days being discussed on a counter in Lattice Ward, and I am going to use about two minutes of your morning and then I am going to let you get on."

Ruslan Petric put the hand set down on the bench so that he would not break it.

"Right."

"I am going to do this in the wrong order on purpose," said Oren Vey, "because I have found in nine years that the first thing you say to a person decides what they hear for the rest of the call. **I do not want anything from you. I want to ask you a question, and I want to give you a form, and I want there to be no target on the question.**"

"That is the opposite of how that normally goes."

"It is exactly how that normally goes, actually, but I have never seen it done and I have been the other side of it about four hundred times, so I am not going to pretend I am inventing it." A door, and then a car. "Here is the thing, and then you can decide. From Monday, my company has started recording, at every intake in this district, what a supplier says when we ask them where the practice we buy was first recorded. Not the field. The answer. In their own words, unedited, with a date, and the name of the person who asked."

"That is not the same as the field."

"No. It is deliberately not the same as the field, and it took me nine minutes on a Monday morning to work out that the field is unfixable and this is fixable, and I want you to know that I lost something to get it." A pause. "**And I want you to know that I am not going to put a company person in the room with you, and that is not a courtesy. It is that I have spent four days watching a company that publishes a document with no source in it, and I have worked out that the question I need asking cannot be asked by anybody I pay.**"

"Who is on the form."

"You are. That is what I am telephoning about."

Ruslan Petric did not say anything for a moment. Somewhere off the corridor a man was rolling something on a pallet and the noise came and went.

"Read me the question," he said.

"The question is: *we hold a training bundle for a supervised release sequence, reference 4-19, modules one and two. We hold a supplier's assembly record. On that record, the field for the originating practice is blank. Please state what your organisation holds in relation to the practice from which that sequence was derived.*" Oren Vey read it flat and slowly. "That is the whole of it. There is no supplier's name on it. There is no lot on it. There is no date range on it. There is nothing on that form that tells you which of about nine hundred bundles in this district I am asking you about, and that is on purpose, and I want you to be suspicious of it, because if I had put a target on it you would have been able to write a sentence that was only true about that target, and in about four years somebody would have quoted it."

"Have you asked anybody else this week."

"I have asked nobody. You are the first."

"Why me."

"Because you are the only person in this city who has put a sheet of paper through a box in order to find out whether a thing can be removed from a record," said Oren Vey. "**And because I was given your name by a man of twenty-two who works for me and who did not have to, and who has not given me a telephone number and never will, and I have sat in an office for four days deciding whether to ring you and I have decided that a man who gives a name and not a number is telling me something about how the whole thing has to be done.**"

Ruslan Petric looked at the piece card with nine readings on it in the wrong order.

"Can I ask you two things and you can refuse both."

"Go on."

"One. Who is going to be in the room with me."

"Nobody," said Oren Vey. "Not me and not anybody who works for me. You will get the form and you will get it by post and there will be a name on the envelope and no company in the room, and if anybody from my company rings you about it before the form arrives you are to ring this number and say so, and I will deal with it and I will not argue with you about it."

"And two." He shifted the hand set a half turn on the bench. "If the answer is that they hold nothing, will you write it down in the way I am about to ask you to write it down?"

"Go on."

"**In the person's own words, unedited, with the date, and the name of the person who asked. And a copy to me. And nothing in an intake system that has got a reference number on it that I cannot see.**"

There was nothing on the line for about four seconds.

"**That is the whole of my instruction," said Oren Vey. "That is the instruction I have given to about nine people in four days and I have fought two managers about the word *unedited* and lost one of them. Say it again slowly and I am going to write it down while you say it.**"

Ruslan Petric said it again slowly, and the operations director of a supply company in this city wrote it down in a car outside a building, and when he had finished he read it back and Petric said yes to the wording, and Vey said, "Right. That is done. Now the last thing and then I will let you go, and this is the only part of the call I have not planned."

"Go on."

"You are going to say no."

"No," said Ruslan Petric. "I am going to say yes, and I want to do it in the right order, which is that I am going to say yes and then tell you what it costs and then tell you why I am doing it anyway, and if I do it in that order then you cannot use any of it against me later and I cannot be used against you later, and both of those matter more than they used to."

"Go on."

"**The first cost is that on about the first of next month there is going to be a piece of paper in a system on the far side of this city with my name at the top of it and nothing else, and in about four years somebody is going to use it as evidence that a company in this district asked a student about a practice and got a blank, and they are not going to read the rest of it, and I will be the first name in a company file in this city for about eleven years over a question with no target on it.** The second cost is that I am going to be the fourth thing in a room. I have been the fourth thing in five buildings in six weeks and I did not know that was what the word was until a man in a lanyard told me in a market in about eleven words, and I have had a fortnight to be annoyed about it and I have not been annoyed for one of it, and I am telling you it is a cost so that you know I know."

"And the reason you are doing it anyway."

"Because there is a box about nine hundred yards from where I am sitting and I have put a sheet in it and got three words back in ballpoint and the three words were *it was not there*, and I have had a form in a company for five weeks with a line in it that describes exactly what I did, and I asked for it to be taken out and somebody looked and wrote me three words and did not pretend there was more to give me." He turned the hand set over. "**Your question has no target on it. That is the first form anybody has ever handed me with nothing to aim at, and I have been handed about eleven of them this term and every one of them had a department on it, and a department on a form is a place where a sentence goes that is only true about a department.** So yes. And because I would like there to be one thing in this city where the question came before the answer, and today is the day it does, and I am the one holding it, and nobody took a vote."

"Agreed," said Oren Vey. "It goes out on the Tuesday. It will be at you in about four days. And Marek—"

He stopped.

"Don't," said Ruslan Petric.

"I was going to say that he will not hear about this from me."

"**I know he will not hear about it from you, and I know why, and I worked that out in about four seconds on the sixteenth week and I have never once said it out loud to him, and I am not going to start on a Wednesday morning with a man in a car.**" He picked the hand set back up. "Send the form. I will fill it in on the day and I will not fill it in early, and that is not me being dramatic, that is because a form filled in on the day is a form filled in on the day."

---

He did not fill it in on the day, and he did not fill it in on the Thursday either, and both of those were decisions and not forgetfulness.

On the Wednesday afternoon he went back to the kitchen in Nine Bridges and did the ninth reading properly and wrote up the whole stop, and the foreman on that line was a man of about thirty-four called Ozzie Wray who had been in the job eleven years and who asked him, halfway through, whether he had done the release course.

"No," said Ruslan Petric.

"Never?"

"Never. I do not have one."

"Right." Ozzie Wray wrote a number on the back of his own hand. "**Do you want one? Because my old fella had one and he was a different man afterwards, and I mean that in about nine different ways and not one of them is a bad one, and the one that annoys me is that he got better at the job and worse at asking for a day off.**"

"No," said Ruslan Petric, and then, because a man of about thirty-four had just told him in nine words what nobody had ever told him and because he had spent three weeks working out that somebody, somewhere, had to be able to answer it, he said the thing he had been carrying. "**Actually — can I ask you what the word is for.**"

"Oz, the confirmation?"

"Yeah."

"It's for the pallet that isn't ready." Ozzie Wray did not stop what he was doing. "That's it, that's the whole of it. She says the word, the lift goes, and if she does not say it then somebody has either walked into it or walked off it, and either way you want a person to have said something before it moves rather than afterwards. That's it. Eleven years and about four million pallets."

"Right," said Ruslan Petric.

"You all right?"

"I'm fine."

"You've gone quiet."

"I've been told about that before today," said Ruslan Petric, and Ozzie Wray laughed at him and went back to the line, and Ruslan Petric stood at a conveyor stop in a kitchen in Nine Bridges for about four seconds with a piece card in his hand and the fourth beat about eleven feet from him in a woman with a lanyard's mouth, described in about nine words by a man who has never heard of it, and understood that it was going to be like that for the rest of his life and that nobody was ever going to connect the two sentences, and that this was the good news.

He filled it in on the Friday of week twenty-two, in about six minutes, on a bench in the practical building with the extractor on, and he wrote four lines and signed it and put it in an envelope with the address printed on a label, and then he took a piece of card and cut one of them out of a box in his own rucksack, and he wrote on it in ballpoint, in a small upright hand, and he did it standing up at the counter in the practical building because he wanted somebody to see him do it and there were four people in the room and all four looked and none of them said anything.

It said this:

*On the Wednesday of week twenty-two I was asked by a company to state, in my own words, what my organisation holds in relation to the practice from which a supervised release sequence is derived. I am not going to answer that question in a telephone conversation and I am not going to answer it on a form that has come out of a system, and the reason is that both of those put your answer inside your building.*

*I am asking instead: answer it in your own words, on your own paper, with the date and the name of the person who asked, and send it to a box, and tell me which box. If you cannot answer it, I am asking you to say that you cannot, in the same way, and I am asking for that in the same envelope, because a company that says it holds nothing has told me something and a company that does not reply has told me nothing and both of those are worth having and only one of them is worth having by Friday.*

*Ruslan Petric.*

He read it twice. Then he put the playing card in his coat pocket, where the other one was, and he went out at about twenty past four and got a bus, and he got off at the top of Weaver Street at about ten to five because he wanted to walk the last bit.

The box was on a corner and it had been on that corner for longer than he had been in this city and it had a name on the plate about the post town and no name on the plate about the town it stood in.

He put the envelope in at about four minutes to five.

**It is in there now, and it was in there about two hours before the woman he copied it to had even opened her own copy, and nobody in this city voted for it, and there is no form anywhere in the world for what a person does when they would rather own a question than be handed an answer.** It will be collected tomorrow. It is four lines and a signature and a box number that came back to him in ballpoint three words and a sentence in a market that a woman of sixty-one invented in about nine seconds so that a thing in a book could not be taken out of it without the taker's name going in the fourth column.

He walked back down Weaver Street to the bus stop in the light with his hands in his pockets, and he was not frightened and he was not proud, and what he was, and it took him the length of a street to work out, was **tired**, in the specific way a person is tired when they have spent five weeks learning that a rule only governs a page and then finding out that it governs a telephone call as well, and that the only thing that has ever got anybody anywhere in this city is a piece of paper put somewhere by a person who then has to live in the same city as it.

The bus came. He got on it at the front and paid and sat down at the back, and there were about nine people on it, and he got out at the Lattice side and walked the last of it because it was quicker than waiting, and at about ten past five on the Friday evening he went past a shop in Lattice Ward with the shutter down and the lamp over the bench on, and there was a man in there with a book shut in front of him and a sheet of paper face down on the bench.

He did not go in. He had no reason to go in, and he would have had to decide in about a second and a half what a man of twenty-two in a shop was doing about a man of twenty-three's Wednesday, and there is no answer to that which is worth the two minutes it would take, and he has spent a term finding that out.

---

He wrote the entry on the Saturday morning with the shutter up and the extractor on, and it is eleven lines long, and it is the shortest he has ever written and he is aware that the shortness is the finding.

*86. Saturday of week 22, and this is the shortest page in this book and I am leaving it that way because I have got nothing to put in it.*

*The Wednesday came and went. A man of forty-one rang a man of twenty-three, and there was a form, and there is no company in the room, and he said yes.*

*And a prediction, made on a Wednesday night about a man in this city who does not know I am making it, and I am putting it in here because in about eleven days I am going to find out whether I am right and I do not want to be able to say afterwards that I did not write it down.* **A person who is good at a thing and has never once been asked where they learned it will describe that thing correctly, in about nine words, to a stranger, on a line, in a doorway, for the rest of their life.** I have watched it four times. A man in a sling has described a radio operator in eleven words to a nurse and a technician. A man on a bank has described a wall leg. A woman of sixty-eight has described a plate in five lines. A man of about thirty-four on a conveyor has described a confirmation as being for the pallet that is not ready, which I have not been told and am predicting, because that is what the sentence has been every single time it has been said in this city in front of me.

*And the reason that is the finding, and it took me until half past nine tonight to be able to put it down.* **I have spent fourteen weeks working out that a slot shaped like a person is portable and cheap and free and that the name is the only part anybody can afford, and I have been carrying that about as a design fault. It is not a design fault. What is stable about the shape is that the understanding sits in a working person's mouth and does not need a card at all, and a man with no card will hand it to the next man he works beside at a word, in a doorway, for nothing, forever.** That is why it is good engineering. A certificate can be lost, mislaid, refused and never issued, and the practice underneath it goes on in a corridor the whole time. **The card is not what is doing the work. The card is the receipt, and a receipt is what you show a company that has stopped answering its telephone, and the entire edifice is standing on about nineteen men in this city who have never been issued anything and have never once been asked to account for it.**

*And therefore, in about eleven days, something is going to happen, and I am writing it down tonight so that I cannot pretend on the Friday that I did not see it.* He is going to be given a piece of paper with his name on it that says he is competent to confirm a release to a learner, and it is going to be free, and it is going to be issued by a woman with a diary who is not doing anything wrong, and he is going to hold it and feel safer, and **he is going to be safer, and that is going to be the exact reason it works, and in about four years somebody is going to ask him whether he needed it, and he is going to say no, and he is going to be right, and both of those things are going to be in the same sentence.** I cannot stop that and neither can anybody else and I have known it since the Friday of week twenty-one, and the only thing I have been able to do about it for fourteen weeks is write it down in a book that a woman of sixty-one said is a different kind of object now that somebody is annoyed rather than frightened, and I have not got an answer and I am not going to get one and the prediction is the whole of what this page has.
*How do I know that. I do not know that.* **I know that a form exists with no target on the question and a name on it that I gave away on a Friday morning in a shop, and I knew on the Tuesday that it was going to be asked on the Wednesday, and I have now had three days of not ringing him and three days of not asking anybody whether it happened, and I have not asked one person, and the reason I have not is that asking is how a company finds out what a technician knew on a Tuesday night about a form on a Wednesday morning, and there is no version of asking that is not that.**

*So this page is eleven lines long and it is about three days.* **In about three weeks somebody is going to ring me, or somebody is not, and either way the next time that name comes up in a conversation I am going to be a man who waited, on purpose, for three days, and I will not be able to prove the three days were for any reason, and I have written it here in a book that nobody reads, which is the only place left where a fact about me is allowed to sit without a form on it.**

*And I have been wrong twice today about why I did not ring him, and I want the second reason written down before I lose it.* **The first reason was that he is twenty-three and I did not want him used as a reason. The second reason is that he would have wanted to be warned, and I have taken that off him.** I did not ask him. That is the whole of it. I have spent a term refusing to be the fourth thing in a room and the thing I have actually been doing since the sixteenth week is finding rooms that only I know about.
