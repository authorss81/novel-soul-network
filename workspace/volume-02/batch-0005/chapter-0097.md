# Chapter 97 — Confirmed

The word started about ten minutes after the second module began and by eleven o'clock it was going about nine times a minute.

That is not a figure of speech and it is not exaggeration, and Marek Senn counted it twice on the back of a docket at about half past eleven, because he had been at the front of the hall with a trolley and a rig on it since about eleven and there was nothing else to do for two hours, and the count is: **nine, ten, eleven. Thirty-one rows of ten pairs and about a third of the pairs are at the release at any moment, and a certified holder says a sentence of about eleven parts, and the sentence contains the word *confirmed* once, and the word is the only part of it that arrives in his own head as a word.**

*He is in scope. I confirm your scope. Do not release on your own judgement. If I do not say it, you withdraw and you report it.*

He wrote the count on the back of a docket and put the docket in his pocket and did not write anything else on it, and he has still got it, and it is the only piece of paper in this case that is about him and is not about anybody else.

It started in week twenty, in a room, through equipment that was carrying something for somebody else, and it is not pain and it is not fear and there is nothing to look at. He has had it checked by nothing and diagnosed by nobody and he has written it in this book exactly twice, both times in the middle of a paragraph about somebody else, both times on a Tuesday, and both times he has stopped writing it. It is this: **in a room where a card holder is confirming somebody, he cannot keep a sentence of his own.** Every time the word comes it takes the end of whatever he is in the middle of and puts a different end on it, and the different end is the end that belongs to that word, and it is not loud and it is not in his ear and it cannot be turned off, and he has tried four things, of which the only one that worked was leaving the room, and leaving the room is not available to him at three o'clock on a Tuesday because his name is not on the programme.

He had known this for most of the term and he had never once said it out loud to anybody, and the last time he had tried to say it in a book he had stopped in the middle of a paragraph about a woman of sixty-eight who had told him that a date turns a woman's patience into a company's exposure. **He had checked the fourth condition twice at about half past six in the morning on the Tuesday of week twenty-five, hoping it would stop him, and it did not, because a hard standing off Fell Road with a customer's gate on it is not Institute equipment and not Institute premises and not under Institute insurance, and he went round it twice and got the same answer both times and told himself he wanted a different one.** He had known it and he had told nobody, and by the Wednesday of week twenty-eight, when the poster came out of a pallet, he was doing the arithmetic every morning before he had his tea, and the arithmetic always came out the same, and what it came out to was that the thing he was about to do was not a rule, and a rule would have stopped him.

---

They came in at about half past nine and the room filled in twenty minutes, which was faster than Monday, because they had been in the building the day before to have their forms checked and to be given a programme, and they knew where to go. Marek Senn stood at the side of the stage area with a trolley with a small rig on it that he had built out of company demonstration stock on a Tuesday of week twenty-five and had not touched since he had torn a card in half in front of a foreman of fifty-eight.

He had asked Nia Bramhall on the Monday morning whether he could have a different item, and she had said there was a different item, and he had said he would take it, and she had said: **then say what you want it to be,** and he had not been able to say a single thing in about eleven seconds, and she had said: right, that is the answer, and had gone back to her diary.

He had four minutes.

At about ten to three a woman with a lanyard came along the front of the hall with a microphone and said the name of the item out loud, exactly as it was printed, and did not say his name, because there was no name to say, and nobody in nine hundred chairs knew that the item belonged to anybody, and the four hundred and six people who had come for a day off were looking at a small trolley with a gate on it.

He took the microphone off her. It is worse than people think, holding one, and he had never held one in his life and he had told nobody that and it was in the list.

"Right," he said. The room made the noise it makes. "**I am not going to do what the programme says and I want to say that in the first ten seconds so nobody wastes any of the next four minutes being confused.**"

Somebody at the back laughed, kindly, the way you do.

"The programme says a maintenance sequence without a supervisory cue. I built one. It is on this trolley. **And I am not able to do it in this room, and I am going to tell you why, and then one of you is going to have to decide what to do with the four minutes, and I am sorry.**"

He stopped there, because the sentence after that one was the sentence he had been carrying for nine days and the room was doing the thing rooms do, which is going about a second and a half quieter and then waiting for the reason to be about the rig.

He said the reason.

"**In a room where somebody is confirming a release, I cannot keep a sentence of my own.** It is not a thing I have decided. It came on in about week twenty and it has not gone and there is nothing to look at and I have not got anybody to say it to, and I have never said it out loud before today. **The demonstration is four minutes long and it ends on a word, and in this hall the word is being said about nine times a minute by about nine people, and every one of those times the end of my sentence comes off and a different one goes on, and by the third minute I will not be able to tell you which one is mine.**"

The room did not do anything at all.

A man in a district fleece in the fourth row said, "Nerves."

Somebody else said, "It's the microphone."

And a woman in the second row, quite loudly and not unkindly, said, "**Have you tried the demonstration before, love?**" and about nine people laughed, and Marek Senn stood on a stage in a hall with a lanyard on and a microphone in his hand and understood, before the laugh had finished, that **there is no shape in this room for a man who cannot do a thing.** There is a shape for a man who is frightened and a shape for a man who has not prepared and a shape for a man who has done the work wrong. There is no shape for a man who has brought a working thing to a room and cannot put his own sentence down inside it, because that is not a thing that happens to people in rooms.

He said it a second time, and he said it worse, because he was embarrassed, and the second time is the one that is in the record.

"**I have built this. It works. I have used it four times on a hard standing off Fell Road with a generator and a barrier and a lamp column and it worked four times out of four and I have written that on a card. It is not a fear. It is not nerves. It is that I cannot be in a room where that word is being said, and I have not been able to be in one since about week twenty, and I have not told anybody, and the reason I have not told anybody is that there is nothing to look at.**"

"Sit down, mate," somebody said, kindly.

He stopped there. He had used about two minutes of the four and he stood with the microphone in his hand in front of four hundred and six people and did not use the other two, and the two minutes were the longest two minutes of a term that had contained a fortnight on a market trestle table and a girl of nineteen with two hundred and forty words.

"**I have got two minutes of this item left,**" he said, "**and I am not going to use them, because the room has not got a shape for a man who cannot do a thing, and if I use them I will be doing a different item than the one on your sheet, and the one on your sheet is the one that failed.**"

He put the microphone down on the trolley.

"There is a rig on this trolley and anybody who wants to look at it is welcome to, and I am not going to talk anybody through it, because I have just told you why."

---

**Davan Rusk was in the third row.**

He was forty-seven and he had been in that department for twenty-two years and he had come on his own time on a Tuesday because a girl of nineteen in a first-year group had said in a common room on the Ninth Street block on the Wednesday of week twenty-four that a member of staff had read a sentence out in front of forty-one people and put his own name against it in a file, and he had thought about that for about a fortnight.

He stood up at about nine seconds after the microphone went down, which is very fast, and the woman with the lanyard gave it to him, and Marek Senn did not know that until about half past three because he was standing at the side of the stage with his back to the room.

"**Mr Senn,**" Davan Rusk said, into a microphone, in a hall of four hundred and six people. "**You have still got the item and so have I, and what is left of it is about two minutes, and I am going to spend it, and I am going to say my name, and I have not said my name in a room in about nine years.**"

Nobody knew who he was, which is the answer to about nine questions at once.

"My name is Davan Rusk. I am an instructor in a practical department in this city. I have been in it for twenty-two years and I have taught the fourth beat in that course to about eleven hundred people and I have never once been asked whether it is necessary, and I have never once said to anybody that it might not be, and that is not a confession of anything except that nobody asked me in a way I could hear."

He put a hand on the trolley, not on the rig.

"**I want to say four things and I have got the two minutes that are left of this item, and the first one is this: he is right, and I do not know what he is talking about, and I am not going to stand here and pretend I do.**"

A murmur.

"**The second one is that what he has said this afternoon is not a thing that has happened before, in my experience, in a room, and I have been in about four thousand rooms. That does not make it true. It makes it new. And a man of twenty-two has just stood in front of four hundred people and been called nervous, and I want to say on the record that I heard the same two words and I did not think them, and I do not know why I did not think them, and that is a better position to be in than the other one.**"

Somebody at the back said something that was not audible.

"**The third thing, and it is the one I came for.** The fourth beat in that course has been taught as somebody else's word since before I came into that department. Not as a shortcut. Not as a company thing. As the safest piece of engineering I have ever been taught, and I still think that, and I would like to say that out loud because he has just been told by four hundred people that he is the man with the strange idea and I am the man from the department and he is about to be the fourth thing in a room for the rest of his life unless somebody says the other half of it. **A person who is taught to finish on somebody else's word, and does it four thousand times, and then goes out on a Saturday at two in the morning on a wet bridge with nobody to say it to, is not being unsafe. I have watched him do it. I have done it. That is not a danger. That is a man doing a four-thousandth repetition of the only completion he was ever given.**"

He turned round and looked at the room and Marek Senn, who was standing at the side of the stage with his back to four hundred and six people, heard him say the next part to the floor.

"**And the fourth thing, and it is the last one, and it is for a boy of nineteen in the fourth row who has worked out that the card rate is different on a Tuesday and I hope to God he keeps that to himself.** We teach the fourth beat as somebody else's word. I do not know why. I have asked twice, in writing, in about nineteen years, and both times the answer came back with a reference on it and nothing else in it. **And I have never met anybody who thinks the fourth beat is a good idea. Not one. I have asked about nine hundred students and I have never had one raise their hand, and I have never put that on a form, because the day I put it on a form it is a survey and a survey is a document and a document is a thing a person can be pointed at for the rest of their life, and I have not got anybody to put that in front of.**"

He gave the microphone back.

"**That was the item. He built it and I watched him build it, and if anybody wants to look at it, it is on the trolley, and do not ask him to talk you through it, because he has just explained why he cannot and he has been quite right, and the last two minutes of it are mine and not his, and I would like that noted in whatever it is you write down.**"

---

The session closed at about four minutes past three, which is on time for the item and twenty-six minutes early for the day, and the woman with the lanyard closed it, and a man with a clipboard said into a microphone that card issue was continuing in the foyer and that everybody's card was valid from the date of issue.

Nobody in the hall knew that anything had happened.

Marek Senn took the rig down off the trolley himself, in about nine minutes, and put it on the trolley, and wheeled it to a corner, and a woman of about twenty-six came over with a roll of tape and asked whether she could put a label on it, and he said yes, and she wrote *demonstration rig — return to depot* on a piece of masking tape and put it on the frame.

"You didn't get to do it," she said.

"No."

"**I've been typing that programme since the spring,**" she said, "**and I didn't write the line at the bottom and I have never once been asked whether it's true.**"

"No," said Marek Senn.

"Right," she said, and put the tape round, and went away.

He went out of the back of the room at about half past three, down the fire stair, and across the landing, and out into the car park, where it was about eleven degrees and starting to get dark, and stood on a slab of concrete for about four minutes and a half.

Then he went and did a job on a gate in Nine Bridges that took him nineteen minutes, and got it right first time, and he wrote the nineteen minutes down, and then he wrote under it that nineteen minutes was the whole of the good part of a day in which four hundred and six people had heard him fail, and that he was going to have to be more careful than that for the rest of the term.

*100. Tuesday of week 30, about half past nine at night, and I am going to write this one about a man of forty-seven who stood up nine seconds after I put a microphone down, and I am going to do that because the entry is not the point and I have to write it somewhere and the somewhere is him.*

*What happened at four o'clock, in order, and none of it is an excuse.* I took the microphone off a woman who had a running order with nine items on it and eight times and a four that is not on the printout. I said in the first ten seconds that I was not going to do what the programme said. I said that I had built a rig and that it works and that I could not do it in that room. **And then I said why, out loud, to four hundred and six people, for the first time in my life, and it took about thirty seconds: in a room where somebody is confirming a release, I cannot keep a sentence of my own, and it is not fear, and it came on in about week twenty through equipment that was carrying something for somebody else, and there is nothing to look at, and I have never said it out loud before.** **The demonstration is four minutes long and it ends on a word, and in that hall the word was being said about nine times a minute by about nine people, and every one of those times the end of my sentence came off and a different one went on.** I counted it twice on the back of a docket. Nine, ten, eleven. It is the only piece of paper in this case that is about me and not about anybody else and it is four numbers on the back of a docket and I am keeping it.

*And the room, and this is the finding and I have not got anywhere to put it except here.* **There is no shape in that room for a man who cannot do a thing.** There is a shape for a man who is frightened and a shape for a man who did not prepare and a shape for a man who did the work wrong, and all three of those are the same shape and it is the shape every room I have ever been in has for me. **There is no shape for a man who has brought a working thing to a room and cannot put his own sentence down inside it, because that is not a thing that happens to people.** A man in a district fleece said nerves. A woman in the second row said have you tried the demonstration before, love, and about nine people laughed, kindly, the way you do. Somebody said sit down, mate, kindly. **I said it a second time and I said it worse, because I was embarrassed, and the second one is the one that is in the record.** I was not believed, and I was not disbelieved, which is different and worse, and I have been not believed four times this term and this is the first one where the reason I was not believed is that the reason I gave is not a reason anybody in the world has a shape for.

*And the part I am not going to soften, and I have written it here so that it is on the page in the first person by the man it happened to.* **I am the reason it failed.** Not the company and not the poster and not the four hundred and six and not the woman with the lanyard and not a word of the small print. A man built a thing that works on a hard standing with a generator, and brought it to a room, and could not do it in the room, and said so, and there was nothing else to say. **I said in a book a fortnight ago that there is no such thing as a non-hinged maintenance sequence, and I said it to a foreman of fifty-eight on a hard standing, and he said then why are you building it, and I said because I have not got a better thing to put in the room, and then I said because I have not got anything at all, and he said right, and then I let nine brown envelopes go out with the technician on the kit on the bottom line, and I did that on purpose, and I knew about the word on the Tuesday of week twenty-five at about half past six in the morning when I checked the fourth condition twice hoping it would stop me and it did not.** The failure was not a rail across an arm. Nobody was hurt and nobody's arm was across anything and the rig is on a trolley in a depot with a piece of masking tape on it. **It is a man of twenty-two standing in front of four hundred and six people not being able to keep a sentence, and it is going to be in a transcript somewhere, or it is not going to be anywhere, and I do not know which and I have not asked.**

*Davan Rusk, forty-seven, twenty-two years in that department, in the third row, on his own time on a Tuesday, because a girl of nineteen said in a common room on the Ninth Street block on the Wednesday of week twenty-four that a member of staff had read a sentence out in front of forty-one people and put his own name against it in a file, and he had thought about that for a fortnight.* **He stood up about nine seconds after I put the microphone down and he gave his name, and he said he had not said his name in a room in about nine years, and nobody in that hall knew who he was, and I have worked out since that not knowing is the single most useful thing a person can bring into a room of four hundred and six, and I have spent a term being the opposite.** He said four things and the room let him say all four, and I am not going to write them down again because they are two hundred yards behind us and I can still hear him, and if I write them out a third time in a different order then this book becomes the place where things I have already said get said again. **What I am going to write down is the part that is not in the room. He did not know that the word *four* was going to be in a hall of four hundred and six people when he stood up. He has not said his name in a room in about nine years, and in nine years nobody has ever stood up behind him, and he had worked that out in a car park, and I have not got a way of doing that and I have had a term to try.** **And the thing I have not been able to put down since is that a man of forty-seven with twenty-two years in a department stood up on his own time on a Tuesday, in a hall he had no ticket for, in front of four hundred people who were not there for the case, and he spent the last two minutes of my four on the fourth beat being the safest piece of engineering he has ever been taught, out loud, on purpose, knowing that four hundred people would carry it out of that room and that no document of it would exist anywhere. He has asked twice in writing in about nineteen years and both times the answer came back with a reference on it and nothing else in it. He has about nine hundred students and not one of them has ever put their hand up, and he has never once put it on a form, because a survey is a document.** And I stood on a stage with a lanyard on and a microphone in my hand for fourteen weeks wanting four hundred people to be told something by somebody with standing, and a man who had it got it by not having a lanyard, and I do not have a way of being happy about that.

*And what that last sentence is, and I am not going to write it as anything else.* **He has not got anybody to put in front of.** He has said the sentence I have spent fourteen weeks trying to find the right half of, in nine seconds, to four hundred and six people who do not know him, on his own time, on a Tuesday, and the reason he could say it is that nobody could be pointed at. **And there is a man in this case who has spent fourteen weeks not saying one true sentence in a room for exactly the opposite reason, and I have spent fourteen weeks telling myself that the difference between us is that he is a member of staff of twenty-two years and I am a technician, and that is not the difference, and I have known it since the Wednesday of week twenty-four, and a girl of nineteen said it out loud in a common room in week twenty-eight while I was standing in it, and it took me nine days to work out what she was talking about and I have not told her.** She wrote *As long as it is Senn's, nobody has to look at it, because there is a man, and he is twenty-two, and he can be the fourth thing in it.* I have been the fourth thing in a room nine times this term and I have been proud of it four times and I have been ashamed of it five times and on Tuesday I was the fourth thing in a room in front of four hundred and six people and there is a woman in the second row who said love to me and I am going to hear it for about four years, and none of that was Davan Rusk's doing and all of it was mine.

*And the two sentences that are not about him, because I have to put them somewhere and the somewhere is this page.* **A woman of about twenty-six with a roll of tape put a label on the rig that said demonstration rig, return to depot, and then said: I have been typing that programme since the spring and I didn't write the line at the bottom and I have never once been asked whether it's true. And I said no. And she said right, and went away.** She is not one of the four. I do not know what her name is and I have not asked and I have decided not to. And Sera Quill was not in that hall and I looked for her twice, and she was right not to be there, and if I ever write a sentence about her in a room I will deserve what I get. **The word is *confirmed*. I have written it now. It has not been in this book before and it is the only word in this case that is a mechanism and not a description, and it is in nine hundred people's mouths a week and it is in a syllabus, and it is the sound my head makes, and there is no switch, and I have four days.**
