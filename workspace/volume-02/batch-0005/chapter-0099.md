# Chapter 99 — Room Four

Half past ten, and the chair was there.

The card on it said M. SENN in a typed strip in a plastic holder, and the chair was against the wall on the left as you come in, not at the table, because the woman of about fifty-eight who had laid the tables out on the Friday had put the cards out in alphabetical order and there was only one card, and a room with one chair in it and six people in it is a different room from a room with a chair at a table.

Room four on the second floor of the Ninth Street block. Two lights and a strip light, a table with six chairs on one side and two on the other, a window that does not open, a flip chart nobody has turned over, and a wall notice about fire exits that is four years old and has a different stair on it.

"Right," she said, and put the folder flat. "**I am going to say four things and then I am going to let people ask questions, and the first is that I do not know what this meeting is about. Nobody in the room told her.

She wrote *no terms of reference produced* on the top line of a sheet and turned it round and showed it to the table.

"**Then that is the first line of the minute and it is the truest thing in the room, and we are going to have it.**"

On the other side of the table: **Talia Venn**, who was a witness and not his. "No."

"**And nobody who could be asked about it was in the room.**"

Marek Senn did not say the second half of the sentence.

She had given it to him on a Friday evening six weeks ago in about nine seconds, and it had been true that evening, and it was not true at half past ten on this Friday, and he was not going to say a true sentence he did not believe, and the reason it was not true was that a man in the third row of a hall on a trading estate had stood up about nine seconds after he had put a microphone down, and that man was at this table with a folder he had not opened, and nobody had sent for him.
