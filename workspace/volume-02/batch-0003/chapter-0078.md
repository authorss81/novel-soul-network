# Chapter 78 — Four Sentences

He rang the bank of the Weir from the shop at ten to eight on the Friday morning of week twenty-one and she picked up on the second ring, which meant she had been holding the telephone.

"Go on," said Nkechi Osei.

"I have asked to come off the Mondays," said Marek Senn. "From the Monday of week twenty-two. The reason is a man called Molloy at the Lattice depot whose Saturday and Sunday overtime was suspended on Wednesday afternoon for a reason that has got nothing to do with him, and he has been covering my road days for three weeks, and I did not know that until a van on Thursday morning, and I have not told you for three weeks because I did not know it until Thursday. That is the sentence and there is not a second part of it."

There was nothing on the line for about four seconds.

"**Then that is the first sentence anybody has ever given me about a Monday that was not about a Monday.**"

"Yes."

"Do not feel good about it."

"I have not."

"I know you have not, that is why I said it." She was not being kind and she was not being hard either. "Now the price, because I gave you the deal and I am going to collect it. **The arrangement is not yours. You cannot leave it by asking.** You have been in a gap that somebody made and nobody wrote down, and if you go off the Mondays then somebody else is going to be in it, and it is going to be a man who is on a rota and does not know what the gap is for, and in about a year he is going to find out, and by then you will be in a different building."

"I know. He told me his name and I have written it down."

"Then do not say it to me. **Go and say his name to him, and say it on a day when it is not going to be a favour, and do not tell me about it afterwards, and do not write it in a book with a date on it, because a book with a date on it is how I would find out.**"

"I am not going to tell you about it afterwards."

"I know. That is why I have told you to do it." A bar, somewhere behind her. "Right. Next Monday is the Monday of week twenty-one and the Monday of week twenty-one is yours, and I am not going to be a woman who takes a sentence and then takes the Monday as well, and you will be on the bank at ten past six and if you are not there I will not say anything about it and I will not stop."

"I will be there."

"And I am not going to say thank you, because you did not do it for me."

Oren Vey rang the shop at ten past ten on the Friday morning of week twenty-one, and he did not ask how he was.

"I am going to be right about six things and wrong about one," he said, "and I have decided to do the six first so that you cannot hear the seventh coming, and then I am going to tell you what I lost on Monday, because I lost it on Monday, and I am telling you four days afterwards because I have spent the four days looking for a version where I did not."

"You lost it on Monday."

"I lost it on Monday morning at about eleven, in a room with four people in it, one of whom is not paid by me, and it took about nine minutes, and I would like you to understand that the person who refused it to me was correct and that she is good at her job and that I am not going to name her because I have spent nine years being the man who does not put a name in a document and I am not going to start because I have lost an argument to a twenty-two-year-old's four sentences."

"Say the six."

"**One.** That bundle is not a company document and I am not going to speculate about who made it, not today, not to a journalist, not in a room, and I have told our own people that in writing this morning. Here is why, and it is the only reason and it is a real one. The first body that names a source in this business stops being able to ask a question for about a year, and the questions I need asking are not about a bundle. They are about nine hundred bundles in this district with a blank field in them, and I cannot ask that question in a company that has spent four days arguing in a canteen about who did this."

"**Two,** and this is the one I am proudest of and it is the one that is going to cost the most, and I am going to give it to you before you can work out that you already know it.

"That document is going to be read by a frightened city as proof that a company has been hiding something, and in about a month a lot of people who have never read a page of anything are going to believe that the danger is a group of people called something, and that a technique is being passed about in secret by people who know what they are doing. **And it will be believed, and it will be believed more strongly than the truth, and every person in this city who has been handed a certificate and told that a card makes them safer is going to keep that card and go home and say nothing to anybody for about four years, and the people who most need to hear a question are the people holding those cards.**"

He let that sit.

"That is what a bundle with names in it and no source in it does. Not to me. To a city that has just been frightened once. And I am right about it and being right does not make it cheaper, and I have got a duty of care to eleven people and about two hundred names, and I am not going to be able to spend a penny on the thing that is actually wrong, because everything I have got for the next year is going on a letter and a phone call and explaining a wall. **You did that. Not on purpose. You were in a room and you were good at the thing and you said a true sentence in a doorway and a woman copied it out.**"

"I did not—"

"You did. Do not argue with me about it on a Friday morning when I have not slept properly since Tuesday." He was not angry and he was not performing. "**Three.** The eleven. We cannot un-name them and I want to be exact about why, because the reason is not a lawyer. The only way to take eleven names out of a document that two hundred people have already read is for a person in an office to decide which of eleven people's medical information this company was entitled to have in the first place, and to sign that decision, and that decision is a judgement about a woman's body and a man's knee and it is a judgement about eleven people who are not in the room. **A data-protection officer is not an ethics committee. I have wanted an ethics committee since about the second of this year and there is not one and I am not inventing one in a Friday morning telephone call.** So the eleven keep their names, we write to all of them, and we destroy the notes at the end of the term on request, and that is what we are doing."

"And the two hundred."

"The two hundred is worse and there is nothing to be done about it at all, because a course register is the product. **A company that cannot tell you who it has taught is a company you cannot invoice. There is no version of this business where the list does not exist.** I have said that sentence out loud about nine times in eleven years and I have never once had to say it to a room of frightened people before."

"**Four.** Molloy. You are right to feel badly about it and you are not to do anything about it this week. The suspension is correct. If a company has a document on a wall that names a customer's yard and a customer is going to be frightened, then the correct response is to stop doing discretionary things in that building until somebody can say what the document means. **The hours come back. They come back in about three weeks and they come back to him before they come back to anybody else, because he is the one who lost them, and I have already written that down and it is dated and it is mine.**"

"You have not said whether you arranged the Mondays."

"No."

"That is an answer."

"That is a refusal, and I made it a refusal on purpose, and I have been making it for six weeks." A chair. "**Five,** and this is the one I have been carrying since Tuesday. You have been asking a woman of forty-six for a number since the middle of the term and she has refused you three times and I know it because she told me before the term began that a technician had asked her twice and that she had watched him not go and ask somebody else, and she said she noticed that as well, and I have had that conversation with about six other people since and it is the single most useful thing anybody has told me about how this works. **I am not going to give you the number. It is not mine, it belongs to a returns system she reads and not to me, and if I give it to you on a Friday morning in a repair shop then it is on a counter in this city by Saturday, and she gives it to people in rooms where somebody writes it down, and she is right to, and I am not going to be the fourth man who goes round her.**"

"You already knew she was being asked."

"I have known for a fortnight," said Oren Vey. "**And I did not warn you, and that is a thing I have done, and I want it named, because you are twenty-two and you have been extremely straight with me since the sixteenth week and I have watched you not ask me a question for a fortnight and I have let you not ask it, because if I had answered it the first time you would have had a number by the Wednesday and been in a completely different position by now, and I did not want you in that position, and I decided that on my own and I have not told anybody I decided it and I am telling you.**"

"And the seventh."

"The seventh is the one I lost," said Oren Vey, "and I am going to give it to you properly, and then I am going to read you four sentences, and I want you to know before I start that I have already read them to a room and that they are in a file with a date on it and my name on the front of it, and **your name is not on it, and I want that stated before I say them, because the reason there is no name on it is not generosity.**

"**The thing I have wanted since the eighth week of the summer is an instruction that a supplier's upstream bundle and licensor field is completed or refused at intake.** Not a conversation. Not a phone call. An instruction, in writing, to every supplier in this district, that the field is either completed or the pack is refused, and that refusing the pack is a legitimate answer and not a threat. I have told that to eleven companies. Four of them refused to send anything at all. Three of the seven that came back are blank, and under all three, word for word, from three companies that have never spoken to each other, in their own words: *the originating practice holds no record of it.*

"On Monday morning I took it to a room. I have taken it to that room twice before. It is mine, I wrote it, I own it, and I do not need anybody's permission, and I have a board that does what I say on this."

"And it failed."

"It failed in about nine minutes and the reason is the fourth thing I have told you twice." He was quiet for a second. "**I lost the only thing I have wanted for ten weeks because of four sentences a man of twenty-two said to me in a repair shop in the middle of week sixteen, and I have been carrying them around like a stone in a shoe ever since and I would like it recorded that I knew exactly what they were the day I said them, because I said them on purpose, and I did not expect to lose on them and I lost on them.**

"Read them to me. Out loud. Because I want to hear whether they still work."

Marek Senn stood in a shop in Lattice Ward on a Friday morning and read four sentences into a telephone.

"A completed field is a name. A refused field is also a name. And there is no version of an instruction that does not put a person's name on a piece of paper, and this company does not keep records of people who have not been told they are in one."

"That is one, and it is hers, and she said it in about eleven words. Go on."

"If you annotate the pack, then the field is a record of a conversation somebody had in a corridor, and in about four years a company that does not exist any more gets cited for a sentence a records clerk of a co-operative read out in a market."

"That is two. Go on."

"Three of your seven suppliers have written to you, in their own words, that the originating practice holds no record of it. So a blank is not a defect. And refusing a pack over a blank is refusing three clean companies for a thing they have told you in writing that they do not have."

"And four."

"So the field cannot mean anything until there is something to complete it with. And there is not. And the only thing that can honestly go in it today is a date and the name of the person who ruled it, and that is not a licensor, and it never will be."

Oren Vey did not say anything for about four seconds.

"Right," he said. "That is what I lost on, and here is what I have instead, and I am giving it to you before you ask, and I am giving it to you as a man who has just lost something and is trying not to be gracious about it.

"From Monday, every intake in this district records in our own system what a supplier says when we ask them the question. Unedited. In their own words. With a date and the name of the person who asked. **I am not getting the field. I am getting the answer, and the answer is worth more than the field, and it is worth more because the field can only ever be completed by a guess and the answer is a fact about a person on a Tuesday.**

"And the first person that is going to be asked properly is going to be asked on a Tuesday, with no target on the question, and there is exactly one person in this city who has been thinking about what happens when you do that, and it is a man of twenty-two and he is not a supplier and he is not in this company, and I have written down that I am going to ring him, and **I am not going to ask you for his name and I am not going to ask you for his telephone number, and if you give me either of them I will not use them, and you should know that I have said that out loud to myself in an office on Wednesday and it is the only reason I have slept since Tuesday.**"

Marek Senn sat down on a stool in his own shop.

"His name is Ruslan Petric and he is twenty-three," he said. "He is a third year and he is not the one you want and he is the only one there is."

"That is the first time in a year you have given me a name without being made to and I am not going to ask you a single question about how you know it." A pause. "**He is going to be the fourth thing in a room and he is going to be twenty-three, and I am going to put him on a Tuesday with a form and no target, and there is nobody in this company who is going to be in that room with him, and I am not going to be in it either, and I have thought about that for two days and I have decided that is the only correct thing to do with a man I am paying nothing and have no authority over.**"

"He put a hand-written sheet through a box at a post office on Weaver Street in the middle of the term, asking for a fact about himself to be taken out, and he got three words in ballpoint on a playing card, and he stood up in a market in front of twelve people and said the second thing in the first person with his name at the end, and a woman invented a rule about the card it went into, and it is in a book in a market at forty lines."

"Then he is the right one, and I have no idea what I am doing, and I am going to ring him."

"**Do not tell him I am going to ring him.**"

"I was not going to tell him anything. I am going to ring him as the operations director of a company that has spent four days being discussed on a counter, which is a completely different conversation, and he will not be able to tell anybody I sent a twenty-two-year-old with four sentences in his mouth." A chair. "And Marek. I have got one more thing and then I am going to a site, and it is the only one in this call that I have not planned.

"**I did not write the sentence on page four. It is eleven years old and it came out of a pack and I did not write it, and neither did the woman before me, and there are three of us who have run this business in this district and none of us wrote it, and every single one of us has shipped it, every year, to about nine hundred people a year.** So if you have been carrying a shape around for a term looking for the man who put it in, I am telling you as the man who is profitable from it: **the shape is older than all of us and the profit is younger and there are three of us in it and none of us built it.** I am not the author. I am not going to be able to become the author, because you cannot author something that was working before you were born into the room. And I would like that in a book in a shop, in your words, because I have been waiting about a year for somebody to write it down in a sentence and not in a speech."

He put the phone down.

---

Marek Senn sat on a stool in a shop in Lattice Ward for about four minutes and did not move, and then he wrote three lines in the small upright hand on the back of a docket, and then he tore the docket in half, because the three lines were a document and a document in a shop is a thing with a date on it, and he had watched a woman in a market on the Saturday of week nineteen say that she wanted two things in a book and not one of them, and he was not going to be the reason a book in a market had to carry an accident in it on top of a Saturday.

He did not ring Ruslan Petric.

That was the whole of the decision and it took him about eleven seconds, and the reason is that on the Thursday of week sixteen he made a refusal on a boy of twenty's behalf without asking the boy, and wrote it down as the only refusal he had ever made that was not for himself, and on Friday of week twenty-one a man of forty-one is going to put a man of twenty-three on a Tuesday with a form and no target, and nobody has asked him, and the first one was a door shut and this one is a door opened, and the second one is worse, and it is worse because it is good.

*81. Friday of week 21. Two telephone calls before eleven and the second one is the reason I am going to be all right in a term and I would like that on the page with nothing under it.*

*The sentence, said at ten to eight.* Four clauses, about forty seconds, and the whole of it is a man asking to come off a rota because a man of thirty-nine is behind on his rent. **And she said: that is the first sentence anybody has ever given me about a Monday that was not about a Monday. Then she said do not feel good about it, and she was right, and I had not.** And then the price, which I knew, and which is worse than the Monday: the gap is not mine and I cannot leave it by asking, and somebody will be put in it, and it will be a man on a rota who does not know what it is for, and in a year he will find out. She said: go and say his name to him, on a day when it is not going to be a favour, and do not tell me about it afterwards. **I have not decided the day. I have written his name in this book twice now and the first thing she told me to do was not to write it down with a date on it, and I am going to keep breaking that rule for about a fortnight and then I am going to find out what happens when I stop.**

A completed field is a name, a refused field is a name, and the person who refused it to him in nine minutes on Monday was correct and he is not going to name her and he has been the man who does not put a name in a document for nine years and he is not starting because he lost an argument.
