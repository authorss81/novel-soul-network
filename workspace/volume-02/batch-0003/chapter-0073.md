# Chapter 73 — Still

He rang the shop from his own flat on the Sunday morning of week twenty at about eleven, with the extractor going in the next room, and when she picked up she did not say the sentence about the day.

He noticed it inside a second and a half and said nothing about it, and she noticed him not saying it, and neither of them mentioned it then or afterwards.

"You rang me," said Talia Venn.

"I rang you."

"It is a Sunday and you have not rung me on a Sunday in a term and a half, and I am not going to say anything about the day, and I want you to notice that I am not saying anything about the day, and I am not going to explain why either." A door closed on her end, somewhere with a corridor in it. "Go on."

"You have to go into a room on the Tuesday."

"I have to go into a room on the Tuesday. That has been in a diary since the Wednesday of week seventeen and it is not a surprise, and I have known for a fortnight what I was going to say in it, and I have been dreading it in a way I would like you to understand is not about the room."

"Then why ring me about it."

"Because of the four sentences I have to say before the sentence, and because one of them is a lie and one of them is a refusal and one of them is a thing I have never said out loud to anybody, and I have got about four minutes of Sunday in me and I would like to use them on somebody who will not try to fix it."

"Say the four."

She took a second.

"One. I have never seen a round. Two. I have been asked twice now for a statement on a practice and I have not got one. Three. There is no form in this district for a person to be asked for a statement about a practice they have not seen, and I looked, and the looking took me about an hour and a half in a library on the Friday afternoon, and I would like it minuted that I did the looking and did not ask you to do it. Four."

She stopped, and he could hear her put something down.

"Four is the lie. And I have been carrying it about for a fortnight and I am going to say it to you because you are the fourth thing in a room and it is what you do to people.

"I am going to stand in a room on the Tuesday with a man who writes things down and say that there is no route by which I can see the practice. And what I am not going to say is that there is no route because **I am a district contractor on the Saltmarket route and I have not got a badge that gets me through a gate, and I do not know one person who would take me on, and I have tried four times since the Wednesday of week seventeen and I have been told three times that it is not for me and once that it would need a name.** So the fourth sentence is going to be *there is no access*, and *there is no access* is not true, there is an enormous amount of access, I have a registration number and I can get into forty buildings and I have never once been able to get onto a bank with a bar on it."

Marek Senn stood in his own kitchen with a cloth in his hand and did not say anything for about four seconds.

"I have been on about seven of them," he said. "Since the Wednesday of week fourteen. Every Monday at ten past six, at the Weir, at the Cutbank, nineteen men, one bar, and about eleven steps, and I have got four inches of one eye from standing on wet stone and nothing else that I have ever been proud of being at."

"Then that is the thing I am not going to ask you for."

"You were not going to ask me for it," he said. "You asked me to say the four sentences."

"I asked you to be in a telephone at eleven on a Sunday, which is a different thing, and I knew what it was, and I have wanted it for a fortnight." A chair, and then the sound of a woman being difficult on purpose. "Marek. If you write me four or five sentences about what a round is, I can put them in front of a man on the Tuesday. Not as evidence. As a statement of practice from a person who has been on one. It would not need his name. It would not need mine. It would be the only sentence in that file that anybody could check, because the practice is a real practice and it goes on every Monday of the season whether anybody is looking or not."

"No."

"You have not said why."

"I have not."

"**Say the why.** I have asked you for nine things in a year and you have said no to all nine and I have made a career out of the fact that they are all real, and I am not going to let this one be the one where you are kind to me."

"**Nineteen men on a bank and a woman who is on four.**"

Nothing at all for about three seconds, and he heard her decide not to ask, which took the same second and a half it always took.

"Go on," she said.

"Nkechi Osei is on four. She has been on four since the Friday of week eighteen and she has told nobody — not her mate, not the foreman, not the men on the round, not me until the Friday I signed a contract in a shop. She asked me for one sentence in a bank in week eighteen and it was smaller than I expected, and it was: if you cannot come on a Monday, tell me on the Friday, before, and not afterwards.

"If I write you four sentences about that round then I am putting a description of a practice in a district file, and in about a year somebody in that room is going to read it and go looking for the practice, and the practice is nineteen men and a bank and a bar. And she will be on it, because she is always on it, and she did not consent to being described, and she is on four, and if the description is good then in four years somebody will come and ask her whether she is all right, and if the description is bad then in four years somebody will come and use her as the example." He put the cloth down. "That is the whole of it and it is not the fourth condition and it is not a principle and it is not the lanyard. It is a person who is standing on a bank on a Monday because a man of fifty-eight put her there after she came off three, and she has told nobody, and I am not going to hand her to a district office because a man writes things down."

"You have said that out loud to me on a Sunday for about eleven seconds," said Talia Venn, "and I want you to know that it is the first time in a year that you have given me a reason I could not have got out of you by being difficult, and I have never once asked you for a thing I could have got by being difficult, and that is the reason it took you nine seconds instead of nine weeks, and I am not going to make anything of it."

"Do not make anything of it."

"I am not. I am going to say the thing that is going to cost me and then we are going to have done."

"Go on."

"There is a minute for Tuesday. It is a form and the man takes it down and it is read out in about four weeks to somebody else, and I have been in that room twice and both times the minute has gone back as a line. And the line is going to be: **declined to supply — the person asked is not independent of the party the request concerns.** That is the phrase and I have chosen it and it is true and it is the only sentence I have got that is true and short and does not name a single person in this city, and I have to say it out loud in a room with a man who writes things down."

Marek Senn did not move.

"That sentence is about me," he said.

"That sentence is about a person."

"That sentence is about me and you have been protecting me with it since the Wednesday of week seventeen and you have never once said so."

"I have never once said so and I am not going to now." Her voice did not change. "And I am not going to apologise to you for it either, and I am not going to be gracious about it, and here is the reason in three sentences and then I am going to go and do a ward.

"It is not a favour to you. It is the only wording in the English language that lets me answer for a refusal without a form full of other people's pages in it, because the minute goes to a regional office and the minute is readable, and *there is no access* is not true and would put four people's names in it to prove a negative. **I have used that sentence twice already this year and it has cost me a fortnight each time, and this is the third, and it is the one I am going to be asked about in a room, and I am going to have to explain a phrase I did not coin to a man who thinks in boxes.** And I am not sorry about it, and I am not going to say sorry, and if you stand there and be decent about it I will put the phone down."

"I am not going to be decent about it."

"No. I know. That is why I told you on Thursday and not on Sunday." A page turned. "I have not asked you one question about the job in eleven days. I told you that on the Thursday night, deliberately, and I had to stop myself twice, and I have stopped myself about nine more times since. **I have been asked a question about the job this morning that is not about the job and I did not see it coming, and you gave me a person in four seconds, and I have been thinking about that on and off since the phone went.**"

"Which question."

"Not now." She was firm about it. "One more thing and then I have to go, and it is about the boy, and it is one sentence and I am only saying it because I said I would not think about anything until Thursday and it is Sunday and I broke that on the Thursday night and I have not broken it since, and this does not count because this is about somebody else."

"Go on."

"I have a form with a box on it that says *source of standard* and I did not fill it in, and I have not filled it in on four return-path sheets since the Wednesday of week seventeen, and nobody has chased me, and I have started to think that the reason nobody has chased me is that the answer I would give is the same on all four, and that the answer is *I do not know where it came from and I have four years of pages that would not tell me*, and that is not an answer anybody can use and it is the only one I have got."

"Tell me the sentence," said Marek Senn.

"You will not tell me which one," said Talia Venn.

"No. I will not."

"Then we are where we were on the Sunday of week nineteen, which is the only place either of us has been for a year." She let a second go by, on purpose. "Right. The thing I have been not saying since Thursday, and I am saying it now because you have given me a person and I owe you one true sentence in return for it. On Thursday I said *we still had an hour.*"

"I heard it."

"You heard it and you have not said one word about it for two days, and I have waited, and I want you to know that I have been waiting, and I am not going to be a woman who waits out a tense."

He put his hand flat on the counter.

"You said *still*," he said.

"I said *still*."

"I noticed inside a second and a half on the Thursday and I did not say anything then, and I have not said anything since, and I want you to know that I noticed and I have been carrying it about for two days and there is nobody I could have said it to, and I am not going to tell you that noticing it was an accident, because it was not, and you would not believe me."

"No," said Talia Venn. "I would not."

"Do you want to say what it means."

"**No.** I have said *no* to a mechanism once already this year and it is the only no of mine that I have not had to repeat, and I am not going to spend it on a tense in a telephone. If you and I are ever going to say that sentence we are going to say it in a room with our hands on a table, and neither of us is going to do it on a Sunday because a man on a bank made us both feel something, and **that is the reason and it is not modesty and it is not because it is too soon.** It is because you have been the fourth thing in five buildings in six weeks and I have been the only one in nine rooms with one voice, and if we say it now it will be because the work is going badly, and I have watched a whole term in this city turn on people mistaking that for something else."

Marek Senn did not say anything for about four seconds, and then he said, "That is fair," which is not the same as agreeing and she was not going to let it be.

"Do not say *that is fair* to me. Say it properly or do not say it."

"I do not know how to say it properly," he said. "I have been trying since about eleven o'clock this morning and I have not got one sentence and I have got about nine that are all about somebody else's bank, and I am not going to use one of those now."

"Then leave it," said Talia Venn, and he heard her not be gracious about that either, and then she said, "I have a ward at half two and I have not eaten and I have to write a thing down for Tuesday before I do either of them, and I want to say the last part while I still have the nerve, and it is about a room and not about a bank.

"You are owed a reply about something. I have known that since the Friday of week sixteen, you have never brought it to me, and I have never asked, and I am not going to ask, and **I am not going to be a woman who finds out a thing about your life in a Monday review or on a Friday morning because somebody in an office is frightened and puts it in an email.** When you are ready you will tell me, and if you never are, then that is also information, and I will not have spent it on you being silent in a telephone on a Sunday. That is the whole of it and I am going now and I am not going to say *we had an hour*, and I have said so on purpose, and you are going to hear that too."

He heard it.

"Ring me on a Tuesday," she said. "And Marek — when you do, do not bring me a document. I have got four on my desk and I could not give one of them to a woman in a wheelchair in Bower Street in week nineteen. **Do not bring me a document.**"

The line went.

Marek Senn stood in his own kitchen for a minute with the cloth in his hand, and then he put it down, and then he picked it up again, and then he went through to the shop and opened the roller shutter out of habit on a Sunday and stood in the middle of a cold floor for about a minute and a half and did not turn the light over the bench on.

At about half past one he sat down at the bench and opened the load book and read the last two pages before he wrote anything, and both of them were his, and he noticed that he had started a habit of reading them and had not decided to.

*76. Sunday of week 20. She rang nothing and I rang her, and I have written it as though I had a reason, and the reason is eleven seconds long and it is that I did not want the Tuesday to be the first time.*

*What she asked me for, in the shape of asking for nothing.* Four sentences about a round. Not evidence. A statement of practice from a person who has been on one, so that the only sentence in a district file about it would be one somebody could check. **It would have been true and it would have been checkable and it would have been the best thing in that file and she wanted it from me in about eleven seconds of saying so, and I said no, and the reason is Nkechi Osei, and I gave the reason out loud in about eleven seconds, and that is twice in a year I have given a person a reason in one breath instead of a fortnight.** I have been not asking for about a year and I have been refusing in about a second, and the difference this time is that I gave her the actual person before she had to be difficult about it, and I have written that down as a method and it is the nearest thing to a rule I have got and it is eleven words long and it is Rafi's.

*What it would have cost her, and she told me, and she was right to.* A minute on the Tuesday, read out in about four weeks to somebody else. **Declined to supply — the person asked is not independent of the party the request concerns.** Twelve words. True. Short. Names nobody. And the reason she has reached for it twice before and will reach for it a third time is that *there is no access* is not true and would take four other people's names to prove a negative in a document a regional office will read twice. So the sentence protects a great many people and it is a sentence **about me**, and she has been carrying that for three weeks and said it out loud on a Sunday afternoon because I gave her a person first.

*The thing she is not going to spend.* She has four return-path sheets with a box saying *source of standard* and she has not filled it in on any of them, and nobody has chased her, and she has worked out that the reason is that the answer is the same on all four and the answer is useless. **I am the only person in this city she could get the answer off and she did not ask me and I did not offer and neither of us mentioned it, and that is the fourth time in a year, and the count is not accidental any more and I do not know what to do with it.** She also said, and said it deliberately, that she has not been told where the sentence on page four came from, and that she is not going to try to find out through me, and that she is not spending a no she has only got one of on a tense in a telephone.

*And then the tense.* She said *still* on Thursday. I noticed inside a second and a half and I said nothing, and I have said nothing for two days, and I said it this afternoon in one sentence, out loud, on purpose, because she is not going to be a woman who waits out a tense and neither am I going to be a man who lets one sit there and calls it professional. **She said no to the rest of it, and not gently, and then she gave me the reason, and the reason is the correct one and I have been trying to think of a better one for two days and there is not one.** She said: if we say that sentence now it will be because the work is going badly, and she has watched a whole term in this city turn on people mistaking that for something else, and I have watched it too, and I have been the example twice. **Neither of us said sorry. Neither of us went and stood somewhere else. She told me not to say *that is fair* and made me either say it properly or not at all, and I could not say it properly, and she let that stand, and that is the receipt.** And she did not say *we had an hour* at the end, and she said on purpose that she was not going to, and I heard her not say it, and I have written that down because I am going to remember the shape of it for a long time.

*What she told me not to do, and I am going to spend the next fortnight not doing.* Do not bring her a document. Not as a favour, not as evidence, not as a favour-with-evidence in it. She has four on her desk and she could not give one of them to a woman in a wheelchair in Bower Street in week nineteen. And the other one: **do not tell her a thing about my life in a Monday review, and she said it as a warning to me and it was the most careful thing anybody has said to me in a year, and the reason is that she has been waiting for me to be ready since the sixteenth week and has not asked, and that is not patience, that is a woman refusing to be a person who finds things out on bad news.**

*Do not know.* Whether the sentence in the minute is a shield. It protects four people's names and it is true and it is about me, and in about four years a regional office is going to read that line and put it in a column, and the column is going to have a heading, and the heading is going to be a word about independence, and there is a version of this city in which that word is a wall somebody put up on purpose so that a person like her could not get to a bank like the one I stand on. **I have written a page about a Sunday and the only thing on it that is a fact about the case is a nine-word phrase that neither of us said to each other, and it is the only sentence in a file anywhere in this city that describes what I am, and it got there because a woman chose a piece of wording to keep other people's names out of a document, and I am not going to be able to unhear it and she is not going to regret it, and I have not got a single move.**
