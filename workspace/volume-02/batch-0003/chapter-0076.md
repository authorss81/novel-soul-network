# Chapter 76 — Eleven

The bundle was on the depot floor at about five past seven on the Wednesday morning of week twenty-one, on a chair by the kettle, and it was about two inches thick and it had an elastic band round it, and there was a sheet of paper on the top of it with a line typed on it that said THREADLINE DISTRICT SERVICE — LATTICE and nothing else, no name, no date, no signature, and not one word about who had put it there.

The depot opened at seven. There were four men in that building. Nobody had seen anybody put it down.

Marek Senn stood over it for a minute without touching it and then went and got a pair of gloves out of the van and picked it up and put it on Wyn Dunleavy's desk, which took him eleven seconds, and then he went outside and stood by the roller shutter for about four minutes because he had a bad feeling in the middle of his chest and he knew what it was and he wanted it to be a different thing.

It was not a different thing. He had a photograph on a hard hat in Nine Bridges, taken on a customer, that showed this depot's gate and a carbon visitor column on a spike by it, and if that column is in a bundle then everything in this city is in a bundle, and there is no version of this where it is anything else.

---

It is a training register. Fourteen pages of a printed grid, in house type, the first page headed *district service — Lattice — course register — 4-19 supervised release — modules 1 and 2*, and the columns are name, employee number, date started, date completed, card issued, and **source of standard**.

About two hundred names.

Somebody has put a pen through the source of standard column on the first page and written one word down the side of it in capitals, and the word is *CARD*, thirty-one times, in the same hand, and it is not a company's hand and it is not anybody's hand in the building.

Then a rota. Six weeks, Monday to Thursday, the depot's whole shift pattern. Four technicians' names against it, days, hours, and a column on the right with a tick in it that says *release callout — available*, and beside one man's name on the Wednesday of week twenty-one somebody has written, in the same capitals, *NOT CARDED. A CARD IS A CONDITION OF A CALLOUT. ASK HIM WHY HE IS NOT CARDED.*

Then eleven sheets, and they are the ones that matter.

They are occupational-health notes. One page each. Name, date of induction, site, and a box that says *any condition, injury or medication affecting your ability to work at height* with the word **NONE** typed into it, and under that a line, and under the line there is more.

Nine of the eleven say *none*, and a condition, and a site.

One says nothing about a condition, and says this:

*Card status: none held. Confirmed by subject as none held, week of 19, by telephone, following incident at place of work. Subject advised that this will be recorded.*

**Sondra Mbeki's line. A woman of thirty-nine, six years on a route, hurt on the Thursday of week nineteen in a compound off the Meridian because a radio operator heard a word and not a number, and the company rang her at about eleven and asked her to confirm on paper that she was not qualified, and she confirmed, because she was in a sling and somebody was asking, and it is in a document that eleven people can now read.**

And the last one is one page, and it is not a health note, it is an induction record for a nineteen-year-old, and it has the same box with the word **NONE** typed into it, and it has a site line, and the site is a goods yard off the Meridian Corridor.

Marek Senn stood in a depot at about ten past seven on the Wednesday morning of week twenty-one and read a customer's address off a piece of paper that had been pushed under his employer's door, and had a bad feeling go all the way down to his boots, and understood that the boy's name on that page was the same name that was going into a box on a sheet next to a kettle nine times at a time in the prefab office on that yard, and that nobody had ever asked the boy whether he wanted either of those.

There is no version of this in which the two are separate. A record and a record about a person are the same record the moment there are two of them, and there are now three, and the third is on a wall in a customer's building in a boy's own handwriting.

---

The last four pages were not about anybody at all.

Page four of a company's induction booklet, photocopied, the four-colour diagram gone flat to grey, four numbered steps, and the line in capitals: **RELEASE IS GIVEN BY ANOTHER VOICE. DO NOT RELEASE ON YOUR OWN JUDGEMENT.**

And underneath it, a second page, which is the bottom of a certificate, and the small print: *the holder is able to perform and confirm a release where the conditions for release are not within the holder's own judgement. The holder is competent to confirm scope to a learner.*

No names. No page before the page. Nothing about where either of them came from, nothing about a design record, nothing about a licensor, nothing about an assembly lot, nothing about a bundle, nothing about a building, and nothing at all about a college or a hall or forty-one people on a Monday morning.

**Somebody has published the two sentences in this city that nobody in this city is allowed to argue with, and has left out every single thing anybody would need in order to say where they came from, and has put in two hundred names, and a customer's address, and the exact note a company wrote about a woman who was hurt because she was good at her job.**

Wyn Dunleavy came in at ten to seven, which was early for him, and read the whole of it standing up in about four minutes without saying anything, and then he said, "When did you first see it."

"Five past seven. It was on the chair by the kettle."

"Touched it?"

"I put gloves on and picked it up and put it on your desk. That's the whole of what I did with it."

"Photographed it."

"No."

"Are you certain."

"I am certain. I have got about nine hundred photographs on a hard hat in Nine Bridges and I would have had my phone out, and I want that on a piece of paper as much as you do, because in about three weeks somebody is going to ask me how long I stood next to it."

Dunleavy looked at him for a second.

"I am going to ask you how long you stood next to it," he said, "and I am going to write down that I asked, and you are going to answer, and it is going in a file this afternoon with a date on it. Not because I think you did anything. Because in about four months there is going to be a review and a review needs a list of what happened in order."

"Eleven seconds," said Marek Senn. "And four minutes outside, and the answer is the same for both of them."

"That is the correct answer and it is also the answer that will not save you if it is not the true one, and it is the true one, and I am going to write down that you said it in that order."

---

A woman from the company's standards side arrived at about half past nine and she was about thirty-four and she had a printed form and she did not sit down.

"Who is in the building."

"Four. Me, Dunleavy, two fitters on the early."

"Was anyone else in the building between six and half past seven."

"No."

"Was the roller shutter up."

"Up. It goes up on the timer at a quarter to six in the morning, I have watched it do it four times this week and I have never once thought about it."

The woman wrote that down.

"Mr Senn. I have to ask you three questions and I am going to ask them in this order and I would like you to answer them without helping me."

"Go on."

"Did you make a copy of any part of that document, in any form, at any time between five past seven this morning and now."

"No."

"Did you photograph any part of it."

"No."

"Is there any person outside this company who has told you about the existence of any part of it, in the last seven days."

Marek Senn stood in a depot and looked at a kettle.

**Talia Venn had rung the shop on the Sunday of week twenty at eleven in the morning and had asked him whether anybody was going to be able to produce a company training register to a district, and he had not answered that, and she had not asked it again, and on Tuesday morning she had gone into a room in Crown Terrace with a man who writes things down and had said nine words about independence and had not named anybody.**

And a boy of twenty had said *the card* into a form in a hall in Crown Terrace on the Friday, and the form was in a building in Crown Terrace, and Crown Terrace was not a company depot, and a bundle had appeared in his employer's building on Wednesday morning with two hundred names in it and a district's address and a note about a woman who was hurt.

"No," said Marek Senn. "Nobody has told me about it."

She wrote it down, and then she read the whole line back to him before she signed it, which nobody in that building had done for anything in the five weeks he had been there, and he said yes to the wording and she initialled it and gave him a copy.

**That is the fifth time in six weeks that his own handwriting has gone into a building that somebody else could find him in, and the first four were a box on a wall, a boy's first line, a refusal, and a sentence about a college, and this one is a set of answers to three questions that are all true and all of which he wanted to give.**

---

Petar Vance caught him at the rack at about eleven with a mug in his hand and did not look at the office.

"You've read it."

"I've read it."

"It's a training register and a rota and a health file. That's all that is. I have been through it four times with a pencil and it is a training register, a rota and a health file, and there are two hundred and eleven names on the register and about two hundred of them are a rough figure I would have given you this morning, and the eleven health ones are the ones that'll cost us, and if anybody in this company asks me what the worst thing in there is, the answer is eleven and not two hundred."

"I know."

"**Then I am going to ask you the other thing and I have asked nobody else and I am asking you because you are the newest and I have got nothing to lose on you.**" Vance put the mug down on the shelf. "Have you read a name off the course list."

There is a list in that building with names on it. He said so out loud to a woman on a telephone on the Sunday of week nineteen and he has not read a name off it since, and it was on a trestle table under a canopy on a district's yard on the Tuesday of week twenty-one for four hours, about four feet away from him, twice, and he did not read it twice.

"No," said Marek Senn.

"Right," said Petar Vance.

And that was it. That was the whole exchange, and it took about four seconds, and Marek Senn stood there with the answer already in his mouth and the other two-thirds of the answer behind his teeth — *there is a list, I have seen it, I have not read it, I could get it, I have not got anybody's name off it and I have not read one off it, and you put me on that course* — and he did not say any of it, because if he said it in that room then in about four days a woman with a clipboard who had been in a room in Crown Terrace on Tuesday morning would have heard the shape of it from somebody, and then the refusal he made on the Sunday becomes the leak, and the boy of twenty becomes a man whose future is the reason a company's staff list is in a bundle.

He has refused the same thing twice in one morning, to two different people, for the same reason, and the second one cost him nothing and the first one was the last time anybody in this company is going to ask him a question he can answer honestly in four seconds.

---

Copies were everywhere by the Wednesday afternoon and nobody could say where any of them had come from.

There was one on the counter of a shop in Lattice Ward at about two o'clock, face up, under a salt and pepper. There was one taped inside the door of a canteen off the Meridian at about half past three and about nine men had read it standing up. There was one in a market at about four, on a trestle table, weighed down with a bag of onions, and a woman of about sixty picked it up, read two pages, put it down, and said to nobody in particular, "Well. That's a list, that," which is what people say.

By Thursday there was a copy in Crown Terrace and one in Bower Street and one at a district office, because that is how a bundle behaves in a city this size: **it is not spread by anybody, it is spread by eleven people who are frightened, and it does not stop while there is a person who has not read it.**

Marek Senn did not ring anybody for about eleven hours.

He stood in a shop at about ten past nine that night with the shop shut and a telephone on the bench and a woman four miles away who had said *do not bring me a document* on a Sunday, and who had refused four things in a year and been refused nine, and who had spent two days this week in rooms explaining a fifteen-word phrase to men with clipboards because a man in a company had given up a person in eleven seconds on a Sunday afternoon.

There is a version of this in which he rings her and says *somebody has published a company list of course holders and I do not know who and I cannot rule myself out*, and it is a true sentence, and it is the sentence that a person who has been good about refusing things for fourteen weeks would say, and it would put a woman's ear against the only piece of information in this city that she has been refused twice and might have paid anything for.

He did not ring her.

He did not ring anybody, and he went to bed at about half past eleven, and the not-ringing was the fifth refusal in six weeks and it was the first one where he knew in advance that it was going to be the right one and was going to be the expensive one, and there was nobody in the world he could tell that to.

At about eleven the next morning a woman from a district office in Crown Terrace rang the shop to ask, very politely, whether Threadline district service was the same organisation as the Threadline named in a document that had been on a counter in Lattice Ward on Wednesday afternoon, and Marek Senn said that it was, and she said thank you, and put the phone down, and that was the whole of the call, and he has thought about it more than any other four words in this entry.

---

*79. Wednesday of week 21. I want to start with the thing that is not in it, because everything else on this page is in it.*

*What is not in it.* The upstream licensor field is not in it. The assembly lot is not in it. A bundle is not in it. A building is not in it. A college, a hall, forty-one people, a member of staff with his name on a page, a demonstrator frame, a girl's forearm — none of that is in it. **Somebody has photocopied the two sentences that nobody in this city is allowed to argue with, and has left out every single thing anybody would need in order to say where either of them came from, and has put in about two hundred names and a customer's address and a note about a woman who was hurt.** The whole of what I have spent a term chasing is the thing somebody left out. I have written that sentence in the book and I am going to leave it exactly where it is, because in about four years somebody is going to use this bundle as a reason not to look at a lot, and it will work, and it will work on me.

*Ten days.* It is ten days since a Sunday afternoon when I said a sentence out loud to a woman on a telephone about a list in my employer's building, and the reason for the sentence was a boy of twenty and the sentence has cost nothing since the day I said it. **Nothing about the refusal has changed and the price of it has moved, because the thing the refusal was about is now on a counter in this city with two hundred names on it and no name on the list I was refusing to read.** So the sentence did not leak. What leaked is the thing the sentence was standing in front of, and it came off a shelf or off a van and not out of a telephone, and I have spent four days waiting for a leak and the leak has not needed me and is not going to.

*My own name is in it, in capitals.* The rota page is the only page in the whole bundle that is a sentence about a person rather than a list of facts, and what it says, in the capitals somebody has been using all week, is that I am not carded, that a card is a condition of a callout, and to ask him why he is not carded. **The person who wrote that thinks they have written about a depot. They have written about a document, and the document is in four places by this evening, and the sentence has stopped being about me and has become a company's, and I did not write it and I cannot take it off it.** The only reason it is true is a record with four fields and a date on it in a system in a building I have been in for three weeks, and one of those four fields says *reason*, and what is in it is that I have not got one I am willing to give. So a line in capitals on a counter in four buildings is now being kept true by a sentence I typed into a form on a Friday morning at a quarter to nine, and there is nobody in the world who can join those two things together except me.

*The eleven, and the fact that there is no telephone in this city whose job it is.* About two hundred names and eleven pages, and not one person in this company has rung one of the eleven to say *this was not us, and we are sorry, and here is what we are going to do.* **There is a woman who will do it tomorrow morning from a company address, and she will put in all eleven of the letters a line that is not in any policy of that company, and she has a line manager who is going to ask her why, and she has told me none of that and she is not going to.** I have not rung any of them and I am not going to, and the reason is not that I have nothing to say to them. The reason is that the only sentence in this city that a hurt worker needs to hear has to come from a person who can be held to it afterwards, and I have spent fourteen weeks refusing to be that person, and today the reason I cannot be that person arrived on a chair by a kettle with an elastic band round it.

*The boy at the post, and this is the part I cannot put down.* His name is on a page in a bundle in his employer's building and his site is on the same page, and the same name is going into a box on a sheet next to a kettle on that yard about nine times a day in his own handwriting, because a man of fifty-eight worked out in four seconds that the price of that box would be a boy of nineteen and not the two men standing next to it, and because I said yes to a boy's pen on a Wednesday of week nineteen. **I cannot warn him. If I drive to that yard today and say one word to him, then a company in four days is going to be told that a technician knew about a bundle before it was on a chair, and I knew it at five past seven, and I had gloves on at five past seven.** I would have to tell Wyn Dunleavy the whole of it to be honest, and the whole of it includes a sheet on a customer's wall with a box on it and a company's instruction pinned over a district's card and a man of fifty-eight who has never once been asked. So I am not going to say a word to that boy before somebody else does, and I have known him five weeks and he is nineteen and he does not know what his handwriting is, and I have got exactly one move and I am not going to spend it on a thing that will be true in about four days anyway.

*What I did not do, at about ten past nine at night, with the shop shut, and the part of it I had not thought about until I sat down.* I did not ring her. **There is no register in this city on which a man can be struck off for a thing he did not do, and there is no register on which a man can be struck off for having had the chance, and I have now been on both sides of that gap inside a fortnight and I have not got anybody I can tell.** She is the person in this city most likely to be asked whether a district contractor can be supplied with a company register, and the answer is going to be no for about four years, and the reason is a man who has seen one and did not give it to her, and she does not know that is the reason, and I have decided tonight that she is not going to find out from me.

*Do not know.* Whether somebody in that yard has already read it. Whether the foreman has had a telephone call this morning. **And whether the word going round this city from Wednesday afternoon is *the technique*, or *the company's records*, and I have got a horrible and I think a correct idea that it is going to become the first one within a fortnight, and that it will be wrong, and that it will be wrong in a way that protects everybody who has a copy and costs everybody who does not, and that I will not be able to prove a thing about it, and that in about a year that belief will be worth money to somebody, and the somebody will not be a person I can name today.**
