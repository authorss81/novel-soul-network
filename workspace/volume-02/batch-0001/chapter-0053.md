# Chapter 53 — Forty-One

The notice went up on the board outside the practical department office on the Thursday morning of week fifteen, on one sheet, with a drawing pin through the top left corner, and it had the department's mark on it and a date in the top right.

**PRACTICAL REFRESHER — REVISION 4-19. Monday of week 19, practical hall, 09:30. All students and staff holding a current 4-19 card. Attendance is not compulsory. Attendance is recorded.**

Marek Senn read it standing up, at about eleven, with four people either side of him, and got to the end of it and read the first two words of it again, and then went and looked at the number.

Forty-one names. He counted them twice, because the first time he got thirty-nine and he wanted to be sure, and it was forty-one, and the last three on the list were a man of about sixty, a woman with her name spelled with a hyphen, and **Davan Rusk.**

"It says all students and staff," said a voice behind him, "and if it says you then it is a draft, and it is not a draft, so read it again and it does not say you."

Darla Vint had a folder under her arm and the look of a woman who has been awake since six.

"It says *holding a current 4-19 card*."

"That is not a criterion, that is a category. The institute holds three hundred and forty and about ninety of us are staff, so there are a bit over four hundred of us, and every one of the four hundred has got one, and that is the number I keep hearing, and it frightens me more than the forty-one does." She turned the sheet about a quarter of an inch so that it was square with the frame of the board, and then stopped with her hand on the edge of it, and did not go on. "I am not going to tidy a noticeboard. I did it once in nine years and it was a man who came in on the Monday and took his name off a list."

She left it square and went.

---

Anneke Roos had eleven minutes between a meeting and a telephone call, and on the Thursday afternoon of week fifteen she used nineteen of them, in the corridor, standing against the wall by the copy room where there is no window, and the telephone call in the next room started without her and went on without her and she mentioned that afterwards in one sentence and not in a good mood.

"You have read the notice."

"I've read the notice."

"Forty-one. Eleven of them took the 4-19 card in September out of the same box, and the box is not the department's, and the department has never owned a box, and every one of those eleven signed a card that says they have read revision 4-19 and been taught by a competent holder." She had her hands behind her back. "I have got a refresher on the Monday of week nineteen for forty-one people and I have got one instructor, because there is one instructor, and I am not going to pretend to you that I could put a second one in that hall who is any use, and I am not going to pretend either that I have anybody else to ask."

"You are asking me."

"I am asking you."

"I cannot do it, and I am going to give you both reasons, and the second one is the one that is going to still be there in September."

Roos did not move and did not look away.

"The first one is the fourth condition of the Working Share," said Marek Senn, "which is a departmental clause, and it is yours, because you signed it and read it out in a room in front of a panel's secretary on the Thursday of week fourteen. It says the holder may not hold a practice alone on any equipment owned or held by the department, on any premises of the department, or under the department's insurance, at any time in this term, and the prohibition is not limited to hours and it is not limited to equipment." He said it as a person reading a line out of a document he had read a great deal. "Forty-one people in a hall in this building for an hour and a half on the Monday of week nineteen is not outside that. It is not a reading. It is not a near miss. It is the thing the sentence was written to stop, and it was written by a woman who has never liked me and has never pretended to and who is not going to enjoy being told by me that I have read it four hundred times."

"That is the correct answer."

"The second one is condition four of the trust's list, which is not yours and is not mine, and it is read off a sheet by a man called Anders Kolt, and it says the holder does not demonstrate, teach, assess or assist at any practice in this department, in this term, on any premises of this department. Same words, different room, different author, and it is the same prohibition." He looked at the board. "And here is the thing I have not been able to put down yet. On Wednesday of week sixteen that one stops existing, because the condition it is in lapses on the Wednesday of week sixteen, and yours does not, and I am going to be standing in a hall on the Monday of week nineteen with exactly one bar across the door instead of two, and I have worked out that I would rather have had two. That is not an argument. It is a fact about me and I would like it on a piece of paper somewhere before somebody else puts it on one."

"That is the answer you were going to give."

"It is the answer I had at ten o'clock this morning and I want you to have both halves of it, because you have spent four years reading people's answers to you and you have told me in the last fortnight that you cannot always tell which half they worked out first." He put his hands in his pockets. "The half I worked out first was the departmental one, and the reason I worked that one out first is that it is mine. It is in a document with my name on the front of it. Condition four is on a sheet in a file and it is not mine and it was written by somebody who thought about me for a paragraph before she wrote it, and I found that out on Wednesday and I have not been able to put it down since."

She took her hands from behind her back.

"In the last four weeks I have had two people tell me what is wrong with that condition," she said, "and one of them was the department's own insurance officer, and what the insurance officer said was *we wrote that clause to stop a twenty-two-year-old being alone in a room with a plate and no cover and no one to fetch him*, and that is true, and I wrote it, and I would write it again tomorrow. And what she also said, and she was not being kind, she was doing her job, is that the clause now says that the only person in this building who is any use with a fourth beat cannot teach a fourth beat, and that in about four years the department is going to have a maintenance problem and no way of growing anybody out of it, and that the answer to that is not to change the clause. The answer to that is for you to stop being twenty-two."

"Four years is a long time."

"It is the length of a term," said Anneke Roos, "and I have forty-one people on a sheet for the Monday of week nineteen, which is three and a half weeks off, and I am going to go and stand in a room with one instructor and a manual that is out of date and I am not going to blame a clause, and you should not let anybody blame it either, and if anybody does blame it I will want to hear about it, and I will not be able to do anything about it, which is also part of what a deputy registrar is."

She looked at her watch.

"You may come to the briefing," she said. "I am holding one on the Friday at half past eleven for the eleven who took the card in September. It is in the practical building, it is a briefing and not a practice, there is no equipment in the room, and nobody will be asked to do anything. You may sit at the back. You will not be asked to say anything, and if anybody asks you to say anything I will say no before they finish the sentence."

"Why are you letting me in at all."

"Because you are going to be in this building on the Monday of week nineteen anyway, and I would rather you were in it looking at the same manual I am looking at than in a shop being right on your own," said Anneke Roos, "and that is not a kindness either. It is triage."

---

Darla Vint was in the copy room at about half past nine the same morning, doing the thing she does with a board that has a drawing pin through the top left corner, which is nothing.

"Fourth line," she said, before he had got to the door. "You have read it four times and you have not asked about the fourth line, which is a record of who is on a sheet in a building, and I want to get to you before somebody in this office does."

"It says attendance is recorded."

"Attendance is recorded. Into what, you are about to ask me, and I am going to tell you, because you have never once asked me and I have noticed that and I have decided it is my job to notice things about you in case you go somewhere." She put a chair the wrong way round to sit on and then left it that way. "It goes on the sheet. The sheet goes in a file. The file goes to the regional office at the end of the term with the marks and the failures in it, because we are a school and schools report, and nobody has to give me permission to send it, it is the ordinary end-of-term return and it has been the ordinary end-of-term return since before you were here."

"Names on it."

"Names on it. Forty-one names, and then present, absent, late, and a column for *reason given* if a member of staff rings in and says a reason, and the column has a tick in it if a member of staff does not ring." She let that sit for a second, which she does not usually do. "In September, out of the hundred and eleven third-years Rusk taught the 4-19 card to, thirty-one did not attend the first session after the half term, and there is a column, and the column is why the department did not find out about any of it for a year, because a boy who did not attend in week four of a term is a boy who was unwell in week four of a term. I know that because I wrote the return."

"And you cannot tell me who was in the room."

"I could tell you in about ninety seconds, and if I did it you would have a list of thirty-one names and no other fact, and you would put it in a book on a bench and in about four years somebody would use it to say that thirty-one people in this city were taught a card that does not match the book in their hands, and every one of those thirty-one would be findable by a sentence in a paragraph, and you would have done to them precisely what this building did to your case with a piece of A4 and a pen."

"I have not asked you for it."

"No," said Darla Vint. "You have not. And I have watched you not ask for it eleven times in fourteen weeks and it is the only reason I have been able to keep on speaking to you in a corridor, and I would like that noted somewhere, and I would like it noted by you, and I am aware that that is me asking you for something and that I have just spent a paragraph explaining why nobody should give you anything."

---

The briefing was on the Friday morning of week fifteen at half past eleven in a room off the corridor that had a lectern in it and eleven chairs and a whiteboard, and Marek Senn sat at the back with his hands on his knees and was not asked to say anything.

Davan Rusk stood at the front with a copy of the department's manual in his hand, and it was revision 3-08, and he put it on the lectern face down, and then turned it round, and said the number out loud before he said anything else, in a room, in the way a man does a thing he has decided to do in advance.

"Revision three-oh-eight. That is the number on the book in your hands. It is not a typing error and it is not a trick question and I am going to spend two hours on Thursday of week nineteen with forty-one people and a book that says something different from the card, and I have been the department's instructor for twenty-two years and I ordered the printing of that book myself in March, out of a teaching budget with eight credits in it, and there is a form with my initials on it and the form is in the department's file and I would like it minuted that I knew."

Nobody said anything. A man of about sixty in the second row said, "Why?"

"Because nobody asked me," said Davan Rusk. "That is the whole reason and it took me thirteen weeks to say it out loud in a room and I am not going to dress it up for eleven people at half past eleven on a Friday morning." He picked the book up. "On the third beat on the card, you hand the decision back. That is what is written on your card. In this book it says the practitioner confirms aloud what the brace has been permitted to touch and returns the next decision to the subject of the brace, and those are the same sentence with a different man in it, and I taught you out of the book for twenty-two years and there is a term's worth of you in this city who can do a release on their own and have never once been asked to, and I think that is the sentence I am supposed to be saying in a room, and I have been not saying it in a room since September."

"Marek," said somebody at the front, and it was a girl of about twenty with a lanyard, and she had not been introduced. "You're the one from the assessment. Are you going to say something about the book?"

"No," said Marek Senn.

She waited.

"He wrote it down," he said. "That's the minute. I'm not going to improve it."

It was about two and a half hours long and Rusk did the whole of it, and Marek Senn wrote nine lines in the back of a spare day sheet and none of them in the load book, and at about two o'clock he asked one question, and it was the only one he asked all day, and he asked it at the end and he asked it into his collar because he did not want a room of eleven people to turn round.

"Mr Rusk. The 4-19 card says the fourth beat is a support beat, and the learner is not expected to do it alone, and the instructor confirms on the beat. Page four of the booklet has a header on it about what happens when a local node does not answer. Is there a version of page four in the manual?"

Davan Rusk looked at the manual for a while.

"Yes," he said.

"Will you read it out."

"It is not in the book," said Davan Rusk, "and the card says it is in the booklet, and the booklet is a Threadline document and I have four of them sealed in a bay in the building, and I have not read page four since the week of the open day, and I am not going to read it out to eleven people at about two o'clock on a Friday because I do not know what is on the rest of the page, and if there is a sentence on it that they have not seen then they will all have it by Friday night whether I read it or not."

He put the book down.

"You've read it," he said. "You read it in week five in a store room and you have been carrying it about ever since like a man carrying somebody else's shopping."

"Yes."

"Then read it to them yourself," said Davan Rusk, "on the Monday of week nineteen, from the front of the room, in the teaching shape, and it will be the fourth beat of the four-19 card, and I will sit at the back and let you do it."

"No," said Marek Senn.
