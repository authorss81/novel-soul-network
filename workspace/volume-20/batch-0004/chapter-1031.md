# Chapter 1031 — The Question He Asks Last

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and about nine people came up that stair in the evening and were asked three things out loud one at a time, and Marek was on that bench from about half past six and did not sit down once.**

---

That Wednesday put nine people through that shop and nine names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and six days, two hundred and seventy-two weeks and two days. The sheet that had been taped to a wall in a fourth district was seventy-nine days old and is in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was sixty-five days old and its whereabouts are not known, and the four words were fifty-six days old.**

---

**He had gone up that stair at about half past six with the day's work already behind him and had put nobody's name anywhere, and about four people who come to that room have said since that he stood at the bench and not behind it for about four hours.**

Marek said: **nobody has been asked anything before they come up.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **nobody has.**

Marek said: **nobody has for four weeks and nobody will while I am standing here.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **that is not a rule. You have never once called it a rule and I have listened for it.**

Marek said: **no. It is a thing I have not stopped doing.**

---

**And then at about half past two, before any of it, he had walked about nine minutes into a second district to a building that prints things, and about four people in that building have said since that he did not knock and that the man of about sixty-one came out of the back of it and let him stand in the doorway.**

Marek said: **I have come with nothing again and I want that written down as a choice and not an accident.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I have four sheets of your type in a drawer and I have had them since the second time you came in here.**

Marek said: **then they are not for this.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **they are not for anything. They are paper and it will keep.**

---

**And then he asked the one he asks last every time, and about four people in that building have said since that he asked it in the same voice as the other three and that Marek answered it in about two seconds.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **what is it for.**

Marek said: **it tells anybody who comes up that stair that three things get asked at that bench, out loud, one at a time, in front of everybody else in the room.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **and what goes over the top of it.**

Marek said: **a heading.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then there is still nothing to print.**

---

**And then he started the sentence he has started three times in about four weeks, and about four people in that building have said since that he got further than he has ever got and that he stopped before the end of the fourth word and that the man of about sixty-one let him.**

Marek said: **the reason there are three things asked at that bench and not one is that a person—**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I will not stop you.**

Marek said: **no. I am going to stop, and I am going to stop now, and I have been about four seconds.**

---

**And then Marek said what he had actually come to say, and he said it to a doorway and not to the counter, and about four people in that building have said since that he said it once and did not say it twice.**

Marek said: **there are four people in this city who would put a heading over that card tomorrow if I let them and I am not going to let them, and I am telling you that I have got four of them and not one of them has offered it to me yet, and every one of the four would want it to say the thing you keep asking me for.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **so I am to understand that you have four people who can write it and no heading.**

Marek said: **I am telling you that I am not going to be the one who asks any of them for it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is four refusals you have not made yet.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man went into a second district with nothing in his hand because he had chosen to and came out with nothing, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and eighteenth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Wednesday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that landing, and they came to thirty-four pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose that had been painted round twice over.

He took the old rose off, cut a proper one in and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A rose painted round twice is a rose somebody has stopped looking at. Roses in that shop are like it, and one of them has been a ceiling that about four of them have washed with a cloth in their hands and no lamp in.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in outdoors on a plate that had been balanced on a sill.

He took the plate down, made an enclosure that sheds and proved the socket from the gate.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A plate balanced on a sill is a plate that is on its way down and the water comes before the fall does. Sockets in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a step somebody has had to put a bucket by.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a flex that had been taped where it crossed the tiled worktop.

He took the tape off, made a proper joint and proved the cooker with the oven on.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**Flex taped onto a worktop is flex under a hot pan every day of the week. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a ring a woman has had to work round.**"

A light on that landing had been put in with a holder that had been glued into its fitting.

He took the holder out with a blade, bedded a proper one and proved the light from the door.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A holder glued into a fitting comes off with the first cold night and takes the fitting with it. Holders on landings are like that, and one of them has been a door that about four of them have had to leave alone.**"

**Thirty-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder never came off the back of that shelf, and no figure for the page it holds is written anywhere on this one.**
