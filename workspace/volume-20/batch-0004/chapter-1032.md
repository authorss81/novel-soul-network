# Chapter 1032 — A Word He Did Not Finish

**Nobody who came up that stair on that Thursday could get one sentence finished, and behind a bench until two was six hours at that counter with four converted units off a service road after that, and Marek came up at about half past seven and stood where he stands.**

---

Seven callers went through that shop on that Thursday and seven names were put against that Thursday's line as they came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and by ten the shutter had come down.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and seven days, two hundred and seventy-two weeks and three days. The sheet taken off a wall in a fourth district was eighty days old and lies in a bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, sixty-six days old is the printing nobody has traced, and the four words were fifty-seven days old.**

---

**He came up at about half past seven and stood where he always stands, and about four people already in that room have said since that he had it ready in his mouth before he got to the fourth stair.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have got it.**

Marek said: **you have not.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I had it on the second landing and I have been carrying it down four flights.**

Marek said: **then say it before you lose it again.**

---

**And he got as far as a word, and about four people in that room have said since that he said the first half of it clearly enough for everybody present to hear what word it was going to be.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **a card with nothing over the top of it is asking somebody to work it out, and if it is going to have anything over it then it should say—**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **stop.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have not finished.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no, and you are not going to, and that word is her word and she is not the one offering it.**

---

**And then she said the rest of it, and she said it in about nine words, and about four people in that room have said since that two of them stopped what they were doing and that nobody looked at the man of about thirty-three while she said it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **put a word over that card and you have written down why we ask them.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have not written anything down.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not, and that is why it has to be somebody else who does not write anything down either, and that is nobody in this room.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three asked her the thing he has asked four times, and about four people in that room have said since that he asked it in a completely ordinary voice, which is the way he asks it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **then where does a person find out what goes over the top of it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is not my question and you know it is not.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I am one of three people holding a thing. I have said that four times and I am going to keep saying it, and it does not matter what goes over the top of a card. I am asking you whether any of you have ever heard where that thing came from.**

Marek said: **no.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **you have not said no to me. You have said that nobody in this room has.**

Marek said: **that is the same answer and I am not going to improve on it for you.**

---

**And then a woman of about forty-four who had come up that evening for the first time said the only useful thing anybody said, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody argued with her afterwards.**

The woman of about forty-four said: **I have been in two rooms in this city where a person is asked three things before they walk in, and in both of them there was a word over the door, and in both of them I could tell you what the word was, and in here I cannot tell you what happens and I have been sitting here for an hour trying.**

Marek said: **that is because it is said out loud and not put on anything.**

The woman of about forty-four said: **no. It is because I have watched four people in here say the same three sentences at that bench and not one of them has explained why.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man got four words into a sentence about a card and was stopped by a woman in front of about nine people, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and nineteenth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Thursday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that corridor, and they came to twenty-seven pounds, exact.**

A fuse in that shop had been put in on a length of flex that had been twisted together and pushed under a terminal.

He pulled the twist out, made the joint properly and proved the fuse from the switch.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**Twist a flex and push it under a terminal and you have built a joint that gets warmer every time somebody switches on. Fuses in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a wall that about four of them have had to flick twice to get a light out of.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in with a flex that had been run through the handle of a gate and left there.

He took it out of the hinge, put it back the short way round and proved the lamp from the path.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**Flex through a gate handle is flex that a gate has in its grip every time anybody shuts it. Lamps in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate that about four of them have had to lift rather than shut.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a box that had been screwed to the underside of a worktop.

He took the box off, chased one into the masonry behind and proved the cooker.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A box screwed under a worktop is a box you need a worktop to get at. Boxes in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a drawer somebody has had to pull out and unscrew.**"

A light in that corridor had been put in on a switch that had been wired to the lamp two doors along.

He traced it, put a switch where the lamp was and proved the light from its own door.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A switch two doors from its lamp is a switch that somebody has to be told about before anybody can help them. Switches in that corridor are like that, and one of them has been a landing where about four of them have stood and shouted.**"

**Twenty-seven pounds is the sum of those four on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**That Thursday had the last of those four rooms shut by half past six with a woman of about thirty behind its door, and the binder on the back of that shelf was not opened, and this page sets no figure at all against the page it holds.**
