# Chapter 1033 — He Came Asking For A Form

**That Saturday ran a counter from about ten with four converted units off a service road after it, and for about four hours that afternoon nobody behind that counter could point anybody at a form, or at the sheet the people had come for.**

---

Six callers came into that shop on that Saturday and six names went onto that Saturday's page as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past four, and nothing in that shop was shut before ten.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and nine days, two hundred and seventy-two weeks and five days. The sheet that came off a wall in a fourth district is eighty-two days old and is in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was sixty-eight days old and was never on that wall at all, and the four words were fifty-nine days old.**

---

**He came in at about half past two with a plastic bag in each hand and did not put them down, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he had the whole conversation with a shop assistant before Marek was in the room.**

He said: **she told me there was a form.**

Marek said: **there is no form.**

He said: **she said there was a thing you fill in.**

Marek said: **there is nothing to fill in.**

He said: **she said the man upstairs asks you three things.**

Marek said: **that part is true.**

---

**And then he asked the question, and it was not an angry one, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he asked it the way a person asks for a bus time.**

He said: **what am I supposed to do with that.**

Marek said: **nothing.**

He said: **I came four stops on a bus for nothing.**

Marek said: **you came four stops on a bus to be asked three things out loud in front of about nine people.**

---

**And then he said where the description came from, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he said it without any weight on it at all and that three of them stopped.**

He said: **my cousin's partner was told there is somewhere you can go and be told about a form. That is all she said. She did not say anything else and I did not ask her anything else, because I am not a person who asks people.**

Marek said: **that is about four weeks old.**

He said: **then it has been about four weeks and nobody has corrected it.**

Marek said: **no, and I am not going to print anything that corrects it.**

He said: **why not.**

Marek said: **because the only thing that stops a thing people say out loud is a second thing people can say out loud, and the second thing is the same thing.**

---

**And then he asked whether he should go up, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that Marek said yes in one word and that the man put both bags down on the floor and had to pick them up again.**

He said: **so what do I do.**

Marek said: **up.**

He said: **with no form.**

Marek said: **with no form.**

He said: **that is where I am going.**

---

**And then at about half past six he went up that stair with the bags and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he was up there for about nine minutes and came down with the bags still in his hands.**

He said: **you never said what it was for.**

Marek said: **no.**

He said: **she said you would.**

Marek said: **I know what she said. I have been four weeks not being able to change what she said, and there are about four people a week coming off that description, and I have not got a way of stopping it that does not make a second description.**

He said: **that is a bad thing to be four weeks into.**

Marek said: **it is.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man carried two plastic bags four stops on a bus to be told there is nothing to fill in and then went up a stair anyway, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and twenty-first night.**

---

**The four jobs that Saturday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that store, and they came to forty-one pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in with a back plate that had been fitted over the face of another one.

He took the second one off, cut the box out and refitted the first properly.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**Put one plate over another and you have made a joint nobody can undo without cutting the wall. Plates in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a switch somebody has had to take a blade to.**"

A light in that yard had been put in on a bracket that had been bolted through a wooden rail.

He moved it onto the masonry and proved the light from the far corner.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A bracket bolted to a wooden rail is a bracket waiting for the rail to go. Brackets in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a fence a man has had to take down and put back.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with an outlet box that had been filled with plaster and then painted.

He opened it up, cleared it and proved the cooker with the grill on.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**Fill a box with plaster and you have packed it shut. Boxes in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a wall a woman has had to have open twice.**"

A lamp in that store had been put in on a flex that had been stapled to the ceiling.

He took the staples out, clip it properly and proved the lamp from the door.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**Staples in a flex are the cheapest warm-up in this city. Flexes in that store are like that, and one of them has been a shelf that about four of them have had to reach past.**"

**Forty-one pounds is the sum of those four on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Saturday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder on the back of that shelf was where it was, and this page writes no figure of any kind against what that binder holds.**
