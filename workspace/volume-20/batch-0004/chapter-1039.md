# Chapter 1039 — What Came Down That Stair On Monday

**That Monday began with about four people not coming up a stair they had come up most weeks since the middle of winter, and Marek did the day's work first and went up at about a quarter to seven and stood at the bench.**

---

Eight callers went through that shop on that Monday and eight names were written against that Monday's page as each of them arrived, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and ten o'clock found the shutter down.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and eighteen days, two hundred and seventy-four weeks to the day. The sheet that was on a wall in a fourth district is ninety-one days old and is in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, seventy-seven days old is the second printing, and the four words were sixty-eight days old.**

---

**The first one came up at about half past six and had been asked three things at a door, and about four people in that room have said since that they recognised him from about nine days ago and that nobody said so.**

Marek said: **you were asked.**

The man said: **I was.**

Marek said: **you were not asked by anybody who has been in this room.**

The man said: **no.**

Marek said: **go and sit down.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three pressed it for the sixth time, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it more quietly than the five times before it and that it went round the room anyway.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **there are three of us holding that word and one of us has it wrong, and I have said that six times now and about four people in this room think I want something.**

Marek said: **you want something.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I want to be one of about four people who can walk in off a street and go up a stair without carrying a thing that somebody gave me on a Friday. I have not got a way of saying that without saying the other thing and I have been asking somebody to say it for me for about a month.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **do not ask me.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I know. I am asking the room.**

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-eight came up at about seven and asked to be asked three things, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the first time in about four weeks that anybody had asked for it out loud.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **can I be asked.**

Marek said: **you can be asked if you want to be.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **I have been standing outside that door for about nine minutes.**

Marek said: **then come in and stand inside it.**

---

**And then at about a quarter to eight he asked her the three things and she answered all three, and about four people in that room have said since that she answered the third one so fast that two people did not hear it and asked her to say it again.**

Marek said: **that is the first of three, and it is the one about what somebody is prepared to put in a room with nine people listening to it.**
The woman of about thirty-eight said: **nothing today and I would like everybody to know that.**

---

**And then he asked her the second one and she stopped before she got to the end of it, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the first time anybody had stopped in the middle of an answer in about four years.**

Marek said: **that is the second of three, and it is the one about what somebody carries out of here and does not leave lying about in here.**
The woman of about thirty-eight said: **I—**

---

**And then Marek stopped asking, out loud, in front of about nine people, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody had heard him do that before.**

Marek said: **that is enough.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **I have not finished.**

Marek said: **you have stopped and that is a whole answer and I am not going to make you do the rest of it, and I am not going to write down that you stopped.**

The woman of about thirty-eight said: **everybody heard me stop.**

Marek said: **yes, and about nine people did not say anything, and that is now the only thing that has happened in this room for about four weeks.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man of about thirty-three said out loud for the sixth time that he is one of three people carrying a thing and that he would rather be an ordinary caller, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and thirtieth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Monday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that stair, and they came to thirty pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in on a plate held by two screws with a third hole empty.

He put a third screw in and proved the socket from the wall.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**Two screws and an empty third hole is a plate that has worked loose once already. Plates in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a bench somebody has had to hold while they changed a bulb.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in on a rose with a lamp in it that had been a different rating.

He changed it for the correct rating and proved the lamp from the path.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp that is the wrong rating is a lamp that has been on about four times as long as it was built for. Lamps in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gable about four of them have had to change a bulb twice a year.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with an outlet behind it that has been in the wall since the wall was built.

He took the old one out, made the hole good and proved the cooker.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**Leave a socket in a wall behind an appliance and it is there for whoever comes next. Sockets in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a cupboard a woman has had to move to reach.**"

A light on that stair had been put in on a two-way switch that only worked one way.

He rewired the pair and proved the light from both landings.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A two-way switch that only works one way is a switch somebody has had to go round a corner to use. Switches on stairs are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have come home up in the dark.**"

**Thirty pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**

---

**About half past six on that Monday shut the last of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder on the back of that shelf was not opened, and no figure for the page it holds is printed on this page.**
