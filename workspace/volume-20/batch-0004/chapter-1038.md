# Chapter 1038 — The Man Who Said It Worked

**A man of about thirty-seven had been standing at a door at the bottom of a car park since six that morning with a chair he had brought himself, and that Saturday was a counter from about ten and then four converted units off that service road.**

---

Six callers came through that shop on that Saturday and six names were written against that Saturday's book as each of them arrived, the last of them let away at about half past four, and that shop stayed open until ten.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and sixteen days, two hundred and seventy-three weeks and five days. The sheet off a wall in a fourth district is eighty-nine days old and is in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, seventy-five days old is the printing nobody has traced, and the four words were sixty-six days old.**

---

**He came up at about half past seven with a folding chair under his arm and about four people in that room have said since that he put it against the wall by the door and did not sit in it and that it was still there at ten.**

Marek said: **you said about a fortnight.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I said about a fortnight. It is ten days.**

Marek said: **you said you would tell me first.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I did tell you first. You knew on the Saturday before last and I started on the Monday and that is exactly the shape I said it would come in.**

---

**And then he said how many, and about four people in that room have said since that he said the number out loud and that nobody had asked him for it, again.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **ten days. Nine of them people come off that road. Nine people. Two said no and went up anyway and one of those two is sitting over there.**

Marek said: **I know which one.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **then you have heard about it and it did not come from me and it still came from me.**

---

**And then he made the third request of that week, and it was not a word, and about four people in that room have said since that it took him a while to say and that he got there by going round it twice.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I am not bringing you a word. I am bringing you the argument for one.**

Marek said: **I do not want the argument.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **you want it. A woman in a first district cannot write a word over that card because four people have asked her in six years and she has said no. A man in a second district has four sheets in a drawer. That is two people who could put one there tomorrow and neither of them will, and both of them have a reason that is a sentence about what is behind the three things, and neither of them will say it.**

Marek said: **that is not an argument, that is a description of a door everybody in this city has shut.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **it is an argument. A person off that description has to be able to say out loud where they are about to go, and I have watched about four of them try to say it this month and every one of them had to say a thing that is not true.**

---

**And then the refusal came, and it came from the woman of about thirty-four, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the first time she had refused anybody in that room.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **no, and I am the person you should have asked, because I have been in two rooms in this city with a word over the door and I could tell you what the word was.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **and could you tell me now.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **no. I could tell you now and I have decided not to and I want it noticed that a woman who sat here and did not answer one of three things is now refusing a man a sentence, and those two things are the same thing.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-seven said the thing that made the room go quiet, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it as a man reading a delivery list and did not enjoy it.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **there is one thing wrong with what I am doing at that door and I have not got round to it and I am going to say it now because about four of you will not.**

Marek said: **say it.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **the only thing anybody can say against me is what that room is for. That is all of it. Ask me why I ask first and I will tell you it is because it went well. Ask me why it went well and you have to say what it was for. I have been waiting about four days for one of you to say it and nobody has.**

---

**And then he said the half of it that is his, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it without being asked and that nobody took it off him.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **it worked. Where I do it, eleven people went in and every one of them knew what was going to be asked of them and eleven people have not walked in off a street since, and I was one of the eleven and I am not giving that back to anybody.**

Marek said: **nobody is asking you to give it back.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **then somebody in this room is going to have to say what this is, and they have not, and about four people a week are going to keep being told something that is not it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man stood in a room with a chair against the wall that he never sat in and asked for an argument instead of a word and was refused, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and twenty-eighth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Saturday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that gate, and they came to forty-three pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in with a blanking plate over a second outlet that somebody had pulled out of.

He took the plate off, made a proper second outlet and proved both.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A blanking plate over an outlet somebody has emptied is a socket that has been written off by whoever last saw it. Sockets in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a bench a person has had to keep a lamp on.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in with a flex wrapped round its own bracket and then screwed on.

He unwound it, took the bracket down and re-fixed the lamp on its own terminals.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**Flex wrapped round a bracket is a flex somebody could not reach, so they reached it another way. Lamps in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a wall a man has had to take a barrow past.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a door interlock that had been bridged out.

He took the bridge out, replaced the interlock and proved the oven door.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A bridge across an oven interlock is an oven that will hold heat against a person. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been an oven door a woman has had to watch a child at.**"

A gate light had been put in with a switch mounted on the wrong side of a gate post.

He took it across to the post it belongs on and proved the light from the path.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A switch on the far side of a gate is a switch you have to reach through the gate to use. Switches like that are like that, and one of them has been a drive that about four of them have come in in the dark.**"

**Forty-three pounds is the sum of those four on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Saturday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder on the back of that shelf did not come off it, and no figure for what it holds is written anywhere in this file.**
