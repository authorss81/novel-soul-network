# Chapter 1036 — Everybody Heard Her Not Say It

**That Wednesday put Marek behind that bench from about half past six with about nine people in that room in front of him, and the day's work had been finished before half past five and the last of it was a lamp in a yard.**

---

Seven callers went through that shop on that Wednesday and seven names were written against that Wednesday's page as each of them arrived, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and nothing on that counter was closed before ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and thirteen days, two hundred and seventy-three weeks and two days. The sheet that was on a wall in a fourth district is eighty-six days old and is in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was seventy-two days old and has never been found, and the four words were sixty-three days old.**

---

**She came up at about half past six with a folder under her arm and about four people in that room have said since that she had not brought it in about a month and that she put it down on the bench rather than on a chair.**

Marek said: **you have come three times asking for this.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I have come about nine times.**

Marek said: **you have asked for this three times.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **three times out loud. The rest of the times I sat on that stair and decided not to.**

---

**And then he asked her the first of the three things out loud at that bench in front of everybody, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody breathed for the whole of it.**

Marek said: **that is the first of three, and it is the one about what a person will put in a room.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **the fact that I am frightened of something and the shape of it and not the name of the person who put it there.**

Marek said: **that is an answer and everybody heard it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I know. I have been waiting four days to find out whether that is allowed.**

---

**And then he asked the second one, and about four people in that room have said since that she gave a different answer to the one she gave on a landing about three weeks ago.**

Marek said: **that is the second of three, and it is the one about what a person takes away with them.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **nothing this week.**

Marek said: **on a landing three weeks ago you said a great deal.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I said a great deal on a landing and I have said a different thing here in front of you and I would like it noticed that it changed and that I changed it in this room and not on a stair.**

---

**And then he asked the third one, and about four people in that room have said since that eight of the nine people there turned their heads away before he had finished the question and that she noticed every one of them.**

Marek said: **that is the third of three, and it is the one about stopping.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **—**

Marek said: **you do not have to say anything.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I know that. I want it on the record that I know that.**

---

**And then she sat there for about nine seconds and nobody said anything, and about four people in that room have said since that they have not had nine seconds of silence in that room in about four years.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **that is what I came for.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you got it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **on a landing it is nothing. It is a woman not saying a word in a corridor with a fan going and nobody in the corridor. Here it is nine people not saying a word.**

---

**And then at about half past eight a man who had been in that room once, in the first week, asked her in the doorway what she had been refusing to say, and about four people who have passed that doorway have said since that she answered him in about nine words and did not go up afterwards.**

The man said: **what were you not saying.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **whether I would go back if somebody asked me to.**

The man said: **that is an answer.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **it is the answer, and it is not for you, and I have said it in a doorway where about four people heard it and none of them wrote it down, which is exactly the shape I wanted it in.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about nine people in that room heard a woman not answer a question and stayed quiet for about nine seconds, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and twenty-fifth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Wednesday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that roof, and they came to twenty-four pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in with the front plate fixed over a plastered edge.

He took the plate off, cut the box square and refitted it.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A plate over a plastered edge is a plate that works loose when the plaster moves. Plates in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a corner that about four of them have had to patch.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in on a flex that had been tied in a knot to shorten it.

He cut the knot out, made it up properly and proved the lamp from the gate.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A knot in a flex is nine inches of heat nobody can get at. Flexes in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a bulb somebody has had to change twice.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a socket at the end of a trailing lead about two feet long.

He took the lead out, put a socket on the wall and proved the cooker.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A cooker on a trailing lead is a cooker somebody has to remember not to lean on. Cookers in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a worktop a woman has had to keep clear.**"

A light on that roof had been put in on a holder screwed to a batten that had been cut short.

He made a proper fixing into the joist and proved the light from the hatch.

"**Four pounds,**" he said. "**A holder screwed to a batten that stops short is a holder that works loose in a wind. Holders on roofs are like that, and one of them has been a hatch about four of them have had to prop open.**"

**Twenty-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**By half past six on that Wednesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder on the back of that shelf stayed where it was, and nothing on this page sets a figure against what that binder holds.**
