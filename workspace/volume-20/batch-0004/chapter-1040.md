# Chapter 1040 — He Ruled A Line Under Nothing

**On that Tuesday Marek took a rule out of that drawer and ruled a line under the top of that card and left the space above the line empty, and it was a bench until two before any of the rest of it.**

---

Seven callers came into that shop on that Tuesday and seven names were written against that Tuesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and it was not shut until ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and nineteen days, two hundred and seventy-four weeks and one day. The sheet that was on a wall in a fourth district is ninety-two days old and is in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, seventy-eight days old is the printing nobody has found, and the four words were sixty-nine days old.**

---

**He had done it before half past six and had not told anybody, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that the card was still in the rail where it has been for about four weeks and that he had taken it out, ruled it and put it back.**

Marek said: **I have ruled a line under the top of it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **under nothing.**

Marek said: **under nothing.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is about the fourth thing anybody has brought up that stair and it is the first one that has come out of my own hand.**

---

**And then he said out loud that he was proposing it, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it standing at the bench and not sitting down, and that he said it twice.**

Marek said: **I am proposing that nothing goes over that line and I want it known that I am not proposing it as an answer.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you cannot put a blank up as a refusal. That is not what a refusal is.**

Marek said: **I know.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then what is it.**

Marek said: **it is the only thing I have got, and it will be blank at the end of this, and nobody has to decide that today.**

---

**And then she told him what a line is for, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it to him and not to the room, and that two of the nine looked at the card when she had finished.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **a line says a thing goes there. Put a line under nothing and you have told about four people a week that somebody knows what goes there and is not saying.**

Marek said: **I know that as well.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then why did you rule it.**

Marek said: **because if there is no line then about four people will put a word in the gap and call it mine, and I would rather the gap is mine.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four said the thing that has not been said in that room for about four weeks, and about four people in that room have said since that she got three words out and then stopped herself.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **this room is a—**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **do not.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I know. I know, and I am going to say that I was about to say it and that I am not going to, and I am saying that instead.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you are allowed one of those every so often and the last one was on a Thursday.**

---

**And then he stood at that bench for about nine minutes looking at the wall behind the chair the woman of about fifty-four keeps her coat on, and about four people in that room have said since that they have noticed him do it before and that nobody has ever seen him look at it directly.**

Marek said: **I have not got a word and I am not going to have one.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I know. I have watched you look at that wall for about four weeks and I have never once known what for.**

Marek said: **there was something on that wall before the nineteen lines went up and I have been working out what it was.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **do not finish that sentence.**

Marek said: **I was not going to.**

---

**And then he went down that corridor about nine minutes to a second district with nothing in his hand and left the card in the rail, and about four people in that building have said since that he told the man of about sixty-one what he had ruled and that the man of about sixty-one did not answer for about nine seconds.**

Marek said: **there is a line under the top of it and nothing above the line.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is a box with nothing in it.**

Marek said: **that is what it is.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I can print that. A box with nothing in it is a thing I can print and I am telling you that I have been waiting four weeks for you to give me something I can print and you have just given me it.**

Marek said: **do not print it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I was not going to. I wanted to see what your face did.**

---

**And then she walked about nine minutes with him from that service road and did not mention the card once for the first half, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he waited until she got there.**

Marek said: **what goes over the top of it.**

Talia said: **nothing, and you have spent a fortnight finding that out from people who would have told you in nine seconds if you had asked them.**

Marek said: **I could not ask them.**

Talia said: **no. And you could ask me, and I would give you the name I have had ready for about nine days, and it would be about four sounds somebody has to say out loud and it would not be any use to anybody here.**

Marek said: **I have not asked you.**

Talia said: **you have not, and I have now said that I have it for the second time, and I am still not going to say it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man ruled a line under the top of a card and left the space above it empty and told a printer and a woman and about nine people in a room that it is not an answer, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and thirty-first night.**

---

**The four jobs that Tuesday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that cupboard, and they came to thirty-six pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a holder that somebody had painted over without taking it off.

He took it out, cleaned it and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**Paint on a holder is paint somebody has put over a problem. Holders in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a ceiling about four of them have repainted round without taking anything down.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in directly under a tap with no back protection.

He moved it up out of the splash zone and proved it from the gate.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A socket under a tap is a socket that gets wet on a Tuesday. Sockets in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a yard somebody has had to cover with a bin bag.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a hob element that tripped the whole board.

He changed the element and proved the cooker with the oven on.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**One element tripping a board is a cooker somebody has had to stop using because of a thing about four inches across. Cookers in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a hob a woman has been cooking one ring at a time on.**"

A light in that cupboard had been put in on a flex pinched by the door every time it shut.

He rerouted it and proved the light from outside the door.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A flex pinched by a cupboard door is a flex that has been pinched about four thousand times. Flexes in cupboards are like that, and one of them has been a door about four of them have had to shut gently.**"

**Thirty-six pounds is the sum of those four on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Tuesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder on the back of that shelf did not come off it, and this file sets no figure of any kind against the page that binder holds.**
