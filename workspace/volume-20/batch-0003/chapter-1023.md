# Chapter 1023 — Sentences That Named The Room

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the woman of about fifty-four did not come up that Wednesday, and Marek noticed at about seven and did not say anything about it to anybody.**

---

That Wednesday took nine callers through that shop and nine names were written against that day's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and ninety-two days, two hundred and seventy weeks and two days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was sixty-five days old and was in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was fifty-one days old, and the four words were forty-two days old.**

---

**He had put a chair back for her at about ten past six without deciding to, and about four people in that room have said since that it stood there empty for about two hours and that nobody sat in it and that nobody moved it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **she said she would come back when you stopped saying yes and then no.**

Marek said: **she did.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **you said yes to a man on a stair and no to the woman who keeps this room, on the same Tuesday, within about four hours of each other, and you have not worked out which of those two was the mistake yet, and neither have I.**

Marek said: **I have worked out which one was the mistake.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **then say it in a sentence and I will carry it up that stair for you.**

Marek said: **no.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-seven came up at about half past eight and put his coat over the back of the empty chair and would not sit down in it, and about four people in that room have said since that he stood up again inside a minute.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **Tuesday. Eleven people went up that stair. Four of them got asked by me. Two said yes and went up and two said no and went up anyway.**

Marek said: **you asked them the three things.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I asked them the three things and I asked them in about four minutes and I wrote nothing and I put no name to it, and one of them asked me who I was working for and I said nobody, and she went up.**

Marek said: **she went up having been asked by a stranger and she still went up.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **that is the part I cannot get past either.**

---

**And then Marek said the first of the four sentences, and it was the wrong one, and about four people in that room have said since that he knew it was the wrong one while he was in the middle of it.**

Marek said: **I am not having this done at the bottom of that stair.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **you are not having it done at the bottom of your stair. I am doing it at the bottom of it and at the doors onto that road and in a car park about nine minutes away, and if you want to complain about all three of those you will have to be a bigger man than me.**

Marek said: **that is not what I meant.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **it is not what you said.**

---

**And then he said the second one, and it was to a woman of about thirty-four who had come up that Wednesday for the first time since she had stopped going into the queue, and about four people in that room have said since that she had been asked three things on a landing by the same man and had answered two of them.**

Marek said: **you have been asked them already and you have come up anyway, and I do not know what you want from this room.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I know what I want from it. I want to be asked again in a room where nine people can hear me not answer.**

Marek said: **you did not answer two of them on the landing.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **no. I went up that stair and now somebody can see me not answering, which is a different thing from doing it on a landing on my own where nothing happened and it might as well not have.**

---

**And then he said the third one, and it was the worst of the four, and about four people in that room have said since that the man of about thirty-three put his hand over his own mouth while it was being said.**

Marek said: **you are doing out there the thing that happens in here, and I cannot have it done twice.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **say what happens in here.**

Marek said: **no.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have been one of three people holding a thing for about a month and I have said four times that I will not say which of us has it wrong, and I am telling you now that you have just said a sentence about that room in front of eight people and that you have not described one thing in it. If it is not a room where something happens then a landing is the same as a bench and your man has said the whole of it, and if it is a room where something happens then he is doing it in the wrong place. Say which.**

---

**And then he started the fourth one and stopped about a second into it, and about four people in that room have said since that nothing in this city has ever cost him as much as that second did.**

Marek said: **the reason we do it in here and not at a door is—**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **go on.**

Marek said: **it is not a reason I can put in a sentence without saying what the room is, and if I say what the room is then I have said it, and I have not said it in about four weeks and I am not starting on a Wednesday in front of nine people.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **then do not start. I will stand on that stair on Friday on my own and you can come and stand there or you can shut that door, and one of those two is what you have got and I have not come up here to take it off you.**

---

**And then he looked at the wall behind the chair the woman of about fifty-four had left standing empty, and did the sum with his hands behind him where nine people could see him doing it and did not say one word of it out loud, and one person in that room has since asked him about that wall and he said he had not been doing anything.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man stood at the bottom of a stair and a man said four sentences in a room and three people refused all four, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and fourth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Wednesday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and a landing, and they came to thirty-three pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in with a holder bent out of shape where a bulb had been forced into it.

He took the holder out, put a new one in and proved the lamp.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A holder bent by somebody's thumb is a holder that has already dropped out once. Holders in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a ceiling that about four of them have put a chair ladder under.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in outdoors under an open-sided shelter.

He took it out, made a proper enclosure and proved it from the gate.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A socket under an open shelter is a socket that is wet by Thursday. Sockets in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a bench a man has had to keep a bag over for four years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with its flex joined to the fixed wiring with two pins and about three inches of it.

He took the pins out, made a proper joint and proved the cooker.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A cooker held on by two pins and three inches is a cooker somebody has repaired in about nine minutes. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker a woman has had to watch for about four years.**"

A light on that landing had been put in with a switch that worked and a lamp that did not.

He found the break in the flex, made it up properly and proved the light from the door.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A switch that works and a lamp that does not is a landing where people stand in the dark working out which of the two is broken. Landings in that block are like it, and one of them has been a front door that about four of them have come home up to.**"

**Thirty-three pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**By half past six on that Wednesday the last of those four rooms was shut, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the ring binder was still on the shelf at the back of that room, and nothing on this page sets a figure against the page it holds.**
