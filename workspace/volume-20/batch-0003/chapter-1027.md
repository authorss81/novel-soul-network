# Chapter 1027 — The Reason He Gave Was True

**Two people were asked three things at that bench in front of everybody on that Wednesday and answered all three, and one of them had been asked on a landing four days ago and changed her answer, and the rest of that day was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road.**

---

That Wednesday took six callers through that shop and six names were written against that day's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and ninety-nine days, two hundred and seventy-one weeks and two days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was seventy-two days old and was in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was fifty-eight days old, and the four words were forty-nine days old.**

---

**The woman of about twenty-nine had said on Friday that she could not change her mind on a landing and could change it in a room, and about four people in that room have said since that she changed the second of the three things out loud in front of everybody and that about four of them did not notice and two of them did.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **I told you what I would do and I have done it. That one was different on Friday and this is what it is now, in front of all of you, and I would like it noticed that it changed and I changed it here.**

Marek said: **noticed.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **I am not going to be asked about it again this week. Somebody promised me once.**

Marek said: **you were promised once by her and I have been holding to it since Monday and you were promised twice by nobody.**

---

**And then at about a quarter to eight the man of about thirty-three came up and did not sit down, and about four people in that room have said since that he stood for about four minutes before he said anything and that Marek let him stand there.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **you have now done it twice and got three answers out of one of them and two out of the other, and I am going to let that sit there for about four minutes and then I am going to ask you about the Tuesday.**

Marek said: **ask.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **why did you refuse her.**

---

**And then Marek gave her the reason again, in about nine words, in front of about nine people, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the same nine words he had given her on the Tuesday and not one word different.**

Marek said: **at a door a person can walk away, and here they cannot.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **that is why you are glad, and it is not why you said no.**

Marek said: **it is the reason I said.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **it is the reason you were glad to have, because you had it ready and it sounded like a reason and it did not make you explain anything about that room. She came up on a Tuesday having thought about it for about two days and she asked for one thing and you gave her a sentence that closes the subject. I have watched men do that for about nine years and I have never once watched one of them be thanked for it afterwards.**

---

**And then Marek said the true thing, and it took about nine seconds, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody said anything at all when he said it.**

Marek said: **she had me. And I knew she had me on the Tuesday before I had finished saying no, and if I had said yes then at about seven in the evening on a Tuesday I would have had to go up that stair on the Wednesday and ask everybody three things out loud, and I have been at that bench for about four weeks and I did not want that. I did not say no because she was wrong. I said no because I did not want to do the other thing.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **louder.**

Marek said: **no.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **not louder. In about nine minutes, when she comes up, in front of these people. She has not been asked since Monday and she was promised once and you have been the one who broke it by saying you would not ask her again in about six days.**

Marek said: **she made a condition and I have kept it.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **you have kept the part that was convenient. You told her she would be asked again this week and then you told a man on a stair this week and not her, and I was in the room when you did it and I am telling you that is not the same as keeping a condition.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-four came up at about half past eight and about four people in that room have said since that she came in, saw about nine faces, and sat down without asking whether she was going to be asked anything.**

Marek said: **you are not being asked anything this evening.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I did not come up to be asked anything. I came up because somebody has been saying a thing in this room for about four minutes and I could hear it from the bottom of that stair.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **he has told nine people that he said no to you because he did not want to ask them three things out loud, and he said it in about nine seconds, and I am not the one who said it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **nine seconds.**

Marek said: **about nine seconds.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then he has at least been quick about it. I took about two days.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man said out loud in about nine seconds that he had refused a woman because he did not want to do the other thing, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and eleventh night.**

---

**The four jobs that Wednesday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and a hallway, and they came to thirty-six pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in under a window with the earth taken off the frames.

He brought the earth up to that box and proved the socket with a kettle.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**An earth off window frames is an earth that stops being an earth the day a frame swells. Sockets in that shop are like it, and one of them has been a window a person has had to shut in the wind.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in with a ceiling rose that had come away from the ceiling.

He made a new box in, fixed the rose to it and proved the lamp.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A rose that has come away is a rose that has been hanging on two screws for about four years. Lamps in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a ceiling that about four of them have put a chair under to reach it.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the earth lead cut short at the back.

He re-terminated it, bonded the case and proved the cooker.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A cooker with its earth cut short is a cooker somebody has already tried to fix. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a wall that about four of them have had a man look at twice.**"

A light in that hallway had two switches at the bottom and none at the top.

He took one out, put it at the top and proved the light from the landing.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A switch at the bottom and none at the top is a stair somebody has to feel their way down. Lights in that hallway are like it, and one of them has been a landing that about four of them have come up in the dark for four years.**"

**Thirty-six pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**From half past six on that Wednesday the fourth of those four rooms was shut, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder stayed on the back of that shelf, and this page prints no figure against the page that binder holds.**
