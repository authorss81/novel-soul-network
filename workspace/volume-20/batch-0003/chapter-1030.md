# Chapter 1030 — There Was Nothing To Print

**A man of about thirty-seven stopped working at the bottom of that stair on the Friday and came up on that Saturday morning to say so, and a Saturday was a counter from about ten and then four converted units off that service road, and Marek was at that counter when he came in.**

---

That Saturday took seven callers through that shop and seven names were written against that day's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let away at about half past five, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and two days, two hundred and seventy-one weeks and five days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was seventy-five days old and was in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was sixty-one days old and its whereabouts are not known, and the four words were fifty-two days old.**

---

**He came up at about half past eleven with his hands in his pockets and about four people in that room have said since that he did not bring a card and that Marek looked at his pockets twice and that neither of them mentioned it.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I stopped at nine o'clock on the Friday. Nine days is nine days and I am not going to be a man who does six.**

Marek said: **how many.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **about nine said no at the landing and about eleven said yes and two of the yeses said a different thing in here than they said to me, and I have written none of it down and I am not going to remember most of it.**

Marek said: **you are not going to remember most of it.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I am going to remember it for about four days and then it goes and that is the deal I would give anybody else.**

---

**And then he said the thing he had come up to say, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it standing up and that four people stopped what they were doing.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I am not going to be able to stop. I have been thinking about it for about nine minutes since Friday and I am not going to be able to stand at the bottom of that stair or a door or a car park or anywhere for the rest of this and not ask somebody three things first. I am going to do it in about a fortnight somewhere else where there is no room at the top of it at all, and I am telling you before you hear it from a person on a road.**

Marek said: **I am not going to ask you not to.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I know you are not. That is why I told you. If I had not told you then in about a month you would have had it from a stranger and it would have been the wrong shape.**

---

**And then Marek said the arithmetic of that week out loud to that counter, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he said it to the till again and that all three of them looked up.**

Marek said: **about four people came up that stair this week who had not come up the week before, and about four people did not come up this week who came up the week before, and I can tell you the four and I cannot tell you why they did not come, and I have been the reason one of them did not come and I know that because I said no to a person on Tuesday.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not said that to me.**

Marek said: **you have heard me say it to a till about four times this month.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I know. In this room you have not. Those are not the same and you know they are not the same, and I am not going to let you get away with it by telling a till.**

---

**And then at about half past two he walked about nine minutes into a second district to a building that prints things, and about four people in that building have said since that he was let in without knocking and stood in the doorway and did not go further in.**

Marek said: **I want a card printed.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **say the four things.**

Marek said: **whose words. Mine.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **in whose hand. Yours.**

Marek said: **Yes.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **under what date.**

Marek said: **I do not want a date on it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then I will not put one. I have printed four things for you and I have asked you that three times and you have never once given me a date and I have never once invented one, and that is not because I am careful, it is because a thing with a made-up date on it is the same object as a thing with a false one on it.**

---

**And then the fourth question, and about four people in that building have said since that the man of about sixty-one did not ask it in the same voice as the other three and that Marek answered it in about nine words.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **and who stands behind it.**

Marek said: **I do. And I have said that out loud in about nine rooms in about four weeks and none of those rooms has a piece of paper in it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is the first time you have said it to me and I have had four answers in three years and three of them were about somebody else.**

---

**And then he asked the one that is not on his list, and about four people in that building have said since that it is the question he asks last every time and that Marek had not got to it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **what is it for.**

Marek said: **it tells anybody who comes up that stair that three things get asked at that bench, out loud, in front of everybody else in the room.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **and what goes over the top of it.**

Marek said: **a heading.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then I have nothing to print. A notice with no heading over it is not a notice, it is a sheet with some words on it, and about four people a week will put that sheet on a wall and it will say three things get asked and it will not say what the three things are for, and you have spent about four weeks taking sheets off a wall.**

---

**And then Marek said the last thing of that Saturday out loud in that doorway, and about four people in that building have said since that he said it to the man of about sixty-one and not to the room.**

Marek said: **I have not got a heading and I have not got anybody to give me one and I have been asked four times in about four weeks and every one of the four of them wanted it to be about somebody else.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then bring me a heading and I will print it. Bring me nothing and I will print nothing, and a drawer is not a promise and it is not a refusal either, and you have known that since the first time you came in here.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man stopped at the bottom of a stair and told somebody he was going to start again somewhere with no room at the top of it, and a printer was asked for a card and had nothing to print, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and fourteenth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Saturday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and an outside wall, and they came to twenty-six pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in with a flex that had been taped where it passed a hot pipe.

He took the lamp down, re-routed it away and proved it.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**Flex taped to a hot pipe is flex that has been warm for about four years. Lamps in that shop are like it, and one of them has been a ceiling about four of them have looked up and wondered why the bulb goes.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate that had come away from the brick.

He took it off, re-bedded it and proved the socket.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A plate that has come away is a plate that has been off that wall already. Sockets in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a wall about four of them have had a plug fall out of.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a socket above it that had no earth.

He took the earth up to the box, made off the ends and proved that socket from the wall.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A socket over a cooker with no earth is a socket that has been wet for about four years. Sockets in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a worktop a woman has had to keep the plug out of.**"

An outside light on that wall had been put in on a bracket that had been welded to a fence which had since moved.

He unhooked it, put a proper wall fixing in and proved the light.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A light welded to a fence is a light that belongs to a fence, and the fence has moved. Lights on that wall are like it, and one of them has been a yard about four of them have come home up to.**"

**Twenty-six pounds is the sum of those four on that Saturday, exact.**
