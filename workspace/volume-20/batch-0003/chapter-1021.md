# Chapter 1021 — The Man Who Asked For A Doorway

**A Sunday was a counter from about ten until about two and the shutter came down early because a Sunday always does, and a man of about thirty-seven came in at about eleven with his coat already open and did not have a lamp with him and did not say why.**

---

That Sunday took five callers through that shop between about ten and about two and five names went onto that day's line, and the last of them was let away at about half past one, and the shutter came down at about two.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and eighty-nine days, two hundred and sixty-nine weeks and six days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was sixty-two days old and was in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was forty-eight days old and had never been on that wall at all, and the four words were thirty-nine days old.**

---

**He stood at the customer side of that counter for about four minutes before he said anything, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he read the board up on two nails while he was doing it and did not go near it.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I am not here about a lamp.**

Marek said: **you are not.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I have a van six nights a week and I am here on a Sunday because I am not driving.**

Marek said: **then you have about an hour.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I have about two, and I want about nine of them, and I cannot have two of them on a Sunday.**

---

**And then he said what he had come for, and he said all of it before Marek had put the till down, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that it was the longest single thing anybody had said at that counter without stopping.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I want the three things asked at the door. Before a person comes up. Not here. At the door, in advance, by somebody, over about nine days, and then they come up or they do not come up, and either one is fine because they have been asked already.**

Marek said: **asked by whom.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **by me. I have done it before.**

Marek said: **done what.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **asked people three things before they went into a room. I have done it about twice and I did it about nine days the second time and I did it in a stairwell and a doorway and a corridor and once on a loading bay outside a building where nobody could hear over the fans.**

---

**And then he told Marek what it had been like from the inside of it, and he told it without being asked and about four people who come to that shop have said since that Marek stopped tidying the shelf behind the counter and did not start again.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I was one of about eleven people once, before I ever asked anybody anything. Somebody asked me three things over about nine days, in advance, in a different room in a different part of this city, and I want to be exact with you about how that went and it went well.**

Marek said: **you are not describing this room.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **no. I am telling you why I know what I am asking for. I have been one of the eleven. It was the best asked week of my life and I have not had a week like it since and I am about thirty-seven and I do not say that often.**

---

**And then Marek said what happens here instead, and he said it slowly and he checked it twice, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he counted them on his fingers on the counter without looking at them.**

Marek said: **here the three things are asked at the bench. In the room. Out loud, one at a time, to the person, in front of everybody else who is in it. Nobody is asked anything before they come up. Nobody has ever been asked anything before they come up.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **in front of everybody else.**

Marek said: **in front of everybody else.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **and if a person cannot answer one of them.**

Marek said: **then they do not answer it and they stay.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-seven said the thing that this whole week turned on, and he said it flatly and without any weight on it at all, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that it was the only thing he said that Marek wrote nothing down about.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **It worked. I want that on your counter before I say anything else. Where I did it, it worked. Eleven people went into that room and every one of them knew what was going to be asked of them and eleven people have not walked in off a street since, and I am one of the eleven, and that is not a thing I would give back.**

Marek said: **you are asking me to take that away from about four people a week.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I am asking you to give it to them.**

---

**And then he said what he would do, and he said it in a voice Marek has described since as a man reading out a delivery list.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I will do it myself. I will not need anybody in this shop. I will take a card down on a Monday and I will stand at the bottom of that stair on a Tuesday and I will ask anybody who comes up those stairs three things, and I will write nothing down, and I will not put a name to anything, and at the end of about nine days I will stop and I will come and tell you how many said no and how many said yes and you can do what you want with that.**

Marek said: **somebody has to stand there.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I will stand there six nights a week for about nine days and I will lose about eleven hundred pounds and I have made my arrangements.**

---

**And then Marek asked him the one that was not about the doorway, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that the man of about thirty-seven gave him a different answer than he had expected to give.**

Marek said: **why.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **because I have been in a room that nobody asked me anything in first and I did not know what was wrong with it, and I have been in a room where a person could not answer and everybody went quiet, and I would rather be asked three things at a door by a man with a clipboard than find out which one I am.**

Marek said: **that is not a reason to ask for a doorway. That is a reason to want to be asked.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I know the difference. I am asking anyway and you can say no and I will come back on a Tuesday and ask you again.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man of about thirty-seven offered to lose about eleven hundred pounds standing at the bottom of a stair for about nine days, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and first night.**

---

**The four jobs that Sunday went into a hall, a yard, a kitchen and a stair, and they came to twenty-one pounds, exact.**

A bell in that hall had its transformer screwed to the underside of the top tread.

He came off it, ran it up the wall beside the banister and proved the bell from the top landing.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A transformer under a tread is a bell that rings when somebody goes upstairs. Bells in that hall are like it, and one of them has been a front door that about four of them have been answered at for four years.**"

A lamp in that yard had been wired from a junction box standing on the ground in a puddle.

He took the box up onto the wall, made off the ends and proved the lamp from the gate.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A box standing in a puddle is a box that has been wet for four years. Lamps in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate somebody has had to keep a ladder out of for about four years.**"

A socket in that kitchen had been put in under a window with the earth taken off a gas pipe.

He found the pipe, took the earth up to the box and proved the socket with a kettle.

"**Four pounds,**" he said. "**An earth off a gas pipe is an earth that moves when the pipe moves. Sockets in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a window a woman has had a kettle stood on for four years.**"

A stair light had been put in with a holder screwed to the string and nothing at all behind it.

He earthed it off the box, put a proper holder in and proved the light from the bottom.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A light with a holder and no earth is a light somebody holds on to in the dark. Lights on stairs are like that, and one of them has been a stair a man has come down with a torch in his hand for four years.**"

**Twenty-one pounds is the sum of those four on that Sunday, exact.**

---

**That Sunday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past one, and behind that door stood a woman of about thirty who was not asked anything and said nothing at all, and the ring binder stayed where it was on the back of that shelf, and not one figure for the page it holds is written anywhere on this one.**
