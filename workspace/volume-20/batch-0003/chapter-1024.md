# Chapter 1024 — The Yes She Could Not Take Back

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and Marek did the day's work first and went up that stair at about a quarter to seven and stood at the bottom of it for about two hours.**

---

That Friday took seven callers through that shop and seven names were written against that day's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and ninety-four days, two hundred and seventy weeks and four days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was sixty-seven days old and was in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was fifty-three days old, and the four words were forty-four days old.**

---

**He had never stood at the bottom of that stair before and about four people in that room have said since that he did it badly, twice, once on each foot, and that he kept his hands in his pockets as though he had been caught.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **you are standing in the middle of it.**

Marek said: **I am standing out of the way of it.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **you are a man stood at the bottom of a stair. That is not out of the way of anything. People will look at you first and me second, and if you want to be out of the way of it you have to come at eight when there is nobody about.**

Marek said: **then come at eight.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **no. I have started at half past six and I am not restarting the count on a Friday because a man in a coat has turned up and wants to watch.**

---

**And then a woman of about twenty-nine came up that stair at about quarter past seven having been asked on the second landing four days ago, and about four people in that room have said since that Marek did not know she was there until she was three steps from the top.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **I said yes to him.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **you did. On Wednesday. About four minutes. I did not write it down.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **I know you did not write it down. That is the trouble. You asked me three things on a landing and I gave you three answers and you carried none of them anywhere and now there is a version of me in your head that is not in any of those four people up there.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I have got nothing.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **you have got a yes. You have got a yes at the bottom of a stair and I cannot now come up here and say no without it being a thing that happened, and I want to be a person who changes her mind.**

---

**And then Marek said the thing he had come down that stair to find out, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it to the man of about thirty-seven and not to the woman.**

Marek said: **is that what it is.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **is what what is.**

Marek said: **You do not lose anybody. Nobody is stopped at the door. Nobody is kept out. And in about four days I have watched you do the one thing I have never seen this room do, which is make it expensive to change your mind, and you did it without raising your voice or writing a word down.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I do not think about that.**

Marek said: **no. I do not think you do.**

---

**And then she went up, and she went up in about nine seconds, and about four people in that room have said since that she went up the way a person goes up a stair they have been up before.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **here I can say a different thing. That is all I wanted. I wanted to be somewhere I could say a different thing after I had already said the first one.**

Marek said: **you can say it up there.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **and if I say nothing up there then I have not said a different thing, I have only said it in a room where eight people heard the first one and nobody heard the second.**

Marek said: **then say it twice.**

The woman of about twenty-nine said: **you cannot ask a person to say it twice. They will do it once.**

---

**And then at about half past seven the man of about thirty-seven asked Marek whether he wanted to stand there on the Monday as well, and about four people in that room have said since that Marek said no and then said yes and then said no again inside about four seconds.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **Monday.**

Marek said: **no.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **you are stood here.**

Marek said: **I have come once and I have seen it and I am not coming back for the watching of it.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **then what will you do with it.**

Marek said: **I do not know. I have been wrong about the stair. I said on Tuesday that a door is where a person can walk away and I meant it and it is not the whole of what a door is, and a stair is where a person has already answered, and I have not got the sentence for either of those and I have had about four days to get it.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **you do not need the sentence. You need to decide whether you want me on that stair.**

Marek said: **I have not got to that.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man stood at the bottom of a stair for about two hours and found out what he had been wrong about, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and sixth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Friday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and an outside wall, and they came to forty-four pounds, exact.**

A light in that shop had been put in with a fuse that was a piece of copper wire bent round a nail.

He took it out, put a proper fuse in and proved the light.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A fuse made of bent wire is a fire waiting for a morning. Fuses in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a wall that about four of them have had a smell in it and blamed the food.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a lead that had been buried under paving twice.

He dug both lengths out, re-routed it and proved the socket.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A lead buried under paving is a lead that gets cut by whoever puts a fence in next. Sockets in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a post a man has had to keep digging at for four years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had its outlet box fitted behind the splashback with the plasterboard cut round it.

He took the box out, cut a proper hole, refit it and proved the cooker.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A box fitted behind a splashback is a box that has to be cut round to get at it. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a wall a woman has had to take a tile off to reach.**"

An outside light on that wall had been put in on a flex run through a window frame.

He took it through the frame properly and proved the light from the yard.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A flex through a window frame is a flex the window has already been known to shut on. Lights on that wall are like it, and one of them has been a yard a man has had to shout into for four years.**"

**Forty-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Friday, exact.**

---

**That Friday had the fourth of those four rooms shut by half past six with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the back of that shelf was not touched, and there is no figure for what the binder holds written in this file.**
