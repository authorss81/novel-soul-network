# Chapter 1022 — She Asked For The Bench

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the woman of about fifty-four was in that room before he was and had been in it about forty minutes saying nothing to anybody.**

---

That Tuesday took eight callers through that shop and eight names were written against that day's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and ninety-one days, two hundred and seventy weeks and one day. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was sixty-four days old and was in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was fifty days old, and the four words were forty-one days old and had been said to about four people for about six weeks.**

---

**She had come up the stair because she had meant to and she had come up early, and about four people in that room have said since that she was the only person in it who had not come about a lamp or a knee or a bus.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have been thinking about this since Sunday night and I have got it down to one thing and I am going to say it once and then I am going to sit down.**

Marek said: **say it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **ask the three things at the bench. In this room. Out loud, one at a time, in front of everybody else who is in here. Every time, to everybody, and not one person in this room walked in here having been asked anything at all.**

Marek said: **that is what happens.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no. It is not. What happens is that one person at a time gets taken to the other side of that room and gets asked three things and everybody else sits here and waits and does not hear it. I have been on both sides of that.**

---

**And then she gave him the reason, and she gave it without stopping, and about four people in that room have said since that Marek sat down on the bench beside the door while she said it, which he had not done in that room before.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have been asked three things at a door about nine times in my life and not one of those nine people was asked anything a second time. They came away and they were polite about it and they went off and I never saw any of them again. If you ask me here, in front of these people, then it has been asked in front of witnesses and a person cannot go off and tell two versions of it, because there are nine other people who heard it.**

Marek said: **they cannot tell any version of it because the person asked it says yes or no and nothing else.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you are not listening to me. I am not asking for an answer. I am asking for a room where three things get said out loud where people can hear them.**

---

**And then Marek refused her, and it took nine words, and he said them to her face and he did not soften them afterwards, and about four people in that room have said since that two of them stopped talking when he said them.**

Marek said: **no. At a door a person can walk away.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and here they cannot.**

Marek said: **here they cannot walk away either and I am not going to be the reason a person cannot walk away.**

---

**And then he said the whole of it out loud, and he said it to her and not to the till, and it took about nine seconds.**

Marek said: **I have had about nine seconds just now and I used all of them, and I want you to know that I did not think about you in them. I thought about the stair.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **the stair.**

Marek said: **yes. I thought about a person standing at the bottom of that stair with a list, and I did not think once about the woman who keeps this room, and that is what I have just done and I am telling you it instead of letting you find it.**

---

**And then she said what she would do about it, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it sitting down and did not stand up again.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have been refused once and I am not going to be refused twice by the same man on the same thing in the same six months.**

Marek said: **you can come back on Wednesday.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I will come back when you have stopped saying yes and then no in the same breath.**

---

**And then at about half past seven four people came up that stair in about nine minutes who had all been asked three things at the bottom of it by a man they had never seen before, and about four people in that room have said since that the woman of about fifty-four did not look up when they came in and that she went on with what she was doing.**

Marek said: **that was you. The one on Saturday said a man with a card stopped her on the second landing.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **yes. And he was polite about it, and he wrote nothing down, and he asked me what I would share and what I would keep and who could stop me. Which is the same three things. In a landing. In about four minutes.**

Marek said: **did he get an answer.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **he did not get an answer from me and he said he was glad I had not given him one, and I did not know what to do with a man who is glad.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a woman of about fifty-four was refused in nine words at a bench she had wanted to use, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and third night.**

---

**The four jobs that Tuesday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and a corridor, and they came to thirty-nine pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in on a round plastic box screwed to the timber behind the plasterboard.

He took the box out, chased a proper box into the masonry and proved the socket.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A round box behind plasterboard is a box nobody can get at when a wire drops out. Sockets in that shop are like it, and one of them has been a bench a woman has had to keep a torch on for four years.**"

A light in that yard had been put in on a lead going through the middle of a joint.

He took the lead off the joint, made it up properly and proved the light from the gate.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A lead through the middle of a joint is a joint that opens the day somebody pulls the cable. Lights in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a fence a man has had to get a ladder over for about four years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had its earth taken from the incoming water pipe.

He took the earth off the pipe, put it on the box and proved the cooker with the oven on.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**An earth off a water pipe is an earth that is off the day somebody turns that tap. Cookers in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a cooker a woman has been afraid of for four years.**"

A lamp in that corridor had been put in with the switch in the wrong room and a flex run through the ceiling.

He took the flex out, put a switch in the room the lamp is in and proved it from the door.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp you have to walk out of a room to put off is a lamp somebody leaves on. Lamps in that corridor are like it, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have come home up in the dark.**"

**Thirty-nine pounds is the sum of those four on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**The fourth of those four rooms was shut from half past six on that Tuesday, and a woman of about thirty was behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf did not come off it, and this file prints no figure of any kind against what the binder holds.**
