# Chapter 1026 — He Did Not Refuse Her Again

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and the woman of about fifty-four came up that Monday on her own time and sat down in the chair that had stood empty for six days and did not take her coat off.**

---

That Monday took eight callers through that shop and eight names were written against that day's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and ninety-seven days, two hundred and seventy-one weeks to the day. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was seventy days old and was in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was fifty-six days old, and the four words were forty-seven days old.**

---

**She had said she would come back when he stopped saying yes and then no, and about four people in that room have said since that he had said nothing at all for about nine seconds before he answered her, which is longer than it takes him to answer anybody.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **Wednesday.**

Marek said: **I know.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and it is Monday.**

Marek said: **I am not going to refuse you again.**

---

**And then she asked him why, and about four people in that room have said since that he answered her in about nine words and that it was the shortest answer he had given anybody in about four weeks.**

Marek said: **because a stranger is asking them first and I cannot be the second.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is not a reason. That is a circumstance.**

Marek said: **it is the only one I have got. I have had about six days to get a better one and this is the sentence that came out and I am not going to improve it for you.**

---

**And then she asked him what he was going to do instead, and about four people in that room have said since that she asked it the way a person asks a thing they have already decided they are going to refuse.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **what will you do.**

Marek said: **ask them here. At that bench. Out loud, one at a time, in front of everybody else in this room, to everybody who comes up, and let the room hear it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **everybody.**

Marek said: **everybody who comes up that stair.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **including me.**

Marek said: **including you, and in about nine minutes, and you can say no to the whole thing now and I will not ask you again today.**

---

**And then she said yes to it and asked for one thing back, and about four people in that room have said since that the thing she asked for was that nobody be asked twice.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **once. Whoever comes up that stair, they get asked once, and then it is finished, and nobody in this room goes up to a person afterwards and asks them again on their own.**

Marek said: **I could not stop nine people asking nine things after this.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no. And you can stop one man in a shop asking about a lamp twice and you have never once tried, and I have watched you do it about nine times in about four weeks and not one of those nine went out of this counter worse than it came in.**

Marek said: **that is a different thing.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is the same thing, and it is the only condition and I am not raising it twice.**

---

**And then at about ten past seven he asked her the three things at that bench in front of about nine people, and about four people in that room have said since that it took about four minutes and that two of the nine left and came back and that nobody spoke while the first two were being asked.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is the first thing.**

Marek said: **I know.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no, that is the first time anybody has said that out loud in here and you have got about nine seconds before this becomes a thing that happens.**

Marek said: **that is the arrangement.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **the second thing, then.**

---

**And then she did not answer the third one, and about four people in that room have said since that she sat in the chair she had not moved from for about nine seconds and then said she was not going to, and that nobody said anything at all when she said it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I am not going to answer the third thing in this room.**

Marek said: **you do not have to.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no. I want it recorded that I have not answered it and not because I could not think of one. I have got one. I have had about nine of them for about four weeks and I have said every one of them in a doorway to about nine people who did not write anything down, and there is one man in a second district who has told me that a thing you say in a doorway is not a record, and he is not wrong, and that is exactly why I am not saying it here either.**

Marek said: **because it is a record.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **because it becomes a record the moment nine people hear it, and I have spent about four weeks not making records.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-seven came up at about half past seven and heard about it from about four people before anybody spoke to him, and about four people in that room have said since that he stood at the bottom of the stair for a moment before he came all the way up.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **you have asked one person in front of nine people and got less out of her than I get in four minutes on a landing.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **and you are going to do it again on Wednesday.**

Marek said: **it is not refused again.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **by whom.**

Marek said: **by me. And it is not refused again in about six days, and after that you and I will be two men doing it in the same week and neither of us will know what the other one did.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and a woman who had kept a room for about nine years was asked three things in it out loud and answered two, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and ninth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Monday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and a garage, and they came to forty-one pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in with a holder that had been painted round and had the lamp hanging out of plumb.

He took the lamp down, re-hung it square and proved it.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp out of plumb is a lamp that has been pulled and put back by whoever was nearest. Lamps in that shop are like it, and one of them has been a ceiling that about four of them have put a chair under.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on an extension lead instead of a socket.

He put a socket in and took the lead away.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A lead instead of a socket is a socket somebody has had to keep one end of a lead in. Sockets in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a wall that about four of them have run a lead to.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a wall that had no earth bar in it.

He fitted one, bonded the cases and proved the cooker.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A cooker on a wall with no earth bar is a cooker waiting for the first person to touch the case. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to check the plug on with a meter.**"

A light in that garage had been put in with a switch that was outside the door.

He moved it inside and proved it from the workbench.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A switch outside a garage door is a switch that is out of reach in the dark. Lights in that garage are like it, and one of them has been a doorway that about four of them have felt for a wall.**"

**Forty-one pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**

---

**That Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms at about half past six, and there was a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the back of that shelf did not come off it, and no figure for the page it holds is printed in this file.**
