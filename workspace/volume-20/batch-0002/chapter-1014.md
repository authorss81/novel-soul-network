# Chapter 1014 — Nobody Asked Her Anything Either

**A Sunday was a counter from about ten and then that shutter coming down at about two, and a man went up about nine minutes on foot to a first floor off that line at about half past one, because a woman who had been at one desk for years had told him in a shop on Friday that she would be in that room on a Sunday and not at her desk.**

---

Five callers came through that shop on that Sunday between about ten and about one, and five names went onto that day's line, and the last of them was let away at about a quarter to one, and that shutter came down at about two and not at about ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and seventy-five days, two hundred and sixty-seven weeks and six days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was forty-eight days old and had been in a bag under a bench since the Thursday before last, the second printing was thirty-four days old, and the four words were twenty-five days old and had been in about four mouths the whole of that time.**

---

**She was already there when he came up, and about four people in that room have said since that she was on a chair against the wall with her coat still on, which nobody in that room does, and that nobody looked at her twice while he was coming up the stair behind her.**

Marek said: **you said a Sunday.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I said a Sunday. I do not come here on a weekday and I cannot be at that desk on a Sunday, and I am not here about a lamp and I have never once been here about a lamp.**

Marek said: **I know that. I am not asking you about a lamp.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **then ask me whatever it is in about four minutes, because I am going to be here until this room shuts and I have not done that in about nine years and I want to see whether it is a thing I have been missing.**

---

**And then nobody asked her anything for about forty minutes, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the longest anybody has gone without saying anything to a person who came in.**

**She did not ask what the three things were. She did not ask who kept the book. She did not ask whether there was anything to be done. Two of the four people there were talking about a bus route and one of them was talking about a knee, and she listened to all of it and said about four words in about forty minutes, and two of those words were about the weather.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not asked her anything.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you came up those stairs to ask her something.**

Marek said: **I came up those stairs because she asked me not to come on a weekday.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you have come anyway.**

Marek said: **she asked me to come on a Sunday and there is a difference and it is not mine and it was hers.**

---

**And then at about ten past two he asked her the one question, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the only question asked in about forty minutes and that he asked it at the door on his way out.**

Marek said: **have you said it to anybody.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no.**

Marek said: **not this week, not since Friday.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I have said it to nobody at all since the day I said it, and I have had about four chances, and I want you to understand that I had them and I did not take them, and that is not me being careful.**

Marek said: **no. I have not got that wrong.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **good. Because I did not say it this week deliberately, after Friday, and I have been asked why about four times since Friday by people who do not know what they are asking.**

---

**And then she said the thing that is the whole of her, and it took about nine words, and about four people in that room have said since that two of the four of them stopped talking about the bus route while she said it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **nobody at that desk has ever asked me what I think about anything. That is why I said it.**

Marek said: **and you would still say it if somebody asked you now.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. That is what I have been trying to tell you since Friday and you have not once let me finish a sentence.**

Marek said: **I know. You have not.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **you have been asking me about dates and hands and names since Friday like a man filling in a thing, and I have been sitting here for forty minutes waiting for you to ask me one question that is about me, and you asked me about a Sunday.**

Marek said: **I did not know that was what the Sunday was for.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. It was not. It was forty minutes of not being asked anything, and it cost me a bus and a half day, and I would do it again on a Sunday.**

---

**And then Marek went down that stair and across to that shop and shut that counter at about two as he shut it every Sunday, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he did the day's four jobs first and went up afterwards, which is not how he does it on any other day of the week.**

Nobody thanked anybody on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and a woman who had said four words to about four people sat on a chair against a wall for about forty minutes and was asked one question at the door, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its eighty-seventh night.

---

**Four things went into a hall, a kitchen, a yard and a stair on that Sunday, and they were the only things that went into any of them, and they came to about half what a weekday comes to.**

A bell in a hall had its transformer screwed to a skirting board at the bottom of the stairs.

He took it off, put it on the wall beside the door and proved the bell from the top landing.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A transformer on a skirting is a bell that goes when somebody leans on the wall. Bells in that hall are like that, and one of them has been a front door about four of them have been locked at eleven for four years.**"

A socket in that kitchen had been put in over a sink on an old lead with the earth taken off somewhere under the worktop.

He found the earth, took it up to the box and proved the socket with a kettle.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A socket over a sink with no earth is a socket that is wet and un earthed at the same time. Sockets in that street are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen about four of them have had a kettle stood on for four years.**"

A lamp in that yard had been hung off a length of flex tied round a nail and over the top of a gate post.

He took it down, made a proper fixing on the post and proved the lamp.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp tied to a nail is a lamp that moves every time the gate does. Lamps in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate somebody has had to prop open with about four years.**"

A stair light had been put in on the stairs with no earth at all and a metal holder anybody could stand on.

He earthed it off the box and proved it with an instrument.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A stair light with no earth is a stair somebody holds on to in the dark. Lights on stairs are like it, and one of them has been a stair a woman has come up with a torch in her hand for four years.**"

**Twenty-two pounds is the sum of those four on that Sunday, exact.**

---

**That Sunday the fourth of those four rooms was shut by half past one with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come off it, and no figure for what that binder holds is written anywhere in this file.**
