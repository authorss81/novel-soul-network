# Chapter 1017 — A Drawer Is Not A Promise

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a bus of about half an hour to a second district, where a man told Marek that nothing would be printed that week, and then told him the more useful thing, which was that a drawer cannot be told apart from a refusal by anybody who does not open it.**

---

That Friday took eight callers through that shop and every one of the eight was written against that day's book before they went, and that shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and eighty days, two hundred and sixty-eight weeks and four days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was fifty-three days old and was in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was thirty-nine days old, and the four words were thirty days old and had been thirty days old since before the wall came down.**

---

**He went out at about half past six and got there at about seven, and about four people who use that building have said since that he did not sit down this time and stood at that desk with his coat still on, and that the man of about sixty-one looked at the coat and then at the clock and let him have the whole of it.**

Marek said: **there is no date. She cannot put one on it and she has tried for about four days.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I know. I asked her for the day it was printed and I have had about nine days of nothing, and I have not chased her, because the day you chase a woman at a desk for a date is the day she stops being somebody you can print for.**

Marek said: **then it will not be printed.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **not by me and not this month.**

---

**And then Marek said the thing he had come to say, which was that he had been about to ask him to break his own rule, and the man of about sixty-one stopped him before he got to it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **do not.**

Marek said: **I had not said anything.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **you had your hands on the counter and you had not sat down and you came in without a piece of paper, and the last time you did that you asked me for a date I told you I could not invent. I am not going to have that conversation again.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **do not say yes like that. I have known you about four years and you say yes when you have given up and when you have been told something true, and about nine times out of ten it is the second one, and this is the first.**

---

**And then he opened the drawer, and about four people who use that building have said since that it is the same drawer as last Wednesday and that it has about nine things in it and that he put a hand in without looking and took out a folded paper and put it back in about four seconds.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is about nine things in there. Four of them I have refused. Five of them nobody has ever asked me for and then stopped asking, or died, or went somewhere else, and I cannot tell you which is which and neither can anybody else, including the people who left them.**

Marek said: **that is not the same as holding them.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **no. A drawer is not a promise and it is not a refusal either, and in about four years I have worked out that the only thing worse than printing a thing nobody stood behind is not being able to say afterwards that anybody asked you to.**

Marek said: **so if somebody had asked you, this week, with three of the four answers and no date.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then I would have printed it, and I would have put it in that drawer, and nobody would have known anything until somebody opened it. I am telling you that I am not a wall. I am a man with a press and a drawer, and about nine of the things in that drawer are my decisions and I have made every one of them, and I would not swap one of them for a rule.**

---

**And then he shut the drawer and did not say anything else about it, and about four people who use that building have said since that he then spent about nine minutes talking about nothing else at all, and that Marek stayed for all of them.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I read the thing in your shop's window about the lamp. About the one with the holder.**

Marek said: **about four people have asked me about it in about four months and it is not the worst thing in that window.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **it is the only thing in that window I could read from the road.**

---

**And then at about half past seven on the Friday there were three people at that counter in about half an hour, and each of them asked Marek where to go, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he gave all three of them the same nothing and that he did not vary it once.**

A man of about thirty-eight said: **my mother was told that is where you go to be told about a form.**

Marek said: **I am not going to say.**

A man of about thirty-eight said: **you are the man who had the sheet.**

Marek said: **I had a sheet. It came down last Thursday before last and it was in a bag under that bench since about nine that morning and it is not going back up.**

A man of about thirty-eight said: **so somebody has lied to my mother.**

Marek said: **nobody has to have. Somebody said a thing that was true when she said it and somebody else heard it and repeated it and now there is a fifth person who has it and none of the five of you have ever been in that room. That is not a lie. I have been trying to work out what to call it for about three weeks and I have not got it.**

A man of about thirty-eight said: **my mother is seventy-one.**

Marek said: **I know what that is and I have not got anything else for you.**

---

**And then at about half past eight the man of about thirty-three came and stood at the customer side of that counter and about four people who come to that shop have said that he had been outside in the rain for about ten minutes before he came in.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I am one of three people holding what she said on that Friday, and I am not going to tell you which of the three of us has it wrong, and that is three times now and I have stopped apologising for saying it.**

Marek said: **I have stopped asking.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **that is new. You asked me nine times in about two months.**

Marek said: **you said it every time and you said one of the three had stopped saying it, and there was nowhere to go with that.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **there is somewhere to go with it now, and it is not about you, and I am telling you because you have been in the middle of this for about three weeks without knowing what the middle of it was.**

Marek said: **then tell me what the middle of it was.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **the three of us have not been in a room together for about four years. Not once. And on Tuesday one of the other two asked me a question and I did not answer it, and I am not going to tell you what the question was, and I am going to tell you that I have not answered it and I am not going to.**

Marek said: **you could have said that four years ago.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I could have, and I did not, and that is the whole of what I have got and it is not about any room in this city.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and three people asked a man at a counter where to go and got the same nothing three times, and a printer told him that a drawer is not a promise and is not a refusal either, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its ninety-second night.**

---

**Three people asked that man where to go and were given nothing, and after that there were four jobs in that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and the four came to a sum.**

A socket in that shop had been put in with the face plate held on by two screws and a blob of sealant.

He took the sealant off, cut it true and fixed the plate properly.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A plate on with sealant is a plate that has to be cut off to be looked at. Sockets on that counter are like it, and one of them has been a counter somebody has had to put a knife to for about two years.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in with a rose that was not fixed to anything but had been painted round to look as though it was.

He took it off, made a box and proved the lamp.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A rose painted round is a rose that is not there. Lamps in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a yard a man has had to hold a ladder under in the dark for about four years.**"

A bell in that kitchen had been wired to a push that was outside the front door of the flat opposite.

He traced it, took the push across to that door and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A bell on the wrong door is a bell somebody answers from the wrong chair. Bells in that street are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had a neighbour in for about two years.**"

A freezer in that second room had been put in with its plug under the machine where the motor had dripped onto it.

He moved it to a socket on the wall and proved it with the freezer running.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A plug under a freezer is a plug that gets wet from the motor. Plugs in that room are like that, and one of them has been a corner somebody has had to keep a tray under for four years.**"

**Thirty-two pounds is the sum of that Friday's four, exact.**

---

**That Friday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf did not come off it, and this page carries no figure at all for what that binder holds.**
