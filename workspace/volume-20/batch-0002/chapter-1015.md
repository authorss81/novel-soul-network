# Chapter 1015 — That Room Does Not Go On Paper

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then about nine minutes on foot to a first floor off a line in Saltmarket, where there were about eleven people, and a man told the woman who keeps that room about a piece of paper before anybody else knew there was one, because he had learned what happens when he does not.**

---

That Monday took eleven callers through that shop and eleven names were entered onto that day's line, and the last of them was let away at about a quarter to nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and seventy-six days, two hundred and sixty-eight weeks to the day. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was forty-nine days old and was in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was thirty-five days old, and the four words were twenty-six days old and had reached a fifth person that week by way of a doorway.**

---

**He came in without saying he was coming, which is what he did, and about four people in that room have said since that he stood by the door again and did not go and stand in the middle of that floor.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have come to tell me something and not to ask me something, and I want that said before you say it.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **about four people in here have noticed that you have not sat down and about four of them have decided you have come about the paper.**

Marek said: **I have. A woman at a desk has nine words written out and she has asked a man in a second district to print them, and he has asked her four questions and she can answer three of them, and I have been asked twice whether I am going to help her with the fourth.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you have come to tell me before you do it.**

Marek said: **I have come to tell you before I do it. I did not tell you about the sheet until the Monday before I paid for it to come down and you said you would have said no, and I have not been able to think of a reason that was good enough since.**

---

**And then about nine seconds went by in that room and about four people in it have said since that the woman of about fifty-four did not use them to ask him anything at all.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **what are the nine words.**

Marek said: **I do not know. She wrote them and I have not read them and I am not going to.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you have told me about a piece of paper you have not read.**

Marek said: **I have told you about a piece of paper with nine words on it that is about this room, and I could have said it in one sentence and I said it in about four because you have been at this longer than anybody who is not me.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is nearly a sentence and you stopped.**

Marek said: **I stopped.**

---

**And then she said it, and it was nine words, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it to Marek and not to the room, and that about four other people in it heard it anyway.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no. That room is not going on any paper.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not argued with me.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you are usually the person in this room who argues with me about whether a thing is a good idea, and you have said yes in about two seconds.**

Marek said: **I did not say yes because it was easy. I said yes because you are not wrong, and I worked out about four days ago that you would not be, and I came up here to find out whether you knew it yet.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have known it since about four minutes after that woman of about thirty-four sat down with a folder and asked for a form.**

---

**And then she asked him the question that was hers, and about four people in that room have said since that she asked it in a completely different voice from the one she had used about the paper.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and if I have it and it is printed anyway.**

Marek said: **then about four people in a first district who said the four words will have been telling the truth, and about four hundred people who have not will have a new thing to be wrong about, and none of them will ever hear the second thing because they will have read the first one standing up in a passage.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and that is why you came up here.**

Marek said: **no. That is why I have not stopped it. I came up here because I have spent about four days working out that I cannot stop it and I could not do it alone, and you are the only person in this city who has had this longer than me without being told anything about it in about four years.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have not had it. I have kept a room.**

Marek said: **I know the difference and I am using the wrong word because I do not have yours.**

---

**And then a woman of about forty-six who has been in that room before came over and asked him whether the nine words were going on a wall, and about four people in that room have said since that he said he did not know, and that it was the first thing he had said all evening that he had not already told somebody else.**

The woman of about forty-six said: **you do not know.**

Marek said: **I do not know. I have told one person in this city about it and she said no and she was not wrong, and I am not going to say anything else about it to anybody until I have got about four days.**

The woman of about forty-six said: **that is worse than the four words.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The woman of about forty-six said: **and you are the one who put the wall there in the first place, except that you took it down, which you have not been asked about in a shop by anybody at all.**

Marek said: **no. I have not been asked about it by anybody in a shop.**

---

**Behind the chair of the woman of about fifty-four there was still a space with nothing standing in it and the mark on that wall where something had been standing for years, and Marek looked at it while she was not talking and did the sum with his hands behind his back, and he did not say it out loud, and about four people in that room have said since that they did not know what he had worked out and that one of them has since asked him about it and he said he had not.**

Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about eleven people were in that room and a woman who keeps it said nine words and was not wrong, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on a table in a shop across this city for its eighty-eighth night.

---

**After that it was work. Four jobs went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and every one of them was priced before it was started.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a holder that was screwed to a piece of wood about the size of a coaster.

He took the holder off, fixed it into the ceiling properly and proved the lamp with a weight on it.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp on a coaster is a lamp that comes down when anybody wipes under it. Lamps in that row are like that, and one of them has been a shop somebody has had to hold a cloth over for about two years.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a length of conduit that stopped two inches short of the box and was joined with a twist.

He cut it back, made the joint inside and proved the socket with a meter on it.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A conduit stopped short and twisted is a socket somebody has joined in the rain under a box. Sockets in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard a man has had to keep a cover on for about four years.**"

A cooker hood in that kitchen had been put in above a gas cooker without a isolator anywhere near it.

He fitted one on the wall by the door, made off the ways and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A hood with no isolator is a hood that is switched at the far end of the room from the fire. Hoods in that street are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to cross for about four years.**"

A light in that second room had been put in on a lamp holder that had been earthed with a mains flex with one core only.

He took the flex out, earthed the holder to the box and proved it with an instrument.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**One core of a flex is not an earth and it is worse than nothing. Fittings in that block are like it, and one of them has been a landing somebody has had to stop touching a rail on for four years.**"

**Forty-two pounds is the sum of that Monday's four, exact.**

---

**That Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder did not come off the back of that shelf, and no figure for what that binder holds is printed anywhere in this file.**
