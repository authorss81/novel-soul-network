# Chapter 1018 — Nobody From Any Of It Came In

**A Saturday was the busiest day that shop has had in about four years, sixteen callers and about four of them waiting by about eleven, and Marek looked up every single time that door went for about nine hours and it was not one of the people he had spent the last three weeks turning away.**

---

That Saturday took sixteen callers through that shop, which is four more than it has ever taken on one day, and sixteen names went onto that day's book in the order they arrived, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and eighty-one days, two hundred and sixty-eight weeks and five days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was fifty-four days old and was in a bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was forty days old and had never been anywhere anybody could name, and the four words were thirty-one days old and were not said once in that shop on that Saturday by anybody at all.**

---

**He had not expected that. About four people who come to that shop have said since that he said so to a man of about forty-one at about eleven and that the man did not know why he was being told it and said so, and that Marek said he was.**

Marek said: **you will not have heard about that room.**

A man of about forty-one said: **no.**

Marek said: **good.**

A man of about forty-one said: **you have looked at that door about forty times since I came in.**

Marek said: **I know. I have been looking at it since about half past six last Tuesday and about four people came in that week and every one of them was somebody I could have stopped.**

A man of about forty-one said: **then stop me.**

Marek said: **you want a light in a stairwell and a man with a ladder.**

A man of about forty-one said: **I want both of those things and I have got about eleven minutes to say them in and I have said the second one first.**

---

**And then at about two the man with the ladder and the two drawing pins came in with a job of his own, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that neither of them said one word about a wall, or a passage, or a sheet, and that it was the longest conversation either of them had ever had about a stairwell.**

A man of about forty-four said: **there is a light in a stairwell of mine that has been going since before I started doing this.**

Marek said: **where is it.**

A man of about forty-four said: **two of the landings are fine and the middle one is not, and it has been like that since I was twenty-nine.**

Marek said: **then you have had it wrong for about sixteen years.**

A man of about forty-four said: **I have had it wrong for about sixteen years and I have never once asked you about it, and I want that noticed as well.**

---

**And then he put the day's money in the drawer with the empty envelope in it, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that it is the first time that drawer has been opened in about four weeks and that he stood looking at the two of them for about four seconds before he shut it.**

**It was the most that shop had ever taken on one day. About nine hours of it was ordinary work that would have come in on a wet Wednesday in about two months, and none of the sixteen said anything about a room off a line anywhere, and Marek wrote that down on no docket and thought about it on about nine separate occasions between about eleven and about six.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people were waiting by about eleven, and a man with a ladder had a fault of sixteen years he had never mentioned, and the best day that shop has had in about four years came to fifty-eight pounds and had nothing whatever to do with anything, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its ninety-third night.**

---

**Everything else that Saturday went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and the four jobs were the biggest four of that week by more than a third.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in with a pendant that hung off a rose with no ceiling rose at all, only a batten and a piece of plaster.

He cut the plaster back, made a proper ceiling box and proved the pendant with a weight on it.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp on a batten is a lamp that comes down with the plaster. Lamps in that row are like that, and one of them has been a shop somebody has had to catch a light in their hand for about two years.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate with two of its holes out of line with the box behind it.

He took the plate off, cut a new one to the box and proved it with an instrument.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A plate with two holes out of line is a plug that has to be held at an angle. Plates in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a wall a man has had to keep a bit of tape over for about four years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been fitted with a socket above it that was not above it but about a foot away and to one side, where a pan handle goes.

He moved it to where it belonged, cut the wall and proved it.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A socket a foot from where the cooker is means somebody reaches across a hot pan to plug it in. Sockets in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a cooker a woman has had to turn away from to plug anything in for about four years.**"

A freezer in that second room had been put in with an old lead that had been extended with a piece of flex and a knot.

He cut it back, made a proper joint and proved it with the freezer running and the door shut.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**A lead tied on with a knot is a lead that has been repaired by somebody who wanted it to stop tripping. Leads in that block are like it, and one of them has been a corner somebody has had a man come out to for about two years.**"

**Fifty-eight pounds is the sum of that Saturday's four, exact.**

---

**That Saturday shut the fourth of those four rooms at about nine rather than half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf did not come off it, and this page enters nothing at all for what that binder holds.**
