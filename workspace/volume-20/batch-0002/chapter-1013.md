# Chapter 1013 — A Leaflet On The Lower Rectangle

**A Friday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off a service road, and a man took a bus of about twenty minutes to a floor of desks in a first district in the evening to ask a woman four questions, and came back past a passage where something had been put up in the morning on the space he had emptied.**

---

That Friday took seven callers through that shop and seven names were put against that day's book before any of them went, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and seventy-three days, two hundred and sixty-seven weeks and four days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was forty-six days old and had been under a long bench since the Thursday before last, the second printing was thirty-two days old and was still not in that bag, and the four words were twenty-three days old and had been said to somebody new that week by a person who had not said them to her.**

---

**He had never been on that floor and about four people who work on it have said since that he came up out of the lift at about half past six, stood at the end of the row of about eleven desks for about two minutes to work out which one he wanted, and then walked the whole length of it instead.**

Marek said: **you are the one who said it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **you have come about nine minutes to say that to me.**

Marek said: **I have come with four questions and three of them are about nine seconds each.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **then ask them outside, because there are people at that desk who have been waiting since this morning and one of them has been about twenty minutes.**

---

**They stood by a window off that room with the lights on behind them, and about four people who work on that floor have said since that neither of them sat down and that he did not have anything in his hands.**

Marek said: **a man in a second district prints things. He has printed four things for me and he asked me four questions and I have three of the answers.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **go on.**

Marek said: **your words, so his building's name goes on it and not yours.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **that is more than I expected to be asked for.**

Marek said: **your hand, so it will be in your handwriting and not in mine.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I had decided I did not mind about that and I am glad you asked.**

Marek said: **under what date.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **a date. Why the date.**

Marek said: **because he will not print it under the day he prints it. He says a date on a thing like that turns into the day it became true, whether it is or not, and there is nothing anybody can put on a sheet of paper afterwards to stop that.**

---

**And then Marek asked her the one that actually mattered and about four people who work on that floor have said since that she went quiet for about nine seconds and then started again in the middle of somebody else's sentence.**

Marek said: **the day you said the first four words. Not the day you said them to the four people. The day a person said them to you.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I do not know what day that was.**

Marek said: **you have about three weeks of it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I have about three weeks of a desk and about four people in it and I have not written one of them down. It was a Tuesday or it was a Wednesday and it was in the middle of the afternoon, and I have gone over it about four times since Friday and I cannot put a day on it, and that is not because of anything to do with that room. That is because I did not write it down when I heard it.**

Marek said: **he will not print it under the day he prints it either, and I have told him so, and he told me why, and I have just told you why.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **so it cannot be printed.**

Marek said: **not by that man. Not this week.**

---

**And then she said the thing that had been in her since Friday, and about four people who work on that floor have said that she said it flatly and did not raise her voice at all.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **then I will put a day on it. I will put the day I am asking for it.**

Marek said: **he has thought about that already and I have brought you his reasons and one of them was that it is not the same thing.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I do not care. I will put a day on it and my hand on it and I will not put my name on the bottom of it, because the bottom line is not where my name goes, the bottom line is where somebody stands behind it, and I am not going to stand behind a piece of paper that says I said a thing wrongly and does not say what it was.**

Marek said: **and if it does not say what it was.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **then about four people will read four words on a wall and never read the other five, and in a month there will be about four of them telling about four other people that there is a fifth thing being said about that room by somebody in a building. And the fifth thing will be about a correction, and nobody will ever know there was not one.**

---

**And then he said the half of it that he had, and about four people who work on that floor have said that he stopped in the middle of it and started again and that she waited.**

Marek said: **there is a second problem and I have not got round to it yet and it is worse than the date.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I have been sitting here for about nine minutes waiting for you to get to it.**

Marek said: **to correct a thing on paper you have to print the thing you are correcting. That is not his rule and it is not anybody's rule, it is how paper works. And everybody who has not read the first four words is going to read them on a wall with something under them, and somebody who has been told nothing is going to walk out of that passage knowing more about that room than any of the four people who had it to begin with.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **yes.**

Marek said: **you said that about four seconds ago.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I did. And you are going to say the rest of it anyway, and I would rather you said it here than in a shop at about nine o'clock at night.**

---

**And then he said it, and it was about nine words, and about four people who work on that floor have said since that two people at two desks looked up and then looked down again.**

Marek said: **the only thing that stops a rumour is a second notice, and a second notice is the rumour again.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **that is what you have come to tell me.**

Marek said: **no. I have come to give you three answers and a bad date. What I have just told you is not going to stop me handing you the fourth thing when you ask me for it on Monday.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I am not going to ask you for it on Monday.**

Marek said: **I will wait until you have been not asking me for about two days.**

---

**And then he took a bus of about half an hour back and walked the length of a passage off a clinic in a fourth district at about half past seven, and about four people who use that passage have said since that he stood in front of the lower of the two clean rectangles for about four minutes and did not touch anything.**

**A man of about twenty-nine with a clipboard had been up at about half past six that morning with tape, and what was on that rectangle was a printed leaflet about a course of about nine weeks, with a day on it and a place to go and nothing else on it, and about four people who use that passage have said that nobody stopped him and that about nine people read it standing up.**

A man of about twenty-nine said: **that is not yours any more, is it.**

Marek said: **it was mine until last Thursday.**

The man of about twenty-nine said: **I did not know that and I would have used it anyway. I put about nine hundred of these up in this district before the middle of the month and nobody has ever taken one of them down, and I am not going to start asking a wall who it belongs to.**

Marek said: **who takes them down.**

The man of about twenty-nine said: **nobody. They are on their own by Friday. That is why the tape is not very good.**

Marek said: **and if somebody comes along with another one.**

A man of about twenty-nine said: **then there are two. I am not the police of a passage. I put up about nine hundred and I have never once thought about the tenth.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and a floor of about eleven desks in a first district was told three answers out of four by a man with nothing in his hands, and a piece of paper went up in a hole in a fourth district at about half past six in the morning by a man who did not know the hole was one, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its eighty-fifth night.**

---

**That Friday's evening work was four jobs, and they went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen in the order he has always done them in.**

A lamp in that kitchen had been wired with the switch in the middle of the flex instead of in the wall.

He cut it out, put a proper switch in and proved the lamp from both ends.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A switch in the flex is a switch somebody has to find by feel. Lamps in that street are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to walk round a table in the dark for about two years.**"

A socket in that second room had been put in at skirting height behind a chair that everybody moved before they sat down.

He raised it to working height on the frame and proved it from the chair.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A socket behind a chair is a socket that gets moved every time somebody moves the chair. Sockets in that room are like it, and one of them has been a room a man has had to shift furniture for about four years.**"

A light in that yard had been put in on a flex that came out through the letterbox plate.

He took it out of the plate, sleeved it where it went through the brick and proved the light.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A flex through a letterbox is a flex that is wet every time it rains hard. Lights in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard a woman has had to look in the box for a key in a storm for about four years.**"

A cooker in that second room had been put in with a socket inside a cupboard and its cover missing and the ways made off on the spot.

He fitted the cover, terminated the spare ways properly and proved the cooker.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A socket in a cupboard with its cover off is a socket in a cupboard that a child finds. Fittings in that block are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had a corner of taken out for about two years.**"

**Thirty pounds is the sum of that Friday's four, exact.**

---

**That Friday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf did not come off it, and not one figure for what that binder holds is entered anywhere on this page.**

---

The tin with its lid down. The shutter. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back of that shelf. A slip with a date on it. A pencil lying in a margin.
Outside those ten, and in no count on this page, stand the cage down one side of that counter, a folded paper in a second district's drawer with one name struck through above another written under it, a leaflet going up on the lower of two clean rectangles with tape that is not very good, and a page bearing nine names.
A dated rule stands above a question that is not this page's, and any person who stands in front of it may read whatever has been kept behind it.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody. A woman at a desk said the only way to correct four words on paper is to print the four words, and a man took about half an hour to cross a city to be told a date she does not have, and a man with a clipboard put about nine hundred leaflets up in that district and has never taken one of them down.**
