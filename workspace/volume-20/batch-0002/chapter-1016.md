# Chapter 1016 — The Green Binding Lay Open

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then the book in the green binding came off the table it lives on and was not shut again until about a quarter to ten, and about nine people came and went through that shop in that time, and one sentence was said in the whole of it.**

---

That Wednesday took nine callers through that shop as well as the nine who came for the book, and nine names went onto that day's line and none of them was one of the nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and seventy-eight days, two hundred and sixty-eight weeks and two days. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was fifty-one days old and was in a bag under the long bench at the back, the second printing was thirty-seven days old, and the four words were twenty-eight days old and had been said that week to a person who had asked for nothing and got nothing.**

---

**The tin came off the table first and its lid came off that, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that it is the only evening of the week when that tin is not shut and that nobody mentions it and that everybody knows.**

A woman of about fifty-one said: **I am going to do mine now, and I am not going to stand about it.**

Marek said: **you never stand about it.**

A woman of about fifty-one said: **I am aware of what I am like and I am not going to discuss it at the counter.**

She put one in and went out.

---

**And then a man of about forty-one came and took one out and put it in his coat, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that neither of them said a figure out loud and that it is the same on every evening and that it is not the same on any evening.**

A man of about forty-one said: **I am not going to say what it is and I am not going to say what it was and I would like to be able to tell you that nobody has ever asked me, and I cannot tell you that, because you asked me once and the asking was not about what is in it.**

Marek said: **I did.**

A man of about forty-one said: **you did, once, and I did not answer it, and you have not asked me since, and that is the longest anybody has gone in about four years.**

---

**And then nobody said anything for about an hour and a half, and about four people who sit in that shop on a Wednesday have said since that they have never once been asked what that evening is and that they would not answer if they were.**

**A woman of about thirty-one came and read for about ten minutes. A man of about fifty-eight argued with a boy of about nineteen about a bus route for about six minutes and lost. A woman of about sixty-two came in, said nothing to anybody, put one in the tin and went out, and about four people have said since that she has never once said anything in that shop and that nobody would have expected it.**

**And Marek stood behind that counter with about nine people in his shop and no money in the till, and found that he had about nine people in one room saying nothing to each other for about an hour and a half, and that it was the only thing that had ever worked, and that he had been in that room four years and had never once seen it come out of anybody's mouth, and that when he tried to put it in a sentence the sentence came out about the building and not about the nine people, so he did not say it out loud and wrote nothing down.**

---

**And then a man of about forty-seven who comes in most Wednesdays said the only sentence of that evening that was not about the book, and about four people who sit in that shop have said since that Marek answered it in four words and went back to what he was doing.**

A man of about forty-seven said: **are you going to be asked anything this year.**

Marek said: **no.**

A man of about forty-seven said: **good. You will not know what to do with it if you are, and neither will anybody else here, and we will all end up standing about it in a worse way than you stand about the tin.**

Marek said: **you are the first person in about four years to have said anything useful about me and you did it in about eleven words on a Wednesday and I have not written any of it down.**

A man of about forty-seven said: **I did not expect you to. If you had I would have stopped saying things in about four years.**

---

**And then at about half past six, with about four of the nine still in the shop, a woman of about thirty-four stood in that doorway for about four seconds and then turned round and went, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that she had a folder under her arm and that she did not come in and that nobody in that shop saw her do it but the man behind the counter.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **no. I am not doing it tonight.**

Nobody said anything, and Marek did not go after her, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he looked at the door once and then at the book and that the book was more interesting than the door and that this was said afterwards by people who noticed it and not by him.

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about nine people were in that shop for about an hour and a half and said almost nothing, and a woman stood in the doorway of it with a folder and did not come in, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its ninetieth night.**

---

**The four jobs of that Wednesday went in after the last of the nine had gone and they went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and they were the four dearest of that week by a distance.**

A fuse in that shop had been fitted with a rewireable one of a different size from the others in the board.

He took it out, put in one of its own pattern and proved the board dead.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A fuse the wrong size is a fuse that has been in about four years without anybody reading what is written on it. Boards in that row are like that, and one of them has been a shop somebody has had to keep spare ones of the wrong thing for about two years.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a lead that had been buried in a flower bed about four inches down.

He dug it up, brought it up to the wall in a proper box and proved it.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A lead buried in a flower bed is a lead that is wet twice a year and is dry and dangerous the other fifty-two. Sockets in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard a woman has had nothing planted in for about four years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been wired from a spur off the lighting circuit with no way of telling the two apart.

He took the spur off, put the cooker on its own way and proved it.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A cooker on the lights is a cooker that trips the lights and a cooker that somebody has worked around for four years. Appliances in that street are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to cook in the dark for about two years.**"

A stair light in that second room had been put in on a fitting that was screwed into a length of timber that was not fixed to anything.

He took the fitting off, fixed a box into the wall and proved it from both landings.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A light screwed into a loose piece of timber is a light that rattles every time somebody opens that door. Fittings in that block are like it, and one of them has been a stair a man has had to hold the rail and his own hand up with for four years.**"

**Fifty pounds is the sum of that Wednesday's four, exact.**

---

**That Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six like every other night, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf did not come off it, and nothing whatever is entered on this page for the page that binder holds.**
