# Chapter 1019 — The Thing He Would Have To Sign

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then that shop, and six people were in it at half past seven on that Monday evening and stayed until the shutter came down at ten, and about nine seconds of what was said in it is the reason a piece of paper was never printed.**

---

That Monday took seven callers through that shop and six of the seven were still in it at about half past seven, and the seventh was let away at about a quarter to six, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and eighty-three days, two hundred and sixty-nine weeks to the day. The sheet that had been on a wall in a fourth district was fifty-six days old and was under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing was forty-two days old, and the four words were thirty-three days old and had been carried about eleven minutes further that day by somebody who was not going to carry them again.**

---

**She had come in at about half past seven with a piece of paper in a plastic sleeve, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that she put it down flat on the counter and squared it up with two fingers before she said anything at all.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **there is a date on it and it is mine and the day I asked for it, and it is in my hand, and the man in that building has not asked me for anything since Friday.**

Marek said: **and the bottom line.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **and the bottom line is empty, and he has said he will not print it with the bottom line empty, and he is not wrong, and I have brought it to you because you are the only person in this city I could put a name to.**

---

**And then the others said what they had come for, in the order they had come, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that Marek answered all six of them and said the thing he had to say in about nine seconds, and that the nine seconds came at the end.**

The man of about twenty-nine said: **there are two clean rectangles on that wall and one of them has my leaflet on it and the other one has been bare for about three weeks, and I put the leaflet there myself and I am not sorry about it, and I would like somebody to tell me what the bare one is for so that I can stop thinking about it.**

Marek said: **I do not know and you should stop thinking about it.**

A man of about twenty-nine said: **that is what I came for.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I am one of three people holding what she said on that Friday and I am not going to say which of the three of us has it wrong, and I have said that four times now, and I have come here tonight to say that I have not told her that. She has asked me twice and I have not told her, and she has not asked me a third time, and I would like that noticed by somebody.**

Marek said: **it is noticed.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four said the thing she had come for, and about four people who come to that shop have said that she said it while she was still standing and had not sat down since she came in.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I was in a queue on Thursday and I did not go in, and I have been in a queue on Friday and I did not go in, and I would like somebody in this room to tell me whether there is any point in a queue any more.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **that is the fourth time and I have stopped being angry about it and I would like to know what to do instead, because I am going to have to do something.**

Marek said: **I know that and I have not got it and I am not going to invent it at this counter.**

---

**And then a man of about twenty-six, who had been standing by the door since about half past six and had not said anything at all, said one thing, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that it was the only thing he said in about two and a half hours.**

A man of about twenty-six said: **she asked me in that room whether there was a form and I said no, and I have thought about that every day since, and I am not asking anybody anything. I am telling six people because there are six people here and I am one of them.**

Marek said: **you did not have to do that.**

A man of about twenty-six said: **no. And I have wanted to for about a week, and I am aware that I have got about no reason at all.**

---

**And then Marek put both hands on that counter and said it, and it was about nine seconds and about eleven words, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that nobody in that room understood it at the time and that two of them have understood it since.**

Marek said: **I would have to sign nine words I have never read, and that is the whole of it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I know.**

Marek said: **you knew that on Tuesday and you brought it up here anyway.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I did. And I want to be exact with you about why, because you have been honest with me in front of about nine people and I am not going to be clever in front of the same nine.**

Marek said: **go on.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **there is a second reason and it is a worse one and I am not going to put it in a shop.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **not so. You are going to let me finish, and then you are going to say the rest of it, and then I am going to go home, and that is what this evening was for.**

---

**And then he said the rest of it, and it was longer, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he did it in about nine seconds and that the room was completely still for the whole of it.**

Marek said: **there is no version of this where I put a name on the bottom line and do not stand behind nine words another person wrote about a room. If I sign it I am a man who read something and did not read it, in a building in a second district, on a day that is written on it, and every person who comes and stands there and asks will be told that by a man who was here when it was decided. If I do not sign it there is nothing. And the thing I have been telling you for about four days is still true and it is the only reason I am saying this: the four words are still out there, and I have not stopped them, and I am not able to.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **you have chosen the sheets.**

Marek said: **the sheets have been under a bench since the Thursday before last. I have chosen not putting anything back. That is not the same as stopping it and I want everybody in this room to have heard me say that, because in about four days one of you is going to tell somebody I stopped it.**

---

**And then nobody argued with him, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that the woman of about forty-three said the sentence that ended it, and that she said it standing up, and that she was not angry.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I am not going to argue with you. You are not wrong, and you have not been wrong since about Tuesday, and I have been coming to this counter for about three weeks hoping to be somebody who could tell me that about myself, and there is nobody in this room who can be told that about themselves, including me.**

Marek said: **I have not said that to anybody in about four years.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. And it is not that anybody in this room is wrong. It is that there is no room in this city which will take that, and I have looked.**

---

**And then Talia said the thing she had come for, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that she had been standing by that counter for about twenty minutes and had not said anything until then.**

Talia said: **that was cheaper than I thought it would be.**

Marek said: **that is not what I did.**

Talia said: **no. And you have not asked me for a name this month or any month, and I have had mine ready since about a week ago, and I am not going to say anything about that, and I would like it noticed that I said nothing about it.**

Marek said: **it is noticed.**

Talia said: **a man at my counter said something to me one night in the autumn about you doing whichever was cheaper. I have been carrying that about for about three weeks and I did not know I was carrying it until about four seconds ago.**

Marek said: **he had me and I had him wrong, and those are two different sentences and I am not going to put them next to each other.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and six people were in that shop from about half past seven until about ten, and a piece of paper in a plastic sleeve went back into a coat with nine words on it and a date on it and an empty line at the bottom, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its ninety-fifth night.**

---

**At about ten the last of that Monday went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and there were four jobs in it and nobody was in the room by then.**

A lamp in that kitchen had been put in on a ceiling rose with two of the terminals left bare and hanging.

He made off the terminals, put the rose back on and proved the lamp.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A terminal left bare over somebody's head is a terminal somebody will find with a ruler. Roses in that street are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to get a chair to for about two years.**"

A switch in that yard had been put in on a plate that had been painted round and had no earth behind it.

He took it off, made the box earthed and refitted it.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A switch painted round with nothing behind it is a switch that was never made safe. Switches in that block are like it, and one of them has been a wall somebody has had a man look at for about four years.**"

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a lamp holder that had come loose in the ceiling and was being held up by the flex.

He took the holder down, boxed it into the ceiling and proved the lamp with a weight.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp hanging on its own flex is a lamp waiting for a foot. Lamps in that row are like that, and one of them has been a ceiling somebody has stopped looking up at for about four years.**"

A cooker in that second room had been put in with a plug and a socket that were both three feet from where either of them was meant to be.

He moved the socket to the cooker, made it good and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A socket three feet from the cooker is a socket that has been put in where it was easy and not where it was wanted. Sockets in that room are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to stretch across for about two years.**"

**Thirty-six pounds is the sum of that Monday's four, exact.**

---

**That Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder did not come off its shelf at the back of that room, and no figure at all for what that binder holds is entered anywhere on this page.**
