# Chapter 1011 — The Woman Who Said It First

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off a service road, and a woman who had been sitting at the same desk for years came in at about half past seven with her coat on and put a folded piece of paper on that counter and did not open it.**

---

That Tuesday took eight callers through that shop and eight names were written against that day's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let away at about half past eight, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and seventy days, two hundred and sixty-seven weeks and one day. The sheet that had been on a passage wall in a fourth district was forty-three days old and had been in a carrier bag under a bench since the Thursday before last, the second printing was twenty-nine days old and had never been on that wall at all, and the four words were twenty days old and were in the mouths of about four people.**

---

**She had not taken her coat off and she did not sit down, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that she stood with one hand flat on the counter the whole time, as though the counter were the only part of the room she was sure of.**

Marek said: **you have not taken your coat off.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. I want to be able to leave in about four minutes.**

Marek said: **you can take it off.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I am not here about a lamp. I have said a thing to about four people and I am here about the thing.**

Marek said: **I am not going to guess.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **then do not. I will tell you, and you will not believe about half of it, and that will take us about four minutes and it is worth it because nobody else in this city will say it to you.**

---

**And then she said the part that had taken her two weeks to get to a shop, and about four people who come to that counter have said since that he stopped what he was doing before she had finished the first sentence of it.**

Marek said: **you said it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I said it.**

Marek said: **to about four people.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **to four that I know of and there may be more of them, and I have been at that desk for years, and I have filled one of those pages in for somebody else as recently as last week.**

Marek said: **and when you said it you meant it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I meant it that day. I am not a person who says things to be unkind. I have never once said it to anybody who I did not think better of afterwards.**

---

**And then he asked her the question he had gone four days to ask anybody, which was how she knew, and about four people in that shop have said that she took about nine seconds and that she did not look away from him while she did it.**

Marek said: **how do you know one of them has it wrong.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I cannot tell you how I know. That is not me being careful. Two of them have been to that desk this week to ask me a question and one of them told me she had it from a person on a bus, and one of them told me she had it from a woman at a desk in a different part of that building, and that woman is not me.**

Marek said: **that could be anybody.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **it could be. And I have thought about that for about four days and I cannot make it be anybody, and that is where I have got to and I am not going further than that in a shop.**

Marek said: **so you know it and you cannot show how you know it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **yes. And if you ask me to prove it I will go back to that desk and I will be about four minutes late for the rest of my life.**

---

**And then she pushed the folded paper about two inches towards him and kept her hand on it, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he did not touch it and did not ask to.**

Marek said: **what is on that.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **that is what I want printed.**

Marek said: **I am not going to read it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. And I want to be exact about why, because I have thought about it. It is nine words. You would have it in about four seconds and then it would be in you for the rest of your life, and there is nobody I could give it to afterwards who has not already heard the first four.**

Marek said: **and what would it do, if it went on a wall.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **it would say it is not that.**

Marek said: **it would have to say what it is, and it would have to print the first four words to do that, or a person reading it standing up will not know which of the two things you are correcting.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I know. I have known since Friday and that is the whole of what I have come to tell you.**

---

**And then he asked the one that is not about words, and about four people in that shop have said that it was the question she had not been asked by anybody.**

Marek said: **and who signs it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I do not know. That is why I am standing here with my coat on.**

Marek said: **there are three things it could be and two of them belong to other people.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **say all three.**

Marek said: **you say it out loud at your own desk, to anybody who comes to that desk, in your own voice, and nobody has to agree with you and nobody has to put a name to it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **yes.**

Marek said: **or a person says it for you, and that person has to be somebody you have asked, not somebody you have found.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **yes, and I have nobody.**

Marek said: **or it goes to that building in a second district and a man in there prints what he is handed and does not put his name to it either.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **he will ask who is behind it.**

Marek said: **he will ask four things. I have been asked three of them.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **which three.**

Marek said: **whose words, in whose hand, and under what date. The fourth is who stands behind it, and I have not got an answer to that one, and I do not think you have either, and I do not think the man expects one. He has printed four things for me and I have got three answers every time and the fourth has never mattered until now.**

---

**And then about nine seconds went by with the till on and the folded paper where it was, and about four people who come to that shop have said that neither of them looked at it and that the shutter went up and down twice in it.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **so it is the first one.**

Marek said: **it is the first one. It is yours, it costs you nothing but your face, and it is the only one of the three that nobody else has to let you have.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I know exactly what it costs. Two people at that desk have known me for years and one of them has a bad hip and sits down when I come round, and she will be told about this before she is told about anything else because she will be in the room.**

Marek said: **I am not going to tell you that is the wrong choice.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. You are going to tell me that it is mine and that you have not got a better one, and that is what I have walked about twenty minutes to hear.**

Marek said: **that is what I am going to tell you.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **go on.**

Marek said: **it is yours and I have not got a better one. I have got four things I could do with the rest of this week and none of them is one.**

---

**And then she picked the paper up and put it inside her coat, and about four people who come to that shop have said that she did it in about two seconds and did not look at it again, and that she went out into a Tuesday evening at about half past eight with about four people crossing that service road.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **I am about four minutes late and I have not been late for that desk in about nine years.**

Marek said: **you have not asked me a single question about any of this that I could have answered.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. I asked you three and you answered about two of them and the third one you did not answer, and I can tell which, and I am not going to tell you which, because you have never once asked me which of four women it was.**

---

**And then at about a quarter to nine a woman of about forty-one came in with a lamp under her arm and asked Marek whether that room was the one behind the second sheet, and about four people who come to that shop have said that he said he could not tell her, and that she said she had never seen a second sheet on any wall in her life.**

Marek said: **I could not tell her.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **you could have told her there is a hundred and fifty of them somewhere and not said where.**

Marek said: **no. That is a figure and not an answer, and it would have been the first thing anybody has said to her about it, and I am not spending that on a Tuesday.**

The woman of about forty-three said: **no. You are not. That is the fourth time somebody has been told nothing by you this month and I have started keeping them.**

Marek said: **you will have about nine by the end of it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a woman who had said it first walked about twenty minutes to a shop and was given the truth and no better offer than her own voice, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its eighty-second night.**

---

**What that Tuesday had left went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and there were four jobs in it and no fifth.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in with a two-pin plug and a length of flex that ran under the door across the yard.

He brought the flex up out of the gap, made a proper entry through the frame and proved the lamp.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A flex under a door is a flex somebody has trodden on for four years. Lamps in that row are like it, and one of them has been a shop a person has had to sweep a mat around for about two years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been fed from a junction box inside the cabinet under the sink, with its cover on by two screws and a washer.

He took the box out, made a proper entry and proved the cooker with the oven on.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A box that has to be shut with a washer is a box somebody has had to shut carefully for four years. Boxes in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had a plumber out to for about two years.**"

A light in that second room had been switched from a switch mounted on the same wall as the fuse board.

He moved it to the frame of the door, away from the board, and proved it from the bed.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A light switched from beside the board is a light somebody turns on with a finger near somebody else's fuses. Switches in that block are like it, and one of them has been a room a man has had to get a torch for four years.**"

An outside light in that shop had an inside switch and a second switch outside on the wall, and nothing joined to the second one.

He took the second one off, made off the ends and proved the light from the yard.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A switch outside that does nothing is a switch somebody has pressed in the dark expecting it to work. Switches in that row are like it, and one of them has been a yard a man has had to shout into for about four years.**"

**Thirty-five pounds is what those four came to on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**That Tuesday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the binder at the back of that shelf did not come off it, and this page puts no figure against the page that binder holds.**
