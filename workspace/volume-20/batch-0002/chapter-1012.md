# Chapter 1012 — The Printer Would Not Invent A Date

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a bus of about half an hour across the city to a second district, where a man who holds a room and a filing system and has printed four things for a repairer in about four years gave him a chair and offered him nothing else at all.**

---

That Wednesday took ten callers through that shop and ten names went onto that day's line, none of them out of order, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and seventy-one days, two hundred and sixty-seven weeks and two days. The sheet that had been on a passage wall was forty-four days old and had been under a bench since the Thursday before last, the second printing was thirty days old and was not in that bag and was not anywhere else anybody knew of, and the four words were twenty-one days old.**

---

**He had sent nothing ahead of him and had written nothing down, and about four people who use that building have said since that he walked in off a bus at about half past four, asked for him by name, and was let straight through without being told to wait anywhere.**

Marek said: **I have come about a thing you would print for me.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **I have four things printed for you in about four years and I have printed nothing for anybody in about two years and I am not going to start.**

Marek said: **I am not asking you to print anything.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is not a request, then. That is a conversation, and I have about nine minutes before the light goes in that room, so sit down.**

---

**And then he took a drawer out from under that desk and opened it, and about four people who use that building have said since that he did not go in far and did not go through it, and that there are about nine things in it and he knows all nine.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **you have never asked me a question that could not be written on one side of a piece of paper.**

Marek said: **I know. I have never wanted anything printed that did not have a person standing behind it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is not why you are here. You are here because four people have told you a thing is wrong and you have run out of things to do with it.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then ask. I will not tell you what I think you ought to print. I have printed for four years and I have never once told anybody what to print and I am not going to start with you.**

---

**And then he asked the first one, and about four people who use that building have said that he asked all four the same way, which was as though he were reading a shelf.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **whose words.**

Marek said: **a woman of about forty-three who sits at a desk in a first district.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **does she want her name on it.**

Marek said: **no.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then write mine. I print it, it is in my building, and anybody who comes at me gets me.**

Marek said: **yes.**

---

The man of about sixty-one said: **in whose hand.**

Marek said: **hers. She wrote nine words on a piece of paper on a Tuesday night and brought them to a shop and did not let anybody read them.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **so it will be her hand on a thing I print.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is unusual and I do not mind it and you should know that I do not mind it, because people assume a man with a press minds about that and I have never once minded about that.**

Marek said: **I did not assume it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **no. That is what I am telling you.**

---

The man of about sixty-one said: **under what date.**

Marek said: **the day you print it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **wrong. The day it is said to have been said.**

Marek said: **why.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **because a person reading it in a passage in a year's time will take the date as the day the thing became true, and it will not be, and there is no way on a sheet of paper to stop them. I have put a wrong date under about four hundred things over the years and I have watched every one of them be believed.**

Marek said: **then what date.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **the day she said the first four words. Not the day of the thing you are correcting. The first day. That way there are two dates on it and a person has to decide which one they are reading.**

Marek said: **and the first day is not printed anywhere.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **no. So you would have to write it, and you have not got it, and I am not going to help you invent one.**

---

**And then he asked the fourth one, and about four people who use that building have said since that Marek sat back in that chair about four inches and that the man of about sixty-one let the question sit there on its own.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **and who stands behind it.**

Marek said: **I do not know.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **she does not know.**

Marek said: **she will not say, and I am not going to ask her, and if I asked her she would tell me about four words about a desk and no name at all.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then it is not a request and it is not a conversation, and it is a piece of paper with nine words on it and nobody's name on it, and I cannot do anything with that.**

Marek said: **that is what I came to find out.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **you came to find out in about nine minutes what I would have told you in about four. Sit there.**

---

**And while he was sitting there the man of about sixty-one took a folded paper out of that drawer, unfolded it, turned it over once and put it back, and about four people who use that building have said since that there is a name struck through on the outside of it and a name written under it, and that he did not offer it to Marek and did not mention it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **that is an old one. Nine years. Somebody wanted a thing printed, and there were two names on it, and the top one was struck through and the other one written under, and a man in this city worked out which of the two was standing behind it and came to this desk and asked me to hold it for about four years.**

Marek said: **did you.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **no. I told him I would and I did, and it is in that drawer, and it has been in that drawer since.**

Marek said: **that is not the same as holding it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **no. It is not. And I am telling you about it because you are about to go and ask a woman to put her hand to something, and I want you to have one thing in your head when you ask her, and that is that a drawer is not a promise.**

---

**And then he put both hands flat on that desk and gave Marek the whole of it in one go, and about four people who use that building have said that he did not raise his voice once and did not editorialise about a single part of it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **here is what I would do and then I am going back to my light. It goes in that drawer and it does not go on a wall. It is not printed until somebody puts a name on the bottom line of it, and I will not print it with the bottom line empty and I am not saying that to be difficult, I am saying it because a thing with nobody on it can be held against anybody later on, including the woman who wrote it.**

Marek said: **and if somebody does put a name on it.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **then it is printed at about the price of two jobs of yours, it goes in that drawer, and about nine people can come and stand where you are standing and read it, and nobody has to be told anything.**

Marek said: **that is the whole of what I came for.**

The man of about sixty-one said: **no. That is the whole of what you are going to be able to offer her. Which is better than what you have got.**

---

**And then he came back on the bus and the man of about thirty-three was at the customer side of that counter with his coat off, and about four people who come to that shop have said that he had been there about a quarter of an hour and had not asked for anything.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **you have been across the city.**

Marek said: **I have been across the city.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I am one of three people who are holding what she said on that Friday, and I am not going to tell you which of the three of us has it wrong, and I have said that twice now in about a month and I will say it again.**

Marek said: **is she one of the three.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **no.**

Marek said: **you have not had to think about that.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **no, and I would like it noticed that I did not have to think about it. She has never been one of us. She has never been anywhere near it. I have been waiting about four weeks for somebody to ask me that and I am the one who has to say it, and now it is said and it is said in a shop.**

Marek said: **then who the three of you are is the only thing left.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **no. It is not, and you are doing the thing you do, which is to take a thing that has been said and ask what is next to it. There is nothing next to it. There is a woman at a desk with nine words and three of us with a word, and none of the four of us has said anything to the other three, and that is not a mystery. That is four people who did not want to be first.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and four questions were asked across a desk in a second district and three of them were answered and the fourth one went home with him, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its eighty-third night.**

---

**Four jobs were left over on that Wednesday and they went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and the first of them was the one he would have done first on any day.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with the cable pinched flat behind it by a piece of plaster.

He took the rose off, freed the cable, made the joint properly and proved the lamp with an instrument.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A cable pinched behind plaster is a lamp that has been dim since somebody put a shelf up. Roses in that row are like it, and one of them has been a shop somebody has had to change a bulb in the dark for about two years.**"

A socket in that yard had been mounted on a batten that ran across the gap where a length of guttering had been taken out years before.

He took the socket off, cut the batten back and fixed the box to something.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A socket screwed to nothing across a hole is a socket that a wind takes the plate off. Sockets in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard a man has had to tape a plate to for about four years.**"

A heater in that shop had been plugged into a lead that went under a fixed shelf with nothing holding it against the board.

He took the lead up, put a proper clip on it and proved the heater on the load.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A lead under a shelf with no clip is a lead that gets worked by a vacuum cleaner and nobody hears it happen. Leads in that shop are like it, and one of them has been a shelf somebody has had a box off twice for about two years.**"

A light in that kitchen had been put in on a fitting whose earth went to its own mounting bracket and stopped there.

He took the bracket off, earthed it to the box and proved it with an instrument.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**An earth that stops at the bracket is an earth to a bracket. Fittings in that street are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to keep the switch off for about four years.**"

**Forty-six pounds is the sum of that Wednesday's four, exact.**

---

**That Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder stayed at the back of that shelf where it has been, and nothing whatever is entered on this page for what that binder holds.**
