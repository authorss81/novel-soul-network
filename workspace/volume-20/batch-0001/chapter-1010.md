# Chapter 1010 — A Bag Under The Bench

**A Friday was a bench until noon and a counter until four and then four converted units off a service road, and a carrier bag sat under the long bench at the back of that shop from about nine in the morning, and a woman of about thirty-four came in at about half past six with a folder and was told the same thing as about four other people, which was nothing.**

---

That Friday took seven callers through that shop and seven names were written against the day before they went, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and sixty-six days, two hundred and sixty-six weeks and four days. The sheet on that passage wall was thirty-nine days old and had been off that wall since about nine the morning before, and the second printing was twenty-five days old, and the four words were sixteen days old and had been repeated by somebody neither of them knew about four times that week.**

---

**The bag was under the long bench at the back of that shop and about four people who come to that counter have said since that the first thing three of them asked about that Friday was whether it was still there.**

Marek said: **it is under the bench.**

A man of about fifty-two said: **is it that one.**

Marek said: **it is two hundred and fifty sheets that had a door on them and nothing else.**

A man of about fifty-two said: **can I have one.**

Marek said: **no.**

A man of about fifty-two said: **why not.**

Marek said: **because it is the whole of what was printed and there is no second one, and if one goes back on a wall this week then I have not stopped anything.**

---

**And then she came in at about half past six with the same folder under her arm, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that she had been in a queue all morning and that it showed in her hands.**

Marek said: **you have been at a queue.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I have been at a queue and I have not gone in.**

Marek said: **why not.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **because I wanted to know whether I would be able to say what you told me, and I cannot say it.**

---

**And then she told him the third thing, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that he stood completely still while she said it and that he did not write anything down.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **a man on a bus said it was where you go when you have already been told what to do.**

Marek said: **that is not what anybody else says.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **no. It is what the man on the bus says.**

Marek said: **so there is a desk, a room and a bus.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **and a wall that is empty now, and the four of them do not agree, and one of them is a woman who has been kind to me for two years.**

---

**And then she asked him what she should do, and about four people in that shop have said since that it was the fourth person that week to ask him that and that he had not given any of them anything.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **what do I do.**

Marek said: **I am not going to tell you.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **you have an opinion.**

Marek said: **I have four, and three of them are about how to get a sheet printed, and none of them is about you.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **that is not an answer.**

Marek said: **no. It is me telling you that there is not one, and I would rather say that than give you a sentence that would be out of date by Friday.**

---

**And then a man of about forty-four came in at about half past seven and said that about four people had asked him that week where to go, and Marek told him the same thing he had told the other three, which was that he was not going to say, and the man asked him why he had put it up in the first place.**

A man of about forty-four said: **you put a door on a wall.**

Marek said: **I paid for it and a man with a ladder put it up and I have paid a man with a ladder to take it down.**

A man of about forty-four said: **so there is less.**

Marek said: **there is a paper that outlives a room and I have found out about that the expensive way, and I am not putting another one up this month, and I am not printing a second thing behind it either.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people who had come to that counter that week on the strength of a woman at a desk were given nothing at all, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on a table in that shop for its seventy-sixth night.**

---

**The last of that Friday went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and it came to four jobs and no more.**

A socket in that shop had been put in above a counter with a machine on a bench in front of it that got wet every time the place was washed down.

He moved it round to the frame of a door and proved it from the bench.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A socket in the splash zone of a wash-down is a socket that gets wet and stays wet. Sockets on that counter are like it, and one of them has been a bench a man has had to keep a cloth over for about two years.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in on a lamp holder with a brass ring round it that had gone green and was now touching the shade.

He took it out, cleaned it up and proved the lamp with it in.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A corroded ring touching a shade is a fitting that is one splash from somebody's hand. Fittings in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard a man has had to look at for four years.**"

A fridge in that kitchen had been plugged into a socket behind it where nobody could reach it without pulling the fridge out.

He moved it to a socket on the wall and proved it with the fridge plugged in.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A plug behind an appliance is a plug nobody checks. Sockets in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to pull a fridge out for about four years.**"

A light in that second room had been put in without any earth at all and the metal part of the holder was within reach of the floor.

He earthed it off the box and proved it with an instrument.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**An un earthed light is a light with metal a person can stand on. Lamps in that room are like it, and one of them has been a floor somebody has had to be careful on for about four years.**"

**Thirty-one pounds is the sum of that Friday's four, exact.**

---

**That Friday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the ring binder stayed at the back of that shelf, and this page enters no figure for the page that binder holds.**
