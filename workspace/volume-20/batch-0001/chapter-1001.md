# Chapter 1001 — A Rumour Getting Ahead Of The Sheet

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off a service road, one of them warm until about nine, and the whole of the evening went in about two hours, because a shop that has been open since seven does not stop at six for anybody.**

---

That Monday took seven callers through that shop and seven names were entered onto that day's line, and the last of them stood at the customer side of that counter for about ten minutes before she went, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and fifty-five days, two hundred and sixty-five weeks to the day. The sheet on that passage wall was twenty-eight days old that Monday, the second printing was a fortnight old, and a thing said at a counter in a first district was five days old and in the mouths of about four people.**

---

**Nobody had sent Marek anything and nobody had told him anything. A man of about forty-five who has read that wall since the week it went up put a question at that counter that had not been put there before, and the answer to it was four words long, and Marek did not have the four words.**

The man of about forty-five said: **my sister-in-law says that is where you go to be told about a form.**

Marek said: **who told her that.**

The man of about forty-five said: **a woman at a desk. She has been at that desk for years and my sister-in-law thinks well of her.**

Marek said: **what desk.**

The man of about forty-five said: **I do not know. My sister-in-law says the woman has a form with things ruled in columns on it and filled one in for somebody else.**

Marek said: **and she has told you.**

The man of about forty-five said: **yes.**

Marek said: **and you have come to the man who paid for the printing.**

The man of about forty-five said: **I have. I have not said it to anybody else and I want that on the counter and not on a sheet.**

---

**And then he said the part that made Marek put a cloth down on that counter and leave it there, which is that about four people had heard the four words and that two of them had not heard them from the woman at the desk.**

The man of about forty-five said: **there is my sister-in-law and there is a woman she works beside and that is two, and my sister-in-law says the second one did not have to be told because she has been going there for years.**

Marek said: **then one of your two heard it somewhere else.**

The man of about forty-five said: **that is what I have been sitting here for ten minutes about.**

Marek said: **did she say where.**

The man of about forty-five said: **no. She said somebody in a queue said it.**

Marek said: **and you have not asked which queue.**

The man of about forty-five said: **I am not going round this city asking a woman in a queue what she has said. I have come to a shop.**

---

**He went up those stairs at about half past six without saying that he was going, and there were about eleven people in that room, and nobody in it was asked what they had come for, and about four of them had come in off that line with nothing on them at all.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not said you were coming.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you never do. Sit or stand.**

Marek said: **stand. It is a minute and I would rather not do it sitting down.**

---

**And then he said it to her instead of to the room, and he said it in about nine words, and about four people in that room have said since that they heard it and that she did not turn round.**

Marek said: **there is a woman at a desk telling people what this room is, and I do not know which desk.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **is that a thing I did.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then it is not mine and you have come up here to tell a woman in a room something that is not hers.**

Marek said: **I know.**

---

**And then he asked the thing he had come up for, and he asked it badly, and it was the wrong question and it was the only one he had, and about four people in that room have said since that she took about nine seconds before she began.**

Marek said: **do you know which desk.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no.**

Marek said: **if you did, would you tell me.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no. I would not, and you knew that when you asked it, and I would rather you had asked me something you already knew the answer to.**

Marek said: **I did not have one.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then you have come up here for nothing, and I have told you so, and you can go down the stairs and not come up on Wednesday.**

---

**A man of about thirty-four at the back of that room said the thing that about four people in there had come to say, and he said it to Marek and not to her, and she did not answer him.**

A man of about thirty-four said: **is any of it written down.**

Marek said: **no.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **then what happens to a person who walks in.**

Marek said: **nothing happens.**

The man of about thirty-four said: **nothing happens.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **nothing happens, and that is not a trick, and there is nothing at the end of it waiting for you, and you are owed that much by somebody.**

---

**And then Marek asked the only question in that room on that Monday that was about anybody's work, and he asked it out loud, and she answered it in one sentence and did not say the woman's name and did not say the desk.**

Marek said: **do you know a woman who fills in forms in columns for people.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I know four and I am not going to narrow it to one for you, and you may write that down exactly as I have said it.**

Marek said: **I am not going to write anything down.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no. You have never once written down a thing in this room and that is the whole of what I have been able to rely on for about four years.**

---

**He went back down that stair and across and into that shop at about half past seven, and the till went on twice while he was standing at it doing nothing, and about four people who come to that shop have said that he counted the second printing out loud twice and stopped both times.**

Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and about eleven people went through a room off that line and not one of them was asked what they had come for, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on a table in that shop for its sixty-seventh night.

---

**What was left of that Monday went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and it took four jobs and not a fifth.**

A strip light in that kitchen had been wired with a flex that ran behind the back of a cupboard and had been run there by somebody who did not want to see it.

He took the cupboard off, cut the flex back to a proper point, sleeved it and proved the fitting.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A flex hidden behind a cupboard is a flex nobody has looked at since it went in. Runs in that kitchen are all behind something, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had a ladder out for about two years.**"

A socket in that shop had been mounted on a piece of plywood about half an inch thick, fixed flat to the wall behind the plasterboard.

He took the plate off, made the box into the wall and proved it with an instrument.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A socket on a piece of ply is a socket that comes off with a thumb. Sockets in that row are on ply, and one of them has been a counter somebody has had to hold on to for four years.**"

A floodlight over that yard had been wired with the earth taken off its own metal case and onto a length of pipe.

He put the earth back onto the case, took it off the pipe and proved the light.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A floodlight earthed to a pipe is a floodlight on somebody's water. Fittings in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard a man has had to keep switching off in the wet for about two years.**"

A bell in that second room had its transformer mounted under a window with the cord hanging across the glass.

He took the transformer down, shortened the cord and fixed it to the frame out of the way.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A bell cord across a window is a bell nobody rings in the dark. Bells in that block are hung like that, and one of them has been a door a person has stopped using for four years.**"

**Thirty-eight pounds is the sum of that Monday's four, exact.**

---

**That Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the ring binder never came off the back shelf, and no figure for the page inside it is printed here or in any file this pass wrote.**
