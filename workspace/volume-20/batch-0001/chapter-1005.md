# Chapter 1005 — He Wrote It And Could Not Finish

**A Friday was a bench until noon and a counter until four and then four converted units off a service road, and a man sat at the back of that shop at about half past four with a docket upside down in front of him and four lines of pencil on it, and did not finish the fifth line before the shutter came down.**

---

That Friday took ten callers through that shop and ten names were written against it as each of them came in, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and fifty-nine days, two hundred and sixty-five weeks and four days. The sheet on that passage wall was thirty-two days old that Friday, the second printing was eighteen days old, and the four words were nine days old and had stopped being what the woman at the desk said.**

---

**He had taken a docket off the pad and turned it round and written on the back of it, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that they read the four lines upside down while he was at the counter and that nobody at the counter could tell what the four lines were about.**

Marek said: **there is no room for it on the front, so I have taken a docket.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **a docket is not paper for that.**

Marek said: **it is paper.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **it is paper with a number on the other side.**

Marek said: **then it will stay a docket.**

---

**And then he put his pen down on the four lines and turned the docket about so that the man of about thirty-three could see it, and about four people in that shop have said that the man did not read it.**

Marek said: **read that and tell me whether any of it is a sentence about that room.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **no.**

Marek said: **why not.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **because there are four lines and no verb in three of them, and a thing that cannot finish itself is not a description, it is a man not knowing.**

---

**And then Marek said the thing he had worked out, and he said it at that counter out loud, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that it was the first time anybody had said it in there and that nobody argued with any of it.**

Marek said: **a sheet says a door. A woman at a desk says a form. Both of those are true and only one of them is on paper, and paper does not lose, so in about four weeks the only version anybody will have heard twice will be the one nobody wrote down.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **that is not a mystery. That is a queue forming.**

Marek said: **no. It is a queue forming round a thing that does not exist, and the queue is the only part anybody can feel.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **and you printed the door.**

Marek said: **I printed the door.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three said the thing about the three, and he said it the way he says it, and it is the fourth time in this matter that it has been said in front of a person who came for nothing.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **I am one of three people holding what she said on that Friday, and I am not going to say which of us has it wrong.**

Marek said: **you have said that three times.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **I have said it three times and I will say it again, and I will not say which, and that is the last of it.**

Marek said: **you could tell me whether it is me.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **no. It is not you. You have never held it.**

Marek said: **I know I have never held it.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **you know that because I have told you twice. I am telling you one thing that is not mine to hold and I am keeping the other.**

---

**And then Marek asked him the question he had gone round four days to ask, and it was about four words long, and the man answered it without sitting down.**

Marek said: **what would you do about it.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **that depends on whether you want it to stop or you want it to be true.**

Marek said: **I do not know which.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **then you will do whichever is cheaper, and it will not be the same thing twice, and you will not say out loud which one you did.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and about four people read four lines upside down while a man at that counter could not finish the fifth, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its seventy-first night.**

---

**One shop, that yard and that kitchen took the last of that Friday, and four jobs went into them.**

A ceiling rose in that kitchen had been fitted to a junction that is not on the circuit, with two cables into a terminal block and nothing else.

He took the rose off, found the circuit it belonged to, re-terminated both and proved it.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**Two cables into a block is somebody who did not know which circuit they were on. Roses in that street are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had a pendant up and down four times.**"

A socket in that shop had been put in with the earth going to the sheath of the flex and nothing at all to the box.

He took the sheath off, put a proper earth in and proved it with an instrument.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**An earth that is a sheath is no earth at all. Sockets in that row are like it, and one of them has been a counter somebody has had two different men look at for about two years.**"

A light in that yard had been put on an old lead with the insulation gone in one place and the conductor showing.

He cut it back, made a proper joint and proved the light with the lamp in.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**Bare conductor under a lamp is a lamp waiting for a damp night. Lights in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard a man has been afraid to touch for about four years.**"

A bell in that second room had been left hanging off a nail with the wires twisted together and nothing connected to anything.

He took it down, put it back on the board and proved it.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A bell on a nail is a bell somebody gave up on. Bells in that block are like it, and one of them has been a door a person has decided not to use for four years.**"

**Forty-three pounds is the sum of that Friday's four, exact.**

---

**That Friday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the ring binder stayed on the shelf at the back of it, and nothing whatever is entered on this page for what that binder holds.**

---

*1008.
Friday of week 333, at ten. That is the two hundred and fiftieth day of this stretch of days. Ten callers came into that shop on that Friday, and ten names were written against that day's book before they went.
**That Friday carried no figure of this matter in a voice anywhere in that city, and a man took a docket off a pad and wrote four lines on the back of it and could not finish the fifth. Forty-three pounds, exact.*

*Conditions of the close.* **That Friday finished with the book in the green binding shut on its table and the tin beside it under its lid, and this page prints no figure against either.
That ninth chair stood with its back to that room at both ends of that Friday and did not turn, and no page in this volume names whoever last turned it.
The room under a building in a first district was dark at ten that Friday and is dark now, and has not been opened since the day it was made.
Four stood on that register of correct acts that changed nothing at both ends of that Friday, and nobody in this city enters it, and writing four lines on the back of a docket is not on it.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects are named below and each of them stands on its own. Two nicks cut into that rail. A card about the size of a hand, three questions on it and no heading, creased once across the middle of it. A board up on two nails. Nine corners torn off nine fronts. The tin with its lid down. The shutter. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back of that shelf. A slip with a date on it. A pencil lying in a margin.
The cage at one side of that counter is outside the ten, and so is a request in a second district's drawer with one name struck out and one written under it. A page bearing nine names lies outside all of them and is given in no count here, and this page does not write it out.
A dated rule stands over a question that is not this page's, and a person standing in front of it may read whatever stands behind that rule.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody. A man wrote four lines on the back of a docket and could not finish, and a man told him that a thing which cannot finish itself is not a description, and neither of them said what would happen next.**
