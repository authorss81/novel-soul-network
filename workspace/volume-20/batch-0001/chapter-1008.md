# Chapter 1008 — The Wage Envelope Was Thinner

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a passage off a clinic in a fourth district, where a man with a ladder and two drawing pins had been waiting about four weeks to be told to take a sheet of paper off a wall, and was told to on that Tuesday, and was paid on that Tuesday, and asked nothing about it.**

---

That Tuesday took twelve callers through that shop and twelve names were written against the day before any of them went, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and sixty-three days, two hundred and sixty-six weeks and one day. The sheet on that passage wall was thirty-six days old that Tuesday, the second printing was twenty-two days old and had never been on that wall, and the four words were thirteen days old.**

---

**He went out at about half past six with a folded wage envelope in his coat and the man with the ladder was already at that passage, sitting on his own toolbox, and about four people who use that passage have said since that he had been coming for about four weeks and had never once asked.**

A man of about forty-four said: **you have come about the wall.**

Marek said: **I have.**

A man of about forty-four said: **which one.**

Marek said: **the one low, by the two drawing pins.**

A man of about forty-four said: **there is only one.**

Marek said: **there is one there. I want that one and nothing else.**

---

**And then the man asked the thing he had been not asking for about four weeks, and Marek told him, and about four people who use that passage have said since that it was about nine words and that the man did not agree with any of it.**

A man of about forty-four said: **what do I tell people who ask me.**

Marek said: **nothing.**

A man of about forty-four said: **they will ask me.**

Marek said: **then they will ask you.**

A man of about forty-four said: **they have been asking me since the week it went up and I have told them a man paid for it. That is a name to a passer-by.**

Marek said: **it is not a name.**

A man of about forty-four said: **it is a man in a shop. That is enough to be found by.**

---

**And he counted it out on the counter of that shop in fives when he got back and neither of them said it out loud, and about four people who come to that shop have said that the envelope was thinner than the one he had brought down to the counter and that he put the empty one in a drawer and shut it.**

A man of about forty-four said: **you have paid me to take a piece of paper off a wall.**

Marek said: **yes.**

A man of about forty-four said: **I have taken about four hundred of these off walls and I have never had anybody pay me for it before.**

Marek said: **then you will come back on Thursday and take it down and I will not be there.**

A man of about forty-four said: **why will you not be there.**

Marek said: **because if I am there it will be a man watching a sheet of paper come down, and I do not want about four people telling that.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty-three came in at about half past seven and stood at the customer side of that counter, and about four people who come to that shop have said that he had been there before Marek got back and had waited.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **you have had it off.**

Marek said: **I have paid for it. It comes down on Thursday.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **one of the three of us has stopped saying it.**

Marek said: **which one.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **no. And I will not say which, and I am not going to say whether it was the same word or a different one.**

---

**And then he said the thing about the wall, and Marek said it was not his to answer and the man said it was not the wall that was the problem, and about four people in that shop have said since that the till went on twice through the whole of it.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **you have taken the only paper off that wall and you have not put anything where it was.**

Marek said: **there is nothing to put there that I am willing to print.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **there is a hole where a thing was, and holes get filled by whoever comes along with a leaflet.**

Marek said: **then it fills with a leaflet.**

A man of about thirty-three said: **and it fills faster than a door did, and you will not know whose it is.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man was paid in fives at a counter and said no figure out loud, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its seventy-fourth night.**

---

**What was left of that Tuesday went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and it was four jobs and nothing else.**

A consumer unit in that shop had two circuits on one neutral bar and no way of telling which was which.

He took the bar out, split the neutrals properly and proved both circuits.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**One bar for two circuits is two circuits that trip together or not at all. Boards in that row are like that, and one of them has been a shop somebody has had to run with the lights for about two years.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in on flex that had been extended with a strip of copper and a twist.

He cut it back, made a proper joint and proved the lamp.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**Flex extended with copper is flex that has been repaired by somebody who wanted it to stop sparking. Flex in that yard is like it, and one of them has been a yard a man has been watching for four years.**"

A socket in that kitchen had been put in above a worktop where the wall behind it was hollow and the box had nothing to fix to.

He took it out, cut the wall properly and fixed the box solid.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A box with nothing behind it is a socket that moves when you plug in. Sockets in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a worktop somebody has been frightened of for about two years.**"

A cooker hood in that second room had been wired to a switch inside the cupboard behind it.

He moved the switch out to the front and proved the hood.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A hood switched from inside its own cupboard is a hood that gets cleaned when the cupboard is cleaned. Fittings in that block are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to move a box to reach for about four years.**"

**Forty-seven pounds is the sum of that Tuesday's four, exact.**

---

**That Tuesday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the binder at the back of that shelf did not move, and nothing at all is entered here for what that binder holds.**
