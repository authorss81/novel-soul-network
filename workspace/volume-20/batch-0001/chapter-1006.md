# Chapter 1006 — A Sunday In That Room

**A Sunday was a counter from about ten and then that room with the shutter down at about two, and about four people were in it, and every one of them had been in it on another Sunday, and not one of them had been in it on a Thursday.**

---

Four callers came through that shop on that Sunday between about ten and about one, and four names went onto that day's line, and the last of them was let away at about a quarter to one, and that shutter came down at about two.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and sixty-one days, two hundred and sixty-five weeks and six days. The sheet on that passage wall was thirty-four days old that Sunday, the second printing was twenty days old, and the four words were eleven days old.**

---

**He went up those stairs at about half past two on a Sunday, which is not when he goes, and about four people were in that room and about four of them were already in it when he came in, and he was the only thing in that room that had happened that day.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have come at the wrong time.**

Marek said: **I know. I wanted to see it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there is nothing to see on a Sunday.**

Marek said: **there is a difference between this and a Thursday and I have not seen it before.**

---

**And he asked her how many people came on a Sunday, and she gave him the figure without arguing about it, and about four people who come to that room have said since that she has never once been asked it in six years.**

Marek said: **how many come on a Sunday.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **about four.**

Marek said: **the same four.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **the same four. Two of them have been coming since before you were in that shop.**

Marek said: **have any of them ever been here on a Thursday.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no. Not one.**

---

**And then he asked the other thing, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the only question asked on that Sunday and that it was asked at about ten past three.**

Marek said: **does anybody say anything in here about what this room is.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no.**

Marek said: **not at all.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **not on a Sunday. They sit. Two of them talk about a bus route and one of them talks about a knee and I do the book, which is nothing.**

Marek said: **the book.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is a book. I do not read anything out of it and I have not read anything out of it since the winter.**

---

**And then he went back down that stair and across the city on a bus of about half an hour and stood in a passage off a clinic in a fourth district at about half past four on a Sunday, and there was nobody in the passage at all, and about four people who use that passage have said since that the sheet was still up by its two drawing pins and that the clinic door was shut and that nobody was waiting.**

Marek said: **nothing out.**

Nobody said anything, and he stayed about ten minutes, and about four people who use that passage have said that he stayed the whole ten minutes and did not touch the sheet.

---

**And then he did the thing he had been doing for about four years, which is that he stood somewhere with his hands in his pockets until he had worked out which of two things was cheaper, and about four people in that shop have said that he came in on the Tuesday and told nobody what he had worked out.**

Nobody thanked anybody on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and the shutter came down at about two and not at about ten, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its seventy-second night.

---

**A bell in a hall had its push mounted on the outside of the wall of the flat it belonged to, so that anybody inside rang somebody else's door.**

He took the push off the wall, put it inside and proved it.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A push on the wrong side of a wall is a bell the next door answers. About four of those fittings in that hall are outside the flat, and one of them has been a door that has gone off in the night for about four years.**"

A socket in that kitchen had its face painted over two or three times with the paint built up round the pin holes.

He cut a new face, refitted it and proved it.

"**Four pounds,**" he said. "**A painted socket face is a socket that has to be forced. About four of those faces in that kitchen have been painted, and one of them has been a plug a man has had to prise out with a knife for about four years.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in with a holder that turned a full quarter in the ceiling rose.

He changed the holder, tightened the rose and proved the lamp.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**A holder that turns in the rose is a lamp that gets knocked every time somebody reaches past it. Lamps in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard somebody has been reaching over for about two years.**"

A stair light in that second room had been wired through a switch on the wrong floor of the stairs so that it came on one step out.

He took the switch across to the floor it belonged to and proved both landings.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A light that comes on one step out is a light a person stands in the dark waiting for. Switches in that block are like it, and one of them has been a stair a woman has come down in the dark for about four years.**"

**Twenty pounds is what those four came to on that Sunday, exact.**

---

**That Sunday the fourth of those four rooms was shut by half past one with a woman of about thirty behind that door, and the binder on the shelf at the back of that room did not come off it, and no figure for what that binder holds is entered anywhere in this file.**
