# Chapter 1007 — He Told Her Before He Paid

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off a service road, and about nine minutes on foot to a first floor off a line in Saltmarket, where a man told a woman what he was going to do with a sheet of paper on a wall before he had done it and before he had paid for it.**

---

That Monday took six callers through that shop and six names were entered onto that day's line, and the last of them was let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and sixty-two days, two hundred and sixty-six weeks to the day. The sheet on that passage wall was thirty-five days old that Monday, the second printing was twenty-one days old, and the four words were twelve days old and had been in about four mouths since before any of it was written down.**

---

**He went up at about half past six and did not sit down and did not stand in the middle of that floor, and about four people in that room have said since that he stayed by the door the whole time, which is not what he did the last four times.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you have not sat down.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you stood in the middle of that floor the last four times.**

Marek said: **I am not doing anything this time. I am telling you something and then I am going to go down the stairs.**

---

**And then he told her, and about four people in that room have said since that she turned round when he started and that she did not turn round back.**

Marek said: **there is a sheet on a wall in a fourth district with a door on it and no list, and I paid for it, and I am going to have it taken down.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **by whom.**

Marek said: **by the man who put it up. He has a ladder and two drawing pins and he has been asking me for about four weeks whether anybody was going to tell him to take it down.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you have not told him.**

Marek said: **I am telling you first.**

---

**And then about nine seconds went by in that room and about four people have said that she noticed the order of it before she said anything about the paper at all.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you told me first.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you told me after you had paid and about four weeks after I told you that you had paid.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you know I would have said no.**

Marek said: **I know. I am telling you anyway, because I am not doing it for you and I would rather you had the chance to say no to a thing that is mine.**

---

**And then he asked her the only question he had come up with, and he asked it plainly and he did not dress it, and about four people in that room have said since that she answered it in about nine words.**

Marek said: **do you want it down.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have never wanted a sheet up or down in six years and that is the third time I have told you so.**

Marek said: **I know it is the third.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then ask me the next thing.**

---

**And then he gave her the reason out loud, in a room with about nine people in it, and it took about nine words, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the first time in about four years that anything in that room had been said for a reason anybody could check.**

Marek said: **a sheet that names a room nobody can describe tells a rumour where to go, and I did that.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you did not know that when you paid for it.**

Marek said: **no. I have known for about four days.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then you have been carrying it about four days.**

Marek said: **yes.**

---

**And then she asked the one question that was hers, and about four people in that room have said since that the room went quiet after it and that nobody answered it for a while.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **are you going to put something else up.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **nothing at all.**

Marek said: **nothing at all.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then about four people who know where to go are going to know nothing at all by Friday, and you know that better than anybody in this city.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and you are going to do it on a Monday morning and tell me on a Monday morning.**

Marek said: **yes.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man told a woman what he was going to do with a sheet of paper before he paid for it and she said she would have said no and he said he knew, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on a table in that shop for its seventy-third night.**

---

**That Monday's last two hours went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and four jobs went into them, each priced before it was begun.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate that was the wrong pattern for the box behind it and had been forced.

He took the plate off, cut a proper one to the box and proved it with an instrument.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A forced plate is a plate that has already been round once too many. Plates in that block are like it, and one of them has been a wall somebody has taken things off four times.**"

A lamp in that shop had been put in with a flex that had been joined with a block connector behind the fitting and left accessible.

He took it apart, made the joint inside and proved the lamp.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A block connector behind a fitting is somebody's temporary that became permanent. Fittings in that row are like it, and one of them has been a shop somebody has been meaning to finish for about two years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had its outlet strip plugged into another outlet strip under the worktop.

He took one of them out, put the appliances on one and proved the whole run.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A strip plugged into a strip is a lead that is warm behind a cupboard. Strips in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had a hot shelf for about four years.**"

A light in that second room had been switched from a switch on the landing and nobody knew which landing.

He traced it, took it off the landing and put it inside and proved it.

"**Nine pounds,**" he said. "**A light on the wrong landing is a light that somebody turns on for somebody else. Switches in that block are like it, and one of them has been a room a person has had to go out of to be able to see.**"

**Thirty-six pounds is the sum of that Monday's four, exact.**

---

**That Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the ring binder did not come off the back shelf of that room, and this page prints no figure for anything that binder holds.**
