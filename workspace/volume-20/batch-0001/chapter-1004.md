# Chapter 1004 — People Stopping At A Wall

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then a bus of about twenty minutes to a passage off a clinic in a fourth district, where a sheet of paper had been low on a wall by two drawing pins for thirty-one days and about nine people stopped in front of it in an hour and not one of them went anywhere afterwards.**

---

That Thursday took eight callers through that shop and eight names were put against it before they went, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight days, two hundred and sixty-five weeks and three days. The sheet on that passage wall was thirty-one days old that Thursday, and the second printing was seventeen days old, and he was standing in front of the first of them with his hands in his pockets.**

---

**He had gone to look at it and not to take it down, and about four people who use that passage have said since that he stood at the far end of it for about an hour and read it from about nine feet away, which is further than a person reads anything they mean.**

A woman of about sixty said: **you have not touched it.**

Marek said: **no.**

A woman of about sixty said: **people have been touching it.**

Marek said: **what do they do.**

A woman of about sixty said: **they put a coat against the bottom so the wind does not get it.**

---

**And about four people in that passage said the four words to each other while he was standing there, and neither of them knew he was there, and about four of them have said since that he did not say anything.**

A woman of about forty-eight said: **that is where you go about the form.**

A man of about twenty-two said: **the fifth column.**

The woman of about forty-eight said: **the fifth column is blank every time and you go again.**

The man of about twenty-two said: **so you go back to her.**

The woman of about forty-eight said: **she is not it. Nobody there is it.**

---

**And then the woman of about forty-eight turned round and found him and asked him, and she asked it in the middle of that passage with about six people standing in it, and about four of them have said since that he answered her properly and that properly was not an answer.**

The woman of about forty-eight said: **you are the one who put it up.**

Marek said: **I paid for it. A man with a ladder and two drawing pins put it up and I told him to put it low.**

The woman of about forty-eight said: **then what is it for.**

Marek said: **I cannot tell you that.**

The woman of about forty-eight said: **you can tell me what is on it.**

Marek said: **a door and that anybody may come.**

The woman of about forty-eight said: **and what they get.**

Marek said: **I have been in that room and I still do not have an answer that would survive being said out loud here.**

---

**And then three people asked him for something at the top of that sheet, and he told them there was nothing at the top of it, and about four people in that passage have said since that nobody put anything there and that nobody went away still asking twice.**

A man of about thirty-one said: **there is no name on it.**

Marek said: **there is nothing at the top of it. I told the man with the ladder to leave it off and he thought I meant the top half.**

A man of about thirty-one said: **that reads as a mistake.**

Marek said: **it reads as what it is.**

A woman of about thirty-one said: **it does not. It reads as a thing somebody could not finish.**

Marek said: **I know.**

A woman of about thirty-one said: **then somebody has to finish it.**

Marek said: **not me and not today.**

---

**And then he noticed that the second hundred and fifty had never come to that wall at all, and that the rumour had got to that passage without it, and about four people who use that passage have said since that he said that part out loud and then said the other part out loud and that the two parts did not go together.**

Marek said: **nobody in this passage has seen the second one.**

A woman of about sixty said: **we have not.**

Marek said: **and every one of you knows about that room.**

A woman of about sixty said: **I know about it from a woman at a counter, and so does my sister, and neither of us has been up that stair in our lives.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and about nine people stopped at a sheet of paper in a passage in a fourth district and about four of them said four words to each other inside a quarter of an hour, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on a table in a shop across this city for its seventieth night.**

---

**What that Thursday had left went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and there were four jobs in it and not one more.**

A lamp in that shop had been wired into the ring and had no earth at all on it.

He took it off the ring, earthed it off the box and proved it with an instrument.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp on the ring with no earth is a lamp somebody has had a shock off in a wet week. Lamps in that shop are like it, and one of them has been a counter a man has had to hold by about two years.**"

A switch in that yard had been put in on a cable that had been cut long and then joined with a terminal block outside.

He cut it back, made a proper joint inside and proved it with the load on.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A joint made outside in the weather is a joint that is wet before it is tight. Switches in that block are like that, and one of them has been a yard somebody has had to keep an eye on for four years.**"

A pull switch in that kitchen had been put above a splashback over the sink where it got wet every time it was used.

He moved it to the frame beside the door and proved it from there.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A pull switch over a sink is a switch a person hesitates to touch with wet hands. Switches in that kitchen are like it, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to dry her hands on a tea towel for about two years.**"

An outside light in that second room had a flex that had been shortened twice and was now about two inches long off the rose.

He took it out, made the flex up properly and proved the light.

"**Seven pounds,**" he said. "**A flex cut short twice is a flex somebody has fixed by cutting it. Lights in that room are like that, and one of them has been a landing a person has had to feel for in the dark for four years.**"

**Thirty pounds is the sum of that Thursday's four, exact.**

---

**That Thursday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the ring binder was still where it is, and this page sets no figure against anything that binder holds.**
