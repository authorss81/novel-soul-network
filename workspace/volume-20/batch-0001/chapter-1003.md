# Chapter 1003 — She Asked For A Form

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off a service road, and about nine minutes on foot to a first floor off a line in Saltmarket, where about sixteen people were in a room that holds nine chairs, and nobody in that room told a woman of about thirty-four what there was instead of a form.**

---

That Wednesday took eleven callers through that shop and eleven names were entered onto that day's line, and the last of them was let away at about a quarter to ten, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and fifty-seven days, two hundred and sixty-five weeks and two days. The sheet on that passage wall was thirty days old that Wednesday, and the second printing was sixteen days old, and the four words were seven days into about four mouths.**

---

**She came in at about half past seven with a folder under her arm and she did not sit down, and about four people in that room have said since that she asked the first person she spoke to and that the first person she spoke to was not the woman who keeps it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **is there a form.**

A woman of about forty said: **no.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **a woman at a desk told me this is where you go to be told about a form.**

The woman of about forty said: **not from me.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **it was not from you. She said so herself. She said she was only saying what everybody says.**

---

**And then she asked the woman of about fifty-four, in front of about sixteen people, and the woman of about fifty-four answered her in about nine words and did not say what the room was for.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **what do people do here.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **nobody here can tell you what it is for, and that is not me being careful. I do not know.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **you have kept this room six years.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **and in six years nobody has written it down.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no.**

---

**And then a man of about twenty-six at the back said the thing about the folder that about four people in that room have said since was the reason the rest of it happened, and he said it to the floor.**

A man of about twenty-six said: **there is a form about her in that folder. She has been to that desk twice and she has a page with columns ruled on it and nothing written in the fifth.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **the fifth is blank on mine as well.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then you have brought me two of them and I am the wrong person and there is nobody else on that floor I can hand you to.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **what do I do with a blank.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is a question for whoever printed it and there is nobody in this room who printed it.**

---

**And then Marek asked her, standing up, what the four words were, and she would not give them to him, and about four people in that room have said since that she said why in one go and that she did not lower her voice about it.**

Marek said: **what did she say to you.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I am not going to say them to you.**

Marek said: **I am not going to write them down.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **the last person I said them to wrote them on a form, and now there are two of us saying the same thing and neither of us said it to the other one.**

Marek said: **that is not what I asked.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **it is the only part of it that matters and you can have it or not.**

---

**And then Marek asked the woman of about fifty-four, out loud, in that room, whether the four words could be printed wrong on purpose so that the first version would be the one anybody had, and about four people in that room have said since that the woman of about fifty-four did not answer him for long enough for it to be noticed.**

Marek said: **could somebody print what she said and print that it is wrong.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **by whom.**

Marek said: **there is a building in a second district that prints things and the man in it has done it twice for me already.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and what would he put on it.**

Marek said: **what you told me. Only turned round.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have told you nothing. And if I did, it would be the first thing anybody in this city has ever written down about this room, and it would be in a handwriting that has never been in this building.**

---

**And behind the chair of the woman of about fifty-four there was a space with nothing standing in it and a mark on the wall where something had stood on it, and Marek did the sum standing up with his hands behind his back and it came to seven hundred and thirty-five days, which is one hundred and five weeks to the day, and he did not say it out loud and nobody asked him for it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and about sixteen people were in a room that holds nine chairs and a woman of about thirty-four went out of it at about a quarter to eight with the same folder she came in with, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on a table in that shop for its sixty-ninth night.**

---

**The rest of that Wednesday went into that shop, that yard and that kitchen, and it came to four jobs and no fifth.**

A consumer unit in that shop had been fitted with a rewireable fuse and a piece of flex where the earth should have been.

He took the fuse out, put an earth in where the flex had been and proved the board dead.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**Flex where an earth should be is a board that will take anybody's fault. Boards in that row are like that, and one of them has been a counter somebody has had a different van look at for about two years.**"

A lamp in that yard had been hung off a length of chain that was not fixed to anything above.

He took it down, made a fixing for it on the beam and proved it with a weight on it.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp on a chain that is fixed to nothing is a lamp somebody has reached up and steadied for four years. Lamps in that yard are like it, and one of them has been a yard a man has been holding a ladder under for about two years.**"

A socket in that kitchen had been put in on a lead that came off a junction box with its cover off and no dead ends on it.

He fitted the cover, made off the spare ways and proved the box.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A junction with its cover off is a box somebody has been reaching into with a screwdriver. Boxes in that street are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to tidy before anybody came in for about four years.**"

A bell push in that second room had been fitted to a bell that is not on the circuit it says it is on.

He found the real one, took the push off the wrong one and proved it.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A push on the wrong bell is a door somebody has stopped answering. Bells in that block are like it, and one of them has been a front door a person has let their visitors wait at for four years.**"

**Forty-one pounds is the sum of that Wednesday's four, exact.**

---

**That Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the binder did not come down off its shelf, and the page inside that binder has no figure printed for it anywhere in this file.**
