# Chapter 1002 — She Did Not Use It Once

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off a service road, and a woman came into that shop at about half past seven with a bag of other people's washing in it and stayed until the shutter was half down, and neither of them said why she had come.**

---

That Tuesday took nine callers through that shop and nine names went onto that day's line, none of them out of order, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand eight hundred and fifty-six days, two hundred and sixty-five weeks and one day. The sheet on that passage wall was twenty-nine days old that Tuesday, the second printing was fifteen days old, and the four words had been in about four mouths for six days.**

---

**She had come off a shift and she put the bag down by the stool and did not take it up again, and about four people who come to that shop have said since that she sat on the customer side, which she has not done in about four months.**

Talia said: **you look like a man who has been told something he cannot put down.**

Marek said: **a man told me on Monday at that counter that his sister-in-law thinks a room off a line in Saltmarket is where you go to be told about a form.**

Talia said: **and he was not lying to you.**

Marek said: **no. That is the part I have not got past. He believed it.**

Talia said: **so somebody said it to him, and he believed her, and that is two people between you and a desk in a first district.**

---

**And then she gave him the thing he did not ask for, and she gave it the way a person hands over a coat, and about four people in that shop have said since that she did it before he had finished standing still.**

Talia said: **there are four desks in that building where a woman fills in a form in columns for somebody else. I could have the names of everybody on that floor inside a week and I have not asked for them and I am not going to.**

Marek said: **you have said that before you have been asked.**

Talia said: **I have said it twice in six weeks now and I would like that noticed.**

Marek said: **noticed.**

Talia said: **do not say it back to me like that.**

---

**And then about nine seconds went by at that counter with the till on and the bag of washing on the floor, and about four people who come to that shop have said that neither of them looked at it.**

Marek said: **why not.**

Talia said: **because you did not ask, and I want to be careful about what I do with a thing I was not asked for.**

Marek said: **I could have asked.**

Talia said: **you could. I have been ready since about half past six.**

Marek said: **I know you have.**

Talia said: **then you know what it costs you, and I am not going to say it out loud in a shop.**

---

**And then she put something of her own on that counter, and she put it down carefully and she did not push it, and about four people who come to that shop have said that he did not touch it.**

Talia said: **a man came to the clinic on Friday with a bad foot and he told me, in about four minutes while I was doing his dressing, what that room is for. His four words are not the same four words your man had.**

Marek said: **no. Only one of them is hers.**

Talia said: **no. And I am not going to give you his, and I am not going to tell you his name, and if you go looking for a man with a bad foot on a Friday you will find him and he will tell you, because he does not know he said anything.**

Marek said: **you cannot stop that.**

Talia said: **no. I can only not hand it to you, and I have not handed it to you.**

---

**And then she said the thing she came to say, and it was about her and not about him, and she said it standing with her hand on the bag of washing and did not pick the bag up.**

Talia said: **I have been asked twice this week by people who are not in my care what that room is for, and I have said the same four words both times, and I have said them to a shopkeeper and to a woman I have never met.**

Marek said: **what four words.**

Talia said: **the ones you have. No. I am not going to say them now that I know they are moving.**

Marek said: **you have already said them twice.**

Talia said: **yes, and one of those was yesterday, and that is the one I am not able to get back.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and two people said four words to each other across a counter in a shop and one of them was on her way to somebody who is not in her care, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its sixty-eighth night.**

---

**That Tuesday's evening went into one shop, one yard and one of those two rooms, and four jobs went into them.**

A socket in that yard had been put in on a lead that came off a joint under a slab with no inspection box over it anywhere.

He lifted two slabs, dug the joint out, made it good and laid the slabs back.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A joint under a slab with no box on it is a joint you find by digging. Runs in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a yard somebody has put a new shed over for about two years.**"

A lamp in that second room had its switch on a wall opposite the door so that anybody standing in the doorway put their own back to it.

He took the switch across to the frame of the door and proved both points.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A switch opposite a door is a switch somebody turns on behind themselves. Switches in that room are like that, and one of them has been a room a man has had to back into for four years.**"

A heater in that shop had been stood on a mat on a wooden floor with its lead going under a skirting board.

He took the lead up, put it in a proper surface box and proved the heater.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A lead under a skirting is a lead somebody has nailed down once and thinks of as fixed. Leads in that shop are all under something, and one of them has been a counter somebody has swept against for about two years.**"

An outside light in that kitchen had a flex that came through the wall with a hole cut round it and no sleeve at all.

He sleeved the hole, made it good and proved the light.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A flex through a hole cut for it is a flex that gets wet at the edge. Lights in that street are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had a lamp stand in the rain for four years.**"

**Thirty-seven pounds is what that Tuesday's four came to, exact.**

---

**That Tuesday shut the fourth of those four rooms from half past six, and a woman of about thirty stood behind its door, and the binder stayed at the back of that shelf where it has been, and no figure for what it holds is printed anywhere on this page.**

*What the day did not settle, and the rest of it.* **Ten objects go by under this one and are not brought together in it. Two nicks in that rail. A card about the size of a hand, creased once across the middle of it, with three questions on it and no heading. A board hanging by two nails. Nine corners torn off nine fronts. The tin with its lid down. The shutter. The book in the green binding. The binder at the back of that shelf. A slip with a date on it. A pencil in a margin.
The cage along one edge of that counter is out of all ten, and so is a request in a second district's drawer in which one name has been struck out and one written beneath it. Outside the ten, and printed nowhere on this page, is a page bearing nine names.
A dated rule stands above a question belonging to somebody else, and whatever is filed behind it may be read by any person who comes and asks for it.
Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody. A woman who could have had four names inside a week said twice, unasked, that she would not ask, and said four words out loud yesterday to a stranger who is not in her care, and would not take them back.**
