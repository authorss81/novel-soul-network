# Chapter 1051 — He Got The Rest Of It Out Loud

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then that shop until ten, and a man behind that counter said out loud, to a woman carrying a folder and to about four other people standing in that shop, the rest of a thing he has been carrying since the Friday.**

---

Eight callers came into that shop on that Monday and eight names were written against that Monday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter was down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and thirty-nine days, two hundred and seventy-seven weeks to the day. What is in the bag under the long bench at the back of that shop is a hundred and twelve days old, the printing nobody has looked for is ninety-eight days old, and the four words were eighty-nine days old.**

---

**He waited until the woman of about thirty-four had put the folder down on the counter and the man of about thirty had come in and stood at the end of it, and about four people in that shop have said since that he waited for both of them because he had decided to.**

Marek said: **I have got the rest of it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **you said on Friday that you had not.**

Marek said: **I have got it now and I am saying it to you in a shop and not in that room, and I am going to say it in that room.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **say it here first then, and say all of it, because I am not going to be in that room on a Wednesday having only had the half of it.**

---

**And he said it, out loud, in one go and without stopping, and about four people standing in that shop heard the whole of it, and the man of about thirty was one of the four and said nothing at all.**

Marek said: **I want a person who cannot answer that last one asked to leave, and not let in and left sitting there.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **asked to leave.**

Marek said: **asked. And going, and not being told he could stay.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **that is the whole of what you have been thinking since Friday.**

Marek said: **that is all of it and I have not got a reason for it.**

---

**And then nobody in that shop said anything for about nine minutes, and about four people in that shop have said since that he waited through the whole of it without asking anybody whether he had said it properly.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **you have not got a reason and you have got a want, and a want without a reason is still a want, and I am not going to be the one who tells you which of the two it is.**

Marek said: **I know which one it is. I said want deliberately and I could have said need.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **Then say it in that room and not in here, because in here it has been four people and a counter and in there it is nine people and a bench.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four asked him one question about it, out loud, and he answered it in one go, and about four people who have been in that shop have said since that she asked it flat and got nothing back at all.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **have you told him.**

Marek said: **no, and I am not going to, and he is going to hear it for the first time in a room with about nine people in it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **that is the cruellest version of that sentence anybody could have picked.**

Marek said: **it is the only one that is honest. If I tell him on a Monday in a shop he gets a day to get used to it, and then it stops being what I actually want, which is that he finds out the same time as everybody else.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty said two sentences at the door on his way out, and about four people in that shop have said since that he had said nothing at all for about nine minutes and then said both of those things at once.**

The man of about thirty said: **what day.**

Marek said: **Wednesday.**

The man of about thirty said: **I will be here. I have not said the other thing and I am not going to say it tonight.**

Marek said: **I know you have not.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man said out loud that he wanted a person asked to leave and gave no reason for it, and a woman told him it was the cruellest version of the sentence anybody could have picked, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and fifty-first night.**

---

**The four jobs that Monday went into that shop, that landing, that kitchen and that yard, and they came to sixty-four pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a plate with the earth taken off it and the metal box left open behind.

He closed it, brought an earth in and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A plate with an open box behind it is a plate waiting for a pair of wet hands. Plates in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a counter about four of them have had to wipe before they could shut a drawer.**"

A light on that landing had been put in on a fitting held on by two screws that had gone into a mortar joint.

He found solid, cut two new seats and proved the light with the door shut.

"**Nineteen pounds,**" he said. "**Two screws into a mortar joint is a fitting that shakes when anybody walks past it. Fittings on landings are like that, and one of them has been a stair about four of them have had to hold the bulb with two hands.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a hob element that had been given a spur off another ring.

He took the spur off, re-terminated the element and proved that ring on its own.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**A ring borrowing from another ring is a ring somebody has had to cook round for about four years. Hob elements in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to time a pan instead of watching it.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in under a downpipe with the lid facing up so that water ran into it.

He turned the lid, sealed the joint and proved the socket from the gate.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**A lid facing up under a pipe is a socket that fills and then does something. Sockets in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a wall about four of them have had to put a bucket under in a storm.**"

**Sixty-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was where it has been for about four years, and this file sets nothing whatever against the page it holds.**
