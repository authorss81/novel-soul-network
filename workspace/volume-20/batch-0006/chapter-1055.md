# Chapter 1055 — What He Said He Was Going To Ask

**That Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and about nine people went up that stair, and a man at that bench was asked by two people in front of everybody what he was going to do on a Tuesday and gave the same answer twice.**

---

Eleven callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and eleven names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter was down at ten.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and forty-eight days, two hundred and seventy-eight weeks and two days. The two hundred and fifty sheets in that bag are a hundred and twenty-one days old, the printing nobody has looked for is one hundred and seven days old, and the four words were ninety-eight days old.**

---

**She was in that room before half past six and she asked him the first thing, out loud, and about four people in that room have said since that she asked it walking to the bench and did not sit down first.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **what are you going to do on that Tuesday.**

Marek said: **I do not know.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **You told me on Friday that you had said that nine words twice this week and that the third time would be in that room. That was a plan with a date on it and a date is not a plan.**

Marek said: **that is the criticism I would have made of it myself, and I made it to a woman on Saturday and not to nine people.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Do not do that. Do not stand in that room and grade your own sentences while nine people are sitting in it.**

---

**And then he said out loud, in front of about nine people, that he was going to say it a third time on that Tuesday and then never again in that room whatever happened, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody said anything at all when he said the second half of it.**

Marek said: **I have said it in a shop and I have said it in this room. I am going to say it on that Tuesday and I am not going to say it in this room again after that, whatever happens on the Tuesday, and I am saying that now so that nobody sits here waiting for a fourth time.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **that is the first time in about four weeks that you have said out loud what you were going to stop doing.**

Marek said: **it is the reason the third time matters. If it goes on afterwards it stops being what I want and starts being what I do.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **that is the second sentence this month I have believed and I am not going to tell you about the first one.**

---

**And then he gave the man of about thirty the date out loud, in that room, in front of about nine people, and the man asked him for it and he gave it, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the fourth time anybody in this city had said that day out loud.**

The man of about thirty said: **you said Wednesday.**

Marek said: **you said Wednesday and I have not written it down. It is the Tuesday of the week after next and I am saying it now because you asked me for it in front of nine people and that is the whole of what a date in this room is.**

The man of about thirty said: **I will be here.**

Marek said: **I know you will and I have not asked you whether you are going to answer and I am not going to.**

The man of about thirty said: **that is the ninth time somebody has not asked me that and I have stopped counting them, which is not the same as stopping.**

---

**And then a man of about thirty-three said out loud, for the eighth time in this volume, that there are three of them holding a thing and one of them has it wrong, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody looked up when he said it.**

The man of about thirty said: **is that room going to keep asking that last thing after Tuesday.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **I have no idea and it is not mine to answer.**

The man of about thirty said: **You have said that four times this month and it is never the same answer twice.**

The man of about thirty-three said: **It is the same answer every time and you keep asking it in a different shape. There are three of us holding this and one of us has it wrong and the one who has it wrong does not know it, and I have said that in this room eight times and I am not going to add anything to it tonight.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four said one thing about the folder she carries, out loud, and got a refusal out of Marek that she had already been given, and about four people who have seen that folder have said since that she has never once mentioned it before a Wednesday.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **somebody stopped me in a corridor outside a clinic on Monday and asked whether I would walk them up that stair before a Tuesday.**

Marek said: **no.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I said it before you and I said it in about nine words and you did not have to be here. That is the third time and I said the same nine words and I have not opened this folder once in front of anybody and I am not going to start on a Wednesday.**

Marek said: **nobody is asking you to.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man said out loud that he would say a thing a third time and then never again in that room whatever happened, and a woman told him to stop grading his own sentences while nine people were sitting in it, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and sixtieth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Wednesday went into that shop, that gate, that kitchen and that landing, and they came to sixty-two pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with the terminal block cracked where the neutral landed on it.

He changed the block and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Nineteen pounds,**" he said. "**A cracked block is a joint that has been made and then used as a step. Blocks in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a rose about four of them have had to change a bulb and wonder why it flickered first.**"

A socket at that gate had been put in on a plate with a earth taken off it entirely and a length of copper twisted round the fixing screw.

He brought the earth into the box and proved the socket from the path.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**Copper round a fixing screw is an earth that ends the day the next lamp goes on. Plates at gates are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to test a lamp with one hand on the metal.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with its outer shell bonded to the live by a strap under the back panel.

He took the strap off, bonded the case properly and proved the cooker with the oven on.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A case bonded to the live is a cooker that is warm to touch before anybody uses it. Straps under back panels are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to put a tea towel on the back of it.**"

A light on that landing had been put in on a two-way with the strappers lifted out and dropped into the box loose.

He put them back across the pairs and proved the light from both landings.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**Strappers loose in a box are two-way switches that are whichever landing you tried last. Switches on landings are like that, and one of them has been a stair about four of them have had to shout up a dark stairwell and mean it as a question.**"

**Sixty-two pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and nothing on the back of that shelf was disturbed, and no figure for the page that binder holds is written in this file.**
