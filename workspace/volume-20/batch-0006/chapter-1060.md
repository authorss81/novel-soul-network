# Chapter 1060 — Nobody Had Been Asked In Advance

**A Wednesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then four converted units off that service road, and in the evening about eleven people walked into a shed on a road in a fourth district with the rails still under the floor, and nobody had told any of them what they were walking into.**

---

Twelve callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and twelve names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and sixty-two days, two hundred and eighty weeks and two days. What is in the carrier bag under that long bench is a hundred and thirty-five days old and does not go back up on that wall, the printing that was never on that wall is one hundred and twenty-one days old and its home has never been named, and the four words were one hundred and twelve days old.**

---

**At about half past five, with about four people in that shop, Marek said out loud the whole of what was left of anything anybody had wanted, once, and about four people who were in that shop have said since that he said it standing at that counter and did not consult anybody first.**

Marek said: **The hundred and fifty sheets are in that bag under the long bench at the back and they are not going back up on that passage wall, and there is not going to be a third hundred of them printed by anybody in this city.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **By anybody in this city.**

Marek said: **By anybody. I have not asked a building in a second district for anything since a Wednesday in the spring and I am not going to start this week.**

---

**And then he said the rest of it out loud, once, and about four people in that shop have said since that the second half of it was about money and that he said it flatly.**

Marek said: **The second hundred and fifty of those sheets was printed by a building in a second district, it was never on that wall, it cost five more days of this shop's takings, and nobody has found where it is and I am not going to keep asking about it after today.**

The man of about thirty said: **You have asked about it in front of me about four times.**

Marek said: **That is the last of them.**

---

**And then he said one thing about a box off that road, out loud, once, and about four people in that shop have said since that it was the shortest thing he said all afternoon and that nobody asked him a follow-up to it.**

Marek said: **I went into that box off that road on Monday morning and there was nothing in it, and I am not going to say anything about where whatever was in it went.**

Nobody said anything.

---

**And then the book in the green binding came off that table at about half past five and about four people watched it come off and nobody said one word while it did, and it was shut again at about ten that night with about four people still in that shop, and nobody said anything about that either.**

**Nobody thanked anybody in that shop that afternoon and nobody forgave anybody, and four things were said out loud once each at that counter and none of them went on anything.**

---

**The day's work at that counter and in those four units had been finished by about four o'clock, and at about half past six he went across the city to a shed on a road in a fourth district with the doors off their tracks, a scarred workbench about eleven feet long at the far end of it and about nine coats on a rail, and about eleven people standing in there who had not been asked anything by anybody, and about four people who walk past that shed have said since that none of them looked like they had been expecting to be there.**

Marek said: **sit down if you want to and stand if you do not. Nobody is going to tell you what this is and I am not going to pretend there is something to be told.**

Nobody said anything.

A man of about forty said: **somebody said three things.**

Marek said: **I am going to ask you three things. One at a time, out loud, with everybody here hearing it, and you can answer all three or none of them, and if you answer none of them you can sit down and stay and nobody in this shed will say one word about it.**

A man of about forty said: **who decides.**

Marek said: **you do.**

---

**And then he asked them, one at a time and out loud, and about eleven people in that shed answered them one by one, and about four people in that shed have said since that nobody had said anything to any of them beforehand and that all of them heard every one of the three being asked.**

A woman who repairs instruments said: **what I would put in, and I would want one thing back that I do not put in, and he stops it.**

Marek said: **noted and not written.**

A man who drives a bus said: **what I would put in is about my mother's hospital, and what I keep out is about a girl at work, and she stops it.**

Marek said: **she can stop it by saying so out loud or by standing up, and both of those work, and nobody has to hold anything.**

A woman who cooks said: **what I put in is about a kitchen. What I keep out is about a man. And she stops it and she has to be allowed to be the one who stops it.**

Marek said: **that is the whole of it and I am not adding to it.**

---

**And then about nine people went, in turn, over what they would share, what they would keep out of it, and who could stop them, and about four people who were not in that shed have said since that nobody kept a count and that nothing was written down anywhere and that nobody stood at the front of it.**

Nobody kept a count.

Nothing was written down.

Nobody stood at the front of it.

A nurse of about fifty said: **who is in charge of this.**

Marek said: **nobody, and I have not asked anybody and I am not going to.**

A student of about twenty said: **who starts it.**

Marek said: **you do.**

The nurse of about fifty said: **I have been in about nine rooms in this city where one person was in charge and I have never once been asked to do anything in any of them.**

The student of about twenty said: **that is not an answer.**

Marek said: **It is the only one I have. There is no one else in this shed who is going to start it and I am not going to do it for you, and that is not a thing I am saying to be hard. It is a thing I say because the last time I started something in a room about four people came afterwards and about four of them did not.**

---

**And then a man at that bench put out his hand for a bar of steel about four feet long and asked a woman who cooks to take the other end of it off him, and about four people in that shed have said since that nobody had agreed a word beforehand and that she said one word that night for the first time.**

A man at the bench said: **hold this.**

A woman who cooks took the other end of it off him in about four seconds.

She said: **now.**

He let go.

Nobody said anything about either of those two people afterwards and neither of them is named on this page.

---

**And then the first practice in that shed began, and about four people who were not in it have said since that there was no panel on the wall, no score on anything, and no single voice that anybody could point at afterwards and say that was the one running it.**

The practice began without a panel on anything and without a score on anything.

Nobody stood where it could be said that one person was doing it.

About nine people put one thing in and about two people kept a thing out and about one person stopped another without being asked to.

---

**And then, at about half past nine, about four people who have been walking past that road since about six have said since that they looked up and saw windows lit in separate rooms across four districts and that no two of the windows was in the same building, and one of them said out loud that he had not known four of those people.**

Windows lit in separate rooms.

Nobody in any of them had been told about any of the others.

The room under the building in that first district stayed dark all evening and was not opened and is not opened on any page of this volume.

---

*Nothing was said out loud in that shed after about half past nine, and nobody thanked anybody and nobody forgave anybody, and a converted tram depot on a road in a fourth district held about eleven people who had not been asked anything in advance.*

---

**The four jobs that Wednesday went into that shop, that landing, that kitchen and that yard, and they came to seventy-seven pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with a dimmer module behind it that had been made for a different lamp and had never once been matched to the one in it.

He took the module out, put the plain switch in and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**A dimmer that was never matched to its lamp is a lamp somebody can dim to nothing and think has gone. Modules in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a bench about four of them have had to change a bulb and found the light was off.**"

A light on that landing had been put in on a fitting where the neutral and the line came in the same way round and were not separated at the terminal at all.

He separated them and proved the light from both landings.

"**Twenty-three pounds,**" he said. "**Two wires into one way round is a fitting that has been wired twice by two people who did not speak. Fittings on landings are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to blow the fuse and go downstairs to find the board.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a lamp inside the hood wired after the cooker and switched off with it.

He took the lamp off that cable and put it on the wall switch.

"**Nineteen pounds,**" he said. "**A hood lamp wired in behind a cooker is a lamp that goes off when somebody boils a kettle. Lamps in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to hold a torch under their hands to find a pan.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate that had been fixed with three screws into brick and one into a hole in a stone that went nowhere.

He took the fourth screw out, filled the hole and proved the socket from the gate.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**One screw into a stone is a plate that is one bad winter away from being on the floor. Plates in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a wall about four of them have had to put the lamp somewhere else until the wind dropped.**"

**Seventy-seven pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened on that Wednesday or on any other, and no figure for the page it holds is printed in this file.**
