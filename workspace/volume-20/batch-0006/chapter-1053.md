# Chapter 1053 — The Woman Who Came Back

**That Friday ran a counter from about ten and then four converted units off that service road, and about nine people went up that stair after six, and a woman of about thirty-four came in through that door with the same folder under her arm that she had carried in on the Monday.**

---

Six callers came into that shop on that Friday and six names were written against that Friday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and the shutter was down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and forty-three days, two hundred and seventy-seven weeks and four days. What is in that carrier bag under the long bench is a hundred and sixteen days old, the printing nobody has traced the home of is one hundred and two days old, and the four words were ninety-three days old.**

---

**She came up at about ten past six on her own and sat down before anybody had said anything, and about four people in that room have said since that she did not ask to be asked this time and that about nine people noticed the difference.**

Marek said: **you have not asked me for anything.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I have come five times and I have got as far as the second of three four times and I am not going to stand on a landing being polite about it.**

Marek said: **that is the first of three.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **my daughter, and no, and I have never once wanted to say that to nine people and I am saying it anyway.**

---

**And then the second of three was asked and she answered it whole, and about four people in that room have said since that it came out faster than the first one and that two of the nine wrote nothing down, as usual.**

Marek said: **that is the second of three.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **My mother's name, and yes, and I have said that one in a kitchen and a corridor and a bus and it does not get easier and I am not going to pretend that it does.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **nobody has said that in this room in about four months.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I have been saying it in nine kitchens for four weeks and it comes out the same every time and I would like somebody to notice.**

---

**And then the third of three was asked out loud and she did not answer it, and said out loud that she was not going to, and about four people in that room have said since that what she said next was not a sentence anybody had heard in that room before.**

Marek said: **that is the third of three.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I am not going to answer that one in here.**

Nobody said anything.

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I have not been able to answer it the four times before either, and I have come back five times, and this is the first time I have said out loud that I am not going to rather than stopping in the middle of it.**

Marek said: **that is more than the last four times gave anybody.**

---

**And then he did not ask her again, and said out loud that he was not going to, and about nine people in that room have said since that he said it before anybody else in there could ask him whether he was going to.**

Marek said: **I am not going to ask it again and I am not going to ask you anything else tonight, and nobody in this room is going to.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I know what I have just done and I would like somebody to tell me whether it was allowed.**

Marek said: **there is nobody in this room who is going to tell you that, because there is nobody in this room who knows.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Then we are all going home not knowing, and I have not had that in about four months.**

---

**And then the woman of about fifty-four said one sentence about it out loud, and about four people who come up that stair have said since that she had not said anything at all in that room since a Friday in the spring.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **that is the first person in about four months who has turned that down out loud instead of stopping halfway and letting about nine people decide what it meant.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Do not make it into a thing that happened here. It happened in me and it happened whether that room was full or not.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I am not making it into anything. I am saying that I noticed.**

---

**And then at that counter, after the shutter was half down, the woman of about thirty-nine asked Marek one question out loud and got an answer out of him that was nine words long, and about four people who have been in that shop have said since that she asked it while she was putting her coat on.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **on that Tuesday, when you say the thing you want, what happens to me.**

Marek said: **I do not know, and I have said that I do not know four times this week.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I have come five times to find out and I have got nine words and a thing I said out loud on a Friday, and I am not going to be standing up there at half past six on a Tuesday if I do not know what it is for.**

Marek said: **You will be standing there or you will not be, and I will not ask you either way.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **that is the first answer anybody has given me that I have believed.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and a woman said no out loud at that bench instead of stopping in the middle of it, and a folder that has been under an arm for about eleven weeks was not opened in front of anybody, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and fifty-fifth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Friday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that cupboard, and they came to seventy-one pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with two cables coming out of it and one of them going nowhere.

He took the dead one out, made the ends off and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Twenty-one pounds,**" he said. "**Two cables out of one rose means somebody has moved a lamp and left the old one in the wall. Roses in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a bench about four of them have had to paint round a hole that was never meant to be there.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in on a plate screwed to the back of a meter cabinet that gets knocked every time the door swings.

He made a longer plate, fixed it to masonry and proved the socket from the gate.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**A plate on the back of a meter cabinet is a plate that comes off with the cabinet. Plates in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to hold a socket together with both hands.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with the grill element's terminal block cracked across one of its pillars.

He changed the block and proved the grill with the door down.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**A cracked block is a terminal that has been done twice by somebody who did not want to pay for the part. Blocks in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to cook with the grill off.**"

A light in that cupboard had been put in on a lamp holder that had been screwed into the chipboard of the door itself.

He took it off the door, made a plate and proved the light with the door open and shut.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**A holder screwed into a door is a holder that moves every time the door does. Holders on cupboards are like that, and one of them has been a door about four of them have had to shut it slowly in the dark.**"

**Seventy-one pounds is the sum of those four on that Friday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Friday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder stayed where it stays on the back of that shelf, and this page carries no figure and no range of any kind for what it holds.**
