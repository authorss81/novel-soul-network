# Chapter 1054 — A Sunday With Nobody Asked Anything

**That Sunday kept that counter open from about ten until about two, and the shutter comes down early on a Sunday, and about four people went up that stair in the afternoon of it, and the rest of that day was four jobs in four rooms off that service road.**

---

Five callers passed through that shop on that Sunday and five names were written against that Sunday's line as each of them arrived, the last of them let away at about half past one, and the shutter came down at about two.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and forty-five days, two hundred and seventy-seven weeks and six days. The bag under that long bench holds a hundred and eighteen days of sheets, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and four days old and its home has never been named, and the four words were ninety-five days old.**

---

**He came up at about one and stood at that bench without sitting down on it, and about four people in that room have said since that the room was so thin that anybody could have counted it and that nobody did.**

Marek said: **there are four of us.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there are five and one of us is behind the counter.**

Marek said: **I am not counting anybody in. That is the thing I have been getting wrong since Monday.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I know. I have watched you do it four times and I have not said anything about it and I am saying something about it now.**

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-nine looked at the strip of wall behind that chair for about nine minutes and then asked the woman who keeps that room a question about it, out loud, and got an answer that was not an explanation.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I have looked at that wall on every visit I have made to this room and there has been nothing on it every time, and I have never once asked what it is.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there is nothing on it.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **that is not the same as saying what it is.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **No. It is not, and I have been in this room about four years and I have never worked out what that wall is for either, and I am not going to stand here and make one up.**

Marek said: **neither have I.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Then two of us have not and I would like it left at that rather than filled in.**

---

**And then at about a quarter to two he began a sentence about a person who cannot answer and stopped four words in, and nobody in that room finished it for him and nobody told him to stop, and about four people in that room have said since that he began it looking at that bench.**

Marek said: **a person who cannot answer that last one ought to be—**

He stopped there.

The woman of about thirty-four said: **four.**

Marek said: **I am not counting.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **It is four, and I have watched you do that twice this week and both times you were counting, and you stopped at four both times, and I want you to know that I have noticed all three of those things.**

Marek said: **I am aware of what I did and I am not going to do the rest of it in here.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-nine said one thing about the room out loud, and about four people in that room have said since that she said it to nobody in particular and that it was the only sentence anybody said there that afternoon.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **There were about nine of us in here on Wednesday and I knew two of them, and there are five of us in here now and I have known one of them since about eleven o'clock this morning, and both of those afternoons I came up a stair to be asked things by people I had not met.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **That is what it is.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **You have just said what it is and you said it about a Wednesday and not about the room, and I am going to let that stand because it was useful and not because it was complete.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and nobody in that room was asked a single thing all afternoon, and a sentence was begun at about a quarter to two and given up at four words, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and fifty-seventh night.**

---

**The four jobs that Sunday went into that passage, that cellar, that kitchen and that yard, and they came to fifty-nine pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that passage had been put in on a rose that had been fixed with a raw plug of wood pushed into the plaster.

He cut it out, put in a proper box and proved the lamp from the door.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A wooden plug in a rose is a rose somebody boxed before there was anything to box. Roses on stairwell walls are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to hold a bulb up by hand.**"

A socket in that cellar had been put in on a plate that had been painted shut round the edge with two coats.

He cleaned it back, made a proper lid and proved the socket from the step.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**Paint round the edge of a lid is a lid somebody has decided twice should not be found. Lids in cellars are like that, and one of them has been a cellar about four of them have had to find a torch to find the socket with.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a lead that had been stapled along the underside of a worktop and then run through a drip tray.

He took the staples out, made it short and proved the cooker with two rings on.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A lead stapled to a worktop is a lead that gets cut by the first person who moves the fridge. Leads in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a worktop about four of them have had to lift up to find out why a ring went dead.**"

A light in that yard had been put in on a fitting that had been left with its cover off and the terminals exposed under a lid that only went on half of it.

He made the lid cover the whole of it and proved the light from the path.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A cover that only goes on half is a cover somebody has had to hold with a thumb for about four years. Covers in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a fitting about four of them have had to change a bulb with two hands.**"

**Fifty-nine pounds is the sum of those four on that Sunday, exact.**

---

**About half past one on that Sunday closed the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf did not come off it, and this file prints no figure of any kind for the page it holds.**
