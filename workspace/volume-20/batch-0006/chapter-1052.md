# Chapter 1052 — The Want Said Out Loud In That Room

**That Wednesday was a counter from about ten until six with a bench standing empty behind it, and about nine people went up that stair in the evening, and the fourth of those four rooms was shut at half past six and the shutter came down at ten.**

---

Ten callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and ten names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about a quarter to nine, and the shutter was down at ten.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and forty-one days, two hundred and seventy-seven weeks and two days. The hundred and fifty sheets in that carrier bag are a hundred and fourteen days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred days old and has not been looked for anywhere, and the four words were ninety-one days old.**

---

**He said it at about half past six, standing, with about nine people in that room and the card in its rail above that bench, and about four people in that room have said since that he did not sit down first and did not wait for a gap.**

Marek said: **I want somebody who cannot say that last one asked to leave that room, and not put in a chair and left in it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **you said it on Monday.**

Marek said: **I said it on Monday in a shop to about four people and I am saying it in here, and you are all of the room, and that is the difference and it is the whole of it.**

Nobody said anything.

---

**And then about nine people in that room said nothing for about nine minutes, and about four people in that room have said since that they counted, and that not one of them said one kind thing to anybody else in it.**

Nobody said anything.

Nobody moved a chair.

The woman of about fifty-four turned her head to the window and kept it there, and did not look at him.

Marek said: **that is nine minutes. I am not going to fill them.**

---

**And then a woman who had come on that week's rumour asked him out loud whether it had been said so that it could be written down, and he said no in one go, and about four people who have been in that room have said since that she asked it in a completely ordinary voice.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **was that said so that it could be written down.**

Marek said: **nothing in this room has been written down in about four years and nothing is going to start tonight.**

The woman of about twenty-six said: **I did not say you wrote it. I asked whether you said it out loud.**

Marek said: **you are the first person in that room to ask me that in about four months and I have not got an answer for it.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty asked Marek, out loud and in front of everybody, to say it a second time on a Tuesday, in that room, while he was standing in it, and about four people in that room have said since that he asked for it and did not explain why.**

The man of about thirty said: **say it again on a Tuesday.**

Marek said: **that is not a thing I can do.**

The man of about thirty said: **You can, you did it on a Monday in a shop and you did it tonight. I am not asking you to mean it more. I am asking you to say it while I am standing in the room, because I have never in my life heard anything said about me and nine other people at the same time.**

Marek said: **which Tuesday.**

The man of about thirty said: **the Tuesday of the week after next.**

Marek said: **That is a date and you have said it in front of nine people and I have heard it, and I am not going to write it down, and if you are not there on that Tuesday I will say it anyway.**

---

**And then a woman who had been in that room four times said one thing out loud, and about nine people in that room have said since that she said it to the room and not to Marek.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I have been in this room four times and I have never once got to the third of three, and I came up tonight to find out whether hearing a man want that to happen changes anything at all for anybody in here.**

Marek said: **I do not know whether it does.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Neither do I and that is the first time in about four weeks that two people in this room have said the same sentence.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a want was said out loud in a room with about nine people in it and about four of them heard the want and nobody said a kind thing for about nine minutes, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and fifty-third night.**

---

**The four jobs that Wednesday went into that shop, that cellar, that kitchen and that corridor, and they came to fifty-six pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in on a back box that had been fitted with its knock-outs left in.

He knocked them out, dressed the cable in and proved the socket from the wall.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A box with its knock-outs left in is a box somebody could reach a finger into. Boxes in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a bench about four of them have had to put a cover plate on somebody's hand.**"

A lamp in that cellar had been put in on a flex that had been buried in the floor under about two inches of concrete.

He cut down, brought it up in a proper box and proved the lamp from the step.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A flex under two inches of concrete is a flex that gets trodden on whenever somebody moves a fridge. Flexes in cellars are like that, and one of them has been a floor about four of them have had to lift to find a break.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with an oven element wired across the live instead of through a terminal.

He put it through its own terminal and proved the oven with the door shut.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**An oven across the live is an oven that works and warms the case as well. Elements in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been an oven about four of them have had to use the top ring for everything.**"

A light in that corridor had been put in on a lamp holder that had been wired with the neutral and the live the wrong way round and had never once been looked at.

He took it apart, put it back the way it was meant to be and proved the light from both ends.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A holder the wrong way round is a holder somebody can change with one hand on the metal. Holders in corridors are like that, and one of them has been a corridor about four of them have had to switch off at the wall.**"

**Fifty-six pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**
