# Chapter 1058 — She Would Not Be Thanked For It

**On that Friday about nine people went up that stair after six and a man asked a woman in that room, out loud and in front of all of them, a question he had been carrying for about ten weeks, and she did not answer it, and somebody else in there said the whole of what she had instead.**

---

Seven callers came into that shop on that Friday and seven names were written against that Friday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past eight, and the shutter was down at ten.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and fifty-seven days, two hundred and seventy-nine weeks and four days. What is under that long bench in a carrier bag is a hundred and thirty days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and sixteen days old and has not been looked for anywhere in this city, and the four words were one hundred and seven days old.**

---

**He asked her at about half past six, out loud, in front of about nine people, and she was standing at that bench when he got to it, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody had any warning that a question was coming.**

Marek said: **I am asking you the thing I asked you about four months ago and you told me to ask it and stop standing about with it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I remember.**

Marek said: **and I am asking it out loud in this room this time, and about nine people are going to hear me ask it, and that is not me making it bigger. That is me finding out whether it gets any easier because nine people hear it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **No.**

---

**And then he asked it out loud, the second time in this volume and the second time it has ever been asked out loud in that room, and about four people in that room have said since that it was nine words and that she did not answer any of them.**

Marek said: **what is this room for.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I am not going to answer that a second time.**

Marek said: **You did not answer it the first time either. You told me to ask it and then you did not answer it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **And I have just told you what I am going to do, and you have heard it, and there is nobody in this room who has to do anything about it.**

---

**And then about nine people in that room said nothing for about nine minutes, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the longest anyone in there has sat, and that nobody said one kind thing to anybody else in it.**

Nobody said anything.

Nobody moved a chair.

Marek said: **That is two times and two nothing. I am not going to ask it a third time, and I want that said out loud so that nobody in here goes home waiting for a third.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I heard you say that.**

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-nine said out loud, in about eleven words, what that room is for, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody repeated one word of it afterwards, and that one of them wrote it down afterwards and threw it away.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Nothing here. We ask you three things, and then you decide.**

Nobody said anything.

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **And nobody is to thank me for it. I have not done anything and I am not going to be thanked for nothing, and if anybody in this room starts I will not answer them.**

Marek said: **I was not going to.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I know. That is why I said it before you could not.**

---

**And then nobody in that room said anything for the rest of that evening, and about four people in that room have said since that two of the nine asked her afterwards what she had meant and were told to go and sit down.**

The man of about thirty said: **what did you mean.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **No.**

The man of about thirty said: **You said nine words and I did not hear all of them.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **Then it was not for you.**

---

**And then Marek said out loud, in that shop after the shutter was half down, both halves of a number he had counted himself, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said the second half first and that he said both of them to a person and not to a record.**

Marek said: **four fewer people came up that stair tonight than came up it on Saturday, and I counted that myself on a piece of paper and I have thrown the paper away.**

The man of about thirty said: **that is my doing.**

Marek said: **I do not know that and I am not going to let you have it either. It could be what she said and it could be the rain and it could be that about four of them heard something at a counter in a first district and did not walk as far as the stair. I have got three things it could be and I have got no way of telling them apart and I am saying both halves of that out loud and neither of them is going on anything.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Friday and nobody forgave anybody, and a woman of about thirty-nine said the whole of what that room is for and then said out loud that nobody was to thank her for it, and a man said out loud that four fewer people came up that stair than had come up it on the Saturday, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and sixty-ninth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Friday went into that shop, that alley, that kitchen and that cupboard, and they came to seventy-three pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with a piece of rubber pushed between the terminals to stop them touching.

He took the rubber out, set the gap and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Twenty-two pounds,**" he said. "**Rubber pushed between two terminals is somebody who could not get the gap where it belonged and did not want to be told. Gaps in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a rose about four of them have had to go round with a tester instead of a screwdriver.**"

A socket in that alley had been put in at about knee height on a junction box with the lid off and the terminals bare.

He made a lid, terminated what was there and proved the socket from the mouth of the alley.

"**Eighteen pounds,**" he said. "**An open box at knee height in an alley is a thing that gets kicked and then gets wet. Boxes in that alley are like that, and one of them has been a socket about four of them have had to walk round it in the dark for about four years.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a cooker outlet that had been painted in instead of screwed.

He unscrewed it, made a proper outlet and proved the cooker with the oven on.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**A cooker outlet screwed on is one that can be taken off by anybody who finds a screwdriver. Outlets in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to keep an eye on who has got a screwdriver in that flat.**"

A light in that cupboard had been put in on a flex that had been run through the door frame and stapled to it on the other side.

He un-stapled it, made a proper entry and proved the light with the door shut.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**A flex stapled to a door frame is a flex that is cut every single time that door shuts hard. Flexes on cupboards are like that, and one of them has been a door about four of them have had to take the door off to find the break.**"

**Seventy-three pounds is the sum of those four on that Friday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Friday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf was not opened and nothing whatever is written in this file against the page it holds.**
