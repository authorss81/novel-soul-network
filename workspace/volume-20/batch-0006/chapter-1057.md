# Chapter 1057 — She Asked Him To Say Something Else

**A Tuesday was a bench until two and a counter until six and then that shop until ten, and at about ten past six a man who had said he was coming back was asked the last of three things at that bench in front of about nine people and could not do it, and about nine seconds after somebody asked him to say something else he said the whole of what he had.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Tuesday and nine names were written against that Tuesday's book as each of them came in, none of them let away before about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and fifty-four days, two hundred and seventy-nine weeks and one day. The hundred and fifty sheets under that long bench are a hundred and twenty-seven days old, the printing nobody has found the home of is one hundred and thirteen days old, and the four words were one hundred and four days old.**

---

**He came up at about ten past six, which was early, and sat down without being asked to, and about four people in that room have said since that he had never once sat down in it before the Friday of the week before last.**

Marek said: **you did not have to come early for this.**

The man of about thirty said: **I have not got anything to be early for. I came early so that I would be sitting down before anybody could look at me.**

Marek said: **that is the first thing anybody in this room has said to me in about four weeks that I have not already thought of.**

The man of about thirty said: **Then I am not going to be any use to you tonight either.**

---

**And then Marek said out loud what he had come to say, in about nine words, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it standing up in front of about nine people and did not look at anybody while he said it.**

Marek said: **I am asking for a person who cannot answer that last one to be asked to go out of that room, and not to be let in and left sitting down.**

Nobody said anything.

Marek said: **That is the third time. I said it in a shop and I said it in this room and this is the third time and I am not going to say it in this room again whatever happens now.**

---

**And then nobody said anything for about nine minutes, and about four people in that room have said since that they counted, and that about nine people sat in that room in which not one of them said one kind thing to anybody else in it.**

Nobody said anything.

Nobody moved a chair.

The woman of about thirty-four said, to Marek and not to the room: **he came early for this.**

Marek said: **I know.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **You cannot know that. He told you and you have had it for about four minutes.**

Marek said: **I know he told me and I do not know what it is for.**

---

**And then Marek asked him the last of three things out loud, in front of about nine people, and the man of about thirty could not answer it and said so in about nine words.**

Marek said: **that is the last of three.**

The man of about thirty said: **I cannot do that one with you all here.**

Marek said: **that is an answer and I am not going to argue with it.**

The man of about thirty said: **Then somebody should say, because I have now said it out loud in a room of nine people and I cannot tell anybody afterwards that I did not.**

Marek said: **You have said it out loud and about nine people heard it and nobody in here is going to write it down, and that is the whole of what I have got for you.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four asked Marek, out loud and in front of everybody, to say something else, and about four people in that room have said since that she asked for it plainly and in front of the man and not quietly at the side.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **say something else. Not that. Say anything else, out loud, now, while he is sitting there.**

Nobody said anything to her.

Marek said, about nine seconds after she had said it: **he may stay and he does not have to answer it, and I do not know whether that is the answer.**

---

**And then about nine people in that room did nothing at all for about nine minutes, and neither accepted that nor refused it, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the longest silence anybody in there has sat in.**

Nobody said anything.

Nobody moved a chair.

The woman of about fifty-four said, out loud and to nobody at all: **that is not a rule and it is not the end of one.**

Nobody answered her.

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **it is not the end of one, and it is not the beginning of one either, and I have heard both of those said in this room in about four years.**

Marek said: **I said I do not know and that is the only part of it I have got.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man who came early so that he would be sitting down before anybody looked at him said out loud that he could not say the last thing with nine people in the room, and nobody accepted what the man at the bench said afterwards and nobody refused it, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and sixty-sixth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Tuesday went into that shop, that landing, that kitchen and that corridor, and they came to fifty-seven pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with a neutral and a line both landed on one terminal and the block had been packed with tape to stop it arcing.

He cut it out, made a proper joint and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**Tape inside a block is a joint somebody has watched arc and then hidden. Blocks in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a rose about four of them have had to switch off at the wall and come back in the dark to find it.**"

A light on that landing had been put in on a switch that had been wired into the line on one side and the lamp on the other and had never once been earthed at all.

He brought an earth in and proved the light from the landing.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A switch with no earth is a switch somebody fitted without turning the power off, and about four of them have got away with it. Switches on landings are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to feel for the switch instead of looking for it.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with its oven lamp wired so that it stayed on whenever the oven was off.

He unwired it and proved it both ways with the door open.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**An oven lamp that never goes off is a lamp that makes a kitchen look like somebody is in it. Lamps in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to shut the door to be alone in a room.**"

A light in that corridor had been put in on a fitting whose conduit had been painted shut where it went into the wall.

He cleaned it back, made a gland and proved the light from both ends.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**Conduit painted shut at the wall is somebody who has tidied a cable run and not a cable run. Conduits in corridors are like that, and one of them has been a corridor about four of them have had to run a new length round the door frame.**"

**Fifty-seven pounds is the sum of those four on that Tuesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Tuesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and nothing on the back of that shelf was disturbed, and no figure for the page that binder holds is printed in this file in any form.**
