# Chapter 1056 — Nobody Answered A Man Who Asked

**That Saturday was the busiest that counter had been in about four weeks and about nine people went up that stair after six who had never been in that room before, and one of them asked out loud, in front of all of them, and a man at that bench did not answer him.**

---

Fourteen callers came into that shop on that Saturday and fourteen names were written against that Saturday's book as each of them came in, and the last of them was let out at about half past nine, and the shutter went down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and fifty-one days, two hundred and seventy-eight weeks and five days. What is in the bag under that long bench at the back is a hundred and twenty-four days old, the printing that has never been on that wall is one hundred and ten days old and has never been looked for, and the four words were one hundred and one days old.**

---

**He was the third person up that stair and he asked it about twenty minutes in, standing, without sitting down and without any of them having told him anything, and about four people in that room have said since that nobody had said a word before he did.**

The man of about twenty-six said: **what is this room for.**

Marek said: **I am not going to answer that.**

The man of about twenty-six said: **you are going to have to.**

Marek said: **No. I have been in that room about four years and I have never said it out loud in it and I am not going to start it on a Saturday with about nine people in here who have not been here before.**

---

**And then nothing was said for about nine minutes, and about four people in that room have said since that they counted, and that not one of the nine said anything kind to anybody else in it.**

Nobody said anything.

Nobody moved a chair.

The woman of about fifty-four looked past him at the window and did not turn her head.

Marek said: **that is nine minutes again and I am not going to fill them.**

---

**And then a woman of about thirty-nine said one sentence to the man of about twenty-six and not to Marek, and about four people in that room have said since that it was the only useful thing anybody said for about nine minutes afterwards.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **you will sit down in about nine minutes and you will be asked three things out loud one at a time in front of everybody in here, and not one of the three will be the one you have just asked him.**

The man of about twenty-six said: **that is worse than being told.**

The woman of about thirty-nine said: **I have not worked out whether it is worse or better and I have been up this stair five times.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty said one thing from the back, out loud, and about four people in that room have said since that he had not said anything else in that room in about an hour and a half.**

The man of about thirty said: **I asked it a different way about four weeks ago and I did not get it then either, so there are two of us who have asked it and neither of us has had an answer.**

Marek said: **There are about nine of us who have asked it. That number is about nine and it is not an exact figure and I have said so out loud because about four people in this room will otherwise write it down as an exact one.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I am not writing it down.**

Marek said: **I know you are not. I said it for the other about eight.**

---

**And then four people in that room asked Marek the same question one after another over about nine minutes, and he gave the same answer four times in about nine words each, and about four people who were in that room have said since that they could have said any of the four for him.**

The man of about twenty-six said: **what is this room for.**

Marek said: **I have told you I am not going to answer it.**

The woman of about forty said: **what is this room for.**

Marek said: **I have told you I am not going to answer it.**

A man of about nineteen said: **what is this room for.**

Marek said: **I have told you I am not going to answer it.**

A woman of about thirty-one said: **what is this room for.**

Marek said: **I have told you I am not going to answer it.**

---

**And then nobody in that room said anything else for the rest of that evening, and about four people in that room have said since that the nine minutes after the fourth asking were the longest of the night, and that the woman of about thirty-four did not open the folder and did not say what was in it.**

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man of about twenty-six asked out loud in a room with about nine people in it what that room was for and got nine words back four times over, and a woman told him that none of the three things he is going to be asked will be the one he has just asked, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and sixty-third night.**

---

**The four jobs that Saturday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that stair, and they came to sixty-eight pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in on a plate with a bolt through it and no nut and a washer holding the whole thing up off the face.

He put a proper nut on it and proved the socket from the wall.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A plate held up by a washer is a plate that has been up about four weeks and everybody has been told it is fine. Plates in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a counter about four of them have had to hold a socket square while they wrote something on it.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in on a bracket whose fixings had gone into the render and not the brick behind it.

He raked the render out, cut two new fixings and proved the lamp from the step.

"**Twenty pounds,**" he said. "**Fixings into render is a bracket that lets go the first time somebody leans on the gate. Brackets in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to stop proping it with a brick.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with two rings on one piece of cable and no means of telling them apart.

He took the ring off, gave it a cable of its own and proved both rings.

"**Seventeen pounds,**" he said. "**Two rings on one cable is two rings that cannot be told apart when one of them is dead. Cables in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a cooker about four of them have had to boil a kettle on the wrong ring.**"

A light on that stair had been put in on a fitting whose cable had been taped where it went through the wall and had gone brown under the tape.

He cut the tape off, made it proper and proved the light with the door shut.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**Tape over a cable is a cable somebody has already seen smoke. Cables on stairs are like that, and one of them has been a stairwell about four of them have had to go up in the dark for about four years.**"

**Sixty-eight pounds is the sum of those four on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Saturday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder on the back of that shelf stayed where it has been, and nothing whatever is set against the page it holds on this page or anywhere in this file.**
