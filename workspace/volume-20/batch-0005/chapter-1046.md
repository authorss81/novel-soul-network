# Chapter 1046 — The Wall Behind Her Chair

**That Saturday went in a bench until two and then that counter until six, and afterwards a man of about thirty stood at the strip of wall behind the chair the woman of about fifty-four keeps her coat on for about forty minutes and would not sit down.**

---

Five callers came through that shop on that Saturday and five names were written against that Saturday's book as each of them arrived, the last of them let away at about a quarter to nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**The four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and thirty days, two hundred and seventy-five weeks and five days. What is in the bag under that long bench is a hundred and three days old, the printing nobody has named the home of is eighty-nine days old, and the four words were eighty days old.**

---

**He put his coat on a chair and then went and stood at the strip of wall behind the one the woman of about fifty-four keeps hers on, and about four people in that room have said since that he chose the wall over about nine chairs and that nobody in the room knew why.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you are stood in the one place in this room that is not a chair.**

The man of about thirty said: **I am stood in it deliberately and I do not know what for.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **I have watched another man stand there for about five weeks and look at it directly and I have never once known what for, and now there are two of you and I still do not know.**

The man of about thirty said: **it is not a thing anybody put there. I have been looking at it since I came in the first time.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **there is nothing on it and there has not been anything on it since before any of us.**

---

**And then he asked her to say it to him once, anywhere, so that he could carry it in, and she refused in about nine words and told him what the asking had been for, and about four people in that room have said since that she did not lower her voice doing it.**

The man of about thirty said: **say it to me once here and I will take it in and I will say it back in there on a Thursday.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **no, and I know exactly what you are doing with it.**

The man of about thirty said: **I am trying to have one day where that room is not nine people and me.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **you could have that day in a bus. You cannot have it at that bench. Every one of us who has ever said anything in there has said it to about nine people and that is not a fault in any of us.**

The man of about thirty said: **I did not say it was a fault.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **It is not one and it is not going to become one, and you cannot carry a thing into that room that nobody else in it has had to carry.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four came up that stair with the folder under her arm and did not open it, and about nine people who come to that room have said since that she said the sentence about being asked twice in a month without being asked.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I have been asked twice this month by people in a first district whether I will walk somebody up that stair for them.**

The man of about thirty said: **by a man or by a woman.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **by a man both times, and both of them had come in off a street with no idea what they were walking into, and I said no, and I am not going to be asked a third time.**

The man of about thirty said: **I did not ask you to walk me up.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **No. You asked to be let in without answering, which is the same request with more words on it, and you asked it of a counter before you ever came up that stair.**

---

**And then he said what it would cost him without saying what the thing was, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it to the coat hooks and that two of the four were people he had not mentioned before.**

The man of about thirty said: **I will tell you what answering it in there would cost and I am not going to tell you the answer. It is about four people, and it is a bus, and it is about four days nobody knows I have spent, and if I say it in front of nine people then those four people know.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **and they know already that you are frightened. That is the piece you have not thought about.**

The man of about thirty said: **I know.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **then say it in there and be frightened at that bench like everybody else, or say nothing and stand there in a room where nine people know your coat and your hands.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man stood at the only wall in that room that is not a chair and was refused an answer by the woman who keeps the room, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and forty-second night.**

---

**The four jobs that Saturday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that corridor, and they came to forty pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in on a plate held on two screws that had gone into a plasterboard face and turned in it.

He put a box in behind it and proved the socket from the wall.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**Screws into a face that is not there is a plate with nothing holding it up. Sockets in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a bench about four of them have had to hold a lamp while they changed a plug.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in on a bracket bolted to a gate post that moves when the gate is shut.

He took it off the post, made a bracket on the wall and proved the lamp from the step.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A bracket bolted to a gate post is a bracket that has to be done again every year. Lamps in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to prop open with a brick.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a supply lead sawn through and rejoined inside a block connector.

He took the block out, made a proper joint and proved the cooker with all four rings on.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A block connector buried in a cooker lead is somebody's repair that was always meant to be temporary. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen about four of them have had to unplug at the wall every morning.**"

A light in that corridor had been put in on a fitting with its holder held in with solder and nothing else.

He re-bedded the holder and proved the light with the door shut.

"**Ten pounds,**" he said. "**A holder held with solder is a holder waiting for a hand. Holders on corridors are like that, and one of them has been a corridor about four of them have had to walk along with their hands down.**"

**Forty pounds is the sum of those four on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Saturday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and nothing on the back of that shelf came off it, and this file prints nothing whatever against the page it holds.**
