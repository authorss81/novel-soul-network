# Chapter 1047 — The Man At The Door Spoke To Marek

**A Monday was a bench until two and a counter until six and four converted units off that service road after it, and a man who has stood at a door for about a month came up that stair with him and talked to Marek for about nine minutes without once looking at him.**

---

Twelve callers came into that shop on that Monday and twelve names were written against that Monday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter was down at ten.

**Those four units off that service road had been there one thousand nine hundred and thirty-two days, which is two hundred and seventy-six weeks to the day. The two hundred and fifty sheets in the carrier bag under the long bench are a hundred and five days old, the printing in a second district is ninety-one days old and is not looked for, and the four words were eighty-two days old.**

---

**He came up at about half past seven with his coat still on and the man of about thirty was already at the back of that room, and about four people in that room have said since that he came in, said what he had to say, and went out again without taking his coat off.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I am not staying.**

Marek said: **you have not taken your coat off.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **that is because I have been standing at a door for about a month and I have not once sat down at one.**

Marek said: **then take it off and sit for ten minutes.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **No. I have come to say one thing and I have said it, and the thing is that I am not coming back up here.**

---

**And then he said out loud why, and the reason was the only one he has, and about nine people who come up that stair have said since that he said it to Marek in the same voice he says everything in.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **the only thing anybody can say against me is what that room is for. You have had that in this room since a Saturday and not one of you has said it, and I have been standing at that door telling about four people a week where to walk to, and I cannot tell any of them one word about what they are walking into.**

Marek said: **I know.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **You know it and you have not said it and neither has anybody else in that room, and I am not going to say it either, because if I say it then I have said it and I have said it as a man who wants three things asked at a door, and it would not be true.**

Marek said: **that is the reason you have not said it.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **It is. It is not a good reason. It is the reason I have got and I have been standing on it for about a month and it is not going to hold up.**

---

**And then he looked at the man of about thirty for the first time and said one sentence to Marek about him and not to him, and about four people in that room have said since that he did it while he was already on his way to the stair.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **that one is not going to manage the last one on Thursday and everybody in here knows it, and I am telling you and not telling him, because he has asked me nothing and I am not going to start.**

Marek said: **I am going to ask it in front of whoever is here.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **I know you are. That is why I came up. If I had not come up I would have spent the rest of the week certain you had arranged something for him.**

Marek said: **I have arranged nothing.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **then I can sleep, and I will not come back up that stair on Wednesday, and you can put that down as something I chose and not as a thing you did.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty said the only thing he said to anybody all that evening, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it to Marek and not to the door.**

The man of about thirty said: **you have been at that door for about a month telling people where to walk to.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **Yes.**

The man of about thirty said: **I have been trying to get in here without answering and you have been trying to get somebody in there who knows what is going to be asked. That is the same job.**

The man of about thirty-seven said: **It is not the same job and I am not going to argue about it in a stairwell.**

Marek said: **nobody in this room speaks for anybody else in this room. You have both said your piece at the same bench and neither of you has said one word to the other and that is how it will stay.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man who has been standing at a door for about a month came up that stair to say he was not coming back and said out loud the only thing anybody has to say against him, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and forty-fourth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Monday went into that shop, that alley, that kitchen and that yard, and they came to fifty-one pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in with its switch wired into the earth.

He took it out of the earth, put it in the line and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A switch in the earth is a switch that puts the whole building on when somebody presses it. Switches in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a counter about four of them have hit when a spanner went down.**"

A socket in that alley had been put in on a plate with a lid wired shut with a staple.

He took the staple out, made a proper gland and proved the socket from the mouth of the alley.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A lid wired shut is a socket somebody has been told to keep closed. Sockets in alleys are like that, and one of them has been an alley about four of them have had to run a lead to.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in on a plate under a sink with a trap above it.

He moved the plate up out of it and proved the sink with the tap running.

"**Thirteen pounds,**" he said. "**A socket under a sink is a socket that gets the one thing that fixes it. Sockets in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a sink about four of them have had to clear with a coat hanger.**"

A light in that yard had been put in on a holder whose threads had been smeared with grease before it was screwed home.

He took it out, cleaned the threads and proved the light from the gate.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**Grease on a thread is grease that turns a holder into a plug. Holders in yards are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to change a bulb in the rain.**"

**Fifty-one pounds is the sum of those four on that Monday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Monday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder on the back of that shelf was not touched, and no figure of any kind is set against the page it holds anywhere in this file.**
