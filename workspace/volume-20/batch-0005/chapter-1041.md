# Chapter 1041 — He Wanted To Sit Without Answering

**A Saturday was a counter from about ten with four converted units off that service road after it, and a man of about thirty came in at about half past seven and asked at that counter whether a person can come up a stair and sit down and not answer anything.**

---

Seven callers came into that shop on that Saturday and seven names were written against that Saturday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter was down at ten.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and twenty-three days, two hundred and seventy-four weeks and five days. The sheet that was on a wall in a fourth district is ninety-six days old and is in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the second printing is eighty-two days old and its whereabouts are not known, and the four words were seventy-three days old.**

---

**He asked it at the counter with four people standing at it and without lowering his voice, and about four people in that shop have said since that he did not ask it quietly and did not ask it to one of them.**

Marek said: **you can come up and sit down and not answer anything.**

The man of about thirty said: **that is not the same as somebody saying I can.**

Marek said: **no. It is not, and I am not going to be the one who says it.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four asked him where the thing had come from, and about four people in that shop have said since that she asked him that and not what he had been told.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **where did you get that you are asked three things.**

The man of about thirty said: **off a woman at a counter, and she was not to blame and I am not going to say one word against her, because everything she said was true when she said it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **she said you are asked three things.**

The man of about thirty said: **she said three things get asked at a bench out loud. That is what I have got and I have had it about four days and I have not got anything else.**

---

**And then Marek said out loud what he was going to do about it and gave a day out loud, and about four people who were at that counter have said since that he gave it before anybody in that shop had asked him for one.**

Marek said: **nobody is going to ask you anything before you come up that stair.**

The man of about thirty said: **I did not think anybody was.**

Marek said: **I know you did not think it and I am telling you now so that you cannot say on a Tuesday that nobody told you. I will ask you three things at that bench, out loud, one at a time, in front of everybody else who is in the room.**

The man of about thirty said: **and if I do not answer the last one.**

Marek said: **then you do not answer it and you stay in that room sitting down.**

---

**And then he got four words into a sentence about what that room is for and stopped himself, and nobody in that shop stopped him, and about four people who were at that counter have said since that two of them had a whole sentence ready.**

The man of about thirty said: **the reason I cannot answer the last one is that nobody can say what this room is—**

Nobody said anything at all.

The man of about thirty said: **I did not finish that.**

Marek said: **you did not.**

The man of about thirty said: **I want you to know I stopped that one myself and that nobody in this shop helped me stop it.**

Marek said: **I was not going to help you with it. I have started that sentence more than once in about five weeks and got nowhere in any of them, and you are the first person who has come in here and nearly finished one.**

---

**And then he asked whether anybody had ever gone up that stair and not answered, and about nine people who come up that stair have said since that a man asked that question in a repair shop on a Saturday evening and got an answer in four words.**

The man of about thirty said: **has anybody gone up and not answered.**

Marek said: **yes.**

The man of about thirty said: **and what happened to them.**

Marek said: **they are still coming.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man asked at a counter for something nobody was going to refuse and did not get it and was told the thing that stops him, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and thirty-fifth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Saturday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that cupboard, and they came to forty-eight pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose that had been painted shut round its edge.

He freed the edge with a blade, cut a proper one in and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Twelve pounds,**" he said. "**A rose painted shut is a rose somebody has stopped reaching. Roses in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a ceiling about four of them have had to get a chair for.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in under a downpipe with its earth lead wrapped round the pipe itself.

He took the earth off the pipe, brought it in and proved the socket from the gate.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**An earth wrapped round a downpipe is an earth a person can cut with a pair of shears. Sockets in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a wall about four of them have had to keep a spade away from.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a thermostat wired to the wrong terminal so that it had never once opened.

He re-terminated it and proved the cooker with the grill on.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A thermostat that has never opened is not a thermostat. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen a woman has had to keep a window open to boil a kettle in.**"

A light in that cupboard had been put in with a flex run out through a fixing screw.

He took the flex out of the screw and proved the light from outside the door.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A flex through a screw is a flex somebody has already paid for. Flexes in cupboards are like that, and one of them has been a door about four of them have had to put a torch in their teeth to work in.**"

**Forty-eight pounds is the sum of those four on that Saturday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Saturday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder on the back of that shelf did not come off it, and this file sets no figure of any kind against the page that binder holds.**
