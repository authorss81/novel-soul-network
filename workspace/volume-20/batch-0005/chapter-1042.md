# Chapter 1042 — The Room Was Thin That Afternoon

**A Sunday kept that counter open from about ten until about two, and the man of about thirty came up that stair at about one and stood at the back of that room for the length of the afternoon without anybody asking him anything.**

---

Four callers passed through that shop on that Sunday and four names were written against that Sunday's line as each of them arrived, the last of them let away at about half past one, and the shutter came down at about two.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and twenty-four days, two hundred and seventy-four weeks and six days. The sheet off that fourth-district wall is ninety-seven days old and sits in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back of that shop, the printing nobody has traced is eighty-three days old, and the four words were seventy-four days old.**

---

**He came up at about one and put his coat on the back of the one chair that had nothing on it, and about four people in that room have said since that he chose that chair and then did not sit in it for about an hour.**

The man of about thirty said: **is that chair anybody's.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **it is not, and take your coat off it or put it on the floor.**

The man of about thirty said: **I will put it on the floor.**

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four said the thing about the third of three things out loud and said it to Marek and not to him, and about four people in that room have said since that she turned her head when she said it so that he would know she had.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **you know which of the three he is going to manage and which one he is not.**

Marek said: **I do.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I have been in this room twice and I have got as far as the second both times, and about four people have been in here at once when I have stopped, and I have not got past the second in front of anybody.**

Marek said: **that is the whole difficulty and it is not a difficulty with him, it is a difficulty with the count in the room.**

---

**And then he said out loud what he could do, and about four people in that room have said since that he said it to the floor and not to anybody in it.**

The man of about thirty said: **I can do two of them. I have thought about them on a bus and I have got the first one word for word and the second one nearly.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I am not going to help you with it.**

The man of about thirty said: **I did not ask you to and I would not take it if you did.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I am telling you that I have been asked by about four people in this city this month whether I will coach somebody, and I have said no every time, and you are the first one I have said no to in this room.**

---

**And then he asked the woman who keeps that room a question and got an answer she has never given anybody else, and about nine people who come up that stair have said since that he walked into it without asking her whether she wanted to be asked.**

The man of about thirty said: **have you ever not answered one.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **twice. I have answered two of the three things in this room and I have never once answered the last one, and you did not ask me for it and nobody has asked me for it in about four months.**

The man of about thirty said: **why has nobody asked you for it.**

The woman of about fifty-four said: **because I have not been in here on a day that anything happened.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Sunday and nobody forgave anybody, and a man stood at the back of a thin room for about an hour and was asked nothing at all, and a woman who keeps that room told a stranger she has never answered one of three things, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and thirty-sixth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Sunday went into that passage, that cellar, that kitchen and that yard, and they came to twenty-five pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that passage had been put in on a rose that had been screwed to a length of timber wedged into the wall.

He took the timber out, boxed it properly and proved the lamp from the door.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**Timber wedged into a wall holds nothing but itself. Roses on stairwell walls are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have had to walk past sideways to get home.**"

A socket in that cellar had been put in on a gland that was smaller than the cable going into it.

He made a gland that fitted and proved the socket from the step.

"**Five pounds,**" he said. "**A gland smaller than its cable is a hole with a lip round it. Sockets in cellars are like that, and one of them has been a cellar about four of them have had to keep a bucket underneath.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a lead dressed into the door frame with staples.

He took the staples out, made a proper joint and proved the cooker with the oven on.

"**Six pounds,**" he said. "**Staples in a frame are somebody's hurry with a loop of cable. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen about four of them have had to shut the door slowly.**"

A light in that yard had been put in on a fitting with no earth at all, because whoever fitted it had run out of cable.

He brought an earth in and proved the light from the path.

"**Eight pounds,**" he said. "**A fitting with no earth is a fitting somebody started and had nothing left to finish. Fittings in yards are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to open in the dark.**"

**Twenty-five pounds is the sum of those four on that Sunday, exact.**

---

**About half past one on that Sunday closed the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder at the back of that shelf stayed exactly where it stays, and this file carries no figure and no range of any kind for the page it holds.**
