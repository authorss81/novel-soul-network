# Chapter 1049 — He Could Not Say It In Front Of Them

**A Thursday was a bench until two and a counter until six and four converted units off that service road after it, and at about half past six that evening a man of about thirty sat down at that bench and was asked three things out loud, one at a time, in front of about nine people, and after the third of them nobody said anything kind to anybody for nine minutes.**

---

Nine callers came into that shop on that Thursday and nine names were written against that Thursday's book as each of them came in, the last of them let away at about half past nine, and the shutter came down at ten.

**Those four converted units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and thirty-five days, two hundred and seventy-six weeks and three days. What is in the bag under that long bench at the back is a hundred and eight days old, the second printing is ninety-four days old and has never been looked for anywhere in this city, and the four words were eighty-five days old.**

---

**He came up at about ten past six, which was earlier than he had ever come, and sat down on a chair at about nine minutes to seven, and about four people in that room have said since that he had never once sat down in it in six weeks of coming.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **you are early.**

The man of about thirty said: **I did not want to be the one who came in last.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **that is the first thing you have said in this room that I believe.**

The man of about thirty said: **it is not about the room. It is about not being able to turn round once I am in it.**

---

**And then the first of three was asked out loud and he answered it in fewer words than he answered it on the Friday, and about four people in that room have said since that about nine people noticed it had got shorter.**

Marek said: **that is the first of three.**

The man of about thirty said: **my mother, and no, and that is all of it.**

Nobody said anything.

The man of about thirty said: **I said it in fewer words than I said it on a Friday. I have been thinking about that for two days and I have decided I am not going to pad it out to make it look better.**

---

**And then the second of three was asked and answered, and this time he said out loud that he would be able to say that one every week for the rest of his life, and about four people in that room have said since that two of them looked at the woman of about fifty-four when he said it.**

Marek said: **that is the second of three.**

The man of about thirty said: **my sister, and no, and I could say that one every week for the rest of my life.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **and what do you do with a person on a Wednesday.**

The man of about thirty said: **nothing. I have not worked that part out. I have worked the other two out and I have not worked that one out and it is the one I am not going to put in a drawer.**

---

**And then the third of three was asked, and he could not answer it out loud in front of about nine people, and about four people in that room have said since that what came out of him first was not a sentence at all.**

Marek said: **that is the third of three.**

The man of about thirty said: **Al—**

He stopped there.

---

**And then nothing was said in that room for about nine minutes, and about nine people in that room have said since that they counted, and that nobody in that room said one kind thing to anybody in it in all that time.**

Nobody said anything kind to anybody.

Nobody moved a chair.

The woman of about fifty-four looked at the window and not at him.

The woman of about thirty-four said, to Marek and not to him: **this room—**

Nobody said anything to her.

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I know. I am going to stop. I am telling you that I stopped.**

---

**And then Marek did not ask him again and did not tell him anything, and said the whole of what he had to say about it out loud, and about four people who come up that stair have said since that it was three sentences and that about nine people heard all three of them.**

Marek said: **that is the third of three, and it has been asked, and I am not going to ask it again tonight.**

The man of about thirty said: **I heard it.**

Marek said: **I know you heard it, and there is nothing else I am going to say to you tonight, and I have been thinking about what to say to you since about eleven o'clock on Monday night and I have not found anything, and you are going to go home without being told anything by anybody.**

The man of about thirty said: **that is what I would have said.**

Marek said: **Then say it to yourself, because I am not going to say it for you.**

---

**And then he got up and went down that stair and stood on the landing about nine minutes and then went out into the street, and about four people who come up that stair have said since that nobody followed him and that one of them wanted to.**

The man of about thirty said: **I am going.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **you do not have to say where you are going.**

The man of about thirty said: **I know, and I am not going to, and that is the only thing I have managed to do in about nine minutes and I have not managed it well.**

Nobody followed him.

He went down about nine minutes' worth of stairs and stood on that landing about nine minutes and then went out into the street.

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Thursday and nobody forgave anybody, and for about nine minutes nobody in that room said one kind thing to anybody else in it, and a man of about thirty could not answer the third of three things out loud in front of about nine people and did not leave because anybody asked him to, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and forty-seventh night.**

---

**The four jobs that Thursday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that stair, and they came to fifty-five pounds, exact.**

A lamp in that shop had been put in on a rose with two cables under one screw on the terminal block.

He separated them, made the ends off properly and proved the lamp from the floor.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**Two cables under one screw is a joint somebody has made to save a minute. Roses in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a ceiling about four of them have had to repaint round.**"

A socket in that yard had been put in with a lid painted to match the wall so that it could not be found a second time.

He found it under three coats, made a proper lid and proved the socket from the gate.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**A lid painted to match the wall is a lid that has been lost twice. Sockets in yards are like that, and one of them has been a gate about four of them have had to get a torch to.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a cord that had been shortened by somebody who had pulled at it instead of unfastening it.

He dropped a new cord and proved the cooker with the grill on.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A lead short enough to trip somebody is a lead that has been pulled instead of unfastened. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been a kitchen about four of them have had to reach behind the fridge for.**"

A light on that stair had been put in on a two-way with the strappers lifted out of the terminals and joined together.

He put them back where they went and proved the light from both landings.

"**Sixteen pounds,**" he said. "**Two-way switches with the strappers out are a light that works from one landing and not from the other. Switches on stairs are like that, and one of them has been a stair about four of them have had to shout up.**"

**Fifty-five pounds is the sum of those four on that Thursday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Thursday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the ring binder on the back of that shelf did not come off it, and no figure for what it holds and no range of one is written in this file.**
