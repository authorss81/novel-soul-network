# Chapter 1048 — Nobody Asked Him Anything That Evening

**A Wednesday opened that shop at ten and did not shut it until about a quarter to ten, and ten callers went through it and about nine others came in at about half past six who were not callers, and the man of about thirty sat at the back with his back to the room for an hour and a half.**

---

Ten callers came into that shop on that Wednesday and ten names were written against that Wednesday's book as each of them arrived, and about nine more people came in after six who were not callers and had not come for a repair, and the shutter came down at about a quarter to ten.

**Those four units off that service road stood at one thousand nine hundred and thirty-four days, two hundred and seventy-six weeks and two days. The sheet that has been off that wall since before this started is a hundred and seven days old and is in a carrier bag under the long bench at the back, the second printing is ninety-three days old and nobody has gone looking for it, and the four words were eighty-four days old.**

---

**Nobody said anything at all for about an hour and a half, and about four people who sit in that shop on a Wednesday have said since that it is the same every time and that they would not answer if they were asked what it is.**

Marek stood behind that counter with a stool he did not sit on.

About nine people sat in that shop and said nothing to each other for about an hour and a half.

Nobody asked anybody anything.

---

**And then a man of about forty-five said the only sentence of that evening that was not one word long, and about four people in that shop have said since that he said it to the counter and not to anybody near him.**

The man of about forty-five said: **you have not asked the one at the back a thing.**

Marek said: **no.**

The man of about forty-five said: **you will tomorrow.**

Marek said: **I will.**

The man of about forty-five said: **then I am not going to be here to watch it. I have come in on a Wednesday for about four years because nothing gets asked on a Wednesday and I am not going to be the ninth person in a room on a Thursday. I am going home now and I would like nobody to make anything of that.**

Marek said: **nobody will.**

---

**And then the man of about thirty turned round on his stool at about a quarter to nine and looked at that bench for about a minute, and about four people in that shop have said since that not one of them went over to him while he was doing it.**

The man of about thirty looked at that bench and at the card in the rail above it and did not say anything.

Nobody in that shop said anything to him.

The man of about thirty turned back round and sat with his back to the room again and stayed there until the shutter was half down.

---

**And then the woman of about thirty-four said one thing to Marek at about ten to nine and got an answer out of him that was not comfort, and about four people in that shop have said since that she put the folder down on the counter when she said it and did not open it.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **you told that man a day in front of about nine people. It was not this Wednesday.**

Marek said: **It was a Friday and he said the day and not me, and I have not written it down, and if it was a Wednesday it was not because of anything I did.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **I know you did not do it. I am asking you what you are going to do with him tomorrow, and you have not got an answer, and I can tell, and I am not going to say what the answer is going to sound like.**

Marek said: **I have not got anything.**

The woman of about thirty-four said: **That is the first true thing you have said to me about this in about four weeks, and I am not going to be kind to you about it.**

---

**Nobody thanked anybody on that Wednesday and nobody forgave anybody, and one man went home early so as not to be in the room on another day, and about nine people said almost nothing between them for an hour and a half, and a copy of that sheet with an empty fifth column was on that table for its one hundred and forty-sixth night.**

---

**The four jobs that Wednesday went into that shop, that yard, that kitchen and that landing, and they came to fifty-four pounds, exact.**

A socket in that shop had been put in on a plate with a screw that went into nothing at all because the box behind it had been taken out for a shelf.

He cut the box out, made the wall good and proved the socket from the wall.

"**Fifteen pounds,**" he said. "**A plate on a box that is not there is a socket somebody has moved a machine in front of. Sockets in that shop are like that, and one of them has been a bench about four of them have had to shift a machine to reach.**"

A lamp in that yard had been put in on a rose with two cables coming out of it and nowhere for the second one to go.

He took the dead one out and proved the lamp from the path.

"**Eleven pounds,**" he said. "**Two cables out of one rose means somebody has moved a lamp and left the old one behind. Roses in that yard are like that, and one of them has been a wall about four of them have had to cut the paint round.**"

A cooker in that kitchen had been put in with a door that closed on the top element.

He took the door off, changed the catch and proved the oven door.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**An oven door that shuts on the element is a door that has been on its way to somebody. Cookers in that kitchen are like that, and one of them has been an oven about four of them have had to leave standing open.**"

A light on that landing had been put in with a lamp in it that had clear glass round it and nothing behind it at all.

He gave it a shade and proved the light with the door shut.

"**Fourteen pounds,**" he said. "**A lamp with clear glass and nothing behind it is a light somebody fitted to be looked at. Lights on landings are like that, and one of them has been a landing about four of them have walked past in the dark.**"

**Fifty-four pounds is the sum of those four on that Wednesday, exact.**

---

**Half past six on that Wednesday shut the fourth of those four rooms, and a woman of about thirty stood behind that door, and the binder never came off that shelf, and this page has no figure and no range of any figure for the page it holds.**
