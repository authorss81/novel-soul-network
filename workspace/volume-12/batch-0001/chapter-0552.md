# Chapter 552 — A Trade Counter On A Wide Road Where About Four Firms Sell Parts, And Nine Crates In A Yard Behind It That Cannot Be Handed Over, And A Man Of About Forty-Six Who Says The Clause Out Loud In A Face To Nobody In Particular And Is Right

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and about two hours on a wide road where about four firms sell parts, and its shape was eleven jobs, and one of the eleven was standing in a yard behind a trade counter being owed about four days by people who were not hiding.**

---

The Thursday carried eleven jobs, all eleven of them dated, and eleven people came through the counter, and the eleventh of them came at about ten past five. Two hours of that Monday went on a wide road where parts are sold, and the counter did two to six, and the bench did the morning, and the rooms off the service road took him at half past six. Nine and then ten is what goes on the ticket and neither figure is the rate, and the difference went to nobody and has gone to nobody on every working day since about the spring. **The heating in that flat goes on at a higher price than it did before the winter, the rise sits on four names on one tenancy, and a fortnight laid out of a cook's own wages by her is still owed by nobody and will be owed by nobody at the month's end.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and sixty-two days, one hundred and thirty-seven weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and sixty-six days, one hundred and thirty-eight weeks to the day, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

---

**The wide road has about four firms in it that sell parts, and three of them sell other parts as well, and the one that matters is a counter with a yellow strip on the edge of it and a plastic folder of guarantee cards behind the strip and a man behind it of about thirty-three who reads a screen and does not look up unless the number in front of him is wrong.**

A man of about twenty-two went along that road on the Thursday at about eleven with a list of four places in his own handwriting that he had made on the Sunday night, which was a different list from the nine names on a docket, and which had four lines on it and the four lines were the four towns and the four towns are about four days of bus travel and are not one another.

**He was not there about anything. He was there because on the Sunday night a woman of about thirty-four had said the word stopped, and the word had a company attached to it, and the company has a counter with a yellow strip on it about two bus rides from that shop, and there is no other way to find a firm in this city than to walk into it.**

---

**There were nine cardboard crates in a yard behind that counter, stacked three high, and they had been there since the Friday, and they were not going to be handed over.**

**The man standing next to them was about forty-six and he had a hi-vis on with the sleeves taken out of it and a pen behind his ear and a docket book he was not writing in, and he was not looking for anybody. He was looking at the crates. He had been looking at the crates for about a week.**

**"They're mine," he said, before Marek had said anything at all. "Not mine, the firm's. I've brought them back because I can't leave them and I can't hand them over and there's nowhere to put them that isn't my own yard and I'm not paying to keep nine crates on my own yard for a fortnight to be sure of it."**

**"For a fortnight."**

**"For as long as it takes."**

---

**He said the clause, and he said it in a face, to nobody in particular, and nobody in that yard had asked him for it.**

**"Deliveries to those four places resume when the returns come back. Every clip that left that yard in four years went out with a card in the box, a card with two boxes on it, one for the day it went and one for the day it came back, and the card is the guarantee, and the guarantee is not a promise by us, it's a record of yours. We can't carry four years of stock on a promise that the card's coming. So the cards come back, or we don't go."**

**"That's the whole of it."**

**"That's the whole of it, and it's correct, and I've read it about nine times and I agree with every word of it and I couldn't change it if somebody paid me."**

**"Who could change it."**

**"Nobody you could get hold of. That's the other thing about it and I'll tell you that as well because you'll work it out and I'd rather you had it from me."**

**Nobody had said that. Marek had been at the yellow strip for about four minutes and the woman of about thirty-four's nine names and the four places and the sheet that went out on the Thursday had been in his head in the order they arrived in, and in that order the sheet was a decision somebody took and a decision can be asked about, and it turns out that a decision can be asked about and the answer is that the person who took it does not answer letters.**

He had said the thing about letters a moment before he had realised that he did not know whether that office answered them at all, and that in eleven years of work he had never once had cause to find out, because nothing he had ever needed had ever come back needing an answer written down.

---

**"You cannot change it."**

**"No."**

**"And you cannot leave them."**

**"I can leave them. I've left them. I'm telling you I have not got anywhere better to put nine crates, and that is a different sentence, and you can hear the difference or you can not."**

**"I heard it."**

**"Good. That's the other thing I wanted."**

---

**Then he named the cost, and he named it in a number, and he named it without being asked and without any appearance of wanting anybody to do anything with it.**

**"Four days a run. That's what I lose when a load sits and I don't get a paid run out of it, and I've had one run not go and I've got another that isn't going either, and that's about four days of my own unpaid sitting on my own drive, and there are about nine of us on that rota and not all of us have a second van."**

**"That's real."**

**"That's real and it's mine and it doesn't help you."**

Marek did not tell him it would be fine. He had about four sentences available that would have made the yard easier to stand in, and he made none of them, because the man had not asked for one and because a sentence offered to a person who has named a cost is nearly always a request that they should stop counting.

---

**Nobody agreed with him. A young man at the yellow strip read a screen. A woman came out of a door with a clipboard and said a thing about a collection that did not apply to this yard. About two people crossed the yard and neither of them slowed down, and about four metres from the crates there is a drain and the drain is the only part of that yard that nobody has ever argued about.**

**Nobody thanked him either. He said the cost and then he picked the docket book up off the wall and put it back in his pocket without opening it, and Marek said, because he had to say something and there was about four seconds of it left:**

**"Can I ask you one thing and you can say no."**

**"Go on."**

**"How long does a card take."**

**"Nine days. Nine working days, which with a weekend on it is eleven, and it is nine days because it goes out with the goods on a van and comes back on a van and the van goes where the van goes."**

**"And if somebody in one of those four places needs to open the box today."**

**"Then they are not open, and they wait, and they ask me, and I say come back in nine days, and they come back in nine days, and everybody is very polite about it."**

**"And that is not anybody's fault."**

**"No,"** he said. **"That is the part that took me a fortnight to understand and it is the reason I am standing in my own yard on a Thursday instead of being cross with somebody. Nobody is at fault. It is just a shape, and everybody inside a shape does the shape, and the shape does not know anybody is in it."**

---

**He noticed, and did not say, and told nobody, that about four of the nine crates carry a stencilled code on the end that is not that firm's code, and that the other five carry the firm's code, and that the two kinds of stencilling are not worn at the same rate and the four are cleaner.**

That is a fact about a yard and not a finding. A yard gets pallets. A yard gets crates that came back from somewhere. **Nobody in that yard could have told him where the other four came from, including the man who owned nine of the ten.**

---

**He left the wide road at about one, and walked back with the four lines in his head, and wrote the second of them on the back of a docket at about half past two, and the second line is the second of the four places, and the second of the four places is where the woman of about thirty-four keeps a shop, and the reason he wrote it is that on the Sunday night she had said she was going to tell four people, and that four of the four had freezers, and that a freezer and a clinic are not the same thing and that he had about two days to work out the difference and had not started.**

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted, and did not say, on that Thursday, was for the man in the yard to have been wrong. That is the whole of it. He wanted about nine seconds of that man saying the clause was the wrong shape, because a wrong shape can be argued with in a kitchen, and a correct shape can only be waited out, and a man who has spent eleven years of work being the person who gets asked is not able to help anybody at all inside a correct shape, and he had about four hours of it in front of him and did not know what to do with the four hours.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
