# Chapter 558 — A Weighbridge Hut In The Third Of The Four Towns, And About Nine Vehicles Standing In A Queue For About Nine Hours On A Monday Because Of A Clause A Man Of About Fifty-Two Has Read And Agreed With And Cannot Change, And A Folder Face Up On A Shelf That Nobody In These Ten Entries Opens

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a room off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was eleven jobs, and about four of the eleven were about nothing at all, and the day's one event happened about four days' bus travel away in a queue that did not move between about ten and about seven.**

**THERE WERE ABOUT NINE VEHICLES IN THE QUEUE AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AT ABOUT ONE AND ABOUT NINE AT ABOUT FOUR, and at no point between about ten and about seven did anybody in that queue move forward, and the reason they were in it is a return card.**

**Here is the whole mechanism, and it takes about eleven seconds to say and it is not a conspiracy and it is not a policy and it is a sheet of paper with a heading on it.**

A firm sells a clip. Every clip leaves with a card in the box, a card with two boxes on it, one for the day it went and one for the day it came back. **A firm cannot weigh a vehicle that is carrying something it has not invoiced, and a clip is a line on an invoice, and a line on an invoice requires a card or a signature, and a driver who has not got one of those two is not carrying the load and is not going over the bridge, and the driver has a slot, and the slot is at four, and the four slots go in the order they came.** And on the Monday of this week, the four places stopped receiving, and a driver who had come to deliver to one of those four places had come back with the load still on, and the card for the load was in the office of the firm in a town four days away, and the office had it, and the office was not going to weigh it here and not going to send anybody about it, and the driver's four o'clock slot was in about six hours.

**And the reason the queue was about nine vehicles and not nine hundred is that nobody else was affected, and everybody in that queue could see that nobody else was affected, and about four of the nine drivers had a part-load that was going to a place that was still being supplied and could have gone to that place instead, and did not, because the part-load and the load were on the same invoice.**

---

**The man who reads the weighbridge is about fifty-two and has read it for about nineteen years and he was in the hut the whole day and he came out of it about nine times.**

**He did not tell anybody it was not his fault. He told one thing, out loud, to a driver, in a face, at about one, and it took about eleven seconds, and about four of the nine drivers in that queue heard it and about four of them have since given an account of it and the accounts agree.**

**"I read the ticket. I weigh the ticket. I don't weigh the lorries and I never have. There's nine of you standing in a line for six hours because a piece of paper is in an office four days away, and I cannot get an answer out of a town four days away, and if I could I still could not, because the man who would be asked is not mine and does not answer for this and has never been asked to."**

**"So what do you do."**

**"I weigh what I can and I put the rest at the back and I write the time down,"** the man said, **"and I have written the time down for about nineteen years and there is a book of it, and the book is not anybody's business, and I have never once seen anybody look at it."**

---

**The book is on a shelf under the window and it is a hardback book with about nine entries a day in it in pencil and the ninth entry for most days is a word, and the word on about four days in a week is the same word, and the word is not a complaint and is not a name and is not a figure, and it is the word that a person who reads a weighbridge for nineteen years writes in a column headed nothing when nine vehicles have not moved for six hours and there is nothing else to write.**

**Nobody has ever read that book. Not the man, and not the four drivers, and not the woman with the van, and the queue outside does not move between about ten and about seven, and nothing in a hut that size gets looked at twice on a day like it.**

---

**What came off the lorries, in the end, was nothing. All nine vehicles went back down the road at about seven with their loads on, and about four of them had taken a day and about half a tank, and one of the four had a driver who had a wedding on the Sunday and did not get to it, and that driver is not named anywhere and did not say anything to anybody about it and has not been asked.**

**About four of the nine went and got food. About four of the nine did not go and got food, and were still there at about seven, and one of those four was a woman of about thirty-four with a van and a clipboard and about nine hours, and she did not say anything about it to anybody, and she has since said one sentence about it in a shop in this city, and nobody in that queue was in the shop.**
