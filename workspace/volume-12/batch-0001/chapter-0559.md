# Chapter 559 — The Engine's Other Half From A Person Who Was In The Room And It Is Longer And It Is Worse And It Does Not Contain His Name, And A Fifth Time Somebody Asked Him For Nine Lines In His Own Words And He Said No And Stopped Constructing Reasons In Advance, And Six Hundred And Seventy-Two And Six Hundred And Sixty-Six Are On The Same Afternoon

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a room off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was a woman of about thirty-four with a second-hand account from a room in the first of the four towns, and a man with a board.**

---

Eleven jobs for the Thursday, all eleven dated, eleven people through the counter, and the last of them at about ten past five. The morning was a bench in Lattice Ward and the afternoon was a counter, and from about half past six it was four rooms off a service road with the heating on. The number on the ticket was nine and then ten, in that order, as it has been since he could read, and about four people in this city have ever asked either question. **That flat's heating is on above what it cost through the winter, four names on that tenancy are paying the difference, and a fortnight a cook put out of her own wages is owed to nobody and will be owed to nobody when this month ends.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and seventy-six days, one hundred and thirty-nine weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and eighty days, one hundred and forty weeks to the day, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

**The nineteenth line on two nails above that bench is six hundred and seventy-two days, ninety-six weeks to the day, and the ask is six hundred and sixty-six days, ninety-five weeks and one day. Six hundred and seventy-two is an age and it is also the day the ask was put to somebody in this city, and six hundred and sixty-six is an age in days and it is also the day the nineteenth line was written on that board. Both of them are on the same afternoon of this day and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating is a closed thing in this case with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it.**

---

**She came in on the Thursday at about four with a hired van's key and a folded docket and no boxes, and she had been in this city for two days this week and had gone back and come up again on the Wednesday night, which cost her about eleven hours each way, and she said that up front, before anything else, and it was not a complaint.**

**"I've been twice this week. I know what that looks like. I've done the sums and I know I look like a person who can't leave a thing alone."**

**"You've come twice to tell me something."**

**"I've come twice because there was a room in the first of the four towns on the Tuesday evening and I was not in it, and I heard about it from the woman who was, and she has told me four things and about two of them I can stand behind and about two I cannot."**

---

**"The first thing she said was that a man came down on the Tuesday with a van and asked whether the four places could be asked to keep a record, and that she said no, and that he asked her whether she could be asked to keep a record, and that she said no again."**

**"I know that part. I have it in a different mouth and it's a worse version and it's about a floor."**

**"It's not worse in that room,"** the woman said. **"It's the same sentence and she said it longer, and I'm going to give you the long one because you had the short one off a man on a Saturday and the short one is the one you'd repeat."**

**"All right."**

---

**Here is the long one, and it took about nine seconds, and she said it in his own shop and she said it as a woman repeating another woman and not as a woman arguing with a man.**

**"She said: I have spent four years telling people in this city that they may refuse, and I have never once been in a room where refusing cost anybody anything, and I have been telling people to do a thing I have not watched anybody do, and that is not a lie, it is a worse thing than a lie, because you can check a lie. And she said: a floor at two in the morning is not a refusal being difficult. It is a refusal being correct, and correct is a thing people do not believe when it is offered to them by somebody who is safe."**

**"And then what."**

**"And then the man in the van said that he was not asking her to keep a record, he was asking her to be the person who says no, and that there is a difference, and she said there is, and then she said: and I have been the person who says no for about four years and about nine of the people I have said no to were in this room."**

**"There was a room."**

**"There were about nine people in that room on the Tuesday and about four of them work in one of the four places and about two of them have a cold store, and the man in the van was not in the room, and she said all of it with the four of them sitting there, and about four of the four of them have said since that they did not know a vote had happened."**

---

**He put the docket down. He did not say anything for about nine seconds and then he said the only sentence in the day that was about himself, and he said it to a woman of about thirty-four in a shop, in a face, and it was not softened.**

**"That is the argument against everything I do and I have never heard it in that form and I have heard it in about nine other forms."**

**"Yes."**

**"Say the other thing."**

**"The other thing is that she asked, at the end, in front of the four of them, whether anybody wanted to write down what had happened, and about four of the four of them said no, in about four seconds each, and she said that was the correct answer, and about four of the four of them said afterwards, to each other in the car park, that it was not a vote, it was a room, and one of them said that out loud."**

**"That is a good sentence."**

**"It is not in your nine lines,"** the woman said. **"Whatever your nine lines are."**

**He did not say that it was a good sentence because it was a good sentence. He said it because a woman of about twenty-four had stood at the end of a room and asked four people whether anybody wanted to write down what had happened, and four of the four of them had said no in about four seconds each, and she had said that that was the correct answer, and that is the second time in nine days that somebody has done a correct thing and got nothing whatever for it. The first one is a man with a clipboard that nobody has ever asked him for. This one is a woman in a room four days away who is owed nothing, who was not thanked, who could not have been thanked because she was not in the room to be thanked in, and who is not going to be told about any of this by anybody.**

---

**About twenty minutes later, at about half past four, a man of about forty-four came in with a board under his arm and asked him for the fifth time.**

**It is the same offer it has been. Nine lines, in his own words, for a board in the second of the four districts of this city, where the page of about nine lines has been on nothing and unadopted for a very long time. He was asked for it about four months ago, and again in a corridor, and again at a counter, and on a day in the autumn he was asked a fourth time and did not take it, and he has not been offered anything since and has not asked for anything.**

**"Nine lines,"** the man said. **"In your words. We would print them as they are."**

**"No."**

**"You want to know why, or you want me to go?"**

**"I want you to go."**

**"Right."**

**"And I'll say the reason once, and then you can print nothing, and I'm not going to say it a second time to anybody in this city including you."**

**"Say it."**

**"Because four places four days of bus travel from this city need a thing to be able to do in one day so that they are able to do it in nine, and I do not have that thing, and the thing I do have is a man and a bench, and if I write nine lines they go on a board in the second of this city's four districts, where about nine people a day stand at a counter, and about four hundred and fifty people in this city will read them, and not one of the nine lines will ever get as far as a gate in the first of the four towns."**

The man of about forty-four stood there for about four seconds and then he said:

**"That's the first time anyone's given me a reason that was about distance."**

**"It's the only reason I've got."**

**"Right,"** the man said, and he put the board down against the wall by the door, and he did not put it down like a person who has been refused, he put it down like a person carrying a thing, and he took it out again in about nine seconds and went.

**He did not ask twice. He has not asked a sixth time. The page of about nine lines is on nothing and has no adopter and is not on either of the two things that were voted on in the spring.**

---

**Here is the small thing, and it is the whole of the growth in the day, and nobody in this shop saw it.**

**He used to construct the reason before the person had finished asking. He had it ready, inside his head, while they were still on the first clause, and it was always four sentences long and it was always the correct four sentences, and the second one was always the true one about himself, and by the time they asked he had already decided which version of himself to be this time.**

On this Thursday the man of about forty-four asked for nine lines, and Marek said no before the second clause, and did not build anything, and there was a silence in the middle of it of about four seconds in which he could have built something and did not.

**He noticed it at about ten that night in a room with four people in it for the heat, and he did not tell anybody, and it is the first time in eleven years of work that a piece of his own machinery has been visible to him and has not been repaired.**

---

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted, and did not say, on that Thursday, was to be in that room in the first of the four towns on the Tuesday evening, and the reason he wanted to be there is not that he would have stopped anything. He would not have stopped anything. He wanted to be in it because about four of the four of them said afterwards that they did not know a vote had happened, and about one of them said that out loud, and he wanted to hear the one who said it, and he is four days of bus travel away, and by the time he could get there the sentence would have been said about four times and to about eleven people, and the eleven are not going to be the nine who were in the room.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
