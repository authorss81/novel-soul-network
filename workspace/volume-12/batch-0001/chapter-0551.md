# Chapter 551 — The First Working Day After An Inspection Nobody Asked About, And About Four Of The Eleven People At One Counter Are Asking About A Small Metal Clip And No Two Of The Four Are Asking About The Same Thing

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a room off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was eleven jobs, and about four of the eleven were about one object, and none of the four wanted the same thing done with it.**

---

Eleven pieces of work were booked for the Monday and eleven dates went in against them, and eleven people came through that counter, and the last of the eleven was at about half past five. Bench in Lattice Ward to two, the counter two to six, and after half past six the four rooms off that service road with the heat on in one of them. Nine and ten is the rate he has quoted since he was sixteen and it is wrong, and it went to nobody, and it has gone to nobody about four times a week for about eleven years. **That flat is still on the heating and the price of it has gone up again since the winter, four names on that tenancy are carrying the rise between them, and a fortnight that a cook laid out of her own wages is owed to nobody and will be owed to nobody at the end of this month.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and fifty-nine days, one hundred and thirty-seven weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and sixty-three days, one hundred and thirty-seven weeks and four days, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

**The rota man had his inspection on the Friday. **About four of the eleven were about a clip.**

It is a small metal thing, about the length of a thumb, with a flat tongue and a turned lip, and it comes in a card of forty and it is the kind of object that a person buys once and then cannot remember buying. It goes across the seam of a cold box, or a chiller, or the lid of an ice cream freezer in a shop, or the door of a dairy chiller on a farm, and it is the whole of why the seal holds, and it costs about the price of a coffee and it is the only part of that seal anybody has ever replaced.

**The first of the four wanted one. The second wanted nine. The third wanted to know whether the ones he had were the current ones and had brought a wrapper in a carrier bag to be shown. The fourth wanted to know whether anybody would still be selling them in three years, which is a different question, and he had brought nothing with him at all, and it took about four minutes to establish that he had brought nothing because he had been told to bring nothing.**

Nobody in that shop sells clips. That has never been a line of that shop's business. **A man of about twenty-eight who came in at about eleven on a Thursday in the spring had described one to him at that counter and had not left one, and had not been asked to, and had said it out loud that it was not for sale in that shop and would not be, and had said why, and the why was about four sentences and involved nobody's money.**

So the four of them had come here because there was nowhere else in this city within a bus ride where an eleven-year-old could have heard of a clip, and three of the four understood that by about half past three, and the fourth did not understand it at all and was told, kindly, at about four, that the shop was not a place for his question, and accepted that.

**He noticed, on the Monday, that the man of about thirty-eight who keeps a rota for a building of about nine flats came in at about one and looked at the shutter rail for a while and did not ask for anything, and he has a printed notice in the inside pocket of a coat and there is an inspection behind him and there has been one on the Friday, and he did not raise it, and nobody raised it with him, and it is not resolved and it is not resolved here by a man looking at a shutter rail.**

---

At about ten past four the door went again and it was the wrong kind of person for that counter, which is a statement about this counter and not about the person.

**She was about thirty-four and she was carrying a folded docket in one hand and she had a hired van's key in the other and there were four cardboard boxes stacked against the wall by the door, and she had a coat on that had been on a bus for about eleven hours and had not been off.**

**"Are you the man who told the man in the spring about the clip."**

**"I am."**

**"Right."** She put the folded docket on the counter and squared it with two fingers of her left hand, which is a thing a person does when they have carried something flat a long way. **"I'll not take up much of your Monday. I've four boxes of them in your doorway and I've been up since Sunday night and I go back tonight, and I want to know one thing, and it isn't about the clip."**

**"Go on."**

**"There are four places that use this clip and I run the shop in one of them, and I have a list of nine names on this docket, and I want to know which of the nine I should stop telling."**

He did not answer, and the reason he did not answer was not that he did not know.

---

**She said the rest of it in about four sentences and she said it in the order a person says a thing they have said to themselves several times.**

**"There's a sheet. It went out on the Thursday, to four places, same sheet, same day. Deliveries stop on the Monday. That's today. So there's four places where the clip stops today and there's me in one of them with a van and no money and forty-one of these on a shelf that nobody has come in for, and one of the nine on this list has a cold store and a freezer and about nine hours of no cold already this year because a fridge went in the summer."**

**"Right."**

**"And here's the part I cannot get round. There's a woman in that town runs a clinic, and she's on this list, and she's a great one for a cold box, and if she hears this from me tonight she'll hear it from me on a Sunday evening with a van outside, and she'll do the sensible thing, which is ask the office on the card whether she is allowed to open it, and asking costs nine days by card and four days by bus, and either way she is sitting on a floor on the Monday morning with a clinic's worth of vaccine in a box and a thermometer that reads nothing."**

**"Is that what happened."**

**"No. That's what will happen if I tell her. That's different. That's me doing it."**

**"Yes. All right."**

**"So which of the nine do I stop telling."**

---

**He had two things he could have said and he said neither of them in the order they were ready, which is a piece of information about him that nobody in that shop was in a position to notice.**

The first was that he did not know, and that a man who does not know is allowed to say so in four seconds and that saying so in four seconds would have been honest and would have cost him about nine minutes of her time.

The second was that he wanted to write her a list of his own before she went.

**The thing he noticed, and did not say, and that is on no form of any kind in this city: the four boxes had been counted twice in pencil on the top lid of the nearest one, and the second count was nine higher than the first.**

She had counted them on the way. Somebody else had counted them before her, and had not told her, and had not put their pencil anywhere near her pencil, and had counted a box she had not opened.

**"I don't know," he said. "I know what a clip is and I know why a seal fails, and I don't know what that sheet says and I haven't read it and nobody has shown it to me."**

**"That's all right. I wanted to hear it in a shop and not on my own."**

**"Right."**

**"I'll go back tonight then. I'll tell the four I have to tell, and the four I have to tell are the four with a freezer, and I'm not going to say the word stopped to anybody with a clinic, and if that costs me the clinic's business then it costs me the clinic's business."**

**"That's a decision you are making in about four seconds."**

**"I've been making it since Thursday. It's just that I've had a van and a long road to make it in properly on."**

She picked the docket up and did not square it this time. **"Is there anything you could put on a piece of paper. Anything at all. Not advice. Just something of yours I could hand a person so it isn't only mine that they're being handed."**

**He wanted to say yes. He did not say yes. What he said was that a piece of paper with his name at the bottom of it would make her the messenger and would be worse than the nine names, and she said that was probably right, and he said he was sorry, and she said she was going anyway.**

That was the whole of it. It took about nine minutes and about half of one of her hours of sleep.

---

**At about half past five he shut that counter and walked back to the bench in Lattice Ward with four boxes of forty in his head, because that is what a clip is, and because forty-one was what she had said and forty a box is and the two numbers did not agree and he had not asked which of them was right and had decided, at about five, on a road, not to ask, because she was on a bus at eleven o'clock that night and because the difference between forty and forty-one is the difference between a bad count and a good one and it is not his.**

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted, and did not say, on that Monday, was to get on the Sunday night bus with her and ask her one question about a clinic, and to be told a number, and to have the number in about eleven hours, and the reason he wanted it is that he has spent eleven years of work being the person who gets asked and the whole of what he wanted on that Monday was to be asked once by somebody four days away, and nobody asked him, and he had said, on the Friday before, in a face, in about nine words, that he was not the person who may carry a message to her, and she had agreed, and he had been right, and being right about it cost him the whole of a Monday.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
