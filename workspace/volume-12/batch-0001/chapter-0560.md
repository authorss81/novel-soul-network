# Chapter 560 — A Sunday Night Bus, A Refused Errand In About Nine Seconds, And The Reason Was That A Thing A Man In This City Asks Her To Carry Four Days On A Bus Is Not Hers And She Has About Four Of Those Already

**The Sunday was four rooms off a service road with the heat on and about four people in them from about half past six, and a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, and a counter shut, and its shape was a woman going back to the second of the four towns on the Sunday night bus, and a folded piece of paper that he put back in a drawer.**

---

**About four people come into those rooms on a Sunday evening for the heat and none of them is asked anything. There is a man of about fifty-one against the north wall with a bag on the floor at his feet who has been there five hundred and eighty-five days, which is eighty-three weeks and four days, and who said nothing this month, and whose request, made in the spring in a warm room, that it be written down somewhere by somebody who is not the man at the counter, is still unmade.**

**There is a woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door. ---

**She had a first-class seat she had paid for herself, and she was going to be at the depot at about half past ten, and she came into those rooms at about seven because she had nowhere else to be in this city for three hours and had decided, on the Wednesday, not to be a person who sits in a café.**

**She had a coat on and a bag and the folded docket and a hired van's key, and she was eating a piece of bread and cheese off a paper napkin on a chair, and she was not tired in a way that showed, and it showed.**

**"You've been up since Friday."**

**"I've been up since Saturday night. I got back in on the Wednesday and I've been here since."**

**"Right."**

**"Don't,"** she said. **"Whatever it is, don't."**

**"I was going to ask whether you had eaten."**

**"I know you were. Ask me something you actually want to know."**

---

**He wanted to know four things. He asked her the smallest one, and the smallest one was a piece of paper.**

He put it on the arm of her chair. It was folded twice. On the outside, in his own handwriting, there was a name and a place: **a name, and then: FIRST OF THE FOUR TOWNS, and underneath: ANY TIME, and underneath that, nothing.**

**"I'd like you to take that when you go."**

She did not touch it for about nine seconds.

**"Who is it for."**

**"A woman of about fifty-two who has a farm in the first of the four towns and a cold store on it and a clipboard on a nail inside the door, and she has kept a third column with no heading on it for about four years, and nobody has ever asked her for it."**

**"Then write to her."**

**"I can't write. There's no form, and a letter takes about nine days, and a card takes about nine days, and a person takes four days each way, and she is the only person in that town who could do the thing in one day that everybody else is waiting nine days for, and I have got a piece of paper with a name on it and I cannot put it anywhere that will arrive before the fourth of next month."**

**"Then it's no use,"** she said. **"I said. That's not me being hard. That's what it is."**

**"It is not no use, because you are going to be in that town on Sunday night and she is a person and people do not have to be written to."**

---

**And here is where it turned, and it turned in about four seconds, and she was not angry and he was not accused and neither of them raised their voice, and that is the whole of the scene and there is not one word of it that either of them will be able to repeat in about a year.**

**"No."**

**"You have not heard the rest of it."**

**"I have heard the rest of it. I have heard the rest of it since the Wednesday,"** she said, **"and I know what it is, and it is that a man in this city has written a name on a piece of paper and wants me to be the reason a person in my own town finds out. And I know what that is. It is a favour. It is a very nice favour, and it costs you nothing, and it costs me four days of being a person who carries things for other people."**

**"It would not cost you anything."**

**"It would cost me the only thing I have got,"** she said. **"About four of us in that town are the ones people go to. I have been one of them since I was nineteen. If I start carrying a folded piece of paper up from a city four days away with a stranger's handwriting on it, then I am not one of the four of us any more, I am a branch of your counter, and I have a shop, and about nine people have a key to my back door, and that is the whole of what I have got and I am not spending it in the first week."**

---

**She said the rest of it standing up, with her bag on her shoulder, and it took about nine seconds and it is the whole of the argument of this whole run of days and she gave it to a man in a warm room on a Sunday without knowing that.**

**"The clinic has not opened her cold box and she is not going to open her cold box and she is right not to, and the man at that counter was right not to change the card, and the man in the yard was right that it is a shape and not a fault. Every single person in this has been right. And about four people in a town are now sitting on floors, and the reason is not that anybody was wrong. The reason is that a whole set of right answers does not make a thing that works, and it has taken me about four hours on a bus to work that out and I am not going to be the one who undoes it by carrying a favour."**

**"That is the whole of it."**

**"That is most of it. I am going to the depot at half past ten."**

**"Right."**

---

**He took the paper back off the arm of the chair and he did not fold it again, and he put it in the drawer in the other room at about half past eight with the other two sheets, and the drawer is on nothing and is not locked and has not been opened.**

**He did not ask her twice. He did not give it to her to take. He did not ask whether she would take it if he paid for the bus.**

**And here is the thing he noticed at about eight o'clock, and did not say, and told nobody, and which is on this page because the load book is the only place in this manuscript where a man of twenty-two is allowed to be seen clearly by a reader without a second person having to notice.**

**She was right. That is not the part. The part is that she was right and he knew she was right about four seconds before she finished saying it, and he had the paper in his hand when he already knew, and he asked her to take it anyway, because he wanted to have offered it.**

**He has been the person who gets asked for eleven years of work. He has never once asked himself what it costs to be the person who asks.**
