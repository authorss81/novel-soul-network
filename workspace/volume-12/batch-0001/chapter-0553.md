**Here is what happened next, and it took about four seconds, and no part of it was a plan.**

Marek had four questions he was entitled to ask anybody in this city. One of them was the one a man in a yard would have answered on Thursday. One of them was about a sheet of paper that had gone to four places. One of them was whether anybody in this city had heard from the woman of about twenty-four.

**He did not ask the third one of a person he was allowed to ask it of, and he asked it of the man of about thirty-two, and the reason he asked it of the man of about thirty-two is that the man of about thirty-two has a rota for nine flats and four of those flats are let to people whose names he knows by heart and who would have said something if anything had happened.**

That is a good reason. It is a real reason. It is the reason a person gives when the true one is worse.

**It is not the reason. The reason is that the man of about thirty-two owed him nothing, had never asked him for anything, and would not be owed a refusal.**

**"Have you heard from her,"** Marek said. **"The woman of about twenty-four. Has anybody in your building heard from her."**

The man of about thirty-two did not answer for about nine seconds. He looked at the part on the bench. Then he said:

**"No."**

**"Right."**

**"No,"** the man said, **"and that's because nobody in those nine flats has anything to do with the woman of about twenty-four, and you know that, and I want to say the other thing before you go and find somebody else to ask."**

**"Say it."**

**"I've got a sister in the third of them,"** the man said, **"and she's had a letter off that office, and if you go and ask anybody else in this city whether that woman is all right, the next thing that happens is somebody tells my sister that somebody's asking, and my sister will think the letter's about her, and she will ask me why, and she will not open the letter, and she will not tell me why."**

**"Right."**

**"That's all. I'm not saying you did it on purpose. You'd not do it on purpose."**

**"No. I did it in about four seconds and I'd have done it in about four seconds again if you'd not been standing here."**

The man of about thirty-two took the part off the bench, paid for it, and went out into the street, and he did not say thank you, and he was not thanked, and he has not raised it since.

**It took him the rest of that Friday, and about an hour of that Saturday, to work out that the sentence he had said on Thursday — a sheet with his name at the foot of it would make her the messenger and would be worse than the nine names — was the same sentence, said by him, to a different person, on a different day, and that he had broken it about four hours after he had built it.**

**He did not unsay it. He was not going to unsay it, and he understood inside about four seconds that a man who goes and says sorry to a person for asking a question is asking a second question, and that the second question is the one where he explains why the first one was reasonable.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
