# Chapter 556 — A Cold Store On A Farm In The First Of The Four Towns, Seen From The Outside, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Who Has Kept His Own Record For About Four Years On A Clipboard, And A Form With No Box On It For What He Writes

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a room off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was one farm in the first of the four towns, put here from the outside of a gate by somebody who was not on it and has never been on it.**

---

The Thursday's list was eleven long and all eleven were dated, and eleven people came through that counter and the last of them at about ten past five. A morning on the bench in Lattice Ward, the counter from two to six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, warm, four of them and one behind. It is nine on the ticket and then it is ten, and it has been that for about eleven years, and the two of them do not add up to anything and are not added up. **The heat in that flat is on and costs more than it did in the winter, four names on that tenancy carry the rise, and a fortnight that a cook laid out of her own money is owed to nobody and will be owed to nobody by the end of this month.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and sixty-nine days, one hundred and thirty-eight weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and seventy-three days, one hundred and thirty-eight weeks and seven days, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

**Nobody in this day's account was in the first of the four towns on this day or on any other day of these ten days, and the man at the counter in this city has never been in the first of the four towns in his life, and that is a sentence about geography and not about a man.**

---

**The farm is on a rise and the gate is a field gate and the cold store is a block building with a compressor on the side of it and a man in the seat inside the door from about six in the morning.**

**What is put here is what four things can be seen to be from the wrong side of a gate by a person who has driven past it once, and the person is the man of about thirty-four who drove up in a van on the Friday and who has not been back to that gate and is not going to.**

---

**ONE. The gate is not locked. It has a chain on it and the chain is not fastened and has not been fastened since about the spring, and there is one track through the mud and about four sets of tyre marks going out and none coming back, because the track out is a van's and the van is not there.**

**TWO. There is a clipboard on a nail inside the cold-store door, at about shoulder height, and it is a shop's clipboard, the kind with a sprung clip, and the paper on it is not the paper of a form and has never been the paper of a form, and it is ruled in three columns in a hand that is not a printed hand, and the hand has not changed in about four years, and the top of the first column says OUT and the top of the second says BACK and the third column has no heading at all.**

**THREE. The three columns are not a ledger of what was sold. They are a ledger of what went into the cold store and what came out of it and how long the seal on the door was open, and the third column — the one with no heading — is the number of minutes a door was open with the compressor running, and he has written that number on about nine days in a fortnight when he can remember it and left it blank on the days he cannot, and he has never left a mark on a day when the door was shut, and there are about four hundred and forty marks in a hand that does not change.**

**FOUR. The clip on the cold-store door is the right one. It was put on in about the spring and it is a clip and it is the kind of clip that comes forty to a card, and the seal has held every day since, and the man of about fifty-two has a machine in a corner of that building that reads the temperature inside and writes it on a strip of paper that comes out about every nine minutes, and he has been keeping those strips in a drawer since about the spring, and the drawer is not locked and is not labelled.**

---

**Here is the whole of what happened to the clipboard, and it is four sentences and it is all of it.**

**Nobody has asked him for the clipboard. He has never offered it to anybody. It has never been sent to a firm, and it is not a form, and there is no box on any form in this business for the number of minutes a door was open with the compressor running, and there is no column for a hand that has not changed in four years, and the man has never once described the arrangement as a record, and describes it, when anybody asks what it is, as the sheet I use.**

---

**THE FIRST CORRECT THING THESE TEN DAYS HAVE SEEN DONE THAT CHANGED NOTHING, and it was done by a person who is owed nothing, and it is not going to be improved on, and nobody is going to thank him for it, and there is no form for it.**

**The man is about fifty-two. He has a farm of about two hundred acres and eleven cows and a brother in the second of the four towns. He has been keeping the third column since about the spring of the year before last. On the Monday this week a printed sheet went to four places and deliveries stopped and the clip stopped coming and he has four weeks of clip in a drawer in that cold store and a new one on the door and no way to buy more until the card comes back, which is about nine days, or eleven with a weekend on it.**

**He did not open a shop. He did not go to another town. He did not write to the office on the card, and the reason he did not write is that the card is a card and a card takes about nine days, and he is in that cold store from about six in the morning, and a person carrying a question four days each way is a person not in a cold store for about four days, and he is the only one of the two.**

**What he did was open the cold store at half past five on the Monday morning, write the date in the first column, and leave the third column blank, and write the word COLD at the top of the page in capitals, and close the door, and go and do the cows.**

**That is a correct thing. It is correct because a record of a door that has not been opened is a record, and a blank third column is a true fact, and a person who writes COLD at the top of a page in capitals has told the next person, whoever the next person is, that everything under the word is about a store that has not been open. He has done it on every morning of this week and on every morning of about the last two years and nobody has ever asked him why.**

**It changed nothing. The clip stopped coming on the Monday and is not coming back for about nine days and the thing he wrote on a clipboard on the Monday morning did not make a single tray of anything last nine seconds longer, and he knows that, and he went and did the cows anyway, and the reason a man keeps a record nobody has asked for is not that it works.**

---

**It is put here at that length because the man at the counter in this city spent about an hour of the Thursday of the week before last trying to work out what a person in a town four days away could do about a card, and he came up with nine lines, and the nine lines were about what a person would have to be able to do in one day in order to be able to do it in nine, and none of the nine lines was that you can write COLD on a page and go and do the cows, and that is the one that was in the room and he did not see it.**

---

**What the man of about twenty-two wanted, and did not say, on that Thursday, was to be told about the clipboard by somebody in that town, and he is not going to be told about it, and the reason he is not going to be told about it is that nobody in that town knows he exists and a man who goes and asks to be told things about a clipboard in a town four days away is a man who has started collecting.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
