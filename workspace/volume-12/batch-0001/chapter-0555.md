# Chapter 555 — The Cost Of A Favour Named On A Person Of About Forty-One Who Does A Thursday Shop And A School Run And Who Did Not Know She Was Carrying Anything And Who Is Not Going To Be Told, In A Face, In About Four Minutes, And No Apology

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a room off that service road from about half past six, and its shape was about four minutes at the back of a shop with the door shut, and neither man in it said the other one was wrong.**

---

Eleven on the Wednesday, eleven of them with a date against them, eleven people through that counter, and the last of them at about ten past four. Lattice Ward in the morning and that counter from two to six, and then the four rooms off the service road where the heat is on and about four people come in for it. The ticket says nine and then ten and the shop does about nine, and the ten is a habit of the pen rather than a fact, and this is not new and it is not going to be fixed this week. **That flat's heating is on at a rate above the winter's, the rise is being carried by four names on one tenancy, and a fortnight a cook put out of her own wages is still unclaimed by anybody at the end of this month.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and sixty-eight days, one hundred and thirty-eight weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and seventy-two days, one hundred and thirty-eight weeks and six days, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

**The twentieth line on two nails above that bench is blank and has been blank on every day of this year and on every day of the year before it, and no one has opened it, and the nineteen lines above it are the whole of what is on that board.**

---

**He came in at about half past two and shut the door behind him, which nobody does in that shop, and put a part on the bench and did not take his coat off.**

**"I want to say a thing and I want to say all of it and then I want to go and do my Thursday."**

**"Go on."**

**"I asked you on the Friday whether anybody in my nine flats had heard from her. I did not ask because of my nine flats. I asked because you were standing there and because you'd have said no quicker."**

**"That is true and it is not what I'm about to say."**

**"I know what you're about to say. I did it on the Friday and I knew I'd done it on the Friday and I did it again on the Friday in my head about nine times to see whether it came out different. It comes out the same. That's not what I'm about to say either."**

**"Then say the thing."**

---

**"I asked my sister-in-law's neighbour,"** the man said. **"That's the whole of it. She's in the third of them, she's about forty-one, she's got a boy of about eleven, and she does the shop on a Thursday and the school run on a Wednesday and she does a bit of both on a Friday, and her name is not going in this conversation and she is not going to be told."**

**"Right."**

**"She had a letter off that office in — I'm not going to tell you when. And on the Friday I went round to her on my lunch, which I don't have, so I lost half an hour I get paid for, and I asked her a thing. Not about the letter. About whether she'd heard from anybody, from anywhere, about anything at all in about four days."**

**"And she said no."**

**"And she said no, and then she said, and this is the bit, she said: I haven't, but I asked the woman at the clinic, because the woman at the clinic is in the third of them and the woman at the clinic knows everything, and the woman at the clinic said there was nothing that week."**

**"So she spent a Thursday asking a person at a clinic a question about a week."**

**"She spent a Thursday standing in a queue at a clinic she does not attend, being pleasant, being fair, being exactly as she'd be to anybody, and then she came home and cooked a Thursday, and she was not late for anything, and she lost about a day of her Thursday in a way that you cannot see from outside the Thursday."**

**"About a day."**

**"About a day, and I am not saying it in a way that asks you to feel bad about it, because if I say it in a way that asks you to feel bad about it then I have made it about me, and it is about her, and she will never know."**

---

**The shop was quiet for about nine seconds. A cage with a man in it, facing away. A roller shutter down to the floor. A tool roll on the bench with a screwdriver down a fourth pocket that has been down that pocket for two years and has not been replaced and costs a Saturday.**

**"Why are you telling me."**

**"Because you are going to go and do the same thing again,"** the man said, **"and because I have got a sister in the third of them who will not open a letter because somebody asked after her, and because the day you do it again and somebody else does it, and those two things meet, the thing they will meet on is her."**

**"I am not going to do it again."**

**"You might,"** the man said, **"and I'm not going to be the one who says you won't, because I said the same thing in about October and I've been wrong about one thing already this week."**

---

**"Is that all."**

**"No. Two things and then I'll do my Thursday."**

**"Go on."**

**"One. You wrote the rule. Not the rule about the office — the rule about not asking somebody else to find out. That one came through the same place as the other one and you had your name nowhere near it and you had your hands near all of it, and I've read the thing on the site and I know what a committee does to a draft."**

**"I did not write it."**

**"No. You didn't. And you're the reason a woman of about thirty-four had something to say when she was asked to say it, and both of those are true, and neither one washes the other."**

**"And two."**

**"Two is that if you ever need to know whether she's all right,"** the man said, **"then ask me, and I'll find out, and it will take me a day and a half and it will cost her nothing, because I have a Thursday and a Friday and I can be the one that is annoying in a way that costs me."**

---

**He did not say yes. That is the whole of the change in the day and nobody in it noticed, because the man had his coat on and his part in his hand and was already at the door.**

**What he did not say yes to was a sentence that was true and was offered in about nine seconds by a person who is owed nothing and who has already paid once this week on somebody else's account.**

**He did not say no either, and he did not say thank you, and the man of about thirty-two went out into the street and did his Thursday and has not come back to that shop about a thing like that since, and there is a rota for nine flats and about nine names on it and the rota goes in a drawer on a Friday.**

---

**He did not apologise. He has thought about it since, on and off, for about eleven days, and the reason he has not is that apologising is a second asking, and the second asking is always the one where a person explains why the first thing was reasonable, and a person explaining why the first thing was reasonable is a person making the second thing about themselves.**

**What he did instead, at about three o'clock on that Wednesday, with the door shut, was to take a sheet of paper out of the drawer where the two other sheets were and write at the top of it the word NO, and under it four lines, and then he put the sheet in the drawer as well, which means there are now three sheets in that drawer, and none of the three is on anything, and the drawer is not labelled and is not locked and has not been opened by anybody in these ten days.**

---

**At about half past five the man of about fifty-one was at the north wall of a room in another district with his bag on the floor at his feet, and had been there five hundred and seventy-four days, which is eighty-two weeks to the day, and had said nothing on any day this month. The request he made in the spring, that it be written down somewhere by somebody who is not the man at the counter, is still unmade and is still owed, and there are now three sheets in a drawer in a shop in this city, and none of them is it, and that is not a reason to put one of them there and it is a reason not to.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
