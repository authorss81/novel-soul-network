# Chapter 586 — The Forty-Third Sitting Of A Room Above A Line In Another District, About Eleven People Up A Stair, Nine Items At Four Minutes, A Tenth Thing That Was One Sentence And Was Not An Item, A Book That Stood At Sixty Lines And Stood At Sixty-One, A Count Of Forty-Eight Announced Of Which Forty-Three Correspond, And Two Figures That Are Each Other

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, a second floor above a line from about ten until about eleven, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was about eleven people up a stair and nine items at four minutes and a sentence that was not an item.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, every one of them dated, eleven people through that counter, the last of them at about ten past five. Bench until two, the counter two until six, the floor above the line from ten to eleven, the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that one tenancy are carrying it between them, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and thirty-eight days, one hundred and forty-eight weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and forty-two days, one hundred and forty-eight weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The eighteenth line on two nails above that bench is seven hundred and fifty-six days old, one hundred and eight weeks to the day, and seven hundred and fifty-six is also the day the man of about fifty-one first sat down against that north wall in this city, and that man is six hundred and forty-four days, ninety-two weeks to the day, and six hundred and forty-four is also the day the eighteenth line was written on that board. One of the two figures is an age and one of the two is a day something happened, they are both on this Wednesday nine feet apart, and neither of the two is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, which is a closed thing in this entry and carries no figure of days off for it anywhere in it.**

The hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and seventy-one days, ninety-five weeks and six days, and the ask is seven hundred and twenty-eight days, one hundred and four weeks to the day, and neither of the two is a day of that heating.

---

About eleven people came up that stair. Six of them had been in the same chair on the last Wednesday of that kind and five had not, and the fifth of those five was a man who had carried a case up a flight on the Sunday night and had a carrier bag in his hand and had put it down at the bottom of the stair and picked it up again and had not asked anybody to take it.

Nine chairs curve in with the two ends turned in, and the ninth of them stands hard against that wall with its back to the whole room, and it did not move in the hour, and nobody looked at it more than once, and whoever moves it is not named in this entry and is not going to be named in any of the ten.

A table. A tin standing open, with no lid on it and no lock on it and none that it has ever carried. A book of four columns face up inside the reach of a woman's right hand from ten until eleven.

**Nine items at four minutes each, and all nine of them went the way nine of them go every fourth Wednesday of that kind for about nineteen years.**

A man of about nineteen in for the second time who had not spoken at the last one and did not speak at this one, and sat, and was not asked. A woman of about thirty-seven who wanted to bring a thing that had happened to a shop four days of bus travel away, told without unkindness that the shop is not hers to carry in there. A man of about fifty-eight who said one sentence about a door and would not say a second. A woman of about forty-six who wanted to know whether a count and a number are the same thing, told in about nine seconds that they are not, and not told why. A man of about thirty-four in for the third Wednesday running who said nothing at all and came again anyway. A person of about sixty-one who has been in that room for nineteen years and who said the one thing in the hour that anybody said twice, and both times it was the word *March*, and nobody wrote it down. A woman of about twenty-seven who wanted to leave something in that room and was told she could leave it on the table, and that the table was not hers. A man of about forty-three who read nine words out loud and then read the same nine words differently, and was asked which of the two he meant, and said both. A woman of about twenty-six who said a true thing about a shop in her own town and was agreed with by nobody in the room.

**The tenth thing was not an item. It was the man with the carrier bag, and it was one sentence, and nobody in that room knew before he said it that it was going to be a sentence and not a thing.**

He was about twenty-nine and he loads stock for a firm four days of bus travel away, and he had been on the Sunday night bus, and he waited until the ninth item was finished and the tin had not been touched.

**"That weight on the second shelf in that store has been mine since the spring."**

**She wrote it in about nine seconds in one line and she asked him not one question about the weight.**

Not what it weighs. Not how it got there. Not whose it was before. Not whether anybody had seen it. She asked him nothing at all about the weight, and he sat in the sixth chair for the rest of the hour and did not say it again.

**The book went in. It was holding sixty lines when she sat down at ten and it was holding sixty-one when the hour was over, and that is the first line that has gone into it since two sittings ago, and the reason is not a rule and is not evidence of anything and is not a change in her: it is that a person said a thing about himself into her face with nine other people in nine chairs within hearing of every word of it, and that is what a line in that book is.**

Nothing went into the tin. The tin stands at seventy-three and has stood at seventy-three for about nineteen years.

**Nobody asked her why it opened. Nobody in that room has asked her why in about nineteen years, nobody asked her why on this Wednesday, and nobody is going to ask her why at the next one. And nobody in that room has ever asked her whether she is all right, and about four of the nine would not know what to ask, and the fifth of the nine has not thought about it, and she has never raised it herself.**

**The count was announced at the end, and a count is announced at a sitting and on no other morning: forty-eight, of which forty-three correspond.** Forty-three of the forty-eight match the forty-three times that book has been open, and the remaining five are older than the book and stand on a sheet of paper she has not shown to a living person. On the nine days before this one the count stood at forty-seven of which forty-two, and the two states are two states, and one and one is a count and is not a distance from anything.

**What he noticed on that Wednesday, and told nobody on the stair or at the counter, is that a man carrying a case up a flight himself is doing the same job as a woman writing a line without asking a question, which is carrying a thing without opening it.**

He has spent four years opening things that were brought to him. The man brought a sentence and did not ask for it to be mended. The woman took it and did not open it either. Both of them carried it shut up a stair and down again, and the book is heavier by one line and nobody knows what the line weighs, and that is the first time in about four years he has watched two people do his own job better than he does it.

**What he wanted, on the Wednesday night, in four rooms with the heat on and about four people in them who were not asked anything, was to have carried the case himself and to have waited at the bottom of the stair while the man went up, and to have been the person left holding a bag that somebody trusts enough to leave. He wanted it for about a minute and then he wanted the minute back, because the man trusted nobody with the bag and carried it himself, and that is why the sentence could be said, and a man of twenty-two holding it would have made it a delivery and not a saying.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Wednesday, which is on no form and which nobody asked for: the stair ran past eleven and he missed his tea in the rooms off the service road, and he ate standing at the bench with the shutter half down, bread and cold, in about four minutes, and the bread caught in his throat and he drank water from the yard tap and did not sit down once, and about four people came in for the heat while he was still standing and none of them remarked on it.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*Conditions and docket.* **The Wednesday ran to eleven jobs with a date against every one of the eleven and eleven people through that counter door, the last at about ten past five.** The day as spent: the bench in Lattice Ward to two, that counter from two until six, the floor above the line in another district between ten and eleven, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six onwards. *The weight, as a record of one sentence, one line, and one question not asked.* **Where: a second floor above a line in another district, nine chairs curved in, a table, an open tin with no lid and no lock, and a book of four columns within reach of a woman's right hand. Who said it: a man of about twenty-nine who loads stock for a firm four days of bus travel away, who came up on the Sunday night on about eleven hours of bus, who carried a case up a flight himself and did not ask anybody to take the bottom of it, and who waited until the ninth item was over and the tin had not been touched. What he said, in about four seconds, once: that the weight on the second shelf in that store has been his since the spring. What the store is: a cold store on a farm above a town four days of bus travel away, with a clipboard on a nail inside its door, three columns in it, no heading on the third, and about four hundred and forty marks in a hand that has not changed in about four years. What she did: wrote it in one line in about nine seconds. What she did not do: ask him what it weighs, how it came to be there, whose it was before, or whether anybody had seen it; she asked him nothing about the weight and he sat in the sixth chair for the rest of the hour and did not say it again. What went into the book: one line, taking it from sixty to sixty-one. What went into the tin: nothing; it stands at seventy-three. Who asked her why it opened: nobody, as in about nineteen years of Wednesdays, and nobody is going to at the next one. Who has ever asked her whether she is all right: nobody in that room. What was announced at the end: forty-eight of which forty-three correspond, against forty-seven of which forty-two on the nine days before. Whether the woman in the fourth column of that store is named in the line: she is not, and nobody asked after her, and nobody has ever asked after her in this room. Owner: a second shelf, a weight, one line in that book of four columns, and a man in a sixth chair who came four days to say eleven words and was thanked by nobody including the woman who wrote them down.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A man of about twenty-nine carried a case up a flight and waited until the ninth item was finished and said one ordinary sentence about a cold store into a woman's face, and the book took it, and she asked him nothing about the weight, and nobody asked her why it opened, and nobody in that room has ever asked her whether she is all right.** Nobody thanked anybody on that stair and nobody thanked anybody at a counter in Lattice Ward, the shutter came down at about ten, and there is no Sunday among these ten entries and a shop does not open on a Sunday. He taught nobody and demonstrated nothing and assessed nobody and assisted nobody on the Wednesday, and nobody put any of the four to him, and standing at the back of a stair for an hour is not one of the four. **He was nine feet from a man who said out loud that a weight is his, and he had known since a Friday evening in the last movement that there is a weight on a second shelf in that store and that nobody in that town is going to move it, and he did not know that these were the same weight, and he has not asked, and the man of about thirty-three who told him about it four days' travel away does not know either, and neither of them will ever put the two together, and that is the whole of what a stair is.** The count of correct things that changed nothing, done by people who are owed nothing, stands at two and does not move here, and the man of about twenty-nine is not a third instance of it, because carrying a case up a flight is not a correct thing done in nine seconds and it changed nothing and he was owed a fare by his own firm. The card in the rail is one thousand and forty-two days old, four days past the room, in a rail that takes two, unmoved and unturned. The rooms are one thousand and thirty-eight days old and warm. The board stands at nineteen lines and its twentieth is unopened. That man of about fifty-one sat against the north wall on the Wednesday evening with his bag down beside him and said nothing at all, and what is owed to him is a written thing, and there is nowhere in this city to write it. The Exchange's book stands at sixty-one and the tin at seventy-three and the difference between the two is not a number and has never been printed as one. Not one of the four words that name a public body is written anywhere in this entry, on the stair, or in that room. The ninth chair did not move and its mover is not named. The woman's page is shut inside a binder on a shelf. The drawer under that bench is shut and is not on anything.
