# Chapter 583 — A First Floor Above A Line Of Shop Units On A Thursday Afternoon With About Nine People In It And A Sheet Going Round The Table, Four Seconds From A Woman Of About Forty-Four Who Keeps A List, Four Seconds From A Man Of About Thirty-Six Who Signs What She Puts In Front Of Him, And Nobody On A Van In A Yard Two Streets Away Asked At All

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was a sheet going round a table in a first floor and one sentence and two answers and a yard two streets away where nobody had been told anything.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, a date written against every one of them, eleven people through the counter, the last of them at about ten past five. The bench until two, the counter from two until six, the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heating in that flat is on and up on what it was all last winter, four names on that one tenancy are carrying the difference, and a fortnight a cook took out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to stay owed to nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and thirty-two days, one hundred and forty-seven weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and thirty-six days, one hundred and forty-eight weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The twentieth line on those two nails has never been opened and the nineteenth is seven hundred and twenty-eight days, one hundred and four weeks to the day. The hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and sixty-five days, ninety-five weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one at that north wall is six hundred and thirty-eight days, ninety-one weeks and one day, and the ask is seven hundred and twenty-two days, one hundred and three weeks and one day. None of the three is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating is a closed thing in this case and no days-off figure is carried for it anywhere in this entry.**

---

**He was not in that room and he was told about it at about half past three at his own counter by a woman of about thirty-four who types for a firm in a second district, who came down a stair and stood at the front of that counter for about four minutes and then went back up, and who has typed sheets for that first floor for about nine years.**

**"They did it in about four seconds each,"** she said. **"That's the whole of it. She asked and he did it and they were finished before the sheet got back to the top."**

**"What did she ask."**

**"She asked whether a household could come off it entirely, and not just stop using the store for a while, but off it, the whole thing, and she said it in one go without stopping and she did not sit down."**

**"And he said."**

**"He said yes."**

**"Did anybody ask whether it was all right with the people it would fall on."**

**"No,"** the woman of about thirty-four said. **"And that's not a thing anybody would have asked, and I'm the one who types the sheets and I thought about it after, on the stairs, and I did not go back up."**

Nobody thanked anybody in that first floor or at that counter or in a yard two streets away.

---

**The room is a first floor above four shop units, one long room, a table, nine chairs, a window with a rail on it and nothing to look at out of it, and there were about nine people in it and a sheet going round.**

She was about forty-four and she keeps the winter list for that row of buildings at the end of the first of the four towns, and the list is a thing she holds and a thing she is asked about, and it is a post in the plainest sense available, which is that nobody can change it except her and one other person and the other person is the man who signs.

He was about thirty-six and he has signed what she puts in front of him for about four years and has never once not signed it, and he is not a villain of anything and was not on that Thursday and could not have stopped it if he had wanted to.

**"There's a family at the end of the row who want to come off it,"** she said. **"Not pause. Off. Everything. The key, the plate, the line, the chain."**

That is about nine seconds and she said it in four.

**"Then they come off it."**

**"Then they come off it."**

**"Will that hold."**

**"It'll hold the way it holds,"** the man of about thirty-six said. **"It'll hold because there's no way of stopping it and there's no way of it stopping either."**

Nobody said anything else about it. The sheet went round.

**And about nine people in this city are on the wrong side of it, and none of them was asked, and all nine of them were in a yard two streets away at about the same time on that Thursday afternoon loading a van for a town four days of bus travel away.**

They are the walking party. Every other week one of them goes out on that van and walks the end of that row from the first building to the last and counts the plates on the board and writes the count on a sheet and brings the sheet back, and the count is about four hundred, and the count is the whole of what proves the arrangement is alive, and it goes on a form that a firm in a second district uses to decide whether to send that town any more stock in the spring. A man of about fifty-eight drove the van. A man of about thirty-one and a man of about forty-four went with him. A woman of about twenty-seven, a man of about twenty-four, a woman of about forty-three, a man of about thirty-six, a woman of about twenty-nine and a man of about forty-seven walked that row on and off for between four and nine years between them.

**A number that is right on a board and a household that has gone are not the same fact, and the man who walks the row is the only person who will ever find out, and from Thursday he has to stop at that house as well.**

**He was not told. None of the nine was told, and about four of them found out on the Friday because the man of about fifty-eight told them in a yard without being asked and without doing it kindly, and about five of the nine did not find out at all that week.**

The man of about fifty-eight is not owed anything. He drove four days in the last movement to stand in a yard and repeat nine seconds that a woman of about fifty-two had said at a gate, and nobody thanked him then and nobody thanked him on this Thursday, and the yard was full and he said it into the side of a van.

---

**What he noticed on that Thursday evening, and told nobody, is that the whole of it took eight seconds and that eight seconds is the correct length of time for a thing that is right.**

There is nothing wrong with any of it. A household asked to come off a thing it does not want has asked correctly and the answer was correct and it was given correctly and fast, and about nine people who will be asked to account for a number that has not changed were not in the room and could not have stopped it and would not have been allowed to stop it, and every one of those two sentences is true at once. **He has been the person a room goes to for four years. He worked out, standing at a counter at about half past four, that being the person a room goes to and being one of the nine the room did not go to are the same job, and that a man of twenty-two is only ever offered one of them, and that the one he is offered is the one that costs nine people who are not in the room their winter.**

**What he wanted, and did not say to the woman of about thirty-four who types, and did not say to the four people who came in for the heat, was to have been in that first floor at about two in the afternoon on a Thursday and to be one of the nine in a yard two streets away, and not to be the man at the counter who the woman of about thirty-four comes down to afterwards. He has never once in about four years wanted to be in a yard with nine people in it, and he wanted it on that Thursday for about four minutes, and the wanting had a shape, and the shape was that he wanted to be the person who is not asked, which is a thing he has never had and has no idea what it is like.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Thursday, which is on no form and which cost him about nine pounds of his own money, is that a machine belonging to a woman of about twenty-nine had been on his bench since the Monday with her name on a docket, and he told her on the Monday it would be ready on the Thursday, and at about five on the Thursday he found out from the woman of about thirty-four who types that she had taken it to a shop in a second district on the Wednesday because she could not wait until the Thursday, and he did not say that he was sorry, because he was not, and he did not say that she was right, because he had said the other thing on the Monday and two of those cannot stand in the same week.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
