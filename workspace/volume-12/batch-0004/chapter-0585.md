# Chapter 585 — A Yard In A Second District On A Monday Afternoon With About Nine People And A Van Being Loaded For A Town Four Days Of Bus Travel Away, Nine Seconds Out Of A Woman Of About Forty-One Naming A Cost That Is Worse Than The Stoppage Was, And Then Four Of The Nine Saying The Other Half Of It To A Man Of About Twenty-Two One At A Time

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a yard in a second district from about three until about half past five, and its shape was nine seconds from a woman of about forty-one about a cost that is worse than a stoppage, and then four people saying the other half of it in a yard.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, a date against every one of the eleven, eleven people through that counter, the last of them at about half past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, that yard from about three until about half past five, the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that one lease are carrying the difference between them, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and thirty-six days, one hundred and forty-eight weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and forty days, one hundred and forty-eight weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The twentieth line on those two nails is still shut. The seventeenth line is eight hundred and eight days, one hundred and fifteen weeks and three days, and the man of about fifty-one at that north wall is six hundred and forty-two days, ninety-one weeks and five days, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and sixty-nine days, ninety-five weeks and four days, and the ask is seven hundred and twenty-six days, one hundred and three weeks and five days. None of those three is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, which is a closed thing in this entry and carries no figure of days off for it anywhere.**

---

**He was in that yard because the man of about fifty-eight who drives the van asked him on the Friday, at the end of a shift, in about four seconds, to be in the yard at three on the Monday, and Marek said he would, and he was in the yard at about ten past three.**

The van is about nine feet long and has a shutter down one side that does not shut past the middle, and it is loaded on a Monday for a Thursday, and about nine people were in that yard and none of them had been told.

She was there too. **She had been in this city for eight days and had not gone back, and the reason she had not gone back is that a stoppage she had been sitting out since the spring ended on the Friday night and there was nothing left in her town to sit out.**

---

**She said the second thing at about ten past four and it took about nine seconds and she said it to the nine of them and not to him.**

**"It is worse than the stoppage was. The stoppage ended."**

Nobody in that yard said a word for about four seconds and a shutter went down about nine feet away.

**"The stoppage took two deliveries and a Saturday off me and it ended on a Friday by itself and nobody had to do anything. This does not end. Every other week one of these nine is going to stand on my step at six in the morning and ask us if we meant it, and there is nothing on that step to sit down on and there is no light on it, and my mother is seventy-one and she is in that house, and the step is ours, and we have to answer it from behind a door with the chain on and it will be every other week for the whole winter and every winter after it."**

**"We could stop asking."**

**"You cannot stop asking,"** she said. **"The number is still on the board in the right place in the line and about four hundred people are going to stand at that standpipe in the dark and the count that comes back on that sheet is the count that decides whether that town gets anything in the spring, and one of the nine is going to have to go down that row and find out which number it is, and the only way to find out is to knock."**

**"Then we write it on the sheet."**

**"You cannot write it on the sheet,"** she said. **"It is not on my sheet. It is on a step in the dark, and the step is mine."**

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody in that yard said the word to her and nobody said it to the man of about twenty-two who had written four names on a docket and who was standing by the shutter doing nothing at all.

---

**Then four of the nine said the other half of it, one at a time, to Marek, over about an hour and a quarter, in that yard, and the other five of the nine did not say anything to anybody all afternoon.**

They said it into his face because he was the only person in that yard who was not on the van and not on a wage out of it, and because he had been introduced on the Friday as the man who wrote the four names down, and because a person who has been handed a thing to write down is a person other people say things to.

**A man of about thirty-one, who has walked that row nine times.**

**"We should have been asked. Not before. Before we'd have said no. Before is when you get a no and a no is a thing you can put in front of somebody. After, we've got this."**

**A woman of about twenty-seven, who has walked that row four times.**

**"I should have been asked and I want it on the record that I should have been asked, and I want it on the record from you, and I don't want it on the record from anybody in that building, because anybody in that building is paid."**

**A man of about forty-four, who has walked that row six times.**

**"We should have been asked, and I'll tell you why, and it's not noble. If we'd been asked we'd have offered. We would have offered to go down that row every week instead of every other week and we'd have done it for nothing, and you'd have got it for nothing, and now it's a step in the dark at six in the morning and that's the whole of what's left."**

**A man of about twenty-four, who has walked that row twice.**

**"We should have been asked because I don't know what I'm doing now and I never learned it and I did the six before it on Friday and I've not done one since."**

**Saying you should have been asked is a different thing from being asked, and all four of the four said it to a man of twenty-two who is on no list and who cannot act on it and who did not act on it, and none of the four asked him to.**

---

**What he noticed on that Monday evening, and told nobody, is that a cost which is worse than a stoppage and does not end is a thing that can only be paid by a person who is not in the room, and that the whole of the arrangement he has been standing next to for four years works because about nine people are in a room in a second district and about four hundred are at a standpipe in the dark, and neither number can see the other, and neither number is wrong.**

There is no form for that and he looked for one on the Friday and there is not one, and a form with a box on it would have made this worse and not better, because the whole of what went wrong is that four people were put on a board as a number and never once as a step in the dark at six in the morning.

**What he wanted, on the Monday night, in four rooms with the heat on and about four people in them who were not asked anything, was to be the man who knocks on that step. He wanted it for about four seconds and then he did not want it, because the man who knocks on that step is a man that step opens to, and the woman of about forty-one said in a yard on a Monday afternoon that the step is hers, and she said it correctly, and he has no right to it and no way of getting there.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Monday, which is on no form and which was not nothing: he gave up a whole Monday afternoon of paid work in a shop that is his own, in a yard two miles from it, in the cold, on the strength of nine seconds asked for by a man of about fifty-eight on a Friday, and he did not ask what he would be paid, and he did not tell the woman of about thirty-four who answers that counter where he was, and she covered for him in about four seconds with a lie about a machine that has not come out of the yard since the Thursday before last, and she did not ask him about it, and he did not thank her, because thanking her would have been about him.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
