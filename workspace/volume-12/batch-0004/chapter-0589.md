# Chapter 589 — A Monday With About Four Hundred Households Behind It And One Winter In Front Of It, A Fortnight Of Knowing Carried By A Person With An Ordinary Job, And Four Households Finding Out Separately And Telling Another With Nobody Organising Any Of It

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was a fortnight of knowing and four households finding out separately and telling another without anybody organising it.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, a date set down against every one of the eleven, eleven people through that counter door, the last of them at about half past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, a back room off the back of the shop for about half an hour in the middle of the afternoon, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and dearer than the winter that is coming, four names on that one tenancy are carrying the whole of the increase between them, and a fortnight a cook paid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody when the month turns.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and forty-three days, one hundred and forty-nine weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and forty-seven days, one hundred and forty-nine weeks and four days, four days past the rooms, and the rail holds two.

**The nineteenth line on two nails above that bench is seven hundred and thirty-nine days, one hundred and five weeks and four days. The hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and seventy-six days, ninety-six weeks and four days, and the ask is seven hundred and thirty-three days, one hundred and four weeks and five days, and the man of about fifty-one at that north wall is six hundred and forty-nine days, ninety-two weeks and five days. None of the four is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating in this case is a shut thing with no figure of days off carried for it on any page of this entry.**

---

**She has known for about a fortnight and she has not acted on it and she is not going to, and the fortnight is the whole of what she is carrying.**

She is about thirty-eight. She keeps the pumping station at the end of a row of buildings in the first of the four towns, she reads its dials, she is the only person in that town paid to know what came through the main in a week, and she came up on a bus about a fortnight ago for a foot valve and put a card with two boxes on a bench in a back room about nine feet by eleven and was told in nine seconds that about four hundred of those cards cannot be got anybody to. She told about four people that afternoon. None of the four thanked her. She went back on a bus with the valve in a bag and the card in her inside pocket and the clip in the other one, and she has done nothing since, and the nothing is correct, and she arrived at the correctness of it in the back room in about four seconds and has not revisited it.

**"I have told nobody since,"** she said. **"Not because I was told not to. Because there is nobody to tell who could do anything, and the two who could are four days away, and a woman who walks down her own row saying a thing nobody can act on is a woman that row stops listening to before the winter."**

He had not gone to find her. She had come down on a bus again on the Sunday night, for washers this time, for the standpipe, and she slept on the same floor in the same second district, and she came to the counter at about ten past two with the washers ticket in her hand and stood there until the shop was empty.

**"Four of them know,"** she said. **"Not from me. I have not said one word since the Monday a fortnight ago. Four households at the standpipe, none of them next to each other, and each of them worked it out standing in the dark with a card in their hand, and each of them told one other household, and that is eight, and nobody organised it, and nobody wrote it down, and nobody thanked anybody for it either."**

**"How did they work it out."**

**"The same way I did. The house on the card is let and is not theirs. One of them went to the house in daylight. One of them asked the family in it. One of them stood at the board at the end of the row and counted the plates and found her own number and found the house and found the two did not meet. The fourth one is a boy of about fourteen who turned the card over and found nothing on the back and said out loud that a card with nothing on the back is a card nobody answers."**

**"What did the other four do with it."**

**"Nothing,"** she said. **"That is the part I came to tell you. Eight households know and eight households have done nothing, and the nothing is the same nothing I did, and none of the eight was thanked for finding it out, and none of the eight asked anybody to do anything about it, and the standpipe queue was the same length this morning as it was a fortnight ago to the household."**

Marek put the washers in a bag and wrote the date on the ticket. The date is the only thing he writes on tickets now that anybody looks at, and she looked at it and put it in her pocket without reading it, the way people put things away that they know they will need and know they cannot use.

**"Will it go on."**

**"Yes,"** she said. **"About four hundred people and one winter, and eight of the four hundred know, and the eight are doing what I am doing, which is carrying it. It goes on the way a thing goes on that nobody has stopped, which is that it goes on."**

She left at about twenty past three. The washers did not rattle in the bag because he had put a twist of paper round them, and the twist of paper is not on any sheet and nobody in that shop will ever count it.

---

**What he noticed on that Monday, and has not put in the notebook and is not going to, is that eight households knowing separately is not the same as eight households knowing together, and that the difference is the whole of the winter.**

A household that works a thing out in the dark with a card in its hand has done it alone and keeps it alone. Four households doing that in four different weeks are four solitudes and not a meeting. Each of the four told one other, and that is four pairs and not a row, and none of the four pairs knows about any of the other three, and the woman who keeps the station knows about all four and has told none of the four about any of the others, because telling them would be organising them, and organising them would make her the person the row comes back to, and she worked that out a fortnight ago in about four seconds in a back room.

**What he wanted, on the Monday night, in four rooms with the heat on and about four people in them who were not asked anything, was to be one of the eight and not the man the eight are told to. He wanted to stand at that standpipe in the dark with a card in his hand that has his own name on it and a house that is not his, and to work it out with his own hands, and to tell one other household and to be thanked by nobody for it, and to carry it the way the eight carry it. He did not say it. He has a bench and a counter and a rail with two cards in it and no card with his name on it anywhere in four towns.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Monday, which is on no form and which nobody saw: he had a pencil on the bench that he keeps short on purpose so that nobody borrows it, and the woman of about thirty-four who answers that counter asked him for it at about four to mark a docket, and he gave it to her and she wore it down to a stub marking eleven dates, and he left the stub on the counter for her and took a new one out of the drawer-side tin for himself, and the new one is longer than he likes and catches on the cuff of his coat, and he is going to keep it that way through the week and not say so.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A woman with an ordinary job who has known for about a fortnight that about four hundred people cannot be found at the end of a card said so at a counter, and the change since the fortnight is that about four of the four hundred have found out separately on their own and about four more have been told one each, and the whole of what has happened in a fortnight is eight knowing and eight doing nothing and nobody organising it.** Nobody was thanked at that counter in Lattice Ward, the shutter came down at about ten, and there is no Sunday in these ten entries and a repair counter does not do business on a Sunday.
