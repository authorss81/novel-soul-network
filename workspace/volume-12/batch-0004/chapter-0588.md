# Chapter 588 — A Second Floor In Crown Terrace On A Friday Afternoon With About Nine Chairs In It And About Nine People And A Sheet Going Round And A Box On That Sheet That Has Been Empty For About Four Years, Four Words Said Into It Once, Nine Seconds After Which The Woman Who Said Them Said She Is Not Going To Say Them Again In A Hall, And Nobody Thanked Her

**The Friday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, a second floor in Crown Terrace from about four until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was about nine people, a sheet, an empty box on it, and four words said once.**

**The second floor in Crown Terrace has about nine chairs in it and a table and a window that faces the back of a building, and it is not the room above a line in another district and it is not the room off the service road, and none of the nine chairs in it is the one that stands hard against a wall with its back to the room in the other building. It has been used on a Friday like this for about four years and about nine people come to it and it has no permanent staff.**

There were about nine of them. A man of about forty-four who deals in small claims. A woman of about twenty-nine who types. A man of about fifty-eight who drives a van out of a yard. A woman of about thirty-one who runs a room. A woman of about thirty-four who came up for the first time and sat on the end chair. **And Dr. Iona Sorn, who is fifty-three and has not been in that room before this and said so in about four seconds when somebody said the word *again*.** And a man of about sixty-one who has a clipboard and takes minutes. And a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a list of eleven. And a woman of about fifty-two who chairs nine chairs and sits on nothing else.

**The sheet went round with a box on it, and the box was for the name of the body that decides the thing, and it had been printed empty, and it had been going round that table for about four years with nothing in it.**

It is the only box on that sheet. Everything else on the sheet is a number or a date or a place.

**The chair read the box out. She did not explain it and she was not asked to and she said afterwards that there is nothing in it to explain, and about four people in that room have understood all four words of it and about five have understood none of them, and nobody asked anybody else in the room to say whether they had.**

**"The Commons Standing Committee."**

That is the whole of it. Four words, in a face, at about four, into a printed box on a sheet, with about nine people in a room about nine chairs and a window that faces a wall.

Nobody said a word for about four seconds.

**Then she said the other thing, in the same room, to the same nine people, in about nine seconds, and nobody had asked her to.**

**"I am not going to say that again in a hall. I said it once in a hall and about four hundred people heard it and not one of them could ask me what any of it meant, and about four of them tried in the road afterwards and were stopped by people who had been in the room, and that was on me. Nine of you can ask me. Any one of you can stop me in the middle of the next thing I say. That is the entire difference between that hall and this floor and it is not the number of people in it."**

**"It is a room."**

**"It is a room with nine people in it who can be answered for,"** she said. **"That is all. Write it in the box. I am not going to explain it and if anybody in this room explains it afterwards I will say out loud that they have not the office to, and I have said that before and I will say it again."**

**The man of about forty-four said: "I still don't know what any of those four words mean."**

**"No,"** she said. **"You do not, and you will not, and it is not your job to."**

**Dr. Iona Sorn, who is fifty-three, said one thing in that room on that Friday, and she said it in about nine seconds and about four of the nine were agreed with her and about five were not, and she is not on any list in this city and her name is not on the sheet and she is not the woman who said the four words.**

**"Nine of us can be wrong in a room and say so out loud. Four hundred cannot, because not one of them can be answered for."**

**"That's not a body,"** the man of about sixty-one said, **"that's a description."**

**"It is a description and the description is the body,"** she said, **"and I have watched a description get entered against a decision four times in this city and every one of the four was made by somebody who thought they were being precise."**

**Nobody thanked anybody.**

The man of about sixty-one wrote the four words into the box in about nine seconds, in a hand that is not hers, and the sheet went round again, and it went round with the box filled in, and about four of the nine noticed, after it had gone round, that nobody had said one word of thanks to the woman who had said it, and about four of the nine noticed it separately, at four different moments, over about an hour, and not one of the four said so out loud, and there is no form on which any of them could have said so out loud, and the woman who said the four words left at about ten past five and did not know that about four people had noticed and is never going to be told.

**Marek was at the back of that room on no list, with nothing in his hands, having not been invited and not having been refused, and he said nothing, and about four of the nine did not know he was there and about five did, and he has not spoken to any of them since.**

**What he noticed on that Friday, and told nobody, is that the difference between that floor and a hall is not how many people are in it. It is whether anybody in the room can be answered for afterwards.**

About four hundred and fifty people stood in a hall on a wide road in the last movement and a woman of about thirty-five said ten words in about nine seconds and was agreed with by nobody and thanked by nobody and given control of nothing, and not one of the four hundred and fifty could have stood up afterwards and said *I will answer for that*, because she was not in front of them and they did not know her. Nine people sat in a room on a Friday afternoon and a woman said four words and four of them could have stopped her before the end of the second sentence, and none of the nine did, and the difference is the whole of what a room is and he has spent four years trying to build one and had it built for him on a Friday and was not on the list for it.

**What he wanted, on the Friday night, in four rooms off a service road with the heat on and about four people in them who were not asked anything, was to have been on that sheet, and to have been one of the nine who could have answered for the four words, and to have been one of the four who said *I did not know what they meant* and meant it, and to have been asked afterwards by the man of about sixty-one whether the four words were right, and to have been able to say yes, and to have been wrong about it in a way that could be corrected. He is not on it. He has never been on one and there is no sheet in this city that he is on and there is no process by which one arrives.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Friday, which is on no form and which nobody will see: he stood in a doorway on that second floor for about nine minutes of somebody else's four words and his coat caught on the latch of a fire door on the way out and tore about four inches of the lining at the back, and he did not notice it until about nine at night, and he has not told anybody, and he is going to wear that coat through the winter, and every time he puts it on he will feel it, and that is the whole of the price of that evening.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
