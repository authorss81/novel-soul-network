# Chapter 584 — A First Floor Above Four Shop Units On A Friday Afternoon, Four Things Taken Out From Under A Box Of Washers And Put On A Table In A Row And Counted Twice By A Woman Of About Forty-Four, And A Man Of About Twenty-Two Writing Four Names On The Back Of A Docket A Second Time And Larger, And Two Figures That Are Each Other

**The Friday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was four things taken out from under a box of washers and put on a table and counted by a woman who was not in a hurry about it.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday, all eleven carrying a date, eleven people through that counter, the last of them at about five. The bench until two, the counter from two until six, that first floor above four shop units from about half past two until about a quarter past four, the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that one lease are carrying the difference, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and thirty-three days, one hundred and forty-seven weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and thirty-seven days, one hundred and forty-eight weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The nineteenth line on two nails above the bench in Lattice Ward is seven hundred and twenty-nine days old, one hundred and four weeks and one day, and seven hundred and twenty-nine is also the day the hold of the man of about thirty-three in that other city began, and that hold is six hundred and sixty-six days, ninety-five weeks and one day, and six hundred and sixty-six is also the day the nineteenth line was written on that board. One of those two figures is an age and one of the two is a day something happened, they are standing on this same Friday afternoon eleven feet apart, and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, which is a closed thing here with no days-off figure carried for it anywhere in this entry.**

The man of about fifty-one at that north wall is six hundred and thirty-nine days, ninety-one weeks and two days, and the ask is seven hundred and twenty-three days, one hundred and three weeks and two days, and neither of the two is a day of that heating either.

---

**They came out from under the box of washers at about half past two and went on a table in a row, and the woman of about forty-four put them in that order herself and she chose the order.**

He was there because she had asked him to come and read back the four names he had written on the back of a docket on the Wednesday, and he had said he would, and he came.

The man of about thirty-six was there with a sheet in front of him and a pen that was not on a string, and about four other people were there, and nobody sat down.

**"Cold key,"** she said, and put her hand flat on it without picking it up. **"Nine inches, flat, a bit of paper round the bow."**

**"Brass plate. Four by two, a number stamped into it, and the number is the one that is still on the board at the end of the row and has not been taken off."**

**"Washing line, a coil, about nine pegs."**

**"Nine feet of chain, a hook at each end, and a spare hook that is on a nail in a kitchen in that town and has never been asked for."**

That is four things in about nine seconds.

**Then she counted them.** She put her finger on each one and said a number, and the numbers were one, two, three, four, and nobody in that room said anything while she did it, and she said the four numbers at the same speed she had said the four names, and then she went back along the row and counted them a second time and got four again.

**"Why twice,"** the man of about thirty-six said.

**"Because there are about nine people in this room and six of you have never seen these four things at the same time as each other, and I am the only one who has seen them twice, and if I put them back under that box now then on Monday there will be a sheet on that table with four lines on it and not one person in this city will be able to say what was written on the four lines."**

**"Then write what was written."**

**"Write it where."**

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

**"On what,"** she said. **"I have a sheet for a count. I have a sheet for a run. I have a sheet for a household that has not been reached and a sheet for a household that has asked to stop and a sheet for a clip that has not come back. There is no sheet in this building for a cold key and there is no sheet in it for a coil of washing line and if there were one I would have written it myself in the spring and I did not, and the reason I did not is that I did not think anybody would come off it."**

**"Are you writing that down."**

**"I am writing it on the back of a docket because that is what the four names are on, and because a man of twenty-two offered to read them back and is standing in the room."**

**"Then read them back."**

He read them back. He read them back twice, the second time slower, and the second time he wrote them out again on the same docket in bigger letters, because she said the first ones were not big enough for a man of thirty-six to read at a table with four people sitting at it.

**The docket with the four names on it is now the only piece of paper in four towns and in this city on which those four things are named. It is a docket. A docket goes into a bin in about four years in a shop in a district that is not this one, and there is no second copy, and the man who wrote the first four names on it is the same man who wrote the second four, and he has not told either of the two people behind that counter in a second district that he wrote them twice, and he has not been asked, and he put the docket in the inside pocket of a coat at about a quarter past four and it is not on anything.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about thirty-six signed nothing that afternoon and said that he was not going to sign a thing about four objects coming back from one household out of about four hundred, and nobody argued with him about it, and that was correct.

---

**What he noticed on that Friday, and did not say, and has not said to the four people who came in for the heat, is that a sheet for every one of the things that can happen to a household and no sheet at all for the thing that happened.**

He has spent four years believing that a person who records things carefully will not lose them. She has just shown him, in about nine seconds, standing up, that the care is in the wrong place: there is a form for a household that has not been reached and a form for a household that has asked to stop and there is no form for a household that has stopped, and the difference between the second and the third is a sheet of paper and about four hours of somebody's Thursday, and nobody in that building had the four hours.

**What he wanted, in four rooms off a service road on the Friday night with the heat on, was for the man of about thirty-six to have asked her for a form and been told there was not one, at which point a man of twenty-two with a docket in his coat would have had a reason to make one, and he did not make one, and he is not going to, because the man of about thirty-six did not ask and a form nobody has asked for is a form a person gets handed, and he has been on the receiving end of about four of those this year and every one of them was reasonable.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Friday, which is on no form at all: he wrote the four names a second time, in bigger letters, at a table, for about four minutes, and in those four minutes the bench in Lattice Ward was standing empty with a cage in it and a man facing away, and about four people came and went through that shop in those four minutes, and one of the four was told by the man in the cage that the man was at a first floor in a second district, and that is where a person in this city learns that a man of twenty-two leaves his own floor to read four words back, and it is not a thing anybody will put on a sheet.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
