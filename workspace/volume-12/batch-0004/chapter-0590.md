# Chapter 590 — A Thursday With A Drawer Stayed Shut And Nine Lines A Sixth Time Not Written, A Person Owed Nothing Saying A True Thing In A Face That Leaves Everybody Worse Including The Speaker, And A Shutter Down At About Ten

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was a true thing said by a person owed nothing that left everybody worse including the speaker.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, a date against every one of them, eleven people through the counter, the last at about half past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and past what it cost through all of last winter, four names on that one tenancy are sharing the extra between them, and a fortnight a cook spent out of her own money is owed by nobody and will still be owed by nobody when this month ends.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and forty-six days, one hundred and forty-nine weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and fifty days, one hundred and fifty weeks to the day, four days beyond the rooms, and the rail takes two.

**The nineteenth line on two nails is seven hundred and forty-two days, one hundred and six weeks to the day. The ask is seven hundred and thirty-six days, one hundred and five weeks and one day, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and seventy-nine days, ninety-seven weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one at that north wall is six hundred and fifty-two days, ninety-three weeks and one day. Not one of the four is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating here is a closed matter with no days-off figure attached to it in any line of this entry.**

---

**He came at about four on the Thursday and he had not been in since the Monday, and he stood at the counter with his hands in his pockets and did not take them out once.**

He is about fifty-eight. He drives a van out of a yard in a second district, he drove four days in the last movement to stand in a yard and repeat nine seconds said by a woman of about fifty-two at a gate, he drives every other Thursday to a town four days of bus travel away with stock and sheets, and nobody has thanked him for any of it, and he is owed nothing, and the owing-nothing is exact and not a figure of speech: no wage outstanding, no favour unreturned, no apology due to him or from him.

**"I have been asked to say whether the count will hold,"** he said. **"So I will say it. The count will hold. About four hundred plates on a board at the end of a row, one number still on it in its place in the line with nobody behind it, and the walking party counting it every other week and bringing it back on a sheet, and a firm in a second district reading the sheet and sending stock in the spring on the strength of it. It holds. That is the true thing and it is worse for everybody including me."**

**"Say why."**

**"Because the household that left is still on the board and the town still gets its stock, and the household is at that standpipe in the dark every morning with everybody else and has to be knocked on every other week at six by one of nine, and the nine have to knock, and the woman at the station has to watch eight households know and say nothing, and the firm has to read a sheet it knows has a gap in it with nothing saying which, and I have to drive it, and every one of those is worse than a stoppage, and a stoppage ended."**

Nobody in that shop said a word for about four seconds. The woman of about thirty-four who answers that counter put her pen down. A man of about thirty-one with a meter under his arm stopped halfway to the door.

**"Then we take the number off,"** the man of about thirty-one said.

**"You cannot take it off,"** the man of about fifty-eight said. **"The number is the only proof the row is alive. Take it off and the count comes back one short and the firm reads one short and the town loses a household of stock in the spring and about four hundred pay for one leaving. Leave it on and one household pays every morning in the dark and nine pay every other week at six. Those are the two, and both are worse, and I am the man who drives the sheet between them, and I am owed nothing for the driving, and I wanted that on the record in a shop from me on a Thursday."**

**"It is on the record,"** Marek said. **"It is not on a sheet. It is in this shop and about four people heard it."**

**"That is the same thing,"** the man said. **"That is what a shop is."**

He took his hands out of his pockets to push the door and put them back in again outside. He did not wait to be answered because there was nothing to answer, and nobody thanked him on his way out, and Marek did not thank him, and the woman of about thirty-four did not thank him, and the man of about thirty-one did not thank him, and the not-thanking was not unkindness and was not noticed by anybody except afterwards.

---

**What he noticed on that Thursday evening, and told nobody, is that a true thing said by a person owed nothing costs more than the same thing said by a person owed something, because there is nowhere to put it.**

A man owed a wage can be paid. A man owed an apology can be answered. A man owed nothing who says a thing that leaves everybody worse has handed the room a debt with no name on it, and about four people in that shop will carry it home without knowing they are carrying it, and none of the four can hand it back, because the man is already out of the door with his hands in his pockets, and he was owed nothing before he came in and is owed nothing now, and the saying has not changed that by a line.

**What he wanted, on the Thursday night, in four rooms with the heat on and about four people in them who were not asked anything, was to have said the other half out loud before the man of about fifty-eight did, which is that the row should keep the number and the household should keep the step and the nine should keep knocking and the firm should keep reading, and to have been wrong out loud in front of about four people who could have told him so. He wanted to be wrong in a way that could be corrected. He did not say it. He has spent four years being right in ways that cannot be corrected and he is tired of it in a way he has never been tired of anything.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Thursday, which is on no form and which cost him his evening: the back room off the back of the shop had stood unemptied for about three weeks with a bucket of ash and swarf under the bench, and he had meant to carry it out on the Friday, and instead he carried it out on the Thursday night after the rooms went quiet, in the cold, in his good coat, and washed his hands twice at the yard tap and still had the smell of it on them when he sat down, and nobody asked him to do it on the Thursday and nobody knows he did it, and the Friday will now be clear for eleven jobs he has not yet dated.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten because there is no Sunday on this day and there is no such thing as a shop on a Sunday.

*A true thing worse for everybody, as a record of four seconds and two answers.* **Where: a counter in Lattice Ward at about four on a Thursday, about four people in the shop, a man with his hands in his pockets. Who spoke: a man of about fifty-eight who drives a van out of a yard in a second district and who drove four days in the last movement to repeat nine seconds from a gate and who drives every other Thursday to a town four days of bus travel away, owed nothing, due nothing, apologised to by nobody.
