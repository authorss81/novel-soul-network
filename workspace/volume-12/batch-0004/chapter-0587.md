# Chapter 587 — A Counter At About Half Past Three On A Thursday, A Man Of About Thirty-Eight Who Keeps A Rota For A Building Of About Nine Flats, One Question In Ten Words That Was Put To Somebody In The Spring And Has Not Been Put To Anybody Since, An Answer He Gave Himself In A Yard Nine Seconds After A Man Of Twenty-Two Did Not Give One, And Two Figures That Are Each Other

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was a man of about thirty-eight with a rota for about nine flats asking one question that had been asked once before and never since.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, a date written against every one of the eleven, eleven people through that counter, the last of them at about half past five. A bench in Lattice Ward until two, that counter from two until six, and the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that one tenancy are carrying the difference between them, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and thirty-nine days, one hundred and forty-eight weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and forty-three days, one hundred and forty-nine weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The hold of the man of about thirty-three in that other city is six hundred and seventy-two days, ninety-six weeks to the day, and six hundred and seventy-two is also the day the ask was put to a person and not to an institution, and the ask is seven hundred and twenty-nine days, one hundred and four weeks and one day, and seven hundred and twenty-nine is also the day that hold began. One of those two figures is an age in days and one of the two is a day something happened, they stand together on this Thursday afternoon in one room in this city, and neither of them is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, which is a closed thing in this entry with no figure of days off for it carried anywhere in it.**

The nineteenth line on two nails is seven hundred and thirty-five days, one hundred and five weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one at that north wall is six hundred and forty-five days, ninety-two weeks and one day, and neither of the two is a day of that heating either.

---

**He came at about half past three on the Thursday and he had come on the Friday before last as well and had not come in between, and he did not sit down, and he had about nine minutes before the man of about thirty-four closed the front of that shop for the four o'clock.**

He is about thirty-eight and he keeps a rota for a building of about nine flats in this city and has kept it for about four years, and the rota is on a piece of card on the back of a door in a lobby, and it is the only card of its kind in this city and he has never been asked to be on anything.

**"You told me this in the spring."**

**"I did."**

**"I did not use it for about four weeks. A woman of about thirty-four who types told me in a second district what you had been asked and what you said, and she said it out loud on a pavement, and I have been carrying it for a month."**

**"Who did you ask."**

**"Four people this week. A man of about thirty-four who has nine crates. A woman of about thirty-one who runs a room. A man of about fifty-eight who cuts things. And a woman of about fifty-two who keeps a clipboard on a nail."**

**"What did they say."**

**"One of them said it was the wrong question. He was not wrong. The other three said they did not know who to ask."**

**"Then they did not ask me."**

**"No,"** the man of about thirty-eight said. **"They did not ask you, and it has not been put to you once since the spring, and I am the only one who has come, and that is because I have got nine flats and a card on the back of a door and no one in this city can tell me who goes first in my own building except you."**

**"Ask it."**

**"Who does the going first when everybody else is waiting."**

Ten words, said in a face, at about twenty to four, to about four people in a shop, and the woman of about thirty-four heard all of it and has written none of it down and was not asked to write any of it down.

**Marek did not answer.**

He stood about nine feet away with a bag of tools and he had the whole of the answer built in about four seconds, because the answer is the answer, and because he had given it away to a man on a Friday evening in the last movement in a different order and with a cold key in it, and because a man of twenty-two who answers a question like that in a shop at about twenty to four becomes the man that question is put to, and there is a card on the back of a door in a lobby in this city that already has his name nowhere on it and he intends to keep it that way.

**The man of about thirty-eight waited about nine seconds and then gave the answer to Marek and to the four other people and to nobody in particular, and the whole of the waiting was the whole of the answer being available from him.**

**"The one who is worst off. That's it. That's the answer and it has been the answer the whole time and nobody has ever written it down."**

**"Say a bit more of it."**

**"I've got a woman on the fourth floor of a building in this city whose hot water is off between six and eight every evening, and a man on the second floor whose is off every night of the week, and every week I put the fourth floor first, and every week two or three people in that building tell me it is not fair."**

**"Is it not fair."**

**"It is exactly fair,"** the man said. **"That is the part I have never been able to say out loud in that lobby. It's exactly fair, and I go home, and I am the one who says the word unfair, and I have been saying the word unfair to four people a week for about four years in a building where I am the only person who is allowed to say it and I am not even a resident."**

**"You could stop."**

**"I could stop and then about nine people in that building have no hot water at all, and about four of them are on the fourth floor and about four of them are not."**

**"That's the answer to a different question."**

**"It is the same question,"** the man of about thirty-eight said. **"That is the whole of it and you know it is, and I have come four miles on a bus to say it into a face and you have not said it, and I am not going to say it a second time, and you are the only person in this city who could have and there is exactly one of you and he is standing about nine feet away with a bag."**

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about thirty-eight said thank you to nobody on his way out, and Marek did not say it to him, and the woman of about thirty-four said nothing to either of them and shut the front of that shop at four.

---

**What he noticed on that Thursday evening, and told nobody, and has not written in the notebook, is that a card on the back of a door in a lobby in this city and a card with two boxes and a metal clip in a town four days of bus travel away are doing the same job, and that about nine flats and about four hundred households are the same size.**

Both of them are a list on a wall with a number against a name. Both of them are worked by a person who is not on the list and who is paid nothing for it or paid a very little. In both of them the person with the worst week goes first, every week, and nobody ever says that out loud, and the reason nobody ever says it out loud is that a sentence said out loud is a rule, and a rule can be complained of, and a card on the back of a door cannot be complained of because there is nobody to complain to and about four of the nine will not know there is a card.

**He has been the person a room goes to for about four years. On that Thursday he worked out, in about four seconds, standing nine feet from a man of about thirty-eight with a bag of tools in his hand, that the whole of the reason he does not answer questions like that is not modesty and is not a boundary he helped write. It is that a man who answers says the rule out loud, and a rule said out loud in a lobby is a rule four people can stand in front of, and he has spent four years building a thing in which nobody has to stand in front of anything, and a question of ten words undoes all of it in about four seconds, and he let it.**

**What he wanted, on the Thursday night, in four rooms with the heat on and about four people in them who were not asked anything, was to say one sentence. Not the answer. The other one: that the man of about thirty-eight had it right, and that the going first was correct, and that the whole of what was wrong with it was that the rule had never once been said out loud, and that saying it out loud would cost the man of about thirty-eight everything he has got in that building inside a fortnight. He wanted to say that and he did not say it, and not saying it is the sixth time in about four years he has had a true sentence ready and has not said it, and the not-saying this time was not the same as the other five, and he has not worked out yet what the difference is.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Thursday, which is on no form and which he will not tell anybody about: he wrote the ten words on the back of his own hand in pencil, a second time, under a line of pencil that has been on that hand since a Friday in the last movement and that he has not been able to make out in about three weeks. There are now two things on the back of his left hand and he cannot read either of them, and he washed that hand on the Wednesday night and both of them are still on it, and neither of them is any use to him and neither of them is for anybody else.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
