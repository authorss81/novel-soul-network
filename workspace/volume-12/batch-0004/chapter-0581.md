# Chapter 581 — Eleven Dated Jobs And A Woman Of About Thirty-Eight Who Came Up On The Sunday Night Bus For A Foot Valve, And A Back Room About Nine Feet By Eleven In Which Nine Seconds Established That Nobody At The End Of About Four Hundred Cards Can Be Found By Anybody

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was eleven dated jobs, a woman who had come up on the Sunday night bus, and a card on a bench in a back room.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, a date written against every one of the eleven, eleven people through that counter door, the last of them at about half past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, a back room about nine feet by eleven off the back of that shop between about half past two and about a quarter past three, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is burning and dearer than the winter's, four names on that one tenancy are carrying the increase between them, and a fortnight a cook took out of her own pay is owed by nobody and is not going to be owed by anybody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and twenty-nine days old, one hundred and forty-seven weeks to the day, four of them and a back one, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and thirty-three days, one hundred and forty-seven weeks and four days, four days past the rooms, and the rail takes two.

**The nineteenth line on two nails above that bench is seven hundred and twenty-five days old, one hundred and three weeks and four days. The ask is seven hundred and nineteen days, one hundred and two weeks and five days, and it has not been put to any other living person since the day it was put. The hold of the man of about thirty-three, in another city, is six hundred and sixty-two days, ninety-four weeks and four days, and the man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those rooms is six hundred and thirty-five days, ninety weeks and five days. Not one of those three is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating in that case is a closed thing with no days-off figure carried for it anywhere in this entry.**

---

**She had come up on the Sunday night on about eleven hours of bus with a bag, and she had slept on a floor in a second district, and she had a foot valve in the bag that she had paid for with her own money and that was the only thing she had come for.**

She was about thirty-eight. She keeps a pumping station at the end of a row of buildings in the first of the four towns and she reads its dials and she is the only person in that town who is paid to know what came through that main that week. The station is at the end of the row and not in the middle of it, and about four hundred people draw from it, and there is one standpipe in the wall of the last building and about four hundred people take turns at it.

She asked for the foot valve at about ten past two and he cut a washer for it and put it in a bag and wrote the date on the ticket, and at about half past two she said:

**"Is there anybody in this city who knows anything about a card."**

**"Depends what the card's for."**

**"It's for getting found."**

He took her out the back. The back room is about nine feet by eleven and it has a bench down one side with a vice on the end of it, a shelf of returns in cardboard boxes with the lids taped, a window that looks at a wall, and one light on a flex he has taped at the join about four times.

She put it on the bench.

**A card with two boxes and a metal clip. Her own name. A house at the end of that row. Both boxes empty, because the card had not been out, and the clip had come off a spring on the bench beside it and she had it in her hand and did not know what to do with it.**

**"That's it,"** she said. **"That's the winter arrangement. Name, house, and the two dates. That's what's on about four hundred of them in that town and I have watched about four hundred people carry one of these up to the standpipe and back again and I have never once seen a person at the house on one."**

**"Whose name's on it."**

**"Mine."**

**"Is anybody at that house."**

**"No,"** she said. **"There's a family in it since the spring that has never heard of any of it. I told a man of about fifty-eight in the spring and he wrote on a pad that the house was let, and I thought that meant he was going to do something about it, and nothing has happened since and it is the winter now."**

The nine seconds were the nine seconds it took him to see the shape of it. Two boxes. Both of them are dates, one out and one back, and both of them belong to the clip and not to the person. A name, which is right, and a house, which is a house and is there and has four walls and a door and somebody's washing on a line behind it, and a house is not a person, and there is no box on that card for a person who has moved, and there is no line under the name for a second address, and the whole of the arrangement is complete and correct and there is nobody in four hundred households at the end of it.

**He worked out in about four seconds that he could not do anything about it, and he worked out in the same four seconds that doing nothing was the correct thing to do, and the two of those arrived together and neither of them was a comfort.**

He is a man who mends what is broken. Nothing in front of him was broken. The card is a correct card and the arrangement is a correct arrangement and the clip comes back on a spring in about nine days whatever is written on it and whether anybody is at the house or not. Nobody in this city can change a card. The two people who could change it are four days of bus travel away, and a man who gets on a bus about a card is a man the four towns come back to, and he worked that out on a Tuesday in the last movement with a notebook shut and it has not changed.

What he said instead was this:

**"You can fill this one in again. That's allowed. The date out, the date back, the clip on the spring, about nine days and it comes back. But filling it in again is a second card and it is not a change to the first one, and the first one is still on that board at the end of your row with your name and that house on it."**

**"So the man comes to the wrong house."**

**"Yes."**

**"And the man's been sent."**

**"Yes."**

**"Right,"** she said, and put the card in her inside pocket, and put the clip in the other one, and neither of those was a decision she looked like she had made.

He said it four times that afternoon. She was one of the four. The other three were the woman of about thirty-four who answers that counter and had asked him once in the spring why he wrote a date on tickets nobody collected, a man of about thirty-eight who came for a belt and who wrote the address down on the back of his own hand in biro, and a man of about fifty-eight who came for four feet of pipe and who said *is that right* and then said *never mind* before Marek had got the second word out.

**Nobody thanked him. Not one of the four said the word, and the woman of about thirty-eight put her hand flat on the card in her pocket twice on the way to the door, which is not the same as thanking a man for telling her something she had known since the spring.**

She left at about a quarter past three. The card did not go on the shelf of returns, because it was not one of those, and there is a shelf of returns in that back room and every one of them is a card about a thing that was wrong with a machine.

---

**What he noticed on that Monday, and has not put in the notebook and is not going to, is that a house is not a person.**

He has spent four years writing things down about people, and about nine of the things he has written down in that time are about a house and are about a person because a house is where a person was on a day. The card at the end of that row has a name and a house on it and the name is right and the house is there and the washing is on the line, and the arrangement has been asking about four four hundred people to go and stand in a street and knock on a door that somebody answers, and a door that somebody answers is not a person being found. **There is no box on that card for the fact that a house is not a person, and he has looked, and he has had about four seconds to look, and there is not one.**

**What he wanted on that Monday evening, in four rooms with the heat on and about four people in them who were not asked anything, was to be the man that four hundred people walk down a street to. He wanted it the way you want a thing you have no business wanting, which is that somebody in about four hundred households would come to a door and find him standing at it, and would not be annoyed, and would hand him the card, and would ask him what to do with it. He did not say it. He has not got a vehicle and he is on no list and he has a bench at about eight o'clock.**

**And the thing he gave up on the Monday, which cost him about twenty minutes of a clear bench and which is not on any form anywhere, is that a man of about thirty-one came in at about four with a meter that he wanted read and a time by which he had to be somewhere, and Marek read it and let the time go past about eleven minutes without saying that the time had gone, because the man had sat down and had stopped looking at the door, and then he said nothing about those eleven minutes to anybody, and there is no line on any sheet in that shop for eleven minutes that a man was not told he had run out of.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
