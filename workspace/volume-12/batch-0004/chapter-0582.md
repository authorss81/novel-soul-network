# Chapter 582 — A Trade Counter At The Foot Of A First Floor In A Second District On A Wednesday Afternoon, A Woman Of About Forty-One With A Carrier Bag And Nine Feet Of Chain Wrapped Round It, Four Things Put Down One At A Time, And Nine Seconds In Which She Says Out Loud What Putting Them Down Costs

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was nine feet of chain wrapped round a carrier bag and a woman of about forty-one putting four things on a counter one at a time.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, all eleven of them carrying a date, eleven people through that counter, the last of them at about half past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, a street in a second district from about two until about three, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heating over that service road is on and dearer than it was through the winter, four names on that one lease are carrying what it costs, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own money is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and thirty-one days, one hundred and forty-seven weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and thirty-five days, one hundred and forty-seven weeks and six days, four days beyond the rooms, and the rail holds two.

**The eighteenth line on two nails is seven hundred and forty-nine days, one hundred and seven weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those four rooms is six hundred and thirty-seven days, ninety-one weeks to the day. The ask is seven hundred and twenty-one days, one hundred and three weeks to the day, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and sixty-four days, ninety-four weeks and six days. None of those three is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and that heating carries no days-off figure on any page of this entry.**

---

**She hired the van on the Sunday night from a firm on a wide road, and it cost her more than the bus and less than the store, and it was standing at the kerb at about a quarter to two with the side door open because she could not get nine feet of chain through the door of a bus and had tried.**

She was about forty-one. She has four of her own, a boy of about nine, and a mother of about seventy-one. She had come up on the same bus as the woman who keeps a pumping station at the end of a row in the same town, and neither of them knew the other, and neither of them has said since that they were on it.

He had told her on the Monday where the counter was. That was the whole of his Monday afternoon. He said it and she asked him whether he would come and stand there and he said he would come and show her which door, and he came and showed her which door.

The counter is at the foot of that first floor, at street level, and it has a yellow strip along the edge of it, and a man of about twenty-six and a woman of about twenty-four work it, and neither of them had ever seen anything come back.

She put the four things down one at a time and named each of them as it went down, and he wrote the four names on the back of a docket because she asked him to, and she said she could not remember all four and he said he would remember them.

**"That's a cold key,"** she said. **"That's for the store on the farm above our row. It's flat and it's about nine inches and there's a bit of paper round the bow that went soft about two winters ago. There are two keys to that store on the whole of that hill and the other one is in a man's pocket in the second of our towns and he has never once used it."**

**"That's a plate,"** she said. **"Brass. About four inches by two, with a number stamped into it, and there are about four hundred of them on a board at the end of our row and you take yours off and you carry it and you put it back when you've had your turn, and if you don't put it back you can't have a turn, and I've never known anybody who couldn't find their own."**

**"That's washing line,"** she said. **"A coil of it with about nine pegs on it. That was strung from our back door to the standpipe across the end of the row and it was the only reason we were ever first, and it came down in the spring and it has not gone up since."**

**"And that's the chain,"** she said. **"Nine feet of it with a hook at each end. It goes round the standpipe and one household has it at a time and the woman with the board writes the number on the plate and moves it along, and it's got a spare hook on it for when a hook goes, and that spare hook is on the wall in her kitchen on a nail and nobody has ever asked her for it."**

Nobody thanked anybody in that shop.

The man of about twenty-six asked her one question, which was whether she wanted the four things put in a bin or put in a drawer, and she said a drawer, and he said there was not one, and she said a shelf then, and he said there was a shelf, and he wrote the four names on a sheet and put the sheet on the shelf face down under a box of washers, and neither of them said why, and neither of them has told anybody.

Then she said what it cost, and it took about nine seconds, and she said it to Marek and not to either of the two behind the counter, and both of the two heard all of it.

**"The key, my mother's hip, the Friday milk, and from the morning four hundred and one of us in the dark at that standpipe."**

Then she said the rest of it, and the rest of it was not nine seconds and nobody has asked her to say it again.

**"The key is the only one we have and the milk goes in on the Friday and we have been sitting this out since the spring with a key in our hand and a plate with our number on it, and the sitting out ends tonight whichever way we go. From the morning I am one of about four hundred at that standpipe in the dark and so is my mother, and she is seventy-one, and I have not told her that yet, and I am telling you before I tell her because you are a stranger, and that is the wrong order, and I am doing it that way on purpose."**

**"You are giving up a plate with a number on it."**

**"I am giving up a number,"** she said. **"You keep the number. The number stays on the board and it stays in the right place in the line and nobody knows it is empty, and that is the part I want written down somewhere. I am not taking a number off a board. There is going to be a gap in the middle of about four hundred and nobody will be able to say which one, and my mother is going to be the one who finds out, at six in the morning, at that standpipe, in the dark."**

Marek carried the chain out to the van himself. Nine feet of it does not weigh anything and the hook at one end is a hook that has been out in weather and it opened the heel of his hand about halfway along the inside, and there is no first-aid box out the back of that shop and there is not a tap in the yard, and he did not say anything about it to the woman of about forty-one or to the man of about twenty-six or to anybody, and he went back to the bench at about ten past three and finished the Wednesday with the heel of his hand closing up under a plaster he put on himself with his teeth.

**The arrangement did not lose a house. That is the whole of what happened to it and it happened in a drawer under a box of washers, and nothing about it was written on the sheet except four names written by a stranger, and the four names are in a hand that belongs to a man of twenty-two and to nobody else in that building, and no person in that building has said that they are glad to have them.**

---

**What he noticed on that Wednesday, and has told nobody and is not going to, is that she said *I am telling you before I tell her because you are a stranger* and that she was right to do it that way, and that being right to do it that way is the whole of what she got.**

She is owed nothing by him. She has a key she cannot use, a plate with a number on it that is still on a board in a town she cannot get to quickly, a line that has not gone up since the spring, and a mother of about seventy-one who does not know yet. What he wants is not any part of that. **What he wanted, in a room off a service road on the Wednesday night, with the heat on and about four people in it who were not asked anything, was to be the person she told before she told her mother, and to be somebody other than a stranger, and to be about a day's ride from the row so that being told would not be the worst ordering of it. He is not. He is a man in a second district with a plaster on his hand that a woman of about forty-one has not asked about.**

**And the thing he gave up, which is not on a form and is not worth the price he paid for it, is that he let the two people behind that counter keep it. He had four things in a carrier bag for about nine seconds and he had a name in his own mouth and one of the two is twenty-six and one of the two is twenty-four and a man of twenty-two with a docket and four names on the back of it could have said the four names out loud and made it a transaction with a person in it. He did not say them, because a man who says a name at a counter in a second district on behalf of a woman from four days away is a man that counter asks next time, and the next time will be a card, and after that it will be the winter.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
