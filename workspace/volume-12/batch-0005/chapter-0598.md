# Chapter 598 — A Friday On Which Four Places Found Out What The Refusal Of Thursday Morning Cost Them Without Asking The Man Who Refused, A Woman Of About Forty-Four In The First Of The Four Towns Who Is Now The Person Four Towns Ask About And Did Not Agree To It, And Four Rooms Off A Service Road Where Four People Answered The Same Question Four Ways Without Asking Anybody

**The Friday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was four costs landing in four towns that week and none of the four of them having been asked.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday, all eleven dated, eleven people through that counter, the last of them at about five past five. The bench until two, the counter from two until six, and the four rooms off that service road from half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and above what it has cost all winter, four names on that one tenancy are carrying the difference, and a fortnight a cook put in out of her own money is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** Those four rooms are one thousand and sixty-one days, one hundred and fifty-one weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and sixty-five days, one hundred and fifty-two weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The nineteenth line on the two nails is seven hundred and fifty-seven days, one hundred and eight weeks and one day. The hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and ninety-four days, ninety-nine weeks and one day, and the ask is seven hundred and fifty-one days, one hundred and seven weeks and two days, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is six hundred and sixty-seven days, ninety-five weeks and two days. None of those four is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and that heating is finished with and this entry holds no figure of days off for it anywhere.**

---

**Four towns, four costs, and the man who caused them was in Lattice Ward on the Friday with eleven jobs dated against them and did not know about any of the four.**

**In the second of the four towns, a woman of about forty-six who keeps a bus depot's daily sheet spent an hour and forty minutes on the Friday writing to a firm in a second district to say that the number she sent is the number she sent last year, and why, and she had to sign it, and she is not a signatory on anything else in her life and had to go and find a pen.**

**In the fourth of the four towns, a woman of about thirty-six who opens a school was asked in writing for the name and the address of the household she took off her list, and she wrote back four lines saying that she is not going to give them, and that the household does not know it is off, and that the firm may put that in its file in her town and that she does not care and that she is sorry for the tone of the first line.**

**In the third of the four towns, a woman of about forty-four who signs for a firm at a gate got on a bus on the Friday morning and went and stood in the first of the four towns for about four hours, because nobody there knows she exists, and she had to explain to about nine people that a number on their own board has been theirs and something else's since the spring.**

**And in the first of the four towns, a woman of about forty-four who keeps that town's winter list found out on the Friday that a man in a second district of a city four days of bus travel away is now sending her the questions about the other three.**

The firm had four envelopes instead of one, and the four had arrived on four days, and a man of about thirty-four who reads sheets for that firm has to work out which town each one is about before he can do anything with it, and he wrote to her, because she is the only name on any of the four that the firm had ever had.

**"He wants to know about the school,"** she said, to about nine people, in the same first floor, **"and about the depot, and about the fields. He does not have anybody else. I have been in this town four years and I have never once been asked about another town and I am not going to be the person who answers for four of them."**

**"Then say no,"** the man of about thirty-three said.

**"Then the four hundred and sixteen get read on the strength of a sheet with a gap in it and nobody in this town is the person who said anything about it, and I do not think that is better."**

She did not say no. She wrote back four lines and one of them said: **that the town answers for itself, that the other three can be asked at their own doors, and that if a person wants to know about the fourth of the four towns they should put a sheet in an envelope and send it down that road.**

**Nobody in that first floor thanked her. She has become, without asking and without noticing, the place four towns' questions stop at, and she is about forty-four and she is owed nothing and she will now be written to about twice a fortnight until the spring.**

**None of the four costs was put to Marek. None of the four towns has heard his name. The refusal was said in a shop in a city four days of bus travel away in about nine seconds on a Thursday morning, and a woman of about thirty-four in that shop said that in about four years being the man who says no to things that work would be the only thing anybody knew about him, and she was right about the four years and neither of them knows how to check the four.**

---

**In four rooms off a service road that evening, four people answered the same question four ways, and none of them asked anybody else, and the question was whether the heat goes off at ten.**

A woman of about thirty-one said ten, because the meter is on a time clock and the meter does not care what anybody decides.

A man of about thirty-three said half past nine, and got up at half past nine and put it out, and nobody complained and nobody said thank you, and the room was cold for about forty minutes afterwards.

A woman of about twenty-seven said nothing at all, and went to bed, and let it be the last one's decision.

A woman of about thirty was in the room from about seven and did not decide it, and was not asked, and was not thanked, and did not say anything about it then or afterwards.

**Four people, one question, four answers, no meeting, no form, no minutes, and the woman of about thirty was the only one of the four whose answer was that there was no answer, and there is no column on any sheet anywhere for that.**

---

**What he noticed on the Friday evening, and told nobody, is that the man of about thirty-eight in the first of the four towns has a rota for about nine flats and the woman of about forty-four has about nine evenings a week, and that the two nines are not the same nine, and neither of those two people has ever heard of the other one, and both of them are keeping a list of about nine people in a town of about four hundred.**

About four hundred people in that town. Nine on a rota. Nine doing evenings. And a school holding nine households' cards, and a depot counting four hundred and sixteen doors, and a gate being signed at two days a week, and a board on a wall with about four hundred numbers on it and one of them written over.

**That is six lists of about nine people in a town of about four hundred, and about four hundred and sixteen doors and one road, and there is no page anywhere in this city that puts any two of them on the same sheet, and if a person wanted to know, in about nine seconds, whether one of the four hundred and sixteen was on the rota, there is no way to find out that does not involve a bus for four days.**

**What he wanted, on the Friday night, with about four people in four rooms who had each answered a question without him, was to be one of the four who did not have to be asked. He has been the person who gets asked for about eleven years. In four rooms off a service road nobody has asked him anything all week and nobody will, and the reason the four answers came out four different ways is that the four of them had been asked by a meter and a clock and a wall, and he was the fifth person in the building and was not on any of it.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Friday, which is on no form and which nobody asked for: there is a bus timetable on the wall of a yard in a second district, behind glass, in the dark, where a man stands to read it, and it was gone dark on the Friday before he went, and the yard gate was locked, and he stood at the gate about four minutes with his face about nine inches from the glass reading a timetable he already knew by heart, and then went home. The man who runs that yard was standing in an upstairs window and saw him at the gate and has not said one word about it to anybody, and did not open the gate, and is not going to, and neither of them will ever mention it.**

Nobody thanked anybody, in four towns or in a shop or in four rooms, and the shutter came down at about ten, and there is no Sunday in this week at all and the hour does not vary from one day to the next.

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A refusal said in about nine seconds in a shop in this city cost four people in four towns about nine hours, a bus, a signed letter and an afternoon of writing instead of driving, and not one of them was asked, and not one of them has heard of the man who caused it.** Nobody thanked anybody on the Friday, in this city or in any of the four places, and the shutter came down at about ten and there is no Sunday in this week. He taught nobody, demonstrated nothing, assessed nobody and assisted nobody, and no request of any of the four was made. **The count of correct things that changed nothing, done by people owed nothing, is still two, being a clipboard on a nail inside a cold-store door in the first of the four towns and four torn pieces of the same list in a coat pocket at that same gate, both of one woman of about fifty-two; none of this Friday's four is a third, since each took minutes rather than nine seconds, each changed a household's spring, and each of the four women is owed nothing by what she paid.** The rail card is one thousand and sixty-five days, in the rail all day, in a rail that takes two, and a return card with two boxes and a metal clip is a different object from it and did not appear.
