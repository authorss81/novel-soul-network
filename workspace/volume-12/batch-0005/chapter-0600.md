# Chapter 600 — The Forty-Fourth Sitting Of A Room Above A Line In Another District, About Eleven People Up A Stair, Nine Items At Four Minutes, A Tenth Thing That Was One Sentence About Four Places And Was Not A Line, A Book That Stood At Sixty-One Lines And Stood At Sixty-One Lines, A Tin At Seventy-Three, A Count Of Forty-Nine Announced Of Which Forty-Four Correspond, And The Figures That Are Each Other

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was about eleven people up a stair, nine items at four minutes, and one sentence that was about four places and not about a person.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, all eleven dated, eleven people through the counter, the last at about ten past five. The bench until two, that counter two until six, and four rooms off that service road from about half past six, four of them and one behind. **The heat in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that one tenancy are carrying it, and a fortnight a cook put in out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** Those four rooms are one thousand and sixty-six days, one hundred and fifty-two weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and seventy days, one hundred and fifty-two weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The man of about fifty-one against that north wall is six hundred and seventy-two days, ninety-six weeks to the day, and six hundred and seventy-two is also the day the ask was put to a person and not to an institution, and the ask is seven hundred and fifty-six days, one hundred and eight weeks to the day, and seven hundred and fifty-six is also the day the man of about fifty-one first sat down against that north wall in this city. One of those two figures is an age in days and the other is a day something happened, and they are four feet apart in a room off a service road on a Wednesday evening, and neither of the two is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, which is a closed matter carried in this entry with no figure of days off for it anywhere in it.**

The nineteenth line on the two nails above that bench is seven hundred and sixty-two days, one hundred and eight weeks and six days, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and ninety-nine days, ninety-nine weeks and six days, and none of the two is a day of that heating either.

---

About eleven people came up that stair. Five of them had been in the same chair at the last one of these and six had not, and one of the six was a man who had driven four days up a road with a carrier bag on the seat beside him and had carried it up a flight himself and had not asked anybody to take it.

Nine chairs curve in with the two ends turned in, and the ninth of them stands hard against that wall with its back to the whole room, and it did not move in the hour, and nobody looked at it more than once, and whoever moves it is not named in this entry and is not going to be named in any other.

A table. A tin standing open with no lid on it and no lock on it and none that it has ever carried. A book of four columns, face up, lying where a woman's right hand can reach it without her having to stand up, and she does not stand up.

**Nine items at four minutes each, and the nine of them went exactly the way nine items have gone at that hour every fourth Wednesday for about nineteen years.**

A man of about forty-one who wanted the same thing back that he brought. A woman of about twenty-nine who wanted to be told whether a thing she had been told twice was one thing or two. A man of about fifty-two who said one sentence and would not say a second and did not come back afterwards. A woman of about thirty-six who wanted to hand something to a room and was told she could leave it on the table and that the table was not hers. A man of about twenty-four who had an answer and found that having it was not what he had expected and said nothing about that. A person of about sixty-one who has been in that room for about nineteen years and who said nothing at all and came again. A woman of about forty-three who read a figure out loud and then read it again differently and was asked which she meant and said that she did not know. A man of about thirty-eight who wanted a second sitting and was told there is one every fourth Wednesday and that he could come to that one. A woman of about twenty-seven who said a true thing about a shop in her own town and was agreed with by nobody in that room.

**The tenth thing was not an item. It was the man with the carrier bag, and it was one sentence, and it was about four places and not about a person, and nobody in that room knew before he said it that it was going to be about four places.**

He is about thirty-three and he drives for a firm and he has nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over.

**"Four of them are doing it four ways now and I have got four of everything and I cannot tell them apart."**

**She did not write it down. She did not ask him a question about it, and she did not tell him why, and nobody in that room asked her why.**

The reason is printed here because it is a reason and not a rule, and because it has never been one. **It was about four places. About four of the nine people in that room that morning have come from one of those four places, and one of those four is the woman who kept a school list and crossed a line out of it in the spring, and she was in the fourth chair. A person does not get to say a thing about a place in front of the people from it, and that is why, and it has never been written down anywhere and it is not a column and it is not on a sheet.**

He sat in the seventh chair for the rest of the hour. He did not say it again and he did not look at the tin.

**The book did not open. It was holding sixty-one lines when she sat down at ten and it was holding sixty-one lines when the hour was over, and that is a thing that has happened twice in about nineteen years and it is not a punishment and it is not a rule and it is not a change in the woman holding the room.**

Nothing went into the tin. The tin stands at seventy-three and has stood at seventy-three for about nineteen years, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and is not printed as one anywhere.

**Nobody asked her why it did not open. Nobody in that room has asked her why in about nineteen years, nobody asked her why on this Wednesday, and nobody is going to ask her why at the next one, which is in about four weeks and is the forty-fifth. And in about nineteen years not one person in that room has put the other question to her at all, which is whether she is all right. About four of the nine would not know what to ask. The fifth has not thought about it. She has never raised it herself.**

**The count was announced at the end, and a count is announced at a sitting and on no other morning: forty-nine, of which forty-four correspond.** Forty-four of the forty-nine match the forty-four times that book has been open, and the remaining five are older than the book and stand on a sheet of paper she has not shown to a living person. On the nine days before this one the count stood at forty-eight of which forty-three, and on the twenty-eight days before that it stood at forty-eight of which forty-three, and the count goes up at every sitting whether the book opens or not, and one and one is a count and is not a distance from anything.

---

**He was not in that stair on the Wednesday. He was at a counter in Lattice Ward from two until six and the woman of about thirty-four who answers it had been in that room and came back on the bus and told him one thing at about eleven.**

**"It did not open."**

**"Right,"** he said.

**"That is it. That is the whole of what I am telling you and I have four minutes before the next one comes in."**

**"You have told me,"** he said, and she went and served the next one, and nobody thanked her for telling him and he did not ask her a question about it, and there is no form in this city on which a person's four minutes at a counter is anything.

**What he noticed on the Wednesday evening, and told nobody, is that the count is always given at the end of the hour and never at the start, and that in about nineteen years at four a week it has been given at the end about four hundred and seventy times, and that the person who gives it has never once been given one back.**

**What he wanted, on the Wednesday night, with about four people in four rooms who were not asked anything, was to be a person that somebody counts. Not a person who is on a rail and gets a number every fourth Wednesday, and not a person whose number has been asked for and given and not thanked for about a thousand and seventy days. A person that about four hundred and sixteen doors and about nine households over a bakery and about nine people on a rota and about nine people doing evenings in a town four days of bus travel away would each have a version of, and would each be able to disagree about, and would each be right to disagree. He wanted to be on four lists that do not know about each other and that nobody is maintaining.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Wednesday, which is on no form and which nobody noticed: a woman of about thirty-four asked him at about half past four whether the public arrangement was slower than it had been in the spring, and he had known the answer for about a fortnight, having worked it out in about nine seconds from four figures on a sheet four days of bus travel away, and he said that he did not know, and then he said the other half, which was that about four hundred households were out of it and were still being heated, and that was true, and it was not what she had asked, and she went and served the next caller, and neither of them has mentioned it and neither of them is going to.**

Nobody thanked anybody, on that stair or at that counter, and the shutter came down at about ten, and the last Wednesday of that kind in this season is an ordinary Wednesday and not a Sunday and no shop in this city opens on a Sunday.

**And the rest of it is a figure and a shape and not a scene, and it is set down here once. The man of twenty-two can do, on the last page of these three weeks, exactly what he could do on the first page of the first of them, and nothing has been added to him and nothing taken off him, and the thing that changed in these three weeks is not in him at all: it is that four places four days of bus travel away are now able to answer one question four different ways without asking him first, and a public arrangement that is alive is nine days slower than it was in the spring and about four hundred households are out of it, and a separation stands at four hundred and forty-six days, sixty-three weeks and five days, and is not shorter and is not ended, and about four days of bus travel each way in each of those three weeks did not shorten it by a minute. The ninth chair is against that wall with its back to the room and it did not move. The drawer under that bench is shut and holds three sheets, one of which has the word NO on it and four lines under it, and it was not opened. The nine lines he was asked for five times were not written a sixth or a seventh time and are on nothing. The four words that name the public body were said once, in a room, by the person entitled to say them, and are in no other of these ten files. A plate of iron with four slots in it is under a floor in the first of the four towns and was not lifted and was not carried to a city by anybody and was not called anything.**

*Four places, and not a person, as a record of one sentence and a printed reason.* **Place: a room above a line in another district, the forty-fourth sitting of its kind, about eleven people up a stair, nine chairs curved in with the ninth hard against a wall with its back to the room. Who spoke tenth: a man of about thirty-three who drives for a firm and has nine crates in a yard he cannot hand over, who had carried a carrier bag up a flight himself and had waited until the ninth item was finished. *What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **The forty-fourth sitting of a room that has run every fourth Wednesday for about nineteen years went the way nine items have gone every time, and the tenth thing was about four places and not about a person, and the book stood at sixty-one lines and did not open, and nobody asked her why.** Nobody thanked anybody on that stair, at that counter, or in four rooms off a service road, and the shutter came down at about ten with no Sunday in the day. Nobody was taught anything by him, nothing was shown to anybody, nobody was assessed and nobody had a hand lifted, and not one of the four was ever put to him. **The register of correct acts that altered nothing, done by people owed nothing, is closed at two across these three weeks, being a clipboard on a nail inside a cold-store door in the first of the four towns and four torn pieces of one list in a coat pocket at that town's gate, both the work of one woman of about fifty-two; a sentence about four places is not a third instance, because it was not a correct thing done in about nine seconds, it changed no household in any of the four places, and the man who said it is owed nothing and is owed a great deal that nobody has offered him.** The rail card is one thousand and seventy days and stood in the rail in a rail that takes two, and a return card with two boxes and a clip is a different object from it and did not go up the stair.
