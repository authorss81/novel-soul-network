# Chapter 597 — A Thursday On Which A Man Of About Forty-Eight Came Down The Road At Nine In The Morning As He Had Been Asked To, Gave The Four Reasons Again In A Face, Was Refused In About Nine Seconds Without A Speech, And Spent The Only Hour Of That Week In Which The Work Would Have Been Easy Copying Four Answers Onto Four Sheets By Hand While About Four People Watched Him Do It And Said Nothing

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was nine seconds at a counter at nine in the morning and then an hour of handwriting with nobody helping.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, every one of them dated, eleven people through that door, the last at about twenty to six. The bench until two, the counter two until six, and then the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and past what the whole of last winter cost, four names on that one lease are sharing it, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own money is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** Those four rooms are one thousand and sixty days, one hundred and fifty-one weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and sixty-four days, one hundred and fifty-two weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The nineteenth line on the two nails above that bench is seven hundred and fifty-six days, one hundred and eight weeks to the day, and seven hundred and fifty-six is also the day the man of about fifty-one first sat down against that north wall in this city, and that man is six hundred and sixty-six days, ninety-five weeks and one day, and six hundred and sixty-six is also the day the nineteenth line was written on that board. One of the two figures is an age and the other is a day something happened, they are nine feet apart on a Thursday morning, and neither of the two is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, which is a closed matter in this entry and carries no figure of days off for it anywhere in it.**

The hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and ninety-three days, ninety-nine weeks to the day, and the ask is seven hundred and fifty days, one hundred and seven weeks and one day, and neither of the two is a day of that heating.

---

**He came at about nine, as he had been asked to, and he was not carrying the diagram and he had not come to argue with anybody.**

**"Four reasons,"** he said. **"Same four. Nine days becomes one. A driver gets paid for going to a place that will take the load. Four places that agree with each other are worth less to me than four places that do not. And your four answers this week were four correct answers and there is nobody in this city who can tell me which town a household in is unless all four of them are the same shape. Ask me."**

**"No."**

**"Say why."**

**"Because I would be the one deciding, and none of the four of them have been asked."**

**That was the nine seconds. He did not add to it, he did not soften it, he did not put a reason inside the reason, and nobody in that shop asked him to.**

The man of about forty-eight stood there for about four seconds.

**"That is the whole of your answer."**

**"That is the whole of my answer."**

**"You have just cost four hundred and sixteen households in one town and about nine in another a findable week, and a man of about thirty-three in a second district of this city about four days of being paid for driving, and a woman of about forty-four in a shop four days of bus travel away about two hours a week she was going to spend anyway, and you have done it in a shop in Lattice Ward in front of a woman of about thirty-four."**

**"Yes,"** Marek said.

**"Say that again in a different way so I know you mean it."**

**"If I had said yes, your four sheets would have gone into one box at about half past nine this morning, and there would be one number in this city that four towns could be found by, and by about April a town that wanted to answer something different would not have anywhere to put it that was not your box. And you have offered me the one hour of this week in which this work is easy, and the price of it is that the four of them cannot say no to you without standing in your office. I am not paying that. I would rather be wrong in about four months and be told."**

The woman of about thirty-four at that counter put her pen down, which she does not do.

**"That is the most expensive sentence anybody has said in this shop since I started here,"** she said, **"and it is a refusal, and you are twenty-two."**

**"I know what I am,"** he said.

**"You are going to be the man who says no to things that work,"** she said, **"and in about four years that is going to be the only thing anybody knows about you."**

**"That is fair,"** he said, and did not say anything else about it, and she did not thank him and he did not thank her.

The man of about forty-eight said: **"I am not going to pretend to disagree with you. I think you are right and I think it will cost you and I think you knew that at about four o'clock on Wednesday, which is earlier than this morning, and I am not going to use that."**

**"You are entitled to use it."**

**"I am not going to,"** he said, and picked up the four sheets, and put them back in the carrier bag in the order they came off his board, and left.

Nobody in that shop agreed with him. Nobody in that shop thanked him.

---

**From about half past nine until about ten he copied four answers onto four sheets by hand.**

There were four sheets in the bag and one of them was the fourth town's with four lines under it, and the four answers had to go into four separate envelopes because there is no arrangement anywhere in this city for four answers to go in one place, and the firm's own rule is one sheet one place, and a man in a second district who reads a sheet with four answers on it will ask nobody and will simply send what he can weigh.

So he wrote them out. Four hundred and sixteen, and one fewer, and the same number again, and the bigger one with the four lines. He wrote each one in full, in ink, in his own hand, and copied the four lines of the fourth one word for word, and put the date on each, and put each in an envelope, and wrote the place on each envelope, and there is a rule about how a place is written on an envelope and he wrote it four times without getting it wrong once and it took him about half an hour.

**That was the hour. If he had said yes at nine, the four answers would have been in one place at about half past nine and the rest of the week would have been easier for him and for about nine hundred people in four towns and for about four hundred and sixteen households. Instead, from about half past nine until about ten on the Thursday of week two hundred and nineteen, one man of twenty-two wrote the same four answers out four times by hand at a counter in Lattice Ward, and it was the only hour of that week in which the work would have been easy, and he was in it, and it was his, and nobody else was in it, and the only cost that has ever been named out loud that was exactly one person's and not anybody else's is this one.**

The woman of about thirty-four stood at the counter and watched him do it and did not offer to help and did not go away. A man of about thirty-one with a meter under his arm came in at about ten to ten, saw what was going on, and asked what it was, and Marek told him, and the man said **"that is a waste of an hour of your life and you know it,"** and Marek said **"yes,"** and the man said **"and it is not mine to talk you out of,"** and left with his meter.

**What he noticed on the Thursday evening, and told nobody, is that the offer had been put to a firm and to nine people on a second floor in one city and had not been put to one of the four places, and that it could not have been, because the four places are four days of bus travel away and the offer was made on a Wednesday and the answer was wanted on a Thursday.**

That is not a fault in him. He is entitled to offer a thing to the people who can weigh a load. He is not entitled to offer a thing to four towns through a person in a city, and he did not do that, and the reason the refusal was in nine seconds is that the offer was already the right size and the only thing wrong with it was where it had been made from.

**What he wanted, on the Thursday night, in four warm rooms with about four people in them who were not asked anything, was to have had the hour back. He did not want the offer to have been made. He wanted the hour from about half past nine until about ten, and it was a real hour and it was a good hour and he spent it writing four answers out four times so that four towns would each get one, and there is nobody in the world he can hand that hour to, including the man of about forty-eight, who has not asked for it and would not take it.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Thursday, which is on no form and which nobody noticed: he ate his lunch at about four in the afternoon, standing up, behind the cage, because he had been in the four rooms' kitchen at about half past seven in the morning and there had been four people asleep in there and he had not wanted to switch a light on. He ate bread and a piece of cheese standing up in about nine feet by eleven with a man in the cage who has his face to the wall and who could hear every mouthful, and he chewed as quietly as a person can chew bread, and the man in the cage turned his head about an inch at the end of it and did not say anything, and Marek has not mentioned it to anybody and is not going to.**

Nobody thanked anybody, at a counter or on a road, and the shutter came down at about ten, and the day is a Thursday and there is no shop on a Sunday in this city or in any of the four towns.

*Five words and a clause, as a record of nine seconds.* **Where: a counter in Lattice Ward at about nine on a Thursday morning, in a shop with a cage in it and a bench and a rail of two, with a woman of about thirty-four behind the counter and the door open. Who asked: a man of about forty-eight who is a credit clerk with a trade counter on a wide road and a correct credit policy, who had been asked the previous afternoon to come at nine and not in a room, and who came. What he gave first: the same four reasons in the same order, being that nine days becomes one, that a driver can then be paid for going to a place that will take the load, that four places agreeing with each other are worth less to him than four that do not, and that four correct answers in four shapes are unreadable in this city unless they are one shape. What he was given: one word, then, when he asked for a reason, a clause of thirteen words. The reason given: that he would be the one deciding, and that none of the four places had been asked. What was said about the cost, by the man who had offered it, and not in anger: that a refusal there costs four hundred and sixteen households a findable week and about nine people in a second district about four days of paid driving, and was made in a shop in front of one woman. What was said in that shop afterwards, and by whom: a woman of about thirty-four, that it was the most expensive sentence anybody had said there since she started and that he was twenty-two; the man of twenty-two, that he knew; the woman, that in about four years being the man who says no to things that work would be the only thing anybody knew about him; the man of twenty-two, that that was fair. The man of about forty-eight said he thought the refusal was right, said he thought it would cost, said he thought the man had known on the Wednesday afternoon, and said he would not use that. Who agreed with the refusal: nobody in that shop, and nobody afterwards in the week who has said so to him. Who thanked him: nobody. Owner: one box at a depot that is not going to be built, one hour from about half past nine until about ten, and four envelopes.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A correct offer, made correctly, by a person entitled to make it, was refused in about nine seconds, and the hour it would have saved was spent by one man writing four answers out four times, and the four answers still went down the road in four envelopes, one to a place.** Nobody thanked anybody that day and nobody admired anything anybody did, and the shutter came down at about ten with no Sunday in the day to alter it. He taught nobody, demonstrated to nobody, assessed nobody and assisted nobody, and none of the four was requested. **The tally of right acts that altered nothing, performed by people owed nothing, stays at two, being a clipboard inside a cold-store door in the first of the four towns and four torn pieces of one list in a coat pocket at that town's gate, both the same woman of about fifty-two, and a refusal is not an instance of it in either direction: it was nine seconds, it changed what about four hundred households are sent, and the man who made it is owed nothing and is owed a good deal that nobody has offered.** The rail card is one thousand and sixty-four days and stayed in the rail in a rail of two; a return card with two boxes and a clip is a different object and was not in this entry and did not go into any of the four envelopes. The rooms are one thousand and sixty days and warm. Nineteen lines on two nails and no twentieth. The drawer under the bench is shut with three sheets inside it. Nobody has yet put a written thing in the hands of the man of about fifty-one at the wall and he has not asked for one. The page belonging to the woman of about thirty is the eighth of eight in that ring binder and was not read, and no one said thank you to her and no one said sorry.
