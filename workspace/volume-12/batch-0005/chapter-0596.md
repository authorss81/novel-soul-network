# Chapter 596 — A Wednesday With Eleven Jobs And About Nine Chairs On A Second Floor In Crown Terrace And A Man Of About Forty-Eight Who Is The Credit Clerk Of A Firm With A Trade Counter On A Wide Road Putting Four Correct Reasons On The Table Before He Asks For Anything, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Five Who Says One Thing In About Nine Seconds And Is Given Nothing For It, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Does Not Answer

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, a second floor in Crown Terrace from about four until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was one thing offered correctly, in a face, by a person who had the standing to offer it.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, every one of them dated, eleven people through the counter, the last of them at about ten past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, the second floor in Crown Terrace four until six, the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and above what it has cost all winter, four names on that one tenancy are between them paying it, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** Those four rooms are one thousand and fifty-nine days, one hundred and fifty-one weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and sixty-three days, one hundred and fifty-one weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The nineteenth line on the two nails above that bench is seven hundred and fifty-five days, one hundred and seven weeks and six days. The hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and ninety-two days, ninety-eight weeks and six days, and the ask is seven hundred and forty-nine days, one hundred and seven weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is six hundred and sixty-five days, ninety-five weeks to the day. Not one of those four is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and no figure of days off is carried for that heating anywhere in this entry.**

---

**The second floor in Crown Terrace has about nine chairs in it and a table and a window that faces the back of a building, and it has been used on a Friday for about four years and it was used on this Wednesday for the first time, and about nine people came, and none of them came because they were told to.**

He is about forty-eight and he is the credit clerk of a firm that has a trade counter on a wide road and a correct credit policy and no villain in it anywhere. He has the standing to offer what he is going to offer and he uses it properly, which means he puts the four reasons it is good on the table before anybody in the room has to ask.

**"I will say why this is good first,"** he said, **"and then I will ask, and if I ask first you should all stop me."**

He put a printed sheet face up. There was a diagram on it that was not a picture of anything. There was a box on it with two compartments and a line between them, and on the left of the box a name and on the right a name, and the two names were not the same name.

**"One return,"** he said. **"At the end of our own route, at the depot, we put one thing. Every place that gets anything from us sends it into that one thing. A card, a sheet, a hole in a board, I do not care what it is. It comes back the same week instead of nine days later."**

**"That is four true things and I am going to give you all four and then stop talking."**

**"One: nine days becomes one, and nine days is the whole of why a household that moved in October is still on last year's list. Two: a firm can weigh a load it has invoiced, and a man can be paid for driving to a place that will take the load, and about nine people in a second district of this city are losing about four days a fortnight not being paid for that. Three: your four places have just answered one question four ways. Four hundred and sixteen, one fewer, the same number again, and one that is bigger, and every one of the four is correct. Four: if I had put that question to you in March, I would have got the same number four times, and you would have got the same wrong number four times, and the four of you would have agreed with each other and been wrong together. You did not agree with each other this week and that is worth something to me."**

He put his hands flat on the table.

**"That is the offer. It is one return at our end, it costs us the box and about four days of a man's time, and we do not get to see inside it. If any one of your four places wants to answer it differently, they still can, because I am not asking you to be the same. I am asking you to be findable. Those are different and I know they are different because four of you have just proved it over four days."**

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

**"Who do I ask,"** he said.

**"Ask him,"** the man of about sixty-one with a clipboard said, **"he has been in every one of those four conversations and he is not in a chair."**

**"I am in a chair,"** Marek said.

**"You have been sitting in it for about four minutes and you have not looked at the sheet once,"** the man of about forty-eight said, **"and the man with the clipboard is right that you have been in all four, and I am asking."**

**"You are asking,"** Marek said. **"I heard you."**

**"That is not a no."**

**"It is not a yes either,"** Marek said, and the man of about forty-eight said that was fair, and sat down, and the sheet stayed on the table.

---

**A woman of about thirty-five was in the fourth chair and she had come because about four hundred and fifty people had come to a hall in this city where she spoke once and was agreed with by nobody, and this was smaller and cooler and she came anyway.**

She organises about eleven rooms in four districts of this city that have each refused to be connected to anything and have never met one another. Eleven of her own rooms were cut in the spring by somebody using her own organisation's printed method, and two of them were open for about nine minutes, and a piece of paper of the right shape put a hospital in this city on a list in about nine seconds, and she has said all of that out loud twice and has been thanked for it once by a person who was thanking her for something else.

**She said one thing in about nine seconds and she said it in a face and she was given nothing for it.**

**"We printed the method,"** she said. **"It is the only thing any of us has ever printed that anybody else could use. It is eleven pages and it is free and about four hundred people in this city have it. If there is one return and it is yours, then in about four years the method is not a thing eleven rooms can hand to each other. It is a form with your box on it, and the box is at your depot, and about four hundred and fifty people in this city will be sending you their refusals in your own handwriting and calling it the method."**

The man of about forty-eight said: **"That is a fair thing to be frightened of and it is not a reason."**

**"It is not a reason,"** she said. **"I know it is not a reason. I have said four true things in rooms this month and not one of them has ever been a reason, and I am not on a list and nothing that happened today was decided by me and I am not going to pretend that it was."**

**"Thank you,"** the man of about forty-eight said, and it was the only time anybody said that word in that room all afternoon, and he said it to a person who had not asked for it.

She said nothing else. She had not joined anything. She was not going to be given a turn and she was not given one.

---

**He left at about ten to six and said one sentence at the door and it was not a refusal, and about four people in that room thought nothing of it until the following week.**

**"Ask me at nine on a Thursday,"** he said, **"and not in here, and I will give you an answer in about nine seconds and you will not like it."**

The man of about forty-eight wrote that down because he wrote everything down.

**What he noticed on the Wednesday evening, and told nobody in four rooms, is that the printed sheet had a number already on it and the number was for a place and not for a person.**

It was in the top corner, in a box of its own, and it was a route and not a name, and he has been on the receiving end of about four hundred numbers that were for a place and not for a person since the spring, and every one of them was a person who could not be found, and this one is a box at a depot where four hundred and sixteen households in one town and about nine more in another have never been a single number in their lives.

The offer would work. That is what the four reasons were for. A firm that puts one return at the end of its own route and does not look inside it would get four answers the same week, and the four answers would still be four answers, and the four of them would be findable, and about four hundred and sixteen doors in a town four days of bus travel away would stop being a street and start being a set of people.

**He wanted, on the Wednesday night, to have been asked in a doorway by a man carrying a clipboard and about four other things, and to have said no in about four seconds and been gone, because that is a refusal that costs nothing and can be said again. He was asked in a room with nine chairs in it in front of nine people, and the refusal is going to have to be said in that room, or in a room like it, or on a counter at nine in the morning in front of one woman of about thirty-four, and either way about four people who do not have to do anything with it are going to hear it.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Wednesday, which is on no form: he sat in the fourth chair of that second floor for about two hours from four until about six and did not look at the sheet that went round once. The sheet is the whole business of that room. It went round nine times and he did not read it, and a woman of about twenty-nine who types for a firm noticed that he did not read it, and looked at him twice, and said nothing to him and nothing about him, and he noticed her noticing and neither of them said a word and she has not mentioned it since and will not.**

Nobody thanked anybody, at a counter or on a second floor, and the shutter came down at about ten, and the day is a Wednesday and this is not a Sunday and a shop does not open on a Sunday.
