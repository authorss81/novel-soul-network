# Chapter 595 — A Monday With Four Sheets Laid Out On One Counter Beside Eleven Dated Jobs, Three Of Them Carrying A Number And The Fourth Carrying A Number And Four Lines Under It About A Woman Of About Forty-Four In The Third Of The Four Towns Who Has Been Signing For The First Town's Deliveries Since The Spring Without Telling Anybody, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Says Out Loud Which Two Of The Four Are Worse Than The Old One And Is Not Consoled

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was four answers to one question laid out in a row on a counter by about eleven o'clock in the morning.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, every one of them dated, eleven people through that door, the last at about half past five. A bench in Lattice Ward until two, the counter two until six, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that one tenancy are carrying the difference, and a fortnight a cook spent out of her own money is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** Those four rooms are one thousand and fifty-seven days, one hundred and fifty-one weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and sixty-one days, one hundred and fifty-one weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail holds two.

**The nineteenth line on those two nails is seven hundred and fifty-three days, one hundred and seven weeks and four days. The hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and ninety days, ninety-eight weeks and four days, and the ask is seven hundred and forty-seven days, one hundred and six weeks and five days, and the man of about fifty-one against that north wall is six hundred and sixty-three days, ninety-four weeks and five days. None of those four is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating is a closed thing and this entry carries no figure of days off for it anywhere.**

---

**The four sheets came down the road in one carrier bag with a man of about thirty-three who was going that way anyway, and he left the bag on the counter at about eleven and drove on.**

Three of them carried a number. The fourth carried a number and four lines under it.

**The first was from a bus depot yard in the second of the four towns. Four hundred and sixteen. The same number as last year, said out loud, in a yard office, to nine people.**

**The second was from a school in the fourth of the four towns. One fewer than the school had been sending, and under it, in the same hand, a line saying that the household had been taken off the list on purpose and that the person who took it off would be the person who explained it if anybody asked.**

**The third was from a row of four hundred and a bit doors in the first of the four towns. The same number as the first town has been sending, and under it a list: about nine evenings a week, and one pair of hands from nine until eleven in a shop that shuts at nine.**

**The fourth was from the working fields in the third of the four towns, and it was not the same number as the third.**

It was larger by about nine.

**Under it, in a woman's hand: that the third town has been signing for the first town's deliveries since the spring, because the driver's route ends at her gate and the gate is where the parcels were, and that nobody in the first town has been told, and that she is telling them now, on this sheet, on purpose, and that if the firm sends one load to two places then two places have to be on one board and about four hundred people in the first town do not know it.**

The woman who wrote it is about forty-four. She keeps a list for eleven field hands and does the gate two days a week for a firm four days of bus travel away because there is nobody else to do it. She wrote four lines and signed nothing.

**All four answers are correct. None of the four is a mistake. Two days ago each of the four places had been asked one question by the same firm on the same printed sheet, and about four days of bus travel each way, and the four of them had gone at it for about nine seconds each and got four different things, and the four things all work.**

---

He said it in a face at about half past eleven with about four people in that shop.

The woman of about thirty-four who answers that counter. A man of about thirty-one with a meter under his arm. A man of about twenty-nine who types for a firm and had come in for a job card. A man of about thirty-eight who keeps a rota for about nine flats and who had no business being there and came anyway.

**"Two of those four are worse than what we had before,"** he said. **"The school and the row. Those two are worse and I am saying it now in a shop and I am not saying it to be interesting."**

**"Which way is the school worse,"** the man of about thirty-one said.

**"A household of two people over a bakery is going to not be found in the spring, and they do not know yet, and nobody in that town is going to knock. That is one household and it is the whole of the cost and she chose it on purpose and she was right to."**

**"And the row."**

**"The row's answer is better than what we had and it costs about nine people's evenings, and about four hundred households get a correct number and do not know that anybody did anything, and one woman is going to be at a table from nine until eleven twice a week until she stops. That is worse than what we had, and it is worse for the people who could least afford it, and the number comes back the same."**

**"And the other two."**

**"The depot is the same as what we had and it is the only one of the four that is not a decision, and she said so in a yard and I would have said so in a yard. The fields is the one I cannot place."**

The man of about twenty-nine said: **"It is bigger. Bigger is better."**

**"Bigger is better for nine households in the fields who are getting something they were not getting,"** Marek said. **"And it is worse for a town that now has a number on it that it did not know was there, and for a firm that will now send one load to two places and read one sheet, and for a road in four months when a driver stops at a gate and cannot work out which town he is in."**

**"That is a bad thing you have just said,"** the man of about thirty-one said, **"about the row, and you have said it about a row of people doing four times the work for nothing."**

**"It is a bad thing,"** Marek said, **"and it is the only one of the four I would have chosen, and I have not said that, and I am not going to, and the fact that I would have chosen it is not a reason for the other three and it is not a comfort to the two."**

**"What is it then,"** the man of about thirty-eight said.

**"It is that I have just said out loud in a shop which two towns are worse than they were, and about nine hundred people in four places will hear that in about four days by a bus, and none of them asked me and none of them will thank me, and I am now the man who has said it, and there is no form on which I can be the man who has not said it."**

Nobody in that shop thanked him. The man of about thirty-eight said he had to go. The man of about twenty-nine read the four sheets again from the top and put them back in the bag in the order they came off the driver's board, which was not the order of the four towns, and did not say that he had done it.

The woman of about thirty-four said: **"You put the fourth one on top."**

**"It had four lines on it,"** the man said.

**"That is not a reason to put it on top,"** she said, **"that is just a reason it is on top."**

---

**What he noticed on the Monday evening, and told nobody, is that three of those four answers are about households and one of them is about a route.**

The depot counts doors. The school counts a card. The row counts four things about one door. All three of those are attempts to get at a person through the only evidence anybody has got, which is a piece of paper and a house.

The fourth one is not about anybody. **The fourth one is about where a vehicle stops.** A number that has been made by asking a route where it ends is a true number and it is not a count of households, and a firm that reads it will send stock, and the stock will arrive at a gate, and about four hundred people in a town that has never been on a board with anybody will be standing at a door in the spring wondering why it is one of them.

Nobody in four towns has ever heard of the other three. There is no list of the four. There is a printed sheet with the same line on it going to four places and nothing anywhere that says the four of them are a thing.

**What he wanted, on the Monday night, in four warm rooms with about four people in them who were not asked anything, was for somebody to have come in and said which two were worse before he had worked it out, and to have been wrong, and to have been argued with. Nobody came. He has spent four years being the person who gets asked and this is the first time in about a year that the answer was the thing that was wanted and he was the wrong side of it.**

**And the small thing he gave up on the Monday, which is on no form and which the woman at that counter noticed and said nothing about: a man of about thirty-one had been asked to sign the second box on a job sheet ever since he started coming, and he had never signed it, and the second box has been empty on every sheet in that shop for about four years, and on the Monday Marek did not ask him, for the first time in about four years, and handed him the sheet and let him go, and the box stayed empty, and the sheet went through with an empty box on it, and the woman of about thirty-four wrote the day out in the book and did not look up and did not say one word about it, and neither did he.**

Nobody thanked anybody, in a shop or in a yard or a hall or a first floor, and the shutter came down at about ten, and the day is a Monday and the shop shut at ten and there is no such thing as a shop on a Sunday anywhere in this city or in the four towns.

*Which two are worse, as a record of one man saying it in a face.* **Where: a repair counter in a shop in Lattice Ward, at about half past eleven on a Monday, with four people in the shop. Who spoke: a man of twenty-two who is not appointed, not consulted and not on a list, and who was not asked. What was on the counter: four printed sheets carrying four answers to one question, one line of question, all four correct, three of them a number and the fourth a number with four lines under it from a gate at the end of a route in the third of the four towns. What he said: that two of the four are worse than what the arrangement was doing before, and named them. The first, worse, being a school list in the fourth of the four towns, where a household of two people over a bakery has been taken off the list on purpose and will not be found in the spring and does not know it and will not be knocked for. The second, worse, being a row of four hundred doors in the first of the four towns, where the number comes back the same and is right and about nine people's evenings and one pair of hands from nine until eleven twice a week have bought it. What he said of the other two: that the depot's is the same as before and is the only one of the four that is not a decision, and that the fields' is the one he cannot place, being better for nine households in that town and worse for a town with a number on it that did not know, and a firm sending one load to two places, and a road in four months. What he refused to say: that the row's is the one he would have chosen, on the grounds that saying it is not a reason for the other three and is not a comfort to the two. What it cost him: that he has now said in a shop which two towns are worse, and about nine hundred people will hear it by bus in about four days, and none of them asked him, and he is the man who said it and there is no form on which he can be the man who did not. Who thanked him: nobody. Owner: four sheets, one carrier bag, and a sentence with about four days of bus travel in it.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **Four places answered one question four ways in about nine seconds each, all four answers are correct, two of them are worse than the arrangement they replaced, and a man of twenty-two said out loud in a shop which two, and nobody thanked him for it.** Nobody thanked anybody in that shop, in that yard, in that hall or in that first floor, and the shutter came down at about ten and this entry holds no Sunday. He taught nobody, demonstrated nothing to anybody, assessed nobody and assisted nobody, and none of the four was asked of him in the course of the day. **The count of proper acts that changed nothing, done by people owed nothing, is still two — a clipboard on a nail inside a cold-store door in the first of the four towns, and four torn pieces of one list in a coat pocket at that same gate, both belonging to one woman of about fifty-two — and nothing in these four answers is a third of it, because each of the four took more than nine seconds and each of the four changed what a household gets in the spring, and the woman of about forty-four who crossed a line out of a school list is owed nothing by the crossing and the woman of about forty-four who signs at a gate is owed nothing by the signing.** The card in the rail is one thousand and sixty-one days old and was in the rail all day in a rail that takes two, and a two-box return card with a clip is a different object from it and is not in this entry. The rooms are one thousand and fifty-seven days and warm. Nineteen lines are on two nails and the twentieth has never been started. The drawer under the bench holds three sheets and is shut. That man of about fifty-one against the wall has still not been given a written thing and has not asked. The page belonging to the woman of about thirty is the eighth of eight and nobody turned to it, and no one apologised to her and nobody owed her an apology.
