# Chapter 574 — A Friday Counter, Eleven Dated Jobs, And A Pad Of Forms With A Box On The Back Of It That Somebody In This City Finally Read Out Loud To Nobody, And Then Four Rooms And Four Sentences Written One Under The Other And No Body To Carry Any Of Them

**The Friday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was a printed box on the back of a form read out loud in a shop for the first time, and then an evening spent writing four things that could not be sent anywhere.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday, every one of them dated, eleven people through that counter door, the last of them at about ten past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, and the service road and the four rooms from about half past six. **The heating in that flat is on and above what it was all winter, four names on that tenancy are carrying the rise, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and five days, one hundred and forty-three weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and nine days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The seventeenth line on the two nails above the Lattice Ward bench is seven hundred and seventy-seven days old, one hundred and eleven weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those rooms is six hundred and eleven days, eighty-seven weeks and two days, and the ask is six hundred and ninety-five days, ninety-nine weeks and two days, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and thirty-eight days, ninety-one weeks and one day. None of those three figures is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and there is no figure of days off for that heating anywhere in this entry.**

---

A woman of about forty-six came to that counter at about half past three with a pad of forms in a carrier bag and asked whether anybody there had ever filled in the box on the back.

Nobody in the shop had.

She put the pad on the counter and turned the top sheet over and put her finger on a printed heading, and then she said the thing out loud, in a shop, at about half past three on a Friday, to about four people, and about four of them did not hear it at all.

> The back of every form has a box for the sentence your own place uses where the arrangement is different.
> Nobody owns the sentence. The first person to write it owns it, the fourth is quoting, and the fifth can be handed it and say it.

**She did not read it twice and she did not explain it and she was not asked to. She took the pad back, put it in the bag, said she was going, and went, and nobody in that shop said thank you, and she has not been back to that shop, and nobody in that shop has said a word about it to anybody including each other, and the pad is on a bench in a firm four days of bus travel away.**

* * *

**About four people came in for the heat that night and none of them was asked anything and one of them has a bad chest and was not asked that either.**

He had a notebook. It is the only notebook in that room and it is not the ring binder and it is not on a shelf with anything else on it.

**What he did on that Friday evening, after the four people had gone, was write four sentences, one under the other, one for each of the four places, in four different sets of words, so that each of the four could be asked the same question in its own words and could answer it in a sentence that belonged to the room it was asked in.**

The first one took him about eleven minutes. The fourth took about four.

**He read all four of them out loud to an empty room and then he put the notebook down and did not do anything else with it, and the thing he did not do is the whole of this entry and it took the rest of the night.**

He has no way to get a piece of paper to a town four days of bus travel away. Not a way that is his. He does not drive for anybody. He is not on a list of anybody. There is a bus that takes about eleven hours and a man of about thirty-three who has driven four days with nine crates and would do it again if he were asked, and asking him is a thing you do to a man once and then you are a man who asks.

**So four sentences are in a notebook in four rooms off a service road, and there are about four hundred and fifty people in this city who could be asked to carry one of them, and he has not asked any of them, and the reason he has not asked is that a man who gives four towns four different sentences is a man the four towns come back to, and he is not going to be that, and the reason he is not going to be that is the reason the woman of about forty-four on a trade counter was right about a sheet of paper on Wednesday, and he worked that out at about eleven at night with the notebook shut.**

**What he noticed on the Friday, and did not say to anybody, and has not put in the notebook because the notebook is for the four sentences, is that there is no mark anywhere on a form that says how many times a box has been filled. The second time a sentence goes into that box and the fifth time are the same piece of paper, in the same typeface, in the same box, on the same sheet, and nothing in this city can tell the two apart, and neither can anybody four days away, and the man of about thirty-three who found the fourth one on a Monday evening did it by driving to four places, and not one person in four towns would ever do that, and that is not a fault in any of them.**

**What he wanted and did not say on that Friday, and has not said to anybody who was not in that room, was to be the man who gave four places four different sentences instead of one sheet, and the not-saying is not modesty. He wanted it and then he worked out that the four sentences in that notebook are worth nothing until a person with a van is standing in a town holding them, and he is not that person, and the wanting is the whole of what the evening was.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*577. Friday of week 211, at ten, and this entry is a counter at about half past three and a printed box on the back of a form read out loud once in a shop to about four people who did not hear it, and then four rooms off a service road and four sentences written one under the other in four different sets of words, and a notebook with nothing at the top of a page and no date on the entry and no person named against any of the four, and a man of twenty-two who has no way to get any of the four to a town four days of bus travel away and has not asked one of about four hundred and fifty people in this city who might have gone.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Friday carried eleven jobs, all eleven dated, eleven people through the counter, the last at about ten past five.** The day as lived: bench in Lattice Ward until two, the counter from two until six, four rooms off the service road from about half past six. At that counter, in the order it has stood for years: a bench, a cage with a man turned away from the room, a roller shutter, a tool roll with one screwdriver down its fourth pocket, a wall list in its sleeve, a shelf of finished work, a rail with two cards in it. Callers: eleven, four refusals. Work: eleven, four declines, nothing escalated, nothing returned. Charge: nine and then ten, wrong, unmoved, not said, and not said to anybody. The heat over the service road: on, dearer than the winter's, four names on that tenancy between them carrying the rise, a cook's fortnight laid out of her own money and owed to nobody. Service-road rooms: one thousand and five days, one hundred and forty-three weeks and four days, four rooms and one behind, warm. Rail card: one thousand and nine days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and one day, four days beyond the room, rail of two. The board on its two nails: twelfth, nine hundred and twenty-five; thirteenth, eight hundred and seventy-six; fourteenth, eight hundred and forty-one; fifteenth, eight hundred and twenty; sixteenth, seven hundred and ninety-five; seventeenth, seven hundred and seventy-seven; eighteenth, seven hundred and twenty-three; nineteenth, seven hundred and one. What went onto that board across the Friday: nothing added, nothing removed, no twentieth line. Binder: shut, on its shelf, not opened. The leaf in it that is hers: the eighth of the eight, not read, not put to her, no apology offered to her. Master's fifth line: blank. Post at the corridor end: five hundred and fifty-three days, seventy-nine weeks to the day. Nine hand copies of a page front: five hundred and seventy-one days, eighty-one weeks and four days, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found. The ask: six hundred and ninety-five days, ninety-nine weeks and two days. The hold: six hundred and thirty-eight days, ninety-one weeks and one day, another city, nothing asked of him and nothing given to him. The separation: three hundred and eighty-five days, fifty-five weeks to the day, not shorter and not ended, and about four days of bus travel each way in the week this entry falls in did not shorten it by a minute. **The room above a line in another district did not sit on the Friday, nothing was announced on any morning of these ten entries except at a sitting, and the count therefore stands at forty-six of which forty-one correspond, where the last sitting left it. The book stands at sixty lines and the tin at seventy-three and the two are not one figure and neither is convertible into the other.**

*What the four sentences were, and what they are not.* **Where they were written: a room off a service road, after about four people had come in for the heat and gone, on the Friday evening. What prompted them: a woman of about forty-six who brought a pad of forms to a counter at about half past three, turned the top sheet over, put a finger on a printed heading on the back, said a thing out loud in about nine seconds, took the pad away, said thank you for the two minutes, and has not been back. What the four sentences are: one question for each of the four places, in a different set of words in each one, so that each place can be asked the same thing and can answer in a sentence belonging to its own room. How long each took: about eleven minutes for the first and about four for the fourth. Where they are: a notebook that is not the ring binder and is not on a shelf with anything, with nothing at the head of the page and no date against any of the four and no name against any of the four. Why they went nowhere: because a man of twenty-two who keeps a bench and a counter has no way of getting a piece of paper to a town four days of bus travel away, has no van and is on no list and did not ask the one man he knows who has driven four days with nine crates, and did not ask about four hundred and fifty people in this city who might have gone. What he gave up by not asking: the only arrangement in which four places can be asked one question and answer it four ways instead of one, which is the arrangement a woman of about forty-four refused a sheet of paper to make on Wednesday, and he is not going to disagree with her about it. Owner: a notebook, and about four hundred and fifty people who were not asked, and one Friday night that did not come back.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A printed box on the back of a form was said out loud in a repair shop on a Friday afternoon to about four people who did not hear it, and at about eleven that night a man of twenty-two wrote four different versions of one question for four places he cannot get to and put the notebook down and left them there.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, and the shutter came down at about ten because this is a Friday and there is no Sunday among the ten days these ten entries cover, and because there is no such thing as a shop on a Sunday, and the two facts are the same fact. He taught nobody, demonstrated nothing, assessed nobody and assisted nobody on the Friday and nobody asked him to. The count of correct things that changed nothing, done by people who are owed nothing, stands at two, being a clipboard on a nail inside a cold-store door in the first of the four towns and four torn pieces of a list in a coat pocket at a gate in the same town, and nothing in these ten entries moves it, and **the woman of about forty-six who turned the pad over and read that heading out loud is not a third instance of it, because she was in a shop buying a carrier bag's worth of paper and the reading took her about nine seconds and nobody asked her for it and she is owed nothing and it changed nothing, and the two reasons together are why it is not on the count.** The card in the rail is one thousand and nine days old and was in the rail all day in a rail that holds two, unmoved and unturned, and a card with two boxes and a clip in a box from a firm with a yellow strip on it is a different object from it and is in this entry only in the sentence about the woman of about forty-six and is not joined to it. The rooms are one thousand and five days old and warm, four rooms and one behind. The board stands at nineteen lines. The man of about fifty-one was at that north wall on the Friday evening with his bag on the floor and said nothing and is owed a written thing. **The four words that name the body which runs that room are not in this entry and are not alluded to in it.**
