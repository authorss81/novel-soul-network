# Chapter 579 — A Hall On A Wide Road On A Monday Evening With About Four Hundred And Fifty People In It, A Man Of About Thirty-Five Who Says Ten Words Out Loud And Is Agreed With By Nobody, And A Woman Of About Nineteen Off A Bus Who Says One Thing And Does Not Have To Be Believed

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, a hall on a wide road from about seven until about nine, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was about four hundred and fifty people and one true sentence and no agreement anywhere in the room.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, dated to the day, eleven people through that counter, the last at about half past five. Bench until two, the counter two until six, a wide road and a hall from about seven, the four rooms from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and dearer than it was through the winter, four names on that tenancy are carrying the rise between them, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and fifteen days, one hundred and forty-five weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and nineteen days, one hundred and forty-five weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The hardboard on two nails above that bench carries twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen and nineteen lines, and the nineteenth of them is seven hundred and eleven days, one hundred and one weeks and four days, and the twentieth has never been started. The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those rooms is six hundred and twenty-one days, eighty-eight weeks and five days, and the ask is seven hundred and five days, one hundred weeks and five days, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and forty-eight days, ninety-two weeks and four days. None of those three is a day anything happened to the heating in this city.**

---

**There was a sheet on a door on a wide road and it said there would be a meeting on the Monday and it did not say what about, and about four hundred and fifty people came in, and about four hundred and seventeen of them had voted in the spring on one of two things in a hall in this city, and the other four hundred and thirty-six had voted on the other, and the two numbers are the only two things in this city that about four hundred people have been arguing about since the spring.**

At about seven a man of about thirty-five got up who is not a doctor and does not practise and is not on any list and is the most visible organiser of a set of rooms in four districts of this city that say no to being connected. He is not the reason anything in this entry went wrong and he is not the villain of it and he was not the villain of the last ten weeks.

**He had a sheet in his hand and he had the only two minutes anybody was going to get, and he used about nine of them on the sentence and the rest of the two on being asked four questions by a woman of about fifty-two who chairs a standing committee of about nine chairs and sits on nothing else, and he answered all four and got nowhere with any of them.**

**"Eleven of our rooms were cut. We printed the method."**

**Nobody in that hall agreed with him. About four of them said the next thing, and one of them said it out loud, and the one who said it out loud was a woman of about thirty-one who has run one of those rooms for about four years.**

**"Then somebody else printed it."**

**"Somebody else printed it,"** the man of about thirty-five said. **"From our sheet. The sheet that says what a room will not do. Eleven people took a copy of a piece of paper that a person in this room wrote, and they used it to find out where the paper is kept in a building, in eleven buildings, and in two of the eleven they were in for about nine minutes."**

**"Who."**

**"I do not know, and if I did I would not be able to say, and the reason I am saying this out loud in this room is that everybody in this room has been reading that sheet in a different town for about four years and every one of you has believed it was yours."**

**"That is not the same as it being printed."**

**"No. It is not the same. It is a copy and a copy of a refusal is not a refusal, and the reason it matters is not the eleven rooms."**

**"Then what is it."**

**"The reason it matters,"** the man said, **"is that in the spring a piece of paper with the right shape on it went into an office in this city and about nine seconds later a hospital in this city was on a list, and the shape of that paper was the shape of our sheet, and nobody in that hospital knows a man of about thirty-five exists, and neither does anybody in that office, and that is the whole of what happened in about nine seconds."**

---

**He was given control of nothing in that room. He did not chair it, he was not on the committee, he was not on a list, and nothing that was decided in that hall on that Monday was decided by him, and about four hundred and fifty people went into that hall with the legitimate reason to be there, which is that nobody may be connected without being asked, and that reason is not wrong and is not being given up tonight and is not going to be given up because of a sentence in it.**

**And nobody thanked him. He was not thanked when he sat down and he was not thanked afterwards and he has not asked to be, and about four people in that hall have said since that he was right, which is a different thing, and one of the four said to another one of the four that being right afterwards is the cheapest thing available to a person who says a true thing to four hundred people.**

**And he is not redeemed by it and nothing in that hall put anything back for him, and the eleven rooms stay cut, and the two that were open for about nine minutes stay open, and the sheet that started it is still on a wall in a depot and on about nine other walls.**

---

**A woman of about nineteen got up about half an hour after that, and she had come up on the Sunday night on a bus of about eleven hours, and she is one of the eleven, and she works nights, and she said one thing and she did not have to be believed by anybody and was not and has not been asked to be.**

**"I was in the fourth one,"** she said. **"I was asleep. I have been asked twice this week whether I think it was my fault for being asleep, and both of those were by people who were not in it, and I have not got an answer for either, and I have come four days to say that in a room and not to be asked a single question about it."**

She sat down. About four hundred and fifty people in that hall did not ask her one question and she has not asked anybody for one and there is no form for it.

**Marek was at the back on the left, uninvited, on no list, with about four hundred and fifty people in front of him and a sheet of paper in his hand that is not his, and he said nothing, and he has now read the two numbers of the spring out of that sheet twice in his own head and has not said them to anybody.**

**Afterwards, in about nine seconds, in the doorway, two different people who had not met him asked him the same question: whether he could send them a copy of the thing the man of about twenty-four had said on a wide road on a Friday. He said yes to both of them. Neither of them asked him whether they could. He has two sheets in his coat with a sentence on them that a man of about twenty-four said in about nine seconds on a Friday and he is the man who made two copies of it in about eleven seconds on a Monday night, and he has not told either of them that, and he has not told either of them about the nineteen-year-old, and he has not been asked to by anybody.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
