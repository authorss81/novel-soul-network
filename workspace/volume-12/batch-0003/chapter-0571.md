# Chapter 571 — A Man Of About Thirty-Three Back In A Yard On A Wide Road With Nine Crates Under A Tarpaulin, And A Word He Says Out Loud Before He Says The Thing, And Nobody In That Yard Has Used The Word Before

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a yard behind a firm on a wide road from about half past six, and its shape was a man saying one word out loud that nobody else standing there had ever had to use.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, a date written against every one of them, eleven people through that counter door, and the last of them at about ten past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer than it was through the winter, four names on that tenancy are carrying the rise between them, and a fortnight that a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody when the month turns.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and one days old, one hundred and forty-three weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and five days, one hundred and forty-three weeks and four days, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

**The nineteenth line on the two nails above the Lattice Ward bench is six hundred and ninety-seven days old, ninety-nine weeks and four days. The hold of the man of about thirty-three, who is in another city and holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and nobody's authority, is six hundred and thirty-four days, ninety weeks and four days. The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of the service-road rooms is six hundred and seven days, eighty-six weeks and five days, and the ask is six hundred and ninety-one days, ninety-eight weeks and five days, and not one of those three is a day anything happened to the heating in this city.**

---

**The man who came into that yard on the Monday evening had driven four days and had a folded sheet in the inside pocket of a coat and nine crates stacked against the far wall under a tarpaulin that had come off about four hours before he arrived and had gone back on about nine minutes after.**

He is about thirty-three. He drives for a firm. He was in that same yard on the Friday of a week about three weeks ago and answered three of four questions about himself in eleven words and then gave the man of twenty-two the fourth one, and nobody in that yard has asked him anything since and he has not been back.

He had nothing to be repaired. That is in the entry because it is the first Monday in about four years in which a man came into that yard at about half past six for no mechanical reason at all.

He put a pad of forms on the bonnet of a car that is not his and squared it up with the heel of his hand.

**"Customary."**

He said the word first, on its own, in about a second and a half, to about four people, and none of the four had a use for it.

**"Say that again."** That was a man of about twenty-nine with a clipboard who keeps the gate.

**"Customary,"** the man of about thirty-three said, and put his thumb on the back of the top form, and **"I have never used that word in my life and I read it about eleven hours ago on the back of one of these, and there is a box down there, and the box has a heading on it, and the heading is that word."**

**"So it's a box."**

**"It's a box for a sentence,"** the man said. **"It says where the arrangement is different here, state the local practice in your own words. That's the line. It's printed. I've had it in a pad about nine months and I never turned it over."**

The yard went quiet in the way a yard goes quiet, which is not quiet at all, and four of them stopped what they were doing and none of them stopped for long.

**"And that's why you're here."**

**"No,"** the man said. **"That's why I'm telling you and then going home. I've been to nine places since the spring and four of them are in the four towns and in every one of those four somebody has already written in that box."**

He said the next part in about nine seconds and he said it to the four of them and not to the man of twenty-two, who was at the end of that yard with a bag of tools and had not come over.

**"It's the same sentence in all four. Three of them are in hands I've never seen. And not one of the four of them knows the other three wrote it, and I asked every one of them, and all four of them said they were the first."**

Nobody thanked him.

**Nobody in that yard said thank you, and nobody has thanked him since, and he did not appear to want it, and about four of the people who heard both halves of it have given an account of that Monday evening to other people and the four accounts agree and none of the four contains a thank you in it.**

---

**The man of twenty-two heard all of it from about nine feet away and crossed about four steps of concrete afterwards and asked one question, and the question was not about the four sentences.**

**"Where did the first one come from."**

**"I don't know."**

**"You drove past it first."**

**"I drove past it first and it was in a hand I'd seen before,"** the man said, **"and I have thought about that for about four days and I still don't know, and I have got nine crates and about four days of waiting left on them and I am not going to stand in this yard and pretend the rest of it is mine."**

**"All right."**

**"Right,"** the man said, and got back in the van, and it went out of that gate, and the gate man wrote the time in the gate book he keeps for the gate and not in one of his own.

---

**What he noticed on that Monday, and did not say to anybody, and has not written on anything that belongs to this shop, is a small thing and it is not about a yard.**

**A man who has come four days to tell you something wrong says the wrong thing first and the fact second. He said the word before he said the sentence, and the word was not in the sentence, and the word was the part he could not carry four days without getting tired of it. Nobody in that yard asked him one question about the four sentences. They asked him whether the gate was open at the weekend, and whether the firm was still running, and whether he wanted the light on in the shed, and all three of those have an answer on a form somewhere.**

**What he wanted and did not say, and has not put in writing and is not going to, was to be the fifth person to find one of those sentences. He wanted it the way he has wanted most things in about four years, which is completely and for about nine seconds, and then he worked out that a man who says out loud that he is going to look for the fifth one is a man that nine other people are going to ask, and there are four towns and a bus takes about eleven hours, and he is one person.**

**And the thing he did instead, at about eight that night, in four rooms off a service road with the heat on and about four people in them who were not asked anything, was this. He took his own hand and turned it over and wrote, in pencil, on the back of it, the sentence. One sentence, four times, because he wanted to see it four times and be certain it was four and not three.**

**Then he did not rub it out, and he did not wash it, and he went to bed with it, and it was still there on the Tuesday morning and it was grey by the Wednesday.**

**The cost of that is not a hand. A man who has spent about eleven years keeping a counter and a bench and a written record has never once made a piece of paper about a person who did not ask him for one, and he has now made one, and there is nowhere in this city to put an objection to it, and the four names of the four places are not on it and he has not got them yet.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

Monday of week 211, at ten, and this entry is a yard behind a firm on a wide road, a man of about thirty-three who came back for no mechanical reason at all, a pad of forms turned over for the first time in about nine months, a word said out loud before the sentence, four places with the same sentence already written in the same box, three of them in hands he has never seen, nobody thanked, and a man of twenty-two who wrote the sentence on the back of his own hand in pencil four times and did not wash it out.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Monday was eleven jobs with a date against each of the eleven and eleven people came through the counter door, the last at about ten past five.** The day as lived: Lattice Ward and a bench until two, the counter from two to six, a wide road and a yard from about half past six. Behind the counter, in the order it has stood for years: a bench, a cage with a man turned away from the room, a roller shutter, a tool roll with one screwdriver down the fourth pocket, a wall list inside its sleeve, a shelf of finished work, and a rail holding two. Callers: eleven, four refusals. Work: eleven, four declines, nothing escalated, nothing returned. What the two prices on the tickets have said, as they have said it for about eleven years: nine, and then ten; neither of them right; neither of them announced; and not said to one person in that shop on this Monday. The heat over that service road: on, above the winter's rate, four names on one tenancy between them carrying the increase, and a cook's fortnight laid out of her own money and owed to nobody. Service-road rooms: one thousand and one days, one hundred and forty-three weeks to the day, four rooms and one behind, warm. The board on its two nails: twelfth, nine hundred and twenty-one; thirteenth, eight hundred and seventy-two; fourteenth, eight hundred and thirty-seven; fifteenth, eight hundred and sixteen; sixteenth, seven hundred and ninety-one; seventeenth, seven hundred and seventy-three; eighteenth, seven hundred and nineteen; nineteenth, six hundred and ninety-seven. The ask: six hundred and ninety-one days, ninety-eight weeks and five days. The separation: three hundred and eighty-one days, fifty-four weeks and three days, not shorter, not ended, and about four days of bus travel each way in the week this entry falls in did not shorten it by a minute. **The room above a line in another district did not sit on the Monday, a count is announced at a sitting and not on a morning, and the count therefore stood where the last sitting left it, at forty-six of which forty-one correspond.** The book on that table stood at sixty lines and the tin at seventy-three and neither of those two was moved by anything in this entry and neither of them is convertible into the other.

*The pad of forms, and the word, and what a man drove four days to say.* **What arrived in that yard: nine crates under a tarpaulin and a pad of forms belonging to a firm of about four, carried by a man of about thirty-three who had driven from a town four days of bus travel away and had a folded sheet in the inside pocket of a coat. What was on the back of the top form, in a printed box, with a printed heading: a word he had never used. What the printed line under the box says: where the arrangement is different here, state the local practice in your own words. How long he had had the pad: about nine months, unturned. Why he came: to say the word and the fact, in that order, and then to go home. What the fact was, in about nine seconds: that he has been to nine places since the spring, that four of them are in the four towns, that somebody had already written a sentence in that box in every one of the four, that the four sentences are the same sentence, that three of the four are in hands he had never seen, and that all four of the people who wrote them believe they were the first and were asked and said so. What he was not asked about the four sentences by anybody in that yard: the gate man asked whether the gate was open at the weekend, about four people asked whether the firm was still running, and one asked whether he wanted the light on in the shed. What was said to him instead of that: where the first one came from, which he does not know, and which he had thought about for about four days. Who was thanked: nobody, then or since, and the four accounts of the evening that exist in this city contain no thank you. Owner: a pad, nine crates, a tarpaulin, and one word.**

*What the Monday did, and what it did not touch.* **A word nobody in a yard on a wide road had a use for came into this city on the back of a printed form, and a man said it out loud before he said the fact, and the fact was that four towns have the same sentence in the same box and that three of the four people who wrote it do not know the other three did.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter either, and the shutter came down at about ten as it does on a Monday, and there is no Sunday among the ten days these ten entries run to and there is no such thing as a shop on a Sunday. He taught nobody, demonstrated nothing, assessed nobody and assisted nobody on the Monday, and nobody put any of the four to him. **The count of correct things that changed nothing, done by people who are owed nothing, stands at two on this entry, being a clipboard on a nail inside a cold-store door in the first of the four towns and four torn pieces of the same list in a coat pocket at the same gate, and nothing in these ten entries moves it.** The card in the rail is one thousand and five days old, four days past the room, unmoved and unturned, and a card with two boxes and a clip in a box from a firm with a yellow strip on it is a different object from it and no such card was in that yard. The rooms off the service road are one thousand and one days old and warm. The board stands at nineteen lines and the twentieth has never been begun. The man of about fifty-one was at that north wall on the Monday evening with his bag on the floor and said nothing and is owed a written thing that nobody in this city has anywhere to write. **The four words that name the body which runs that room are in none of these ten entries and are not alluded to in this one, and the reason is a decision and it is paid once later, in a room, by the person entitled to say them.**
