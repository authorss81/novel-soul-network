# Chapter 576 — The Forty-Second Sitting Of A Room Above A Line In Another District, And Nine Items At Four Minutes, And A Tenth Thing That Was A Question Put To A Woman And Answered Truly And Wrongly, And The Book Stays At Sixty Lines, And The Count Is Forty-Seven Announced Of Which Forty-Two Correspond

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, a second floor above a line in another district from about ten until about eleven, and a room off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was eleven people up a stair and nine items at four minutes and one question that was not an item.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, every job dated, eleven people through that counter, the last at about ten past five. Bench until two, the counter two until six, the room above the line from ten to eleven, the four rooms off the service road from about half past six. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that tenancy are carrying it, and a fortnight that a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and ten days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and fourteen days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The ask is seven hundred days, one hundred weeks to the day, and it has not been put to anybody else since the day it was put. The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those rooms is six hundred and sixteen days, eighty-eight weeks to the day, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and forty-three days, ninety-one weeks and six days. The heating in that flat is a separate matter and carries no days-off figure on any page of this entry, and none of the three above is a day of it.**

---

About eleven people came up that stair. Six of them had been in the same chair on the last Wednesday of that kind and five had not, and the fifth had a case with three pads in it and had carried it up a flight on the Sunday night and had not asked anybody to take the bottom of it.

Nine chairs curved in with the two ends turned in, and the ninth of them stands hard against the wall with its back to the whole room, and it did not move in the hour, and nobody looked at it more than once, and whoever moves it is not named in this entry and is not going to be named in any of the ten.

A table. A tin standing open, with no lid on it, and no lock on it, and one that has never carried a lock in the whole nineteen years of that room. A book of four columns face up inside the reach of a woman's right hand from about ten until about eleven.

**Nine items at four minutes each, and all nine of them went to the chairs the way nine of them have gone every fourth Wednesday for about nineteen years.** A man of about thirty-one with a question about a form, told in about nine seconds what the form was actually about. A woman of about forty-four who wanted to bring a thing that had happened to somebody she works beside, told without unkindness that it was not hers to carry in there. A man of about nineteen in for the third time who had not spoken on the first two and spoke on the third, in about eleven words, and about four people in that room turned their heads. A woman of about twenty-seven who wanted to know whether anybody had to believe her, told in about nine seconds that nobody in that room had to believe anybody. A man of about fifty-eight who said one sentence and would not say a second. A man of about thirty-six in his ninth Wednesday in a row who had come to be near the window. A person of about sixty-one who has been in that room in nineteen years and who said the one thing in the hour that anybody said twice, and both times it was the number seven. A man of about thirty-three who said nothing for four minutes and then the whole of it in eleven seconds and would not say it again. And a woman of about forty-four who asked whether a room could refuse a thing without becoming the thing it refused.

**The tenth thing was not an item. It was the woman with the case, and it was a question, and nobody in that room knew before she asked it that it was going to be a question rather than a thing.**

---

**"Before me this year, who else has said the same words to you?"**

Eleven words, said to the woman who holds the room, with a case standing at the end of the table and a pad in it and nobody in that room on a list.

**"Four,"** the woman said. **"Four of them said it to me and I did not write any of it down, and the fifth was a man from a town four days of bus travel away in the spring and I did not write that either, and I am not going to stand here and tell you that it went in here and became true, because it did not."**

**"I didn't ask you that."**

**"No. You asked me who, and I have told you who. I have never once known whether a thing is true because of a room and I have had nineteen years in this one and I am not going to learn it in an hour at the end of a Wednesday."**

Nobody said a word.

**"I typed one of them,"** the woman with the case said. **"In a first floor in a second district, on the Monday of a week ago, on my own machine, and I did not know it was the same as the other four until a man of about fifty-eight said it out loud to me in front of about eleven people, and I want to know whether that is a thing that has happened in this room nineteen times or whether it has happened in no town at all."**

**"I could tell you what I wrote,"** the woman said, **"and there is nothing in it that is yours, and if there were something in it that was yours then I would read it back to you in front of these nine people, and there is not, and you may sit down."**

**The tenth thing ended in about nine seconds after that and nobody in that room was unkind and nobody in that room agreed about anything.**

---

**The book did not open. It was holding sixty lines when the woman sat down at ten and it was still holding sixty lines at eleven, and nothing went into the tin either, and the tin stands at seventy-three and has stood at seventy-three for about nineteen years.**

**The reason is not a rule and it is not evidence of anything and it is not a change in her, and it is this: in about nineteen years she has been told, by people who meant it kindly and by people who wanted a line out of her, what a line in that book is. What goes in that book is what somebody says about themselves, into her face, with nine other people sitting in nine chairs within hearing of every word of it. What came up that stair tonight was a question about four other people and a case belonging to a firm, and every part of it was about somebody else, and a book of four columns has no column for a person asking whether other people said a thing first.**

Nobody asked her why. Nobody in that room has asked her why in about nineteen years and nobody asked her why tonight and nobody is going to ask her why at the next one.

**The count was announced at the end, and a count is announced at a sitting and on no other morning: forty-seven, of which forty-two correspond.** Forty of the forty-seven match the forty Wednesdays the book has been open for, and the remaining five are older than the book and stand on a sheet of paper she has not shown to a living person.

**On the six days before this one the count stood at forty-six of which forty-one, and the two states are two states, and one and one is a count and is not a distance from anything.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The tenth thing, as a record of one question and about nine seconds.* **Who came up the stair: a woman of about thirty-four who types for a firm four days of bus travel away, with a case of three pads, on the Sunday night, on about eleven hours of bus, having carried it up a flight herself. What she asked, in eleven words, to the woman who holds the room: before me this year, who else has said the same words to you. What she was told, and it was true: four of them had said it to her and she wrote none of it down, and the fifth was a man from a town four days away in the spring and she did not write that either, and she would not tell the room that going in there had made any of it true, because it had not. What the caller said next: that she did not ask that; and that she has had nineteen years in that room and has never once known whether a thing is true because of a room and is not going to learn it in an hour. What the caller then said: that she typed one of the four, on her own machine, in a first floor in a second district, and did not know it was the same as the other three until a man of about fifty-eight said so in front of about eleven people, and that she wanted to know whether that had happened in that room nineteen times or had happened in no town at all. What she was told in answer: that she could be told what was written and there was nothing in it that was hers, and that if there were, it would be read back to her in front of those nine people, and there was not, and she could sit down. What the tenth thing was in total: about nine seconds after that. What happened to the book: nothing. What happened to the tin: nothing. Who asked her why: nobody, and nobody has in nineteen years, and nobody will at the next one. Owner: a case at the end of a table, a stair, and a chair that is hard against a wall and is not named as anybody's.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A woman asked a question in a room that has taken sentences for about nineteen years and was answered in a way that was true and was not the answer, and the book stayed shut at sixty lines, and the reason is a column that does not exist and is not a rule and is not a change in the woman who holds the room.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter either, and the shutter came down at about ten, and there is no Sunday among these ten entries and a shop is not a thing that opens on a Sunday. He taught nobody, demonstrated nothing, assessed nobody and assisted nobody, and nobody put any of the four to him, and the room he went to is a room he is on no list for, and he stood at the back of it and did not take the second chair for the fifth consecutive Wednesday of that kind, and **he knew on the Monday of that week, and has known since the Monday of a fortnight ago, that a fifth identical sentence was typed in a first floor in a second district, and he has not said a word of it to anybody in this city and is not going to, and the reason is that the only way to say it is to repeat four other people's sentences, and he has a fifth on the back of his own hand and there is nobody he is entitled to give it to.** The count of correct things that changed nothing, done by people who are owed nothing, stands at two, being a clipboard on a nail inside a cold-store door in the first of the four towns and four torn pieces of a list in a coat pocket at a gate in the same town, and nothing in these ten entries moves it, and the woman of about thirty-four who put the case down is not a third instance of it, because the case is her job and the firm pays her for the typing and she is owed a wage by the people who sent it. The card in the rail is one thousand and fourteen days old, four days past the room, unmoved and unturned, and a card with two boxes and a clip in a box from a firm with a yellow strip on it is a different object from it, and the woman who carried the case on the Sunday night has never had one on her and did not ask about one. The rooms are one thousand and ten days old and warm. The board stands at nineteen lines.
