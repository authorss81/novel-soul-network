# Chapter 575 — A Yard Behind A Firm On A Wide Road With The Tarpaulin Off Nine Crates, Four Of Them Clean Where Five Are Not, And A Man Of About Thirty-Three Who Can Say Where The Four Came From And Did Not Say It Three Weeks Ago And Says It Now And Is Not Thanked

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a yard behind a firm on a wide road from about half past six, and its shape was nine crates in the open with four marks on four of the nine that a fortnight of rain would not have made.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Monday, a date against each of the eleven, eleven people through that counter, the last at about half past five. Bench until two, counter two until six, the service road and the four rooms from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that tenancy are carrying the difference, and a fortnight that a cook put out of her own money is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody when the month turns.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and eight days, one hundred and forty-four weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and twelve days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and four days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The sixteenth line on two nails above the Lattice Ward bench is seven hundred and ninety-eight days old, one hundred and fourteen weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those rooms is six hundred and fourteen days, eighty-seven weeks and five days, and the ask is six hundred and ninety-eight days, ninety-nine weeks and five days, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three, in another city, is six hundred and forty-one days, ninety-one weeks and four days. None of those three is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating in that case is a closed thing with no days-off figure carried for it on any page of this entry.**

---

**The tarpaulin was off all nine crates and had been off since the Friday, and about four people had walked round them twice in three days and had said nothing, because there is no form on which a person says that a crate is new.**

Five of the nine carry a stencil with three coats of paint under it, the white gone to a grey, the grey gone in at the corners where a lifting hook has caught it since the spring before last.

Four of the nine carry a stencil with no paint under it at all, because the stencil is newer than the wood.

**The man of about thirty-three who drives for a firm had come back to that yard on the Monday for the first time in three weeks and had got as far as untying the tarpaulin on the Friday and had not gone near the crates since, and at about half past six on this Monday he stood in the middle of the nine and said the thing he had been carrying and had not said the last time.**

**"Those four came out of the shop two doors down,"** he said. **"The man in there is about fifty-eight and he has cut stencils for about thirty years and he made those four on a Thursday for a firm four days of bus travel away who sent him a frame."**

**"A frame."**

**"A frame is the thing you lay the card on to cut it,"** the man said, **"and you cannot cut a stencil without a frame, and the stencil is the picture and the frame is the only part that is worth having."**

Nobody in that yard said anything.

**"There are about four frames inside two miles of here and about nine of them in the four towns and one of the nine came from another one of the nine, and there is no mark on a frame that says whose it was, and I asked the man of about fifty-eight which one his came from and he told me and he told me the truth and he did not know it was a copy either."**

**"Why four crates."**

**"Because four is what the firm asked for,"** the man said, **"and because the four of them are the ones nearest the door, and a crate goes nearest the door when it is new, and I have watched that be true in nine yards for about nine years and I have never once watched it be the other way round."**

Nobody thanked him. **He has not been thanked for it, has not asked to be, and about four people in that trade have since repeated the part about the frame and none of them has repeated the part where he said he did not know which one his came from either.**

---

**The man of twenty-two walked down to the second unit at about seven and asked the man of about fifty-eight one question and got an answer in about four seconds and then paid him for four stencils.**

**"You made them."**

**"I made them."**

**"You cut them off a frame a firm four days away sent you."**

**"I cut them off a frame a firm four days away sent me,"** the man said, **"and they wanted four by Thursday, and I did four by Thursday, and I did not ask them what the frame was for and I have cut stencils off other people's frames since I was twenty-two and that is the trade."**

**"Right."**

**"You'll not get a cheaper price for asking me a question instead of asking the firm,"** the man said, and went back to his bench, and did not ask why.

**What Marek did with that was pay him, in the shop's money and out of the shop's margin, for four stencils to the same size, so that the four clean ones are a job he has paid for and not a favour he has been handed. The shop's totals have been out by a pound since the Monday of a week ago, when a man of about sixty-one left a pound note in a small tin at the back of a bench with no docket against it, and this is the second thing that has gone into that week which cannot be reconciled against anything, and neither of the two is about the stencil.**

**What he noticed on that Monday, and did not say to anybody, and told nobody including the man of about fifty-eight, is that nobody had asked the man of about thirty-three a single question in three weeks and every one of the four people who had walked round those crates had stopped at the four clean ones, and had gone round the outside of them, and had not touched them, and had not said why. A man who has never heard of frames and has not got a form for a thing that is new will walk round a new crate the way anybody walks round a man standing in a doorway.**

**What he wanted and did not say, on the Monday night, in four rooms with the heat on and about four people in them who were not asked anything, was to ask the man of about fifty-eight one more question, which is where he learned the frame, and he did not ask it, because the answer to that question is the name and the address of a person who owns another one, and a person who owns two frames is a person you would have to give something to.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*578. Monday of week 212, at ten, and this entry is a yard behind a firm on a wide road with a tarpaulin off nine crates, five of them worn and four of them clean, a man of about thirty-three who can say where the four came from and did not say it three weeks ago and said it in about nine seconds and is not thanked, a frame that came from a frame, a man of about fifty-eight in a unit two doors down who made four stencils to a Thursday order and did not ask what the frame was for, and four stencils paid for out of a shop's margin that has been out by a pound since a man of about sixty-one walked out of it with ninety-three pence in his coat.*

*Conditions and docket.* **The Monday was eleven jobs with a date against every one, eleven people through that counter, the last at about half past five.** The day as lived: bench in Lattice Ward until two, the counter from two until six, a wide road and a yard from about half past six, a unit two doors down from about seven. Standing at that counter in the order it has stood for years: a bench, a cage with a man turned away, a roller shutter, a tool roll with one screwdriver in its fourth pocket, a wall list in a sleeve, a shelf of finished work, a rail with two cards in it. Callers: eleven, four refusals. Work: eleven, four declines, nothing escalated, nothing returned. Charge: nine and then ten, wrong, unmoved, unannounced, and said to nobody. Two reconciliations that could not be made: a pound note in a small tin at the back of a bench with no docket, and four stencils bought out of margin and entered as a cost against a week in which the shop took nine pounds and ten for repairs. The heat over the service road: on, dearer than the winter's, four names between them carrying it, a cook's fortnight laid out and owed to nobody. Service-road rooms: one thousand and eight days, one hundred and forty-four weeks to the day, four rooms and one behind, warm. Rail card: one thousand and twelve days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and four days, four days beyond the room, rail of two. The board on its two nails: twelfth, nine hundred and twenty-eight; thirteenth, eight hundred and seventy-nine; fourteenth, eight hundred and forty-four; fifteenth, eight hundred and twenty-three; sixteenth, seven hundred and ninety-eight; seventeenth, seven hundred and eighty; eighteenth, seven hundred and twenty-six; nineteenth, seven hundred and four. What went onto that board across the Monday: nothing added, nothing removed, no twentieth line. Binder: shut, on its shelf, not opened. The leaf that is hers: the eighth of eight, unread, not put to her, nobody apologised to her. Master's fifth line: blank. Post at the corridor end: five hundred and fifty-six days, seventy-nine weeks and three days. Nine hand copies of a page front: five hundred and seventy-four days, eighty-two weeks to the day, eight of nine unfinished, the first disagreement in the fourth line not found. The ask: six hundred and ninety-eight days, ninety-nine weeks and five days. The hold: six hundred and forty-one days, ninety-one weeks and four days, another city, nothing asked and nothing given. The separation: three hundred and eighty-eight days, fifty-five weeks and three days, not shorter and not ended. **The room above a line in another district did not sit on this Monday, nothing was announced on it, and the count stands at forty-six of which forty-one correspond. The book stands at sixty lines and the tin at seventy-three.**

*Four clean stencils and one frame, as a record of what was known and when.* **What the four are: four of nine crates in a yard, carrying a stencil with no paint under it, because the stencil is newer than the wood. What the five carry: three coats under a stencil gone grey and gone in at the corners where a hook has caught it. Who could say where the four came from and had not said it in three weeks: a man of about thirty-three who drives for a firm, who untyed the tarpaulin on the Friday and did not go near the crates again until the Monday. What he said, in about nine seconds: that the man two doors down is about fifty-eight and cut them on a Thursday for a firm four days of bus travel away that sent him a frame; that a frame is what you lay the card on to cut it; that the stencil is the picture and the frame is the only part worth having; that there are about four frames inside two miles and about nine in the four towns; that one of the nine came from another one of the nine; that there is no mark on a frame that says whose it was; and that the man of about fifty-eight told him the truth about that and did not know it was a copy either. Why four crates and not five: because four is what the firm asked for, and because the four nearest the door are the four nearest the door on a rule that is true in nine yards and has never once been true the other way. Who was thanked: nobody, and he has not asked to be. What was asked of the man of about fifty-eight, in about four seconds: whether he made them, and whether he cut them off a frame a firm four days away sent him. What he said: yes, and yes, and that they wanted four by Thursday and he did four by Thursday and did not ask what the frame was for, and that he has cut stencills off other people's frames since he was twenty-two and that is the trade. What was paid: four stencils to the same size out of a shop's margin, so that the clean four are a job paid for and not a favour handed over, in a week whose totals cannot be reconciled because of a pound note in a tin. Owner: nine crates, one frame with no mark on it, and about four people who have said the part about the frame to other people.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **Four stencils that have never had a coat of paint under them turned out to have been cut on a frame that came from a frame, and a man of about thirty-three said so in a yard in about nine seconds and was not thanked, and a man of about fifty-eight said in about four seconds that he did the work and did not ask, and both of those facts are true and neither of them is anybody being at fault.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter, and the shutter came down at about ten, and there is no Sunday among the ten days these entries cover and a shop is not a thing that exists on a Sunday. He taught nobody, demonstrated nothing, assessed nobody and assisted nobody, and the four stencils are not teaching and the man of about fifty-eight did not learn a thing from the man of twenty-two except the price of the same cut twice. The count of correct things that changed nothing, done by people who are owed nothing, stands at two and does not move here, and the man of about fifty-eight is not a third instance of it, because four stencills to a Thursday order is thirty years of work and he is owed a wage for it by people who will pay him. The card in the rail is one thousand and twelve days old, four days past the room, in a rail that holds two, unmoved and unturned, and a card with two boxes and a clip in a box from a firm with a yellow strip on it is a different object from it and the man of about thirty-three has not got one on him and did not say he had. The rooms are one thousand and eight days old and warm. The board stands at nineteen lines. The man of about fifty-one was at that north wall on the Monday evening and said nothing and is owed a written thing that nobody in this city has anywhere to write. **Those four words are not in this entry.**
