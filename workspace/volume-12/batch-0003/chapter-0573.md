# Chapter 573 — A Thursday Counter And A Man Of About Thirty-Three With Nothing To Be Repaired, And A Refusal Of About Nine Seconds Given At A Trade Counter On A Wide Road By A Woman Of About Forty-Four Who Is Right, And Nine People On Wages On The Wrong Side Of It

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and four rooms off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was about four o'clock, a man with nothing to be repaired saying nine seconds out loud about a woman who had said no to a single sheet of paper.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Thursday, dated to the day, eleven people through the counter door, the last of them at about half past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, the rooms off the service road from about half past six. **That flat is on the heating and it is dearer than it was through the winter, four names on the tenancy are carrying it, and a fortnight that a cook laid out of her own wages is owed to nobody and is going to be owed to nobody at the end of the month.** The rooms are one thousand and four days, one hundred and forty-three weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and eight days, one hundred and forty-four weeks to the day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The twentieth line on two nails above that bench has never been begun, and the line above it is seven hundred days, one hundred weeks to the day. The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those rooms is six hundred and ten days, eighty-seven weeks and one day, and the ask is six hundred and ninety-four days, ninety-nine weeks and one day, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and thirty-seven days, ninety-one weeks to the day. Not one of those three is a day anything happened to the heating in this city.**

---

**He came in at about ten past four with nothing in his hands, which nobody in that shop can remember him doing, and he stood at the end of the counter for about four seconds before he said anything at all, and then he said nine seconds and stopped.**

**"There's a woman at the trade counter with the yellow strip on it, and she's answered the counter there for about nine years, and about two o'clock this afternoon a man from the head office asked her to type four sentences onto one sheet and send it out to all four places, and she said no in about nine seconds and she was right."**

**"Right about what."**

**"About the sheet."** He turned the pad over on the counter without opening it. **"The four sentences are the four sentences. He wanted them on one sheet so that anybody in any of the four places could be handed one without having to go and find the other one. She said that a sentence a person wrote is not a standard and it stops being a sentence the second it is on a sheet, because then it is a thing a person can be given, and a person can be given a thing and read it out, and in about a month there will be a man in a town four days of bus travel away who has said a sentence he did not write, and somebody in an office four days the other way will believe it, and neither of those two people will ever find out whose it was."**

**"Nine seconds."**

**"Nine seconds and about four words of him afterwards, which were that it would save about four days."**

**"And?"**

**"And she said that a person who is waiting does not need to be saved four days, he needs to find the person who wrote it, and those are two different jobs and only one of them is hers."**

The man of about thirty-three put his hands in his pockets. He had driven in from the bus depot that afternoon and his coat was the one he had been driving in for a fortnight.

**"Nobody in that office agreed with her and the man from the head office has gone back to his desk and will not raise it again and about four people in that room said afterwards that she was difficult."**

---

**What the man of twenty-two did next is in this entry because it is the part of the Thursday that cost him something and it took about nine seconds of its own.**

**"What are the four places called."**

The man of about thirty-three looked at him.

**"You want the four places."**

**"I want the four names."**

**"Why."**

**"Because there are nine people on wages in one of them,"** Marek said, **"and they are the ones your refusal is about, and they have not been asked, and they will not be told, and I am going to sit here tonight and not do anything about it, and I would rather have the four names while I am sitting here not doing anything about it."**

The man of about thirty-three gave him four names in about eleven seconds and did not ask for them back and has not asked for them back.

**Marek wrote the four on the back of a docket in pencil, one under the other, with no addresses under any of them, and put the docket in the front of the counter with about nine others, which is a thing a man who keeps a written record does with a piece of paper and is not supposed to do with a piece of paper he made himself.**

**"Are you going to do anything about it,"** the man of about thirty-three said.

**"No."**

**"Right,"** the man said, and **"that's the first person in about four years in this city who has told me no to that question, and everybody else in about nine places has told me they were going to,"** and he went out, and he has not come back into that shop since, and the four names are on a docket in the front of a counter and not on anything else.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The refusal, as a record of about nine seconds and about nine people.* **Where it happened: a trade counter on a wide road, the one with a yellow strip along the edge of it, at about two on a Thursday afternoon. Who refused: a woman of about forty-four who has answered that counter for about nine years. She is not on anything, she is not a doctor, she does not run a clinic, she has never run a clinic, and she is not the woman of about thirty-one who keeps one in the first of the four towns and refused four correct things in a week in the last fortnight of the previous movement, and the two women have never met and are not to be brought together. What was asked of her: that she type the four identical sentences onto one sheet and send that sheet out to all four places, so that anybody in any of the four could be handed one without going back to find the person who wrote it. What she said, in about nine seconds: that a sentence a person wrote is not a standard, and that it stops being a sentence the moment it is on a sheet, because then it is a thing a person can be given, and a person can be given a thing and read it out, and in about a month a man in a town four days of bus travel away will have said a sentence he did not write, and somebody in an office four days the other way will believe it, and neither of those two people will ever find out whose it was. What the man from the head office said afterwards: that it would save about four days. What she said to that: that a person who is waiting does not need to be saved four days, he needs to find the person who wrote it, and those are two jobs and only one of them is hers. Who was right: she was, and the four days do not get saved, and the four sentences stay in four towns. Who was on the wrong side of her being right: about nine people on wages at a firm in the second of the four towns, whose week is made of those four days, none of whom was asked and none of whom has been told. What happened in that office afterwards: the man from the head office went back to his desk and did not raise it again, and about four people in the room said she was difficult. Who was thanked: nobody, and the four people who have said since that she was difficult have not said the other thing. Owner: a pad, a case, and a refusal that costs about nine wages and one sheet of paper.**

*What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A woman at a trade counter said no to a single sheet of paper in about nine seconds and was right, and about nine people on wages in a town four days of bus travel away are on the wrong side of her being right and were not asked, and a man of about thirty-three carried the fact to a repair counter with nothing in his hands because he had nowhere else to put it and there was nowhere else.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter and the shutter came down at about ten, and there is no Sunday among the ten days these ten entries cover and there is no such thing as a shop on a Sunday. He taught nobody, demonstrated nothing, assessed nobody and assisted nobody on the Thursday, and nobody asked him to, and the thing he was given to do — four names, written in pencil, on the back of a docket — is not any of the four and is a thing he made for himself with no authority behind it. The count of correct things that changed nothing, done by people who are owed nothing, stands at two and does not move here, and **the woman of about forty-four is not a third instance of it, because the sheet would have been typed on her own machine and by her own hand and she was asked for it in the ordinary course of a job she is paid for, and a person who refuses an instruction inside a job she is paid for is doing her job.** The card in the rail is one thousand and eight days old, four days past the room, unmoved and unturned, and a card with two boxes and a clip in a box from that yellow-edged counter is a different object from it and appears in this entry only inside a sentence about a firm four days away that is not this firm. The rooms are one thousand and four days old and warm. The board stands at nineteen lines.
