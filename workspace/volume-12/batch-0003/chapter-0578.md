# Chapter 578 — A Trade Counter On A Wide Road At About Seven In The Evening, A Man Of About Twenty-Four With A Sheet Of Paper In His Hand Reading Nine Words Out Loud To A Woman Who Keeps A Parts Counter, Ten Words Back From Her, And Then Nine Seconds From A Man Of About Thirty-Three That Finish Nothing

**The Friday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a trade counter on a wide road from about seven, and its shape was a man of about twenty-four reading nine words out loud to a woman who sells fittings, and her saying ten back, and about four people hearing both.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Friday, a date against every one of the eleven, eleven people through that counter, the last at about half past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, a wide road from about seven, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six, which he got to at about ten past seven. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on that tenancy are carrying the increase, and a fortnight that a cook put out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and twelve days, one hundred and forty-four weeks and four days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and sixteen days, one hundred and forty-five weeks and one day, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The eighteenth line on two nails above the Lattice Ward bench is seven hundred and thirty days, one hundred and four weeks and two days. The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those rooms is six hundred and eighteen days, eighty-eight weeks and two days, and the ask is seven hundred and two days, one hundred weeks and two days, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three in another city is six hundred and forty-five days, ninety-two weeks and one day. Not one of those three figures is a day anything happened to the heating in this city.**

---

**He came at about five past seven with a sheet of paper in his hand and there are about four people on that counter at that hour and two of them work there and one of them is a man of about thirty-three who has a van out of a yard two doors down and has about four days of waiting left on nine crates.**

He read it out loud. He did not introduce himself and nobody asked him to and the woman who keeps that counter, and who is about fifty-eight and has kept it for about nine years, did not stop what she was doing for the first sentence.

**"About four hundred and fifty people in this city can say it and one of them said it in a hall in the spring and none of us owns it."**

**"Say what?"**

**"That anybody may refuse anything."**

**"That's on the wall in the depot,"** the woman said, **"and it has been on that wall since I was thirty."**

**"Then a man of about twenty-four read it out in a hall to about four hundred and fifty people in the spring and got a decision out of them, and it is on a wall in a depot, and neither of those two things is wrong, and about four hundred and fifty people in this city have it now and not one of them can put an objection to it anywhere at all."**

**"Right,"** the woman said, and put a box of washers down, and **"what's that got to do with fittings."**

The man of about twenty-four held the sheet up so that the two of them could both see the paragraph at the bottom of it, and he read the paragraph out loud too, and it took about four seconds, and it is not printed in this entry.

**Then the woman of about fifty-eight who keeps that parts counter said the thing that made the evening, and she said it without any weight on it at all, in about three seconds, to a man she had not met before.**

**"A counterfeit is a habit nobody owns, so any of us can."**

**Ten words. She had not read the sheet. She was looking at the box of washers when she said it and she said it the way a person says a thing that has been true in front of her for about thirty years and does not know it has a name for, and about four people heard it and only one of the four wrote it down, and the one who wrote it down was a man of about twenty-two standing about nine feet away with a bag of tools who wrote it on the back of his own hand in pencil and has not been able to make the pencil mark any clearer since.**

---

**The man of about twenty-four took about four seconds and then said the other half of it, out loud, in a face, to about four people, and it is the reason the whole of the last ten days turns where they turn.**

**"And that is why the first one is a route and not a nuisance. A nuisance you can answer. A habit you cannot answer, because nobody is in charge of it, and a person who copies a habit four days away is not doing anything wrong and is copying me, and the difference between a habit and a copy of a habit is that one of them is mine."**

Nobody in that shop agreed with him.

**About four people were there and one of them is a man of about thirty-three who has a van and nine crates and about four days of waiting left on them, and he said the second half of that exchange in about nine seconds, and it took nine seconds and it was correct and nothing was resolved by it.**

**"Is it still yours,"** the man of about thirty-three said.

**"Yes."**

**"It isn't,"** the man said. **"It's on a wall in a depot and it's been on a wall in a depot longer than you've been in this city and about nine of us have been saying it in four different towns for about four years without ever hearing each other, and if it is yours then you have got it in about four weeks and we've had it in four years, and that is not a thing anybody can be proud of, and I do not mind. That's all. I do not mind."**

**"That's not—"**

**"I'm not saying it to be difficult,"** the man said. **"I'm saying it because you're going to go and use it, and you should know what you've got, and what you've got is ours, and you have not asked us."**

**Nobody thanked anybody on that wide road on that Friday evening, and the man of about twenty-four did not say thank you to the woman of about fifty-eight, and she did not ask him to, and he has not been to that counter since and she has not mentioned it to anybody who has been in that shop.**

---

**What he noticed on that Friday evening, and did not say, and has not put in the notebook, is that the man of about twenty-four said the nine words and did not look at him once in about eleven seconds, and that a man saying a true thing out loud to about four people in a trade counter at about seven in the evening looked at the four people and not at the one standing nine feet away with a bag, and that the one standing nine feet away was the only person there who has written any of it down, and that is what being the person a room goes to looks like from the outside, and he has never seen it from the outside before.**

**What he wanted and did not say, on the walk back, is to be a person owed nothing who says a true thing in nine seconds and is agreed with by nobody. He has wanted that on and off for about four years. On the Friday evening he worked out that the woman of about fifty-eight had it by accident, at about thirty years old, in a shop, and had never once spent it, and had a wages bill and a stock list and a box of washers, and that is the whole of the difference and it is not a difference of courage.**

**And the thing he did not do, and the reason, is this: at about twenty past seven, with the man of about thirty-three standing about four feet away and the yard two doors down, he had the fifth sentence on the back of his own hand in pencil, and he was within about nine seconds of turning his hand over and saying it out loud, and he did not, because the man of about thirty-three has nine crates and about four days of waiting left on them and about four hundred and fifty people in this city are about to hear what a man of about twenty-four said in about four seconds, and one more sentence off a stranger's hand is not a thing you put in front of anybody on a Friday night.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
