# Chapter 572 — A First Floor Above A Line Of Shop Units In A Second District On A Wednesday Evening, And A Man Of About Fifty-Eight Asking One Question In Eleven Words And Being Given An Honest One That Was The Wrong One, And A Pad Of Two Hundred Forms Shut In A Case And Carried Down A Stair

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a first floor above four shop units in a second district from about half past six until about nine, and its shape was one question put by a stranger and one true answer given to it by a paid woman who knew before she had finished that it was not the answer he had come for.**

---

Eleven jobs on the Wednesday, a date against every one, eleven people through that counter, and the last of them at about five. Bench until two, the counter two until six, and the stair from about half past six. **The heat in that flat is on and dearer than the winter's, four names on one tenancy are carrying the difference, and a fortnight a cook put out of her own wages is owed by nobody and will be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** Those rooms are one thousand and three days old, one hundred and forty-three weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and seven days, one hundred and forty-three weeks and six days, four days past the room, and the rail takes two.

**The thirteenth line on two nails above that bench is eight hundred and seventy-four days, one hundred and twenty-four weeks and six days. The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those rooms is six hundred and nine days, eighty-seven weeks to the day, and the ask is six hundred and ninety-three days, ninety-nine weeks to the day, and the hold of the man of about thirty-three, in another city, is six hundred and thirty-six days, ninety weeks and six days. None of those three is a day anything happened to the heating in this city, and the heating in this case is a closed thing with no figure of days off for it in it anywhere.**

---

**It is not the room above a line in another district. That room runs on a Wednesday every fourth Wednesday and has run for about nineteen years and it is in another district from this one, and the two have never met and are not going to be introduced, and nothing was announced in the room on this Wednesday because a count is announced at a sitting and this was not a sitting.**

There were about eleven people on that first floor. A woman of about thirty-four had come up on the Monday with three pads of forms in a case and had carried the case up a flight and had not asked anybody to help her. A man of about fifty-eight had come up on the Sunday night on a bus of about eleven hours and was sitting with his coat still on. Two of a firm of about four were at a table with their own pads open. Nobody was chairing anything and there was no list and there was nothing on the wall.

The pads had been on that table since the Monday and the boxes on the backs of the forms had been filled in by nine different people in three weeks and the pages had been turned.

**"Can I send the sentence I use to the other places?"**

He said it to the woman of about thirty-four because she was the one with the case and the pads and because he had worked out in about four days that she was the one who would know.

Eleven words. He said them the way a man says a sentence he has been carrying up a stair for about an hour.

**"Yes,"** the woman said. **"That is what the box on the back of it is for."**

**"Right."**

**"And I will tell you the rest of it since you have asked me and I would rather you had it from me than find it in about a month,"** she said, **"which is that I have typed four of them this year already and I did not know that they matched."**

The man of about fifty-eight did not say anything for about nine seconds.

**"You didn't know."**

**"No. I type what comes into the tray. There are four of you in four towns and I do the same pad for all four and I have never had the sheets side by side, and I looked at them once in the spring because a mark on one of them was under the wrong line and I had to find out whether the mark was mine or the machine's, and that is the only time I ever saw them together, and I put them back in about four seconds."**

**"They were the same then."**

**"They were the same then."**

**"That's not what I asked."**

**"No,"** the woman said. **"It is what I was asked. I am the one who types. Nobody in this room asked me whether it should be sent and I have not got a vote and I would have said the same to a firm or to a man with a van."**

---

**Nobody in that room said the other half. It stood there and about nine people in it knew where it was and not one of them said it, and the half is this: a man wanted to know whether a sentence he wrote would still be his if it arrived in a town he had never driven into, and there was nobody on that first floor who could answer it, and the woman who could have answered it was four days of bus travel away and does not answer forms.**

**What he noticed, and did not say, and told nobody, and has not written down anywhere that is on a shelf, was that the box is the only part of the form without a date on it. Every other box on that sheet has a date under it and a name above it and the box for the local practice has neither, and that is the whole reason four identical sentences sat in four towns for four months and nobody knew, and it is not anybody's decision, and it is a box with no date on it.**

**What he wanted and did not say is to have been the person in that room who said the second half out loud before anybody else did, and the reason he did not say it is that the second half takes one sentence and one person and the person who says it in a room of eleven becomes the person that room goes to, and he has spent about four years being the person that rooms go to and has not been thanked for it once and is not going to be thanked for it in a first floor above four shop units on a Wednesday.**

He stood at the back for about two hours and said nothing at all.

**At about nine the woman of about thirty-four put the five hundred and forty sheets back into the case in order and shut the case and put it under the table where it had been on the Monday, and she carried it down the flight herself and did not ask either of the two men from the firm to take the bottom of it. The pad did not go home with her and it did not go to a post office and it will be on a bench in a firm four days of bus travel away on Monday morning with the fifth sentence in the top sheet and the fifth sentence in the carbon underneath it, and the sixth sentence will be typed on the Tuesday, and nothing in this city is going to arrive in any of the four places before the sixth or the seventh.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The question and the answer, as a record of eleven words and about nine seconds.* **Where: a first floor above four shop units in a second district, not the room that runs every fourth Wednesday and has run for nineteen years, and the two have never met. Who was there: about eleven people, three pads of forms in a case carried up a flight by a woman of about thirty-four who types for a firm four days of bus travel away, a man of about fifty-eight who drives a bus in the second of the four towns and came up on the Sunday night on about eleven hours of bus, two people of a firm of about four, and a man of twenty-two at the back on no list, not asked, and there was nothing on the wall and nobody chairing. *What the day did, and what it did not touch.* **A man of about fifty-eight asked a question in eleven words on a Wednesday evening and a woman paid a wage by a firm four days away gave him the true answer to the question she had been asked, which was not the question he had come four days with, and both of them knew it inside about nine seconds and neither of them said the other thing, and the pad went back in its case and will be typed again on the Tuesday.** Nobody thanked anybody at that counter either, and the shutter came down at about ten, and there is no Sunday among these ten entries and there is no such thing as a shop on a Sunday. He taught nobody, demonstrated nothing, assessed nobody and assisted nobody, and nobody put any of the four to him, and the request he turned down this Wednesday was not a request but a man of about thirty-eight standing at his counter at about ten past four with a card that has two boxes on it and a metal clip in a box, from the firm with a yellow strip on the edge of its counter, who wanted to know why it takes nine days, and was told nine days, in about four seconds, and did not ask a second question. **A card with two boxes and a clip in a box is a different object from the card in the rail along the front of that counter and the two are not joined in this entry, and the man of about thirty-eight is owed nothing and is owed nine days.** The count of correct things that changed nothing, done by people who are owed nothing, stands at two, being a clipboard on a nail and four torn pieces of a list in a coat pocket, and it does not move here, **and the woman of about thirty-four who put the pad in the case is not a third instance of it, because she was asked to do the thing by the firm that pays her and she is owed a wage by the people who sent the pad, and that is the whole of what the count of two is for.** The rooms off that service road are one thousand and three days old and warm. The board stands at nineteen lines. The man of about fifty-one was at that north wall on the Wednesday evening with his bag at his feet and said nothing. **The four words that name the body which runs that room are nowhere in this entry.**
