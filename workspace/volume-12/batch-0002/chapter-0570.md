# Chapter 570 — A Second Floor In Crown Terrace With Nine Chairs In It And About Nine People In It, And A Question About Whether A Thing Should Be Connected To A Thing, And A Woman Of About Forty-Four Who Is Owed Nothing Deciding In About Four Seconds Not To Open The Door, And A Reason About A Person In A Bed, And Nobody Agreeing With Her And Nobody Thanking Her

**The Thursday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a second floor in Crown Terrace from about seven until about half past eight, and its shape was a question, and about four seconds, and a door that stayed shut, and about nine people who did not agree.**

---

The Thursday was eleven on the docket and the dates were in against all eleven and eleven came through that counter and the last of them at about half past five. Bench until two, the counter two until six, four rooms off that service road from about half past six with the heat on, and a second floor in Crown Terrace from about seven. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer than it was through the winter, four names on that tenancy are carrying the rise between them, and a fortnight that a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and ninety-seven days, one hundred and forty-two weeks and three days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is one thousand and one days old, one hundred and forty-three weeks to the day, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

**The fifteenth line on two nails above that bench is eight hundred and twelve days old, one hundred and sixteen weeks to the day, and the man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those service-road rooms is six hundred and three days, eighty-six weeks and one day, and the ask is six hundred and eighty-seven days, ninety-eight weeks and one day. Every one of the three above is an age, and the heating in that flat is a separate matter with no days-off figure carried for it on any page of this book.**

**It is a second floor in Crown Terrace with nine chairs in it and about nine people in the chairs and a table with nothing on it, and the four words that name the body that runs that room were not said in it on this Thursday, and are not said in this entry, and have not been said in this city in the ten weeks this book has been running, and the reason they stand in this sentence is that the room sat for about an hour and a half and nobody in it said them and about four people in it noticed that nobody had.**

The nine chairs were nine chairs and not eight, and there was no ninth chair against a wall with its back to the room, because that is a different room in another district and its chair did not move on Wednesday and is not this one, and the two rooms are not joined anywhere in these ten entries.

**The question on the table was this: whether a thing should be connected to a thing.**

Not a machine to a machine. A list, made in this city, of about eleven buildings in four towns that could be asked a question and be answered in a day, should be connected to a room in this city that holds about nine chairs, so that somebody in one of the four places could put a thing in front of nine people in this city on a Wednesday and have it written down.

**About four of the nine people in that room thought it should. About four thought it should not. One had not come to have an opinion and said so in about four seconds and was not asked again.**

The man of about thirty-three who drives for a firm was in the fourth chair and had come up on the Sunday night and had about four days of bus travel behind him and had a piece of paper in his coat that he did not put on the table and did not offer, and about four people in that room knew he had it and nobody asked.

**And the woman of about forty-four was in the seventh chair and she is owed nothing.**

She is not on a list. She does not keep the minutes and does not take them. She came to that floor on the Wednesday of a week ago, in a car, with a woman, and has been in that room twice, and she is there on this Thursday because about four people in it asked her to come and she came, and she has no post and no vote and is not on anything and has not asked to be and has not been asked to be and would say no if she were asked.

**She decided in about four seconds not to open the door, and the door is a real door and it is at the end of that room, and it has a handle and it is not locked, and it has not been opened in about nineteen years by anybody, and the question in front of that room was whether it should be opened this week.**

---

**"It's a list and a room,"** the man in the second chair said. **"That's all it is. Nine lines, a Wednesday, and a person in Crown Terrace writing down what a person in a town says, and the town gets an answer in a day instead of nine, and the whole thing costs about a sheet of paper and about four minutes of somebody's Wednesday."**

**"It costs a door,"** the woman of about forty-four said. **"And it costs the first person who is put in a chair in it, because everybody in the four places would find out in about a month that the one thing they had said no to for four years had been written down, and after that there is no room in any of the four of them where anything is said that is not written down, and the room does not know that it has done that. The room will think it is a service."**

**"It's not a room,"** the man said. **"It's a Wednesday."**

**"It's a room,"** she said, **"and a Wednesday, and about a hundred and ninety Wednesdays a year, and you can have the Wednesday or you can have the room and not both, and that is the whole of the trade and nobody in this room has been told it before tonight, including the woman who runs it."**

**"You haven't been in it."**

**"No."**

**"Then you're deciding about a room you haven't been in."**

**"Yes,"** she said. **"And I'm the only person in this room who has been in the other one, and I have been in the other one about four times, and I have not been in this one, and I have still got to say no, and the reason takes about four seconds and I am going to say it now and then I am going to leave and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be asked."**

---

**"In April of this year there was a woman in a bed in a room in the first of the four towns for about nine days, and her name is on a piece of paper in the book of four columns about nine feet from where you are sitting, and the reason that room is shut is her."**

Nobody spoke for about nine seconds.

**"Her sister asked me in the spring whether it was right, and I said yes, and it was right, and she is alive because of it, and nobody has ever written down a line about her anywhere except a column with no heading in a cold store four days of bus travel from here, and that column was written by a man who was not asked to write it and does not know she is in it."**

**"And a list of eleven is not that."**

**"A list of eleven is not that on the Wednesday it is made,"** the woman said, **"and it is that in about a month, and the month is the whole of it, and you cannot have the Wednesday without the month, and I will not be the person who says yes to the Wednesday on the grounds that the month is somebody else's department."**

She stood up. She did not say anything else. She said it in about four seconds and she gave the reason and the reason was about a person in a bed and about a piece of paper in the book of four columns in another district of this city, and she left the room in about nine seconds, and the door at the end of that room stayed shut.

**About nine people were in that room and not one of them agreed with her. About four of them said so out loud in about four seconds each and one of them said that she had used a person to win an argument, and the woman of about forty-four was already on the stairs and did not hear that and would not have answered it and has not been asked about it and has not offered to answer it.**

**Nobody thanked her. Nobody in that room has thanked her and about four of them have said since that she was right, which is a different thing and is not the thing that was owed, and she has said, in a car, on the way down, to the woman who brought her, that being right afterwards is the cheapest thing available to a person who said no in a room of nine.**

---

**And Marek was not in that room. He was on a landing in Crown Terrace at about seven and about nine minutes and he was not on a list and was not asked to come and came anyway, and he heard about half of it through a door that was shut, and what he heard was a woman's voice and about four words of a man's and then about nine seconds of nothing and then a chair.**

**What he noticed, and did not say, and told nobody, was that the door at the end of that room was not locked and had not been opened in about nineteen years, and that in about eleven years of his working life he has never once been in a room where the answer to a question was decided by a person who had not read anything.**

**What he wanted and did not say, and has not written down, and did not put in the drawer with the other three sheets, was to be in the seventh chair. He wanted it for about nine seconds while a woman's voice was coming through a shut door and then he did not want it, and the not-wanting is the whole of what the ten days of these entries have done to him, and he is not going to say that to anybody, and nobody in that building is going to tell him that he said it, and the landing was empty.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
