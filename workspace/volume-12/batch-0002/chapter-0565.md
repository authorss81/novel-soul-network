# Chapter 565 — The Same Weighbridge Hut In The Third Of The Four Towns Seen A Second Time From A Different Angle, And A Hardback Book Under A Window That Nobody Has Ever Read Including The Man Who Keeps It, And A Woman Of About Forty-Three With A Hot Tap In Her Own Yard Who Is The Only Person Anybody Calls

**The Monday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, and a room off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was about nine vehicles at a weighbridge in a third town four days of bus travel away, put here by somebody who was in one of them and who has been in the same queue once before and who is not going back.**

---

The Monday held eleven and all eleven were dated and eleven people came through that counter and the last of them at about half past five. The bench until two, the counter two until six, and the four rooms off that service road from about half past six with the heat on. **The heating in that flat is on and dearer than it was through the winter, four names on the tenancy are carrying the increase, and a fortnight a cook laid out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and eighty-seven days, one hundred and forty-one weeks to the day, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and ninety-one days, one hundred and forty-one weeks and four days, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

**The fourteenth line on two nails above that bench is eight hundred and twenty-three days old, one hundred and seventeen weeks and four days, and the sixteenth is seven hundred and seventy-seven, one hundred and eleven weeks to the day, and the nineteenth is six hundred and eighty-three days, ninety-seven weeks and four days, and the twentieth line has never been started.** The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of those rooms is five hundred and ninety-three days old, eighty-four weeks and five days, and the ask is six hundred and seventy-seven days, ninety-six weeks and five days, and neither of those is a day anything happened to the heating in this city.

---

**It is put here from the second vehicle in that queue, and the person putting it here is a woman of about thirty-four with a hired van's key and a clipboard, and she was in that queue on the Monday of a fortnight ago and wrote about nine words about it afterwards and has not been in that town since.**

**There is a stoppage. It began on the Friday and it is a stoppage, not a fault, and a stoppage in a trade is four firms in a row deciding that they will not move anything for about four days while they find out whether they are allowed to, and about nine vehicles are in a queue at a weighbridge in the third of the four towns because the road in has one weighbridge on it and the vehicles cannot go past it without a ticket.**

**The man who reads that weighbridge is about fifty-two and has read it for about nineteen years and he was in the hut all day on both Mondays and he came out of it about nine times each day, and he said one true thing out loud on the Monday of a fortnight ago in about eleven seconds and nobody in that queue has mentioned it since, and it has been printed in four places in this city and not one of the four places is that queue.**

**The book is under the window on a shelf and it is hardback and it is about two inches thick and it has about nine entries a day in it in pencil and it has had them in it for about nineteen years.**

**Nobody has ever read it. Not the man of about fifty-two, who keeps it and who has never turned back more than about four pages in it at a time. Not the four drivers. Not the woman of about thirty-four. Not the man of about thirty-three who stood behind her on the first Monday and who has not come back. Not a firm, not an office, not a person in this city, and not Marek, who was told about it in a shop on the Monday of a fortnight ago and who has not asked about it, because a book that nobody has read for nineteen years is not a thing that gets asked about, and a person who asks about it is a person who wants it opened, and it is not his, and the man who keeps it has never offered it.**

**It is on a shelf under a window in a hut about nine feet by eleven in a town four days of bus travel from this city and it is not opened in this entry and it is not going to be opened. That sentence is here so that nobody mistakes a book being named for a book being available.**

---

**And the man of about fifty-two added an entry on the Monday afternoon of this week, and the entry is one word, and the word is not printed in this entry.**

He wrote it at about four. The queue was at about nine vehicles and had not moved since about ten. He wrote it in the ninth column, which is the column with no heading on the printed sheet he uses, which is the only column on that sheet with no heading, and it is the only column in that hut that has no heading, and it has had no heading since about the spring of a year in which the man was about forty-nine.

**The man of about fifty-two has written a word in that column on about four days in a week for about nineteen years and the word is the same word on about four days in a week, and it is not a complaint, and it is not a name, and it is not a figure, and it is not about any of the nine vehicles in the queue or about the man of about forty-four whose office has the card, and it is what a person who has been weighing the same thing for nineteen years writes in a column headed nothing on an afternoon when nine things have not moved for six hours and there is nothing else in the column to write.**

**He was asked about it once, about four years ago, by a young man with a clipboard who was not in that queue and who asked every person in that hut the same question, and the man of about fifty-two said, in about four seconds, that it was the time, and the young man wrote the time down, and the time was not what was in that column, and the young man has not been back and does not know that.**

---

**And here is the thing this entry is for, and it is not the book, and the book is only in it because the book is in that hut and everything else in that hut is the queue.**

**A woman of about forty-three with a hot tap in her own yard is the only person anybody called.**

There is no word for what was done on that Monday in that town and the only available description is that about nine drivers, of whom about four had a part-load that could have gone somewhere else and could have gone on a different invoice, and about nine things that could each have been put off, and a woman with a kitchen, a hot room, a cold room, a yard and a tap in it that has run hot for about seventeen years, and that woman was the only one of the available people that any of about four drivers in that queue went to.

**They did not go to the firm, because the firm has an office in a town four days away and the office does not weigh anything and has never been asked to. They did not go to the man of about fifty-two, because he weighs tickets and has said in a face that he does not weigh lorries and never has. They did not go to each other, because the four of them were in the same queue and being in the same queue is not a reason to go to a person.**

**They went to a woman of about forty-three with a hot tap in her own yard, in the second of the four towns, on foot, about four of them, and about eleven hours away, and they arrived at about eleven at night, and she made them tea, and none of the four of them had met her before, and she listened to the whole of it in about nine seconds and then said one thing and then gave them the address of a man in a second town who is not on any list and who has never been asked to be on any list.**

**Her name is DELPHINE ARDEN, and she has run a kitchen in that town for about nine years, and about four people are on her wages, and the hot room is a hot room and the cold room is a cold room, and the tap at the back of the yard has run hot since she was about twenty-six. She is named here once and she is not named on any title of this book and nobody in that town has ever said her name in a room with a table in it, and about four people have said it in a doorway at about eleven at night this month and she heard one of them and not the other.**

**What she said was, in a face, to about four men who had driven four days to stand in a queue: I have a tap and a cold room and four people on my wages and I am going to have to tell the four of them on the Friday that there is a stoppage, and you have told me where to send the four of you, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to remember it.**

**Nobody thanked her. Two of the four said thank you on the way out of her kitchen, in about nine seconds, in a doorway, and she has said since that she heard one of them and not the other, and she has said that she is not owed a thank you and that a person who thanks a woman in a doorway at eleven at night is a person who thinks the door is the difficult part.**

**And the man she gave them the address of is about thirty-eight, and he took about nine of them on in the second week of the stoppage, and he was not asked to, and he was not thanked, and he has never been to this city, and he does not know that anybody in this city knows his name.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
