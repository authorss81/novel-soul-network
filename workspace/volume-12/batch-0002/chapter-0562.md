# Chapter 562 — The Forty-First Sitting Of A Room Above A Line In Another District, And Nine Items At Four Minutes, And A Woman Of About Thirty-Six Who Came Four Days On A Bus And Said A True Thing To A Woman's Face And Was Answered In Two Words That Are Not A Line In Any Book, And The Count Is Forty-Six Announced Of Which Forty-One Correspond

**The Wednesday was a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, a counter until about six, a second floor above a line in another district from about ten until about eleven, and a room off a service road from about half past six, and its shape was nine items at four minutes each and a tenth thing at the end of the hour that was not an item and did not go in.**

---

Eleven jobs for the Wednesday, all eleven of them dated, and eleven people came through that counter and the last of them at about ten past five. Bench until two, the counter two until six, the room above the line from ten to eleven, the four rooms off that service road from about half past six. **The heating in that flat is on and it is more than it was through the winter, four names on that tenancy are between them paying the difference, and a fortnight a cook put out of her own wages is owed by nobody and is going to be owed by nobody at the end of this month.** The service-road rooms are nine hundred and eighty-two days, one hundred and forty weeks and two days, four of them and one behind, and they are warm.

The card in the rail along the front of that counter is nine hundred and eighty-six days, one hundred and forty weeks and six days, four days more than the room, and the rail takes two.

**The ask is six hundred and seventy-two days old on this Wednesday, ninety-six weeks to the day, and it is a question put to a person in this city and not to an institution, and it has not been put to anybody else since the day it was put.** The hold of the man of about thirty-three is six hundred and fifteen days, eighty-seven weeks and six days. The man of about fifty-one at the north wall of the service-road rooms is five hundred and eighty-eight days old, which is eighty-four weeks to the day, and he has a bag on the floor at his feet and has said nothing this month.

**And two figures on this afternoon are each other without knowing it, and this entry does not join them. The service-road rooms are nine hundred and eighty-two days old and three hundred and sixty-two is also the day the separation happened, and the separation stands at three hundred and sixty-two days and nine hundred and eighty-two is also the day that room was first used. One of those two is an age and one of the two is a day something happened, and both of them are on the same afternoon of this Wednesday, and a room is a building and a separation is a Thursday, and the sentence that puts a verb between them is the sentence that is not written here.**

---

**About eleven people came up that stair. Six of them had been in the same chair on the last Wednesday and five had not, and one of the five had not been in that room in about nineteen years and came for a reason she gave and it was a reason about a person of about thirty-one who could not get there.**

Nine chairs curved in with the two ends turned in, and the ninth of them is the one hard against the wall with its back to the whole room, and it did not move in the hour, and nobody looked at it more than once, and whoever moves it is not named in this entry and is not going to be named in any of the ten.

A table. A tin with no lid and no lock and never a lock. A book of four columns face up inside the reach of the woman's right hand from about ten until about eleven.

**Nine items at four minutes each, and all nine of them went to the chairs the way nine of them have gone every fourth Wednesday for nineteen years.**

The nine were: a man of about thirty-one who had a question about a form and was told in about nine seconds what the question was actually about; a woman of about forty-four who wanted to bring a thing that had happened to a person she works beside and was told, without unkindness, that it was not hers to carry in there; a man of about nineteen who came for the third time and had not spoken on the first two and spoke on the third, in about eleven words, and about four people in that room turned their heads; a woman of about twenty-seven who wanted to know whether anybody had to believe her and was told in about nine seconds that nobody in that room had to believe anybody, which is the only thing anybody in that room has got right for nineteen years; a man of about fifty-eight who said one sentence and would not say a second; a man of about thirty-six who was the ninth Wednesday in a row and who had come to be near the window; a person of about sixty-one who has been in that room in nineteen years and who said the one thing in the hour that anybody said twice, and both times it was the number seven; and a man of about thirty-three who said nothing at all for four minutes and then said the whole of it in eleven seconds and would not say it again.

**The tenth thing was not an item. It was a woman of about thirty-six who had come up on the Sunday night with about four days of bus travel behind her, and who had a card in her hand with a clip on it, and who had been in a building with nine people in it in the first of the four towns nine days earlier and had said a true thing to about nine faces, and who had come here because somebody in her own town had told her that this was the room where you said a thing to a woman's face and it went into her book.**

---

**"I don't want it in her book."**

**"Then why are you up here."**

**"Because you said it went in and I want to know that it did."**

**"That is a different sentence,"** the woman said, **"and it is a better one, and I am going to say the other half of it once and then I am going to ask you the question, and if you answer it the way you have just answered that one then something goes in the book, and if you answer it the way people usually answer it then nothing does, and I have never once known in advance which of those two things is going to happen and I am not going to start on the forty-first."**

**"Go on."**

**"What did you say in that room."**

The woman of about thirty-six put the card down flat on the table, the clip side up, and did not push it towards anybody.

**"I said that a person had been in a bed in my town for about nine days in the spring, and that the thing that would have come for that person was four days away on a bus, and that I had sent for it, and that I am not going to send for anything ever again, and that I am not sorry."**

**"That's six things."**

**"It's one thing said six ways and you can have it in one line or you can have it in the whole of it and the whole of it takes about nine seconds and I've got the bus at about one."**

**"Then have the whole of it."**

**And she had the whole of it, and it took about nine seconds, and she said it to a woman's face with her hands flat on a table, and Marek was standing at the back of that room and had not come up the stair for it and did not sit in the second chair and would not sit in the second chair, and none of what follows is a thing he said.**

**"I had a man in a bed in my own house in the spring and he was there from a Monday to about a Thursday, and I was not a nurse and I was not a doctor and I had a tap that runs and about four neighbours, and on the Tuesday afternoon I decided that the thing I wanted was a person in a white coat from a town four days away, and I sent a man on a bus with money, and he got there on the Friday morning, and the man was dead on the Thursday night, and it was the correct thing to do and it did not work, and I have spent about six months being the woman in a town who sent for the right person."**

**"And the connection."**

**"And the connection,"** she said, **"is that a man in this city with a bench and a counter has spent about four years telling about four hundred people that they may refuse anything, and he has never once been in a room where refusing cost anybody anything, and I do not blame him for that and I want to say so to his face, and I have never met him and I am not going to be introduced and that is the arrangement."**

Nobody in that room said a word.

**The fifth chair was empty, and it had not been empty on any of the six Wednesdays before it, and the woman who sits in it keeps a shop four days of bus travel away and went back on the Sunday night on a seat she paid for herself, and nobody on that landing said out loud that she was not in the room, and about four of the eleven people up that stair had worked that out from the fact of the chair and none of them said it.**

---

**"Do you want it in the book,"** the woman said.

**"No."**

**"Then I can't put it in."**

**"I know you can't. I want it on your book, not in it."**

The woman of about thirty-six said the next thing and it took about four seconds and nobody else in that room had any part in it.

**"You are the first person in about four days who has not asked me what the card is for, and I have said what I said in a room with nine people in it and four of them work in one of the four places and two of them keep a cold store, and not one of the four of them wrote any of it down, and she asked them at the end whether anybody wanted to write down what had happened, and they all said no, and she said that was the correct answer, and she is right and I am not going to argue with it in here."**

**"Thank you,"** the woman said.

**"That's all right."**

**Then the woman said thank you, out loud, in that room, for the first time in about nineteen years that anybody in that room has heard her say it to a person who had not been thanked by anybody else in the week, and she said it before she had decided to, the way a person says a thing when the sentence has arrived in their mouth before their opinion has, and it came out as two words and it went nowhere, because a book of four columns does not have a place for a person being thanked.**

**The book did not open. It stood at sixty lines before the hour and it stood at sixty lines after the hour, and nothing went into the tin either, and the tin stands at seventy-three and has stood at seventy-three for about nineteen years.**

**And the reason is not a rule and it is not evidence of anything and it is not a change in her, and it is this: in about nineteen years she has been told, by people who meant it kindly and by people who wanted a line out of her, what a line in that book is. A line in that book is a thing a person said about themselves, to her face, while nine other people sat in nine chairs and could hear it. A woman of about thirty-six said a true thing to her face. It was about a bed in her own house in the spring and a bus and a man who was dead by the Thursday, and every part of it was about somebody else, and a book of four columns has no column for a woman telling you what happened to a person in her own house, and the two words came out of the woman's mouth about four seconds before the question finished being asked, and by the time the question was finished the two words had used up the moment, and she is not going to start on the forty-first.**

Nobody asked her why. Nobody in that room has asked her why in about nineteen years and nobody asked her why this morning and nobody will ask her why at the next one.

**The count was announced at the end and a count is announced at a sitting and on no other morning: forty-six, of which forty-one correspond.** Forty of those correspond to the forty Wednesdays the book holds, and five of the five predate the book on a sheet she has never shown anybody.

**On the nine days before this one the count was forty-five of which forty correspond, and the two states are two states, and one and one is a count and is not a distance from anything.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
