# Chapter 415 — A Room Offered By A Man Who Is Owed Nothing, And Nine Seconds Of No

The Monday of week one hundred and fifty-three was spent on a bus each way and on the ground in the second of four towns on that road for about five hours, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about fifty-eight who keeps the only written record in that town offered a man of twenty-two a room, unasked, in about nine seconds, and the man of twenty-two said no in about nine seconds, and nobody improved on the no and nobody thanked the man of about fifty-eight, and the room is still there, and the cost of the saying is not named by anybody and is not going to be, and two people on a bus that day each said out loud what the day had cost them and nobody in this case asked either of them about it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and the eleven were eleven jobs in a town he has not worked in before, done in the order the card gave them, and the card is a card in date and carries no address. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is five hundred and ninety-five days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is five hundred and ninety-nine days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday of the fortnight before last, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and nobody in that town has been told whose hand is on it.

---

The town had a set of scales on a concrete pad by the road and a hut about eight feet by six beside it with a window in one side, and inside the hut there was a table, a stove, a kettle, a book on the table, and about four things on the walls that are not on any list of anything.

There were four lamps in that street and one of them had come on in the spring that was not on in the winter, and the shop shut at four.

A lorry was on the pad when the bus came in and the man at the scales wrote something down in the book while it was being unloaded, and he did it without being asked and he did it the same way he has done it every time, and the driver did not get out and did not ask him anything and neither of those two things has ever happened on this pad.

---

**He is about fifty-eight. He is not a clerk. He holds no post. He is owed nothing by anybody in this case, and he is not a villain, and he is not named on any surface and in no document and in nobody's mouth, and nobody has ever asked him to explain the book and he has never offered to.**

Marek got off at about ten with about five hours before the bus came round, and he did the eleven jobs in the order the card gave them, and about four of them were the same fault in four different places, and nobody in that town had seen him before and nobody asked him where he had learned any of it and nobody was thanked.

At about half past two he was standing on the concrete pad with a cup of tea out of the kettle in the hut, which the man of about fifty-eight had poured for him without being asked and without saying anything about it.

Then the man said the thing, and he had been near the table with his back to the window, and he said it to the door rather than to Marek, and it took him about nine seconds.

"**There is a room in the back of this. Nobody sleeps in it. There is a bed in it and there is a table in it and there is a window that shuts, and it is yours if you are ever on this road again and want somewhere to be.**"

Then he did not say anything else, and he did not press it, and he did not look round, and he picked up the cup he had poured and rinsed it.

Marek said no in about nine seconds.

"**I have to be back on Thursday. And I would not know what to do in it.**"

"**Right.**"

"**No. Thank you.**"

"**It is not a thing to thank me for. I have a stove.**"

**Nobody improved on the no. Nobody thanked the man of about fifty-eight for offering it and nobody thanked Marek for refusing it. Nobody asked him whether he would think about it and nobody asked him whether he would come back, and he did not say he would do either, and about four times in the next hour he could have asked a question about the book and did not, and nobody offered.**

He did not ask what the book is for. He did not ask to see it. He did not ask who he would send it to. He did not ask whether anybody reads it. He drank the tea and he asked about the scales and was told what the scales are for, and about the four lamps, and was told which one came on in the spring, and about the shop that shuts at four, and was told that as well.

**The cost of the nine seconds is not on anything and is not going to be, and he did not name it in the hut or on the bus or afterwards in a warm room off a service road, and there is no line about it on two nails and there is no column in any book in this case for a thing like it, and that is not a finding and is not a refusal to look, and the reason is that he has not worked out what it is.**

---

**And then the man of about fifty-eight said the other thing, and it was the thing that was actually said to a stranger that day, and it took him about nine seconds, and he was owed nothing, and nobody thanked him, and nobody asked him who he would send it to.**

"**Nobody has come to ask me anything since the spring. I write it down anyway. I have nowhere to send it and I am not going to stop.**"

He said it while he was rinsing the cup and he did not stop washing it and he did not look up, and then he put the book back the way it had been, spine out, on the table under the window, and it is a book he has been keeping by hand for about nine years because a lorry that is not written down is a lorry that did not come, and it is not a book anybody has ever asked him for.

Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was being dramatic about it. Nobody asked him to stop. Nobody wrote it down.

**That book is a book a man keeps by a set of scales on a road, and it is not the book in a back room in this city, and it is not the book a bus driver in a depot in this city keeps in his own hand, and it is not the page of paper with about eleven lines on it, and it is not the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts, and it is not a folder lying face up on a table in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields in this city, and nobody in this case turns that folder over and nobody in that town went near it. There are four books on this road in four days and three of them have nobody to send them to, and no clause in this case is going to hold any two of them together.**

---

**The costs, and there are two of them on this one bus, and both of them are people who do not live in this city, and neither of them is a villain and neither of them is thanked.**

A man of about fifty-seven was on that bus who had come up the road to do a day's work in a shop that shut at four and who had not got to the shop. He said it to the window, unasked, and it took him about nine seconds.

"**I lost the day and I lose the day's pay with it, and I had told them Sunday I was coming, and I am not going to say here why I did not get there.**"

Nobody asked him. Nobody said he was wrong to come and nobody said he was right to come.

A person of about nineteen was on that bus who had come up the road to look at a kitchen and did not get to a kitchen, and who said that in about nine words and in their own words and unasked.

"**I came for one morning and I have had one whole day and I have not seen a kitchen, and that is a day I do not get back.**"

Nobody asked. Nobody said it was a shame. Nobody improved on it and nobody thanked them and nobody said they were wrong to come, and the two of them and the man of twenty-two were on the same bus for about two hours and none of the three knew the other two were on it.

---

At about four the shop shut and the bus came round, and the man of about fifty-eight was still at the pad with the book on the table under the window and the kettle on the stove behind him, and the fourth lamp in that street was on in the daylight and has been since the spring, and the room in the back of that hut is a room and nobody is in it and nobody has said that out loud.

**A card in a coat pocket is a seventh object and it is in a town and it is not a card in a rail in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and that rail holds one card where it held two, and it is not the wall list in its plastic sleeve in that shop, and it is not the hardboard on two nails in a room off a service road, and it is not the master's original in a fourth pocket, and it is not the page of paper with about eleven lines on it, and it is not the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts. The man at the scales was not asked whose card it was and Marek did not say and the man did not ask and the card went back into a coat pocket on a bus and has not been out of it since the Wednesday of the fortnight before last.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
