# Chapter 419 — Four Passengers Written Into A Book That Goes Nowhere, And A Man Who Says So Out Loud

The Wednesday of week one hundred and fifty-four was a day on which a man of about forty-three who drives a bus out of this city finished at six and went home, and the one thing that is true of it is that **he wrote into a book of his own that on the Thursday of the week before last four passengers did not travel out past a point on that road because the road is not there past it, that the bus turned at a farm, and that the four of them got off there and made their own arrangements, and he said out loud, unasked, that the entry is right, that it is in the book the way it should be, that the book is not sent to anybody, and that it will not change anything, and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him to send it and nobody asked him whether the four were told anything before they got off.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and four days old and is warm. The card is six hundred and eight days old, four days more than that room is, and it is in a coat pocket, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

**This is a depot in this city and not a room, and about four other people in that building were leaving at the same time as the man of forty-three and were not interested in a book.**

---

The depot had a bus standing in it with its offside door open, and an office door with a wired light in the upper half of it that was shut, and a table, and a chair, and a stove, and a book on the table, and a window that looks at about nine hundred feet of concrete.

The man who drives that bus is about forty-three. He has driven for that company about eleven years. He keeps the book himself, in his own hand, in the way it is written, and the company has never asked him for it and has never asked him to keep it and has never read it and does not know it is there.

**The book is a bus driver's book. It is not the four-column book on a table in a back room in this city, which is a woman's, and it is not a book kept by hand at a set of scales on a road, which is a man's and has nobody to send it to, and it is not the page of paper with about eleven lines on it, and it is not the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts. Three books in this case have nobody to send them to and a fourth has a woman in front of it, and no clause in this case is going to hold any two of them together.**

He had about nine minutes before the last of the people in that building went.

---

He wrote it while the offside door was still open and the light was still up, in the way he writes everything in that book, which is the day, and the service, and then what happened, and no reason.

**On the Thursday of the week before last, four passengers did not travel out past a certain point on that road, because the road is not there past it. The bus turned at a farm. The four of them got off at that farm and made their own arrangements from there.**

Then he said it out loud, unasked, to about four other people in that building who were leaving at the same time as he was, and it took him about nine seconds.

"**That entry is right and it is in the book the way it should be. The book is not sent to anybody. It will not change anything.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him to send it. Nobody asked him to tell the company. Nobody asked him whether the four were told anything before they got off. Nobody asked him why the bus could not go and nobody has ever asked him and the company has never asked for a record of why a bus cannot go past a certain point and does not want one.

**He is owed nothing. He is not a hero and he is not a villain and he is not in anybody's case, and about four people in that depot have since said that the man writes everything down and about four have said nothing, and neither group has asked the other why, and nobody has said out loud that he will not be asked about it again, and that is simply how it stands.**

He shut the book, and the book went on the shelf it lives on with about nine others on it, and he went home at six.

---

**And the same road, in this case, is a road a man's book is the only written thing about, and the four passengers who got off at that farm on that Thursday are not on anything and were not asked anything and made their own arrangements, and about four of the people in this case have been up that road in the last nine days and not one of them has been told by anybody that a book in a depot in this city has the same fact in it in a different hand.**

Nobody in this case has asked to see that book. Nobody in that depot has been asked for it and nobody would give it and there is nobody to give it to.

Nobody thanked anybody.

*422. Wednesday of week 154, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is four passengers who did not travel written into a bus driver's own book at a depot in this city, a bus turned at a farm because the road is not there past it, nine words said out loud to four people leaving at the same time, a book that is not sent to anybody, and nobody thanked anybody.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and this entry is for a day in which none of those eleven was done where this case usually follows him. That depot: a bus standing with its offside door open, an office door with a wired light in the upper half of it that was shut, a table, a chair, a stove, a book on the table, a shelf with about nine books on it, and a window looking at about nine hundred feet of concrete. Who was in that building at about ten to six: about five, of whom four were leaving at the same time as the man of forty-three. The man: about forty-three, eleven years with that company, keeps the book himself in his own hand, and the company has never asked him for it, has never asked him to keep it, has never read it, and does not know it is there. What was written, and in the way that book is written, meaning the day, the service, and then what happened and no reason: that on the Thursday of the week before last four passengers did not travel out past a certain point on that road because the road is not there past it; that the bus turned at a farm; and that the four of them got off there and made their own arrangements. What he said, unasked, in about nine seconds, to about four people in that building: that the entry is right, that it is in the book the way it should be, that the book is not sent to anybody, and that it will change nothing. Times anybody thanked him: none. Times anybody asked him to send it: none. Times anybody asked him to tell the company: none. Times anybody asked whether the four were told anything before they got off: none. Times anybody asked why the bus could not go past that point: none, and nobody ever has. Whether the company has ever asked for a record of why a bus cannot go past a point: no, and it does not want one. Whether that book has been seen by anybody in this case: no, and nobody has asked, and nobody would hand it over and there is nobody to hand it to. The books in this case, and the number of them: four in this stretch of road and this city, of which three have nobody to send them to and one has a woman in front of it, and this depot's is not the one with the woman in front of it. Rate: nine and ten, uncorrected, told to nobody. Heating: on, dearer, four names on a lease dearer still, a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid and that is not resolved. Room off that service road: six hundred and four days, and warm. Card: six hundred and eight days, four days more than the room, in a coat pocket, and the rail at the end of the counter in the repair shop holds one card. Two nails, nineteen lines, and no twentieth: the twelve five hundred and twenty-four, the thirteen four hundred and seventy-five, the fourteen four hundred and forty, the fifteen four hundred and nineteen, the sixteen three hundred and ninety-four, the seventeen three hundred and seventy-six, the eighteen three hundred and twenty-two, the nineteen three hundred. None of those eight is an anchor on this date and none of them is a day. What is on two nails has not changed: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, about fourteen names, nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off, the first of them a cook's and has never moved, the newest the only promise on it. A wall list in a plastic sleeve in the repair shop in another district is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. A ring binder on a shelf: eight things in it, the eighth of them a folded page that is unread and was not read, and the woman's page is one of the eight and is shut in with them. Fifth pocket: empty, drawer shut, and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in. Man of about fifty-one with a tool bag: in the room four miles off, and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty weeks. Man of about thirty-three: not in that depot, holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for about thirty-three weeks and six days, given nothing and needed nothing. The Exchange did not sit on this day; its last sitting was forty-two days ago and its next is fourteen days from today; book fifty-four lines, telephone seventy-three, count announced thirty-two times of which twenty-seven correspond, nineteen years of it, and none of the four is convertible into another and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and is not given as one. The ask: two hundred and ninety-four days, that being forty-two weeks, counted from the day it was asked and not from any sitting since. A folder in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields: not turned over, not asked for, and not a fourth book on that road, and nobody in that depot and nobody four miles off has been told about the other one. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 422.**

*The entry, as a record of a correct thing that changed nothing.* **Who wrote it: a man of about forty-three who drives a bus out of this city and has driven for that company about eleven years, unnamed, owed nothing, not a hero, not a villain, not in anybody's case. When: about ten to six on the Wednesday, with the offside door still open and the light still up. Where: a depot in this city, at a table, in a book that is his own and is kept in his own hand and in the way it is written. What was in it: the day, the service, and then what happened and no reason; that on the Thursday of the week before last four passengers did not travel out past a certain point on that road because the road is not there past it; that the bus turned at a farm; and that the four of them got off there and made their own arrangements. What he said, unasked, in about nine seconds, to about four people in that building who were leaving at the same time: that the entry is right, that it is in the book the way it should be, that the book is not sent to anybody, and that it will not change anything. Times anybody thanked him: none. Times anybody asked him to send it: none. Times anybody asked him to tell the company: none. Times anybody asked whether the four were told anything before they got off: none. Times anybody asked why the bus could not go past that point: none, and nobody ever has. Whether the company wants a record of that: no, and never has wanted one. Where the book went afterwards: onto a shelf with about nine others on it. Whether he will be asked about it again: nobody has said out loud that he will not be, and he is not going to be asked, and nobody in that depot said so either. Owner: him, and a shelf, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody asked a man of forty-three a single question about a book he wrote in about nine minutes, and nobody thanked him and nobody improved on it, and about four people in that depot have since said that the man writes everything down and about four have said nothing. A hardboard on two nails in a room four miles off carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and a card in a coat pocket is not that board and the wall list in a plastic sleeve in a repair shop is not that board and a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it is a fifth object and a page of paper with about eleven lines on it is a sixth in a fourth building and a sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others, and a book at a set of scales on a road is not any of them, and a folder face up on a table in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields is not a book on that road and was not turned over and was not asked for. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Wednesday. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is unread. The Exchange did not sit and its book did not grow and no number was given into its tin, and the two books stayed two books. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 422.**
