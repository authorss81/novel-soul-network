# Chapter 417 — The Second Run After A Fortnight, And A Post Bare For Twenty-One Weeks

The Friday of week one hundred and fifty-three was again a second floor in Crown Terrace from about half past six until about nine, and the one thing that is true of it is that **the run went for the second week running after a fortnight off and nobody said how long it took and nobody asked, the post at the corridor end has now carried nothing for one hundred and forty-seven days, which is twenty-one weeks, the laminated sheet is still in the second drawer on the left and the drawer still does not lock, the far fire door is still being held open with the same folded sheet of paper, and the man of about thirty-nine who came once in about four years is not on that floor and nothing said tonight says he was ever on it, and nobody has chased anybody in that room and nobody did tonight.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is five hundred and ninety-nine days old and is warm. The card is six hundred and three days old, four days more than that room is, and it is in a coat pocket, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

**No town and no place-name is named anywhere in this entry, and no distance between here and there is given in miles, and the day is described by what happened on a floor.**

---

About half past six nine people came up that stair, and about seven of them are on a company's list and about four of them are on none of anything, and about four of those four came for the second week running, which is the first time anybody has come for two weeks running in about as long as this case has been going, and nobody took a count of it and nobody has ever said that out loud.

The corridor was the same corridor. The about nine doors along it were shut, as they are during a run. The near fire door latched. The far fire door had a folded sheet of paper wedged under it and had had one since the Friday before the fortnight, and the man of about fifty-eight who drives that building's van put it there, and he was not in the building tonight and nobody has asked him why not and nobody has asked him about the paper at all.

There was a post at the corridor end with nothing on it. It has now carried nothing for one hundred and forty-seven days, which is twenty-one weeks. Nobody in that company has asked for it to be filled and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it and nobody has said the word out loud in that corridor in about as long as this case has been going.

In the second drawer on the left of the desk by the near fire door there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and the drawer does not lock, and it is still in there, and **no separate count is printed for that sheet in this entry, and a reader loses nothing by that, because the post's own figure above is the figure that walks and the two available dates for the sheet differ by twenty-eight days and picking one of them here would be a guess.**

---

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard.

**Nobody said how long it took. Nobody asked. Nobody referred to last Friday. Nobody referred to the fortnight, and nobody has ever asked the about four on that floor why they come, and nobody chased anybody in that room and nobody did tonight, and about nine people went up that stair at about half past six and about nine people came down it at about nine, and the two numbers are the same and nobody counted either.**

At about ten to eight a man of about thirty in overalls who is on the company's list said one ordinary sentence about the stairwell light, and it was about the stairwell light, and nobody took it up.

At about nine the chairs went up and the stair emptied and the lights went off in an order that has nothing to do with anything.

---

A write-up went into a file three Fridays ago in the way that a company writes things up, and it went into a building four miles away, and it was not read that week, and nothing on that floor is different because of it.

**That is also the whole of what happened on the Friday before the break, and there is nobody on that floor who could tell you the two things apart, and the two Fridays have never been in the same sentence in that building, and the man of about thirty-nine who wrote the first of them is not on the floor tonight and no reason is given and nobody asks for one.**

**Nobody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one, and that is a thing nobody has ever put anywhere and not a thing somebody has looked for and failed to find.**

Nobody thanked anybody.

*The post at twenty-one weeks, as a record of a thing nobody has asked about.* **The post: at the corridor end of a second floor in Crown Terrace, bare since the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, which is one hundred and forty-seven days and that is twenty-one weeks. What it carried before it went bare: nothing anybody in this case can name, and nobody in that building has said out loud what is meant to go on it in about as long as this case has been going. Times anybody in that company has asked for it to be filled: none. Times anybody has asked what is meant to go on it: none. The drawer beside it: the second drawer on the left of the desk by the near fire door, and it does not lock, and there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it in it, and it is still in there, and a separate count is not printed for it here. Whether the post and the sheet are the same question: nobody in that building has ever said so, and about four people there would say they are not and about four would say they are, and the two groups have not spoken. Owner: the corridor, and nobody.**

*The four who come, as a record of a reason nobody has ever been given.* **On the floor: about nine at half past six. On a company's list: about seven. On nothing: about four. How many of those four came for the second week running after a fortnight off: about four, which is the first time in about as long as this case has been going that anybody has come twice in a row, and nobody took a count of it and nobody has ever said it out loud in that building. Why they come: never asked, by anybody, in about as long as this case has been going, and about four people in this city have wondered about it and about four have not. Whether anybody was chased in that room: nobody, and nobody ever is. Whether anybody said how long the run took: nobody. Whether anybody referred to the Friday before the break: nobody. Whether the two Fridays have ever been in the same sentence in that building: no. Whether the man of about thirty-nine who wrote the first of them up was on the floor tonight: no. Whether anybody said what he said when he did it: nobody has said it in that building, and it is not said in this entry. Owner: a stair, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody said how long the run took and nobody asked, and nobody referred to any other Friday, and nobody on that floor has ever been told what any Friday in that building was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was. A hardboard on two nails in a room four miles off carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and a card in a coat pocket is not that board, and a wall list in a plastic sleeve in a repair shop is not that board, and a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it is a fifth object, and a page of paper with about eleven lines on it is a sixth in a fourth building, and a sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, a folder in a weighbridge hut was not turned over, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Friday. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in that binder and is unread. The Exchange did not sit on a Friday and its book did not grow and no number was given into its tin. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 420.**
