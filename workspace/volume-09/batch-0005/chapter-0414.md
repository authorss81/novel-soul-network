# Chapter 414 — The First Run In A Fortnight, And A Post At The End Of A Corridor

The Friday of week one hundred and fifty-two was spent on a second floor in Crown Terrace from about half past six until about nine, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a run that had been off for a fortnight went again for about nine minutes, and nobody said how long it took and nobody asked, and a post at the corridor end has now carried nothing for one hundred and forty days, which is twenty weeks, and there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it in a drawer that does not lock and no separate count is printed for it here, and a man of about thirty-nine who buys training for the company that runs that floor did not come and no reason was given and nobody asked for one, and the far fire door is being held open with a folded sheet of paper that somebody wedged there and nobody has taken away.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is five hundred and ninety-two days old and is warm. The card is five hundred and ninety-six days old, four days more than that room is, and it is in a coat pocket and has been since the Wednesday before last, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

**The whole of that day was on that floor. About nine people came up that stair between about half past six and about nine and about nine went down it at about nine, and that is the whole of what is known about where anybody in this case was.**

---

The same floor as the last time this building ran it, in the same corridor, with the same about nine folding chairs against the wall and the same desk by the near fire door and the same stair.

There was a post at the corridor end with nothing on it. It has carried nothing for one hundred and forty days, which is twenty weeks, and nobody in that building has asked for it to be filled, and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it, and about four people in that company know it exists and about four do not.

**In the second drawer on the left of that desk there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and the drawer does not lock, and it has been in there for as long as the post has been bare, and no separate count is printed for that sheet anywhere in this entry, because two different dates for it exist in this case and differ by twenty-eight days, and neither of the two is printed here and a reader loses nothing by that.**

The far fire door was propped with a folded sheet of paper, and it had been there since the Friday before last, and about four people who came up that stair in the fortnight the run was off walked past it and about four did not notice it, and the two groups have not met.

About eleven people were on the floor between about half past six and about nine. About seven of them are on the company's list. About four are on none of anything, and about four people in this city have wondered in about as long as this case has been going why the four come at all, and nobody has ever asked any of the four.

---

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard.

**Nobody said how long it took. Nobody asked. Nobody referred to the fortnight, and nobody referred to the Friday before the fortnight, and nobody has ever asked the about four on that floor why they come, and nobody chased anybody in that room and nobody did tonight, and about nine people went up that stair at about half past six and about nine people came down it at about nine, and the two numbers are the same and nobody counted either.**

At about ten to eight a woman of about twenty-eight who is on the company's list said one ordinary sentence about the fire door. It was about the fire door. Nobody took it up.

At about nine the chairs went up and the stair emptied and a man of about twenty-eight who works in that building put the lights off in an order that has nothing to do with anything.

---

**The man of about thirty-nine who buys training for the company that runs that floor was not on it, and nobody said he was not coming, and nobody said he had been and had not.** He came once, on his own time, in about four years, and wrote it up in his own company's form, and said out loud that it changes nothing, and was not thanked, and that is the whole of what is known about him in that building. Nothing said tonight says that he was ever on that floor, and nothing on that floor has ever said it either, and no reason is given tonight, and nobody asked for one, and the wall calendar that would have joined two Fridays to each other is not on the floor and is not in that building.

A man of about fifty-eight who drives that building's van was not in the corridor at about nine either. He put the paper there on the Friday before last and he has not been asked about it and nobody has asked him why not, and the paper is still there.

---

Nothing on that floor was different at nine o'clock from what it was at half past six, and nothing in the building was different at nine o'clock from what it was at half past six, and a person standing in that corridor at nine would not be able to say that anything had been done on it that day except that nine people had been in it.

**Nobody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one, and that is a thing nobody has ever put anywhere rather than a thing somebody has looked for and failed to find.**

Nobody thanked anybody.

Friday of week 152, at about ten on a second floor in Crown Terrace, and this entry is one run of about nine minutes after a fortnight, about eleven people on a floor, seven on a company's list and four on none, a post bare for twenty weeks, a laminated sheet in a drawer that does not lock with no count printed for it, a fire door held open with a folded sheet of paper, and nobody saying how long it took.*

A post at the corridor end, bare since the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, which is one hundred and forty days and that is twenty weeks, and nobody has asked for it to be filled. A separate count for the laminated sheet: not printed, because two dates for it exist in this case and differ by twenty-eight days, and the choice between them would be a guess and a guess is not a figure. People on the floor: about eleven between half past six and about nine, about seven on the company's list and about four on none. Who called it: a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Whether the man of about thirty-nine who buys training for that company was on the floor: no, and no reason was given and none was asked for, and nothing in this entry says he was ever on it. The ask: two hundred and eighty-two days, that being forty weeks and two days, counted from the day it was asked and not from any sitting since. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 417.**

*The post and the drawer, as a record of two things in a corridor that nobody asked about.* **The post: at the corridor end of a second floor in Crown Terrace, bare since the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, which is one hundred and forty days and that is twenty weeks. Times anybody in that company has asked for it to be filled: none. Times anybody has asked what is meant to go on it: none. How many people in that company know it exists: about four. How many do not: about four. The drawer: the second drawer on the left of the desk by the near fire door, and it does not lock, and there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it in it. Whether a separate count is printed for that sheet: no, and the reason is printed in the same sentence as the number is not, and the reason is that two dates for it exist in this case and they differ by twenty-eight days and choosing between them at a desk would be a guess. Times anybody has asked to see the sheet: none. Times anybody has asked for it to go back on the post: none. Whether the sheet and the post are the same question: nobody in that building has ever said so. Owner: the corridor, and nobody.**

*The fortnight, as a record of a thing nobody cancelled.* **The run: off for a fortnight. Who cancelled it: nobody, and nobody wrote down why it was off, and nobody has asked since. What it did on the Friday after the fortnight: it went, nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. How long it took: not said and not asked. Whether anybody referred to the fortnight: nobody. Whether anybody referred to the Friday before the fortnight: nobody. Whether the about four on that floor who are on no list were asked why they come: no, and nobody ever has. Whether anybody was chased in that room: nobody, and nobody ever is. Whether the man of about thirty-nine who buys training for that company was on the floor: no. Whether a reason was given for that: no. Whether anybody asked for one: no. Whether a write-up of any kind came out of tonight: none, and a write-up went into a file in a building four miles away three Fridays ago in the way that his own company writes things up, and nothing on that floor is different because of it. Owner: a company, and a calendar that is not on the floor.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody said how long the run took and nobody asked, and nobody referred to any other Friday, and nobody on that floor has ever been told what any Friday in that building was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was. A hardboard on two nails in a room four miles off carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and a card in a coat pocket is not that board, and a wall list in a plastic sleeve in a repair shop is not that board, and a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it is a fifth object, and a page of paper with about eleven lines on it is a sixth in a fourth building, and a sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, a folder in a weighbridge hut was not turned over, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Friday. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in that binder and is unread. The Exchange did not sit on a Friday and its book did not grow and no number was given into its tin. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 417.**
