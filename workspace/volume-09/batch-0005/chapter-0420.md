# Chapter 420 — A Van Driver Names What Four Strangers Cost Him, And Nineteen Lines Stand At Three Hundred And One Days

The Thursday of week one hundred and fifty-four was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and from about half past six in one room off a service road in the Lower Wards, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about fifty-four who drives a van for a shop in this city, who said a thing to a woman's face in a back room on the Wednesday of the week before last that put a line into a book that is nineteen years old, was in that room and said out loud what four strangers asking after a road have cost his shop, and nobody in that room asked him about it, and both of those facts are printed, and about four days of nine days of work in this city are gone and the other five are not going to be and a man of about thirty-four does not yet know, and on two nails in that room a line that is three hundred and one days old is still the only promise on the board and nothing was added to it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and five days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and nine days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday of the fortnight before last, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and **the rail at the end of the counter in the repair shop holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday, and the card in the coat pocket is in a coat and the two have not been in one building since.**

---

He had not told anybody he was not going back, and nobody had asked him to say, and the sheet of paper in an office in a third district has four of nine days marked against his name and nobody in that office has telephoned.

There was nothing on the counter in that room. There is a counter in it and there has been nothing on that counter on any Thursday for about a year.

**A bell-push on the wall by that door does not ring by itself, and is not broken, and is now known not to be broken, and a part for it is on a van on a Wednesday, and about four people in that building know that and about four do not, and nobody has been asked about the other four things the man who fitted it could see in that building.**

---

He came in at about half past six and sat down at the end of the second bench and said nothing for about nine minutes, and about four people noticed the nine minutes and about four did not.

Then he said it, unasked, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular, in his own words, and it was about four seconds of preamble and then the sentence, and nobody in that room asked him a single question about it afterwards and nobody thanked him and nobody improved on it and nobody said he was right.

"**Four of them in a fortnight. All in the shop, all wanting the same road, and I have told them the truth four times, and the truth has not stopped one of them, and I am not going to lie to the fifth. That is what it has cost me and I have not said it to anybody so I am saying it here.**"

Nobody asked him about it.

**He is not thanked. He is owed nothing. He is not a hero and not a villain and not in anybody's case. He is not going to be asked to say it again, and nobody has said out loud that he will not be, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who drives a van should not have to tell four strangers where a road is and about four have said nothing at all, and the two groups have not met and are not going to.**

**The cost is named, and the fact that nobody asked him about it is named, and neither of those is a thing anybody in that room did about it. That is all. The two facts are on this page and nowhere else and they are the whole of what came of it.**

---

A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink saying nothing. A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in the room and said nothing, and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty weeks and one day, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Thursday.

**Two nails in that room carried a hardboard with nineteen lines on it and the eight ages are set out in the docket below. Nothing was added to it on this Thursday and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and no twentieth line is opened anywhere in this week, and the reason is not restraint: adding a promise to a board in order to have something to publish is adding a promise and not a line, and a twentieth line would be a new day and a new figure and a new thing to be wrong about, and this board has been the same nineteen lines since the first of them was written.**

The newest of the nineteen is a lift mechanic's. Eleven words, signed, about the middle. It is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and one days old.

---

**And the arithmetic of the last ten days is in that room and was said by nobody.**

Four days of nine are gone. The other five are not going to be, and about nine weeks from now there will be a mark against nine of them on a sheet of paper, and a man of about thirty-four in a third district will not remember agreeing to any of it, and nobody in this case has been told and he has not said.

Four sentences sit in the head of a man of twenty-two and are on nothing. Four places said one sentence each to the same stranger, in about nine seconds each, by four people with ordinary jobs, and the four sentences are not the same sentence, and the four places have not met, and nobody has been told what the other two said, and about two of the four do not know the other three were asked anything. Nothing has been adopted. Nothing has been written down. There is no list. Nobody has anywhere to put four sentences and nobody has started a place to put them, and a room off a service road in this city is not that place and does not have a board for it and there is no board in that room for anything but the nineteen lines.

**The two refusals in this week were made by two people with ordinary jobs who were owed nothing, in about four seconds each, and neither of them was thanked for either, and one of them has been asked the same question by three people from this city in nine weeks and has answered it three times differently and cannot remember which was true, and nobody has told him about the other refusal and he does not know there was one.**

**The separation is four months and three weeks old and is not shorter and is not ended.** Nothing in that room bears on it, and about four people in that room have a life that does not involve either of the two people in it and about four do not, and neither group has asked the other anything.

**A card in a coat pocket is a third object and the coat is the only place it has been since the Wednesday of the fortnight before last. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop is a fourth and is not that board. The hardboard is a fifth. The master's original is in a fourth pocket with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines on it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others, and it was not adopted and nothing was signed on it this week and nobody in that room has been asked to sign it.**

**The Exchange did not sit today. Its last sitting was fifteen days ago, and the one before that was forty-three days ago, and its next is thirteen days from today, and at that one the book does not open. The four figures are a book at fifty-four lines, a tin at seventy-three, a count announced thirty-two times of which twenty-seven correspond, and nineteen years of it, and none of the four is convertible into another and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and is not printed as one anywhere.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
