# Chapter 412 — The Twenty-Seventh Sitting, A Book That Opens, And Two Figures That Are Each Other's Days

The Wednesday of week one hundred and fifty-two was the twenty-seventh sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and it was the first sitting of this one, and the one thing that is true of it is that **the book opened and grew by one line because a man of about fifty-four who drives a van for a shop in this city said a thing to the holder's face in about four seconds and it was not a request and there is no name against it, and the tin did not move, the count came out at thirty-two of which twenty-seven, the ninth chair was against the wall with its back to the room at the start of the morning and against the wall at the end of it, and four miles off in a different building a man of twenty-two was at a bench and was not in that room and knows none of what happened in it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and then at a counter until about six. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is five hundred and ninety days old, and five hundred and ninety is not a date, and there is a thing about that in the conditions paragraph below, and is warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and nobody asked them anything.

The card in the coat pocket is five hundred and ninety-four days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday before last, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten, and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

**There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. It did not move in about four hours. Nobody in that room looked at it more than once, and about four people in that room have wondered in all of that time who put it there, and nobody has ever asked, and it is not going to be asked, and the asking is not a thing this morning had room for.**

There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, shut for about forty minutes of it and open for about four.

There were about eleven people, and there was a young man of twenty-one who has climbed those stairs since he was seventeen and a young woman of twenty-one who has climbed them since she was fifteen and about five others who this morning are not being described.

**She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.**

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece.

The first was a person who wanted to know whether a book that has not grown for two sittings is a thing anybody can be worried about, and she said that it is a book, and that it grows when somebody speaks to her, and that she is not going to say anything else about it, and she wrote nothing.

The second was a man of about forty-seven who said a thing twice in a different order and she wrote nothing, and he said thank you and she said yes.

The third was a person who asked her a question about a road and she answered it in about nine seconds and wrote nothing, and the answer has not been written down by anybody and is not repeated here.

The fourth was the man of about forty-four who put the ask a long time back and has not put it since. He was in that room and he did not put it and she did not offer it and nobody in that room wanted anything offered, and about four people in that room noticed that and about four did not.

The fifth was nothing anybody has reported.

**The sixth was the one that is on the page. A man of about fifty-four who drives a van for a shop in this city came up those stairs that morning, and he drives it most days and unloads at about nine places, and one of the places is a set of scales on a concrete pad by a road a long way from this city, with a hut about eight feet by six beside it, and the man at the hut writes down the name of the van and the day every single time and has never once asked the driver for anything, and the driver has never once asked the man what the book is for.**

He said it to her face, in about four seconds, and it was not a request and it was not a question, and he did not say it twice.

"**I unload at the hut by the scales. He writes my name in a book every time and I do not have to ask him to. I have never once asked him what that book is for and I am not going to ask him now.**"

She wrote it. It took her about four seconds and she wrote it in the fourth column and she did not ask him a question and he did not say anything else, and about four people in that room did not hear it and about four said nothing, and neither group has said anything about it since.

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked him which road, and nobody asked him whose book, and nobody asked him whether he had ever wanted to read it, and nobody said that it was a good thing, and nobody said that it was a small thing, and nobody said that the two of them ought to talk. He has not been back and about four weeks have gone by as this is taken, and there is no name against that line, and nothing in that book has ever carried a name.

**The book was at fifty-three lines when the morning began. It is at fifty-four now. It grew by one, and it grew because somebody said a thing to her face, which is the only way it has ever grown and the only way anybody in this case can say that it grows. The book on that table in the hut by the scales is a different book in a different building on a different side of a road, and it is a book a man keeps by hand because a lorry that is not written down is a lorry that did not come, and the two of them are not in one room and have never been in one room and this morning neither of them was in the other's.**

---

**No number was given into the tin this morning.** The tin stands at seventy-three, and it stood at seventy-three at the last sitting four weeks ago, and it stood at seventy-three at the one before that, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved, and nobody in that room wanted a number.

The count came out as it comes out at every sitting. It is thirty-two. Twenty-seven of them are the Wednesdays this book has sat on, and five are older than the book, and the five are on a sheet she has never shown anybody and did not show this morning either.

The book is at fifty-four lines. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced thirty-two times. The whole business is nineteen years old. **Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and every one of the four is the whole of itself, and none was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and was not given as one.**

Nobody thanked her. Nobody said anything about the two sittings before this one that did not open, and she has not said anything about them, and nothing about that has ever been said in that room, and nobody in that room has ever put two sittings together and asked her about the pair of them, and if anybody did she would give them four seconds of something else and nobody would ask her again.

---

**The ask is two hundred and eighty days old, which is forty weeks, counted from the day it was asked at the seventeenth sitting and not from any sitting since, and the figure before this one was thirty-nine weeks and five days and belongs to a Monday and not to this morning.** Nobody in that room asked her for that rule. She has said six times that the last time she says it is the last time, and she has kept that six times, and this morning nothing was put in the space it left and nothing was offered in its place and nobody in that room wanted anything offered.

---

**And the two figures that came out of that room's own paperwork, and which are not the same kind of thing as each other, and which a person reading them in a hurry gets wrong.**

The room off a service road in this city is five hundred and ninety days old. That is an age, and it is how long a converted shop unit with four rooms and a back one has been standing with the heating on in it, and it is not a day and it is not when anything was switched on, and the number is not a date anybody in this case has. **Five hundred and ninety is also the day the seventeenth line was written on a hardboard on two nails in that room, by a clerk of forty-four, signed, about being findable, and the seventeenth line is therefore five hundred and ninety days old on a day that is five hundred and ninety, and both of those are true at once and neither of them is a day.**

The seventeen lines are three hundred and sixty-two days old. That is an age as well, and it is how long a line has been on a board, and **three hundred and sixty-two is the day the room off that service road opened, so the room and the seventeenth line are each other's day, and the room is five hundred and ninety days old on a day that is the day the seventeen lines were written, and the seventeen lines are three hundred and sixty-two days old on the day the room opened.** Nobody has ever put those two numbers next to each other on a surface, and nobody is going to, and a person in a hurry who reads either of them as a day has read a thing that is not there.

---

Four miles off that landing and four miles off that room, a maintenance rota for a building of nine flats over a canteen came down off a nail again at about four in the afternoon and nobody collected it, because it is not anybody's, and because a man of twenty-two handed it over a fortnight ago and a man of about fifty-six who works for a contractor took it and has not been in that building since.

At about half past six the shutter came down on a service road and a man of twenty-two walked four miles to a room, and about four people were in it, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink saying nothing, and in the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is two hundred and eighty-six days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a third object in a third building and has been since the Wednesday before last. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop is a fourth and is not that board. The hardboard is a fifth. The master's original is in a fourth pocket with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines on it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The book on a table in a hut by a set of scales on a road on the far side of this city is a seventh object and is not any of the six, and the four-column book that grew by one line this morning is an eighth, and those two books have never been in the same building and no clause in this case is going to put them there.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
