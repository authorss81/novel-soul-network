# Chapter 404 — About Eleven People On One Floor For About Nine Minutes, And A Man Of About Thirty-Nine Who Came On His Own Time And Wrote It Up

The Friday evening of week one hundred and forty-nine was the first of ten Fridays in a volume that runs to a Thursday in seven months, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a run went on the second floor of a building in Crown Terrace for about nine minutes, and nobody said how long it took and nobody asked, and a man of about thirty-nine who buys training for the company that runs that floor had not watched a run in about four years and came on his own time and was not on the company's list and not on anybody's, and afterwards he wrote down what he saw in the form his own company uses, correctly, and said out loud that it would change nothing, and nobody thanked him and he is not here again.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. He was four miles away from that floor and at a bench all day and does not appear on that floor, and the two places are not joined anywhere. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. The heating is on and costs more, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved. The room off a service road is five hundred and seventy-one days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday evening for the temperature and nobody asked them anything.

The card in a rail in a repair shop in Lattice Ward is five hundred and seventy-five days old, which is four days more than the room is, and that building is four miles from that floor and is not that building and knows nothing about it.

---

There were about nine folding chairs on that floor. There is a corridor with a fire door at each end of it and about nine doors along it that are not opened during a run. There is a post at the corridor end with nothing on it, and there has been nothing on it for one hundred and nineteen days, which is seventeen weeks. There is a desk by the fire door with four drawers, and in the second drawer on the left of it there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and the drawer does not lock, and the sheet has been in there for the same one hundred and nineteen days.

**About eleven people were on the floor, seven of them on the company's list and about four on none, and the difference between the two groups is not the subject of the evening and nobody made it the subject. About four of the eleven are on no rota at all, and that is a fact about about four people and not a rate, and it is on the back of a woman's own card in a different building and not on anything in Crown Terrace. The other seven are on a list held in an office four miles away that has about nine hundred doors on a line of it and no room in this case in it.**

The woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard called the run. She has a lanyard and it is on a card and the card is not a form and it does not carry a rate.

---

**He came up the stair at about twenty past six and there was nobody at the desk to ask him for anything, and about four people on that floor did not know what to do with a person who was watching, and two of them looked at him and one of them said a thing to the other one that he could not hear, and the man of thirty-nine stood at the wall and let it happen, and about nine minutes later he was in a chair at the back and nobody had asked him a single question about why.**

**Nobody stopped him. Nobody asked him who he was. Nobody asked him to sign anything, and there is nothing on that floor to sign, and there is nowhere on that floor to put an objection to a person standing on it, and that is not a complaint and it is what the room is.**

At about half past six a person on that floor who works there came past him with a folder and said, "**You are welcome to sit. If you are here for the company, the woman at the front has the list.**"

He said he was not on the list and that he had come on his own time and had not watched a run in about four years.

"**Then sit at the back.**"

He sat at the back.

---

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front.

**Nobody said how long it took. Nobody said anything for about nine seconds afterwards. About four people on that floor have said since that the run was about nine minutes and about four have said it was longer, and neither group has measured it and neither group is going to.**

The man of about thirty-nine asked one question. He asked it in the corridor afterwards, of a person who works on that floor, and it was in about four seconds on his side and about four seconds on hers, and it was the whole of what he asked all evening.

"**What is it for.**"

"**It is so that a person who needs something from this company knows who to ask and what to say, and it takes nine minutes and we do it every week.**"

**That is the answer he had heard in a corridor in another building before. It is correct. Nobody improved on it, and nobody asked the woman of about thirty-six with the lanyard what she thought the run was for, and that question was put to the holder of the Exchange a long time ago and four seconds of something else came back and nobody asked her again, and about four people on that floor have said since that the run is for the company and about four have said it is for the room, and neither of those two groups has asked her.**

---

He wrote it up. He did it after the run, on the ramp outside, on a phone, in the form his own company uses, which has a heading and about five fields and a box at the bottom for anything else.

It took him about four minutes and he filled in the five fields correctly, and the fields are not a rate and they are not on a form that anybody in Crown Terrace will ever read.

Then he said it out loud, unasked, to nobody in particular, in about nine words.

"**I have done it the way we do it. It goes in a file. Nothing in that room will be different on Monday because I wrote it down, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.**"

**Nobody thanked him. Nobody corrected him. Nobody sent it anywhere that matters, and the person it went to is in a building four miles from that floor and did not read it that week. He is owed nothing. He is not in anybody's case, and he is not a villain, and nobody improved on it, and the only evidence that any of this happened is that about five fields on his own company's form have six words in them.**

He did not come a second time, and nobody has asked him why not, and about four people in that building have said that a man came once and about four have said nothing, and the two groups have not met and are not going to.

---

The chairs went up at about nine. The fire door at the near end was latched and the one at the far end was propped with a folded sheet of paper that had been there since the week before, and nobody said that to anybody.

**Two nails in a room four miles from that floor carried a hardboard with nineteen lines on it, and two of those numbers have to be handled carefully and are handled here in the sentence rather than in a note. The twelve lines are four hundred and ninety-one days old, and that figure is an age and not a date; the reason it has to be said out loud is that four hundred and ninety-one is the day the thirteenth line was written, so the same number is at once how long one thing has been up and when a different thing was put up. And the thirteenth line is four hundred and forty-two days old, and that is also an age and not a date, and four hundred and forty-two is the day the twelve lines were written. So each of those two numbers is an age of one object and the day another object was written, and a reader who takes either of them for a day has taken a fact about a building for a calendar, and about four people in this case could hold both of those readings at once and about four could not, and neither number is a day anybody in this case has been to.**

The other six on that board: the fourteen is four hundred and seven days old, the fifteen three hundred and eighty-six, the sixteen three hundred and sixty-one, the seventeen three hundred and forty-three, the eighteen two hundred and eighty-nine, and the nineteen two hundred and sixty-seven. The nineteen is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board, and an evening in Crown Terrace has not made it more or less true.

Nothing was added to that board on this Friday and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and the card in a rail in Lattice Ward did not move, and a wall list in a plastic sleeve in that shop is a different object from that board and has never been in the same room as it.

Nobody thanked anybody.

*The man of thirty-nine, as a record of a correct thing that changed nothing.* **Who: a man of about thirty-nine who buys training for the company that runs that floor, unnamed, owed nothing by anybody in it, and not the person who put him there. When he came up the stair: about twenty past six. Whether anybody asked who he was: nobody. Whether anybody asked him to sign anything: no, and there is nothing on that floor to sign. Whether anybody on that floor knew what to do with a person watching: about four did not and about two said a thing to each other about it. How long since he had watched a run: about four years. Whether he was on the company's list: no, and he said so, and he was told to sit at the back. What he asked: one question, in about four seconds, in the corridor afterwards, of a person who works on that floor, which was what the run was for. The answer: that it is so a person who needs something from that company knows who to ask and what to say, and that it takes nine minutes and they do it every week. Whether the answer was correct: yes, and nobody improved on it, and nobody asked the woman who called the run. What he did next: filled in about five fields of his own company's form on a phone, on the ramp, correctly, in about four minutes, and that form is not a rate and is not on anything anybody in Crown Terrace will read. What he said afterwards, unasked, to nobody in particular, in about nine words: that he had done it the way his company does it, that it goes in a file, and that nothing in that room will be different on Monday because he wrote it down, and that he was not going to pretend otherwise. Times anybody improved on it: none. Times anybody thanked him: none. Times anybody corrected him: none. Times anybody sent it anywhere that matters: none, and the person it went to did not read it that week. Whether he has been back: no, and that is what makes the thing a thing and not a favour. Owner: him, and a form, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody said how long the run took and nobody asked, and about four people on that floor have an opinion about how long it took and about four have a different one and no one has measured either. The post at the corridor end has carried nothing for seventeen weeks and nobody in that building has asked for it to be filled, and the laminated sheet has been in a drawer that does not lock for the same seventeen weeks and nobody has asked to see it. Nobody on that floor has ever been told what any Friday in that building was for by anybody, and the man of thirty-nine asked once and was answered by somebody who was not the woman who called the run. A hardboard on two nails carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, and the card in a rail in Lattice Ward did not move, and a wall list in a plastic sleeve in that shop is a different object. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Friday. Nobody in that building and nobody four miles away has been told about the other one. The Exchange did not sit and its book did not grow. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 407.**
