# Chapter 402 — A Man Who Holds Four Buildings Says One Thing About A Rail, And A Man With A Book Reads A Meter

The Wednesday morning of week one hundred and forty-nine was a Wednesday, and the one thing that is true of the evening of it is that **a man of about thirty-three, who has held four buildings and about nine hundred doors for two hundred and two days with no stamp and no reference and no date and nobody's authority behind him, said in a warm room off a service road the truest sentence anybody has said in this case in about two hundred days, and it was about a rail of card slots at the end of a counter in a repair shop in a district four miles away, and nobody wrote it down and nobody asked him to say it again and nobody thanked him.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two, then four miles away for about three hours on a rota he did not write, and then at a counter in a room off a service road until about six. The word was at nine and ten, and nobody has been told it. The heating is on and costs more, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved. The room off that service road is five hundred and sixty-nine days old and it is warm.

The card in a rail in a repair shop in Lattice Ward is five hundred and seventy-three days old, which is four days more than the room is, and that building is in another district and neither building was in the other one tonight.

---

He came in at about half past six and the kettle had been on since about five and the room had about nine people in it and a cook of twenty-seven at a sink saying nothing at all.

There are four rooms and a back one, and about four of the nine people in that room on that evening had been in it before and about five had not, and neither of those two numbers is on anything. There is a board over the door with four words on it in chalk in a woman's hand, and the chalk is nearly gone and nobody has rubbed it out and nobody has written it again. There is a hardboard on two nails carrying nineteen lines, of which the newest is a lift mechanic's and is the only promise on it. There is a ring binder on a shelf with eight things in it. There are nine chairs. There is a counter with nothing on it, and there has been nothing on that counter on any Wednesday for about a year. There is a kettle. There is a bell-push on the wall by the door that does not ring by itself and has never rung by itself and is not reported as broken, and about four people in this room know it is there, and about four do not, and no one has ever asked the two groups whether they noticed.

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty, and nobody asked her anything, and nobody thanked her, and nobody apologised to her, and the page that is hers is the eighth of the eight things in the binder and it is unread and it was not read on Wednesday, and she comes for the temperature and the temperature costs more than it did, and both of those are correct.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in the room and had said nothing since he sat down.

The man of about thirty-three came in at about seven and sat at the end of the second bench and did not say anything for about nine minutes, and about four people noticed the nine minutes and about four did not.

---

He said it at about ten past seven, and it is worth printing exactly, because it is the only long thing anybody said in that room on that evening, and because a sentence of about four seconds would not have carried it, and because about nine people were in that room and about four of them heard all of it.

"**I have been looking after four buildings and about nine hundred doors for two hundred and two days, and I have needed nothing off anybody and I have been given nothing, and I have not asked anybody for anything in any of it, and here is the thing I have worked out and I have not said to anybody yet. Nobody finds anything in four buildings because nobody is looking for anything. That is the whole of it. I can walk through nine hundred doors this month and every one of them will be shut, and not one person behind any of them is hiding, and not one of them is stopping me, and every one of them will tell me the building is fine if I ask, because that is what a person says when they have been asked and not when somebody is looking. And it is the same in a rail of card slots at the end of a counter, and it is the same on a board on two nails, and it is the same on you, Marek, and I am not saying that to be unkind, I am saying it because you are the only person in this room who is findable in four places and you are the reason about four people are afraid of a knock, and a line a man of twenty-nine wrote on that board has stood there for two hundred and sixty-five days and nobody has done a thing about it, and that is not because anybody decided not to. It is because a line on a board is not a thing anybody is looking for either. Nobody writes it down because nobody writes anything down here, and if it were written down it would be on a thing, and if it were on a thing somebody would be able to say that somebody should look at it, and there is nowhere in this city to say that to, and I have looked, and there is not one. That is my two hundred and two days. Nobody asked me for it and I am not going to be thanked for it and I would like it written on nothing at all.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

**Nobody wrote it down. Nobody asked him what the cost of it was, and nobody asked him to say it again, and nobody thanked him, and nobody said he was right. About four people in that room heard all of it. About four heard the first two sentences and the rest of it was said over a kettle, and nobody improved on it, and nobody in that room has said since that he was right, and nobody has said since that he was wrong.**

**He has needed nothing and been given nothing in two hundred and two days, which is twenty-eight weeks and six days, and nobody has renewed any asking and nobody has stopped it, and he said his whole of it in one go to about nine people and got nothing back for it, and he is the subject of one row in this case and he is not thanked.**

**The rail he was talking about is in another district and there is a card in it that is five hundred and seventy-three days old and the hardboard he was talking about is on two nails in the room he was sitting in, and the two of them have never been in one room with each other and are not in one room now, and the sentence he said held them apart and did not join them, and that is the only thing in it that was careful.**

---

At about half past seven a man of about forty-seven came in who reads meters for four buildings in that district, and he has read them for nineteen years, and nobody in that room had ever seen him in it before and nobody has seen him in it since.

He went down the passage to where the meter is, and he did not ask anybody for anything, and he came back with the number in his own book and the book is about the size of a hand and has no heading on the front of it and belongs to him.

He said it, unasked, standing by the counter with nothing on it, and it took him about nine seconds.

"**That is the right reading and I have written it in my own book like I have written it every Friday for nineteen years, and nobody has ever once asked me what the number says, and it does not matter what it says, and that is the job.**"

**Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him to read a second meter. Nobody has ever asked him to come back on a different evening, and he does not come on a different evening, and nobody in that room will ever know his name, and about four people have since described the evening and about four have described the meter, and the two groups have not met.**

**He changed nothing. He was owed nothing. He is not a hero and not a villain and is not in anybody's case, and about four people in that room have since said that a man came and read a thing and went, and about four have said nothing, and neither of those two groups has met, and no one in that room asked him for a second evening and none of them ever will.**

---

At about ten to eight a man who comes in on most Wednesdays asked the man of twenty-two whether a thing on the wall in his own building was the right one, and had brought it with him in a carrier bag, and the man of twenty-two said no.

"**I am not going to look at it. If I look at it then it is a thing I have looked at, and then you will want to know what I thought, and then it is a thing you will bring back.**"

The man said, "**You could just tell me if it is the right one.**"

"**No.**"

**Nobody said anything. Nobody said that he had been right, and nobody said that he had been unkind about it, and the man took the carrier bag away with him and did not bring it back, and about four people in that room had an opinion about that exchange and about four had not, and the two groups did not discuss it and never have, and nobody improved on it, and the bag went home with the man and the wall stayed as it was.**

---

**They came and went until about ten. A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was not in that room and has not been in it, and on the next working day of the week she will state a limit in about four seconds and give no reason for it. A man of fifty-three who works at a counter in another district was not in that room, and the rail at the end of his counter is in a building this room has never been in. A man of twenty-nine who works on lifts was not in that room, and his line is on the two nails and is two hundred and sixty-five days old and is the only promise on them, and nobody in that room said his name and he was not there to be named.**

**Said one thing in about a minute, said nothing else for about nineteen years of Fridays, said no in one word to a man with a carrier bag, and read a meter in a passage in about four minutes, and the room shut at about ten with nineteen lines on two nails exactly as it was at half past six, and a fortnight that a cook laid out is still paid for by nobody, and the man of about thirty-three has now been holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for two hundred and two days and has been given nothing and has needed nothing and nobody has thanked him for either.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The one thing said in the room, as a record of about a minute and no writing.* **Who: a man of about thirty-three, holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for two hundred and two days, that being twenty-eight weeks and six days, with no stamp, no reference, no date and nobody's authority behind him. When: about ten past seven on the Wednesday evening, after about nine minutes of not saying anything. How long it took: about a minute, and it is the only thing said in that room that evening that was not four seconds or nine seconds. What it was about: a rail of card slots at the end of a counter in a repair shop in a district four miles away; a board on two nails in the room he was sitting in; and being findable, which he gave as the reason about four people are afraid of a knock. Whether it was correct: it is not known and nobody asked, and it is the only sentence anybody in this case has said that somebody has offered to write on nothing at all. Written: nothing, and there is no pen in that room that is not somebody's own. Asked him to say it again: nobody. Asked him what it cost: nobody. Thanked: nobody. Said he was right: nobody. Heard all of it: about four. Heard the first two sentences: about four, and the rest of it was said over a kettle. Whether it joined a card in a rail to a board on two nails: no, and it did not have to, and they are a third object and a fourth and neither of them was in the other's room tonight. Owner: him, and about four people who heard, and nobody.**

*The meter, as a record of a correct thing that changed nothing.* **Who: a man of about forty-seven who reads meters for four buildings in that district and has read them for nineteen years, and who is not in anybody's case and is owed nothing. When: about half past seven, on his own way, unasked. What he did: went down the passage to the meter in the room off that service road, read it, wrote the number in his own book, which is about the size of a hand and has no heading on the front of it and belongs to him. What he said, in about nine seconds, standing by a counter with nothing on it: that the reading is right and that he has written it in his own book the way he has written it every Friday for nineteen years, and that nobody has ever once asked him what the number says, and that it does not matter what it says, and that this is the job. Whether it was correct: yes. Whether it changed anything: no, and it was not meant to. Times anybody improved on it: none. Times anybody thanked him: none. Times anybody asked him to read a second meter: none. Times anybody asked him to come back on a different evening: none, and he does not, and about four people in that room have not given him their names and he has not given them his. Owner: him, and his own book, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody thanked the man of thirty-three and nobody wrote him down, and the only thing he got for two hundred and two days is a sentence that four people heard. A hardboard on two nails carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and the card in a rail in Lattice Ward did not move, and a wall list in a plastic sleeve in that same shop is a different object and is not that board.
