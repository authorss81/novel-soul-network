# Chapter 401 — Two Cards In A Rail, And A Place-Name On One Of Them

The Monday morning of week one hundred and forty-nine was a Monday like the several hundred Mondays before it, and the one thing that is true of it is that there were **two cards in a rail at the end of a counter in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, where there has been one for as long as the man of fifty-three who stands at that counter has stood at it, and nobody had looked at the second one for about two years because it belongs to nobody in that shop and a rail is not a register, and on one side of it there is a place-name in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in that building, and on the other side there is a week-number with nothing written after it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. He was at the bench in Lattice Ward until about two and then at the counter until about six. The word was at nine and ten, and he has said it out loud to one person in nine volumes and is not going to say it to anybody else. The heating is on and costs more, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved. The room off a service road is five hundred and sixty-seven days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and nobody asked them anything.

The card in the rail in that shop is five hundred and seventy-one days old, which is four days more than the room is, because the card went to its rail on the Thursday of the week before the room opened and it has been in that building ever since.

---

He was on the bench from about half past seven and the shop was doing what it does.

There is a bench along one wall with a lamp on an arm above it, and a parts cage behind the bench, and a shutter that goes down at about six, and a shelf of finished work waiting on people who have not come for it yet. There is a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it that has never been opened in front of anybody in this case. Along the front of the counter there is a rail of card slots, and the rail is about the length of a forearm, and it is not a register, and it has never been a register, and there is no book behind it anywhere in that building.

He had eleven jobs on the Monday and did ten of them before two, and about four of the ten were the same fault in four different buildings, and the eleventh was a catch on a fire door in a building he has not been inside, and he went to it and came back from it in under an hour, and the man who let him in said thank you and he said it was nothing, and it was nothing. It is worth saying that about nine hundred doors in four buildings in this city carry a number off a line of somebody else's list, and that not one of them is on a card in a rail at the end of a counter in Lattice Ward, and that no chapter of anybody's week is going to make those two facts sit near each other.

At about half past one, on about the ninth job of the morning, the man of fifty-three came back to the counter with his tea and found the man of twenty-two standing at the end of it with the rail in front of him, and he looked at the rail and then at him.

"**You are stood at my rail like it is a queue.**"

"**It is a queue of two.**"

The man of fifty-three put his tea down and looked at the rail again, and then he said the thing that had been in his mouth all morning, which was that there was one card in it in the spring and that he had not put the second one in.

"**I did not put that one in. It came in with a bundle of work in a box off a van, and it went into the rail because the rail is where the cards go, and I have never once known who owns it and I am not going to start knowing now.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment. There was a kettle in the back and it clicked off.

The man of twenty-two put his hand flat on the counter and not on the rail, which is a thing he decided to do at the time and did not afterwards tell anybody he had decided.

"**How long has it been in there.**"

"**Since before you started.**"

"**Somebody has looked at it.**"

The man of fifty-three picked his tea up.

"**I have looked at it. That is not the same and you know that is not the same.**"

---

The second card is about the size of a playing card and is the ordinary stock of about four hundred businesses, and it is renewable and removable, and it carries no title and no office and nothing at all on the back of it. The first card in the rail belongs to the shop and has been there since the shop had a counter. The second one has a place-name written across the middle of the front in a ballpoint that has gone brown at the thick of the strokes, and the name is **NETHERBECK**, written the way a name is written when somebody is writing it onto a docket or a wrapper or the back of an envelope, without a county and without a district and without anything that says where the county is.

The hand is not the hand of the man of twenty-two and it is not the hand of anybody in that building, and he has never seen it anywhere, and he has no idea at all whose it is, and he did not say a name in that shop and was not asked for one.

The other side carries a week-number and then a space about the length of a thumb, and nothing after it, and nobody has ever written anything in that space, and there is no line ruled there and no field and no box and no name for the space. It is not a form. It is a card in a rail, and about four hundred businesses in this city issue a card of that size and about nine of those four hundred have ever had two of them in one rail at the same time, and neither of those figures is on a form either.

He stood with it for about four minutes. In about four minutes a person can work out most of what there is to work out about a piece of card, and what there was to work out was this: that it is not addressed, it is not numbered, it is not filed, it is not anybody's, and there is no procedure anywhere in that shop or in that building or in that district for turning somebody ought to look into somebody looks. A man of fifty-three looked at it. A man of twenty-two looked at it. Nobody in the history of that rail has ever been *asked* to look at it, and being asked and looking are two different acts and only one of them is a thing a day can require.

Then he said the thing he had been building since about half past one, and it was about nine seconds and he did not have it ready before he said it.

"**If somebody asked me to look after this card, I would not know where to put the answer. I have nowhere to put it and no way of knowing afterwards whether I have put it there.**"

The man of fifty-three said, in about four seconds, "**Nobody is going to ask you to look after it.**"

"**I know that.**"

"**Then it is not a cost.**"

"**It is a cost if it is asked.**"

The man of fifty-three did not agree and did not disagree, and wiped a place on the counter that was already clean, and then, out of nowhere and without looking up, he said the other half of it, and it took him about nine seconds.

"**Eight years I have been the end of that rail. People put cards in it and take them out and I have never once been asked to keep one, and I could not tell you in a morning which ones I have had. That is the whole of what I know about it, and I am fifty-three, and that is a thing to be.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him what it had cost him to be the end of a rail, because he had said it, and nobody asked him to say it again, and the man of twenty-two offered nothing and said nothing back, and he did not say that he would come back and find out. Said one thing about it, out loud, unasked, and nobody improved on it, and about four people in that shop have since described the counter and about four have described the rail, and the two groups have not met.

---

A young one came in at about ten past two with a bag of trim off his own car and wanted to be shown, in about four minutes, how to take a hinge off a door without marking the frame, and the man of twenty-two said no.

He said it in about four seconds and he did not soften it and he did not give a reason, and the young one said *you do it every day* and he said, "**I do it every day and I am not going to stand here and show you how, and that is the whole of my answer to you today.**"

Nobody said anything. The man of fifty-three went and opened the inner bolt of the shutter about twenty minutes early, because it was a Monday and there was nothing else to do, and the young one took his trim and went, and nobody said that the man of twenty-two had been unkind about it, and nobody said he had been right about it either. He is twenty-two and he has said no to about four requests to show somebody how to do something in the last year and about nine of them were reasonable, and neither of those figures is a rate.

---

About half past six the shutter came down and Marek walked four miles to a room off a service road in the Lower Wards, and the room was warm, and about four people were in it for the temperature and were not asked anything by anybody.

**The rail is at the end of a counter in one building. The hardboard is on two nails in a second building, four miles off and on the other side of the district. The tool roll with the fourth pocket is in the first building and is about four feet from where the rail is and has never been in the other one. None of those three has ever been in one room with either of the other two, and the reason is not a rule anybody made. It is that they are three things and there is nowhere in this city that they would be put together.**

In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth thing in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

The nineteenth line on that hardboard is a man of twenty-nine's. Eleven words, signed, about the middle. It is two hundred and sixty-three days old and it is the only promise on that board, and a card in a rail in another district has not made it more or less true, and nothing was written on two nails anywhere on this Monday.

**A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink and said nothing. A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was in that room and said nothing, and nobody has asked him about anything for one hundred and forty-eight days, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it. A man of about thirty-three was not in the room and has not been in it since the last Wednesday, and nobody has asked him for anything and nothing has been given to him.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The two cards, as a record of a rail with no order.* **What the rail is: about the length of a forearm, fixed to the front of a counter in a repair shop, in slots, and it is not a register, it has never been a register, and there is no book behind it anywhere in that building. Cards in it on this day: two. Cards in it before this day: one, and that one is the shop's own, and that has been the state of it for as long as anybody can account for, which is about eight years. How the second arrived: in a box of work off a van, before the man of twenty-two started working there, and it went into the rail because the rail is about where cards go, and about four hundred businesses in this city issue a card of that size, and nobody in that shop has ever been told which of the four hundred issued this one. Front of the second card: a place-name in a ballpoint gone brown at the thick of the strokes, written without a county and without a district and in the form a name takes on a wrapper or on the back of an envelope. Back of it: a week-number and then an empty space about the length of a thumb, with nothing in it, and no line ruled there and no field and no box and no name for the space. Whose the hand is: not known, and it is not the hand of the man of twenty-two, and it is not the hand of anybody in that building, and about four people in that shop have since said that it looks old, and nobody has compared it with anything. No name was said in that shop on this day and none was asked for. Whose the card is: not stated, and the man of about fifty-three at that counter has never once been asked to keep one and could not in a morning say which he has had. Where it went at about ten to six: back into the rail, into a slot that is not recorded, so that there is no slot it came from and no slot it goes to, and about nine people in that district would not be able to say which of about forty slots it is in either. Times anybody has looked at it: the man of fifty-three, and once today by the man of twenty-two. Times anybody has been asked to look at it: none, in the history of that rail. Times anybody has written anything about it: none, and nobody has anywhere to write it, which is about the whole of it. Times it has been photographed: none. Times anybody has been asked to keep it: none. Whether a rail of card slots is findable by anybody who is not looking for something: no, and nobody has ever needed it to be findable.
