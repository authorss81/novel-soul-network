# Chapter 403 — One Door And Not Two, In About Four Seconds, With No Reason Given

The Thursday morning of week one hundred and forty-nine was spent in a yard and a stairwell in Lattice Ward, at the doors of a building a man of about thirty-three has held for two hundred and three days with nobody's authority behind him, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors said a limit out loud, in about four seconds, and gave no reason, and made the not-giving the whole of the condition, and the man of twenty-two took it and said nothing back and did not ask for the second one and did not ask her to write it down and did not say he would manage, and about four people in that yard noticed and none of them said anything.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. He was in a yard and a stairwell in Lattice Ward from about ten until about half past eleven, and at a bench from about half past twelve until about six. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. The heating is on and costs more, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved. The room off a service road is five hundred and seventy days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and nobody asked them anything.

The card in a rail in a repair shop in that same district is five hundred and seventy-four days old, which is four days more than the room, and it did not move on this day and nobody looked at it, and the building it is in is not the building he was standing outside.

---

He had been asked about this door about four times this year. Once in the spring by a man who wanted a cupboard fixed in a kitchen in the same building. Once in the summer by a woman who wanted a window done and who gave up. Once in the autumn by a man of about twenty-eight who wanted to know whether the person who signed the lease would talk to him, and the answer had been that the lease is not his. And once about nine days ago by a person whose business it was and who was told to come back on a Thursday.

**There is a yard about the size of a tennis court, a stairwell with about six doors off the first landing, a bin store with a door that does not shut properly, and a blue door at the far end that is the one anybody wants. There is no sign on it, and there is no number on it, and it does not carry a number of its own, and about nine hundred doors in four buildings in this city carry a number off a line of somebody else's list and not one of them carries a number that anybody in that building can read.**

The woman of about thirty-four comes on a round and it is her whole job and she has done it for about six years, and she does not have a clipboard about nine hundred doors carrying a number off a line of somebody else's list, and she does not have a form, and she has never been asked to keep a record of what she has opened and what she has not.

She came in the gate at about ten past ten with a key ring on a hook at her belt and about nine of the other keys on it are not for this building.

There is no way to stand in a yard like that and wait to be a person somebody has to deal with. That is worth about nine seconds of thinking on a Thursday morning and the man of twenty-two did it while he was waiting. You can be a person who is owed something, which gets you nothing and is fair. You can be a person who has been refused before, which gets you nothing and is not fair and is not a claim. You can be a person somebody said no to about four times this year, which is a fact and not an argument. What you cannot be is a person who has waited longer, and the yard is full of people who have waited longer, and about four of them were in it that morning.

**The building itself is four buildings' worth of somebody else's money and nobody's idea. A man of about thirty-three has kept it standing for two hundred and three days, which is twenty-nine weeks, with no stamp and no reference and no date and nobody behind him, and he has needed nothing and been given nothing, and the doors on it carry a number off a line of somebody else's list, and the woman with the key ring has opened some of those doors and closed them again and has never once asked him anything, and he has never once asked her anything, and neither of those two facts is going to change this morning.**

---

**She got to the blue door at about half past ten and he was already standing about four feet away from it with his hands in his pockets, and she did not stop for him and she did not start a conversation, and she put the key in and turned it and the door came open on the second try, and she stood in the doorway with it held behind her, and about four people in that yard went on with what they were doing and about four looked over.**

He said the thing he had come to say, and it took him about nine seconds and it was about the spring and the cupboard and the autumn and the window.

She listened to all of it. Then she said, in about four seconds:

"**I will open this one today. I will not open a second one this week, and I am not going to be asked why, and you are not going to ask me again next week either.**"

---

**He said nothing.**

That is the whole of what he did and it is worth being exact about it, because it is the sort of thing a person can get wrong by accident and he did not get it wrong by accident. He did not ask for the second one. He did not say that a second one would be easier. He did not ask her to write it down. He did not say he would come back. He did not say he would manage. He did not say thank you, because she had not thanked him and had not given him anything, and a man who thanks a woman for a limit in about four seconds has turned her limit into a gift and she had not offered one.

He said nothing for about nine seconds, and then he said, in about four seconds, that it was all right.

She said, "**It is not all right. It is a limit. Those are two different things and I would rather you had the second one.**"

He said, "**All right.**"

And then he said thank you, and she said no, and he took that as well.

There were about four other people in that yard that morning, and about four of them noticed that the man of twenty-two had not turned a limit into a task, and about four did not notice anything at all, and the two groups have never spoken about it, and one of the four who noticed has since said that he was careful and the other three have said nothing.

**Nothing was resolved. The condition that a man of twenty-two can hear a limit in a room and not turn it into a job has been on the page five times now, and it was paid once, in a workroom on a Wednesday four months ago, in about four minutes, in two sentences about where a load goes and where a desk goes, and nobody in any room has remarked on either fact since, and neither of those two facts is on anything at all. This is not that afternoon, and nobody who was in that yard has ever said that it was, and it is not going to be.**

---

Then she said the other thing, and it was unasked, and it took her about nine seconds, and she said it with her back to the stairwell door.

"**I am the one who says it, and there is nobody above me in this building on a Thursday, and nobody who can come in afterwards and say I was right or that I was wrong. That is the cost of it. I have been the one who says it for about six years and about nine hundred doors and nobody has ever once asked me what it is to be the one who says it.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked her. Nobody asked her whether she would reconsider, and nobody asked her to justify the limit, and nobody asked her what the two hundred and three days of a man who holds this building without anybody's authority behind him had to do with her round, and the man of twenty-two did not say that the cost was a hard thing to carry and he did not offer to take any of it and he did not say he would be back next week.

**She locked the blue door behind her and went on to about nine more doors in that building and about four in the next one, and she did not look back at him, and he did not follow her, and about four people in that yard have since said that the man was sensible and about four have said nothing at all about it and neither of those two groups has met.**

---

He went and got a bus and was at a bench at about half past twelve and did eleven jobs. Two of them were in the same building he had been standing outside at ten o'clock, and he did not mention that to anybody and nobody asked him, and the man of fifty-three who works at a counter in a different part of the same district was at that counter at about two and did not know any of it.

**A man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts came in at about half past three and asked him a question about a bracket and got the same bracket as last time, and neither of them said anything else, and about four people in that shop have said that he is a good man for a part and about four have said he is slow, and both of those are about the part and neither of them is about anything else.**

At about half past six the shutter came down and he walked four miles and sat down in a warm room in which about nine people were not asked anything, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink, and in the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question and nobody thanked and nobody apologised to.

**Two nails in that room carried the same nineteen lines, and they have been the same nineteen since the first of them was written. The newest of them is a lift mechanic's — eleven words, signed, about the middle — and it is the only promise on that board and it is two hundred and sixty-six days old. Nothing was added to it on this Thursday, and nothing was removed, and nothing was rubbed off, and a limit spoken in a yard in one district eight hundred yards from that room has not made it more or less true. The card in a rail in Lattice Ward is a different object and is in a different building and was not in that room and has never been in it. The wall list in a plastic sleeve in that shop is a third thing and has never been on two nails.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
