# Chapter 407 — The Second Of Two Fridays, And A Fire Door Held Open With A Folded Sheet Of Paper

The Friday evening of week one hundred and fifty was the second Friday this month that building ran, and the last Friday before the run stops for the summer, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about thirty-nine who buys training for the company that runs that floor did not come, and no reason was given and nobody asked for one, and the run went again for about nine minutes with nobody saying how long it took, and a man of about fifty-eight who drives that building's van said out loud, unasked, at about nine o'clock, in about nine words, that he had done it right and that the door is a fire door and that the place he told is the place you tell and that it changes nothing until somebody with a van comes, and nobody thanked him and nobody moved the paper and nobody told the company.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and then at a counter until about six. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. The heating is on and costs more, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved. The room off a service road is five hundred and seventy-eight days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Friday evening for the temperature and nobody asked them anything.

The card in a rail in a repair shop in Lattice Ward is five hundred and eighty-two days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it did not move on this day and is four miles from that floor.

---

The same floor. The same about nine folding chairs. The same corridor, the same stair, about nine doors along the corridor that are not opened during a run, one desk by the fire door with four drawers and the second drawer on the left of it not locked.

**The about nine doors along that corridor are offices, or the doors of offices, and a run does not go into any of them, and nobody on that floor has ever asked why not. About four people in this case have noticed in about four years that a training run in a corridor of about nine closed doors is a thing that happens outside nine rooms rather than inside them, and about four have not noticed, and neither group has said anything about it, and it is not going to be said tonight either.**

**The woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard has been on that floor on eight of the ten Fridays that building has run this spring. On two of the ten she was not, and no reason was given for either and none was asked for, and about four people on that floor have noticed and about four have not, and she has never mentioned either Friday to anybody.**

There is a post at the corridor end with nothing on it, and there has been nothing on it for one hundred and twenty-six days, which is eighteen weeks. In the second drawer on the left of that desk there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and it has been in there for the same one hundred and twenty-six days, and nobody in that building has asked to see it and nobody has asked for it to go back on the post.

About eleven people were on the floor, about seven of them on the company's list and about four on none.

**The seven come up that stair because their employer has their name on a piece of paper about four miles away, and the four come because somebody told them it was worth a Friday, and the second of those two reasons is the only one anybody on that floor has ever given for anything, and it was given in about four seconds in the corridor by a man of about fifty-two who does not work for the company and has not been on that floor since the spring. Nobody asked him where he had heard it. Nobody has asked him since.**

**The man of about thirty-nine was not on it. Nobody said he was not coming. Nobody said he had been and had not. Nobody has ever said, in that building or anywhere else, that he came once in about four years and wrote it up in his own company's form and said out loud that it would change nothing, and that is not a withholding. It is that nobody in that building has anything to connect the two Fridays with except a wall calendar, and the wall calendar is not on the floor.**

---

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard.

**Nobody said how long it took. Nobody asked. Nobody referred to the other Friday, and nobody has ever asked the about four people on that floor who are on nobody's list why they come, and nobody has ever chased anybody in that room and nobody did it tonight, and about nine people went up that stair at about half past six and about nine people came down it at about nine and the two numbers are the same and nobody counted either.**

**At about eight something a woman of about twenty-eight who is on the company's list said one ordinary sentence about a fire door, and it was about the fire door, and nobody took it up, and the man of about fifty-eight who drives that building's van was not in the corridor at the time and did not hear it and has not been told what was said.**

**What went somewhere afterwards and what did not. The write-up the man of thirty-nine did last time went into a file in a building four miles away and it was not read that week, and nothing in that room is different because of it, and that is the whole of what happened on the Friday before. It is also going to be the whole of what happens on this one, and about four people in that building have said the word nothing out loud about both Fridays and about four have said nothing at all, and neither group has counted anything.**

**A man of about thirty-three held four buildings and about nine hundred doors for two hundred and eleven days with nobody's authority behind him, which is thirty weeks and a day, and he has never once been in that building in Crown Terrace and knows nobody on that floor and has not been told a thing about it.** He was four miles away at about half past seven on the Friday and was holding a door open for a woman with a pram on a service road, and neither of those two facts is in the other one's building, and that is the arrangement and it is not going to change this month.

---

At about nine o'clock, on the ramp, a man of about fifty-eight who drives that building's van was standing by the fire door at the end of the corridor with a folded sheet of paper wedged under it.

That fire door has not latched by itself since a Tuesday. He reported it in the correct place. The correct place has not replied.

**It is worth being exact about the reporting, because it is the whole of the thing. He told the person whose job it is, on the Tuesday, in about four seconds, and he told them the thing that is wrong and not the thing he thinks should be done about it, and he asked for nothing, and he did not chase it on the Wednesday or the Thursday, and on Friday he found a piece of paper in a drawer by the desk and put it under the door so that the corridor is not left standing open all night by a wind, which is what was happening, and which is a thing about a wind and not about anybody.**

He said it, unasked, to nobody in particular, and it took him about nine seconds.

"**I have done it right. It is a fire door. The place I told is the place you tell. It changes nothing until somebody with a van comes.**"

**Nobody thanked him. Nobody moved the paper. Nobody photographed it, and nobody told the company, and the paper was still there on Tuesday and it is still there now, and about four people in that building know it is there and about four do not and the second group includes the woman of about thirty-six with the lanyard.**

He is not a hero. He is not a villain. He is a man of about fifty-eight who drives a van and is owed nothing and is not in anybody's case and will not be asked about it again, and about four people in that building have since said that the door should be fixed and about four have said that the paper should not be there, and the two groups have never spoken and are not going to.

**Nobody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and there is nothing to read, and that is not a thing anybody has gone looking for and not found. It is a thing nobody has ever put anywhere, and about four hundred competent people in this city can each read a printed paragraph and not one of them has anywhere to put an objection, and about nine of those four hundred have stood in that corridor and none of them has.**

---

**The chairs went up at about nine and the stair emptied and the corridor was a corridor again and a man of about twenty-eight who works in that building put the lights off in an order that has nothing to do with anything.** Nothing in that floor had changed, and nothing in the room four miles off a service road had changed either, and a hardboard on two nails there carried the same nineteen lines it carried on Monday, of which the newest is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is two hundred and seventy-four days old.

Nobody thanked anybody.

Friday of week 150, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is the second of two runs, a post bare for eighteen weeks, a laminated sheet in an unlocked drawer for the same eighteen weeks, a fire door held open with a folded sheet of paper by a man who is right, and nothing joined to anything.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench until about two and a counter until about six. People on the floor: about eleven, about seven on the company's list and about four on none. Who called it: a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Attendance of the run: nine. How long it took: not said and not asked. Whether any Friday on that floor was referred to: none. Whether anybody was asked why they come: nobody, and nobody has ever been chased in that room. Whether the man of about thirty-nine who came once in about four years was on the floor: no, and no reason was given and none was asked for. Who held the far fire door open, and with what: a man of about fifty-eight who drives that building's van, with a folded sheet of paper, because the door has not latched by itself since a Tuesday, and because he reported it in the correct place, and the correct place has not replied. Times anyone thanked him: none. Times anyone moved the paper: none. Times anyone photographed it: none. Times anyone told the company: none. Rate: nine and ten, uncorrected, told to nobody. Two nails, nineteen lines, and no twentieth: the twelve four hundred and ninety-eight, the thirteen four hundred and forty-nine, the fourteen four hundred and fourteen, the fifteen three hundred and ninety-three, the sixteen three hundred and sixty-eight, the seventeen three hundred and fifty, the eighteen two hundred and ninety-six, the nineteen two hundred and seventy-four. None of those eight is an anchor on this date and none of them is a day. What is on two nails has not changed: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, about fourteen names, nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off, the first of them a cook's and never moved, the newest the only promise on it. A ring binder on a shelf: eight things in it, unread, the eighth of them a folded page, and the woman's page is one of the eight and is shut in with them. Fifth pocket: empty, drawer shut. The Exchange did not sit on this day and does not sit on a Friday in nineteen years; its last sitting was the Wednesday of the fortnight before, sixteen days ago, and its next is the Wednesday of next week, twelve days from today; book fifty-three lines, telephone seventy-three, count announced thirty-one times of which twenty-six correspond, nineteen years of it, and none of the four was turned into another one. The ask: two hundred and sixty-eight days, that being thirty-eight weeks and two days, counted from the day it was asked. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 410.**

*The fire door and the paper, as a record of a correct thing that changed nothing.* **Who: a man of about fifty-eight who drives that building's van, unnamed, not a villain, owed nothing, and not in anybody's case. When: about nine o'clock, on the ramp, at the end of the corridor, unasked, to nobody in particular. What he had done before: reported that the fire door at the end of that corridor has not latched by itself since a Tuesday, in the correct place, and the correct place has not replied. What he had done tonight: wedged a folded sheet of paper under it, which is not a repair and is not a fault either and is a thing a person does when a thing will not do what it is supposed to do and the only other option is standing there. What he said, in about nine seconds: that he has done it right; that it is a fire door; that the place he told is the place you tell; and that it changes nothing until somebody with a van comes. Whether it was correct: yes. Whether it changed anything: no, and it was not intended to. Times anybody thanked him: none. Times anybody moved the paper: none. Times anybody photographed it: none. Times anybody told the company: none. Times anybody asked him why he had not chased it: nobody. Whether the paper is still there: yes, on the Tuesday, and on this Friday, and since. About four people in that building have said the door should be fixed and about four have said the paper should not be there, and the two groups have never spoken. Whether he is a hero: no. Whether he is owed anything: nothing, and he is not owed that either. Owner: him, and a folded sheet of paper, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody said how long the run took and nobody asked, and nobody referred to the other Friday. The post at the corridor end has carried nothing for eighteen weeks and nobody in that building has asked for it to be filled, and the laminated sheet has been in a drawer that does not lock for the same eighteen weeks and nobody has asked to see it. Nobody on that floor has ever been told what any Friday in that building was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one. A hardboard on two nails carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, and the card in a rail in Lattice Ward did not move, and a wall list in a plastic sleeve in that shop is a different object. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Friday. Nobody in that building and nobody four miles away has been told about the other one. The Exchange did not sit and its book did not grow. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 410.**
