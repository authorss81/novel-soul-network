# Chapter 405 — A Chair At A Table That Is Not The Other Table, And Nobody Took A Vote

The Monday evening of week one hundred and forty-nine was a Monday in which nothing happened, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of twenty-one was given a chair in a room off a service road in this city, at a table that is not the table he was at in an earlier volume, and he is on it, and it is not a reward and it is not an apology and nobody thanked anybody for it and nobody took a vote about it and no name is against the chair, and the word he said once in a room in another volume is not forgiven and is not withdrawn, and he is not a yes to anything, and both of those are true at the same time this evening and neither of them cancels the other.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward all day and came in at about half past six. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. The heating is on and costs more, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved. The room off a service road is five hundred and seventy-four days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening, none of whom was asked anything.

The card in a rail in a repair shop in Lattice Ward is five hundred and seventy-eight days old, which is four days more than the room, and it did not move on this day and is in a different building and nobody in that room has ever been in it.

---

The table in this room is not the table Rafi Pell was at in an earlier volume.

That earlier table was a table four people bought in a rented room over a kitchen, and it is in a different district and it belongs to about eleven people who own their own hours, and nothing about that arrangement was carried here and nothing about it was copied, and there is no connection between that room and this one except that one man has been in both.

This table is a table that came with the room. It has been in it since before anybody started keeping anything on two nails in that building, and it is not bought and it is not here because anybody decided.

He came in at about ten past seven with a courier's bag and put it down by the leg of the table and sat down in a chair that had been empty, and nobody moved a chair and nobody brought him a chair and nobody said that a chair was free.

**There are about nine chairs in that room and about six of them were taken that Monday, and about nine people came in over the whole evening and the ones who came in later stood.** The rest were pushed in at the angles they had been pushed in at since somebody left them, and there is no person in this case whose job it is to push chairs in, and there is no form about the chairs, and about four people in that room have noticed that the chairs are never in the same place twice and about four have not, and the second group includes the man of twenty-two.

He came in at about ten past seven because the room is warm, and he has been coming to warm rooms on Mondays for about a term, and nobody in that room has ever said to him that he might, and he has never once asked whether he might, and about nine weeks ago a woman of about thirty-one with a key in her pocket said a thing about a diary in a different volume and none of that is on tonight's page and none of it was mentioned.

---

"**Are you on the list.**"

The woman of about thirty-six who comes on a Wednesday and a Monday asked it from the kettle, and there is no list in that room and there has never been a list in that room and there is nothing to be on.

"**There is no list.**"

"**No.**"

"**Then sit down.**"

He was already down.

---

**Nobody said out loud that he had been given anything.** Nobody said it was because of the Tuesday or the van or the four thousand leaflets or the third line on the back of an invoice, and nobody said it was not because of those things either, and the one question anybody in that room has ever asked about why a courier is in a warm room off a service road on a Monday evening is a question that nobody has asked, in this volume or in an earlier one, and a question nobody has asked is not the same as a question that has been settled.

**Nobody took a vote about him. Nobody has ever taken a vote about him. A room that votes about a chair has become a thing this case has spent nine volumes refusing to become, and the man of twenty-two knows that, and he did not say it out loud, and nobody asked him to.**

**At about half past seven a man who comes in on Mondays asked him whether a thing that had to be in Saltmarket by about ten could go in a van instead, and the asking was ordinary and the answer was about nine seconds and neither of them was about the room.**

Rafi said, "**Yes, if somebody is going to be standing there at about ten anyway. If nobody is going to be standing there then it goes in a bag on the bus and the bus is slower, and the choice is not mine, it is about who is already there.**"

The man said, "**Right.**" and went out.

Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked him for it. Nobody wrote down what he said and there is nothing in that room to write it down on, and the man who went out did the thing and it was done and nobody has since asked him how it went.

---

The word came up at about half past eight, and it came up because a woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors in four buildings was in the room on her way through from a round and said, half to herself, that she was tired of being asked to be the one who says it.

Nobody answered her.

Rafi Pell said, at about four seconds, "**I had a word once, in a room, about a thing that was not the same as another thing, and nobody took it back, and nobody has ever said it was not true, and I have not asked anybody to take it back in about two years.**"

He did not say the word. He did not need to. There is not one person in that room who has not heard it.

**Nobody said it was not important. Nobody said it was over. Nobody said he had grown out of it, and nobody said he had not, and nobody improved on it, and the woman of about thirty-four said thank you, which is a thing she says to a courier about a delivery and not about a word, and Rafi said you are welcome, and the room went back to what it is on a Monday.**

He is twenty-one. He is a courier and a worker in a cooperative of about eleven people who own their own hours, which means that nobody sets a start and nobody sets a finish, and that a day with no work on it is a day with no pay on it, and that about four people in that cooperative have known both halves of that sentence since they walked in and about four learned the second half about a term ago.

He is a person other people rely on in a room this city runs, and he is also the one who was given a chair, and the room does not depend on him being in it and would not be a different room this evening if he were not, and about four people in that room have said one of those things out loud and about four have said the other, and the two have not met.

At about twenty past nine a woman of about forty asked him, in about nine seconds, whether a thing could be carried on a Sunday, and he said yes, and that the answer to that is always the same and is never about the Sunday, it is about whether a person is going to be standing at the other end of it. Nobody wrote it down. Nobody has ever written down a delivery in this case. A courier's third line on the back of an invoice is not a fifth line and has never been written on two nails in this city and is not going to be.

---

At about nine he said he had to go and do a round, and nobody asked him to stay and nobody said it was a shame, and he took his bag off the leg of the table and went.

**In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked anything, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday, and she comes for the temperature and the temperature costs more than it did.**

Two nails in that room carried a hardboard with nineteen lines on it, and the newest of them is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is two hundred and seventy days old, and nothing was added to it on this Monday and nothing was removed and nothing was rubbed off, and a courier sitting down for about an hour and a half did not make it more or less true.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
