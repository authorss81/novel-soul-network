# Chapter 409 — The Card Comes Out Of The Rail, A Name Is Said Out Loud Once In A Shop, And The Question Is Refused In About Four Seconds

The Wednesday afternoon of week one hundred and fifty-one was spent at the end of a counter in a repair shop in Lattice Ward, and the one thing that is true of it is that **the card came out of the rail and went into the coat pocket of a man of twenty-two and the rail held one card where it held two, and a name was said out loud in that shop for the first time in the whole of it, in a mouth, once, by the man of twenty-two to a man of about fifty-three who works at that counter, and the man of twenty-two asked one question and was refused in about four seconds by a person who is right, and the refusal was a limit and not a wall, and nobody improved on it and nobody thanked anybody.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. He was at the counter in Lattice Ward from about one until about six. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. The heating is on and costs more, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved. The room off a service road is five hundred and eighty-three days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and nobody asked them anything.

The card in that rail is five hundred and eighty-seven days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it is four days more than the room has ever been, and **a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up, so the figure is the same this afternoon as it was on Monday, and the object is in a coat pocket, and those are two facts about one card and not two cards.**

---

He had come in at one on a Monday and looked at it for about four minutes and put it back. He had come in on the Wednesday of the week after and asked a man of fifty-three where the heat went in this building and got an answer in about nine seconds. He had been in on the Monday of this week.

At about a quarter past one on the Wednesday he put his hand flat on the counter beside the rail and said, "**I am going to take the other card out.**"

The man of fifty-three said, "**I know which one.**"

---

It came out with two fingers. It is about the size of a playing card, it is the ordinary stock of about four hundred businesses, and it is renewable and removable, and it carries no title and no office and nothing on the back of it.

The front of it has a place-name on it in a ballpoint that has gone brown at the thick of the strokes, and the name is **NETHERBECK**, written the way a name is written onto a wrapper, without a county and without a district.

The other side carries a week-number and then an empty space about the length of a thumb, and nothing after it.

He turned it over once, in his own hands, at the end of that counter, and put it in the inside pocket of his coat, and the rail at the end of that counter holds one card where it held two at half past one, and it will hold one card for as long as nobody puts the other one back, and nothing in that shop knows that and nothing in that shop has been told it.

**Nobody photographed it. Nobody wrote it down. Nobody tore it up. Nobody shouted anybody down, and nobody in that building is a villain, and the man of fifty-three did not ask him to put it back and did not tell him to keep it.**

---

Then he said the name.

He said it standing up, at about half past one, in about four seconds, and he said it once, and it was a name of two words and the second of them is his own, and about four people in that building did not hear it and about four did, and neither of those two groups has said anything about it since.

"**That is my father's name. Evan Senn.**"

He did not say who his father was and nobody asked. He did not say when and nobody asked. He did not say what the hand on the card was, and he did not say that the hand on the card was not his own, and the man of fifty-three looked at the rail for about four seconds and then looked at the counter.

"**I have never heard that name in this building.**"

"**No.**"

"**Then it is not a thing I can do anything with.**"

"**No.**"

---

Then he asked the question, and there was one, and he said before he asked it that he was not going to come back and ask it a second time whichever way it went.

"**Where is it.**"

The man of fifty-three said no in about four seconds.

"**No. I work at a counter. I do not know what I am allowed to hand to a person, and there is nobody in this building I could ask about that, and I would rather say no than be wrong about it.**"

**Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked him to explain the not-knowing. Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong, and nobody in that building has said since that he should have answered, and nobody in that building has said since that he should not have.**

Then, unasked, about nine seconds, and he did not frame it and nobody had asked him anything:

"**Being the person a stranger asks is the whole of what this counter is. I have no way of knowing which answers I am allowed to give him, and I am not going to find that out by getting one wrong.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him to say it again. Nobody offered to find out for him whether he was allowed. The man of twenty-two did not say that it was a hard thing to be at the end of a rail and did not thank him for it and did not tell him what he was going to do instead, and he was not thanked for asking.

---

He left the shop at about two and stood on the pavement for about four minutes with a hand on a pocket.

What he had: a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a town on it in a hand that is not his own, that he cannot act on, that opens nothing, that is not a key and is not a ticket and is not a form, and that is four days older than a room he is going to walk to at half past six this evening.

What he also had: a name said out loud in a shop, in a mouth, once, to a man who refused him, correctly.

**And he had nobody to be angry at. That is the whole of the cost of this afternoon and it is about nine words long and he did not say it out loud: the man is not a villain, has done nothing wrong, is owed nothing, and is right, and there is nobody in this case to be angry at, and about four hundred competent people in this city can each read a printed paragraph and not one of them has anywhere to put an objection, and a man of twenty-two standing on a pavement in Lattice Ward is the last person in the world who can be the one to start.**

He went back in at about half past two and did two jobs and said nothing about any of it.

---

At about half past six the shutter came down and he walked four miles to a room off a service road, and about nine people were in it, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink saying nothing, and in the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and her page is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read.

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is two hundred and seventy-nine days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed and nothing was rubbed off.

**A hardboard on two nails is not a card in a rail. A card in a rail in a pocket is not the wall list in a plastic sleeve in that shop. Neither of them is a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it and a blank fifth line. None of them is the page of paper with about eleven lines on it, which is in a fourth building, or the sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts, which is not any of the others. A third object in a third building, and the two have never been in one room, and tonight one of them was in a coat pocket on a service road and the other was on two nails four miles away and neither of them knows the other exists.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
