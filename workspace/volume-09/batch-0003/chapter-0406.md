# Chapter 406 — A Van In The Morning, A Counter At Two, And A Rail Of Card Slots With No Order In It

Then the man of twenty-two said the thing he had come to say, which was about the rail.

"**There are two cards in it and there have been one for about eight years, and the second one is in about four hundred businesses' stock and it has a town on it, and nobody is keeping it, and there is no order to that rail, and there is no list behind it, and there is no number on either card, and it is not a document. **The man of about twenty-nine put the bracket down on the counter, which is the first physical thing anybody in this case has done in about a year in response to a sentence about a rail, and he did it without deciding to, and about four people have noticed that and about four have not.**

"**Then somebody has to look.**"

I have looked for about four days and there is no form anywhere in this city that says somebody ought to look at a card in a rail, and there is no room that anybody can go in and say it to, and if there were, about four hundred people could go and say it, and none of them has, and neither would I, and that is not anybody being lazy. **Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked him which rail. Nobody asked him which town. Nobody said that it was a good piece of work and nobody said that it was useless, and the man of about twenty-nine picked the bracket up again and asked him about the price of a different one, and they talked about the price of a different one for about four minutes, and that conversation is the whole of what was done with the finding in that shop on that afternoon.**

The man of about twenty-nine is not the author of a mechanism. He does not have a procedure and he does not have a list and he has not been asked to keep one. He is a person with an ordinary job who works on lifts and who bought a bracket and went home, and he is owed nothing and he was not thanked.
