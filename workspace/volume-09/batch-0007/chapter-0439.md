# Chapter 439 — The Twenty-Ninth Sitting, A Book That Opens, And A Figure Of Forty-Eight Weeks

The Wednesday of week one hundred and sixty was the twenty-ninth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and the one thing that is true of it is that **the book stood at fifty-four lines when the morning began and it stands at fifty-five lines now, and the tin did not move, and the count came out at thirty-four of which twenty-nine correspond, and the ask is three hundred and thirty-six days old on that Wednesday, which is forty-eight weeks, counted from the day it was asked and not from any sitting since, and four miles off in a different building a man of twenty-two was at a bench and knows none of what happened in that room.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. He was at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and then at a counter until about six. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and forty-six days old, and it is warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and nobody asked them anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and fifty days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten, and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

**There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. It did not move in about four hours. Nobody in that room looked at it more than once, and about four people in that room have wondered in all of that time who put it there, and nobody has ever asked, and its mover is not named and is not going to be named.**

There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, open for about four hours and not shut once.

There were about eleven people, and about four of them are not described this morning.

**She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.**

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece.

The first was a person who wanted to know whether a book that has not grown for one sitting is a thing anybody can be worried about, and she said that it is a book, and that it grows when somebody speaks to her, and that she is not going to say anything else about it, and she wrote nothing, **and nobody in that room has put two sittings together and asked her about the pair of them.**

The second was a woman who had brought a list of hours and asked whether the hours were the business of the room, and she said they were not, and she wrote nothing.

The third was a person who asked her a question about a road and she answered it in about nine seconds and wrote nothing, and the answer has not been written down by anybody and is not repeated here.

The fourth was the man of about forty-four who put the ask a long time back and has not put it since. He was in that room and he did not put it and she did not offer it and nobody in that room wanted anything offered, and about four people in that room noticed that and about four did not.

The fifth was nothing anybody has reported.

**The sixth was a man of about thirty in overalls who works on a floor in Crown Terrace, and he said a thing to her face about a stairwell light that stays on all night and the people who go up and down under it, and he did not ask for it to be written down, and she wrote it down, and nobody thanked him and nobody improved on it and there is no name against the line and he is owed nothing.**

Nobody improved on the line. Nobody said it was the wrong line. Nobody has asked him about it since, and he has not mentioned it, and about four people in that room have thought about it and about four have not, and the two groups have not met.

---

**The book was at fifty-four lines when the morning began. It is at fifty-five lines now. One line was written in it on this Wednesday and nothing was taken out of it, and a book of four columns has stood at fifty-five lines since about eleven and will stand there until somebody says a thing to her face that is not a question.**

The book on a table in a hut by a set of scales on a road on the far side of this city is a different book in a different building, and the book in a cupboard under a stair in a block of flats is a different book in a different building, and the book in a depot in this city is a different book in a different building, and none of the three has ever been in that room and this morning none of them was in the other’s.

**No number was given into the tin this morning.** The tin stands at seventy-three, and it stood at seventy-three at the last sitting four weeks ago, and it stood at seventy-three at the one before that, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved, and nobody in that room wanted a number.

The count came out as it comes out at every sitting. It is thirty-four. Twenty-nine of them are the Wednesdays this book has sat on, and five are older than the book, and the five are on a sheet she has never shown anybody and did not show this morning either.

The book is at fifty-five lines. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced thirty-four times. The whole business is nineteen years old. **Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and every one of the four is the whole of itself, and none was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and was not given as one.**

Nobody thanked her. Nobody said anything about the sitting before this one and she has not said anything about it, and nothing about that has ever been said in that room.

---

**Nobody in that room asked her for the rule about turning one figure into another. She has said six times that the last time she says it is the last time, and she has kept that six times, and this morning nothing was put in the space it left and nothing was offered in its place and nobody in that room wanted anything offered. It is the one thing anybody in this case has wanted from that room and it is not wanted today.**

---

**And the ask, and the figure, and why forty-eight weeks and not forty-seven weeks and two days.**

The ask was made by a man of about forty-four at the seventeenth sitting, on a Wednesday, and it is three hundred and thirty-six days old today, which is forty-eight weeks. It is counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since. **It is not forty-seven weeks and two days, which is a Friday’s figure and belongs to a Friday five days ago and is the row above this one and is not this one, and a load book that carries one and not the other is carrying a number for a different day.**

Nobody in that room put the ask. Nobody in that room has put it at any of the four sittings in about four months, and about four people in that room have wanted to and about four have not, and she has not offered it once and nobody has asked her for it.

---

Four miles off that landing and four miles off that room, a maintenance rota for a building of nine flats over a canteen came down off a nail again at about four in the afternoon and nobody collected it, because it is not anybody’s, and because a man of twenty-two handed it over a fortnight before a Thursday like that one and a man of about fifty-six who works for a contractor took it and has not been in that building since.

At about half past six a shutter came down on a service road and a man of twenty-two walked four miles to a room, and about four people were in it, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink saying nothing, and in the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and forty-two days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card in a third building and has been since the Wednesday it came out of that rail. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop is a fourth and is not that board. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The book in a cupboard under a stair in a block of flats is a seventh, the book in a plastic box by a set of scales on that road is an eighth, and the book on a shelf in a depot in this city is a ninth, and those three have never been in that landing and no clause in this case is going to put them there.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
