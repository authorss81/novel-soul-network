# Chapter 431 — A Van With About Nine Chairs, A Line Left Empty, And A Fourth Day Asked For

The Monday of week one hundred and fifty-eight was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a mobile clinic in the Lower Wards until about four and from about half past six in one room off a service road, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a child’s care-link was written in about nine lines with the last of the nine left empty because the person a follow-up was meant to see was not in the van, and the mother asked for a fourth day off and named what it costs out loud unasked and nobody thanked her and nobody improved on it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and thirty days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and thirty-four days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

The van stands on a patch behind a laundry with its side door open and a step down. There are about nine chairs in it and a curtain and a lamp and a board on the wall with a clip holding a sheet.

**The van is not a clinic with a room to it and it is not on anybody's list of places to go, and a person who has not been in it before will not find it by looking for it.** It is a van. It comes on a Wednesday and a Monday and it is on a patch behind a laundry because the laundry lets it stand there, and nobody has asked the laundry why and nobody has asked the van where it goes on the other four days.

A woman of twenty-four who is a resonance nurse works out of that van and has done so for about nine months. She was at the door from about ten with a pencil and a list that is not a register.

A mother came at about eleven with a child who had a cough that would not settle. The mother works cleaning four offices at night and had already lost three days in a fortnight to bring the child in.

The nurse listened and wrote and did not hurry the mother. The child sat on the second chair with a coat still on and said nothing.

About four people waited outside on crates and about four did not wait at all and went away and came back later.

---

"**She has coughed through three nights and I have lost three days bringing her.**"

"**I can see her today and I can see her again if she is brought.**"

The nurse wrote eight lines while the mother talked and left the ninth of them empty. The person a follow-up was meant to see was not in the van on Monday and nobody in that van pretended otherwise.

**The sheet on that wall is not a record of anybody's visit and there is no second copy of it and nothing about the eight lines goes anywhere after the van shuts.** The list the nurse carries is a list of who is expected and not a list of who came, and the two are not the same document, and the mother is on the first and not on the second because the second is not a thing anybody keeps. When the van shuts the sheet goes on the clip and the clip stays on the wall, and the ninth line is on it, and it is empty, and the next person to read that wall will read an empty line and not know what it was for.

The mother looked at the empty line and did not ask for it to be filled.

Then the mother said it, unasked, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular, in her own words, and it was about the days and not about the cough.

"**I have asked for a fourth day off and it is on me and nobody will pay it and I am asking anyway.**"

Nobody thanked her for that. Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her which office and nobody asked her what the fourth day would cost in wages. Nobody said she was right and nobody said she was wrong.

**She is owed nothing and she is not in anybody’s case and the fourth day is hers to lose and nobody in that van asked her to lose it.**

---

He was at the bench until about two and at the counter until about six and heard none of what happened in the van. At about half past six he walked four miles to the room and sat at the end of the second bench.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about thirty-three weeks and five days, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room and was not in this city on Monday, and his hold went on for about thirty-seven weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and twenty-six days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The sheet on the wall of the van with about nine lines on it is a seventh and it is not any of the six and it was written today.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
