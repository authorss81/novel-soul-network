# Chapter 436 — What Was Said Out Loud In Branstead To A Room That Did Not Want It

The Wednesday of week one hundred and fifty-nine was spent on a bus and in the second of the four towns and from about half past six in one room off a service road, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about fifty-eight who keeps the only written record in that town said out loud to a room that did not want it that a thing two of them do is not a rule and he cannot make it one for anybody, and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody improved on it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and thirty-nine days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and forty-three days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and it went into the second of the four towns in that pocket and came home in the same pocket and went into no rail.

---

The second of the four towns has a set of scales on a concrete pad by the road and a hut about eight feet by six with a window, a table, a stove, a kettle, a book on the table, and a box on a shelf by the door with about four hundred cards in it in plastic sleeves. Four lamps stand in the street and one of them came on in the spring that was not on in the winter.

About four people work that pad. A man of about twenty-six has been there since the spring and has been the second person on the check every single time, and that a check whose second person is always the first person is not a check has still not been said out loud by anybody.

A man of about fifty-eight keeps the only written record in that town and is not a clerk and holds no post and is owed nothing and is not thanked. He was in that hut on Wednesday with the book open and the box on the shelf.

The practice is nearly right and it is nearly right in the same way: a weight written in the book by hand and a count of pallets off a lorry by a second person against the number on the ticket, and if the two do not agree the lorry does not go. It has caught one thing in about nine years and it cannot catch a load that was wrong before it came there.

About four people were in the hut at about two. The man of about fifty-eight stood up with his back to the stove and said it to the room, and the room did not want it.

"**A thing two of us do is not a rule and I cannot make it one for anybody.**"

About four people in that hut looked at the table and about four did not look up at all, and nobody said yes and nobody said no and nobody asked him what he meant. One of them turned a card over in its sleeve and put it back. The lorry outside kept its engine running.

**He had said the same shape of thing in an earlier week to the window and then signed the one line on a card that says the person signing confirms it is done at that site, in his own name, and the two halves do not agree with each other and about four people in that hut have said so since and about four have said nothing and the two groups have not met.**

Nobody improved on what he said on Wednesday. Nobody thanked him for it. Nobody asked him why he signed when he says he cannot make anything one for anybody.

---

He came home on the bus and walked four miles to the room at about half past six. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was not in that room and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-five weeks, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room on Wednesday, and his hold went on for about thirty-eight weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and thirty-five days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The card in a plastic sleeve in a box by a set of scales in the second of the four towns is a ninth object and is merged with none of the six.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
