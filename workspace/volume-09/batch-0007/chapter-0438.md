# Chapter 438 — Six Hundred And Forty-Four Days Old On A Day That Is Six Hundred And Forty-Four, And A Limit Heard Twice

The Monday of week one hundred and sixty was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six and from about half past six in one room off a service road, and the one thing that is true of it is that **he heard the same condition a second time in public and did the same thing, and about four people in the room noticed and none of them said anything, and four miles off nothing moved except a figure that is an age and not a day.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and forty-four days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and forty-eight days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

**Four miles off nothing in this case moved, and the room is six hundred and forty-four days old, which is an age and is not a day, and six hundred and forty-four is also the day the eighteen lines were written, and both of those are true at once on Monday.**

The eighteen lines are **three hundred and sixty-two days old.** That is how long a line has been on a board, and it is an age, and it is not a day, and it is not when anything was switched on or switched off, and a person reading that figure as a day has read a thing that is not there.

**Three hundred and sixty-two is also the day the room was opened.** It was opened on a Monday a long way back with four rooms and a back one, and it has been warm ever since and it costs more than it did, and nothing about that day is on anything in this case.

**So on this Monday the room off a service road is six hundred and forty-four days old on a day when the eighteen lines are three hundred and sixty-two days old, and six hundred and forty-four is the day those lines were written and three hundred and sixty-two is the day the room was opened, and neither figure is the other, and no page in this case has ever put the two together and neither ever will.**

The other seven on that board are the twelve, at five hundred and sixty-four, the thirteen, at five hundred and fifteen, the fourteen, at four hundred and eighty, the fifteen, at four hundred and fifty-nine, the sixteen, at four hundred and thirty-four, the seventeen, at four hundred and sixteen, and the nineteen, at three hundred and forty, which is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it has been there for forty-eight weeks and four days and nothing has ever been done about it.

**There is no twentieth line. Not on this Monday and not anywhere in this week, and the reason is not restraint: a twentieth line would be a new day and a new figure and a new thing to be wrong about.**

---

He was at the bench until about two and heard nothing there. At about half past six he walked four miles to the room and sat at the end of the second bench.

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about thirty-five weeks and five days, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room on Monday, and his hold went on for about thirty-nine weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors was at the far end of the room with a folded list. She said out loud to the room that the rota for the building of nine flats over a canteen has nobody to take it and nobody to carry it through the winter.

He heard it. It was a limit and it was his own in the same way the one before it had been, and turning it into a job would have cost days he had already agreed to elsewhere. He did not turn it into a job. He said nothing and stayed where he was.

About four people in that room noticed and none of them said anything. About four did not notice at all. The cook kept her back to the room. The woman in the chair nearest the door did not look up.

**He had heard the same condition once before in public and done the same thing, and he did it again on Monday, and the condition is now on the page five times and can be paid in the days after these, and nobody in that room remarked on either fact and neither fact is on anything.**

---

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The folded list on the back of a chair is a seventh and it is not any of the six.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*441. Monday of week 160, at about ten on a service road, and this entry is a room six hundred and forty-four days old on a day the eighteen lines are three hundred and sixty-two, a limit heard a second time in public and the same thing done, about four who noticed and none who said anything, nineteen lines on two nails with the newest of them three hundred and forty days old and still the only promise on that board, and nobody thanked anybody.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and his day was a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and a counter until about six, and then a room from about half past six. That room: four rooms and a back one off a service road, warm, four names on a lease. How old it is today: six hundred and forty-four days, which is an age and not a day, and six hundred and forty-four is also the day the eighteen lines were written. What the eighteen lines are today: three hundred and sixty-two days, which is an age and not a day, and three hundred and sixty-two is also the day that room was opened. Whether both are true at once: yes, and neither is the other. Rate: nine and ten, uncorrected, told to nobody, and it has been said out loud to one person, once in all of it. Heating: on, dearer, four names on a lease dearer still, a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid and that is not resolved. Room off that service road: six hundred and forty-four days, and warm, and about nine people came in on the evening for the temperature. Card: six hundred and forty-eight days, four days more than the room, in a coat pocket, and the rail in the other building holds one card. Two nails, nineteen lines, and no twentieth: the twelve five hundred and sixty-four, the thirteen five hundred and fifteen, the fourteen four hundred and eighty, the fifteen four hundred and fifty-nine, the sixteen four hundred and thirty-four, the seventeen four hundred and sixteen, the eighteen three hundred and sixty-two, which is an age and is not a day and three hundred and sixty-two is the day that room was opened, the nineteen three hundred and forty. None of those eight save the two named is an anchor on this date and none of them is a day. What is on two nails has not changed: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, about fourteen names, nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off, the first of them a cook’s and has never moved, the newest the only promise on it, written by a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts, in its forty-eight weeks and four days. A wall list in a plastic sleeve in that same shop is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. A ring binder on a shelf: eight things in it, the eighth of them a folded page that is unread and was not read, and the woman’s page is one of the eight and is shut in with them. Fifth pocket: empty, drawer shut, and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in. The nine hand copies of the front of a page of paper: begun in their thirty weeks, about eight of the nine unfinished, and the first disagreement, a figure in the fourth line, right in six copies and wrong in three, not found and not going to be found by anybody in this case. Man of about fifty-one with a tool bag: at the wall, and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-five weeks and five days, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it. Man of about thirty-three: not in the room and not in this city, holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for about thirty-nine weeks and four days, given nothing and needed nothing, and nobody has asked him for anything and nobody has stopped it. The Exchange did not sit on this day; its last sitting, the twenty-eighth, was twenty-six days ago, and its next, the twenty-ninth, is two days from today; book fifty-four lines, telephone seventy-three, count announced thirty-three times of which twenty-eight correspond, nineteen years of it, and none of the four is convertible into another and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and is not given as one. The ask: three hundred and thirty-four days, that being forty-seven weeks and five days, counted from the day it was asked at the seventeenth sitting and not from any sitting since. What was heard in public: the same condition a second time, and the same thing done, and about four noticed and none said anything. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 441.**

*The two figures, as a record of one pair that is two things.* **What the first figure is: how long a room has been open, six hundred and forty-four days. Whether that figure is a day: no, and it is not when anything was switched on or off. What the same figure also is: the day the eighteen lines were written. What the second figure is: how long those lines have been on a hardboard, three hundred and sixty-two days. Whether that figure is a day: no. What the same figure also is: the day that room was opened. Whether both are true at once today: yes, and neither is the other. Whether anybody in this city could misread either figure in a hurry: about four could and about four could not. Whether there is a twentieth line: no. Owner: two nails, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **A hardboard on two nails carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and there is no twentieth line, and the card in a coat pocket is not that board and the wall list in a plastic sleeve in the repair shop is not that board and a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it is a fifth object and a page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth in a fourth building and a sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Monday. A drawer stayed shut and a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened and a folder in a weighbridge hut was not turned over. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in that binder and is unread. The Exchange did not sit and its book did not grow and no number was given into its tin. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 441.**
