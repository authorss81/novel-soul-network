# Chapter 437 — The Eighth Friday, A Post Bare Twenty-Seven Weeks, And Two Plain Lines

The Friday of week one hundred and fifty-nine was again a second floor in Crown Terrace from about half past six until about nine, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a run went on that floor for the sixth week running and nobody said how long it took and nobody asked, and it is also the eighth of the Fridays that building has had, and those two are not the same count and nobody on that floor has ever been given either, the post at the corridor end has now carried nothing for one hundred and eighty-nine days, which is twenty-seven weeks, one of the about four who come on no list of anything was not there on Friday and about four people on that floor noticed and about four did not.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and forty-one days old and is warm. The card is six hundred and forty-five days old, four days more than that room is, and it is in a coat pocket, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

**The shop did not know what had happened on that floor the Friday before and would not have known what happened on this one.** Eleven on the docket and eleven in date, the last of them at about two, a counter until about six, a shutter, and then four miles on foot. Nothing on that docket was for the building in Crown Terrace and nothing on it was for the company that runs the floor, and nobody who came to that counter said the name of that company, and the one time in about a year that anybody in that building has telephoned that shop it was about a fire door.

The part the woman had left on the Friday before was still on the shelf where it was put, and the piece had come in on the Monday, so it came off the shelf at about ten on this Friday and he fitted it in about half an hour. She waited and did not watch and did not thank him, and she paid and went, and he wrote the day against her name and nothing else. **He did not tell her it had been sitting on a shelf for four days and he is not going to, and the four days are not on the docket and there is no column on that docket for them.**

**The whole of the evening was on that floor. About nine people came up that stair between about half past six and about nine and about eight went down it at about nine, and that is the whole of what is known about where anybody in this case was, and he was not on it.**

---

**It was the eighth of these Fridays, and there are two more of them, and neither of the two has happened yet. The one before it was a week back and the one before that was a fortnight back and the one before that was a week back and the one before that was a fortnight back, and nobody who comes up that stair has ever been told the distances between them.**

**Two counts of them are kept here and they do not agree with each other and both are correct. This is the eighth, and it is also the sixth week of the run, and the run is counted from a different Friday than the eighth is counted from, and the gap between the two counts was the same on the Friday before this one and has been the same since the run began. Neither figure has ever been put to anybody in that company. The woman of about thirty-six with the lanyard knows it is the eighth, and the man of about thirty in overalls knows it is the sixth week of the run, and neither of them has ever said either figure out loud. The woman of about twenty-eight and the man of about twenty-eight know one of the two and not the other, and neither of them has been asked which. Nobody has ever compared the two counts in front of anybody else.**

The same floor, in the same corridor, with the same about nine folding chairs against the wall and the same desk by the near fire door and the same stair and the same stairwell light.

There was a post at the corridor end with nothing on it. It has now carried nothing for one hundred and eighty-nine days, which is twenty-seven weeks, and nobody has asked for it to be filled and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it.

**In the second drawer on the left of that desk there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and the drawer does not lock, and no separate count is printed for that sheet anywhere in this entry, because two different dates for it exist in this case and differ by twenty-eight days, and the post’s own figure above is the one that walks.**

The far fire door was still propped with a folded sheet of paper. Nobody has asked the man of about fifty-eight who drives that company’s van to take it away and nobody has ever asked him anything, and he was not in that building on Friday and has not been in it since the Friday before last.

---

About eleven people were on the floor. About seven of them are on the company’s list. About four are on none of anything, and one of those about four was not there, and he has been on that floor for four weeks running and he is not on anything, and nobody in that building has asked him anything this week and nobody noticed the gap until the chairs went up.

Nobody chased anybody. Nobody said anything about the gap. Nobody took a count of the four on no list in six weeks and nobody has ever taken one.

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Nobody said how long it took and nobody asked and nobody referred to any other Friday.

At about ten to eight a man of about thirty in overalls who is on the company’s list said one ordinary sentence about the stairwell light. It was about the stairwell light. Nobody took it up.

At about nine the chairs went up and the lights went off in an order that has nothing to do with anything.

> A thing two of us do is not a rule.
> No hand can make it one for anybody.

* * *

Four miles off, in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards, two nails carry a hardboard with nineteen lines on it, and the eight ages on it are set out in the docket below, and nothing was added to it on Friday and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

The other seven besides the newest on that board are the twelve, at five hundred and sixty-one, the thirteen, at five hundred and twelve, the fourteen, at four hundred and seventy-seven, the fifteen, at four hundred and fifty-six, the sixteen, at four hundred and thirty-one, the seventeen, at four hundred and thirteen, and the eighteen, at three hundred and fifty-nine, and the nineteen, at three hundred and thirty-seven, which is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it has been there for forty-eight weeks and one day and nothing has ever been done about it.

**There is no twentieth line. Not on this Friday and not anywhere in this week, and the reason is not restraint: a twentieth line would be a new day and a new figure and a new thing to be wrong about, and a board that has carried nineteen lines for this long does not get a twentieth because nobody has come to it with a twentieth thing to put on it and nobody has ever asked anybody to bring one, and the four who come on no list have not said on any Friday that anybody wrote down.**

---

**Nobody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one, and that is a thing nobody has ever put anywhere rather than a thing somebody has looked for and failed to find.**

The man of about thirty-nine who buys training for the company that runs that floor was not on it, and nobody said he was not coming, and nobody said he had been and had not.

Nobody thanked anybody.

*The gap on the floor, as a record of one person who was not there.* **What is on that floor on a Friday: about eleven, of whom about seven are on the company’s list and about four are on nothing at all. The run of the absent one before he was absent: four Fridays, and it had not been four Fridays before that, and it will not be five unless he is there. Whether anybody in that building noticed the gap before the chairs went up: the woman of about thirty-six with the lanyard, the man of about thirty in overalls, and two others whose names are not on anything. Whether the lanyard woman said anything to the man in overalls about it: no. Whether the man in overalls said anything to her: no. Whether either of them mentioned it on the way out: no, and they went out at about nine and the gap closed over and the chairs went up. Whether anybody has asked him why he was not there: nobody, and nobody ever has. Whether anybody has asked the woman who calls the run whether she noticed: no, and she has not said. Whether the chair he uses is a chair of his: no, it is a folding chair against that wall and there are nine of them and the nine are not in anybody's name. Whether the four on no list are four people or a number: nobody has ever counted them, in six Fridays or in one, and the count has not been taken since the floor began. Whether anyone has compared that four with the about seven on the list: no. What the absence cost: nothing anybody has said, and nothing anybody is going to be asked about, and no absence of this kind is written down. Owner: a folding chair against a wall, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **The run was not written down anywhere, and on this Friday it was not written down twice, because the one who was not there was not written down either. It is not on the laminated sheet, which is still in the second drawer on the left of that desk in a drawer that does not lock, and it is not on the wall, and the woman who called it did not write it down and has not told anybody that she called it. The post at the corridor end is a day older than it was and still bare, and nobody has asked for it to be filled and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it, and the folded sheet propping the far fire door is still propping it and still has nothing written on it. Nobody on that floor has ever been told what any Friday in that building was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard says what any Friday was.**

**A hardboard on two nails four miles off carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and there is no twentieth line, and and none of the six surfaces in this file is one of the others and none of them is that board and the six are six on this day and not five and not seven. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, a folder in a weighbridge hut was not turned over, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Friday. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in that binder and is unread.
