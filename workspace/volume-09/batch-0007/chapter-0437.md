# Chapter 437 — The Eighth Friday, A Post Bare Twenty-Seven Weeks, And Two Plain Lines

The Friday of week one hundred and fifty-nine was again a second floor in Crown Terrace from about half past six until about nine, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a run went on that floor for the sixth week running and nobody said how long it took and nobody asked, the post at the corridor end has now carried nothing for one hundred and eighty-nine days, which is twenty-seven weeks, one of the about four who come on no list of anything was not there on Friday and about four people on that floor noticed and about four did not.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and forty-one days old and is warm. The card is six hundred and forty-five days old, four days more than that room is, and it is in a coat pocket, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

**The whole of that day was on that floor. About nine people came up that stair between about half past six and about nine and about eight went down it at about nine, and that is the whole of what is known about where anybody in this case was.**

---

**It was the eighth of these Fridays. The one before it was a week back and the one before that was a week back and the one before that was a week back and the one before that was a week back, and it is the eighth and there are two more of them in the run and neither of the two has happened yet, and nobody in that company has ever put a number on a Friday in a room and about four people on that floor could tell you it was the eighth and about four could not.**

The same floor, in the same corridor, with the same about nine folding chairs against the wall and the same desk by the near fire door and the same stair and the same stairwell light.

There was a post at the corridor end with nothing on it. It has now carried nothing for one hundred and eighty-nine days, which is twenty-seven weeks, and nobody has asked for it to be filled and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it.

**In the second drawer on the left of that desk there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and the drawer does not lock, and no separate count is printed for that sheet anywhere in this entry, because two different dates for it exist in this case and differ by twenty-eight days, and the post’s own figure above is the one that walks.**

The far fire door was still propped with a folded sheet of paper. Nobody has asked the man of about fifty-eight who drives that company’s van to take it away and nobody has ever asked him anything, and he was not in that building on Friday and has not been in it since the Friday before last.

---

About eleven people were on the floor. About seven of them are on the company’s list. About four are on none of anything, and one of those about four was not there, and he has been on that floor for four weeks running and he is not on anything, and nobody in that building has asked him anything this week and nobody noticed the gap until the chairs went up.

Nobody chased anybody. Nobody said anything about the gap. Nobody took a count of the four on no list in six weeks and nobody has ever taken one.

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Nobody said how long it took and nobody asked and nobody referred to any other Friday.

At about ten to eight a man of about thirty in overalls who is on the company’s list said one ordinary sentence about the stairwell light. It was about the stairwell light. Nobody took it up.

At about nine the chairs went up and the lights went off in an order that has nothing to do with anything.

> A thing two of us do is not a rule.
> No hand can make it one for anybody.

* * *

Four miles off, in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards, two nails carry a hardboard with nineteen lines on it, and the eight ages on it are set out in the docket below, and nothing was added to it on Friday and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

The other seven besides the newest on that board are the twelve, at five hundred and sixty-one, the thirteen, at five hundred and twelve, the fourteen, at four hundred and seventy-seven, the fifteen, at four hundred and fifty-six, the sixteen, at four hundred and thirty-one, the seventeen, at four hundred and thirteen, and the eighteen, at three hundred and fifty-nine, and the nineteen, at three hundred and thirty-seven, which is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it has been there for forty-eight weeks and one day and nothing has ever been done about it.

**There is no twentieth line. Not on this Friday and not anywhere in this week, and the reason is not restraint: a twentieth line would be a new day and a new figure and a new thing to be wrong about, and a board that has carried nineteen lines for this long does not get a twentieth because a writer needs something to publish.**

---

**Nobody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one, and that is a thing nobody has ever put anywhere rather than a thing somebody has looked for and failed to find.**

The man of about thirty-nine who buys training for the company that runs that floor was not on it, and nobody said he was not coming, and nobody said he had been and had not.

Nobody thanked anybody.

*440. Friday of week 159, at about ten on a second floor in Crown Terrace, and this entry is one run of about nine minutes for the sixth week running, the eighth of these Fridays, a post bare for twenty-seven weeks, a laminated sheet in a drawer that does not lock with no count printed for it, one of about four who come on no list not there and about four who noticed, and four miles off nineteen lines on two nails with nothing added and nothing removed.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and this entry is for a day whose second half was not spent in this case at all. That floor: a second floor in Crown Terrace, one corridor, about nine doors along the corridor that are not opened during a run, one fire door at each end, the far one propped with a folded sheet of paper and the near one latched, one stair, one stairwell light, one desk with four drawers, and in the second drawer on the left of it one laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, in a drawer that does not lock. A post at the corridor end, bare since the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, which is one hundred and eighty-nine days and that is twenty-seven weeks, and nobody has asked for it to be filled and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it. A separate count for the laminated sheet: not printed, because two dates for it exist in this case and they differ by twenty-eight days, and the post’s own figure is the one that walks. People on the floor: about eleven between half past six and about nine, about seven on the company’s list and about four on none, and one of those about four was not there, and he had been on that floor for four weeks running. Who called it: a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Attendance of the run: nine. How long it took: not said and not asked. Whether any other Friday was referred to: no. Who this Friday is in the run: the eighth of them, and there are two more in the run and neither of the two has happened yet, and nobody in that company has ever put a number on a Friday in a room. Whether the man of about thirty-nine who buys training for that company was on the floor: no, and no reason was given and none was asked for, and nothing in this entry says he was ever on it. Whether the man of about fifty-eight who drives that company’s van was in the building: no, and he was not there on the Friday before this one either, and nobody has ever asked him about the paper. Rate: nine and ten, uncorrected, told to nobody. Heating: on, dearer, four names on a lease dearer still, a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid and that is not resolved. Room off that service road: six hundred and forty-one days, and warm. Card: six hundred and forty-five days, four days more than the room, in a coat pocket, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up. Two nails, nineteen lines, and no twentieth: the twelve five hundred and sixty-one, the thirteen five hundred and twelve, the fourteen four hundred and seventy-seven, the fifteen four hundred and fifty-six, the sixteen four hundred and thirty-one, the seventeen four hundred and thirteen, the eighteen three hundred and fifty-nine, the nineteen three hundred and thirty-seven. None of those eight is an anchor on this date and none of them is a day. What is on two nails has not changed: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, about fourteen names, nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off, the first of them a cook’s and has never moved, the newest the only promise on it, written by a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts, in its forty-eight weeks and one day. A wall list in a plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. A ring binder on a shelf: eight things in it, the eighth of them a folded page that is unread and was not read, and the woman’s page is one of the eight and is shut in with them. Fifth pocket: empty, drawer shut, and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in. The nine hand copies of the front of a page of paper: begun in their twenty-nine weeks and four days, about eight of the nine unfinished, and the first disagreement, a figure in the fourth line, right in six copies and wrong in three, not found and not going to be found by anybody in this case. Man of about fifty-one with a tool bag: not in that building and not in this city on that day, and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-five weeks and two days, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it. Man of about thirty-three: not in the room and not in this city, holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for about thirty-nine weeks and one day, given nothing and needed nothing, and nobody has asked him for anything and nobody has stopped it. The Exchange did not sit on this day and does not sit on a Friday; its last sitting, the twenty-eighth, was twenty-three days ago, and its next, the twenty-ninth, is five days from today; book fifty-four lines, telephone seventy-three, count announced thirty-three times of which twenty-eight correspond, nineteen years of it, and none of the four is convertible into another and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and is not given as one. The ask: three hundred and thirty-one days, that being forty-seven weeks and two days, counted from the day it was asked at the seventeenth sitting and not from any sitting since. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 440.**

*The gap on the floor, as a record of one person who was not there.* **On the floor on Fridays: about eleven. On a company’s list: about seven. On nothing: about four. The run of one of the about four: four weeks running before this Friday. Whether he was there on this Friday: no. How many people on that floor noticed: about four. Whether anything was said about it: nothing, and nobody chased anybody and nobody ever does. Whether anybody has asked him anything: nobody, and nobody ever has. Whether anybody took a count of the four on no list across the six Fridays: nobody, and nobody ever has. Owner: a chair, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody said how long the run took and nobody asked, and nobody referred to any other Friday, and nobody on that floor has ever been told what any Friday in that building was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was. A hardboard on two nails four miles off carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and the card in a coat pocket is not that board, and the wall list in a plastic sleeve in a repair shop is not that board, and a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it is a fifth object, and a page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth in a fourth building, and a sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, a folder in a weighbridge hut was not turned over, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Friday. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in that binder and is unread. The Exchange did not sit and its book did not grow and no number was given into its tin. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 440.**
