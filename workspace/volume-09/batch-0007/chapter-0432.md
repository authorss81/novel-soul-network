# Chapter 432 — A Door Found Open, Shut Properly, And Said To Change Nothing

The Wednesday of week one hundred and fifty-eight was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six and from about half past six in one room off a service road, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors found one of them standing open in the afternoon and shut it properly and wrote it where she writes such things and then said out loud unasked that it changes nothing, and nobody thanked her and nobody improved on it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and thirty-two days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and thirty-six days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

She goes in fours and eights with a folded list on the back of a chair. About nine hundred doors and a pencil and no stamp and no reference and nobody’s authority behind her, and that is the whole of how she works.

On Wednesday afternoon in the third building she found the door to the back stair standing open on its hinges. It had been standing open since the morning and nobody had touched it. The lock had not caught because a screw in the plate had worked loose.

She fetched a driver from her bag and set the screw and shut the door twice to check the catch. She wrote it on her list in her own hand with the time beside it. The whole of it took about twenty minutes and nobody saw her do it.

About four people came through that stair while she worked and about four did not, and none of them asked her what she was doing.

---

She came in at about half past six and sat near the middle of the room with her list folded. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was not in that room and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-four weeks, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room on Wednesday, and his hold went on for about thirty-seven weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Then she said it, unasked, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular, in her own words, and it was about the door and not about anything else in that room.

"**I found one standing open and I shut it and I wrote it down, and there is nowhere for me to send it, and it changes nothing.**"

Nobody asked her about it. Nobody thanked her. Nobody said she was right. Nobody said the office would not have cared. Nobody asked her which building and nobody asked her which door.

**She is owed nothing and she is not in anybody’s case and the shut door is shut and nobody in that room asked her to shut it.**

---

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and twenty-eight days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The folded list on the back of a chair with a time beside one line is a seventh and it is not any of the six.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The door, as a record of a correct thing that changed nothing.* **Who did it: a woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors, owed nothing, not in anybody’s case. When: Wednesday afternoon in the third building, about twenty minutes, nobody watching. What was wrong: a back stair door standing open since the morning, a screw worked loose in the plate so the lock had not caught. What she did: set the screw with a driver from her bag, shut the door twice to check the catch, wrote it on a folded list with the time beside it. What she said out loud afterwards, unasked, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular: that she found one standing open and shut it and wrote it down, and there is nowhere for her to send it, and it changes nothing. Times anybody thanked her: none. Times anybody improved on it: none. Times anybody said she was right: none. Times anybody said the office would not have cared: none. Whether she was asked to shut it: no. Whether she will be asked to say it again: no. Owner: a folded list, and nobody.**

Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Wednesday. A drawer stayed shut and a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened and a folder in a weighbridge hut was not turned over. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in that binder and is unread. The Exchange did not sit and its book did not grow and no number was given into its tin. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 435.**
