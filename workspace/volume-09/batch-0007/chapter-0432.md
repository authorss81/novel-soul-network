# Chapter 432 — A Door Found Open, Shut Properly, And Said To Change Nothing

The Wednesday of week one hundred and fifty-eight was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six and from about half past six in one room off a service road, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors found one of them standing open in the afternoon and shut it properly and wrote it where she writes such things and then said out loud unasked that it changes nothing, and nobody thanked her and nobody improved on it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and thirty-two days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and thirty-six days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

She goes in fours and eights with a folded list on the back of a chair. About nine hundred doors and a pencil and no stamp and no reference and nobody’s authority behind her, and that is the whole of how she works.

**There are four buildings she goes into and there are about nine hundred doors between them and the number is not the same in any of the four.** Nobody has asked her for the number and she has not offered it and she gave it once, in a room, to somebody who wrote it down, and that is the only time it has been written down anywhere, and the person who wrote it did not ask her to check it. She does not know that it is wrong and has never been told and would not say so if she were.

On Wednesday afternoon in the third building she found the door to the back stair standing open on its hinges. It had been standing open since the morning and nobody had touched it. The lock had not caught because a screw in the plate had worked loose.

She fetched a driver from her bag and set the screw and shut the door twice to check the catch. She wrote it on her list in her own hand with the time beside it. The whole of it took about twenty minutes and nobody saw her do it.

About four people came through that stair while she worked and about four did not, and none of them asked her what she was doing.

The stair is the way up and the way down for everybody in that building and there is no other way, and the people on it did not stop and did not slow down and would not have known what to say to a woman with a driver in her bag at a door that is open. She put the driver back in the bag and shut the bag and stood at the bottom of the flight for about a minute with the list folded under her arm, and then she went up two floors and did the next one on her round, and the next one on her round was a door that was shut and needed nothing.

**There are about nine hundred doors and she does not have a list of them.** She has a folded list of what she did on a day and the folded list goes in the bag and comes out of the bag, and the nine hundred are not on it and are not on anything, and the order she does them in is an order she keeps in her own head and has never written down, and the head that keeps it is the only record of it that exists.

---

She came in at about half past six and sat near the middle of the room with her list folded. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

**The list went into the bag when she sat down and the bag went on the floor by her feet and neither of them was opened again that evening.** The line with the time beside it is the last line on it and there are four above it and the four are the four that needed nothing, and a line that says a door needed nothing is on the same list as a line that says a door was standing open since the morning, and there is nothing on the list to tell the two apart except the words.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was not in that room and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-four weeks, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room on Wednesday, and his hold went on for about thirty-seven weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Then she said it, unasked, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular, in her own words, and it was about the door and not about anything else in that room.

"**I found one standing open and I shut it and I wrote it down, and there is nowhere for me to send it, and it changes nothing.**"

Nobody asked her about it. Nobody thanked her. Nobody said she was right. Nobody said the office would not have cared. Nobody asked her which building and nobody asked her which door.

**She is owed nothing and she is not in anybody’s case and the shut door is shut and nobody in that room asked her to shut it.**

---

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and twenty-eight days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The folded list on the back of a chair with a time beside one line is a seventh and it is not any of the six.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The door, as a record of a correct thing that changed nothing.* **Who did it: a woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors, owed nothing, not in anybody’s case. How many doors that is: about nine hundred, and she goes in fours and eights, and this one was not in a group of eight and was not in a group of four and was found on her own round and not because anybody sent her. What was wrong with it: a back stair door standing open on its hinges since the morning, and a lock that had not caught because one screw in the plate had worked loose in the plate and not in the door. What she did about it: twenty minutes’ work, a driver out of her own bag, and the door shut and the catch tested twice. What is on her list in her own hand: a building, a stair, a door, a time, and her own mark, and nothing else — no number of the door, no tenant, no landlord, no reference, no stamp, and nothing that would let anybody find the door again except the stair. Whether the line is a report: no. Whether it is an inspection: no. Whether it is a defect: no, and nobody has called it one. Whether it is a claim: no, and there is nobody to claim from. Whether the people in that building were told the door had been standing open: no, and none of them knew, and the door is shut. Whether the same plate has been loose on any other door she has passed: nobody has looked, and she has not gone back. Whether the door is on a list of hers that anybody else holds: no, and there is no second copy and there is no second book. Whether she will be asked to say it again: no. Whether she was asked to shut it: no. What it changed: the door, and nothing else, and the door would have opened again by the following week on the same screw if the screw had not been set. Owner: a folded list on the back of a chair, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody in the third building was told that the door had been standing open since the morning. The screw is set and the catch holds and it was tested twice and nobody has tested it since, and the door is shut, and the line on the folded list is the only account that it was ever open, and the folded list is on the back of a chair in a warm room four miles off and it is not a register and there is no second copy of it and no second book and no column for a door number. Nobody rang anybody about it. Nobody paid her for the twenty minutes and nobody is going to, and she did not ask.**

**A hardboard on two nails carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and there is no twentieth line, and and none of the six surfaces in this file is one of the others and none of them is that board and the six are six on this day and not five and not seven, and the folded list on the back of a chair is a seventh and is not any of the six. Nobody read the ninth line in a ring binder and the folded page at the bottom of that binder is where it was on Wednesday, and it is not the folded list that was on the chair. A drawer stayed shut and a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened and a folder in a weighbridge hut was not turned over. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in that binder and is unread.
