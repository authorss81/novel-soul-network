# Chapter 422 — A Card In A Plastic Sleeve, Nine Words About Being A Rule, And A Signature Under It

The Wednesday of week one hundred and fifty-five was spent on a bus each way and on the ground in the second of four towns on that road for about five hours, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a working practice on a set of scales came nearly right and not quite, a card in a plastic sleeve was put in a box by the pad, a man of about fifty-eight who keeps the only written record in that town said out loud, in about nine words, that a thing two of us do is not a rule and cannot be made one for anybody, and then signed the one line on the card that says he does it, and nobody improved on either half of that and nobody thanked him for either half, and the card went on being used from that afternoon by somebody who was not asked to stop.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and the eleven were eleven jobs in a town he has not worked in before, done in the order the card gave them, and the card is a card in date and carries no address. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and eleven days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and fifteen days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday of the fortnight before last, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and **it did not come out of that pocket in that town, and the card in a plastic sleeve in a box by a set of scales is a different object and was never in the same building as it.**

---

The town had a set of scales on a concrete pad by the road and a hut about eight feet by six beside it with a window in one side, and inside the hut there was a table, a stove, a kettle, a book on the table, and about four things on the walls that are not on any list of anything.

There were four lamps in that street and one of them had come on in the spring that was not on in the winter, and the shop shut at four.

**He is about fifty-eight. He is not a clerk. He holds no post. He is owed nothing by anybody in this case, and he is not a villain, and he is not named on any surface and in no document and in nobody's mouth, and nobody has ever asked him to explain the book and he has never offered to, and the room he offered in an earlier week is in the back of that hut and nobody is in it and nobody has said out loud that it is still there.**

There was a man of about twenty-six at the pad who had been at it since the spring. He is the older one's brother-in-law and he drove a tipper for a firm in this city until the spring and he does the middle shift now, and nobody in that town asked him where he had come from and nobody has asked him since and there is nothing anybody would want answered.

---

A lorry was on the pad when Marek got there and the man of about fifty-eight wrote it down in the book while it was being unloaded, and he did it without being asked and he did it the same way he has done it every time, and the driver did not get out and did not ask him anything and neither of those two things has ever happened on that pad.

Marek did the eleven jobs in the order the card gave them, and about four of them were the same fault in four different places, and nobody in that town had seen him before and nobody asked him where he had learned any of it and nobody was thanked.

---

The card had come in a bundle with about four hundred of them, in the middle of the spring, in a plastic sleeve, from the firm whose lorries come to the pad. It is stiff card about the size of a playing card and it has a space at the top for a lorry's plate and a printed form under it with four boxes in it and one line at the bottom, and the bottom line is the only part anybody has ever argued about.

The form says: weigh, load, go. The four boxes are for the four checks. The bottom line says: **I confirm this is done at this site by the person signing.**

"**That is a card off a lorry.**"

"**It is a card off a lorry.**"

"**It has been in that box since the spring and about nine hundred lorries have come through that pad since the spring and it has been in that box the whole time.**"

"**Then it is a card that has been in a box.**"

---

What they had started doing without the card, in the second week of the spring, was this. The man of about twenty-eight wrote the weight in the book. The man of about twenty-six counted the pallets off the lorry and the number off the ticket. If the two numbers did not agree, the lorry did not go.

**It is nearly right, and it is nearly right in a way that is easy to miss. In about nine years it has caught the same thing once, and the once was a lorry that had been part-loaded at the far end and had come in light, and the number on the ticket had been made out at the far end before the pallets were on. It cannot catch a load that was wrong before it came to this pad at all, and it cannot catch a driver who counts out loud and gets his own count right by accident, and in about nine years of doing it that way it has caught one.**

And the second person is the difficulty. There are about four people who work that pad. In about four months of doing it, the second person has been the same man every single time, or the man of about twenty-six every single time, and a check where the second person is always the same as the first person is not a check.

Nobody in that town had said that out loud before Marek's second visit and he was not going to be the one to say it, and he did not say it, and he has not said it since, and it has not been written down.

---

Then the firm rang. They rang the pad, and they did not ask whether anybody wanted the card, and they asked whether the site was doing it.

The man of about fifty-eight put the phone down and stood at the table for a while, and the kettle was on behind him and the four lamps in that street had one of them on that had not been on in the winter.

"**A thing two of us do is not a rule, and I cannot make it one for anybody.**"

It took him about nine seconds and he said it to the window and not to anybody, and it is the whole of what he said about it, and nobody improved on it, and nobody asked him what he was going to do, and nobody said he was right.

Then he took the card out of the box and turned it over and signed the bottom line, and he signed it with his own name, which is the only time in about nine years that his name has gone near that pad in writing, and the book on the table has never had a name in it either and does not have one now.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him why he had said one thing and done the other. Nobody said he had contradicted himself, and about four people in that hut have since said the two halves of that afternoon do not agree with each other and about four have said nothing, and the two groups have not met.

---

The man of about twenty-six used the card from that afternoon. He did not ask. He was not asked to stop and nobody stopped him. He put the plate number in the top box on the next lorry and then worked down the four boxes and then turned the card round and put it on the table under the window, and the man of about fifty-eight looked at it and did not say anything about it, and then the man of about twenty-six picked the card back up and put it in the box.

**A card in a plastic sleeve in a box by a set of scales on a road on the far side of this city is a ninth object and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing, and the card in a coat pocket in the inner pocket of a coat that is over a chair in that same hut is a different card in a different pocket and the two have been in one hut for about five hours and the firm does not know the second one exists and neither does anybody in that town.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
