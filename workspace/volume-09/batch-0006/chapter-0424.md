# Chapter 424 — The Fifth Week Of A Run On One Floor, A Post Bare For Twenty-Three Weeks, And A Van Driver In A Corridor For About Four Minutes

The Friday of week one hundred and fifty-five was again a second floor in Crown Terrace from about half past six until about nine, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a run went on that floor for the third week running and nobody said how long it took and nobody asked, the post at the corridor end has now carried nothing for one hundred and sixty-one days, which is twenty-three weeks, the laminated sheet is still in the second drawer on the left and the drawer still does not lock, and the far fire door was still being held open with the same folded sheet of paper, and for the first time in about a month a man of about fifty-eight who drives that building's van stood in that corridor at about nine and said one ordinary sentence about the stairwell light and nobody took it up and nobody asked him about the paper he put there.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and thirteen days old and is warm. The card is six hundred and seventeen days old, four days more than that room is, and it is in a coat pocket, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

**The whole of that day was on that floor. About nine people came up that stair between about half past six and about nine and about nine went down it at about nine, and that is the whole of what is known about where anybody in this case was.**

---

The same floor as the last three times, in the same corridor, with the same about nine folding chairs against the wall and the same desk by the near fire door and the same stair.

There was a post at the corridor end with nothing on it. It has now carried nothing for one hundred and sixty-one days, which is twenty-three weeks, and nobody in that company has asked for it to be filled, and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it, and about four people in that company know it exists and about four do not.

**In the second drawer on the left of that desk there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and the drawer does not lock, and it has been in there for as long as the post has been bare, and no separate count is printed for that sheet anywhere in this entry, because two different dates for it exist in this case and differ by twenty-eight days, and neither of the two is printed here and a reader loses nothing by that.**

The far fire door was propped with a folded sheet of paper, and it had been there since the Friday of the week before last, and about four people who came up that stair in the fortnight the run was off walked past it and about four did not notice it, and the two groups have not met.

---

About eleven people were on the floor between about half past six and about nine. About seven of them are on the company's list. About four are on none of anything, and about four of those four have now come for three weeks running, which nobody took a count of and nobody has ever said out loud in that building.

**He is about fifty-eight. He drives that company's van. He is not in this city on any other day of this week and he is not on that floor on any of the other four Fridays this month, and he is not the man who keeps a written record at a set of scales on a road on the far side of this city and he has never met that man and does not know that one exists.**

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard.

Nobody said how long it took. Nobody asked. Nobody referred to the fortnight, or to the Friday before the fortnight, or to the Friday before that, and nobody has ever asked the about four on that floor why they come, and nobody chased anybody in that room and nobody did tonight, and about nine people went up that stair at about half past six and about nine came down it at about nine, and the two numbers are the same and nobody counted either.

---

At about ten to eight a woman of about twenty-eight who is on the company's list said one ordinary sentence about the stairwell light. It was about the stairwell light. Nobody took it up.

At about nine the chairs went up and the stair emptied and a man of about twenty-eight who works in that building put the lights off in an order that has nothing to do with anything, and **the man of about fifty-eight was still in the corridor when he did it, and he looked at the propped fire door on his way past and did not touch it, and nobody asked him about it and nobody has ever asked him about it and nobody in that building has ever asked that man about anything.**

Then he said his sentence, to about four people in the corridor, unasked, and it was about the stairwell light and it took him about four seconds.

"**That light has been on since about four and it is daylight out of that window.**"

Nobody agreed with him. Nobody disagreed with him. Nobody asked him what he wanted done about it and nobody has, and about four people on that floor have said the light should be left alone and about four have said the opposite, and the two groups have not met.

**He is not a hero and not a villain and is owed nothing and is not in anybody's case, and nobody in that company has thanked him for anything in about as long as this case has been going, and nobody has asked him to take the paper away and nobody has asked him to put a better one there.**

---

A write-up went into a file a fortnight ago in the way that a company writes things up, and it went into a building four miles away, and it was not read that week, and nothing on that floor is different because of it.

**The man of about thirty-nine who buys training for the company that runs that floor was not on it, and nobody said he was not coming, and nobody said he had been and had not. He came once, on his own time, in about four years, and wrote it up in his own company's form, and said out loud that it changes nothing, and was not thanked, and that is the whole of what is known about him in that building. Nothing said tonight says that he was ever on that floor, and nothing on that floor has ever said it either, and no reason is given tonight, and nobody asked for one, and the wall calendar that would have joined two Fridays to each other is not on the floor and is not in that building.**

Nobody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one.

Nobody thanked anybody.

*427. Friday of week 155, at about ten on a second floor in Crown Terrace, and this entry is one run of about nine minutes for the third week running, a post bare for twenty-three weeks, a laminated sheet in a drawer that does not lock with no count printed for it, a fire door held open with a folded sheet of paper, a van driver in that corridor for about four minutes at nine o'clock saying one sentence about a stairwell light, and nobody saying how long the run took.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and this entry is for a day whose second half was not spent in this case at all. That floor: a second floor in Crown Terrace, one corridor, about nine doors along the corridor that are not opened during a run, one fire door at each end, the far one propped with a folded sheet of paper and the near one latched, one stair, one stairwell light, one desk with four drawers, and in the second drawer on the left of it one laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, in a drawer that does not lock. A post at the corridor end, bare since the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, which is one hundred and sixty-one days and that is twenty-three weeks, and nobody has asked for it to be filled. A separate count for the laminated sheet: not printed, because two dates for it exist in this case and they differ by twenty-eight days, and the choice between them at a desk would be a guess. People on the floor: about eleven between half past six and about nine, about seven on the company's list and about four on none, and about four of those four have now come for three weeks running. Who called it: a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Attendance of the run: nine. How long it took: not said and not asked. Whether the fortnight was referred to: no. Whether any other Friday was referred to: no. Whether the man of about thirty-nine who buys training for that company was on the floor: no, and no reason was given and none was asked for, and nothing in this entry says he was ever on it. The man of about fifty-eight who drives that company's van: in the corridor at about nine for about four minutes, which is the first time in about a month, and he is not in this city on any other day of this week and is not on that floor on any of the other four Fridays of this month, and nobody asked him about the folded sheet of paper under the far fire door and nobody has ever asked him about it and nobody in that building has ever asked that man about anything. What he said, unasked, in about four seconds, to about four people: that the stairwell light had been on since about four and it was daylight out of that window. Times anybody agreed with him: none. Times anybody disagreed with him: none. Times anybody asked him what he wanted done about it: none, and none ever has. How many on that floor have said the light should be left alone: about four. How many have said the opposite: about four. Whether the two groups have met: no. Whether a write-up came out of tonight: none, and one went into a file in a building four miles away a fortnight ago and was not read that week and nothing on that floor is different because of it. Times anybody thanked anybody: none. Rate: nine and ten, uncorrected, told to nobody. Heating: on, dearer, four names on a lease dearer still, a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid and that is not resolved. Room off that service road: six hundred and thirteen days. Card: six hundred and seventeen days, four days more than that room, in a coat pocket, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up. Two nails, nineteen lines, and no twentieth: the twelve five hundred and thirty-three, the thirteen four hundred and eighty-four, the fourteen four hundred and forty-nine, the fifteen four hundred and twenty-eight, the sixteen four hundred and three, the seventeen three hundred and eighty-five, the eighteen three hundred and thirty-one, the nineteen three hundred and nine. None of those eight is an anchor on this date and none of them is a day. What is on two nails has not changed: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, about fourteen names, nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off, the first of them a cook's and has never moved, the newest the only promise on it. A wall list in a plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. A ring binder on a shelf: eight things in it, the eighth of them a folded page that is unread and was not read, and the woman's page is one of the eight and is shut in with them. Fifth pocket: empty, drawer shut, and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in. The nine hand copies of the front of a page of paper: begun in their twenty-five weeks and four days, about eight of the nine unfinished, and the first disagreement, a figure in the fourth line, right in six copies and wrong in three, not found and not going to be found by anybody in this case. Man of about fifty-one with a tool bag: not in that building and not in this city on that day, and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-one weeks and two days, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it. Man of about thirty-three: not in the room and not in this city, holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for about thirty-five weeks and one day, given nothing and needed nothing, and nobody has asked him for anything and nobody has stopped it. The Exchange did not sit on a Friday and it does not sit on one;  its last sitting was twenty-three days ago and the one before that was fifty-one days ago, and its next is thirty-three days from today; book fifty-four lines, telephone seventy-three, count announced thirty-two times of which twenty-seven correspond, nineteen years of it, and none of the four is convertible into another and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and is not given as one. The ask: three hundred and three days, that being forty-three weeks and two days, counted from the day it was asked at the seventeenth sitting and not from any sitting since, and the figure before this one belongs to a Thursday and is not this one. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 427.**

*The post and the drawer, as a record of two things in a corridor nobody asked about.* **The post: at the corridor end of a second floor in Crown Terrace, bare since the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, which is one hundred and sixty-one days and that is twenty-three weeks. Times anybody in that company has asked for it to be filled: none. Times anybody has asked what is meant to go on it: none. How many people in that company know it exists: about four. How many do not: about four. The drawer: the second drawer on the left of the desk by the near fire door, and it does not lock, and there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it in it. Whether a separate count is printed for that sheet: no, and the reason is printed in the same sentence as the number is not, and the reason is that two dates for it exist in this case and they differ by twenty-eight days. Times anybody has asked to see the sheet: none. Times anybody has asked for it to go back on the post: none. Whether the sheet and the post are the same question: nobody in that building has ever said so. The far fire door: propped with a folded sheet of paper since the Friday of the week before last, put there by the man of about fifty-eight who drives that company's van, and nobody has asked him about it and nobody has asked him to take it away and nobody has asked him to put a better one there. Owner: a corridor, and nobody.**

*The four who come, as a record of a reason nobody has ever been given.* **On the floor: about nine at half past six. On a company's list: about seven. On nothing: about four. How many of those four have now come for three weeks running: about four, and nobody took a count of it and nobody has ever said it out loud in that building. Why they come: never asked, by anybody, in about as long as this case has been going, and about four people in this city have wondered about it and about four have not. Whether anybody was chased in that room: nobody, and nobody ever is. Whether anybody said how long the run took: nobody. Whether anybody referred to any other Friday: nobody. Whether anybody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it was for, by anybody: no, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one. Owner: a stair, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody said how long the run took and nobody asked, and nobody referred to any other Friday. Nobody asked the man of about fifty-eight a question about the folded sheet of paper under the far fire door and nobody has ever asked him and nobody in that company has ever asked that man about anything. A hardboard on two nails in a room four miles off carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and the card in a coat pocket is not that board, and the wall list in a plastic sleeve in a repair shop is not that board, and a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it is a fifth object, and a page of paper with about eleven lines on it is a sixth in a fourth building, and a sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, a folder in a weighbridge hut was not turned over, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Friday. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in that binder and is unread. The Exchange did not sit and its book did not grow and no number was given into its tin. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 427.**
