# Chapter 430 — Eleven Sentences At A Sink, Said By A Woman Who Came Off Nights, To Nobody Who Asked

The Thursday of week one hundred and fifty-seven was spent in a kitchen in this city from about half past six until about nine, and the one thing that is true of it is that **Lena Senn, who is about fifty-eight and came off nights and has not been asked for anything since a Wednesday evening in a kitchen nineteen years ago, said the cost of saying it out loud first, and then said eleven sentences at a sink about a table in a rented room in a town that is not this city, and nobody asked her for them and nobody is going to and she has not been asked to say them again, and nobody thanked her and nobody improved on any of it, and a woman of about twenty-four who is a resonance nurse said one thing about that kitchen and not one word about any man in it, and then telephoned none of it and told nobody, including him, and that is her decision and her own.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and the eleven were done in two districts of this city and the last of them finished at about six and he was not in the room off a service road and does not go to it on a Thursday. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and twenty-six days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and thirty days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and **it went to a kitchen in this city in an inside pocket over the back of a chair and came home in the same pocket at about nine, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building still holds one card where it held two.**

---

The kitchen had a table in it with two plates on it that had not been put away, a window over a sink, a tap that is cold at six in the morning, a chair, a floor, and about nine things on a shelf that are not on any list.

**Lena Senn is about fifty-eight. She came off nights and she goes in at about nine. She is not an oracle and not a keeper and not a fighter and not a device, and she does not know anything this case has not already been told by a document, and what she has is a memory that is nineteen years old and there is one of it. She has not been asked for anything in this case since a Wednesday evening in a kitchen nineteen years ago, and she was not asked today.**

**Talia Venn is twenty-four and is a resonance nurse and works out of a mobile clinic in the Lower Wards with about nine chairs in it. She had been in that flat for about an hour when he came and had been drying, and she was going to leave before he came, and did not, and nobody asked her to stay and nobody has thanked her for staying. She and Marek Senn are Thursdays four miles apart in separate workrooms and have been for four months and four weeks and three days, and that is not shorter and it is not ended, and nothing in that kitchen bears on it, and they said good evening.**

---

He came in at about half past six with his coat on and did not take it off, and Lena Senn was at the sink with her back to the room, and the other one was at the table with a tea towel in her hands.

Nobody said anything for about four minutes.

Then Lena Senn said the first thing, and it was not the eleven sentences and it was the cost of them, and she said it to the window and not to anybody, and it took her about nine seconds.

"**Nobody has asked me for anything in nineteen years and nobody is going to now.**"

Nobody thanked her for that. Nobody asked her what it had cost her. Nobody said she was brave and nobody said she was daft. Nobody improved on it.

Then she washed a plate and she dried her hands and she said, "**Right**", and she said the eleven sentences, and she said them the way a person reads out a shopping list off the side of a tin, and she did not stop in the middle of any of them and she did not say them again.

---

"**He told me the answer was settled before that room filled.**"

"**He had spent his working life being the man an answer went through, and that was the job he had, and he was good at it.**"

"**There were about nine of us in it and none of us had been asked.**"

"**He put his own hand flat on the thing that carried the answer, and it took about four seconds, and he did not say a word while he was doing it.**"

"**He was not being brave. I asked him that afterward and he told me he was stopping one thing, and that is every single thing he ever said to me about it.**"

"**After that he could not do the thing his hands had always done, and he could not tell anybody that he could not do it, and I was the one person he could have told and I have not once said I knew.**"

"**He was on a ward inside the week and dead inside the year.**"

"**The form says he walked away from it. A man signed that form. The man was not lying and the signature is his and the sentence on the form is not all of what happened in that room.**"

"**He was in a rented room with a table in it in a town you have never been to, and it is not any of the places you have heard me mention.**"

"**Nobody wrote any of it down that day and nobody has written it down since, and I am not writing it down now and there is nowhere I could put it.**"

"**You have not asked me for it, so this is not an answer to you, and do not ask me to say it a second time.**"

---

Nobody said anything for a while. There was a sound of a road outside and a tap that was not the tap in that room.

Nobody asked her a question about any of it. Nobody asked her which man signed the form and nobody asked her what town and nobody asked her how she knew the number of people in that room. Nobody asked her whether she had told anybody. Nobody said thank you. Nobody said it was brave and nobody said it was terrible and nobody said he was right to do it and nobody said he was wrong.

**She is not going to be asked to say it again. Nobody in that kitchen said out loud that she will not be and nobody ever will, and a person who has been asked to say a thing twice has been asked to be a record, and nobody in this case has asked anybody to be a record in nineteen years.**

**Nothing was signed. Nothing was refused. Nothing was agreed. There is no document, no card that opens anything, no letter, no photograph, no second account from anybody who was in that room, and there is one of it and it is a woman's and it is nineteen years old and it is not going anywhere.**

---

**And then the woman of twenty-four said one thing, and it was one thing, and it was about that kitchen.**

She had been drying for about an hour and she put the tea towel down on the table before she said it, and she said it to the room and not to anybody in it, and it took her about nine seconds.

"**Nine people eat at that table and that tap is cold at six in the morning and nobody in this building has ever been asked what for, and the Assembly has not asked us either, and that is the one thing in nine years of it that nobody had to teach me.**"

Nobody improved on it. Nobody asked her what she meant. Nobody asked her which nine years and nobody asked her which building.

**Then she did not say anything else. Not about the eleven sentences, not about the form, not about the man who signed it, not about the town. She did not ask Marek Senn one question about any of it, and he did not ask her one, and neither of them said a word about it for the rest of the evening, and that was hers to do and not his, and he is not going to ask her about it and she is not going to be asked.**

**She telephoned none of it. Not to anybody, not to the clinic, not to a desk, not to a person, and she wrote none of it down, and about four people in this city would give a great deal to know that she was in that kitchen and about four would not, and the two groups have not met. She is not thanked. She is owed nothing. She is not a mechanism and she holds no post and she is not the answer to anything, and the not telling is her decision and nobody has improved on it.**

---

Lena Senn went in at about nine with a cloth bag over a chair with things in it, which is a thing she has done for a very long time, and the kitchen was clean and there were two plates on a rack and there was nothing on the table.

Marek Senn left at about nine. Talia Venn left at about nine and they left separately and nobody saw them out and nobody on that street said anything about either of them.

**The separation is four months and four weeks and three days old and it is not shorter and it is not ended, and nothing in that kitchen softens it by a minute, and the two workrooms are four miles apart and will be four miles apart on Friday.**

---

**And the answer to what a man of twenty-two came up that road for is in the mouth of a woman of about fifty-eight who came off nights, and it is about a man and not about a mechanism, and no villain was required and no device was involved and there is no such thing as a thing on a table in a rented room that opens anything. He did not go looking for a power and he did not leave his family for one. He put his own hand flat on a thing in a room with about nine people in it, in about four seconds, on purpose, to stop one approved answer going through in a building where about nine people were in it, and it left him unable to do the thing he had been able to do and unable to say so, and he was on a ward inside the week and dead inside the year, and the record that says he walked away is one that a man signed, and the signature is real and the sentence is not the whole of the truth.**

**There is no card, no document, no record, no photograph, no hospital letter and no second person's account. There is a woman at a sink and eleven sentences and one of them, and nobody read it out of anything, because there is nothing to read it out of.**

---

Nobody thanked anybody.
