# Chapter 427 — Six Friday Afternoons, One Of Them The Sixth, And A Figure Of Four Hundred And Ninety-One That Is An Age And Not A Day

The Friday of week one hundred and fifty-six was again a second floor in Crown Terrace from about half past six until about nine, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a run went on that floor for the fourth week running and nobody said how long it took and nobody asked, the post at the corridor end has now carried nothing for one hundred and sixty-eight days, which is twenty-four weeks, one of the about four who come on no list of anything was not there on Friday and about four people on that floor noticed and about four did not, and four miles off, on a hardboard on two nails, a line written by a first-year of nineteen in an earlier season is four hundred and ninety-one days old, which is an age and not a day, and four hundred and ninety-one is also the day that line was written, and both of those are true at once on the Friday that is the sixth of these.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and twenty days old and is warm. The card is six hundred and twenty-four days old, four days more than that room is, and it is in a coat pocket, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

**The whole of that day was on that floor. About nine people came up that stair between about half past six and about nine and about eight went down it at about nine, and that is the whole of what is known about where anybody in this case was.**

---

**It was the sixth of these Fridays. The one before it was a week back. The one before that was a fortnight back. The one before that was a week back. The one before that was a week back. And the one before that was the first of them, when the run came back after being off a fortnight, and it is the sixth and there are four more of them in the run and none of the four has happened yet, and nobody in that company has ever put a number on a Friday in a room and about four people on that floor could tell you it was the sixth and about four could not.**

The same floor, in the same corridor, with the same about nine folding chairs against the wall and the same desk by the near fire door and the same stair and the same stairwell light.

There was a post at the corridor end with nothing on it. It has now carried nothing for one hundred and sixty-eight days, which is twenty-four weeks, and nobody has asked for it to be filled and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it.

**In the second drawer on the left of that desk there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and the drawer does not lock, and no separate count is printed for that sheet anywhere in this entry, because two different dates for it exist in this case and differ by twenty-eight days, and the post's own figure above is the one that walks.**

The far fire door was still propped with a folded sheet of paper. Nobody has asked the man of about fifty-eight who drives that company's van to take it away and nobody has ever asked him anything, and he was not in that building on Friday and has not been in it since the Friday of week one hundred and fifty-five.

---

About eleven people were on the floor. About seven of them are on the company's list. About four are on none of anything, and **one of those about four was not there, and he has been on that floor for three weeks running and he is not on anything, and nobody in that building has asked him anything this week and nobody noticed the gap until the chairs went up.**

Nobody chased anybody. Nobody said anything about the gap. Nobody took a count of the four on no list in four weeks and nobody has ever taken one.

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Nobody said how long it took and nobody asked and nobody referred to any other Friday.

At about ten to eight a man of about thirty in overalls who is on the company's list said one ordinary sentence about the stairwell light. It was about the stairwell light. Nobody took it up.

At about nine the chairs went up and the lights went off in an order that has nothing to do with anything.

---

**Four miles off, in a room off a service road in the Lower Wards, two nails carry a hardboard with nineteen lines on it, and the eight ages on it are set out in the docket below, and one of those eight needs its own sentence rather than a place in a list.**

The thirteen lines are **four hundred and ninety-one days old.** That is how long a line has been on a board, and it is an age, and it is not a day, and it is not when anything was switched on or switched off, and it is not a date anybody in this case has, and a person reading that figure as a day has read a thing that is not there.

**Four hundred and ninety-one is also the day that line was written.** It was written by a first-year of nineteen who came into this case a long way back and said out loud that it was an opt-out, and it has been on that board ever since and it carries on independently of every other line up there, and nobody has ever taken it down and nothing has been added to it and nothing has been removed from it and nothing has been rubbed off it.

**So on this Friday, which is the sixth of these Fridays, the thirteen lines are four hundred and ninety-one days old on a day that is four hundred and ninety-one.** Both of those are true at the same moment and neither of them is the other, and no page in this case has ever put the two together and neither ever will, and about four people in this city could misread that figure in a hurry and about four could not, and the two groups have not met.

The other seven on that board are the twelve, at five hundred and forty days, the fourteen, at four hundred and fifty-six, the fifteen, at four hundred and thirty-five, the sixteen, at four hundred and ten, the seventeen, at three hundred and ninety-two, the eighteen, at three hundred and thirty-eight, and the nineteen, at three hundred and sixteen, which is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it has been there for forty-five weeks and one day and nothing has ever been done about it.

**There is no twentieth line. Not on this Friday and not anywhere in this fortnight, and the reason is not restraint: a twentieth line would be a new day and a new figure and a new thing to be wrong about, and a board that has carried nineteen lines for this long does not get a twentieth because a writer needs something to publish.**

---

**Nobody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one, and that is a thing nobody has ever put anywhere rather than a thing somebody has looked for and failed to find.**

The man of about thirty-nine who buys training for the company that runs that floor was not on it, and nobody said he was not coming, and nobody said he had been and had not.

Nobody thanked anybody.

*430. Friday of week 156, at about ten on a second floor in Crown Terrace, and this entry is one run of about nine minutes for the fourth week running, the sixth of these Fridays, a post bare for twenty-four weeks, a laminated sheet in a drawer that does not lock with no count printed for it, one of about four who come on no list not there and about four who noticed, and four miles off a line on two nails that is four hundred and ninety-one days old on a day that is four hundred and ninety-one.*

*Conditions and docket.* **Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening, and this entry is for a day whose second half was not spent in this case at all. That floor: a second floor in Crown Terrace, one corridor, about nine doors along the corridor that are not opened during a run, one fire door at each end, the far one propped with a folded sheet of paper and the near one latched, one stair, one stairwell light, one desk with four drawers, and in the second drawer on the left of it one laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, in a drawer that does not lock. A post at the corridor end, bare since the Friday of week one hundred and thirty-two, which is one hundred and sixty-eight days and that is twenty-four weeks, and nobody has asked for it to be filled and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it. A separate count for the laminated sheet: not printed, because two dates for it exist in this case and they differ by twenty-eight days, and the post's own figure is the one that walks. Who called it: a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Attendance of the run: nine. How long it took: not said and not asked. Whether any other Friday was referred to: no. Whether the man of about thirty-nine who buys training for that company was on the floor: no, and no reason was given and none was asked for, and nothing in this entry says he was ever on it. Whether the man of about fifty-eight who drives that company's van was in the building: no, and he was not there on the Friday before this one either, and nobody has asked him about the folded sheet of paper and nobody ever has. Whether a write-up came out of tonight: none, and one went into a file in a building four miles away a fortnight ago and was not read that week. Times anybody noticed the gap in the four: about four. Heating: on, dearer, four names on a lease dearer still, a fortnight a cook laid out that nobody has repaid and that is not resolved. Room off that service road: six hundred and twenty days. Card: six hundred and twenty-four days, four days more than that room, in a coat pocket, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up. Two nails, nineteen lines, and no twentieth: the twelve five hundred and forty, the thirteen four hundred and ninety-one, the fourteen four hundred and fifty-six, the fifteen four hundred and thirty-five, the sixteen four hundred and ten, the seventeen three hundred and ninety-two, the eighteen three hundred and thirty-eight, the nineteen three hundred and sixteen. The thirteen is four hundred and ninety-one days old and four hundred and ninety-one is the day that line was written, so that figure is an age and is not a day, and it is the only one of the eight on this date that is also its own anchor, and it is the sixth of these Fridays that carries it. What is on two nails has not changed: nineteen lines, thirteen hands, about fourteen names, nothing added, nothing removed, nothing rubbed off, the first of them a cook's and has never moved, the newest the only promise on it, and the thirteen is an opt-out written by a first-year of nineteen and it carries on independently. A wall list in a plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. A ring binder on a shelf: eight things in it, the eighth of them a folded page that is unread and was not read, and the woman's page is one of the eight and is shut in with them. Fifth pocket: empty, drawer shut, and nobody has ever asked him to fill it in. The nine hand copies of the front of a page of paper: begun in their twenty-six weeks and four days, about eight of the nine unfinished, and the first disagreement, a figure in the fourth line, right in six copies and wrong in three, not found and not going to be found by anybody in this case. Man of about fifty-one with a tool bag: not in that building and not in this city on that day, and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-two weeks and two days, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it. Man of about thirty-three: not in the room and not in this city, holding four buildings and about nine hundred doors for about thirty-six weeks and one day, given nothing and needed nothing, and nobody has asked him for anything and nobody has stopped it. The Exchange did not sit on a Friday; its last sitting was thirty days ago and the one before that was fifty-eight days ago, and its next is twenty-six days from today, and the book opens at that one; book fifty-four lines, telephone seventy-three, count announced thirty-three times of which twenty-eight correspond, nineteen years of it, and none of the four is convertible into another and the difference between the book and the tin is not a number and is not given as one. The ask: three hundred and ten days, that being forty-four weeks and two days, counted from the day it was asked at the seventeenth sitting and not from any sitting since, and the figure before this one belongs to a Wednesday and is not this one. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 430.**

*The thirteen lines, as a record of one figure that is two things.* **What the figure is: how long a line has been on a hardboard on two nails in a room off a service road in this city. How old it is today: four hundred and ninety-one days. Whether that figure is a day: no, and it is not when anything was switched on or off and it is not a date anybody in this case has. What the same figure also is: the day that line was written. When it was written: by a first-year of nineteen who came into this case a long way back and said out loud that it was an opt-out. Whether both are true at once today: yes, and neither is the other. Whether the eight ages on that board have ever been printed together with the day any of them was written: no, and they are not going to be. Whether anybody in this city could misread the figure in a hurry: about four could and about four could not, and the two groups have not met. Whether the line is still there: yes, and it carries on independently, and nothing has been added to that board and nothing has been removed from it and nothing has been rubbed off it. Whether there is a twentieth line: no, and not here, and a twentieth line would be a new day and a new figure and a new thing to be wrong about. Owner: two nails, and nobody.**

*The gap on the floor, as a record of one person who was not there.* **On the floor on Fridays: about eleven. On a company's list: about seven. On nothing: about four. The run of one of the about four: three weeks running before this Friday. Whether he was there on this Friday: no. How many people on that floor noticed: about four. Whether anything was said about it: nothing, and nobody chased anybody and nobody ever does. Whether anybody has asked him anything: nobody, and nobody ever has. Whether anybody took a count of the four on no list across the four Fridays: nobody, and nobody ever has. Owner: a chair, and nobody.**

*What the day did not touch.* **Nobody said how long the run took and nobody asked, and nobody referred to any other Friday, and nobody on that floor has ever been told what any Friday in that building was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was. A hardboard on two nails four miles off carried the same nineteen lines and was not written on, nothing was added to it, nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and the card in a coat pocket is not that board, and the wall list in a plastic sleeve in a repair shop is not that board, and a tool roll with a fourth pocket in it is a fifth object, and a page of paper with about eleven lines on it is a sixth in a fourth building, and a sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. A drawer stayed shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened, a folder in a weighbridge hut was not turned over, and the folded page at the bottom of a ring binder is where it was on Friday. The woman of about thirty in the chair nearest the door was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not apologised to, and her page is the eighth thing in that binder and is unread. The Exchange did not sit and its book did not grow and no number was given into its tin. A fortnight that a cook laid out is paid for by nobody. Nobody died. Nobody thanked anybody. Entry 430.**
