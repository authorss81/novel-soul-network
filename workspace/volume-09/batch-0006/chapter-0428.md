# Chapter 428 — A Woman Of About Thirty-One Names What Going Back Has Cost Her, And Nobody In That Room Has Ever Asked Her About The Other Day

The Monday of week one hundred and fifty-seven was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and from about half past six in one room off a service road in the Lower Wards, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a woman of about thirty-one, who came up that road in an earlier fortnight to see somebody in the third of those four towns and lost a whole day and was on a bus at six in the morning instead and said so out loud on that bus to nobody, went back on a Tuesday and saw who she came to see, and on the Monday evening she said out loud, unasked, in about nine words, what going back has cost her, and nobody in that room has ever asked her about the first day, including the man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts, whose eleven words are the only promise on the board she was sitting under.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and twenty-three days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and twenty-seven days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday of the week before the one with the road in it, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

He had said yes to nine days of grade-two work in two districts of this city in a corridor five weeks ago, and seven of the nine were gone and the other two are not going to be. Nobody had telephoned. The sheet of paper has a pen mark against his name for seven of the nine and has no column for a reason and no column for a town.

**Nobody in this case has ever asked the man of about thirty-four who wrote that name against nine days whether the nine are going to happen, and he has never telephoned about them, and about four people in this case have noticed that he has not and about four have not.**

---

She had been to that town to see a person she has not named, and she has not named him now, and nobody in that room this evening knows that she was ever on that road, and she did not say and was not asked.

She had gone back on the Tuesday of the week before last, in her own time, on a bus in the morning and a bus in the evening, and she had about seven hours on the ground and she saw who she came to see.

Then she said it, at about ten to seven, to nobody in particular, unasked, in about nine seconds.

"**I lost the day first time and I have paid for the day I went back on, and that one is mine and that one is on me and it is not the same as the other.**"

Nobody asked her about the first day. Nobody asked her who she had gone to see. Nobody asked her what it cost in money and nobody asked her whether she had told anybody about it.

**She is owed nothing and she is not a villain and she is not a hero and she is not in anybody's case, and nobody thanked her and nobody improved on it, and she is not going to be asked to say it again, and nobody has said out loud that she will not be, and about four people in that room have said since that a day you chose and a day that was taken off you are not the same thing and about four have said nothing, and the two groups have not met and are not going to.**

Nobody said she was right. Nobody said she was wrong. Nobody said she should not have gone the second time either.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts was at the far end of the second bench on Monday evening and had not heard her, or had heard her and had not moved, and his eleven words are the only promise on the hardboard on the wall she was sitting under, and nobody in that room has ever put those two things next to each other, and nobody in that room knows that the line above the newest one on that board was written by a person who did not have a reason to write it.

A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

---

**The separation is four months and three weeks and one day old and it is not shorter and it is not ended, and nothing in that room bears on it, and the man of twenty-two has not said it out loud to anybody this month and nobody has asked him and nobody is going to.**

He did not ask her anything. That is the whole of what he did about it, and it took him about four seconds, and about four people in that room noticed that he did not ask and about four did not.

Nobody in that room has ever asked her about the first day and nobody in this case ever has and nobody is going to.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
