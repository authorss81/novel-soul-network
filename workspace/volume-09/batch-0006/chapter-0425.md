# Chapter 425 — Four Doors Out Of About Nine Hundred, And A Woman Standing Nine Feet Away Who Counts The Same Nine Hundred

The Monday of week one hundred and fifty-six was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and from about half past six in one room off a service road in the Lower Wards, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about thirty-three, who has held four buildings and about nine hundred doors since a Thursday in an earlier season with no stamp and no reference and no date and nobody's authority behind him, said out loud in a warm room, unasked and in about nine words, that about four of those doors have been standing open since before he took it on and he has never told anybody and is not going to be thanked for it, and nine feet from where he said it a woman of about thirty-four was standing, and her job is checking about nine hundred doors, and nobody in that room introduced them and neither of them knew the other was in it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and sixteen days old and is warm, and about four people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and twenty days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday of the fortnight before last, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

He had said yes to nine days of grade-two work in two districts of this city in a corridor four weeks ago, and five of the nine were gone and a sixth was the Monday of last week, and the other three are not going to be. Nobody had telephoned. The sheet of paper has a pen mark against his name for six of the nine and has no column for a reason and no column for a town.

---

He came in at about half past six and did not sit at the end of the second bench. He stood at the wall by the door for about four minutes, and a cook of twenty-seven was at a sink saying nothing, and in the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

**A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was not in the room on Monday evening. Nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-one weeks and five days, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it.**

**A woman of about thirty-four was at the far end of the room with a folded list on the back of a chair beside her, and she checks about nine hundred doors, and she was not at that counter and did not come from that counter, and she is not a clerk in any office in this city and holds no post and is owed nothing and is not a category and is not a name anybody in that room has.**

---

He said it at about ten to seven, to nobody in particular, and it took him about nine seconds.

"**About four of them have been standing open since before I had it. I have never said that to anybody and I am not after a thank you for it.**"

Nobody asked him which four. Nobody asked him what standing open means in a door. Nobody said he should tell somebody. Nobody said he had done well to say it and nobody said he had done badly to say it in a warm room to nobody.

**He is not a hero and not a villain and is owed nothing and is not in anybody's case. Nobody has asked him for anything in about thirty-five weeks and four days and nothing has been given to him and he has needed nothing. He is not going to be asked to say it again and nobody has said out loud that he will not be, and about four people in that room have said since that four doors out of nine hundred is nothing at all and about four have said nothing, and the two groups have not met and are not going to.**

---

The woman at the far end of the room had her back to him when he said it.

She has checked about nine hundred doors for about six years, and she does it in fours and eights with a folded list on the back of a chair, and she has never been to a room off a service road in her working day and came because of the temperature, and the temperature costs more and that is correct and both of those are the reason.

**Nobody in that room put the two of them together. About four people in that room knew by the Tuesday that the man who holds four buildings and the woman who checks nine hundred doors had been in the same room nine feet apart on the same Monday evening, and about four did not, and neither of them has been told that the other was there, and neither of them asked, and nobody thought to tell either of them, and it was not done on purpose by anybody.**

They left at about nine, separately, and he left first.

---

Two nails in that room carried a hardboard with nineteen lines on it and nothing was added to them and nothing was removed from them and nothing was rubbed off them. The newest of the nineteen is a lift mechanic's, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and twelve days old.

The first of the nineteen is a cook's and has never moved and is still the first line. **About four people in this city can now say, out loud, that a man who has stood in a yard for twenty minutes and not been asked one question has also stood in a warm room and been asked nothing and said one true thing, and about four cannot, and nobody has put the two facts on the same surface.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
