# Chapter 421 — A Man Of Twenty-Nine Who Works On Lifts Says So Out Loud, And Nobody Says Thank You

The Monday of week one hundred and fifty-five was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six, and from about half past six in one room off a service road in the Lower Wards, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts went into a block of flats on a Sunday, put the truth of what he had done in that building's own log against an entry that said nothing was wrong, and then said out loud, unasked, in about nine words, that he had done it and that it would change nothing, and nobody thanked him and nobody improved on it, and the eleven words he wrote on a hardboard on two nails are still the only promise on that board and they are three hundred and five days old and nothing was added to them and nothing was removed from them and nothing was rubbed off them.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and nine days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and thirteen days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card's age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

He had said yes to nine days of grade-two work in two districts of this city in a corridor three weeks ago, on a sheet of paper beside a laptop, and four of the nine were gone on the Thursday of the week before last and the fifth of the nine was Monday of the week before last, and the other four are not going to be. Nobody had telephoned. The sheet of paper has a pen mark against his name for five of the nine and has no column for a reason and no column for a town.

The rail had about forty slots in it and one card in one of them, and the man of about fifty-three who stands at that end of the counter had not mentioned the card that was not there since a Monday a fortnight ago, and nobody had asked him about it.

---

The block was four floors of flats off a street two streets back from that shop, twenty-two flats and one lift, and the lift had been standing at the ground floor with its doors shut since Saturday evening.

**The log is a book. It sits in a cupboard under the stairs in a plastic wallet and it has a column for a date and a column for what was found and a column for what was done, and it has no column for a name, and it has never had one, and about four people in that building have written in it and about four have read it and neither group has ever mentioned the other to anybody.**

The caretaker had written on the Friday afternoon that the lift was running normally and that no fault had been reported. That was not true and he had not meant it to be untrue, and he had written it at four o'clock on a Friday with a queue behind him at the door.

---

The man of about twenty-nine had come in on the Sunday on his own time, which is what he does, and he had been in the machine room for about two hours with the doors open on two floors and nobody in the car.

He wrote in the log, under the Friday entry, in the same hand as the caretaker, that the car had stopped between the second and the third on the Friday evening, that the cause was a worn door operator and a limit switch that had been sticking, and that both had been changed on the Sunday, and that the car had then run eleven times up and down empty.

**He did not write his name, because there was nowhere to write it, and he did not write the caretaker's name either, and he did not rub the Friday line out, and he did not draw a line through it, and he left the two entries sitting one under the other with a day between them and about nine days of nothing in between.**

The caretaker read it on the Monday morning at about eight, standing, with a cup in his hand.

"**You have put that it was not right on Friday.**"

"**It was not right on Friday.**"

"**I said it was right on Friday.**"

"**You said what you saw on Friday. I am not saying you were wrong. I am saying it was not right, and it is on there now under yours, and my name is not on it because there is nowhere to put it.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody said he should have rung somebody. Nobody asked him whether he was going to be paid for the Sunday, and he was not, and he did not raise it, and the firm that sends him has never paid anybody for a Sunday and has never been asked to by anybody he has worked for.

---

He came in at about half past six and sat down at the end of the second bench and said nothing for about nine minutes, and about four people in that room noticed the nine minutes and about four did not.

A cook of twenty-seven was at a sink saying nothing. A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about thirty weeks and five days, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

Then he said it, unasked, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular, in his own words, and it was about the log in the cupboard under the stairs and not about anything else in that room.

"**I have put the true thing in that book and it is under what he wrote, and there is nowhere on the page for me, and it changes nothing.**"

Nobody asked him about it. Nobody thanked him. Nobody said he was right. Nobody said he should have said something to the office. Nobody said the office would not have cared. **He is not going to be asked to say it again, and nobody has said out loud that he will not be, and about four people in that room have since said that a man who works on lifts ought to be able to leave a name somewhere and about four have said nothing at all, and the two groups have not met and are not going to.**

**He is owed nothing, he is not a hero, he is not a villain, he is not in anybody's case, and the correct thing he did on Sunday is a correct thing and it is on the page and it changed nothing.**

---

**Two nails in that room carried a hardboard with nineteen lines on it and the eight ages are set out in the docket below. Nothing was added to it on this Monday and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it, and no twentieth line is opened anywhere in this week, and the reason is not restraint: adding a promise to a board in order to have something to publish is adding a promise and not a line, and a twentieth line would be a new day and a new figure and a new thing to be wrong about, and this board has been the same nineteen lines since the first of them was written.**

The newest of the nineteen is that man's. Eleven words, signed, about the middle. It is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and five days old, and he was sitting under it when he said the thing about the book in the cupboard, and about four people in that room have worked that out and about four have not.

The first of the nineteen is a cook's and has never moved and is still the first line.

---

**And the man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall for the whole of that evening and said nothing, and the man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts was at the other end of the same bench and said one sentence, and the two of them have never spoken to each other in this case and neither of them is asked anything by anybody.** A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room and was not in this city on Monday, and his hold went on for about thirty-four weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him, and he has not been told any of this and nobody has told him anything.

---

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master's original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The book in a cupboard under the stairs in a block of flats off a street two streets back from that shop is a seventh, and it is a caretaker's book with a plastic wallet on it, and it is not any of the six.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
