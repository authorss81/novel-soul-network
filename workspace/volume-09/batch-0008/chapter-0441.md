# Chapter 441 — A Rail That Is A Queue Of One, And A Bar Fitted Across The Inside Of A Door

The Monday of week one hundred and sixty-two was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six and from about half past six in one room off a service road, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a bar of mild steel was fitted across the inside face of a door in a building in the third of the four districts so that a person could get out of the room with one hand, it went on the docket as a bar and nothing else, nobody thanked him and nobody improved on it, and along the front of that counter a rail has held one card where it held two since the Wednesday it came out, and a man of about fifty-three who has stood at that end for about eight years says out loud, unasked and in about nine seconds, that it is a queue of two and is a queue of one.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and fifty-eight days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and sixty-two days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

The morning had a bench in it and the afternoon had a counter, and neither of them had anything to do with the building in the third of the four districts, and nothing on that docket was for the company that runs the floor of a second floor in Crown Terrace, and nobody who came to that counter said the name of that company.

A woman of about seventy came in at about one with a door off its hinges in her own hands.

It was an ordinary door, the kind that is in about four buildings, and it had come away from the frame at the top hinge and the second hinge had dropped half out of the leaf, and she had carried it the length of two streets on a cart because she could not lift it further than that.

**She did not want a new door.** She wanted to be able to open it and shut it, and she said, standing at the end of the counter with her coat still on, that her grandson could not get out of that room when the latch had dropped, and that this had happened twice, and that she was seventy-one and not going to be able to lift it again by spring.

He asked her where the hinges were and she said they were on the frame and the frame was the wall, and he said he would look.

---

The door went on the docket at about ten past one as a door, and under it he wrote *frame*, and under that he wrote the third of the four districts and the street, because that is what goes on a docket.

He was there until about four and twenty minutes. The frame was sound. Both hinge leaves were sound. What was wrong was that the door had been hung so that the latch dropped before the leaf had come home, and that it had been hung that way by somebody who had wanted it to close by itself and had not cared that it would then not open from the inside without a hand on a handle that was nine feet away from it.

**He cut a bar of mild steel at the length of the opening, squared both ends, and fixed it in two staples on the inside face of the frame at about the height of a person's hip.** There were four screws. He put two of them in with a slotted driver and drew the other two tight enough to be certain and no tighter, so that a person with a thin pair of hands could lift the bar off the staples with one hand and put it back afterwards without looking for a tool.

The door shut and latched the same as it had before. The bar is a bar. It is not on any register, it has no code, it is not a fitting, it does not appear in any list of anything, and nobody asked him for one.

He wrote *bar* against the job and nothing else. He did not write what it was for and he was not asked, and he was not asked by anybody afterwards, and there is no column on that docket for what a thing is for.

**The shelf of finished work along the far wall holds about nine things at any one time and nobody can say what any of them are for at a glance.** A gate that opens the wrong way. Two handles for a pair of doors that no longer exist. A hinge that has been in a drawer since the spring. He puts them up when a thing is finished and he takes them down when he has not got a use for one, and there is no list of the shelf and there has never been one, and the shelf is not a stock record and does not go into any count anybody makes.

He swept the floor at about ten past four because the woman had tracked in with the cart, and he swept the grit out of the aisle by the parts cage where a man works with his back to the room all day and would not see a person do it and would not have said anything if he had.

**About four people came to that counter between about four and about six and about four of them were told no to something and the fifth was told a thing could be had on Thursday and not before.** He wrote that on the docket too. There is one docket and it is on the bench under the lamp and it is the only record of the day and it is not sent to anybody.

**Nobody came in between about half past five and about six and the shutter was down to the height of the counter and the light over the bench was the only light on the street that he could see from where he was standing.**

---

The woman paid and went and did not thank him and did not watch him do it. Nobody in that shop watched him do it. The counter had a lamp on an arm above it and a shelf of finished work along the far wall and about nine people came to it over the day, and about four of the nine were told no to something, and the ninth was the woman with the door.

**Nobody said the word bar to anybody in a way that meant anything else.** It is a piece of flat mild steel, about the length of a forearm, in two staples, and about four people in four buildings have one fitted by somebody in this case and nobody in this case has been told about the other three, and none of the four has been written down, and the woman who asked for it cannot say afterwards what she asked for because nobody asked her.

---

He walked four miles to the room at about half past six and sat at the end of the second bench. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about thirty-seven weeks and five days, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-one weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

A man of about fifty-three who has stood at the end of that counter for about eight years came to the counter on Monday at about four, which is not his usual hour, and stood at the end of it and looked along it.

**The rail is about forty slots and it has held two cards for as long as anybody there can remember and it held two until the Wednesday it came out.** There is no index and no book behind it and no slot marked and nobody in that building was told which slot it came out of, and the absence of a card from a rail is not an entry in anything.

He said it to the end of the counter and not to him, in about nine seconds, and then went back to his own end of it.

"**It is a queue and it was two and it is one and it has been one since the Wednesday.**"

Nobody asked him what he meant by it being a queue. Nobody asked him which two it was. Nobody said it was a good job or a bad one. Nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him, **and he has known it for a fortnight and has not been asked about it once and is not going to be, and the rail is a rail and holds what it holds.**

---

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and fifty-four days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it.** The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The bar in two staples on a door frame in the third of the four districts is a seventh and it is not any of the six and it was fitted today.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.

*The bar, as a record of a thing fitted for nobody in particular.* **What it is: flat mild steel, about the length of a forearm, in two staples, four screws, on the inside face of a frame in the third of the four districts, at about the height of a person’s hip. What it does: a person on the inside can lift it off with one hand, and the door still shuts and latches the same as it did before. What it does not do: it does not lock, it does not hold, it does not stop anybody coming in from outside, and nothing on the outside of that door tells anybody a bar is on the inside of it. Whether anybody has been told what it is for: nobody, and he was not asked and did not say, and the woman who came with the door could not say afterwards what she asked for because nobody asked her in words. What is on the docket: a door, a frame, a street, and the word bar, and there is no column for what a thing is for and there is no second copy of that docket anywhere. Whether it is a fitting: no, and it is not a device and it is not on any surface in this case. Whether it can be acted on by anybody: yes, by the person behind the door, and only by that person. How many people in this case know one is fitted: one. How many know where: one. How many know of another: none. Owner: a door frame, and nobody.**

How many it held for eight years: two. How many it holds now: one.
