# Chapter 442 — A Bar In The Second Of The Four Towns, And A Man Who Has Been Inside Four Buildings

The Wednesday of week one hundred and sixty-two was spent on a bus to the second of the four towns and in a hut about eight feet by six with a window and a stove and a kettle, and from about half past six in one room off a service road four miles from the counter, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about fifty-eight who keeps the only written record in that town cut a bar out of a pallet strap and fixed it across the inside of a door in his own hut with four screws, he did not say why and nobody asked him, and in the same fortnight about four people have done that in about four towns and none of the four knows about the other three, and a man of about fifty-six on a contractor's books has been inside four buildings he was not sent into and nobody has asked him to explain the four.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and sixty days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and sixty-four days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and it went into the second of the four towns in that pocket and came home in the same pocket and went into no rail.

---

The bus set down at about half past two at a set of scales on a concrete pad by the road, and the pad has been patched twice and the patch is visible from the road, and a shop on the other side shuts at four.

The hut is about eight feet by six. It has a window, a table, a stove, a kettle, a book on the table, and a box on a shelf by the door with about four hundred cards in it in plastic sleeves, one of which has a space for a plate and four boxes and one line at the bottom.

A man of about fifty-eight keeps the only written record in that town. He is not a clerk, he holds no post, he is owed nothing and he is not thanked, and nobody outside that hut has asked him what his job is because he does not have one.

He was in the hut on Wednesday with the book open and about four people in it and a lorry that would not go.

**The practice is nearly right and it is nearly right in the same way it has been for about nine years.** A weight written in the book by hand and a count of pallets off a lorry by a second person against the number on the ticket, and if the two do not agree the lorry does not go. It has caught one thing in about nine years. It cannot catch a load that was wrong before it came there.

About four people work that pad. A man of about twenty-six has been there since the spring and has been the second person on the check every single time, and that a check whose second person is always the first person is not a check has still not been said out loud by anybody.

**The concrete has a lip about nine inches high all the way round it and the lorry comes up to the lip and not over it, and a person standing on the pad has to be about a foot higher than a person on the ground to see the top of a load, and everybody who works there knows that and nobody has written it down.**

The weighbridge hut is eight feet by six and there is one chair in it and the chair is for whoever is writing and there is no second chair, so the second person on the check stands. He has been standing since the spring.

A lorry came off that road at about ten past two with a second one behind it that did not stop. The man of about fifty-eight watched it go from the window of the hut and wrote nothing down, and the man of about twenty-six watched it go and said nothing, and the two behind him were doing something with a clipboard and were not watching.

**The book on that table is the only written record in that town and it is a hardback with a quarter of one side used and the rest of it blank, and it has a date at the top of each page and a weight and a count and a second pair of initials in the margin, and there is no column for a reason and no column for a refusal.**

About four people were in the hut at about two. The stove was going. Nobody was talking and nobody had been talking for about an hour before he stood up.

**The window in that hut faces the pad and not the road, and a person standing at it can see the top of a load and cannot see what is behind a load, and about four people have worked that out in about nine years and about four of them are the same man.**

---

He was at the table at about half past two with the book shut in front of him and a piece of flat steel about the length of a forearm lying beside it.

**He said nothing about it while he worked.** He cut the bar with a hacksaw, took the burr off the two ends against the concrete outside, squared them on the anvil by the stove, and fixed them into two staples he had already set in the frame of the door at about the height of a person's hip, on the inside face, and he used a slotted driver and he put two of the four screws in loosely and drew the other two until he was certain and no tighter.

The door was a back door. It had a latch on it and a key on the outside and nothing on the inside except the bar, and a person could lift the bar off with one hand and go out and come back and put it up again without a tool.

He did it in about twenty minutes and he did not say what it was for while he did it and he did not say afterwards.

The man of about twenty-six looked at the table. The two behind him did not look up. The lorry outside went, and the pad was empty by about three, and the book on the table was not written in on Wednesday and the bar is not in it.

**Nobody asked him why. Nobody said it was a good idea. Nobody asked whether the other doors in that building had one, and one of them has, and it has been there since before the spring.**

---

He came home on the bus and walked four miles to the room at about half past six. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was not in that room and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-eight weeks, and nobody has renewed the asking and nobody has stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-one weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

**A man of about fifty-six on a contractor's books was in that room on Wednesday, which is not his evening, and he has a folded sheet in his inside pocket with five names in five hands on it, and he has been inside four buildings in this city that he was not sent into, and nobody in this case has ever asked him to explain the four and nobody in this case is going to.**

He did not explain them. He sat at the far end and he did not say where he had been that day and he did not say it on Thursday either.

He is owed nothing and he is not thanked and nobody has put the four buildings beside each other in his hearing.

---

**A man of about fifty-six in this city has been handed a maintenance rota for a building of nine flats over a canteen by a man who cannot say out loud why he does not want it, and the rota came down off a nail in a building of nine flats over a canteen in an earlier week and nobody collected it, and the man who took it has not been in that building since, and the man of about fifty-three who has stood at the end of a counter for about eight years has not been asked about any of it.**

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and fifty-six days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it.** The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The card in a plastic sleeve in a box on a shelf in a hut on that road is a ninth object and is merged with none of the six, and a bar across the inside of a door in that hut is a tenth and is merged with none of the others.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
