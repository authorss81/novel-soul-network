# Chapter 443 — Five Hundred And Eighty-One Days On A Board, And A Request Refused In About Nine Seconds

The Thursday of week one hundred and sixty-two was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six and from about half past six in one room off a service road, and the one thing that is true of it is that **the twelve lines on that hardboard are five hundred and eighty-one days old and five hundred and eighty-one is the day a landlord put the heating on, so the figure has to be read as an age of a piece of writing and not as a date, and a man of about forty-three asked a man of twenty-two to come to a building for an hour and tell four people what to do, and was told no in about nine seconds, and nobody improved on it.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and sixty-one days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Thursday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and sixty-five days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

Two nails carry a hardboard with nineteen lines on it, and eight of those lines are old enough to have a figure attached to them, and one of the eight is old enough that the figure is also something else entirely.

**The twelve lines are five hundred and eighty-one days old. Five hundred and eighty-one is how long twelve hands of writing have been on a piece of hardboard, and it is an age, and it is not a day, and it is not when anything was switched on or switched off, and a person reading it as a day has read a thing that is not there.**

**Five hundred and eighty-one is also the day a landlord put the heating on in this city.** The heating is a closed thing in this case. It went off once and a landlord turned it on once, and nobody in this case counts it in weeks, and there is no figure of days off for it anywhere in this case and there is not going to be one, and the fortnight of it that a cook laid out in a kitchen in this city is paid for by nobody and is not going to be paid by a number on a board.

The other seven on that board are the thirteen, at five hundred and thirty-two, the fourteen, at four hundred and ninety-seven, the fifteen, at four hundred and seventy-six, the sixteen, at four hundred and fifty-one, the seventeen, at four hundred and thirty-three, the eighteen, at three hundred and seventy-nine, and the nineteen, at three hundred and fifty-seven, which is fifty-one weeks to the day, which is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it has been there for fifty-one weeks and nothing has ever been done about it.

**There is no twentieth line. Not on this Thursday and not anywhere in this week, and the reason is not restraint: a twentieth line would be a new day and a new figure and a new thing to be wrong about, and nobody has come to that board with a twentieth thing to put on it and nobody has ever asked anybody to bring one.**

---

He was at the bench until about two and at the counter until about six.

A man of about forty-three came in at about half past two and had come for a different thing and then did not say what he had come for, and stood at the end of the counter for about a minute and then asked.

He said he fitted stairs. He said there were four people in a building in the second of the four districts who had been told their room had to go with another room, and that they had said no, and that he had heard from a woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors that there is a man of twenty-two in this city who asks people what they will not do rather than what they will do.

He asked for an hour. He said he would pay for the hour. He said the four of them would be in one room and that the man of twenty-two would not have to say anything he had not said before.

**He had the part with him in a paper bag and he put it down on the counter while he asked and he picked it up again when he had said the thing and he carried it out with him.** Nobody in that shop looked at the bag. Nobody in that shop knew what was in it and nobody has since and nobody is going to ask.

**He did not say the name of the company and he did not say which building and he said the word room three times and the word rota not once, and the man at the bench heard all three of the rooms and did not hear anything else.**

**He was told no in about nine seconds and the nine seconds are the whole of it.**

He said he could not come and that there was no version of the week in which he could come and that this was not about the hour and was not about the money. He did not give a reason. He did not give a condition. He did not ask the man of about forty-three to come back.

The man of about forty-three said that was fair and paid for a part he had brought and left with it.

**Nobody improved on the refusal and nobody thanked him for it and nobody asked him again.** Nobody in that shop asked him why and nobody in the building in the second of the four districts was told that he had been asked, because he was not told, and the four of them are still four people in one room with no more than they had on Thursday morning, and none of them is in this case and none of them has a name in it.

He teaches nobody, demonstrates nothing to anybody, assesses nobody and assists nobody, and has been asked to do one of those four things more times than anybody in this case has kept a count of, and the count is not on anything and is not going to be.

---

He walked four miles to the room at about half past six and sat at the end of the second bench. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Thursday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about thirty-eight weeks and one day, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-two weeks without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors was at the far end of the room with a folded list. She did not ask him anything on Thursday and she did not say the name of a man of about forty-three and nobody at the far end of that room said anything at all about a building in the second of the four districts.

**Her bag was on the floor by her feet and the list went into it when she sat down and neither of them was opened again that evening, and there is a line on that list for Thursday and it is a line that says a door needed nothing, and there are about nine of those on the sheet and one of them, from an earlier week, has a time beside it.**

---

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it.** The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others and did not get a fourth person in it on Thursday.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
