# Chapter 450 — The Thirty-First Sitting, A Book That Does Not Open, And A Card Going Back Into A Rail

The Wednesday of week one hundred and sixty-eight was the thirty-first sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and the one thing that is true of it is that **the book stood at fifty-five lines when the morning began and it stands at fifty-five lines now, and the tin did not move, and the count came out at thirty-six of which thirty-one correspond, and the ask is three hundred and ninety-two days old on that Wednesday, which is fifty-six weeks to the day, counted from the day it was asked and not from any sitting since, and at about four in the afternoon in a repair shop four miles off, a piece of card that had been in a coat pocket for one hundred and nineteen days, which is seventeen weeks, went back into a rail of about forty slots at the end of a counter, and a card in a rail is not a ticket and not a key and it opens nothing.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is seven hundred and two days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card is seven hundred and six days old, four days more than the room is. A card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up and it does not start again when a card is put down, and it has been seven hundred and six days old every single day of it, including the hundred and nineteen it spent in a coat pocket.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and they came down at about eleven, and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on, and this is the thirty-first and last of them that anybody in this case will write down.

**There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. It did not move in about four hours. Nobody in that room looked at it more than once, and about four people in that room have wondered in all of that time who put it there, and nobody has ever asked, and its mover is not named and is not going to be named, and it is against the wall this morning for the last time in this case and it is going to stay against the wall.**

There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, open for about four hours and not shut once.

There were about eleven people, and about four of them are not described this morning.

**She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.**

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece, and none of the six is described here, and that is not a change and is not a pattern and is not a rule and is not a judgement and is not in anybody's mouth.

The first was a question. The second was a question. The third was a person who did not ask one and said nothing for four minutes. The fourth was the same as the third. The fifth was nothing anybody has reported. The sixth was nothing anybody has reported.

**Nobody said a thing to her face that was not a question, and that is the whole of why the book is where it is, and it has been the whole of it at every one of the five sittings of this year and at every sitting before them, and nobody has ever told her that and nobody is going to.**

**The book is at fifty-five lines. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced thirty-six times. The whole business is nineteen years old. Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and every one of the four is the whole of itself, and none was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the first two of them is not a number and was not given as one, and it has never been a number in nineteen years and it is not going to be one.**

**Nobody asked her for the rule about turning one figure into another.** She has not given it in nineteen years and she has kept her promise five times in a year of this and once in the year before it, and she kept it again this morning, and nothing was put in the space it left and nothing was offered in its place, and nobody in that room wanted anything offered.

**No number was given into the tin this morning, and none has been given into it on any day of this year.** The tin stands at seventy-three, which is where it stood when this year began, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody stood up. Nothing about any sitting before this one has ever been said in that room.

---

The ask was made by a man of about forty-four at the seventeenth sitting, on a Wednesday, and it is three hundred and ninety-two days old today, which is fifty-six weeks to the day, and it is counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since.

**It is not fifty-two weeks, which was the figure four weeks ago and belongs to a Wednesday four weeks ago and is not this one, and any sheet that carries that one and not this one is carrying a number for a different day.**

Nobody in that room put the ask. Nobody in that room has put it at any of the five sittings of this year, and the man who put it has not put it since, and no one else in that room has put it since either, and she has not offered it once and nobody has asked her for it, and it is still open and it is still not going to be answered and nobody has said it will not be.

---

He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at a counter from about two until about six, and the rail of about forty card slots is along the front of that counter and it has held one card since a Wednesday in the spring before this year began.

**He put the card back into it at about four in the afternoon.** It came out of the inside pocket of a coat that has a hook behind a bench, and he stood at the front of the counter and looked along the rail, which has no order in it and no index and no book behind it and no slot marked, and he put it into a slot and not the slot it came out of, because nobody could say afterwards which of the forty that was.

The rail holds two. It held two for eight years and it held one for a hundred and nineteen days, which is seventeen weeks, and it holds two now, and a man of about fifty-three who has stood at that end for about eight years looked at it and said nothing.

**He put his hand into the coat pocket afterwards and found the pocket empty and did not stop, and the pocket is the inside pocket of a coat on a hook behind a bench and there is nothing in it and there has been nothing in it since about four in the afternoon, and nobody saw him put his hand in and nobody is going to ask him about it.**

**The man of about fifty-three took his cup, which he does not drink from, and put it on the end of the counter and went back to his own end of it, and the counter has about nine people on it over a day and the rail is behind the till and nobody at that counter looks at the rail in the ordinary way of a Tuesday.**

**Nobody thanked him for putting it back. Nobody said anything about it. Nobody in that building has ever been told which slot it came out of or which slot it went into, and the day it came out is not written down anywhere anybody has shown him, and the absence of a card from a rail was not an entry in anything and the presence of one is not either.**

**A card in a rail is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing. It has a place-name on one side in a hand that is not the hand of the man who is holding it now, and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, and no title and no office and nothing on the back of it, and there is nothing on this side of the counter that will let a person who is not a clerk do anything at all with it.**

A man of about twenty-two could have shown it at a gate on a road in an earlier week of this year and one person would not have asked him one question, and he worked out in about four minutes that it would work, and he did not, and nobody was told that he had worked it out and nobody is going to be, and it cannot be done by anybody now and it was not done by him, and that is said once and is not going to be said again.

---

He did not go four miles that evening. He walked to the room at about half past six and sat at the end of the second bench and did not stay long.

**A woman of about thirty-four who checks about nine hundred doors was at the far end with a folded list and she did not look up, and a man of about thirty-one who works nights was at the wall and did not say anything, and about four people came in for the temperature and went out again, and the kettle was on and nobody made a cup of anything, and the shutter on that service road came down at about ten, and nobody thanked anybody.**

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Wednesday and is not going to be read.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was not in that room and nobody has asked him about anything for about forty-four weeks to the day, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room and is not in this city, and his hold went on for about forty-seven weeks and six days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him, and it is going to go on.

Nobody in that room said a word about a card, and nobody in that room has ever been told that a card went anywhere, and nobody asked him about the spring and nobody is going to.

---

**Nothing in this case was closed today and nothing in this case has been closed in a hundred and nineteen days, and the things that were open are open.**

The printed paragraph is on the back of a page of paper and it is not withdrawn and it cannot be, and about four hundred competent people in this city can each invoke it and not one of them has anywhere to put an objection, and nobody put one there this year. Four rooms in four districts have each refused a merge by word and two of them have never told the other two and no two of them have met. Nine hand copies of the front of a page of paper are in their thirty-eighth week and two days and about eight of the nine are unfinished, and the first disagreement, a figure in the fourth line that is right in six copies and wrong in three, has not been found and is not going to be found by anybody in this case. There is a copy of the front of that page face up on a table in a weighbridge hut in the working Ashfields and nobody in that hut has turned it over and this case did not turn it over either. Nine keys are needed for a room of about nine people and two exist and the first of the nine still cannot be asked back by anybody. The licensor is unnamed, the field is blank, the master's fifth line is blank, the drawer is shut, a pencil box in a street in another district was not opened. The heating is on and costs more and four names on a lease cost more than they did and a fortnight a cook laid out is paid for by nobody and is not going to be paid by anybody in this case. Nine doors came off their hinges in four buildings on a road on the far side of this city in an earlier week of this year and nobody is paid to put them back. A week went from about eleven people in a room in one of the four towns and it has not come back. A sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not on anything and has not been adopted by anybody.

**The standing question and the forward question, both written once on a service road, are in nobody's mouth and are not in anybody's mouth today and were not in anybody's mouth in any of the four hundred days this case has run, and declining to say them is not the same as forgetting them and is not going to be written down anywhere as having been forgotten.**

---

**A man of twenty-two keeps one room off a service road and one written record across a district. He runs no sessions and he teaches nobody and he demonstrates nothing to anybody and he assesses nobody and he assists nobody, and he has been asked to do one of those four things more times than anybody in this case has kept a count of, and the count is on nothing. He is on a maintenance rota he did not write, and the rota names a person he does not trust as the one who takes it, and he handed it over in an earlier year and it has not come back and nobody has asked him whether it was the right call and nobody is going to.**

**The six surfaces in this case are six. The card in a rail in a repair shop in Lattice Ward is the first of them and it is a rail this afternoon and it was a coat pocket on the other nine days of this year, and a card in a rail is not a card in a pocket, and that is a fact about today and it is not a merger of anything with anything. The wall list in its plastic sleeve in that shop is a second and has never been on two nails. The hardboard on two nails is a third. The master's original is a fourth, in a fourth pocket in a tool roll, with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a fifth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The card in a plastic sleeve in a box by a set of scales on a road on the far side of this city is a seventh and is merged with none of the six.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
