# Chapter 447 — The Tenth Friday, Eleven People On A Floor Four Miles Away, And Nothing Joined To Anything

The Friday of week one hundred and sixty-three was again a second floor in Crown Terrace from about half past six until about nine, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a run went on that floor for the eighth week running and nobody said how long it took and nobody asked, and it is also the tenth of the Fridays that building has had, and those two are not the same count and nobody on that floor has ever been given either, the post at the corridor end has now carried nothing for two hundred and seventeen days, which is thirty-one weeks, and about eleven people were on that floor on the Friday and none of them was in a room four miles off and none of them was in a town on the far side of this city and nobody has put the two together and nobody is going to.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them at about two in the afternoon. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and sixty-nine days old and is warm. The card is six hundred and seventy-three days old, four days more than that room is, and it is in a coat pocket, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

**The shop did not know what happened on that floor the Friday before and would not have known what happened on this one, and nothing that happened on a road on the far side of this city on Wednesday has reached anybody who was on that floor and nobody who was on that floor was anywhere near it.**

---

**It was the tenth of these Fridays, and it is the last of them.** The one before it was a week back and the one before that was three weeks back and the one before that was a week back and the one before that was a fortnight back, and the Fridays in that building do not arrive at a fixed distance from each other, and nobody who comes up that stair has ever been told what the distances between them are.

**Two counts of them are kept here and they do not agree with each other and both are correct. This is the tenth, and it is also the eighth week of the run, and the run is counted from a different Friday than the tenth is counted from, and the gap between the two counts has been the same since the run began. Neither figure has ever been put to anybody in that company.** The woman of about thirty-six with the lanyard knows it is the tenth, and the man of about thirty in overalls knows it is the eighth week of the run, and neither of them has ever said either figure out loud. The woman of about twenty-eight and the man of about twenty-eight know one of the two and not the other, and neither of them has been asked which.

The same floor, in the same corridor, with the same about nine folding chairs against the wall and the same desk by the near fire door and the same stair and the same stairwell light.

There was a post at the corridor end with nothing on it. It has now carried nothing for two hundred and seventeen days, which is thirty-one weeks, and nobody has asked for it to be filled and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it.

**In the second drawer on the left of that desk there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and the drawer does not lock, and no separate count is printed for that sheet anywhere in this entry, because two different dates for it exist in this case and differ by twenty-eight days, and the post’s own figure above is the one that walks.**

The far fire door was still propped with a folded sheet of paper. Nobody has asked the man of about fifty-eight who drives that company’s van to take it away and nobody has ever asked him anything, and he was not in that building on Friday.

---

About eleven people were on the floor between about half past six and about nine. About seven of them are on the company’s list. About four are on none of anything, and all of those about four were there.

Nobody chased anybody. Nobody took a count of the four on no list in eight weeks and nobody has ever taken one.

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Nobody said how long it took and nobody asked and nobody referred to any other Friday.

**Nobody in that building knew that about four things had gone wrong on a road on the Wednesday, and nobody on that road knew that eleven people had been on a floor in this city on the Friday, and the two facts stand four miles apart in one direction and in different cities in the other, and nothing joins them.**

Nobody in that building was saved by anything that happened on that road and nothing that happened on that road reached them. A person was on that floor at half past seven on the Friday and was not helped by a single one of the four things and would not have been and nobody has told them anything, because there is nothing anybody could tell them, and because a person who is not in a thing is not saved by it and is not missed by it and does not know its name.

At about nine the chairs went up and the lights went off in an order that has nothing to do with anything.

At about ten to eight a woman of about twenty-eight said one ordinary sentence to the man of about twenty-eight about the light on that stair and nothing else, and the two of them have never been asked which of the two counts they have.

---

He was at the bench until about two and at the counter until about six and heard nothing of Wednesday except that a man of about thirty-four had been in and gone.

**The steel went out of that shop in the same paper bag the man of forty-three had carried a part in in on Thursday, and nobody in that shop said so, and the bag is a bag, and there is no way of knowing whose bag it was and no way of asking.**

A man of about fifty-six on a contractor's books came in at about half past five on the Friday and bought a bar of flat steel, mild, about the length of a forearm, and paid for it and took it away with him, and nobody asked him what it was for and nobody has since.

**He bought the same length of the same section that is across the inside of a door in the third of the four districts, and nobody in that shop has been told that, and he did not say, and the man at the bench did not ask, and the two of them have not compared notes and will not.**

He was at the counter until about six and then he did not go four miles, and the room off that service road was warm on the Friday evening and about four people went in for the temperature and none of them was asked anything, and nobody at the far end of it knew that a run had gone on a floor in this city, and nobody in that room was in a room in any of the four towns.

**About nine people had come into that room over six nights and about four of them had come in more than once and the room does not count them and has no way of knowing who has been twice, and the man of about thirty-one who works nights has been in it on the other four of the seven nights and has never been asked for anything.**

**The temperature in that room is the reason four people were in it on Friday and the man of twenty-two was in it for about forty minutes and came out into a street that was colder than it had been at six, and the heating in that room is on and dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did.**

**That is the tenth of those Fridays and it is the last one there will be in this case, and nobody on that floor was told it was the last, and nobody asked, and the chairs went up at about nine in an order that has nothing to do with anything, and the stairwell light is still on and will be on all night.**

---

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and sixty-five days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it.** The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The bar in that shop on Friday afternoon is not one of the six and is not the bar in the third of the four districts and is not the bar in the second of the four towns, and the three are three.

Nobody thanked anybody.
