# Chapter 449 — The Thirtieth Sitting, A Book That Does Not Open, And Four Minutes At A Bench

The Wednesday of week one hundred and sixty-four was the thirtieth sitting of an Exchange that has run on four-week spacing for nineteen years, and the one thing that is true of it is that **the book stood at fifty-five lines when the morning began and it stands at fifty-five lines now, and the tin did not move, and the count came out at thirty-five of which thirty correspond, and the ask is three hundred and sixty-four days old on that Wednesday, which is fifty-two weeks, counted from the day it was asked and not from any sitting since, and at about half past four in the same city, four miles off and in a different building, a man of twenty-two told a woman of twenty-four what his father did in about nine sentences in about four minutes and she stayed and she did not offer to make it better and nobody thanked anybody.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and seventy-four days old and is warm, and about four people went in on the Wednesday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and seventy-eight days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

There was one room above a line in a building in Saltmarket. There were shutters on that landing and they were up at about ten and they came down at about eleven, and the woman who holds that room has said so at the start of every sitting for nineteen years and nobody in that room has ever told her not to and nobody has ever asked her to go on.

**There were nine chairs in a curve with the two ends turned in, and one chair set hard against the wall with its back to all of it. It did not move in about four hours. Nobody in that room looked at it more than once, and about four people in that room have wondered in all of that time who put it there, and nobody has ever asked, and its mover is not named and is not going to be named.**

There was a table. There was a tin with no lock on it and never one. There was a book of four columns, face up, within reach of her right hand the whole morning, open for about four hours and not shut once.

There were about eleven people, and about four of them are not described this morning.

**She was not on any sheet of it. Nothing in that room has ever carried her name and nothing ever will.**

---

Six items, at four minutes apiece.

The first was a person who wanted to know whether a thing that had been done in a room in one of the four towns on the Wednesday before had to be done in this one, and she said that it was not a thing she knew anything about, and she wrote nothing, and the person did not press her and nobody in that room pressed her.

The second was a woman who had brought a list of hours again and asked whether the hours were the business of the room, and she said they were not, and she wrote nothing.

The third was a person who asked her a question about a door and she answered it in about nine seconds and wrote nothing, and the answer has not been written down by anybody and is not repeated here.

The fourth was a man of about fifty-six on a contractor's books who had come up the road and had been in a room above a line in this city before, and who asked her nothing at all, and she offered him nothing, and he said nothing for four minutes and then said one ordinary sentence about a stairwell light, and it was about the stairwell light.

The fifth was nothing anybody has reported.

**The sixth was nothing anybody has reported either, and the sixth item took its four minutes, and about four people in that room have said since that they do not know what it was about, and nobody in that room has asked and nobody is going to.**

---

**Nobody said a thing to her face that was not a question.** That is the whole of why the book is where it is, and it is not a rule and it is not a judgement and it is not written anywhere and nobody has ever been told about it by anybody, including her.

Nobody asked her to open it. Nobody mentioned that it had not been opened. Nobody in that room wanted anything offered and nobody offered anything.

**The book is at fifty-five lines. The tin is at seventy-three. The count has been announced thirty-five times. The whole business is nineteen years old. Four figures, on a table, in a morning, and every one of the four is the whole of itself, and none was turned into another one this morning, and the difference between the first two of them is not a number and was not given as one.**

**Nobody asked her for the rule about turning one figure into another.** She has not given it in nineteen years and promised, in front of nine people, to say it for the last time, and she kept that promise again this morning, and nothing was put in the space it left and nothing was offered in its place.

**No number was given into the tin this morning.** The tin stands at seventy-three, and it stood at seventy-three at the last sitting four weeks ago, and it stood at seventy-three at the one before that, and nobody counted it and nobody asked why it had not moved.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody stood up. Nothing about the sitting before this one has ever been said in that room.

---

The ask was made by a man of about forty-four at the seventeenth sitting, on a Wednesday, and it is three hundred and sixty-four days old today, which is fifty-two weeks to the day. It is counted from the day it was asked and never from any sitting since.

**It is not three hundred and sixty-two days, which is a Monday's figure and belongs to a Monday three days ago and is not this one, and it is not three hundred and sixty-five days, and any sheet that carries one of those and not this one is carrying a number for a different day.**

Nobody in that room put the ask. Nobody in that room has put it at any of the four sittings in about four months, and the man who put it has not put it since, and no one else in that room has put it since either, and she has not offered it once and nobody has asked her for it.

---

He was at a bench in Lattice Ward from about half past seven until about two and at a counter from about two until about half past four.

**Between about two and about half past four there were about nine people at that counter and about four of them were told no to something and the rest were not told anything either way, and the docket went under the lamp and it is the only account of the day and it does not have a column for the four minutes and it is not going to get one.**

**At about half past four the shutter was half down and the lamp on the arm was on and Talia Venn came in off the street and stood at the end of the counter for about four minutes.**

He told her what his father did. He told it in nine sentences. It took about four minutes and there was no scene in it and nobody came in and the counter stayed empty for the whole of it.

**The lamp on the arm was on and the shutter was half down and the door it shuts off is four miles from the door of a building with shutters that go up at about ten, and the two of those sets of shutters have nothing to do with each other and nobody has ever put them together.**

He said, in nine sentences, that his father had spent his working life being the person an answer went through; that one afternoon in a room with about nine people in it he had put his own hand flat on the thing that carried the answer, in about four seconds, and had said nothing while he did it; that he had not been brave and had not been asked to be; that afterwards he could not do the thing his hands had always done and could not tell anybody that he could not do it, and the one person who could have been told was not told for nineteen years; that he was on a ward inside the week and dead inside the year; that the form that says he walked away has a man's real signature on it and the man was not lying; that the answer was settled before the room filled and about nine people were in it and none of them was asked; that his mother said it in eleven sentences at a sink and wrote none of it down and there is nowhere she could put it; and that there is no card that opens anything and no key and no right and nothing on the back of the page that is a proof, and there is one memory of it and one memory is not an archive.

**She stayed for the whole of the four minutes. She did not offer to make it better and she did not say it was not his fault and she did not say she was sorry and she did not ask him a question about any of it. She said, at the end, that she had a van at about six and she was going to use the time, and she said nothing else about it.**

He wrote none of it down. She wrote none of it down. Nobody knows that it was said except the two of them, and neither of them is going to tell anybody, and nobody has been asked.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody thanked him. Nobody was thanked.

---

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and seventy days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it.** The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The book in a plastic box by a set of scales on that road is a ninth object and is merged with none of the six.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
