# Chapter 444 — The Ninth Friday, A Post Bare Thirty Weeks, And Two Figures That Are Each Other's Days

The Friday of week one hundred and sixty-two was again a second floor in Crown Terrace from about half past six until about nine, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a run went on that floor for the seventh week running and nobody said how long it took and nobody asked, and it is also the ninth of the Fridays that building has had, and those two are not the same count and nobody on that floor has ever been given either, the post at the corridor end has now carried nothing for two hundred and ten days, which is thirty weeks, and the card in a coat pocket in another building is six hundred and sixty-six days old and six hundred and sixty-six is the day the only promise on a hardboard four miles off was written, and that line is three hundred and fifty-eight days old and three hundred and fifty-eight is the day the card went into the rail it came out of.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about two in the afternoon. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and sixty-two days old and is warm. The card is six hundred and sixty-six days old, four days more than that room is, and it is in a coat pocket, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up.

**The shop did not know what happened on that floor and would not have known what happened on this one.**

**A Friday counter is shorter than a Wednesday one and nobody has ever worked out why and nobody has asked.** About four people came in over the whole afternoon and two of them were told no to something and one of them was told the same thing she had been told the week before, and the week before that, and she said she knew, and he wrote nothing about the two times she has said it.

**The man in the parts cage had the radio off all afternoon and it is off every Friday, and nobody in that building has ever asked him to have it on and nobody has ever asked him why he has it off.** Eleven on the docket and eleven in date, the last of them at about two, a counter until about six, a shutter, and then four miles on foot. Nothing on that docket was for the building in Crown Terrace and nothing on it was for the company that runs the floor, and nobody who came to that counter said the name of that company.

---

**It was the ninth of these Fridays, and there is one more of them, and it has not happened yet.** The one before it was three weeks back and the one before that was a week back and the one before that was a fortnight back and the one before that was a week back, and the Fridays in that building do not arrive at a fixed distance from each other, and nobody who comes up that stair has ever been told what the distances between them are.

**Two counts of them are kept here and they do not agree with each other and both are correct. This is the ninth, and it is also the seventh week of the run, and the run is counted from a different Friday than the ninth is counted from, and the gap between the two counts has been the same since the run began. Neither figure has ever been put to anybody in that company.** The woman of about thirty-six with the lanyard knows it is the ninth, and the man of about thirty in overalls knows it is the seventh week of the run, and neither of them has ever said either figure out loud. The woman of about twenty-eight and the man of about twenty-eight know one of the two and not the other, and neither of them has been asked which.

The same floor, in the same corridor, with the same about nine folding chairs against the wall and the same desk by the near fire door and the same stair and the same stairwell light.

There was a post at the corridor end with nothing on it. It has now carried nothing for two hundred and ten days, which is thirty weeks, and nobody has asked for it to be filled and nobody has asked what is meant to go on it.

**In the second drawer on the left of that desk there is a laminated sheet with about nine lines on it, and the drawer does not lock, and no separate count is printed for that sheet anywhere in this entry, because two different dates for it exist in this case and differ by twenty-eight days, and the post’s own figure above is the one that walks.**

The far fire door was still propped with a folded sheet of paper. Nobody has asked the man of about fifty-eight who drives that company’s van to take it away and nobody has ever asked him anything, and he was not in that building on Friday and has not been in it since the Friday before the one before this.

---

About eleven people were on the floor. About seven of them are on the company’s list. About four are on none of anything, and all of those about four were there, and none of them was asked anything this week.

Nobody chased anybody. Nobody took a count of the four on no list in seven weeks and nobody has ever taken one.

The run went at half past seven. Nine people, one floor, about nine minutes, called from the front by a woman of about thirty-six with a lanyard. Nobody said how long it took and nobody asked and nobody referred to any other Friday.

At about ten to eight a man of about thirty-nine who works in that company and buys its training sat at the back and said nothing for about nine minutes, and then said an ordinary sentence about the weather to the man beside him, and it was about the weather.

At about nine the chairs went up and the lights went off in an order that has nothing to do with anything.

---

**The card in a coat pocket is six hundred and sixty-six days old, and six hundred and sixty-six is the day a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts wrote the only promise on a hardboard on two nails, and that writing is three hundred and fifty-eight days old, and three hundred and fifty-eight is the day the card went into the rail it came out of.** Both of the figures are ages. Both of them are also days. Neither of them is a date anybody could use to arrive anywhere, and the two of them have never been on one sheet in this case and nobody in this city has ever been handed both of them together.

**There is a bar across the inside of a door in the third of the four districts and a bar across the inside of a door in the second of the four towns on that road and neither of them is on any surface in this case and nobody has been told that there are two of them, and one of the two was fitted on a Monday four days ago by a man of twenty-two who did not say what it was for and was not asked.**

Nobody in that building has ever been told what any Friday in it was for, by anybody, and no sheet and no form and no line on any hardboard in this case says what any Friday was, and nobody has gone looking for one.

Nobody thanked anybody.

What is written about it afterwards: nothing, and there is no sheet for it and no form and no line, and the woman who called it did not write down that she called it and has not told anybody that she did. Whether that figure is a date: no, and it is not a day anybody could arrive on and it is not when the card was written. What the same figure is: the day a man of about twenty-nine who works on lifts wrote the only promise on a hardboard on two nails.
