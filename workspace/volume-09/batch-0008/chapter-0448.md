# Chapter 448 — Six Hundred And Seventy-Two Days And Three Hundred And Sixty-Two, And A Bar Used The Wrong Way Round

The Monday of week one hundred and sixty-four was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six and from about half past six in one room off a service road, and the one thing that is true of it is that **the room off that service road is six hundred and seventy-two days old and six hundred and seventy-two is the day the ask was asked, and the ask is three hundred and sixty-two days old and three hundred and sixty-two is the day that room opened, and a man of about thirty-one who works nights lifted a bar off the inside of a door at about two in the morning to get in rather than to get out and has said so out loud in about nine words to nobody in particular, and nobody improved on it and nobody repaid him for the four screws.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and seventy-two days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and seventy-six days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

**The room off that service road is six hundred and seventy-two days old. Six hundred and seventy-two is the day a man of about forty-four asked a woman in a room above a line in this city for a rule she has not given since an earlier week, and that ask is three hundred and sixty-two days old today, and three hundred and sixty-two is the day this room was opened.**

Both of the figures are ages. Both of them are also days. Neither of them is a date anybody can arrive on, and the two have never been handed to anybody together in this case, and nobody in this city has ever seen them next to each other on one sheet, and the woman who made that ask is four miles off in a building with shutters and does not know that either number is also something else.

The ask will be reported on Wednesday, in about two days, because the Exchange sits on that Wednesday, and the figure it will be given is the one counted from the day it was asked and not from any sitting since, and it is not the figure above and the figure above is not the one for Wednesday.

---

He was at the bench until about two and at the counter until about six.

A woman of about forty-five came in at about one with a bar across the inside of a door in a building in the second of the four districts and said she had got out of a room on it at about four in the morning and nobody had known she was in the room.

**She has a bag with a zip on it and the zip was broken and had been taped and the tape had come off again, and nobody in that shop asked her about the bag and nobody has since.**

**Nobody asked her what room and nobody asked her what she was doing in it at four in the morning and nobody asked her whether anybody knew.** She is not thanked. She has not been thanked. She does not know the man of twenty-two fitted the thing that got her out, and he did not say so and she did not ask, and the bar is not a fitting and has no code and is on no list, and he heard her say the second half of that sentence and did not put his hand up and nobody at that counter put a hand up.

---

At about half past six he walked four miles to the room and sat at the end of the second bench.

A man of about thirty-one who works nights was at the far end with his coat still on, and he had been in that room for about two hours before he came, and about four people were in it and none of them was asked anything.

**He is in that room on the other four nights of a week and has been for about two years, and nobody in that room has ever asked him why a man who works nights is there at half past six in the evening, and he has never said, and the woman nearest the door has never once looked up while he has been there.**

**About four people came into that room on Monday for the temperature and a fifth came in for about two minutes and stood by the door and went out again, and nobody stopped him and nobody knew why he came, and he was not asked and he was not thanked and he is not going to be asked.**

Then he said it, unasked, in about nine seconds, to nobody in particular, in his own words, and it was about the door and not about anything else in that room.

"**I lifted a man's bar to get in and not to get out and there was a woman inside who wanted to be left alone.**"

Nobody asked him which door. Nobody asked him whose bar. Nobody asked him why he wanted to get in at two in the morning to a room somebody had shut themselves into. Nobody said that a bar is not a key and does not open anything to anybody who is on the wrong side of it, and nobody improved on what he said.

**He is owed nothing. Nobody improved on it. Nobody thanked him and nobody is going to. The four screws are not repaid and the door is still on its hinges and the woman who was inside it has not been asked about it and is not going to be, and the man of about thirty-one who works nights did not say it to her and has not said it to her and nobody has put the two of them in a room.**

A woman of about forty-five got out of a room on a bar in the same week. A man of about thirty-one got into one. Neither of them knows the other and neither of them has been told and the two of them are not a lesson and are not on anything.

---

A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was not in that room and nobody has asked him about anything for about thirty-nine weeks and five days, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-three weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody in that room said a word about a road about two hundred miles off, and nobody in that room knew that a man of about fifty-four who works nights at a depot lost about eleven people a week on Wednesday, and the man of about thirty-one who works nights and the man of about fifty-four who works nights at a depot have never met and nobody has said their names in the same breath and nobody is going to.

---

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and sixty-eight days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it.** The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others. The bar across the inside of that door in the second of the four districts is not any of the six and it is not a card and it is not a ticket and it opens a door for one person on one side of it and for nobody at all on the other.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
