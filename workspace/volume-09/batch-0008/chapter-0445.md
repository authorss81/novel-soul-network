# Chapter 445 — Nine Words At The End Of A Counter, Four Minutes At A Bench, And A Card That Goes Back In A Pocket

The Monday of week one hundred and sixty-three was spent at a bench in Lattice Ward until about two and at a counter until about six and from about half past six in one room off a service road, and the one thing that is true of it is that **a man of about fifty-three who has stood at the end of that counter for about eight years offered the piece of card in the coat pocket in about nine words to a man of twenty-two, and the man of twenty-two worked out at a bench that it would work, in about four minutes, and did not use it, and the reason it would have worked is a fact about a piece of card and not a wound, and nothing happened to anybody and nobody is hurt.**

---

Eleven on the docket and eleven in date; eleven attended, the last of them about six in the evening. The word was at nine and ten and is told to nobody. **Heating: on, dearer, and four names on a lease cost more than they did, and a fortnight a cook paid for is paid for by nobody and is not resolved.** The room off that service road is six hundred and sixty-five days old and is warm, and about nine people came in on the Monday evening for the temperature and none of them was asked anything.

The card in the coat pocket is six hundred and sixty-nine days old, which is four days more than the room is, and it has been in that pocket since the Wednesday it came out of that rail, and a card’s age does not stop because a card has been picked up, and the rail at the end of the counter in the other building holds one card where it held two before that Wednesday.

---

The offer came at about one, at the end of the counter, and the man of about fifty-three said it once and did not say it a second time.

He had not come for it. He came at about one the way he comes, to stand at the end of the counter with a cup he does not drink from, and he waited until the man at the bench had the lamp on the arm swung round.

"**Show them that card at the gate and nobody there will ask you.**"

Nine words and a card. That was the whole of it and it took about nine seconds.

**Nobody else heard it.** The parts cage was open and the man in the cage had his back to the room and the lamp was between them. Nobody asked what was said. Nobody asked what the card was. The man of about fifty-three said nothing else and drank nothing and went back to his own end of the counter at about ten to two.

Nobody thanked him for offering it and nobody improved on it. He is owed nothing and he holds no post and he is not the answer to anything.

---

He worked it out at the bench between about one and about two, with the lamp on the arm and nothing in front of him.

**The bench had a cutting mat on it and a coil of wire and a tin of screws that is not on any list and a card behind the lamp with a part number on it that is a part number and not a card of any other kind, and the lamp threw a circle about the size of a plate and he worked the whole thing out inside the circle and did not move out of it.**

**The place-name on one side of it is a yard on a road, and there is a gate at that yard with a hut at it, and there was a man in that hut who knew the man who left the card, and that man is not the man who wrote the hand on the card and is not a relative of the man carrying it and has not been in this city in about two years.**

He worked out in about four minutes that it would work. The reason it would work is that a person at a gate who is shown a card with a hand he recognises does not ask where a stranger has been, and does not write it down, and does not tell anybody, because the whole of what the card is worth to the man who wrote it is that it costs nothing to hand it over once and it cannot be handed over twice.

He sat with that for a while and then he did not do it.

**Nobody saw him not do it.** The man in the cage had his back to the room for an hour. The man of about fifty-three was at the far end. About four people came to the counter between one and two and were served and one of them was told no to something and none of the four asked him anything.

**The counter has no till on it and the money is in a box under it and the box is not locked and there is about nine pounds in it and nobody has counted it in about two years and the man of about fifty-three has never counted it either and neither has anybody else.**

The card went back into the inside pocket of the coat and the coat went on the hook behind the bench, where it goes every day, and it will be in that pocket tomorrow and on Friday and on the day the room comes back to fifty-five lines for the thirtieth time and the thirty-first time after that.

---

The cost of it is not a wound and nothing happened to him and nobody was harmed and no door closed on anybody.

**The cost of it is that a person who is findable is a cost to the people who come to the places he is findable at, and a card in a rail is a findable thing, and the card in that pocket has a place-name on it that about four hundred competent people in this city could each read differently and every one of them right, and the man who put that card in that rail put it there expecting to come back, and the man of fifty-three at the end of the counter has not been asked what he knows about the yard and is not going to be.**

**From Monday that card is not a card he forgot to put back. It is a card he has decided to carry, and a card he has decided to carry is a different object from a card that fell out of a pocket, and nothing about it is different on the outside and no one can see the difference.**

He is twenty-two. He is four miles from a room off a service road and about four miles from a counter and neither of those is the gate on that road. Nobody knows that he worked it out. Nobody is going to be told.

---

He walked four miles to the room at about half past six and sat at the end of the second bench. A cook of twenty-seven was at the sink saying nothing. In the chair nearest the door was a woman of about thirty whom nobody asked a question, nobody thanked and nobody apologised to, and the page that is hers is the eighth of eight things in a ring binder on a shelf and is unread and was not read on Monday.

A man of about fifty-one with a tool bag was at the wall and had said nothing for about thirty-eight weeks and five days, and nobody had renewed the asking and nobody had stopped it. A man of about thirty-three, who holds four buildings and about nine hundred doors with no stamp and no reference and no date, was not in that room, and his hold went on for about forty-two weeks and four days without anything being asked of him and without anything being given to him.

Nobody at the far end of that room had heard about the nine words and nobody at the far end of that room is going to be told. The man of about fifty-three is not in that room and has not been in it for about two years and holds no post in it.

---

Two nails in that room carried nineteen lines and the newest of them is a lift mechanic’s, eleven words, signed, about the middle, and it is the only promise on that board and it is three hundred and sixty-one days old, and nothing was added to it and nothing was removed from it and nothing was rubbed off it.

**A card in a coat pocket is a piece of card about the size of a playing card with a place-name on one side and a week-number with nothing after it on the other, in a hand that is not the hand of the man carrying it, and it is not a ticket and it is not a key and it opens nothing and it has no title and no office and nothing on the back of it.** The wall list in its plastic sleeve in the repair shop in Lattice Ward is a fourth object and has never been on two nails. The hardboard is a fifth. The master’s original is in a fourth pocket in a tool roll with a blank fifth line and a drawer that is shut. The page of paper with about eleven lines in it is a sixth, in a fourth building. The sheet of about nine lines in a room in the second of the four districts is not any of the others.

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter came down at about ten.
