# Terminology Bible: The Soul Network

Use these forms consistently in outlines and prose. Capitalization is for formal names; ordinary conversation may use the shorter form when it sounds natural.

## Network terms

- **Soul Network:** The common public name for the connected practice network. It does not mean that the network stores a complete soul.
- **Civic Weave:** The formal name for Nacre’s public network and its physical relay infrastructure.
- **network:** The shared field formed by active, consensual practice connections. It is not a single mind.
- **node:** A person, device, or exchange room that can participate in a link. A person is never reduced to a node in narration.
- **thread:** A live connection carrying observations between participants.
- **share:** A bounded, consensual exchange of a technique’s observations.
- **offer:** The clear statement of what a person is willing to share and for how long.
- **agreement:** The participant’s visible acceptance of a specific offer. Silence, proximity, and inherited workplace consent are not agreement.
- **withdrawal:** A participant’s clear choice to end future use. It may disrupt a practice but must be respected.
- **care-link:** A narrow emergency share made when a person cannot answer. It has a visible reason, a limited duration, and a later review.
- **aftertaste:** The temporary sensory, emotional, physical, or informational residue left by a share.
- **attention debt:** The accumulated load that makes a person less able to distinguish their own senses and decisions from the link.
- **unmooring:** A severe condition in which a person cannot reliably separate another practice or feeling from their own. It can leave lasting damage.
- **exchange room:** A larger relay space in a clinic, school, workshop, or public building.
- **relay:** A person or exchange room that carries a weak or distant link. A relay can expose connection traces and may be corrupted.
- **quiet:** A deliberate absence of connection. Quiet is a valid state, not a failure state.
- **line:** A synthesized pattern that multiple observations can support. A line is provisional until tested.
- **lineage:** The recorded history of a technique: sources, changes, boundaries, and consent records. A lineage can be incomplete or falsified.
- **technique:** A structured embodied practice with an anchor, beats, release, boundary, and aftertaste.
- **boundary-release beat:** The observable completion beat in which a practitioner applies the boundary, confirms or refuses further sharing, and returns the choice to act to themselves. A printed boundary without a performed check is not enough.
- **practice:** The act of performing, testing, or teaching a technique.
- **counterpoint:** An independent disagreement or alternative used to test a shared line. It reveals conflict, not automatic truth.
- **return path:** The original Civic Compact safeguard that shows how a technique is shared, how long it lasts, and how a participant exits. The public Compact did not treat silence as consent.
- **Crown Clause:** A hidden Marrow Falls amendment inserted behind the public Return Path; it treats silence as provisional consent and routes action toward an approved civic sequence.
- **Crown Key:** The convergence authority in the Crown Vault that invokes the Crown Clause across prepared civic systems. It can override local release cues through a prepared pattern, but every withdrawal then routes through its holder. It is not a mind-reading device.
- **Crown Root Interface:** A one-time physical maintenance credential in the Crown infrastructure, distinct from ordinary local-network consent, that can inspect the full weave and temporarily validate a response for linked systems while its witnessed council link is open. It is not a new personal power; Marek destroys it during the final Unbinding.
- **hinge:** An intentionally incomplete beat that rewards a key-holder and narrows a learner’s practical independence.
- **key-holder:** The person whose cue or authority a hinge rewards. The term describes a function, not goodness or rank.
- **Conductor:** Iona Sorn’s public or hidden title as the architect of forced coordination.
- **Conductor’s Choir:** The loose hidden network of people and techniques seeded with Crown hinges.
- **First Knot:** Marek’s first stable two-person link and his first deliberate choice to trust another person.
- **Open Weave:** The final federated form in which local networks can cooperate without one privileged center.

## Consent permissions

- **Use:** Permission to use a shared technique during the stated purpose.
- **Remember:** Permission to retain a faint echo after the stated event.
- **Derive:** Permission to compare the trace later and make a new practice, subject to the original boundary.
- **Boundary:** What the technique must not affect or take.
- **Release:** The action or cue that ends attention safely.
- **Scope:** The people, place, task, and time to which a share applies.

Characters may say “I’ll give you use, not a keepsake” or “remember the beat, not the feeling.” These phrases are in-world language, not interface commands.

## Civic and geographic terms

- **Nacre:** The modern coastal city-republic where the first volumes take place.
- **Nacre Sound:** The coastal water and shipping channel around the city.
- **Lattice Ward:** University, laboratories, and the central civic exchange.
- **Saltmarket:** Waterfront market, kitchens, repair stalls, and the Saltmarket Exchange.
- **Nine Bridges:** Residential transit district.
- **Ashfields:** Industrial yards and the Marrow Falls edge.
- **Quiet Quarter:** Mixed-income district with a strong culture of deliberate privacy.
- **Crown Terrace:** Administrative and wealthy medical district above the Crown Vault.
- **Lower Wards:** Flood-prone neighborhoods with informal maintenance crews.
- **Meridian Corridor:** Freight and industrial route between Nacre and the inland plateau.
- **The Verge:** Independent towns that share techniques selectively and may refuse Nacre institutions.
- **Pale Harbor:** Working harbor where old network routes meet; a regional location, not a hidden realm.
- **Marrow Falls:** Industrial district and site of the outage that exposed the Crown Clause.
- **Civic Spine:** Nacre’s public relay and maintenance system.
- **Civic Spine 7:** Marek’s night-shift repair hub.
- **Nacre Assembly:** The city’s public deliberative government.
- **Continuity Office:** The government office responsible for emergency plans, network licenses, and failure investigations.
- **Solo Seal:** A practical license proving a person can perform a core technique alone. It does not grant or replace a Working Share.
- **Working Share:** A limited license to practice with named partners, earned through a separate witnessed assessment.
- **Civic Practitioner:** A person trained and recognized to maintain public infrastructure.
- **Practice Commons:** The first public, cooperative technique library and teaching network.
- **Civic Commons:** The final public institution that governs the federated Civic Weave. It is not a ruler or a single building.

- **Choir of Practice:** An independent cooperative of practitioners, teachers, repairers, and care workers.

## Historical terms

- **the Hush:** The forty-seven-year-old synchronization disaster that made consent boundaries a public necessity.
- **the Civic Compact:** The rebuilding agreement that created the Civic Weave and Return Path. Its public version required visible exits and did not authorize silence as consent.
- **Marrow Falls outage:** The fourteen-year-old emergency failure during which Iona inserted the Crown Clause into a working copy of the Compact’s emergency protocol.
- **the Crown Clause:** See Network terms; do not use “Crown Law” as a synonym.
- **the Nine-Minute Hush:** The final crisis, not a second historical event. Its first nine minutes begin in Volume 15 when Iona activates the Crown Key; the cascade can then continue for hours or days.

## Interface language

The Soul Network’s interface uses short, plain prompts. It does not use numeric levels, inventory lists, software jargon, or predictive claims it cannot support.

Useful examples:

- “One practice is missing a beat.”
- “Two sources agree. One person has withdrawn.”
- “This share carries feeling as well as technique.”
- “The line is possible. It is not proof.”
- “Do you offer the next step?”
- “A return path is open. Someone must choose to close it.”

The interface may be wrong, incomplete, or influenced by a hinge. Characters must investigate and act for themselves.

## Words to avoid or define on first use

Avoid technical software terms such as API, protocol, backend, runtime, token, latency, and deployment unless a character uses them as an ordinary modern misuse and the scene explains the intended meaning. Prefer “share,” “line,” “exchange room,” “relay,” “return path,” and “withdrawal” in the fictional vocabulary.
