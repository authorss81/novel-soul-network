# Character Bible: The Soul Network

## Relationship and cast rules

- The only romantic relationship is Marek Senn and Talia Venn. It is slow, contested, and built around consent in both intimate and public life.
- No character is written as a reward for Marek’s power. Allies have their own work, limits, and reasons to leave.
- Family relationships are not secret fuel for instant power. Lena Senn knows more than she says because she was protecting a choice, not manufacturing a chosen one.
- Antagonists may be sympathetic, but sympathy explains their methods; it does not erase the people they harm.

## Marek Senn — protagonist

**Age:** 22

**Occupation:** Third-year student at the Nacre Institute of Applied Practice; night-shift equipment repairer at Civic Spine 7

**Appearance and habits:** Lean, sleep-deprived, with a split thumbnail and a habit of counting exits before entering a room. He wears old work gloves under his school jacket and keeps a pencil behind one ear. He is better with tools and people than with his own future.

### Current want

Pass the Solo Seal, keep his scholarship, and obtain a legal place to work. He wants a small, respectable life: a room he can afford, a job that does not humiliate him, and enough certainty to stop being the family’s problem.

### Need

Marek must learn that usefulness is not the same as self-sufficiency. He has to accept help, give up control, and let other people carry decisions that belong to them. His mature need is to build interdependence without turning it into ownership.

### Central flaw

He takes responsibility before he has been asked and hides the cost. He calls this responsibility because admitting that he needs help feels like evidence that he is weak. In a network, this flaw becomes dangerous: he can decide that a person consented to a dangerous share when they only consented to a smaller one, or keep a link alive because stopping feels like abandoning them.

### Fear

That he will become a burden, a false expert, or the person whose private need hijacks a public system. He also fears that the best version of himself can only exist by using other people.

### Strengths

- Exceptional observation of physical sequences, wear marks, timing, and repair context.
- Calm under a concrete emergency when someone gives him a useful job.
- Willing to learn from a practitioner who knows a different craft.
- Can admit a mistake when a consequence is visible, though he often admits it too late.

### Weaknesses

- Poor at self-advocacy and at performing alone.
- Overworks, skips meals, and treats rest as a technical problem.
- Can mistake a clever workaround for consent.
- Has an impulse to prove that a dangerous system can be managed by the right individual.

### Initial lie

“If I can just become useful enough, nobody will need me to ask for anything.”

### Final change

Marek stops asking whether he deserves the network and starts asking who has the right to shape it. He helps create a public institution that can correct him, then remains one participant in it rather than its sole guardian.

## Talia Venn — primary romance and civic advocate

**Age:** 24

**Occupation:** Resonance nurse at a mobile public clinic; part-time student for a civic-advocate certificate

**Appearance and habits:** Compact, alert, and precise with her hands. She carries a consent clipboard even when no institution has asked her to. She remembers people’s preferred names before she remembers their diagnoses.

### Want

Make consent a real right in clinics, schools, workplaces, and emergency services. She wants a standard that survives fear, fatigue, and a person’s inability to explain what a “default” means.

### Need

Talia must stop treating every risk as a problem she can personally manage. She needs to share authority with people who do not share her training, and to accept that a public rule can protect her without making her its gatekeeper.

### Flaw

She is so focused on preventing coercion that she can miss the ordinary dependence people form after a safe choice. She may also hide exhaustion behind competence and call personal caution “professional boundaries.”

### Relationship with Marek

During Volume 01, Talia first catches him using a classmate’s observation without asking for a durable share. Their early attraction is real but inconvenient: he wants help without admitting the need, and she wants clear language before trust. They become collaborators, learn each other’s work, separate when Marek tries to protect a node by making decisions for them, and return only after he gives her the same right to refuse that he asks of everyone else. Their final closeness is chosen in ordinary moments, not awarded after a confession.

### Arc

Talia moves from documenting individual consent to designing public institutions that can inspect themselves. In Volume 11, the Assembly appoints her Nacre’s first provisional independent consent ombud under a limited, renewable mandate. After the final crisis, the Assembly formally confirms the role; at no point does Marek appoint or control her. She remains romantically partnered with him but institutionally separate and politically capable of opposing him.

## Rafi Pell — roommate, first node, physical counterweight

**Age:** 21

**Occupation:** Bicycle courier, part-time mechanic, student of urban transport practice

**Appearance and habits:** Broad-shouldered, impatient with paperwork, always carrying two tools he does not own. He trusts a working hand before a confident explanation.

### Want

Keep his rented repair bay and prove that practical skill deserves a place in a city that rewards credentials.

### Flaw

Rafi says yes before he understands the boundary. He is generous, proud, and vulnerable to anyone who makes refusal look like cowardice.

### Function and arc

He is Marek’s first voluntary partner, not a disposable sidekick. A seeded balance technique makes Rafi physically dependent on a cue, and his recovery forces Marek to stop treating consent as a one-time signature. Rafi later co-founds a worker-owned relay cooperative and remains one of the people who can tell Marek when he is becoming authoritarian.

## Sera Quill — public maintenance mentor

**Age:** 45

**Occupation:** Lead repairer at Civic Spine 7; former emergency practice instructor

**Appearance and habits:** Broad hands, a clipped voice, and a metal braid of tools worn under her shirt. She writes warnings on the backs of forms because she distrusts neat records that fit on one page.

### Want

Keep the public network running without allowing another Hush. She wants apprentices who know how to stop.

### Flaw

Sera has survived by making decisions quickly and keeping the people who disagree with her outside the room. She mistakes secrecy as protection.

### Function and arc

Sera initially treats Marek’s ability as a dangerous shortcut. She teaches him that a return path is a physical maintenance responsibility, not an idea. Her own history with the Conductor’s Choir makes her both a valuable witness and a source of guilt. By the final volume she leads the public institution’s maintenance council and can refuse Marek.

## Dr. Iona Sorn — the Conductor, central antagonist

**Age:** 53

**Former role:** Chief architect of the Civic Weave’s emergency procedures; consultant during the Marrow Falls inquiry

**Appearance and habits:** Elegant, restrained, and almost physically still when others are afraid. She remembers the names of obsolete devices and the names of people who once trusted her.

### Want

Prevent a new Hush by making the network capable of acting without debate, delay, or scattered responsibility. She believes a city that can hesitate is a city that can die.

### Flaw

Iona treats consent as a luxury available to people with time, safety, and a choice. She cannot imagine that a person may rationally choose a slower action that preserves their own agency.

### Method

She seeds the Crown Clause into incomplete techniques, uses emergencies to normalize it, and cultivates people who believe centralized action is the only humane response. She does not need to read thoughts or control every body. She builds a situation in which people begin to ask her permission because the technique has learned to reward her authority.

### Relationship with Marek

Iona recognizes his counterpoint scar and once taught him that a missing beat is a question, not an order. She later argues that he is wasting lives by preserving refusal. Her betrayal is personal because she helped create the habits that make him useful, then tries to use those habits against him.

### Resolution

Iona survives the final crisis physically, loses the Crown Key, and faces public judgment and supervised work. She is not forgiven simply because she helps with reconstruction. The new system makes her expertise useful only under conditions she cannot unilaterally change.

## Oren Vey — corporate pressure and early antagonist

**Age:** 41

**Role:** Operations director of Threadline, the company that sells relay hardware, training kits, and practice subscriptions

**Appearance and habits:** Carefully dressed, generous with small favors, and never raises his voice in a meeting.

### Want

Make technique sharing predictable enough to insure, sell, and scale. He wants a market in which a person’s attention can be treated as a low-friction input.

### Flaw

Oren believes that people will consent to anything that is convenient and that harm is a problem of poor design. He is not the hidden architect; he is the person who makes the hidden architecture profitable.

### Arc

Oren becomes evidence, not the final enemy. He loses control of Threadline’s public systems, faces a criminal inquiry, and may cooperate with the Civic Commons because self-interest finally aligns with accountability. His survival keeps the ending from becoming a simplistic “kill the company” resolution.

## Leo Marr — the Quiet House organizer

**Age:** 35

**Role:** Privacy engineer, former relay technician, organizer of the Quiet House

**Appearance and habits:** Moves through rooms by checking exits and carrying more tools than the job needs. Speaks softly when she is threatening someone.

### Want

Ensure no person can be made to share by a device, employer, state, or beloved community. She wants the option to disconnect to be real.

### Flaw

Leo treats other people’s dependence as evidence that they have surrendered judgment. She is willing to impose a quiet on patients who need a link, which makes her movement both a defense and a danger.

### Arc

Leo initially sabotages the first public exchange rooms, then works with the Civic Commons under strict limits. She never becomes comfortable inside an institution, and her refusal is part of what makes the final institution trustworthy.

## Iven Sore — Continuity Commissioner

**Age:** 58

**Role:** Head of the Continuity Office and the official custodian of emergency records

**Appearance and habits:** Soft voice, immaculate coat, and a habit of answering a question with a smaller question.

### Want

Keep Nacre calm enough to function. He believes public ignorance is sometimes the least dangerous medicine.

### Flaw

He equates secrecy with stability and treats citizens as variables in a plan. He suppresses the Marrow Falls records and later uses the Hinge attacks to justify emergency powers.

### Resolution

Iven exposes the records and accepts institutional review. He is not physically destroyed; his authority is broken and he must live in a system that can no longer hide behind his title.

## Lena Senn — Marek’s mother

**Age:** 49

**Occupation:** Care worker at a long-term residential clinic

**Appearance and habits:** Warm with strangers and blunt with family. She carries a small cloth bag of practical items and never discusses the Hush unless her hands are busy.

### Want

Keep Marek and his sister safe from the network that once injured him. She wants a normal family, even if she no longer believes normal is possible.

### Flaw

Lena protects people by deciding what they can know. Her silence around the childhood link creates distance between her and Marek.

### Function

She is the first person who tells Marek that his ability is not a gift. She remembers the night of the unsafe link and knows the official story is incomplete. She becomes an active witness in the final institution rather than a rescued damsel.

## Nell Senn — Marek’s younger sister

**Age:** 16
**Role:** Secondary-school student interested in stage combat, illustration, and ordinary life

Nell is not a hidden node or a chosen inheritor. She resents the family’s careful silences about the network and wants a life that does not revolve around Marek’s exams or Lena’s work. Her presence keeps the cost of public network decisions in the family’s ordinary rooms. She may learn a technique, refuse one, or remain deliberately unconnected; the story does not use her as a victim or a cure for a hinge.

## Evan Senn — Marek’s father and missing historical thread

**Age at disappearance:** 41

**Role:** Transit technician and participant in the Marrow Falls counterpoint test

**Status:** Presumed dead; no resurrection or secret return

Evan left Marek a scratched sequence card with four beats and a blank fifth. The card is a practical tool, not a prophecy. Evan’s later choice was to sever his own link at the cost of his health and public identity so that a dangerous Crown response could be interrupted. His absence remains a wound even after the reason is known.

## Asha Reed — student and public voice

**Age:** 19

**Role:** First-year student who organizes a low-cost practice study group

Asha wants technique education not to be reserved for people who can pay for private instructors. Her initial mistakes come from publishing without testing; her growth is toward patient organizing and a public language that does not flatten different crafts. She later represents a district in the first Practice Commons council.

## Dessa Kwan — everyday practitioner

**Age:** 27

**Role:** Line cook, part-time care worker, and instructor in shared kitchen techniques

Dessa demonstrates that the network is not a secret weapon. Her techniques involve timing, heat, repair, and feeding people. She refuses to let institutions turn her recipes into a proprietary standard. Her ordinary competence gives the public network a credible life beyond combat and crisis.

## Supporting cast introduced in Volume 01

These are working Volume 01 characters. They are canonical for the volume but should not be expanded into protagonists or given arcs beyond what the volume outline assigns them.

- **Davan Rusk:** 47. Practicum instructor at the Nacre Institute, Threadline-certified, twenty-two years in the department. He is the key-holder for the missing fourth beat by position and by belief: his certification taught him that the instructor supplies completion, and he has never had cause to doubt it. He is not a villain and must never become one. His function is to make the volume's first antagonist — a procedure that rewards one approved answer — into a decent man's career. By the end of Volume 01 he is a reluctant witness who has to admit that his own training produced the cue he supplied.
- **Tam Arrick:** 20. Third-year student, night work at a Saltmarket kitchen. The classmate Marek saves in Chapter 3 and whose release becomes cue-locked as a result. He is the volume's first victim and its most exacting critic, not a prop: he tells Marek to stop speaking for him, he writes his own recovery plan, and by Chapter 50 he holds it. His injury, lost wages, and slow recovery are permanent costs.
- **Hollis Pratt:** 51. Continuity Office field assessor. Procedural, fair, and genuinely frightened of what a precedent would cost the city. He arrives as forms, conducts the formal review, and declines to trace the licensor on the grounds that the bundle is a commercial matter. He is the face of the limit of institutional authority in Volume 01.
- **Mal Farrow:** 61. Former courier, now works the fish market at Saltmarket, bad hip, clinic patient under a care-link in Volume 01 Movement II. Recurring and ordinary. He exists so the volume is not only about students, and so that a care-link is a real thing with a real review date.

### Secondary Volume 01 figures, established in Chapters 1–10

Added as the batch prose introduced them. Same rules: working characters, no arcs beyond what the volume assigns, and none of them a hidden authority.

- **Anneke Roos (Deputy Registrar, practical department):** 54, nineteen years in the building. She is the institution's throat: courteous, exact, and the person who decides what a complaint becomes. She offers Marek the technician track and tells him plainly what each version of the truth costs, refuses to have the 4-19 stock reissued, and characterizes the kit as a hardware matter if he stays quiet. Whether that is an offer, a defence, or a way of buying time stays unresolved through Movement II.
- **Darla Vint (department administrative officer):** the person who can alter an access note, and who does. She rewrites the Institute's pre-signed facilities form as equipment-only with the absence of any individual practice named, and lets Marek countersign the request for that. Small, competent, and the best argument the volume has that a procedure can be improved from inside by a person with standing.
- **Petar Vance (Threadline field service engineer):** services 4-19 at the Institute, both satellite sites, and the Saltmarket clinic. He is not a villain; he is protecting warranty exposure and asking why somebody pulled the wax paper off a crate. His service reference **SB-4-19-03** is the first thread that can be followed upward.
- **Gus Haldane (courier crew lead, Saltmarket west berth):** slipped on wet concrete in Chapter 8 while a boy was holding a crate, and was rescued by nobody. He is owed a full account of the word, and he is the reason the record exists outside the Institute.
- **Odile Fenn (third-year, Group C):** the pair of hands on the plate who could not act because the load-holder had not called it. Her inability to act is the shape of the whole problem in one person.
- **Bern Colley (clinic patient):** a refuse driver with a crushed ring finger, present in Chapter 6 when Talia makes the correction in front of him. He is why the correction had to be made in plain language.
- **A real Institute:** it is a place where a manual reprint costs eight credits, a drill record is a physical sheet with a name box, and a route drawer holds a key nobody ever asked for back. The building is the volume's first antagonist and it is not a person.

## Relationship map and boundaries

- **Marek–Talia:** slow-burn romantic partnership; conflict over who may decide for whom; final shared life remains voluntary and separately accountable.
- **Marek–Rafi:** friendship and first-node partnership; tested by bodily dependence and later renewed through worker ownership.
- **Marek–Sera:** mentor relationship; she teaches limits while he repeatedly tries to exceed them; they become co-governors, not master and student at the end.
- **Marek–Iona:** ideological inverse and former mentor; she recognizes his potential and tries to turn his usefulness into her central authority.
- **Marek–Lena/Evan:** family mystery and the lesson that a person can leave a path behind without leaving a child a destiny.
- **Talia–Iona:** professional and moral opposition; Talia sees the human cost of Iona’s certainty without becoming a symbolic counterweight to Marek.
- **Talia–Leo:** alliance built on shared refusal of hidden defaults; they disagree about emergency access and public risk.
- **Iven–Iona:** former colleagues; Iven suppresses the truth, Iona exploits the power he preserves. Neither is the sole architect of the final crisis.
- **Asha–Dessa:** patient institutional friendship that keeps the Civic Commons grounded in teaching and food, not only crisis management.
