### Chapter 0181 — "Somewhere Else For Eleven Minutes"

**The Monday morning of week fifty-nine, from ten past ten to half past eleven, in a room off a street in Nine Bridges. Load-book entry 184, and it says in its own opening label that this entry is a woman of thirty-four with a clipboard and eleven names on a list that is not a district list, and a corridor with four people in it who noticed where he was standing.** A first-floor room with a table, two chairs, a window that opens about four inches, a plastic wallet with eleven names on a card in it, and a fire door on a spring that does not latch. **She did not ask him to leave. She asked him to be somewhere else for eleven minutes and said out loud that she wanted to know which of the two she had done before he went through the door, because a person who is asked to leave has been asked to leave and only one of those two is in a file anywhere.** The reason, given to the card and not to him: with a man in the room the answer to her one question is given in a voice people use on a man they have been told about, and in about four years the only record of the morning would be that a man of twenty-two was outside the door. He went out. **Four people went up and down that landing in eleven minutes and every one of them noticed where he was** — a man of thirty-four on nights going in for a shift, who used the one sentence he is allowed once a week, which is the one that says a room is a room; a woman of about thirty-eight in a district jacket who asked whether it was a queue, was told no, and sat on the step at the bottom and was still there when the door opened; a foreman of fifty-eight with a site diary and no reason to be on that landing, who said out loud that he was going to say nothing about what he had just seen; and a boy of nineteen carrying card offcuts up the wrong stair on purpose. **One of them left a folded sheet wedged in the fire door overnight so that the door would stay where it was last pushed, and it was gone in the morning and is not on anything.** Behind the door, eleven minutes, **five of the eleven asked: four yes and one no, six not asked, and she is going back on Monday. The no was not followed by a reason and was not offered a second question.** Her cost at the door is the method and not the answer: those four may have said yes in a voice she cannot account for, she has six people she has not asked at all, and there is no form in this city that takes an account of either. And then the harder half, said on a landing: there is a woman whose name is in a box in pencil four miles away, and the half of that where nobody did anything wrong, and she has not got the other half and has a car park to get to. **He wrote nothing down and the reason is that if it were in his book then the only copy of it in this city would be in his book, and he has been trying for a term and a half not to be that, and he does not know whether that is integrity and has not decided.**

### Chapter 0182 — "The Back Of A Work Card"

**The Tuesday morning of week fifty-nine, from about one in the afternoon to half past four, in a residential building in Nine Bridges and a motor room off the same yard. Load-book entry 185, and it says in its own opening label that this entry is the back of a work card in biro with four names on it and a date on the front, and a woman of twenty-nine with sixteen Fridays on the back of her own card in the same wallet.** A floor share running since the spring, four people on it, all four asked for it, one of them after a fortnight because she wanted to be asked twice and was not. **The man of thirty-four ended it and said before anything else that he wanted it noticed first, because the only things this city believes are the things somebody asked for, and that it is the first time in about a term that something in this case has been his.** There is no form, and the reason there is no form is that a form would want a reason and he has not got a reason, he has a man who is going to have to stand on his own floor at ten past four in the morning and say a thing four times. **It went on the back of a work card in biro, forty words, read back to him, and he said those were the words he wanted and not the words he would have used, because a pen is a decision and a biro is a note.** The warning he was given before he carried it out is the finding and it is on the card: **he has spent a term teaching four people that a release waits for a man, ending the share does not un-teach that, and if he does it properly then in about a week one of the four will not do the fifth step at all, because a step a man ends for you is a step that waits for a man.** He agreed and said it should go on the card. **Marguerite Okpara, twenty-nine, six years and about nine hundred lifts, on a Friday list because her employer pays for it, came up the stairs with a tool bag for a noise in the lift and was not asked for anything.** She wrote it, put it in a plain envelope with nothing on it, and carried it out through a fire door into a stairwell at ten past two; nobody in that building saw it leave. **She said yes because nobody asked her to, and said that if he thanked her then in about four years somebody would ask her whether she would do it again and she would have to think about a favour, and she would rather it was a lift; and named her own cost, which is that sixteen Fridays on the back of her own card have been in the same wallet since the spring and a person who opens that wallet in about four years has got both at once.** He said the thing he had been keeping, that he has said no twice to being the word on his own floor, that the second no had no reason on it, and that he has wanted for about a fortnight for somebody to notice that the no came first both times. Nobody noticed and he noticed it himself and said he was aware of how small that is. **Neither was thanked.** At ten past four the following morning he stood on his own floor and said it four times.

### Chapter 0183 — "Nobody Took It From Her"

Nobody took it from her. Load-book entry 163, and it says in its own opening label that this entry is the back of a sheet, and fourteen, and a woman of forty-six telling him what the pencil is going to cost, and a corridor on Thursday of next week that he is not going to be able to be quiet in.** The forty-first line has something in it for the first time in nineteen years: **fourteen lines in pencil, on the back, upside down relative to the front, in the space where the conditions go, and it is not a condition.** Nine are in the building in Bower Street, three in the second building, and two in a stairwell in Saltmarket that a company plated in the year the stairwell was new and that are not mistakes, and she put them on the sheet anyway because a condition is a thing and this is a thing and if she does not write it down then in about four years there will be one number left in her head and she will be sixty-two and she will have moved house twice and one of them was to a smaller flat, and that is not a threat, it is a fact about how she would remember things. **The back of the sheet goes in the same ring file as the front, the ring file goes in a cupboard on a ground floor in another part of this city, and there are about nine hundred sheets in that file going back nineteen years, and there is no column for it, no box on it, and no form anywhere in this city that takes a discrepancy.** The fourth building has a set of plans in a drawer on a hook in a basement that nobody has stood in front of since the spring. **They did not go down. He wrote in his own book that nobody in this case has earned a basement, and that a basement is the room where the records are, and that if he is the first person through that door then he is the man who went through a door, and about eleven people a week are being taught to wait for that. Ines Kolar said at the top of the stairs that she had never been down and was not going today, and asked him to write it down as a thing she said and not a thing he found out.** **The price, said before he asked: in about four weeks a woman of thirty-six is going to come into that office and do the sheets up, because that is her job and she has done it for about nine years and does not know her, and she is going to turn that sheet over because everybody turns it over, and she is going to see fourteen lines in pencil in the space where the conditions go, and she is going to ask what the pencil is. And she will say there was a discrepancy and that she wrote it in pencil on her own sheet and that it has not been actioned, which is true and is nine words and does not help, because the woman of thirty-six will have to decide what to do with fourteen lines that a woman of forty-six wrote on the back of a sheet instead of in a column, and there is no column, and she will do whatever she does and he will not know. And when somebody asks her about the pencil she is going to have to say that a man of twenty-two asked her a question in a corridor in the week of forty-seventh that her sheet could not answer, and that is his name, and it is the only place his name is going to be in this city as a result of anything she has done, and she worked that out on the Monday and not on the Thursday.** **What it is worth, in her words, and he had not asked: two weeks ago a man could stand in that corridor and say the door and the wall do not agree and about four people in four buildings would have told him he was talking nonsense; in about four weeks there is a piece of paper in a ring file in a cupboard that says fourteen times, in a hand, that they do not; and that is worth about a fortnight, and in about a year it will be worth nothing, because the pencil will come off, and then the only thing anybody has got is a woman of forty-six who says it on a Thursday.** And then the price on the landing: **in four weeks the only thing that has changed in this city is a sheet with pencil on the back of it, and about eleven people a week are still going to be taught that a release waits for a man in a lanyard, and he is still that man, and he did that on purpose in a room with nine people in it.** She said yes. He said he was not asking her to make it into more than it is, and she said she was not going to and was not going to pretend either, and then gave him the fortnight and the year. **The close, in the repair shop: five things in the drawer under the bench, four of them other people's sentences; a jacket over the back of the chair by the bench with a corner of card in the inside pocket of it, about two inches by three, which nobody has ever held out a hand for in a term and a half; a form four miles away with a box in pencil and a woman's name in it and no reference number and nothing typed and it cannot come out; about forty-one people on a thing that cannot be ended; about nine hundred doors a year off a company's list; plans in a drawer on a hook; six lines in a register whose fourth column has not had a person's name in it in fourteen years and a woman of thirty-four there who has not been asked what she thinks of that; and on the Thursday of next week a woman of thirty-eight who works nights is going to stand in that corridor with him while he asks it, and the answer will not be about him. There is no post and there is no office whose business it is to look at a stranger's wrist and there is not going to be one, and about eleven people a week are still waiting for a man in a lanyard that four weeks of work has not made unnecessary. Nobody has said so.**
