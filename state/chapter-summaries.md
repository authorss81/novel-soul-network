**941 — Monday of week 317, day 2105, entry 944. Nine callers. THIRTY-NINE POUNDS. HE ASKED HER WHO A PIECE OF PAPER WAS FOR AND SHE SAID SHE DID NOT KNOW, IN A ROOM.** He asked it badly and knew he was asking it badly while it was going out of his mouth: *Who is it for.* She said she did not know, then said that there are four lines at the top of that form and one of them says no person on this form was asked anything, and he said that is not what he asked. **Then he said the true sentence out loud to the middle of that room — you cannot tell a person about a day that is about them if you do not know which person it is about — and nobody argued with it and nobody took it up.** About four people in that room have said since that they had each assumed one of the nine was somebody they had spoken to, and **not one of them can name one of the nine.** He asked one more question at about ten past eight: *Then how did nine rows get on that sheet.* The man of about thirty-three said: somebody wrote them down. **Nobody in that room asked him what he meant, and Marek did not ask him what he meant, and that was a decision.**

**942 — Tuesday, day 2106, entry 945. Nine callers. THIRTY-NINE POUNDS. THE NINE WERE WRITTEN DOWN ONCE, BY SOMEBODY, IN A ROOM IN A SECOND DISTRICT.** The man of about thirty-three said it to the rail and not to anybody and the woman of about forty-three asked him two questions and got two answers and neither of them was a name. **He said he has been in that room twice and has never seen the hand the nine were written in, and that he has not been back since, and that the room will not show him again.** In the yard afterwards, without being asked, he gave the reason: a request about four years ago for a copy of a page, a signed sheet with a reason at the top, an envelope that has not come back, and a letter written in his own hand about the envelope. **He is right about all of it, and four people who have dealt with him have said so, and one has said it is the most correct position anybody in this matter has taken and that it cost him two of his own.** He said the way in is one sentence: **it has to be in writing and it has to have a name on it.** A woman of about sixty said her two copies of the sheet disagree in the fifth column, that in one it is ruled and in the other it is not, and that in both there is nothing in it. **Nobody asked her whose copies they are or where they are, and she noticed about four seconds after she stopped talking.**

**943 — Wednesday, day 2107, entry 946. Nine callers. THIRTY-SEVEN POUNDS. HE WENT UP A FIRST FLOOR WITH ONE QUESTION WRITTEN ON THE BACK OF A DOCKET.** He asked whether anybody had been told about the date on that sheet and she said no, and then told him he had written *anybody* and that it was the widest word he could have used. **He has said since that he had not noticed choosing it.** She said the answer to the question he had not asked — does it have to be told — and said **yes**, and then told him what the answer costs in four points: one, that to tell nine people there are nine records of nine people; two, that each record has to have been made and by somebody; three, that the day after there is a person in this city holding nine names written down once and never asked about; four, and that person is him. **She put her thumb on the fourth one and left it there for about a minute, and he has said since that it was the worst minute of that week and that he was not in a position to notice at the time.** He got two words into asking her to use the office she holds. She stopped him and said in one sentence why: **she could do it in about nine days and then it would be a finding of that office, and then it would be on that office, and the only way that office can answer what it did with nine people is to hand them back.** He said he would not ask her again. She said all right. **And then, after a while, looking at the docket and not at him, she said that he has come up there four times in two years and has got two words out of her twice and one whole question once, and it is always the ones that cost him something.** He said he knew what it cost. **She said: no, you know that you would pay it, and that is not the same, and I am not going to be the person who finds out which of those two it is.**

**944 — Thursday, day 2108, entry 947. Nine callers. THIRTY-FIVE POUNDS. A WOMAN WANTED A NOTICE OFF A WALL AND THE MAN WHO PUT IT UP SAID NO WITH A REASON FROM NINE YEARS.** The woman of about forty-three wanted the printed form taken down off a passage in a fourth district. The man of about thirty-eight who keeps the clinic there said he carried it out of that room on the Tuesday before last, put it up himself in about twenty minutes, asked nobody, and is not taking it down. **His reason: in nine years of that clinic people are told what day they are coming in and are not asked whether that suits them; about four have not come; he knows which four and has never asked any of them; and when you take a notice down, the only people who see it are the ones who were already looking — and he has watched that happen on a wall about nine times.** He said the last part to Marek and not to her. **Marek said the reason was correct. Nobody said anything. He said it twice, the second time slower, and about four people in that room have said since that he did not seem to understand he was making it worse each time.** Then she asked him what the notice is for, and nothing came out of him for about four seconds, and what came out was: **I do not know. I have looked at it every day for two weeks and I cannot say what it is asking anybody to do.** She said: it is not asking anybody to do anything, that is what is wrong with it. He said: I know. **And that was the last thing anybody said about the notice that night.**

**945 — Saturday, day 2110, entry 948. Seven callers. THIRTY-TWO POUNDS. **946 — Sunday, day 2111, entry 949. Four callers. TWENTY-TWO POUNDS. THE SHUTTER CAME DOWN AT ABOUT TWO. The woman of about forty-three said **we will not do it and we will not write it and we will not say afterwards that we did not know her name**, and when Marek said *then say her name* she said no, and when he said that was the same as saying she did not know it, she said **it is not, and I am not going to be the person who says her name out loud in this room again.** Talia told the woman that nobody in that building could tell her whether the day was hers, that she was allowed to go and look and nobody would stop her and nobody would go with her, and that **looking is free and going on your own, and if you find out it is yours nobody will be able to take it off you, and if you find out it is not yours nothing will have happened to you at all.** Then the woman asked the question the afternoon was about: **and what do I do with the day.** Nobody in that room said one word. **947 — Monday, day 2128 — NO. Monday of week 318, day 2112, entry 950. Nine callers. FORTY-EIGHT POUNDS. **948 — Tuesday, day 2113, entry 951. Nine callers. THIRTY-NINE POUNDS.
