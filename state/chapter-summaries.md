# Chapter Summaries

Keep these compact. Two to five sentences each. Update after every batch.

## Volume 01 — The Missing Beat (Chapters 1–50)

### Chapter 0001 — "Night Shift Ledger"
Civic Spine 7, Tuesday of week 1, 01:40. Marek clears the night list, counts exits on arrival, and finds a third Threadline 4-19 practice plate with a scorched return contact. Sera wants it signed out as a vendor fault; he repairs it instead and writes the fault on the back of the form, using Evan's scratched four-beat card as a shim. He walks home, sends Nell a lie about money, sleeps four hours, and discovers from the Thursday roster that the practicum will drill on exactly the revision he has been repairing all night against a manual printed at 3-08. Ends on the discovery: the whole city is one delivery, and he is the only signature on it that says anything.

### Chapter 0002 — "The Practicum"
Practical room two, Thursday of week 1, 09:20. The team rescue drill on 4-19 stock. Rusk teaches from a laminated script and rewards exact cue timing, and the fourth beat has a name in that script — *scope* — and nothing in the technique answers it. Marek runs the sequence correctly on the first pass, which surprises everyone including him. When Tam's boot comes off the ladder, Marek's own count arrives at the fourth place and finds a gap about two seconds wide with a person falling into it. His body gives out on him: metallic taste, and the floor loses its color.

### Chapter 0003 — "Four Beats"
The same room, seconds later. Marek offers Rafi a share in one bad sentence in under four seconds and Rafi says yes; the interface says only that two practices agree and one beat is not answered, and offers nothing further. The line lasts about four seconds and shows Marek the shape of the hole. He finishes the release himself in Rusk's cadence, Tam lets go, and the rescue works. Then his own anchor fails, the line collapses, and he is left with a headache, an unnamed floor color, and a piece of Tam's terror running in his chest. Rusk tells him kindly that he hesitated. The drill sheet ends with *Senn* written in the name box on line four, and Tam's own note: *He said it. I let go because he said it. Both are true.*

### Chapter 0004 — "Aftertaste"
Student housing and Civic Spine 7, that night. Marek cannot name a screwdriver for ninety seconds, writes the fourth beat down three times and gets three different beats, and refuses Rafi when Rafi asks to try again. Sera teaches him that a return path is a physical cut in metal with a location, and refuses to let the Spine's public relay be used as laboratory equipment. On the wall of handwritten notes he finds a fourteen-year-old Marrow Falls log with the phrase *last approved civic sequence* and a signature he cannot place; Sera refuses to discuss it and tells him not to ask, and he does not give the page back. He opens the load book and starts logging attention debt by hand. Ends with the realization that the cheapest thing available to him is four hours of sleep.

### Chapter 0005 — "The Seal Run"
Institute exam hall, Thursday of week 4. Nine candidates, three examiners, and a scoring condition that puts Rusk's voice inside the seal. The first candidate completes the run perfectly by turning her head at the fourth beat. Marek performs anchor, load and close correctly, Rusk begins the word *scope*, and Marek declines it in public and tries to do the fourth beat alone. It is not there — the beat is where somebody else answers. The board records *not completed, fourth beat not performed, substitute cue declined by the candidate, deficiency recorded pending review.* He is offered a technician apprenticeship, a transcript line he cannot fix, and a provisional witnessed assessment on Thursday of week 7 with the scholarship suspended. The line he cannot unwrite is *performance dependent on external cue*, and nobody in the building has yet said the sentence *the fourth beat is missing.*

### Chapter 0006 — "Not a Keepsake"
Talia's mobile clinic, Tuesday of week 5, evening. Marek arrives in person because he cannot answer her text, carrying a four-week-old echo of Tam's practiced release that he never asked to keep. Talia stops him outside the vehicle in plain words and in front of Bern Colley, a patient whose hand she is then obliged to explain the situation to, because the nurse is not using a man on a table as a reference. She lays out use, remember and derive, and the Institute's pre-signed facilities form, which confers nothing at all on any individual practice. He goes to Tam, apologizes badly, is hit in the bad shoulder, and is told: use for the rescue, no retention, no repetition, never again. Talia offers a bounded alternative — two performances, use and remember, no derive, written scope with a date and a review, and she keeps a copy. At the clinic door he tells her the truth he has offered nobody: Tam's fear is still in him. He learns that the clinic has been fitting 4-19 since September on a card from 3-08.

### Chapter 0007 — "A Line Is Not Proof"
Clinic treatment bay and the Institute kit store, Tuesday of week 5, night. Talia performs her brace twice on a table with a bag of bricks, and the fourth beat turns out to be a sentence and an empty hand and a decision handed back. Marek writes the difference down five times, gets it wrong four times, and then sees the trap he has walked into: two observations by two people trained by the same twelve people is company, not evidence. The interface says only that two practices agree and one boundary is not performed, and then says nothing at all. In the store room, with a brass key nobody ever asked back, he counts twenty-four plates, photographs the bundle label and its two empty lines, finds the eleven-page instructor booklet that names the instructor's cue as the expected completion and calls it *a support beat… not a step in the technique*, and finds no design-approval page anywhere in it. The dread stops being about his competence and becomes a product with seven identical revision lines and two blank ones. He leaves the crate exactly as he found it, copies the count by hand, and reads a department notice that puts Tam on a west berth loading dock at half past six the following Tuesday.

### Chapter 0008 — "The Voice He Hates"
Courier dock, Saltmarket waterfront, west berth, Tuesday of week 6, 19:20. Tam's work-setting release check goes fine four times, and then a courier's heel goes off wet concrete and a pallet of iced crates starts to lean. Marek watches Tam get to the third beat and stop, with nobody available to say the word. He says it himself — Rusk's exact cadence, from outside a barrier, on a public street — and it works, and in the second that follows he hears what he has become. He stops, refuses to say another word in that voice, and takes the narrow way instead: Tam's own flat hand on the crate edge as the boundary check. The band goes. The crate takes Tam's forearm and two tendons. He kneels in the meltwater and admits exactly what he did, and the worst of it is that the institute would have praised him. Tam tells him he now has two voices in it, and the second one is his.

### Chapter 0009 — "What the Record Says"
Institute practical department office and corridors, Wednesday of week 6. The week-one drill record has been corrected to *student intervention at the point of release. Equipment sound. No further action.* Deputy Roos offers him the whole thing on paper — technician track, a reference, a hardware-fault framing — and tells him honestly that she cannot do both. Rusk admits in the corridor that he personally ordered the obsolete 3-08 manual in March, and tells Marek to take his evidence to somebody who can be harmed by it. A Continuity Office form arrives with category (ii) *attributable to the trainee* already ticked in another hand. The plates are locked in a caged store — locked, not quarantined — and a Threadline field service engineer, Petar Vance, arrives with a service card and a set of precise questions about the wax paper. When Vance reaches for a plate, Marek makes everyone state purpose, duration and retention aloud, and the department's pre-signed form is rewritten as equipment-only with no individual practice named, countersigned by Marek. He copies the label, the count, three booklet pages, the manual requisition and the log line by hand, and gives a copy to Gus Haldane on the dock. He does not file a complaint, because a complaint brings new plates in a new lot and nothing left to count.

### Chapter 0010 — "A Different Shoulder"
Saltmarket Exchange, a courier ramp, and a hospital ward, Thursday of week 6, evening. Marek asks the Exchange for a boundary release in front of the whole room and is made to say who he is and what happened; Dessa Kwan agrees on terms and shuts down any performance put on for an audience. He watches her hand off a hot pass nine times in one service by naming out loud what the pan touched and handing the decision back, and he watches an unnamed fish porter of sixty take a full crate on his chest, narrate his own release to himself, and get up. Rafi says yes before Marek has finished the question, and Marek makes him answer properly; Rafi's courier balance step has the same missing fourth beat and he has compensated for two years with his left shoulder against a wall, which he calls technique. In the ward Tam takes his own recovery plan away from the department, says he wants an observer who was never taught the card, and names the fish porter Farrow. Marek writes down four lines, then writes what he does not know beneath them, and closes the batch with the count: three independent lines visible in one evening, one directly compared, seven days to an examination he cannot pass.
