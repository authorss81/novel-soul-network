**`981`** A Saturday, five days after the Monday on which Sera Quill said for the second Monday running that she was not deciding. **A woman of about forty-five asks Marek for one sentence — said out loud, saying the finding is not his — and is refused by him and then refuses herself in the same shop: if she says there is a finding then she has said who found it, and she will not be a second voice.** The man of about thirty-three asks for the sheet with the empty fifth column to go into a drawer instead of lying on a table, and is refused in about nine seconds on the grounds that a promise about a sheet cannot be broken for a week and a day. He asks Marek to be the one who asks, and is told no, and says he will ask her himself. She says she is going to find somebody to say it and it will not be either of them. Nine callers, twenty-seven pounds.

**`982`** A Sunday, the movement's only one, and the shutter comes down at two. **The man of about thirty-three asks Marek to carry a thing home for his mother, and Marek refuses and tells him to take her himself.** The refusal is nine words and the cost is named: if Marek asks then the asking is his and the finding is his from that minute, and she has kept her name off this for a year. He says she cannot get there on her own and has no car and is the wrong age for a bus in the dark. Marek says the woman who keeps the room has one every four weeks and has never told anybody in advance, because telling in advance would want a list, and there is no list. Four callers, thirty-one pounds.

**`983`** A Monday. **Nobody at the counter says the name of the woman who has read a page, and nobody asks her.** The woman of about forty-three comes in at about eight and tells Marek to ask her about the sixth column in front of the queue or not at all. He asks. Six callers, thirty-four pounds.

**`984`** A Tuesday, and the hardest page of the movement. **Marek asks Sera Quill to be the person the finding stands in front of. She does not answer for about nine seconds. She then names a second cost: the only true answer puts the woman of about forty-five in the room as a second holder and she has not asked her. **`985`** A Thursday, and the reason she keeps the room is asked for the first time in six years and answered in one sentence: **a room nobody is asked anything in is the only kind she has ever been able to stay in.** She says she has never been asked it, and that about four people have told her what she ought to do with the room, and that he is the first to ask why she has got it. **`986`** A Saturday. **The man of about thirty-three says he asked his mother, in front of himself, and was told she will answer to a room and not to her son.** He asks Marek to hold the sheet; Marek refuses and explains that putting it in a drawer breaks the first of three things and not the third.
