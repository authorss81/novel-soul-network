- **`1001`** A Monday, day 2217, week 333. **The engine arrives at Marek's own counter.** A man of about forty-five who has read the sheet on that passage wall since the week it went up asks Marek a question nobody has asked there before and tells him that a woman at a desk has said four words to his sister-in-law, that the four words are that a room off a line in Saltmarket is where you go to be told about a form, and that two of the about four people who have them did not both get them from that woman. **Marek goes up at about half past six without saying he was going, and there are about eleven people in that room, and he asks the woman of about fifty-four which desk it is, and she says no, and says that if she did she would not tell him, and he asked her a question whose answer he already knew.** He asks her instead whether she knows a woman who fills in forms in columns for people, and she says she knows four and will not narrow it to one and he may write that down exactly as she has said it. **Nobody in that room is asked what they came for, a man of about thirty-four asks whether anything is written down and is told no, and nobody is owed anything at the end of it.**
- **`1002`** A Tuesday, day 2218, week 333. **The relationship, and it is the same shape as Volume 19's with one thing changed.** Talia comes into that shop at about half past seven with a bag of other people's washing in it and sits on the customer side, which she has not done in about four months. **She tells him, unasked, that there are four desks on that floor and she could have the names of everybody on it inside a week and has not asked and is not going to, and she says she has said that twice in six weeks and would like it noticed.** Marek does not ask. About nine seconds go by with the till on. She then puts a thing of her own on the counter and does not push it: **a man came to the clinic on Friday with a bad foot and told her in about four minutes what that room is for, and his four words are not the same four words Marek's man had, and she will not give them to him and will not name him.** She closes by saying she has been asked twice this week by people who are not in her care and has said the same four words both times, and one of those was yesterday, and that is the one she cannot get back.
- **`1003`** A Wednesday, day 2219, week 333. **The day the movement exists for, and nobody answers anybody.** A woman of about thirty-four comes through the door off that line at about half past seven with a folder under her arm, does not sit down, and asks the first person she speaks to for a form. About sixteen people are in that room. She tells the woman of about fifty-four that a woman at a desk told her this is where you go to be told about a form, and the woman of about fifty-four says **nobody here can tell you what it is for, and that is not me being careful. I do not know.** A man of about twenty-six says there is a form about her in the folder and that its fifth column is blank, and she says the fifth is blank on hers as well. **Marek asks her what the four words were and she refuses and gives her reason in one go: the last person she said them to wrote them on a form, and now there are two of them saying the same thing and neither said it to the other.** Then Marek asks the woman of about fifty-four, out loud, in front of about sixteen people, whether somebody could print what was said and print that it is wrong, and is told that there is a building in a second district that has done it twice for him already, and that whatever she told him would be the first thing anybody in this city had ever written down about that room and would be in a handwriting that had never been in the building. **Behind the woman's chair there is a space with nothing in it and a mark on the wall, and Marek does the sum standing up with his hands behind his back, and it comes to seven hundred and thirty-five days, which is one hundred and five weeks to the day, and he does not say it out loud and nobody asks him for it.**
- **`1004`** A Thursday, day 2220, week 333. **He goes and stands at the wall and it is going faster than the paper.** He is in a passage off a clinic in a fourth district for about an hour, reading the sheet from about nine feet off, and about nine people stop at it and **not one of them goes anywhere afterwards.** About four of them say the four words to each other while he is there, one of them saying the fifth column is blank every time and you go again, and the other saying she is not it and nobody there is it. **A woman of about forty-eight turns round, finds out he is the one who paid for it, asks what it is for, and he says he cannot tell her that and can tell her what is on it, which is a door and that anybody may come, and that he has been in that room and still does not have an answer that would survive being said out loud in a passage.** Then three people ask for something at the top of the sheet; there is nothing at the top of it; a man of about thirty-one says it reads as a thing somebody could not finish; a woman of about thirty-one says then somebody has to finish it; and Marek says not him and not today. **Nothing is set.**
- **`1005`** A Friday, day 2221, week 333. **A man tries to write it down and cannot, and a man tells him why, and neither of them does anything.** Marek takes a docket off the pad, turns it round, and writes four lines on the back of it, and about four people in that shop read them upside down while he is at the counter and none of them can tell what they are about. He asks the man of about thirty-three to read the four lines and tell him whether any of it is a sentence about that room, and is told **there are four lines and no verb in three of them, and a thing that cannot finish itself is not a description, it is a man not knowing.** Then Marek says the thing he has worked out, out loud, at that counter: a sheet says a door, a woman at a desk says a form, both are true, only one is on paper, paper does not lose, and so in about four weeks the only version anybody will have heard twice will be the one nobody wrote down. He is told it is a queue forming round a thing that does not exist. He says **I printed the door.** The man of about thirty-three then says it for the fourth time in this matter and adds that one of the three of them has stopped saying it, and will not say which. **Marek asks him what he would do about it and is told it depends on whether he wants it to stop or wants it to be true, and that if he does not know which he will do whichever is cheaper and it will not be the same thing twice.**
- **`1006`** A Sunday, day 2223, week 333, and the shutter comes down at about two and not at about ten. **He goes up at half past two, which is not when he goes, and finds about four people in that room and about four of them already in it when he comes in.** He asks how many come on a Sunday and is told about four, and the same four, and that two of them have been coming since before he was in that shop. **He asks whether any of them has ever been there on a Thursday and is told no, not one.** He asks whether anybody says anything in there about what the room is and is told no, not on a Sunday: they sit, two of them talk about a bus route, one talks about a knee, and the woman of about fifty-four does a book which is nothing and which she has not read anything out of since the winter. **Then he takes a bus of about half an hour across the city and stands in the same passage in the fourth district at about half past four, and there is nobody in it at all, and the clinic door is shut, and the sheet is still up by its two drawing pins, and he stays ten minutes and does not touch it.** That Sunday's four jobs came to twenty pounds, the lowest of the movement.
