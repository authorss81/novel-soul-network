## Volume 01 — The Missing Beat (Chapters 1–50)

### Chapter 0001 — "Night Shift Ledger"
Civic Spine 7, Tuesday of week 1, 01:40. Marek clears the night list, counts exits on arrival, and finds a third Threadline 4-19 practice plate with a scorched return contact. Sera wants it signed out as a vendor fault; he repairs it instead and writes the fault on the back of the form, using Evan's scratched four-beat card as a shim. Ends on the discovery: the whole city is one delivery, and he is the only signature on it that says anything.

### Chapter 0002 — "The Practicum"
Practical room two, Thursday of week 1, 09:20. The team rescue drill on 4-19 stock. Rusk teaches from a laminated script and rewards exact cue timing, and the fourth beat has a name in that script — *scope* — and nothing in the technique answers it. Marek runs the sequence correctly on the first pass, which surprises everyone including him. When Tam's boot comes off the ladder, Marek's own count arrives at the fourth place and finds a gap about two seconds wide with a person falling into it. His body gives out on him: metallic taste, and the floor loses its color.

### Chapter 0003 — "Four Beats"
The same room, seconds later. Marek offers Rafi a share in one bad sentence in under four seconds and Rafi says yes; the interface says only that two practices agree and one beat is not answered, and offers nothing further. The line lasts about four seconds and shows Marek the shape of the hole. He finishes the release himself in Rusk's cadence, Tam lets go, and the rescue works. Then his own anchor fails, the line collapses, and he is left with a headache, an unnamed floor color, and a piece of Tam's terror running in his chest. Rusk tells him kindly that he hesitated. The drill sheet ends with *Senn* written in the name box on line four, and Tam's own note: *He said it. I let go because he said it. Both are true.*

### Chapter 0004 — "Aftertaste"

### Chapter 0005 — "The Seal Run"
Institute exam hall, Thursday of week 4. Nine candidates, three examiners, and a scoring condition that puts Rusk's voice inside the seal. The first candidate completes the run perfectly by turning her head at the fourth beat. Marek performs anchor, load and close correctly, Rusk begins the word *scope*, and Marek declines it in public and tries to do the fourth beat alone. It is not there — the beat is where somebody else answers. The board records *not completed, fourth beat not performed, substitute cue declined by the candidate, deficiency recorded pending review.* He is offered a technician apprenticeship, a transcript line he cannot fix, and a provisional witnessed assessment on Thursday of week 7 with the scholarship suspended. The line he cannot unwrite is *performance dependent on external cue*, and nobody in the building has yet said the sentence *the fourth beat is missing.*

### Chapter 0006 — "Not a Keepsake"

### Chapter 0007 — "A Line Is Not Proof"
Clinic treatment bay and the Institute kit store, **Thursday** of week 5, night. Talia meets him at the door and tells him she is **changing the terms of the offer she made on Tuesday** — the clinic owns no brace, and a demonstration that is not the thing on the sheet makes every scope she has ever written a lie. She writes a new scope in front of him, reads it back, makes him copy it word for word, and signs under it while he puts his name on the other side of the line. Then she performs a brace twice on a table with a bag of bricks, and the fourth beat turns out to be a sentence and an empty hand and a decision handed back. Marek writes the difference down five times and gets it wrong four times, and then sees the trap: two observations by two people trained by the same twelve people is company, not evidence. The interface says only that two practices agree and one boundary is not performed, and then says nothing at all. He writes himself a rule in the smallest handwriting he owns — *do not let wanting press two things together until they look like evidence* — and says aloud that he needs three people he has never met. In the store room, with a brass key nobody ever asked back, he counts twenty-four plates, photographs the bundle label and its two empty lines, and finds the eleven-page instructor booklet that names the instructor's cue as the expected completion and calls it *a support beat… not a step in the technique*. The 3-08 manual in his jacket says **the practitioner**; the 4-19 says **the instructor**. He cannot tell why, and names four live explanations — bad print run, wrong revision printed, a supplier assembling somebody else's book, or a person who chose it — and states that he has no way at all to tell which. The dread stops being about his competence and becomes a product that people signed for. He leaves the crate exactly as he found it, copies the count by hand, and reads a department notice that puts Tam on a west berth loading dock at half past six on **Tuesday of week 6 — five days away**. He knows the one useful thing he could do with five days and it is the thing he swore in Chapter 6 he would never do again.

### Chapter 0008 — "The Voice He Hates"
Courier dock, Saltmarket waterfront, west berth, Tuesday of week 6, 19:20. **He had five days between learning about the work-setting check and the day of the dock — Friday of week 5 to Tuesday of week 6, a window in which nothing else in the batch happens — and he spent all of them working out a version of the conversation he did not have. He had already tried the version once, badly, on the Tuesday of the week before, in a doorway on Sennet Row, and had been told to come to the front of the room like a person.** Tam's check goes fine four times in forty-five minutes, and then a courier's heel goes off wet concrete and a pallet of iced crates starts to lean. Marek watches Tam get to the third beat and stop, with nobody available to say the word. He says it himself — Rusk's exact cadence, from outside a barrier, fifty feet across a running diesel ramp — and it works, and in the second that follows he hears what he has become. He stops, refuses to say another word in that voice, and takes the narrow way instead: **he asks rather than gives, tells Tam plainly that he is not handing over a thing and that he is the last person in the city who should be saying the word to him, and offers to say no to anything Tam asks for.** Tam's own flat hand goes onto the crate edge as the boundary check. The band goes. The crate takes Tam's forearm and two tendons. He kneels in the meltwater and admits exactly what he did, and the worst of it is that the Institute would have praised him. Tam tells him he now has two voices in it, and the second one is his, and tells him he needs to say something before the ambulance does.
