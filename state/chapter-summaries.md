**944 — Thursday, day 2108, entry 947. Nine callers. THIRTY-FIVE POUNDS. A WOMAN WANTED A NOTICE OFF A WALL AND THE MAN WHO PUT IT UP SAID NO WITH A REASON FROM NINE YEARS.** The woman of about forty-three wanted the printed form taken down off a passage in a fourth district. The man of about thirty-eight who keeps the clinic there said he carried it out of that room on the Tuesday before last, put it up himself in about twenty minutes, asked nobody, and is not taking it down. **His reason: in nine years of that clinic people are told what day they are coming in and are not asked whether that suits them; about four have not come; he knows which four and has never asked any of them; and when you take a notice down, the only people who see it are the ones who were already looking — and he has watched that happen on a wall about nine times.** He said the last part to Marek and not to her. **Marek said the reason was correct. Nobody said anything. He said it twice, the second time slower, and about four people in that room have said since that he did not seem to understand he was making it worse each time.** Then she asked him what the notice is for, and nothing came out of him for about four seconds, and what came out was: **I do not know. I have looked at it every day for two weeks and I cannot say what it is asking anybody to do.** She said: it is not asking anybody to do anything, that is what is wrong with it. He said: I know. **And that was the last thing anybody said about the notice that night.**

**945 — Saturday, day 2110, entry 948. Seven callers. THIRTY-TWO POUNDS. THE DAY FELL AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM KNEW, AND AT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON ONE OF THE NINE CAME IN AND GAVE HER NAME OUT LOUD.** The form lay on its own table from about half past nine with a date in a column on nine rows and nothing in the other four columns of any of them. **Nobody turned it over. Nobody put a hand on it. Seven people came and went and about four of them have said since that they had walked past the notice and that if they had known it was made in that room they would have stood in the doorway instead of coming in.** At about four in the afternoon a woman of about fifty-one came in, did not sit down, and said her own name into about nine people. Then: **I want to know whether that day is mine.** The woman of about forty-three put her hand flat on the table a foot from the form, did not touch it, and said the sentence the volume is built out of: **if I write your name on that it is a name on that, and a name on that is an answer you did not give.** Then she said the other half of it: **and if I do not write it down then you have said a thing in this room and it is gone.** The man of about thirty-three said the only thing anybody said for about a minute: **I have stood at that rail next to you** — and did not finish it, and nobody finished it for him. **Marek asked her one question and wrote three on the back of his hand and crossed two out: is the day yours. She said: that is what I came to find out. He said: do you know. She said: no.** She went at about half past six and nobody tried to stop her. **Nobody in that room asked her one question about why she came, and nobody has since, and she has said that she would have answered.**

**946 — Sunday, day 2111, entry 949. Four callers. TWENTY-TWO POUNDS. THE SHUTTER CAME DOWN AT ABOUT TWO. HE ASKED HER WHETHER SHE WANTED HER NAME WRITTEN DOWN AND SHE SAID NO IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS.** She came back at about one and nobody had been told and there is nothing in that room by which anybody could have been told; about four people noticed and about four have said since that they did not say anything to each other about it. She sat down, having not sat down on the Saturday. **Talia was in that room for the second time in a week, about a clinic rota, and heard the whole of it, and has said since that if she had come in on the Saturday it would have gone differently and worse and that she cannot decide whether that is a reason to come in more or less.** He asked her whether she wanted her name written down and **she said no in about four seconds, before he had finished the last word**, and gave a reason that was not about herself: because you would have to have a place to put it, and you do not have a place to put it, and if you wrote it on that sheet then the only copy of it in this city would be on a sheet nobody has ever asked anybody about. The woman of about forty-three said **we will not do it and we will not write it and we will not say afterwards that we did not know her name**, and when Marek said *then say her name* she said no, and when he said that was the same as saying she did not know it, she said **it is not, and I am not going to be the person who says her name out loud in this room again.** Talia told the woman that nobody in that building could tell her whether the day was hers, that she was allowed to go and look and nobody would stop her and nobody would go with her, and that **looking is free and going on your own, and if you find out it is yours nobody will be able to take it off you, and if you find out it is not yours nothing will have happened to you at all.** Then the woman asked the question the afternoon was about: **and what do I do with the day.** Nobody in that room said one word. Nobody in that room said one word for the rest of the afternoon. **Marek said one thing before she went: I am going to go and find out and it will not be this week, and if I find out I am not going to be able to tell you first. She said: I know you will not be able to do that and that is not what I would want.**

**947 — Monday, day 2128 — NO. Monday of week 318, day 2112, entry 950. Nine callers. FORTY-EIGHT POUNDS. A SIXTH COLUMN HEADED *told*, AND NOBODY WAS APPOINTED TO ANYTHING, AND THE WORD IS IN A MARGIN.** The man of about fifty-two kept that six-column sheet in the inside pocket of a coat that has been to about nine funerals and said he had taken it out about nine times and put it back. The woman of about forty-three said the column will say what we told and it will not say what they were told, and when he asked it or not she said **the day you told them, or the day it was** — and that stopped the room for about nine seconds. **The man of about thirty-three said it standing up: it is a column and not a person and a column is cheaper than a person, and that is why it will happen, it does not matter what any of us wants tonight.** Then the man of about fifty-two said a column needs a name at the bottom of it, not his, because a column with nobody's name under it is a column nobody has to answer for and that is how we got here. **He said: I will do it.** And about four people said nearly the same thing within about nine seconds of each other and none of them said it to him, and the sharpest was: **because you have been right in that room about four times and you have not been the person who did anything about any of them, and I do not want the first thing you do to be a column.** He has said since that she was right. Nothing was decided and the sheet went back into the coat. **And then at about half past nine, with the room emptying, Marek found one word written in pencil in the right-hand margin of a copy of that form, outside all six columns: it said *told*, and it had been rubbed out with a thumb and about half of it is still legible. About four people in that room know who wrote it and not one of them will say. Nobody turned that form over.**

**948 — Tuesday, day 2113, entry 951. Nine callers. THIRTY-NINE POUNDS. A BUS OF ABOUT TWENTY MINUTES TO A RECORDS ROOM, AND A NAME WRITTEN UNDER A NAME THAT WAS CROSSED OFF.** A man of about sixty-one read the request standing up and said the last part as a question even though it was not one, which is how he tells you whether he has understood it. He said: **about four years ago there was a request for a copy of a page from a room in a fourth district; it was a good request and I would have said yes if I had been asked in person; it came out in an envelope and it has not come back; I wrote for it once and I got an answer that was about the envelope.** Marek was on the point of saying that was not the same thing and stopped, and worked out about nine seconds later that it was. Then he turned the request round and pushed it a foot and said **there is a name at the top of it in a hand that is not yours**, and when Marek said that is the room it came from he said **no it is not, and if I do not know whose name it is then I am not sending a page to a person.** So Marek read the top of it again and found the woman of about forty-three's name there, and crossed it out with a pen he had in his pocket for that and had never once used, and wrote his own underneath it, standing up, with the date. The man watched him do it and said nothing at all while he did it, and **about four people who have dealt with him have said he has never watched anybody do that before.** Then he said: **you have asked me for a page about nine people you cannot name, in your own name, on a piece of paper that will sit in a drawer in this room, so you are the one who will be holding that it came out of here.** Marek said yes. He said: **then it will not come to you. Not the page. Not now and not after and not when you have made up your mind about it.** And then: **I will have an answer in about nine days and you will not like it.**

**949 — Wednesday, day 2114, entry 952. Nine callers. FIFTY-SIX POUNDS. HE CAME UP THE STAIR UNINVITED WITH THE BOOK UNDER HIS ARM AND WOULD NOT READ IT OUT, AND HE WOULD NOT PUT IT IN MAREK'S HANDS EITHER.** He put the book on the table and said it is not out of the room and it will not be. **I have found the page. I am not going to tell you what is on it.** When Marek said *you have found it and you are not going to read it out*, he answered the rest of it before he had finished: **because I am a landlord and a filing system. I hold it and I let it out and I do not decide what is said about it. If I read nine names out in this room then those nine names have been read out in this room, and it will be because of me, and in about four years somebody will ask me why I did it and I will not have anything to say that is not *they asked me*.** He said it has to be read by somebody, and that it cannot be him, cannot be the woman of forty-three, and cannot be Marek — **because the request is signed with Marek's name on it, and a man who asks about nine people should not be the man who finds out who they are. That is not a rule anybody wrote down. That is me, and I have had nine years to find somebody who does not want the paper back, and I have not found him, and I am not going to start now by handing it to the one man in this city who has already signed something about it.** Talia asked him to repeat it and said one word, which was please. **And Marek did not ask the next question. He has said since, to about four people separately, that he had it ready and that the sentence began with her name and the room did not come into it and it stayed in his head, and that he has not written it down anywhere.** **She was in that room and she has said that she knew what the unasked question was, and that she did not look at him while he was deciding, and that she decided not to look on purpose, and that both of those were decisions and one was a rule she made for herself and the other was not.** Then he said the part about the holding: **whoever reads it is holding it from the moment they open it, and there is no way round it, and you have signed up for the other half of it and the other half is that you will never know. I have carried it for nine years. You are about to carry it for about nine days and you will not like that either.** He took the book under his arm and went down that stair, which took him about four minutes, and nobody said anything while he was going.

**950 — Thursday, day 2115, entry 953. Nine callers. THIRTY-FOUR POUNDS. HE ASKED A WOMAN HE WORKS FOR TO READ A PAGE AND SHE SAID YES BEFORE HE HAD FINISHED THE SENTENCE, AND THEN SAID WHAT YES WOULD COST.** He caught her on that stair at about four past eight and **said *mine* first, which is the wrong order and which he has thought about since.** She said **no** to the page and then said **yes** to reading it and would not let him put the two in the same breath. She said she will not do it on a Thursday and will not do it in that room, **because that room has spent four years learning that it cannot hold a person's name, and I am not going to open it in that room and put nine of them on a table.** Then she asked the only question she needed to ask: who asked for it. Whose hand wrote it. Hers to begin with, and it is crossed off. **She read the request standing up on the stair and turned it round and turned it back and gave it to him, and she said right.** Then: **when I open it I am holding it. Not the man in the second district. Me.** And: **you will not know what is in it, and you will not be able to help, and you will find that very hard, and I have watched somebody find it very hard before.** Talia came down that stair having heard all of it from half a landing up and said one sentence, to the wall rather than to him: **if she reads it then it is hers and not yours, and if you go on asking her about it then you have made it yours again in the only way that was left.** She has said since that she has been annoyed with herself about that for two days. And then Sera Quill said the last thing about it, about herself and not about him: **I am not doing it on my own. Two people holding one page is not two people reading it. It is one page with two names against it, and if I am alone in a room with nine names then the nine names and I are the same thing, and I have been the only one in a room too many times.** He said who. **She said: I have not asked anybody yet.** She has said since that she has four people in her head, that she would ask one of them within about nine days, that if she had not asked by the end of the month she would not read it at all, and that the man who signed it will find that out at the same time as everybody else.
