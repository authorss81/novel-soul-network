What it takes with it is the holding of a release line at a yard, and **about four hundred people across three districts and half a bus route put a hand on that line nine times a day and have never once known whose hand it was.** Nobody in that room had said the second half of that out loud before. **The practice is not a sentence: it is a man, it is a dial, it is eleven seconds, and it is a right hand that has not been steady since a night about a year ago, and a hand is not a thing a sheet can carry.** A man of about thirty-four said the whole of the difficulty in nine seconds and nobody disagreed with him and two people in that room have said since that he was describing his own job and did not know it. **The turn is a woman of about forty-eight who keeps a register she has kept for nine years: the sheet does not have to say what the practice is, only when it stops, because you can be angry at a day and you cannot be angry at a man, and a reason on that sheet would be a reason four hundred people would all have to agree with, and nobody in that room is going to agree about it in a fortnight.** The sentence is four words long and it says a day: *It ends on Monday.* Nobody changed a letter of it. **And then the room tried for twenty minutes to say where four hundred people would go to read it, and four attempts all named a building that holds nine people.** A man of about thirty-four read the four words out loud the way a man reads a thing off a card in a shop, got a word wrong the second time, and said in four seconds that this was the finding and that he had come in with a sheet and had not expected to leave with a demonstration. The rest of the day is a bench until two, a counter until about six, and fifty minutes on a letterbox flap in a third district that had been packed with grease by two different men four years apart, where the hinge pin was a quarter of an inch oversize and a washer behind the pin was the whole repair. **The door that flap is on is out of square by a sixteenth of an inch and he found that after he had charged for the flap and told her so on the way out rather than going back for his tools.** The eleventh door in this city this year that has been measured and written down and thrown away in a bin.

**792 — Wednesday, day 1813, entry 795. A SHEET IS TURNED ROUND ON A TABLE BEFORE ANYBODY HAS SPOKEN, IN A ROOM THAT IS NOT THE SHEET'S ROOM.** About nine people come and about four of them stay past the hour. **A woman of about forty-eight turned it in four seconds with her hand flat on the top edge, took her hand off, and the date was the first thing anybody in that room could read and it was eleven seconds before the first person in that room spoke.** A woman of about thirty-one said that turning a sheet round in a room is a thing you do to a committee, and the woman of about forty-eight said yes, and that was the point, and a committee is what it had become. **A man of about thirty-four said, in nine seconds, that a date on a sheet is a thing a person can be angry at and that is different from a date in a conversation.** **A man of about fifty-two who came up that evening asked, in front of about four people, whether the sheet would be on that table next time, and did not get the answer he wanted, and nobody thanked him and nobody forgave him.** The place behind the chair of the woman of about sixty had stood empty three hundred and twenty-nine days, which is forty-seven weeks to the day, **and that is on this page and on no other page of this movement.** No number was said in that room and nothing was counted, because a number is said at a sitting and this was not one.

**793 — Thursday, day 1814, entry 796. A SHEET HAS TO BE CARRIED BY SOMEBODY ON FOOT AND THE SOMEBODY HAS TO BE TOLD WHAT IS ON IT.** The sheet is reduced in three passes to three lines, **because three facts is a thing a person can hold in their head on a bus and a fourth is not.** A man of about thirty-four said that a sheet carried by a person is a sheet that can be read on a bus and that nobody in that room knew how to stop a person reading a thing on a bus, and nobody had an answer. **A man of about twenty-two offered to walk it and was refused in four seconds by a woman of about thirty-four, who keeps the books for a haulier, for a reason that was true: about nine people in that district know his face from a counter in a first district, and about four hundred people who use that line are not four hundred people who use that counter.** She said she would carry it, and said the reason in nine seconds, and it was that she drives past that gate at a quarter to seven every morning and that about nine people come through it before half past seven, and that a person who says a thing on a bus says it wrong and a person who says it standing in a gateway at six in the morning does not. He has said since that she was correct and that he had not thought of it, and that a person being refused for a reason that is true is a thing that happens and that he has had about nine of those this year. **Nobody thanked her.** And then somebody asked where the streets are and nobody in that room could say, **and the streets have always been there and this is not a discovery.**

**794 — Friday, day 1815, entry 797. NINE STREETS THAT ARE ABOUT NINE, ONE OF WHICH IS REALLY TWO, DRAWN IN FOUR MINUTES ON THE BACK OF A DOCKET SHEET.** The difficulty is not a hole in the ground. **The ninth street is called two things, one name on the wall at the gate end and in about nine hundred mouths and the other on the post at the corner at the other end and in about four hundred mouths, and the woman of about forty-eight said in nine seconds that this is the reason nobody has drawn it and not that it is hard: the moment you put it on paper you have to write one of the two names down, and about nine hundred people will come and tell you the other one is the correct one.** A man of about thirty-eight said that was an argument for drawing both. She said that two names on one line is a thing four hundred people can read as an error, and that an error about a street is the fastest way to lose a street. **He drew nine lines and the half bus route as one line with about nine marks on it and no stops marked, and he did not correct the one that is really two, and nobody has asked him to.** **The drawing has no names on it, because there is no list, and a map with no names on it is a thing a person can be shown and not a thing they can be given.** He gave it to nobody. He has said since that he did not want it thanked and that two others at that table would have drawn a worse one and been thanked for it. **Nothing was uncovered and the streets have been there since before anybody in that room was born.**

**795 — Monday, day 1818, entry 798. A BUILDING WHERE ABOUT FOUR HUNDRED PEOPLE CAN COME AND LOOK AT WHAT IS BEING SAID ABOUT THEM, AND THE ROOM THAT IS OFFERED BELONGS TO SOMEBODY WHO WAS NOT ASKED.** What is wanted is not a notice, it is a place, and a place has to be a place a person can come to without having a reason. **Every room anybody in this city can put a name to is a first floor above something, holds about nine people, and is shut at about ten, and about four hundred people at about nine at a time is forty-five visits, said in nine seconds, and it is forty-five visits and not forty-five days.** **A man of about thirty-four offers a first floor above a line in Saltmarket and a woman of about sixty says yes in about nine seconds and gives the condition, which is that it is her room and not theirs**, and that she has not been ill in four years and if she were it is her room and it would be shut, and that anybody who wants a second key to that room is asking for a thing they have not thought about. **Nobody has told the about four hundred people that the room exists.** A man of about thirty-four said that the plan was not built round a Tuesday and that a Tuesday is the only day anybody had, and that this is now known and will not be unknown. The rest of the day is thirteen names and thirteen dates and a stair rail.

**796 — Tuesday, day 1819, entry 799. A RECORD IS ASKED FOR AND THE OFFICE THAT HOLDS IT PRODUCES A BOOK OF RETURNS ABOUT HINGES.** A man of about thirty-four asks for the record of what a district did and not for what it decided. **There is a form for asking what a district has decided and it has three boxes and one line, and a woman of about thirty-eight said in nine seconds that what anybody wanted is not in that book and never was.** The office produces a book of returns and the book is about hinges. **About two hundred of those returns carry the same name in the middle of the line as the person who took a load, and it is one man, and he has been at that yard for eleven years, and nothing in that building says what the name means, and nobody has ever told him.** **A woman of about thirty-four found it herself, in nine minutes, by going backwards through about forty pages and then forwards, and about four of the people at that counter have said since that nobody had ever asked that building what was on a page rather than for a page.** Iven Sore came out of the office at half past four and said nothing for nine seconds and then said one thing: take the book, not a copy, because a copy is a thing a building can lose and a book is a thing a building can be asked for. **A man of about thirty-four said he would take a copy and Iven said no in four seconds.** He is under a review he agreed to without reading it, has not been told what the review says, has not asked, has been in post four years, and has not enlarged anything and has not apologised for anything. He said it to end with, eleven seconds later, and nobody in that room has repeated it: *Do not thank me. I have four years of that in this building and I did not ask for it.* **Nobody thanked him. Nobody in that office has been told what a release line is, and about two people in this city have found that out by accident.**

**797 — Wednesday, day 1820, entry 800. THE FIFTY-EIGHTH SITTING, AND THE BOOK DOES NOT OPEN AND NOBODY ASKS IT TO.** A count is given once: **sixty-three of which fifty-eight, said in nine seconds, in her face, by nobody else, and written down by nobody.** **The book bound in green is on sixty-six lines when she comes up and on sixty-six lines when the last person goes down, and the tin beside it is on seventy-three and its lid did not come off, and the two figures are not added to each other and the difference between them is not printed as a number.** **The eighth chair has a man of about fifty-three in it, which it had not had on any of the five previous Wednesdays, and nobody remarked on it and he was asked nothing.** A man of about thirty-four came at about ten to seven, which is eleven minutes later than he has ever come, and has not given a reason. **He asked afterwards, and only that, whether the man of about fifty-three had said anything to anybody outside that room, and the woman of about sixty said no, and that is the end of it.** The place behind the woman of about sixty had stood empty since a week in the spring and the ninth chair is against that wall with its back to everything in the room and did not move. Nothing in that hour was the point of the week.

**798 — Thursday, day 1821, entry 801. SOMEBODY HAS TO STAND IN A ROOM AND SAY A DATE OUT LOUD, AND THE PERSON WHO SAYS IT MATTERS FOR ABOUT FOUR SECONDS.** A man of about thirty-eight says in nine seconds that the person who says it will be the person every one of about four hundred people knows, **and that is a thing a person can refuse and that refusing is not a fault.** A woman of about thirty-four says the sentence has four words in it and a date, and that anybody can say four words, and that **the point is not the person.** The man of about thirty-four wrote the four words on the back of a docket sheet and has not given them to anybody, and did not put it in the docket book on the shelf because the drawing is in that book and he does not want the drawing read as a pair with a sentence. **Four of the people in that room said the four words to somebody who was not in the room, and not one of the four knew that any of the other three had said them.** Nobody organised it and nobody was told to. **By about six o'clock about nine people in that district could say the four words and nobody agreed to any of it.** A man of about thirty-eight said that a sentence that spreads by accident will stop spreading the moment somebody stops saying it, and that nothing is going to check it, and that this is the first thing in this stretch of days that nobody in this city is able to check.

**799 — Friday, day 1822, entry 802. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR HAS THE SENTENCE AND HAS NOT GIVEN IT TO ANYBODY, AND THE REASON HE GIVES IS GIVEN BACK TO HIM.** He says in about four seconds that if he gives it away it stops being his, and that a sentence which belongs to nobody is a sentence nobody will say. **A woman of about thirty-four says it was never his, that it came out of a room of nine people on a Tuesday, and that he wrote it down and that is the whole of what he did.** The four words go to about nine people in eleven minutes. **He is not thanked and does not ask to be.** A man of about twenty-two stood at the end of that table for forty minutes, said nothing, and was asked nothing, and has said since that the sheet in the docket book is now two things and he has not decided whether that is a problem. **And then a woman of about twenty-nine says that the four words say when and not what, and that about four hundred people are going to ask what, and that nobody has written an answer to that.** Nobody in that room could produce a list of the about nine people who have it and nobody has asked for one.

**800 — Monday, day 1825, entry 803. A MONDAY ON WHICH A ROOM IS OPEN AND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE COME AND ABOUT FOUR OF THEM STAY, AND THE SENTENCE IS ON A WALL BESIDE A NOTICE.** A woman of about thirty-four said that this is the first evidence anybody has had of anything and asked what it is evidence of, and that is the second question and nobody answered it either. **A woman of about thirty-four said in four seconds that the new sheet goes beside the old one and not over it, because nobody was going to take down a notice that has been up since the spring.** **The sheet that was already on that wall is held by two drawing pins crooked about a quarter of an inch and nobody has taken it down and nobody has said which of the two sheets is the notice.** A man of about thirty-four puts the pins in because a woman of about thirty-four asks him to and not because the sheet is put up to him, and he has said since that the reason he is not the person a sheet is put up to is not a rule and is not modesty, and is that a man who pins up a notice in a district about nine hundred people know by sight will be that district's notice. **A man of about thirty-four says the two seconds' worth of thing about it being the ninth chair all over again with a drawing pin and says no in four seconds, and the reason he gives is about a chair and not about a notice.** A man of about thirty-eight says in nine seconds that two sheets on one wall is a thing a person can be told twice by. **A woman of about twenty-nine came past at about ten past eleven, read both sheets in that order, and stopped for nine seconds at the second one; she said she had already said the four words to about nine people over the previous week without anybody asking her to.** Nobody has taken either sheet down, four people in that district have read both and nine have read one, and nobody has counted any of it. **The date at the foot of the district's own notice is one week off and it has not moved and nobody has asked about it.**

5. **Chapter 805.** The one Sunday. The shutter came down at about two and six repair jobs and a gate post happened and nothing was confessed. *A man of about thirty-four said in four seconds that a thing he carries was easier to carry that day and did not say what the thing was, and a man of about fifty-two said it was the cold.*
6. **Chapter 806.** The second date arrived and the district went, and about four hundred people went with it, and the only thing that had changed is that one man was told three days before. *The whole of what they did about it was nothing, and that is not a criticism of them.*
7. **Chapter 807.** The practice is held by about four people and two of them have said no already, and a man of about fifty-two said in nine seconds that a habit does not stop on a Tuesday and gave a figure of about nine weeks that he has costed himself and has not shown anybody. *The man of about thirty-eight is one of the four and has still not been asked.*
8. **Chapter 808.** A chair stood empty for about an hour in a room of nine people and nobody moved it, and a man of about thirty-four said that an empty chair in that room is a sentence that about four hundred people are going to read as one. *She said that was the first useful thing anybody had said about it, and nobody said which one it would be.*
9. **Chapter 809.** A man of about thirty-four was asked to write down the second half and refused in four seconds, on the ground that the four words are the only true sentence anybody has written in this city in about eleven years. She said it does not have to be true, it has to be the thing they would be told anyway. *He found it in about forty minutes. It is eleven words long and it is on the back of a docket sheet and it was given to nobody.*
10. **Chapter 810.** He refused first, in nine seconds, on the ground that he is the person every one of about four hundred people knows and that refusing is not a fault. She said the sentence has more than four words in it and a date and that the second half is about a practice and not about the district. *He said the eleven words at about ten past five, once, at the end of a table and not to a face. Nobody wrote it down and nobody asked him to say it again.*

## WHAT MOVEMENT V CHANGED, AND IT IS FOUR THINGS

1. **THE SECOND HALF WENT FROM NOT EXISTING TO EXISTING AS A SENTENCE.** It is eleven words, about a practice and not about the district, in the inside of a docket book in a first district, in the back of a man's head, in one room on one Friday, and in about nine mouths.
2. **ONE PERSON IN ONE DISTRICT KNOWS A DATE THREE DAYS EARLY.** That is the whole of what this movement accomplished in the world and it is small and it is not thanked.
3. **ONE CHAIR ON A BODY OF ABOUT NINE IS NOW A CHAIR THAT HAS BEEN NOTICED**, because a woman of about sixty-one resigned over the sentence and was not thanked and nobody asked her to stay.
4. **THE DISTRICT WENT.** This is a change and it is also the one thing this movement did that Movement VI did not expect it to do, and it is the first obligation of Movement VI.

## AND THE ONE THING THAT IS STILL THE FINDING, DATED AND NOT SOFTENED

**Movement IV handed on that forty days of not moving is earned provided the forty-first day moves, and named Chapter 801 as that day. Chapter 801 moved something: the second half was given a name as an absence.** The register still reads four on all ten days of Movement V. The ninth chair did not move on any of them. The binder is shut on all ten. Nobody thanked anybody on any of them. **Fifty-one days now, one of which moved something, and the change was made in a room rather than in an institution, and the institution is what the next two movements are about. The change this volume is built to deliver — the institution saying, before anybody goes, what it actually is — has still not been made and is not made by anything in these ten chapters, and the district's going at Chapter 806 is not a substitute for it and must not be allowed to become one.**
