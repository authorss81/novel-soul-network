## Core canon

- Setting: Nacre, a modern coastal city-republic. The formal public network is the Civic Weave; the common name is the Soul Network.
- Protagonist: Marek Senn, 22, failing practical student and night repairer at Civic Spine 7. He wants to pass the Solo Seal and keep a stable, legal place to work.
- Marek’s flaw is taking responsibility before being asked and hiding its cost. His need is to accept chosen interdependence without turning it into ownership.
- Primary romance: Talia Venn, resonance nurse and consent advocate. They are not partners at the opening.
- Family: Lena Senn is Marek’s mother; Nell Senn is his younger sister; Evan Senn is his missing father.
- Recurring allies: Rafi Pell, Sera Quill, Asha Reed, and Dessa Kwan.
- Central antagonist: Dr. Iona Sorn, the Conductor. Oren Vey, Iven Sore, and Leo Marr represent commercial, institutional, and privacy pressures before the Crown Clause’s final use.

## Power rules

- A share carries observations and technique structure, not private thoughts, innate talent, or full memories.
- Consent is scoped and can be withdrawn. Use, Remember, and Derive are separate permissions.
- Connections produce aftertaste and attention debt. Overload can cause physical symptoms, emotional bleed, and lasting unmooring.
- A hinge is not merely a missing boundary label. It deletes the boundary-release beat that should apply the limit and complete the practice, then installs a key-holder’s cue as the learner’s expected replacement; it narrows practical independence but is not universal mind control.
- The opening brace is distributed by Threadline from an unnamed upstream technique bundle. Threadline is the known contracted assembler and seller, not the original author of the Crown Clause.
- Marek begins at Stage 0, Latent Counterpoint. His eventual stages end with Open Weave and the final Unbinding.

- The original Civic Compact required a visible Return Path and did not permit silence as agreement. Iona inserted the hidden Crown Clause into a working copy of its emergency protocol during the Marrow Falls outage; it treats silence as provisional consent and seeds hinge techniques.
- Evan Senn deliberately severed his own link to interrupt a Crown response; he is not resurrected and leaves no hidden inheritance.
- Iona’s final conflict is the Nine-Minute Hush, which begins only in Volume 15. In Volume 13 the coalition exposes the Crown Root Interface as a council-witnessed physical maintenance credential, not a personal power. Marek refuses the Crown Key, distributes the Return Path, and destroys that interface—giving up city-wide inspection and emergency validation while retaining only consent-based access to local networks.
- The ending is the Civic Commons: a voluntary, inspectable public institution with local councils, withdrawal rights, maintenance, and no single owner.
- The Assembly appoints Talia a provisional independent consent ombud in Volume 11 and formally confirms the role after the final crisis. She is not Marek’s subordinate or reward; their relationship remains a chosen, bounded partnership.
- No new final enemy, hidden bloodline, secret higher system, or universal consent is permitted.
