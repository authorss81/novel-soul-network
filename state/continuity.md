# Continuity State

Canon established in the bible, the series and volume outlines, and now in Chapter prose through Chapter 10.

## Core canon

- Setting: Nacre, a modern coastal city-republic. The formal public network is the Civic Weave; the common name is the Soul Network.
- Protagonist: Marek Senn, 22, third-year at the Nacre Institute of Applied Practice, night repairer at Civic Spine 7. He wants a license, a wage, and to stop being the family's problem.
- Marek's flaw is taking responsibility before being asked and hiding its cost. His need is to accept chosen interdependence without turning it into ownership.
- Primary romance: Talia Venn, resonance nurse and consent advocate. They are not partners at the opening.
- Family: Lena Senn is Marek's mother (nights at a long-stay clinic on Kestrel Row); Nell Senn is his younger sister, 16; Evan Senn is his missing father.
- Recurring allies: Rafi Pell (roommate, courier), Sera Quill (Spine 7 lead repairer), Asha Reed (Movement IV onward), Dessa Kwan (from Chapter 10).
- Central antagonist: Dr. Iona Sorn, the Conductor. Oren Vey, Iven Sore and Leo Marr represent commercial, institutional and privacy pressures before the Crown Clause's final use.

## Power rules

- A share carries observations and technique structure, not private thoughts, innate talent, or full memories.
- Consent is scoped and can be withdrawn. Use, Remember and Derive are separate permissions. Silence, proximity and a pre-signed workplace form are not agreement.
- Connections produce aftertaste and attention debt. Overload can cause physical symptoms, emotional bleed, and lasting unmooring.
- A hinge is not merely a missing boundary label. It deletes the boundary-release beat that should apply the limit and complete the practice, then installs a key-holder's cue as the learner's expected replacement; it narrows practical independence but is not universal mind control.
- The opening brace is distributed by Threadline from an unnamed upstream technique bundle. Threadline is the contracted assembler and seller, not the author of the Crown Clause.
- Marek begins at Stage 0, Latent Counterpoint. His eventual stages end with Open Weave and the final Unbinding.

## Canon now fixed in Chapter prose (Chapters 21–30)

### The Choir of Practice

- A co-operative of **212 members** — nurses, repairers, two teachers, a midwife, four drivers, a man who mends organ pipes — that meets on market days on a trestle table at the Saltmarket Exchange, next to the noticeboard. **Eleven people on market day, and nine of them are at the table at a time.** Every public beat of the Choir happens *in front of nine people*; the two numbers are not interchangeable and are not a contradiction. A quarterly, four times a year, a hundred and forty pages, printed in the district, **nine hundred addresses**, a free copy of the last one on the table and the table itself is free to read.
- **A lineage has four columns and no fifth: origin, revisions, boundaries, refusals.** The origin says who wrote it, where, what they were doing at the time, in a sentence a person can understand, not a department's word. Revisions say what changed, when, by whom, and **what they said the reason was**. Boundaries say what the technique may not touch and who may stop it. **Refusals say what the co-operative asked for and was told no.**
- **Everything on a sheet is a claim.** The Choir prints lineages it is quite sure are wrong, with the year it became sure in the margin, because a co-operative that only prints what it believes is a church. In four years it has printed four it is sure are wrong.
- **A request is either five pounds out of a print fund (the tin has four pounds and change in it) or free on a public-safety ground.** A safety request goes into the request book in full — name, technique, ground, date — is read out at the next market day to anybody who comes to the table, and if it is upheld the requester is **named in the quarterly with the ground**, and there is no mechanism for taking either back out. **Everything about a person, published by them, is published by name: corrections, disputes, requests, refusals.**
- **New people. Marda Szabo**, 51, orthopaedic nurse turned records clerk, a member for eleven years, does the lineages and the market table. **Ekow Nartey**, 33, keeps the request book, wears a market coat, tells you the cost of a thing before you ask for it, and reads corrections out loud to nine people in a market. **Beattie, Yusuf** — a member with a daughter, who voted against printing a distributor's design reference and is right, and is named in the minutes.
- **The rule about the box.** There is **one** design record in the co-operative's holdings, in a shoebox, in a brown envelope, and the line on the envelope is *you keep this, it is the only one anybody has ever sent us*. **It does not come out on a request, from anybody, for any reason.** The reason is not a rule: the last time a document came out of that box on somebody's request, the person who brought it in was named in a newspaper, she is a member, she still comes to market day, and she does not speak to nine of them. **It has been in the box for two years, and the member brought it in having been in a room in the middle of August.** **Chapter 25 is where the co-operative breaks this deliberately, by vote, eight to three, and the three noes are printed** — Sarr, Beattie and Ekow, with Marda in the majority and one of the three. **Chapter 25 puts the promise on the table in front of nine people before it breaks it**: Marda tells Marek, in the room, that the sheet on the trestle is out of the box, that she told him on the Wednesday and refused him, that she has not changed her mind and has been outvoted, and that the member was told at her own table and has not withdrawn it. **Do not break this rule again after Chapter 25.**
