# Continuity State

Canon established in the bible, the series and volume outlines, and now in Chapter prose through Chapter 10.

## Core canon

- Setting: Nacre, a modern coastal city-republic. The formal public network is the Civic Weave; the common name is the Soul Network.
- Protagonist: Marek Senn, 22, third-year at the Nacre Institute of Applied Practice, night repairer at Civic Spine 7. He wants a license, a wage, and to stop being the family's problem.
- Marek's flaw is taking responsibility before being asked and hiding its cost. His need is to accept chosen interdependence without turning it into ownership.
- Primary romance: Talia Venn, resonance nurse and consent advocate. They are not partners at the opening.
- Family: Lena Senn is Marek's mother (nights at a long-stay clinic on Kestrel Row); Nell Senn is his younger sister, 16; Evan Senn is his missing father.
- Recurring allies: Rafi Pell (roommate, courier), Sera Quill (Spine 7 lead repairer), Asha Reed (Movement IV onward), Dessa Kwan (from Chapter 10).
- Central antagonist: Dr. Iona Sorn, the Conductor. Oren Vey, Iven Sore and Leo Marr represent commercial, institutional and privacy pressures before the Crown Clause's final use.

## Power rules

- A share carries observations and technique structure, not private thoughts, innate talent, or full memories.
- Consent is scoped and can be withdrawn. Use, Remember and Derive are separate permissions. Silence, proximity and a pre-signed workplace form are not agreement.
- Connections produce aftertaste and attention debt. Overload can cause physical symptoms, emotional bleed, and lasting unmooring.
- A hinge is not merely a missing boundary label. It deletes the boundary-release beat that should apply the limit and complete the practice, then installs a key-holder's cue as the learner's expected replacement; it narrows practical independence but is not universal mind control.
- The opening brace is distributed by Threadline from an unnamed upstream technique bundle. Threadline is the contracted assembler and seller, not the author of the Crown Clause.
- Marek begins at Stage 0, Latent Counterpoint. His eventual stages end with Open Weave and the final Unbinding.

### The kit and its paper trail

- **Threadline Practicer Kit, Rescue Brace, rev 4-19.** Assembly lot **TL-4-19-0311**. Assembled by the Threadline Service Depot, Lattice. Service ref **SB-4-19-03**.
- The bundle label's **upstream technique bundle** and **upstream licensor** lines are both ruled and both **empty**, drawn with the same rule on the same day. This must stay unanswered through Chapter 50.
- Service notice **SB-4-19-03** reads: *Return contact, revised for district distribution. Not a fault. Do not claim. Prior practice continues unchanged.* It is the first thread that can be followed upward.
- The 4-19 **instructor notes booklet** is eleven pages. Page 3 sets out the four-beat drill; page 4 carries the emergency header *where a local node does not answer, the last approved civic sequence is used as the provisional response*. It states the fourth beat is a support beat, that the learner cannot perform it alone and is not expected to, that the instructor confirms scope aloud on the beat, that the learner releases on the instructor's confirmation, and that the learner must not proceed on a self-initiated release. **No page in the booklet carries a name, a date, or a set of initials, and Chapter 7 makes no claim about where the substitution came from.** Marek names four live possibilities in Chapter 7 — a bad print run, a card printed against the wrong revision, a supplier assembling a bundle out of somebody else's book, or a person who sat down and chose it — and states plainly that he cannot tell which, because a chosen omission and an accidental one look identical from inside a store room. **The design-approval entry and revision history that make the substitution deliberate do not appear anywhere in Chapters 1–10 and are reserved for the Chapter 25 midpoint proof.** Chapters 1–10 establish only the absence, the script's own words, and the 3-08/4-19 wording difference (*the practitioner confirms aloud* against *the instructor confirms aloud*).
- The Institute's manual is **3-08**. Beat four on 3-08 reads: *The practitioner confirms aloud, at this point, what the brace has been permitted to touch, and returns the next decision to the subject of the brace.* Rusk personally requisitioned the 3-08 reprint on the eleventh of March, four months before 4-19 landed, from an eight-credit teaching budget. The manual is never updated during the volume.
- The drill sequence is four beats: **anchor, load, close, scope**.
- The nine crates were moved from the open kit store into a chained caged store, the **crates intact and the wax seals on them**, on a laminated card wired to the cage reading **TRAINING STOCK — 4-19 — LOCKED PENDING SERVICE. Access: department only. Equipment handling only.** Locked, not quarantined.
- A Threadline field service engineer, **Petar Vance**, services 4-19 at the Institute, both satellite sites, and the Saltmarket clinic. He is not a villain; he is a service engineer protecting warranty exposure.
- **O. Vey, Operations — Threadline** is the authorizing name on the department's purchase docket. Named on paper only; he does not appear in person in Movement I.

## Mystery and ending constraints

- The original Civic Compact required a visible Return Path and did not permit silence as agreement. Iona inserted the hidden Crown Clause into a working copy of its emergency protocol during the Marrow Falls outage; it treats silence as provisional consent and seeds hinge techniques.
- Evan Senn deliberately severed his own link to interrupt a Crown response; he is not resurrected and leaves no hidden inheritance.
- Iona's final conflict is the Nine-Minute Hush, which begins only in Volume 15. In Volume 13 the coalition exposes the Crown Root Interface as a council-witnessed physical maintenance credential, not a personal power. Marek refuses the Crown Key, distributes the Return Path, and destroys that interface, giving up city-wide inspection and emergency validation while retaining only consent-based access to local networks.
- The ending is the Civic Commons: a voluntary, inspectable public institution with local councils, withdrawal rights, maintenance, and no single owner.
- The Assembly appoints Talia a provisional independent consent ombud in Volume 11 and formally confirms the role after the final crisis. She is not Marek's subordinate or reward; their relationship remains a chosen, bounded partnership.
- No new final enemy, hidden bloodline, secret higher system, or universal consent is permitted.
