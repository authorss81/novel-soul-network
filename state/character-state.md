**A man of about thirty-six**, who stood outside that door for about nine minutes in the wet on the Sunday of Chapter 1093 with his hand flat on the door and then took it off and did not knock, who came in because a woman at a counter told him what happens in that room, who said out loud that he had come to see whether the room was like the sentence, and who was asked nothing at all for about forty minutes. **On the Monday of Chapter 1099 he asked Marek at that counter to put his name at the bottom of a sheet about an awning outside that shop and was refused.** He is on two pages of this movement and is not the man of about thirty-four, who has never been through that door.

**A man of about forty-nine**, who is on Chapter 1097 alone and asks one question out loud at that bench, which is whether the man of about thirty is coming back next Thursday, and gets back about four words.

**A woman of about forty-five**, who is on Chapter 1091 and Chapter 1096 and Chapter 1098 and has been in that shed more than once and has said out loud that she thought the first answer would be about his mother, that she put nothing in at all for about nine minutes on that Thursday, and that a man at a counter in another district has been talking about that shed and that she cannot repeat a word of it.

**A woman of about fifty-eight**, who is on Chapter 1097 and Chapter 1099, who asked at that counter for a note of her two visits and was told the two visits are worth the same as a lamp and that the lamp is on a sheet, and who has money in a coat and has said out loud that she is not going to put it in that envelope tonight.

## 2. WHO SAID SOMETHING NEW, AND NOBODY'S WANT CHANGED

**The man of about thirty answered all three questions out loud, one at a time, in front of about nine people, and told them before he began that he had had three weeks to find out whether he would say them the same and that he would not.** What he would put in was about a kitchen in a second district and about a woman in that kitchen he is not related to, and **this file records that and does not record the sentence, and no page of this movement repeats it or carries it.** What he would keep out was four days and a bus and about four people, and who could stop him was his mother saying so, and **both of those are printed once, in his mouth, on the one page where he says them, and are carried into no other chapter of this movement.** He came back on the Thursday after, was asked nothing at all for about two hours, and said out loud that what has come back through two districts is not his answer but what the room did after it. **He wants the same thing he wanted three weeks ago and he has established that he cannot have it, and his want has not changed.**

**The man of about fifty-one asked everybody and got a rhyme, and said out loud that he is not going to spend one of the about nine on the man who gave him three, and said out loud that the light in that shed is his to pay for after three weeks of not saying so, and fed that meter on a Sunday without a word.** His want has not changed and he is still right to hold it. **The woman of about fifty-four**, who is on all ten pages, had the flex lamp wound round her hand on the Thursday, put a coat hanger under the flex on the Friday, fought with a fly over a bowl of water for eight days, tried a saucer, tried two saucers, took both off on the Wednesday night and put the cloth back, then fed the fly with bread for a day, then gave up and went back to the cloth. **The woman of about thirty-eight** was refused a note of her two visits and is told a bin is not worth thanking anybody for. **The woman of about thirty-four is on no page of this movement at all and was asked nothing anywhere in it, and the woman of about thirty-nine is named in no scene and no load-book row and was asked nothing by anybody.** Talia is on no page of this movement at all and was thanked by nobody and saved nobody.
