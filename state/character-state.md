- **MAREK SENN, 22. UNCHANGED AND STANDING EXACTLY WHERE HE WAS STANDING ON THE FIRST PAGE OF THIS STRETCH OF DAYS AND ON THE LAST, AND NO NEW CAPABILITY IS DESCRIBED ON ANY OF THE TWENTY CHAPTERS OF THIS VOLUME.** He is not faster, not wiser, not more certain and not more liked. **What he did in twenty days is hear one printed sheet with nothing in the box on it, be asked to carry a sentence up a stair and say no, carry nine chairs in nineteen trips, not be in the room when a book opened, be given a figure and refuse to write it down, be told a cage is not a hole, watch a man put nine sheets back in nine cages, refuse a man with a folder a figure he really does not have, walk nine hundred yards to ask a man of fifty-eight four questions and get four answers, and be given back a question by a courier before he was asked it.**
- **HIS TWENTY SMALL THINGS. His ten from the first stretch: he does not ask the man of about forty to come back on the Wednesday; he does not ask who carried a question to a place four days of bus travel away; he does not tell a woman of about fifty-nine the number of rooms or what four of them said; he does not walk past the bottom of the Saltmarket stair; he does not ask for the fourth run's sheet to keep; he does not offer nine pounds; he does not ask a woman of about twenty-four to finish the five pieces of paper she has left; he does not take a strip of paper off a form home; he does not say who told a man at a gate that a kitchen stopped it; he does not go up the stair in Saltmarket. His ten from the second stretch: he does not take a sheet out of the box to keep; he does not ask who carried the question to the ninth place; he does not ask a woman of about fifty-nine to hold it again; he does not ask the woman of about twenty-four what the word on the ninth sheet was; he does not write two hundred and six down; he does not ask which cage a man of forty-four has never emptied; he does not take the strip with DECISION on it and put it in a fourth pocket; he does not say one hundred and one; he does not ask a man of about forty-nine whether the folder is the same folder; he does not ask for the ninth sheet.**
- **HIS TWENTY WANTS, ALL DISTINCT. To be the person the fourth run was printed for; to hear a question read out in a room where he is not the only one being asked it; to be the first person to say that a room of nine people is not a street; to be in the room when the book opened; to be the person who has the number and is asked to keep it; to empty a cage; to be one of the eleven people who were not in a room; to be the person who printed the word on the form; to be the person a number gets checked against; and to be a sheet that comes back.** Plus the ten from the first stretch, which are in the archive of this file. **THE WANT OF BEING ASKED IS ON THE PAGE THREE TIMES IN THIS MANUSCRIPT ACROSS ITS LIFE AND WAS NOT USED A FOURTH TIME, AND HIS TEN WANTS OF THE SECOND STRETCH WERE CHECKED AGAINST THOSE TEN AND AGAINST THE TEN BEFORE THEM BEFORE ANY OF THEM WAS WRITTEN.**
- **HIS TWENTY NOTICES ON NO FORM. From the first stretch: four hundred and fifty sheets in a bin outside a post office; a steel rail by a door with a cartridge in it; a woman of about twenty-four who had put nine sheets in a blue folder; a mark in a hand that was not his own; a black bin bag of unposted returns; the second column of a form; a woman standing at a counter who had read the leaflet and folded it; a wire cage behind a post office; a telephone box with no telephone in it; and a strip of paper with glue on it. From the second stretch: a rubber band replaced twice on the hinge of a cage; four cardboard boxes with a scuff on the top face; a sheet of A4 with *raised* written over *show*; eleven chairs in a hall and nine wanted; a man counting nineteen and stopping at nineteen; a machine edge on three sides of a strip and a hand-cut edge on the fourth; a folder with the bottom edge gone soft on the side that sits against a body; a fire exit sign and a door beside it that is not the fire exit and has no closer on it; a pen that went back into the block in a different hole; and a word in capitals in the middle of a page of lower case.**
- **LEO MARR, ABOUT THIRTY-FIVE, IS ON THE PAGE ONCE, AT CHAPTER 665, AND SHE IS SPENT HERE.** She came to a counter and not to a room, said in advance that she was not going to be the fourth person to offer money to a woman four days of bus travel there and back, and gave out loud in about four seconds a figure she has had for about nine years and has never given to anybody: **two hundred and six wire cages outside post offices in this city.** She is given control of nothing, no chair, no form, no thanks, and is on no list. **She called a cage a hole with a lid on it and was told by a man of twenty-two that it is not, agreed that it is not, and said she had used the word because a figure about places would be asked where it came from and a figure about holes would not.** She is right about the size of the two numbers and wrong about a reason, and both halves were said out loud. **She is not redeemed, is not the villain, is not the author of the printed sheet, and does not know that the printed sheet exists as anybody's argument. She is not in Movement I.**
- **THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO EMPTIES THE CAGES IS THE FIRST OF THE TWO NAMES THIS MOVEMENT SPENT.** He has emptied nineteen wire cages on one round on a Friday for about nine years, about four hundred and fifty printed sheets have come out of those nineteen in about four weeks and the figure is his and is on nothing, and nine of the nineteen had a mark already in the box ruled at the bottom of the sheet. **There is no rule anywhere for what happens to a sheet that comes back with a mark on it: a woman in a first floor in a second district told him about a drawer about four years ago and the man who had the round before him told him about the same drawer, and the drawer is in that office and he does not go into it. He put all nine back.** He is right about every part of it and nobody told him he was right and he was not thanked. **He said in about four seconds that lifting the flap is the job he is paid for and that working out what happens and knowing whether it is allowed are two different jobs and he gets paid for one.** He has never opened one cage in nine years, on a wall by the works with three walls, and told a stranger about it at the door in four seconds because it was not a secret, and said *go and be somebody else's problem about it*. He is not a villain and not a victim and is not on a list.
- **THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-NINE WHO OPENS THE DOORS OF A HALL IS THE SECOND.** She is in a third district, she has been opening those doors for about eleven years, she held a showing of hands in a room with nine chairs in it, and she said in about nine seconds that nine people in a room is nine people in a room and is not a road and has no door number. **She said she would get nine, that she has had eleven on other Wednesdays, that one was asleep and one was at work and one had a shift that did not finish and one has a child with nobody else on a school night, and that there are seven she cannot account for and would not invent seven reasons for a man at a counter to divide by something.** She refused a percentage of non-participation in nine words and gave the reason that there is no arrangement to support and she will not be the reason that a sentence about a majority of a district exists. **She is not asked to do it again and did not volunteer it and is not on a list and holds no authority whatever.**
- **THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO KEEPS A WAREHOUSE IS THE PERSON WHO ADDED THE EIGHT NUMBERS UP IN HIS HEAD.** He did it on a stair on Wednesday night of Movement II, told nobody, came down to a shop on the Thursday morning specifically to be rid of it, was not asked for it, and left his folder on the counter and went out without it. **He has said one number out loud in this manuscript that he made up and told everybody he made it up, and he has now made a second one up in his head and told nobody at all.** He is not thanked, is given nothing, and is not on a list.
- **THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-NINE WITH A FOLDER ASKS FOR THE NUMBER OF DOORS AND IS REFUSED IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS.** He says his paper goes to four people who cannot be asked in a room in about four weeks and that a number he found himself is worth about nine times a number he was given. **He has two figures of his own and would rather have the one from the man who empties the cages, and he is not given either.** He said four people had refused him that week and that he could tell the reasons and would not say which one this was. He is not a villain and is owed nothing.
- **RAFI PELL, ABOUT TWENTY-TWO, IS ON THE PAGE ONCE IN MOVEMENT II, AT CHAPTER 670, AND GIVES BACK A QUESTION BEFORE HE IS ASKED IT.** He is a courier and a mechanic and is in a worker co-operative of about eleven people who own their own hours. He came in with a bag of chain he did not want explained and said he was not going to ask a man of twenty-two to hold anything, and gave the reason in about four seconds: **a person who holds nine numbers becomes the place the numbers come back to, and then has to be able to say what one word on one sheet was, and a woman of about twenty-four told a room about the word and did not tell the room the word, and she was right not to.** He said *don't be pleased with me, it's not a favour, I just don't want to be the reason.* He is not forgiven anything and is not given a chair and was not thanked.
- **THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO WHO CHAIRS ABOUT NINE CHAIRS ASKED NINE ROOMS ONE QUESTION IN NINE BUILDINGS IN ONE AFTERNOON AND SAID SHE WOULD NOT SAY WHAT IT WAS FOR.** She put a tenth copy on a counter and said she wanted and was not asking, and he said no in four seconds and gave a reason that was not the reason. **On the Friday of the movement's close she said the ninth room will be asked again in about four weeks or it will not, that she does not know which, and that she will not decide it in advance; and when a man said that sounds like a rule she said it is not a rule, it is a Wednesday, and that most things in this city are Wednesdays and none of them has been written down, which is why none of them can be blamed for anything.** She is the only person entitled to say what a set of nine addresses is and she is not the chair of this volume.
- **THE WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-FOUR CAME UP THE SALTMARKET STAIR WITH A BAG AND SAID ONE THING TO A FACE AND THE BOOK OPENED.** She said she had counted the people and nobody had counted the people: nine rooms asked a question on Wednesday afternoon, eight wrote a number and one wrote a word, and in all nine the number was larger than the number of people actually in the room, out by eleven in hers. **She is not thanked, is given nothing, and is not on a list, and she said in about four seconds that she would put a returned sheet on a table and not go back in twice and that it was not fair, it was just what she does.** She has not been asked to carry anything again and was not asked about the ninth sheet.
- **THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY IS STILL IN THE CHAIR THE DOOR IS BEHIND AFTER HALF PAST SIX ON EVERY ONE OF THE TWENTY DAYS AND HER PAGE IS STILL THE EIGHTH OF EIGHT IN A RING BINDER ON THE BACK SHELF, AND IT DID NOT COME OUT ON ANY OF THEM.** Nothing whatever was put to her and nothing ever is, and nobody has ever opened that binder in her presence or out of it, and there is nothing owed to her in this city by anybody in it and no apology of any kind has ever been offered to her.
- **THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE FOUR DAYS OF BUS TRAVEL THERE AND BACK IS ON NO PAGE OF EITHER MOVEMENT.** She has a season ticket on the Wednesday and not on the Saturday, she said no to two people about nine pounds in Movement I, she put a sheet in a box in her own supply room with about nine people waiting, and **she is not asked for money a third time and a fourth person who said in advance she would not be one was a woman who had kept a figure for nine years.** She is not grateful, not furious, on nobody's side, given nothing, thanked for nothing, and not rescued.
- **TALIA VENN, TWENTY-FOUR, IS ON NO PAGE OF EITHER MOVEMENT, WHICH IS A DECISION AND NOT AN OMISSION.** A printed card in her name went up on the door of that repair shop in Movement I with a second line whose whole job was to name him in order to remove him, and he did not take it down. **Neither of them says a word about the other in the third person in any of the twenty chapters of this volume so far, and that is the whole of the finding and it is a finding and not a scene.**

- **COLIN NALL, ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT, IS THE FIRST OF THE TWO NAMES MOVEMENT I SPENT AND HE HAD NO NAME AT ALL UNTIL A REVIEW-REPAIR PASS GAVE HIM ONE, AND THAT IS RECORDED HERE BECAUSE A LATER MOVEMENT THAT INVENTS A SECOND NAME FOR THE SAME MAN IS A CONTINUITY BREAK.** He owns a two-man print shop on a road off Saltmarket, he is sixty next year, there is him and a lad on Thursdays, and he prints on Tuesdays and Fridays. **He printed the sheets and he set none of the type and he will not say who set it, and he paid about nine pounds for a tea chest of dead type in the spring, and the paragraph came out of it. He is right about the paper, the ink, the count and the money, and he is going to stop because nobody has given him an order and so there is nothing to invoice. He is not rescued, not thanked, does not soften, is not the villain and is not a device.** He says his name exactly once, at Chapter 654, and he says it when he is asked what is on the fold of paper in the drawer: *my name*, and it has never once been an invoice, and Marek says he is not going to write it down, and the printer says he was never going to and that this is not why he said it. **The name is said once more in Movement I, at Chapter 660, which is the permitted use from 655 onward. Two of the volume's about four permitted uses are spent and two are held, and `outline/volume-14.md` is the document that says so.** The machine is a Heidelberg older than the partnership and there is one other in this city, owned by a man of about thirty-three who does not print. He keeps a chase with nine ruled lines in it and nothing in them and has had it since the spring and has not thrown it out. He gave the money for three runs in a fold of paper in a drawer and never called it anything.
