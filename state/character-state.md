# Character State

Updated after every batch. Tracks where each character is at the end of the last written chapter, what they want, and what they will refuse to do. Canon detail lives in `state/continuity.md`; this file tracks movement.

## Marek Senn — protagonist

**At the end of Chapter 20:** twenty-two, third-year, failing, no license. Headline transcript line: *performance dependent on external cue*, due to be reprinted in March and, if he signs, amended to *supplementary completion route pending* — an amendment he has refused twice on the record. Keeps a hand-written load book with a *Do not know* section and four headed sections on the last full page. **He has worked out that he spent nine weeks being the word in the room, and he has written down that the reason is not helpfulness.**

**Physical state:** bad right shoulder from a jammed gate in Nine Bridges, still bad at the end of Chapter 20, never re-injured. Split thumbnail on the hand he braces frames with; pencil behind one ear; work gloves under his school jacket. Recurring low-load symptoms after contact — sour or metallic taste, a two-second loss of a named color, a headache behind the left eye, a smell of a fish cold store at five in the morning that is not his and did not go. Sleep debt, rent overdue.

**Wants now, in order:** a room, a date, and three people who do not agree. Underneath that, the old want, still running: to be the man other people can rely on so that nobody ever has to ask him for anything. **Underneath that, the one he found on Saturday at a bench: not to be believed, but to be in a room where a question is allowed to stay open.**

**Learned this batch, in his own words:**
- A plate takes a shape when you put a load through it and nobody takes it off, and copper that has done it once will do it every time. (Ch 12, Rusk, volunteered; Ch 13, Talia, on the plate in her own hand)
- A care-link is a visible badged emergency with a reason, a duration on the clock rather than on judgement, and a review, and you read it out loud to a man on the floor before it is needed. (Ch 13)
- A fair sentence is not the same as a useful one, and I am going to want them to be the same every single time for the rest of my life. (Ch 13, Talia, and he writes it down)
- Somebody who does the right thing on purpose for forty years still says no to a stranger who wants a report, and the no is worth more than the yes. (Ch 13, Mal Farrow)
- Two performances of one thing is one thing. A false agreement is not a failure of the network; it is a failure of the man who wanted it. (Ch 17, and this is a violation of his own Chapter 7 rule rather than a rediscovery of it)
- Withdrawal is not betrayal and a reason about you is not a reason about me. (Ch 17, Rafi; Ch 18, written down)
- A record of what was done to a man belongs to the man. (Ch 18)
- I have been the person in every room who decides whether the room is told anything. I cannot bear a question being left in a room, so I go and make sure a question gets answered, and a question answered by me is a question taken off the table. (Ch 20, at a bench, with a marker cap in his teeth)

**Will refuse:** to use a share he has not been given; to accept a correct transcript that requires a false cause; to let Tam's recovery plan be written for him; to quote all but the worst of a boy's own words; to have Talia's scope renewed for him; to answer a no with a better question; to be thanked in a corridor.

**Cannot yet do:** hold a line alone; perform the fourth beat; reproduce his four seconds; tell the difference between a practice that grew out of work and one installed into it; ask a stranger for something he cannot explain in one sentence; sit in a room with a plate on a wall and not want anything from it; get a two-way answer out of anybody who has no obligation to him.

**Flaw, in operation, now named by three people and by him:** he is the key-holder. Talia in a vehicle in week 9, Tam on a piece of order form on the Thursday of week 9, Rafi in his own front room on the Saturday of week 9, Roos in her office on the Friday of week 8, and Sarr in a corridor on the Sunday of week 8. **He has now written down that this is not a virtue and that the nine weeks of damage came out of good manners.**

## Talia Venn — primary romance, consent advocate

**At the end of Chapter 20:** twenty-four, resonance nurse on the Saltmarket route, trained in the same course family as Marek's department and says so. Has fitted twenty-six 4-19 braces since September and has an *interference noted* column with six entries in it, one of which is a hip belt she altered herself which did not let go, which she wrote up as *variable release under load*. Has produced a visible, badged, written care-link for a mobile route with no ambulance within nine minutes, and reads it aloud to a man on a floor. **Has run four sites' worth of moral weight through one Saturday morning post.**

**Position after Chapter 13:** she refused to widen Mal Farrow's care-link to suit a student's evidence, and then, when he asked in the right way and in writing, wrote a scope in ninety seconds in a vehicle with the patient reading it upside down and signing his own version of it, and closed it on the page forty minutes later with a review date. **Position after Chapter 18 — the important one:** she closed her Chapter 7 scope and refused to renew it, and gave the reason: *a scope is not a relationship, and it is not a thing that gets extended because a person needs it to go on being true.* She has refused him five times. She is not softening any of them and she has told him so.

**Want:** a standard that survives fear, fatigue, and a person who cannot explain what a default means. Now also: a record of what was done to her patients that she does not have to defend on a chart in front of a registration board.

**Flaw, in operation:** she can see a coercion and be slow to see an ordinary dependence. She took two days to work out that a changed offer needs a changed sheet, and she did not get it right the first time. **In this batch she is also slow to see that the man she is correcting is not a special case, which is the thing she told him on the telephone in Chapter 20: *if you cannot stand in a room and be told no by a stranger, then you have not stopped arranging, you have only changed the sentence.***

**Will refuse:** to soften a correction; to accept a signature, a device, a proximity or a pre-signed form as a yes; to sign a retention she has not written down; to release a patient's chart because a student wants it; to become a fourth source.

**Boundary with Marek:** collaborators, and now something with a cost in it. No warmth has been exchanged and none is owed. The last exchange was a refusal on each side and the naming of the Choir of Practice as information rather than help. **She has told him she is not going to help him find a fourth person, and he accepted it.**

## Rafi Pell — roommate, first node, physical counterweight

**At the end of Chapter 20:** twenty-one, courier, carrying tools he does not own. His balance step has no fourth beat and he has compensated for two years with his left shoulder against a wall. **He has now named the mechanism himself, unprompted: the wall is a place where somebody has to be.** He has a two-year shoulder mark.

**What changed:** he was put in a room he was never told was a room, on the Wednesday of week 9, by a man who never announced there was a thing to leave — and he has said, in plain words, that being polite to him is the same as not telling him. **He is not withdrawing. He is going to count to two out loud, visibly, before he answers anything, for the rest of his life, and to be embarrassed about it, and he is staying.** He has also told Marek that the first no is going to cost more than the first yes did, because the first yes was free. He has heard about the Thursday class and predicted, accurately, that Marek will not last twenty minutes in it.

**Want:** keep the rented repair bay, be a person whose hands are worth something in a city that rewards credentials.

**Flaw, in operation:** said yes before he understood the boundary. **The remedy is now mechanical and public: he counts to two out loud before he agrees to anything, and he is not going to be embarrassed about it later when Marek has decided he is being difficult.**

**Status against the volume plan:** the first repeat victim, not the opponent. His dependence and recovery belong to Movement IV and Volume 02 and must not be resolved in Volume 01. **His Chapter 20 withdrawal was correct, was not punishment, and he has said so in the room and out loud afterwards.**

He has had eleven of eleven attempts come back *nothing, I just stopped*, he has logged breaking his own column two, and he has produced a partial result: eleven seconds with his own right hand flat on the top edge of a nine-kilo box at 07:08 on the Wednesday of week 9, which failed at 07:04 on the strap.

**What he has said that matters this batch:** *This is not a fourth beat. This is the opposite of a fourth beat.* *You do not get to arrange anybody else.* *Get me three people who do not agree.* *I've got no technique, two tendons in my forearm, and I've read more about the inside of this thing than you have.* He walked eleven minutes in the cold to give Marek a copy of his own imitation of Marek's voice, on purpose, so that Marek would be the only person in the city who knew. He made Marek promise to read all of his words or none of them, including the finger.

**What he has refused:** Rusk, the department physio, Odile Fenn, Wick Oyelaran, Dessa Kwan, and Mal Farrow. **Farrow's is the only door still open and it is open on Farrow's condition: when the boy asks, and not before.** No observer has been found. That hunt belongs to Movement III.

**Function:** the volume's most exacting critic and the person who proves that a successful rescue can create a new harm. Never a prop and never a reconciliation.
