## THE TWO WHO ARE NAMED NOW

**MAREK, twenty-two, and it is the first movement of this manuscript in eighteen volumes in which he is named on every page he is on.** He is at a bench until two, a counter until about six and then about two hours of work in a fourth district. **He has a want in this movement and it is stated out loud on Chapter 941 and it is that nine people be told there is a date that is about them. He gets nowhere with it on every one of the ten days and he is the one who is stopped on every one of the ten days.** He asks the wrong question first and knows he is asking it wrongly while it is still going out of his mouth. He asks Talia one question in a room on Chapter 943 and gets the answer and the cost and does not ask her to use her office. He has a sentence ready on Chapter 949 about her and does not say it. **He signs a request about nine people he cannot name on Chapter 948 and it is the first document in this matter with a name on it and the name is not one of the nine's, and he has said since that he did not know when he signed that the page would not come to him and that he would have signed it anyway.** He ends the movement having asked a woman who works for him to read a page he is not permitted to read, and having been told that he will not be able to help.

**TALIA, twenty-four, and she is named too, from Chapter 941 onward.** **She did not use the office she holds on him at all in this movement, and that is a fact about these ten days and not a statement about any other ten days.** She is in the room on Chapter 946 on a Sunday about a clinic rota and hears the whole of it and says the only useful thing anybody says to a woman of about fifty-one that afternoon, which is that looking is free and that if the day turns out to be hers nobody will be able to take it off her. **She is in the room on Chapter 949 and she knows what the unasked question was, and she did not look at him while he was deciding, and she decided not to look at him on purpose, and one of those two was a rule she made for herself and the other was not.** She comes down a stair on Chapter 950 having heard the whole of an arrangement about a page from half a landing up and says one sentence to the wall rather than to him, and has been annoyed with herself about that for two days. **She approved nothing and signed nothing and saved him from nothing on any of the ten days.**

## THE PEOPLE WHO WERE THERE

**The woman of about forty-three, who holds the room above the line and made the sheet.** She is told on Chapter 941 who a piece of paper is for and says she does not know, in front of about nine people, in one sentence, and then says that there are four lines at the top of the form and one of them says no person on this form was asked anything. **She has had that sheet on her table since before the spring and has never once been able to put a face to a row on it, and she says that as a fact about herself and not as a complaint.** She wants the printed form taken off a wall on Chapter 944 and is refused by the man who put it up there. **On Chapter 945 she says the sentence this volume is built out of — that if she writes the name it is an answer the woman did not give, and that if she does not write it down then a woman has said a thing in a room and it is gone — and then she refuses, on Chapter 946, to say the name out loud a second time.** She refused a sixth column on Chapter 947 on the ground that it would say what the room told and not what anybody was told.

**The man of about thirty-three, who copies files out of rooms in other districts.** He says on Chapter 941 that somebody wrote the nine down once, and on Chapter 942 that it was a room in a second district that is a landlord and a filing system, and that he has been in it twice and has never seen the hand. **He has said the two things he has to say on Chapter 947 and neither was asked for: that a column is cheaper than a person, and that is why it will happen, and that he said the second time on Chapter 929 of Volume 18 because he had worked out he had only ever said it once.** On Chapter 945 he tells a woman of about fifty-one that he has stood at that rail next to her and does not finish the sentence.

**The man of about fifty-two, who keeps a register and who said on Chapter 940 of Volume 18 that nobody is holding the sheet.** He brings a six-column version of the form out of his coat on Chapter 947, with the word *told* penciled over the sixth, and says it has been in the coat since the winter before. He offers to be the name at the bottom of the column and **about four people in that room say nearly the same thing within about nine seconds of each other, and one of them tells him he has been right four times and has not been the person who did anything about any of them, and he has said since that she was right.** The sheet went back into the coat and he has not taken it out since.

**The man of about thirty-eight, who keeps a clinic in a fourth district.** He carried the printed form out of that room and across the city on the Tuesday of week 315 of Volume 18 and put it up on a wall himself, and on Chapter 944 he refuses to take it down and gives a reason out of nine years of a clinic where people are told what day they are coming in and are not asked whether that suits them. **He is not made to give way on any page of this movement and his position is correct and is argued against by nobody except by a man of twenty-two who says so out loud, twice.**

**The woman of about fifty-one, who is one of the nine and is new to these pages.** She came into that room on the Saturday of day 2110 having read the notice on a wall in a passage, and gave her name out loud into about nine people, and asked whether the day on that sheet was hers. **Nobody in that room could tell her. She came back on the Sunday at about one unasked, and when he asked her whether she wanted her name written down she said no in about four seconds, before he had finished the word, and it was not written.** She asked what she was to do with the day and nobody in that room said one word. **She is the first person in this matter to put a name into the air and the first person in it to refuse to have that name put on paper, and both of those happened without anybody persuading her of either.**

**The man of about sixty-one, who holds the records room, and who is new to these pages.** Nine years at that desk, one chair at it and a second on the landing that nobody waits in, and he reads everything standing up. He refused the man of about thirty-three twice and gave the reason out loud for the first time on Chapter 942: a request about four years ago for a copy of a page, a good request, and an envelope that has not come back, and a letter written in his own hand in his own room about the envelope. **On Chapter 948 he refuses to put a page into the hands of a person he cannot name and refuses to put it in his own name either, and then reads Marek's name back to him off the request. On Chapter 949 he comes up that stair uninvited with the book under his arm, says he has found the page, and will not read it out, because he is a landlord and a filing system and does not decide what is said about what he holds.** He is not wrong on any of it and no page of this movement says he is.

**SERA QUILL, forty-five, lead repairer at Civic Spine 7.** She is on no page of Volume 18's sixty and she is on the last page of this movement and it is the first page of hers in the sequence this volume was planned on. **Marek caught her on a stair on Chapter 950 and asked her to read a page and he said *mine* first, which is the wrong order and which he has thought about since.** She said no to the page and yes to reading it and told him she will not do it in that room, because that room has spent four years learning it cannot hold a person's name. She read the request standing up and turned it round. **She said that opening it makes her the person holding it, that she will have to decide what to do with nine names before she tells him whether she has read them, that he will not be able to help, and that she is not doing it on her own — and that she has four people in her head and has not asked one of them.**

**A woman of about sixty, who has two copies of the sheet and has had them for a long time.** On Chapter 942 she said the two copies disagree in the fifth column, that in one of them it is ruled and in the other it is not, and that in both of them there is nothing in it. **Nobody in that room asked her whose copies they are or where the two copies are, and she noticed about four seconds after she had stopped talking.**

## AND THE TWO ABSENCES THAT ARE RECORDED AND NOT CLOSED

**`Rafi Pell`, `Dessa Kwan`, `Oren Vey`, `Iven Sore` and `Lena Senn`.** All five are at zero on all ten of these files. **No page was invented for any of them and the reason is a writer's judgement rather than a census: none of the five is the right person for a records room in a second district, a clinic corridor in a fourth district, a form about nine unnamed people, or a stair. That is a different statement from the Volume 18 finding and it is deliberately given here as a judgement and not as a defence.**

**The woman of about thirty, who is not a character and is apparatus.** Behind the shut door of the fourth of those four rooms from half past six on nine of the ten days and from half past one on the Sunday. Not named, not a category, not counted, not described, not asked a question, and nobody has apologised to her. **She is not the same person as the woman of about fifty-one and the two are never in a room together and no page of this movement puts them in one.**

**Iona Sorn** is the last enemy in this manuscript, is in public custody, is unanswered, is not absolved, and is at zero on all ten of these files.
