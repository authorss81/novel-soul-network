**Volume 13 is COMPLETE at Chapter 650. Movement V is COMPLETE and there is no chapter phase left in this volume; the next phase is `workspace/volume-13/VOLUME-CLOSE.md`, at the volume root. The ceiling of six is at FIVE, with one name held for the close. The ten days' small things, wants and notices are all new and none repeats the twenty before them, and none is a version of the Chapter 567 refused errand, the Chapter 601 unwalked hour, the Chapter 630 unanswered question or the Chapter 631/639 want of being asked, which is now on the page twice and is not used a third time.**

- **MAREK SENN, 22. UNCHANGED AND STANDING EXACTLY WHERE HE WAS STANDING ON THE FIRST PAGE OF THIS STRETCH OF DAYS AS ON THE LAST, AND NO NEW CAPABILITY IS DESCRIBED ON ANY OF THE FIFTY CHAPTERS OF THIS VOLUME.** He is not faster, not wiser, not more certain and not more liked. **What he did in ten days is go to a town he has the address of, get handed a plan he is not entitled to hand anybody, write nine addresses down from memory and get one of them wrong in one place, watch a woman of about twenty-four carry nine pieces of paper out of a room, see a word he had ready said by somebody else first, watch a man of about thirty-four go past a shop twice without coming in, refuse to be told the answer to a question that needed a room a day and a night of bus travel away, hear how many people an arrangement does not reach, and not go up a stair he has not been up in about four weeks.**
- **HIS TEN SMALL THINGS, ALL DISTINCT.** He does not write down the name of a woman of about twenty-nine who reads things out loud in shops, and writes the day, the room and the word pen instead. He does not put two of the nine hand copies side by side, on a bench, with all nine of them in front of him, for about four seconds. He does not ask a woman in a supply room to put a condition on a second sheet, and so puts it in his head a second time. He does not say the word before the woman entitled to say it says it. He does not put his own name anywhere, because there is no column for it. He does not apologise to a man in a kitchen, or to a man in a room, and gives the reason in about four seconds in each case. He does not write four hundred on anything. He does not go up the stair in Saltmarket. He does not repeat a sentence about a month that a woman said to a hand truck and he lets it die in that room. He does not say who told a man of about fifty at a gate that a kitchen stopped it, and does not ask the woman four days of bus travel away what she was told and by whom.
- **HIS TEN UNSAID WANTS, ALL DISTINCT.** To be the person a name is said to. To be written on one of the nine. To be one of the nine rooms. To have the nine pieces of paper be nine conditions. To be able to say the count out loud to somebody in this city. To be told to go away, in about four minutes, in a supply room, and he would have gone. To be able to give a number of his own to a person at a counter. To be the man a gate and a clinic both tell. To be asked to carry one. **THE WANT OF BEING ASKED IS ON THE PAGE TWICE IN VOLUME 13, AT CHAPTERS 631 AND 639, AND IS NOW ON THE PAGE THREE TIMES IN THIS MANUSCRIPT ACROSS ITS LIFE, AND A VOLUME 14 WRITER HAS TO FIND A FOURTH SHAPE OF IT OR NOT USE IT.**
- **HIS TEN NOTICES ON NO FORM, ALL DISTINCT.** Five things in a fourth pocket in an order he has never written down, and he knows what is on three of them and has never known what is on the fourth. A corner of paper about the size of a thumbnail in a bin under a window. Nine trays in a supply room, eight labelled in another hand and the ninth dated four days old. A ninth peg behind a door that has been empty since the previous Wednesday and a coat that is being worn by somebody nobody in that school will account for. Nine sheets cut from a pad with a blade that was not sharp, and the ninth one that is not an address. Two people standing nine feet apart on a pavement outside a shop, both of them one of the nine, neither knowing it. A lorry in a yard that had not been there since the spring. A number written on the back of a hand in biro and rubbed out and written again. A rectangle of clean concrete under a crate that has not been stacked since the spring. **Four inches of dust under a nineteenth line on a board of two nails, with a clean band about two inches across in the middle of it where a hand went once.** A gap between the third and fourth of about nine chairs that is wider than the gaps between any of the other pairs.
- **THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE IS ON THE PAGE AND SHE IS NOT A SPEND, NOT A REWARD, NOT A RESCUER, AND NOT LARGER THAN SHE IS.** She has run a clinic four days of bus travel there and back, which is a day and a night each way, for about four years, and keeps a supply room in a school on the far side of that town. **She was given authority over the plan in a room in about nine seconds by a man who said in the same breath that he had no right to ask her, and she said no four times in about nine seconds each — that she is not a person who answers for arrangements, that he is not the person to ask, that a person who is owed a month cannot also be given the thing that took it, and that she has been a room for about four years and is not doing it to somebody else with a name on a piece of paper — and then she said yes in about nine seconds, to the boxes on a hand truck, and attached one condition: nothing goes through a person. SHE SAID OUT LOUD THAT IT IS A BILL AND NOT AUTHORITY.** A man of about fifty-one at a desk said it was a lot to put on somebody who has already got it and was told it was less than he had got. A woman of about nineteen with a box of staples did not look up. **He told her she is owed a month and about fourteen weeks and now the rest of it as well, and she said she knew, that nobody is going to give it back, that she does not want it back, that she wanted it said out loud to a person who has to hear it, and that he is the fourth and the other three were not going to do anything about it either.** She is not grateful, not furious, on nobody's side, given nothing, thanked for nothing, and not rescued.
- **NELL FARROW, ABOUT TWENTY-FOUR, IS THE FIFTH SPEND AND SHE IS THE CHEAPEST THING IN THE VOLUME.** She does ironing three days a week on a wide road and has a season ticket and has never been given anything to do by anybody who chairs anything. **She was named in a room in about nine seconds by a woman of about fifty-two, with nothing in it about why**, and the woman of about fifty-two said that not one person in any of the nine buildings will ever find out that she did it, and that this being the arrangement and not an accident. **She said in about four seconds that if a room says no to her she will keep the paper, bring it back, put it on the table and will not go back in twice, and then said that this is not fair, it is just what she does, and went down the stair.** At a doorway at about four she asked in about nine seconds whether it was nine or eight and a description, was told nine, said that was what she had been told, went down and came back up four minutes later with the bag full, and left without waiting to be thanked. **She is not on a list, there is not going to be one, and nothing in this volume improved her situation by one thing.**
- **THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO WHO CHAIRS ABOUT NINE CHAIRS IS THE ONLY PERSON ENTITLED TO SAY WHAT A SET OF NINE ADDRESSES IS, AND SHE SAID IT ONCE, AT CHAPTER 646, IN ABOUT NINE SECONDS, AND DID NOT DEFINE IT, AND IS NOT SAYING IT AGAIN.** She signed a form at Chapter 647 and said in about nine seconds that the two public rules it went under were about nineteen years old, that she did not write them, and that she was not going to pretend she had. She said one word at Chapter 640 and has said nothing else in a room since. **She is not a device, she is not the chair of this volume, and she is not to be given a standing she has not got.**
- **SERA QUILL, FORTY-FIVE, LEAD REPAIRER, IS AN EXISTING NAMED CHARACTER AND NAMING HER IS NOT A SPEND.** She used the condition of Chapter 606 on the Monday of week 232 and is not on the page again in this volume.
- **THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO WORKS IN A KITCHIN IS ON THE PAGE FOUR TIMES IN MOVEMENT V AND IS OWED A THING AND IS OWED IT BY SOMEBODY WHO CANNOT PAY IT.** He was told the route was off by a woman of about twenty-nine who reads things out loud in shops, and he asked one question at a counter in about nine seconds and got the true answer and said that was all right. He went past the shop twice without coming in. He asked the incompleteness question in a room in about nine seconds and was answered in about nine seconds by a person entitled. **He has nine things in grease pencil on the back of his hand and the eighth of them has been gone over with a thumb four times, and there is no other list of the nine in this city, and the man who wrote them was a man with a clipboard at a gate and Marek never gave him that list and has never had it.**
- **THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WITH NINE CRATES IN A YARD HE CANNOT HAND OVER IS ON THE PAGE ONE LAST TIME AT CHAPTER 649 AND IS NOT FORGIVEN.** He stood about nine feet away with his coat on and heard the whole of the incompleteness and was given nothing, promised nothing and told nothing, and went down the stair at about eight. **Eight of the nine crates are stacked and the ninth is not, and the ninth has not been stacked since the spring.** He is not a villain, not owed a thing in this manuscript, and **no scene is coming in which a yard is emptied and this volume does not promise one.** He was told at a sitting in the spring that he is not the person in that room who decides anything, and nothing that happened in this city in about five months has changed that, and nobody has forgiven him or repaired him or thanked him.
- **THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY AT A GATE HAS A DIFFERENT BELIEF AT THE END OF THIS VOLUME AND NOBODY HAS TOLD HIM IT IS DIFFERENT.** He came about nine hundred yards to a shop on a wide road at Chapter 647 and asked the only man in this city who can correct his account, and was not answered, and went out and did not stop in the street. He has stopped believing that a kitchen stopped it and has started believing that it was never written down, and **the second belief is not better and the volume does not say which is.**
- **RAY TOPPING, ABOUT FORTY-ONE, IS THE FOURTH SPEND AND HE IS AT THE END OF THE VOLUME WITH NOTHING.** The same van, the same firm, about nine years. He is the reason the central attempt worked and is not its author, and at the last time he is on the page he is a driver in a garage with a pencil tally rubbed out and begun again. He is not thanked, is given control of nothing, is on no list, does not soften, and nothing that happened in the fortnight improved his situation by one single thing.
- **DR. IONA SORN, FIFTY-THREE, IS SPENT AND IS MENTIONED ONCE IN MOVEMENT V, IN A CORRIDOR, IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS, AS A FACT AND NOT A SCENE: SHE WENT TO THE FINAL ACTIVATION CHAMBER WITH THE REMAINING PATTERN.** She told nobody in this city that she was going, she did not say where it is, and nobody in this city has asked. **She is not made larger in that sentence than she is anywhere else in this manuscript and she may not be made larger in Volume 14.**
- **TALIA VENN, TWENTY-FOUR, IS BACK IN THIS CITY AND IS ON NO PAGE OF MOVEMENT V, WHICH IS A DECISION AND NOT AN OMISSION.** The condition of her return was used on him in Movement IV by another person on another authority. **Neither of them says a word about the other in the third person in any of the fifty chapters of this volume, and that is the whole of the finding and it is a finding and not a scene.**
