## The two spends of Movement I, and what may not be re-spent

1. **A woman of about thirty-four, who carried a form back on a bus and put it on a table.** She works in a second district. On the first Monday she came back from a town two days of bus travel away, having been handed the form at a stop with a timetable on the wall, and she put it down at about eleven in a first floor above a line in Saltmarket, nine feet from a man on a stair who was not asked anything. **She asked the man at the stop three questions on the bus and he answered two: that it is done every day, and that everybody there does it. She asked how long and he said he did not know and that it had not occurred to him that anybody would want to know.** She works in a second district and has four hundred people on a form she did not write, she has been asked by nobody to explain it, she has told nobody what it is, and she says she does not know what it is. On Chapter 829 she says the four words on the form are the four words the man at the stop used and that she wrote them herself on the back of a receipt on the Sunday. On Chapter 830 she stood behind a table in a hall in a fourth district for about seven hours, said almost nothing, and was not thanked and was not asked how she was and says she was not thanked and did not expect to be and that the day was long. **She is not the author of the form, she did not choose the word in the box, and nobody in this city has corrected her.**
2. **A woman of about twenty-nine, who asked the only question anybody asked on the Tuesday.** She is not a member of anything. She asked whether anybody in this city knows whether the thing four hundred people do over there is safe, and nobody in that room answered her, and nobody wrote her anything down, and she did not ask twice. **About four people in that room have said since that they wanted to answer her and could not think of one sentence that would not have been a lie.** On Chapter 824 she told the room rather than the man of about fifty-two that she had asked the man at the stop three questions, and the man said she was right and was not going to ask her to say it again.
